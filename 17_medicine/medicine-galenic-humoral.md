# Galenic medicine

Galen of Pergamon (129-216 CE) — physician to Marcus Aurelius, Commodus, and Septimius Severus — produced the most influential and longest-dominant medical synthesis in Western history. His ~600 surviving works systematized the Hippocratic humoral framework, integrated Aristotelian natural philosophy, and added Galen's own extensive anatomical research (mostly on apes and pigs, as human dissection was prohibited in his Roman context) into a unified theory of physiology, pathology, pharmacology, and therapeutics. The Galenic synthesis was the standard Western medical model for approximately 1700 years.

## The four humors

The basic framework: health is the balance of four humors — blood (warm + moist), yellow bile (warm + dry), black bile (cold + dry), phlegm (cold + moist) — each associated with one of the four primary qualities (hot/cold/wet/dry) and with one of the four elements (air/fire/earth/water). Each individual has a constitutional balance (the *crasis* or temperament) — sanguine, choleric, melancholic, phlegmatic. Disease is excess or deficiency of one humor; treatment restores balance through diet, regimen, pharmacology (drugs of opposite quality), or evacuation (purging, bleeding, emesis).

## The extended transmission

The Galenic corpus was preserved primarily in Greek (Byzantine) and Arabic (Islamic) transmission lines:

- **Byzantine**: continuous Greek-language reading of Galen through the late antique and Byzantine medical schools (Oribasius, Alexander of Tralles, Paul of Aegina).
- **Arabic**: massive 8th-9th c. translation movement at the Bayt al-Ḥikma (Ḥunayn ibn Isḥāq + colleagues translated nearly the entire Galenic corpus); Galen became the foundation of [[medicine-unani|Unani / Tibb-i Yūnānī]].
- **Latin (early)**: limited 6th-12th c. Latin Galen from the Salerno school.
- **Latin (high medieval)**: massive 12th-13th c. retranslation from Arabic at Toledo (Gerard of Cremona); this is the principal route by which Galen entered the European universities.
- **Latin (Renaissance)**: 15th-16th c. retranslation from Greek originals arriving from Byzantine refugees after 1453.

The Galenic theory remained the medical orthodoxy in European universities into the 18th c.; the slow displacement by biomedical anatomy + chemistry + bacteriology occurred c. 1750-1900.

## Cross-tradition connections

- **[[medicine-hippocratic]]**: the parent tradition Galen systematized.
- **[[medicine-unani]]**: the Arab-Islamic direct continuation; Avicenna's *Canon* is the principal Galenic textbook from the Islamic side.
- **[[medicine-islamic-golden-age]]**: the institutional context of Arabic Galen.
- **[[medicine-ayurveda]]**: structural-parallel humoral system (tridoṣa); no direct genealogical connection.
