# Siddha medicine (Tamil Siddhar system)

Siddha medicine — Tamil *cittar maruttuvam* — is the classical medical system of the Tamil-speaking Dravidian south, transmitted as Tamil verse attributed to the **Cittars** (Skt *siddha*, "perfected/accomplished one"), a lineage of antinomian Shaiva-Tantric yogi-adepts. It is one of the four great scholarly medical systems of South Asia alongside [[medicine-ayurveda|Ayurveda]], [[medicine-unani|Unani]], and [[medicine-tibetan-sowa-rigpa|Sowa Rigpa]] — and the one with the strongest **iatrochemical / alchemical (rasavāda)** character: where classical Ayurveda is primarily a herbal and dietetic system, Siddha is built as much on calcined **mineral and metallic medicines** and on a mercurial alchemy aimed at a deathless body. Today it is a state-recognised system under India's Ministry of **AYUSH** (Siddha is the **S**), taught at the National Institute of Siddha, Chennai.

## The eighteen Siddhars

The tradition is attributed to the **patiṉeṇ cittarkaḷ**, the "eighteen Siddhars" — legendary Tamil adepts held to have mastered medicine, alchemy, yoga, and the eightfold *aṭṭamā-siddhi* powers. Their head is **Agastya (Agathiyar)**, the Vedic sage adopted into Tamil tradition as its founding culture-hero; other central figures are **Tirumūlar**, author of the *Tirumantiram* (the yogic-philosophical root text and part of the Shaiva *Tirumurai* canon); **Bogar (Bhoga)**, the alchemist associated with the mercurial *nava-pāṣāṇam* idol of Murugan at Palani; and Konkanar, Karuvūrār, Pulippāṇi, Theraiyar, and Yūki, among others. The lists vary; the "eighteen" is a schematic sacred number, and the historical Cittars behind the names are largely irrecoverable. The Cittar stance is deliberately heterodox — anti-caste, anti-temple-ritual, iconoclastic — and their medicine is inseparable from their yoga and alchemy: the physician-adept heals the world with the same mineral preparations by which he perfects his own body.

## Humoral theory — the mukkuṟṟam

Siddha diagnosis rests on the **mukkuṟṟam**, the "three faults," a humoral triad structurally parallel to the Ayurvedic tridoṣa:

- **Vātam / vali** (wind) — movement, breath, the nervous functions (≈ *vāta*)
- **Pittam / azhal** (fire, bile) — heat, digestion, transformation (≈ *pitta*)
- **Kapham / aiyam** (phlegm, water) — structure, cohesion, fluid (≈ *kapha*)

These rest on the five elements (**aimpūtam**, pañcabhūta) and are read through a distinctive diagnostic art — pulse (*nāḍi*), the tongue, complexion, voice, eye, touch, stool and urine (the *eṇvakai-t-tērvu*, "eightfold examination"). Siddha further maps the body through 96 *tattvas* (principles) and a subtle-body yoga of channels and centres shared with Tantra. The humoral overlap with Ayurveda is real, but the systems are distinct lineages with distinct vocabularies, distinct texts, and — decisively — a different therapeutic centre of gravity.

## Iatrochemistry — cuṇṇam, paṟpam, centūram, and muppu

Siddha's signature is its **mineral–metallic pharmacy**, produced by an alchemical laboratory practice (rasavāda). Classes of preparation include:

- **Cuṇṇam** (*chunnam*): calcined alkaline oxide preparations, fixed by repeated firing
- **Paṟpam** (*parpam*, ≈ Skt *bhasma*): fine calcined "ash" of a metal or mineral, rendered ingestible
- **Centūram** (*sindūram*): red oxide/sulphide compounds produced by controlled heating
- **Kaṭṭu / mezhugu / kuru**: fixed (solidified), waxy, and consolidated mercurial forms

At the centre stands **mercury** (Tamil *irasam*, Skt *parada*), "killed" (detoxified and fixed) and reborn as in Sanskrit rasa-śāstra, and the notorious **nava-pāṣāṇam** — the "nine poisons," a class of arsenical–mercurial compounds — from which Bogar is said to have cast the worshipped Murugan idol at Palani. Above all sits the **muppu** (from *mu-*, "three"): a secret triple-salt preparation, the "universal salt" and *ne plus ultra* of Siddha alchemy, regarded as the Tamil counterpart of the philosophers'-stone/elixir and guarded as the tradition's deepest secret. The therapeutic horizon is **kāya kalpa** — rejuvenation and radical longevity — and, at its limit, **deha-siddhi**, the perfected, deathless body of the Cittar himself.

## Cross-tradition significance

Siddha's most important cross-tradition placement is inside the world of **Indian mercurial alchemy**. David Gordon White's standard study is literally titled *The Alchemical Body: Siddha Traditions in Medieval India*: he treats the Tamil Cittars and the Sanskrit/Nāth **rasa-śāstra** ([[alchemy-rasayana-hindu|rasāyana]]) not as neighbours but as one alchemical culture — the same mercury identified with Shiva's seed and sulphur with Shakti's blood, the same "killing," fixation and resurrection of mercury, the same twin goal of transmuting metals and transmuting the body toward immortality. On this reading Siddha is the **Tamil-language, Dravidian-inflected member of the pan-Indian mercurial tradition**, and its wire to rasāyana is the strongest in this node.

Two framings must be kept honest. Against **Ayurveda**, the parallel is a shared humoral grammar (mukkuṟṟam ≈ tridoṣa) carried by a *separate* lineage — Siddha claims a Dravidian antiquity independent of the Vedic-Sanskritic stream, so the sensible statement is "sibling systems sharing a South Asian humoral substrate," not "a Tamil dialect of Ayurveda." Against **Paracelsian iatrochemistry** ([[medicine-paracelsus-iatrochemistry]]), the parallel is one of **independent convergence**: two traditions that each grew a pharmacy of calcined metals (mercury, sulphur, arsenic, antimony, gold) out of an alchemical matrix, with no documented contact — a striking case of the same alchemy-into-medicine move made in Tamil Nadu and in Renaissance Europe alike. The kindred mercurial longevity practice of **Tibetan Sowa Rigpa** (the *tsotel* refined-mercury preparation) shows the same stratum a third time.

## Disputes

**Dating.** The Siddha corpus resists dating. It survives as Tamil verse in palm-leaf manuscripts, much of it copied or composed late (the bulk 13th-18th c.), under names of Cittars who cannot be historically fixed; even the *Tirumantiram*, traditionally 6th-7th c., is often assigned a later received form. Claims of hoary Dravidian antiquity for the medical system as such are traditional rather than documented (Weiss 2009 is the careful modern treatment).

**Siddha vs rasa-śāstra: one lineage or two?** White (1996) folds the Tamil Siddhas and the Sanskrit/Nāth mercurial tradition into a single alchemical body; other scholars stress the Cittars' distinctly Tamil, anti-Brahmanical identity and treat the Tamil Siddha and the Sanskrit rasa traditions as convergent-but-distinct. The node wires them as a **parallel-form** and flags that whether this is common descent or close convergence is unsettled.

**The Bogar-China legend.** The tradition holds that the Cittar Bogar (Bhoga) travelled to China (or came from it) and exchanged alchemical knowledge — sometimes tied speculatively to Chinese cinnabar alchemy. This is **folklore, not documented transmission**; the genuine parallel to Chinese waidan/neidan is the shared mercury-and-immortality logic, which is better read as convergence than contact.
