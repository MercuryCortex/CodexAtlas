# Tibetan medicine (Sowa Rigpa)

The Tibetan medical tradition — *bod kyi gso ba rig pa*, the "Tibetan science of healing" — is one of the most extensively documented Buddhist medical systems and a particularly clear example of a synthesis tradition: indigenous Tibetan and Bön sources combine with Indian Ayurvedic, Greek (via Arab-Persian mediation), and Chinese inputs in a programmatic 8th-12th c. consolidation. The canonical text is the *rGyud bzhi* (Four Tantras), traditionally attributed to Yutok Yonten Gonpo the Elder (Yuthok the Senior, 8th c., legendary), redacted by Yutok Yonten Gonpo the Younger (12th c., historical).

## The three nyepa

The Tibetan system uses three principal physiological factors (*nyepa*, parallel to but not identical with Ayurvedic *doṣa*): *rlung* (wind, analogous to Ayurvedic *vāta*), *mkhris pa* (bile, analogous to *pitta*), and *bad kan* (phlegm, analogous to *kapha*). The triadic structure clearly reflects Ayurvedic ancestry; the Tibetan applications are distinct, particularly in their integration with Buddhist meditation and psychological frameworks.

## The Four Tantras

The *rGyud bzhi* is structured as four sequential tantric-instruction texts:

- **Root Tantra** (*rTsa rgyud*): overview, the three-nyepa system, the diagnostic categories.
- **Explanatory Tantra** (*bShad rgyud*): embryology, anatomy, the channels (*rtsa*), pathology, materia medica.
- **Oral Instruction Tantra** (*Man ngag rgyud*): the longest section — therapeutic procedures for specific diseases.
- **Final Tantra** (*Phyi ma rgyud*): diagnostic techniques (pulse, urine), pharmacology, regimens.

The tantric-text format is theologically loaded: the system is presented as the medical teaching of the Medicine Buddha (Bhaiṣajyaguru / Sangye Menla), framing the practitioner's work in a soteriological frame.

## Practice today

Sowa Rigpa is a state-recognized traditional medical system in India (formally recognized by the Ministry of AYUSH 2010), Bhutan, Nepal, Mongolia, and the Russian republics of Buryatia, Tuva, and Kalmykia. The principal training institutions in the diaspora are the Men-Tsee-Khang (Tibetan Medical and Astrological Institute) in Dharamshala, India, founded by the Dalai Lama in 1961, and the Chagpori Tibetan Medical Institute in Sikkim. In Tibet itself the Chagpori Mentsikhang (founded by the 5th Dalai Lama in 1696, destroyed during the Cultural Revolution, partially reopened 1980) was the historic apex institution.
