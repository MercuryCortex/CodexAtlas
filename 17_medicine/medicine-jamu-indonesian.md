# Jamu

The continuous indigenous Indonesian herbal-medicine tradition — multi-herb decoctions, powders, oils, and topical preparations developed across the archipelago and codified in Java in the Hindu-Buddhist Mataram period (c. 800-1000 CE). The Borobudur (Central Java, c. 825) relief panels depict plant-medicine preparation, and Old Javanese palm-leaf manuscripts preserve specific formulations. Jamu is one of the very few continuously-practiced traditional medical systems in Southeast Asia and is today both a domestic everyday practice and a substantial commercial-pharmaceutical sector.

## Composition and method

Typical jamu preparations are multi-herb combinations: roots, rhizomes (turmeric, ginger, galangal are central), leaves, barks, and seeds, often combined with honey, palm-sugar, or coconut oil as a vehicle. Formulations are categorized by purpose: *jamu sehat* (general wellness), *jamu galian singset* (women's-postpartum-care), *jamu kuat lelaki* (men's vitality), *jamu pelangsing* (slimming), *jamu pegel linu* (musculoskeletal pain), and many others. Preparation methods include boiled decoctions (drunk fresh), dried-powder formulations, and oils for topical application.

## The Jamu Gendong tradition

A distinctive contemporary feature: the *jamu gendong* — women who carry baskets of fresh-prepared jamu through residential neighborhoods, walking established routes, dispensing customized preparations on the spot. The tradition is concentrated in Central Java (particularly the Sukoharjo, Wonogiri, and Solo regions) and is one of the most visible everyday survivals of pre-modern medical practice in modern Southeast Asia.

## State integration

Indonesia formally regulates jamu through the National Agency of Drug and Food Control (BPOM); jamu preparations are classified into three regulatory tiers — *jamu* (traditional empirical), *obat herbal terstandar* (standardized herbal extracts), and *fitofarmaka* (clinically-trialed herbal pharmaceuticals). The Indonesian pharmaceutical industry's substantial jamu sector is a rare example of a traditional medical system being scaled industrially while retaining its traditional formulations.

## Cross-tradition connections

- **[[medicine-ayurveda]]**: the principal influence on Javanese-era jamu via the Hindu-Buddhist transmission; many specific botanicals (turmeric, ginger, *Centella asiatica*) appear in both traditions.
- **[[medicine-chinese-traditional]]**: secondary influence through Sino-Indonesian trade networks.
- **[[medicine-unani]]**: introduced by Arab-Indonesian traders + later Islamic networks; substantial syncretic overlap in some Sumatran traditions.
