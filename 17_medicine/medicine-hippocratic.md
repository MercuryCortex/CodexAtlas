Hippocratic medicine (c. 460–360 BCE and the tradition it spawned) represents the most consequential intellectual event in Western medical history: the attempt to explain disease entirely through natural causes — the first systematic rejection of divine or demonic causation as a sufficient medical explanation.

## The Hippocratic Revolution

**Hippocrates of Kos** (c. 460–370 BCE) is the founding figure of Western rational medicine, though he likely did not write most of the texts attributed to him. The *Hippocratic Corpus* (60+ texts, composed over roughly 400 BCE–50 CE) establishes:

1. **Natural causation**: Disease has natural causes observable in the environment (climate, water, diet, seasonal change) — not primarily divine punishment
2. **The examination imperative**: The physician must observe the patient systematically — pulse, temperature, urine, stool, breath, complexion, posture, speech
3. **Prognosis as professional standard**: The physician should be able to predict the disease's course — the ability to predict built trust and defined medical competence
4. **Do no harm**: The foundational constraint — interventions must not worsen the patient's condition
5. **Clinical records**: The Hippocratic case histories (especially *Epidemics* I and III) are the oldest surviving systematic case records in any medical tradition

## The Oath — Medical Ethics as Sacred Contract

The **Hippocratic Oath** (c. 4th century BCE) is the oldest surviving medical ethics document and the founding text of professional medical ethics. Its structure is a divine contract:
- Opens by invoking Apollo Physician, Asclepius, Hygieia, and Panacea as witnesses
- Establishes the teacher-student transmission chain
- Prohibits: surgery by physicians (a separate guild), abortion, giving poison, sexual relations with patients
- Commands: patient confidentiality (the first documented medical confidentiality obligation)

The Oath is simultaneously a **professional boundary document** and a **theological commitment** — the physician swears to divine witnesses. Modern medical ethics (informed consent, confidentiality, non-maleficence, beneficence) is the secularized descendant of the Oath's structure.

## The Four Humors — A Flawed but Durable Framework

The **humoral theory** attributed to Hippocratic tradition (elaborated by Galen):
- **Blood** (sanguine): warm, moist; spring; heart
- **Yellow bile** (choleric): warm, dry; summer; liver
- **Black bile** (melancholic): cold, dry; autumn; spleen
- **Phlegm** (phlegmatic): cold, moist; winter; brain

Health = dynamic balance. Disease = imbalance. Treatment = restore balance through diet, exercise, purging, bloodletting.

The humoral framework is **wrong** as physiology but **correct** as a research methodology: it drove systematic observation of patient history, environment, diet, and season. The framework that generated the observations outlasted the observations. The humoral system dominated Western and Islamic medicine for 2,400 years — until Germ Theory (1860s–1880s) finally replaced it.

## MASSIVE WIN: The Air–Miasma–Germ Theory Chain

Hippocratic texts (*On Airs, Waters, Places*; *On the Sacred Disease*) argue that disease spreads through corrupted air and environmental conditions. This is **wrong mechanistically** but **right epidemiologically**: the environments the Hippocratics identified as disease-causing (swamps, bad water, overcrowded cities) are genuinely disease-causing environments — because they harbor mosquitoes (malaria), contaminated water (cholera, typhoid), and airborne pathogens.

The chain: Hippocratic miasma theory → Galen's "bad air" (malaria = *mala aria*) → Islamic miasma medicine → 19th-century sanitarian movement (John Snow, Edwin Chadwick) → Pasteur/Koch Germ Theory.

The **sanitarian reforms of the 1840s–1860s** — based on Hippocratic-derived miasma theory, which was *wrong* — prevented cholera epidemics through better sewage and water supply, which *worked* because the real agent was waterborne bacteria. Wrong causal theory, correct intervention. Hippocrates's framework saved lives through Germ Theory's era by accident.

## Galen — The Pinnacle and the Prison

[[galen-of-pergamon]] (129–216 CE) synthesized the entire Hippocratic tradition into a systematic encyclopedic medicine. His authority in the Islamic and European Middle Ages was so absolute that his errors — particularly in anatomy (he dissected animals, not humans, and extrapolated to human anatomy) — became canonical:
- He described a 2-chambered heart with a porous septum (allowing blood passage between chambers) — no such pores exist
- This error survived for 1,400 years until Vesalius (1543) and Harvey (1628) corrected it

The Hippocratic tradition's greatest achievement (systematic observation) was buried under Galen's greatest failure (authority-worship): no one could contradict Galen without contradicting God's physician.
