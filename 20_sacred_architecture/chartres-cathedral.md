# Chartres Cathedral

## What it is

**Chartres Cathedral** (formally *Cathédrale Notre-Dame de Chartres*, "Our Lady of Chartres") is the **canonical model of French High Gothic architecture** and the **best-preserved medieval stained-glass programme in the world**. Built ~1194–1220 in a single rapid campaign after a fire destroyed the previous Romanesque cathedral, it preserves **175 medieval stained-glass windows** of the original ~186 — by far the most extensive surviving programme of medieval stained glass from any single building.

Chartres is dedicated to the Virgin Mary, who is enshrined here under her relic **the Veil of the Virgin (*Sancta Camisia*)** — a tunic-fragment traditionally identified as worn by Mary at the Annunciation, donated to Chartres by [[charles-the-bald|Charles the Bald]] ~876 CE. The relic made Chartres one of the four principal Marian pilgrimage destinations of medieval Western Europe (alongside Le Puy, Rocamadour, and Walsingham — and later Lourdes).

The cathedral is inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List 1979 (File 81).

## History

**~4th c.** — Early Christian community at Chartres on the site of a pre-Christian Druidic sacred grove (per medieval tradition; the historical evidence is limited).

**~876 CE** — Charles the Bald donates the **Veil of the Virgin** to Chartres. The cathedral becomes a major Marian pilgrimage destination.

**1020** — A fire destroys the Carolingian cathedral; [[bishop-fulbert-of-chartres|Bishop Fulbert]] (1006–1028) rebuilds in Romanesque style. Fulbert's crypt (St. Lubin's crypt) — the largest Romanesque crypt in France — survives beneath the current cathedral as the principal physical link to the pre-Gothic building.

**~1020–1194** — The Fulbert Romanesque cathedral stands. The west front (Royal Portal) is added ~1145 in early Gothic — the surviving Royal Portal sculptures are the earliest large-scale Gothic-figural sculpture in Europe.

**June 10, 1194** — **The Great Fire**. Most of the Romanesque cathedral burns; only the western towers (with the Royal Portal between them) and the crypt survive. The Veil of the Virgin is initially thought lost but is recovered intact from the treasury below — a miraculous-recovery story that motivates the immediate rebuilding campaign.

**1194–1220** — **The Rebuilding**. The cathedral is rebuilt in a single ~26-year campaign — remarkably rapid by medieval cathedral standards. The architectural plan is the canonical High Gothic three-storey-elevation cathedral: arcade + triforium + clerestory, with quadripartite ribbed vaults and a clerestory of large lancet-and-rose stained-glass windows. The architects' names are not securely attested — the master masons remain anonymous.

**1220–1260** — Stained glass programme installed; final liturgical fittings completed.

**1260** — Cathedral consecrated by Louis IX (Saint Louis) in the presence of three archbishops and an extraordinary gathering of medieval clergy.

**1506** — Northern spire (the "New Spire") rebuilt in elaborate Flamboyant Gothic after a lightning strike. This is why Chartres' two western spires are asymmetric: the southern is ~107 m (the original 12th-c. simple spire); the northern is ~115 m (the 1506 Flamboyant rebuild).

**1789–1799** — French Revolution: some sculpture damage, especially on the West Front; ironically, much of the stained glass is preserved by being deemed not worth destroying.

**1836** — Wooden roof above the vaults destroyed by fire; cast-iron roof structure installed (one of the earliest large-scale uses of cast-iron in cathedral architecture).

**1939–1945** — During WWII, all of the medieval stained glass is **removed by hand** (panel by panel) and stored in cellars and bunkers across France for safety. The American Colonel Welborn Barton Griffith Jr. famously **disobeys orders to shell the cathedral** during the Liberation of Chartres in August 1944 (he had been ordered to fire on the cathedral on suspicion that German artillery observers were in the spires; he reconnoitred personally, found the cathedral empty of Germans, and is killed by sniper fire shortly after; the cathedral and its glass are saved).

**Post-war** — All stained glass returned and reinstalled. The cathedral remains in active use.

**1979** — UNESCO World Heritage inscription.

**2009–2017** — Controversial interior cleaning + restoration; some scholars argue the cleaning has compromised the medieval patina; defenders argue it has restored the original luminous interior.

## Architectural form — the canonical High Gothic cathedral

Chartres is the **canonical model** of the French High Gothic three-storey-elevation cathedral:

**Plan**:
- **Length**: ~130 m
- **Width at transepts**: ~64 m
- **Nave width**: ~16.4 m (the widest medieval Gothic nave in France)
- **Vault height**: ~37 m
- **Cross plan**: a Latin cross with seven-bay nave, a single-bay crossing under a small lantern, and a double-aisled apsidal chevet with seven radiating chapels

**The three-storey elevation** (Chartres' defining innovation):
1. **Arcade**: pointed-arch arches on compound piers, separating nave from aisles
2. **Triforium**: a low blind arcade of three arches per bay, between aisle-roof and clerestory
3. **Clerestory**: lancet-and-rose stained-glass windows, the lancets paired with a small rose above

This **three-storey elevation** replaces the earlier **four-storey elevation** (arcade + tribune-gallery + triforium + clerestory) of Notre-Dame de Paris and other early Gothic cathedrals. The omission of the gallery-storey allows the **clerestory windows to be much larger** — the great increase in stained-glass area that defines High Gothic luminosity.

**The stained glass**:
- 175 surviving medieval windows (~ten lost; some replaced in 19th-c. or modern)
- Total surface ~2,500 m²
- Subject-matter: extensive Marian programme (the Marian Window in the south choir is iconic), Old Testament typology, Gospel narrative, lives of the saints
- **Blue of Chartres** — the cobalt-blue glass particular to Chartres' windows; the recipe (sodium-rich glass with cobalt + copper additions, fired to particular temperatures) was apparently not replicated elsewhere; modern attempts to match it have only partly succeeded. The deep blue is most luminous in the great rose windows of the transepts and west front.

**The labyrinth**:
- A 12-petal, 11-circuit labyrinth (~12.9 m diameter) inscribed in the pavement of the nave
- The medieval pilgrim could walk it as a substitute for the actual pilgrimage to Jerusalem (the petals symbolised the seven sacraments + the way of the cross + the heavenly Jerusalem at the centre)
- One of the principal surviving medieval church-labyrinths; closely related to the labyrinths at Sens, Saint-Quentin, Reims, and Amiens (most of which are now lost or damaged)
- Still walked by pilgrims, especially on Fridays during Lent when the chairs are removed

**The West Front (Royal Portal)**:
- Three deeply-recessed portals
- Sculptures from the 1145–1170 campaign (pre-fire); survived the 1194 conflagration
- **Old Testament kings and queens** flanking the doors as column-figures — the earliest large-scale Gothic figural sculpture (and the foundation of Gothic statuary)

## Theological / cosmological function

Chartres is conceived as the **architectural body of the Virgin Mary**:
- The **plan** (Latin cross) is the cosmic-cross-as-Mary's-body (medieval theological-architectural tradition)
- The **stained glass** transforms the interior into a luminous Marian narrative — the pilgrim walking through Chartres traverses the Virgin's life story window by window
- The **rose windows** of the three principal façades (West, North, South) are the **cosmic icons** of the cathedral — the West Rose depicts the Last Judgment; the North Rose depicts Mary surrounded by Old Testament kings and prophets; the South Rose depicts Christ surrounded by the four evangelists and the twenty-four elders of the Apocalypse
- The **labyrinth** in the nave is the **pilgrim's path** — the cosmographic journey through the seven sacraments to the heavenly Jerusalem at the centre

For the **Marian cult** ([[theme-marian-cult]]): Chartres is the architectural apex of medieval Marian devotion in Western Europe. The Veil of the Virgin relic, the multiple Marian windows, the dedication, the medieval pilgrimage practice — all combine to make Chartres the most fully developed instance of architectural Marianism in High Gothic Europe.

## MASSIVE WIN — the canonical node of the French High Gothic transmission chain

Chartres is the **canonical model** at the centre of the cleanest documented architectural transmission chain in medieval Europe:

> **[[saint-denis-abbey-paris|Saint-Denis Abbey]] (Abbot Suger, 1135–1144)** — birth of Gothic: pointed arch + ribbed vault + ambulatory of stained glass
> → **[[notre-dame-de-paris|Notre-Dame de Paris]] (begun 1163, four-storey elevation)** — early Gothic experiments
> → **Chartres Cathedral (1194–1220, three-storey elevation)** — **the canonical High Gothic model**
> → **[[reims-cathedral|Reims Cathedral]] (begun 1211)** — French royal coronation cathedral; extends the Chartres model
> → **[[amiens-cathedral|Amiens Cathedral]] (begun 1220)** — extends the Chartres + Reims model to even greater scale
> → **[[beauvais-cathedral|Beauvais Cathedral]] (begun 1247)** — the tallest Gothic vault attempted (48 m); collapsed twice
> → **[[cologne-cathedral|Cologne Cathedral]] (begun 1248)** — Master Gerhard, trained at Amiens, brings the French High Gothic vocabulary to Germany
> → **[[salisbury-cathedral|Salisbury Cathedral]] (begun 1220)** — English Gothic adaptation
> → **[[strasbourg-cathedral|Strasbourg Cathedral]] (begun 1276)** — German Gothic continuation
> → **[[milan-cathedral|Milan Cathedral]] (begun 1386)** — Italian Gothic late-form
> → **[[seville-cathedral|Seville Cathedral]] (begun 1402)** — Spanish Gothic late-form (largest Gothic cathedral by area)

This is **the cleanest documented religious-architectural transmission chain in medieval Western Europe** — a continuous tradition of master-mason training, architectural drawing, and conceptual replication that spans ~250 years and ~3,000 km. Each cathedral is a documented descendant of its predecessors; the master masons frequently travelled, trained at multiple sites, and brought the vocabulary forward.

What Chartres uniquely contributes: **the three-storey elevation that becomes the canonical High Gothic form**. Notre-Dame de Paris had pioneered Gothic but in a four-storey elevation; Chartres demonstrates that the gallery-storey can be omitted and the clerestory greatly enlarged — releasing the luminous stained-glass programme that defines High Gothic.

For **Christian sacred architecture broadly**, Chartres' MASSIVE WIN is the **luminous interior as Marian-theological space**:
- The cathedral's interior is architecturally engineered for **maximum stained-glass area**
- The blue-of-Chartres glass produces a particular luminous-blue interior atmosphere
- Walking through Chartres is walking through the Marian narrative AS architecture-and-light
- The Marian cult and Gothic-architectural innovation are **co-developed** — Chartres' Marian programme drives the architectural innovations, and the architectural innovations enable the Marian programme

This is one of the most fully developed cases of **religious-theological content shaping religious-architectural form** in world history.
