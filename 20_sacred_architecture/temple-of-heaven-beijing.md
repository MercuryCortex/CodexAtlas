# Temple of Heaven (Tiantan, Beijing)

## What it is

The **Temple of Heaven** (*Tiantan*, 天壇) is the **principal architectural monument of the Chinese imperial cult of Heaven** — the great southern altar-complex of Ming–Qing Beijing where the **Son of Heaven** ([[mandate-of-heaven|the emperor]]) personally performed the annual **winter-solstice sacrifice to Heaven** (*jisi tian*). It is the most articulate built expression of the Chinese cosmological grammar of **round Heaven / square Earth** (*tian yuan di fang*) in monumental architecture.

Built **1406–1420** under the [[yongle-emperor|Yongle Emperor]] as part of his great construction of Beijing, restructured into its present form by the [[jiajing-emperor|Jiajing Emperor]] in 1530 (when the Heaven and Earth altars were separated), and rebuilt after a 1889 lightning strike under [[guangxu-emperor|Guangxu]], the complex covers ~273 ha — **larger than the [[forbidden-city-beijing|Forbidden City]] itself** — and is enclosed by a double wall whose **southern enclosure is square** and whose **northern enclosure is rounded**, instantiating the round-Heaven-square-Earth cosmology at the **plan-of-the-whole-precinct** scale.

UNESCO World Heritage (1998, File 881).

## History

- **1406–1420** — built under [[yongle-emperor|Yongle Emperor]] (Ming) as a single combined **Altar of Heaven and Earth** (*Tian Di Tan*), aligned on Beijing's central axis ~3 km south of the [[forbidden-city-beijing|Forbidden City]].
- **1530** — under [[jiajing-emperor|Jiajing Emperor]], the combined altar is **split**: Heaven retains the southern altar (this site), Earth gets a new northern altar (the [[temple-of-earth-beijing|Ditan / Temple of Earth]]), Sun gets an eastern altar (Ritan), Moon gets a western altar (Yuetan). The four-altar **cardinal ritual ring** around the Forbidden City is the form of Beijing-as-cosmogram from this point on. The southern altar is rebuilt with the present three-tiered round form.
- **1545** — Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests (*Qiniandian*) rebuilt under Jiajing.
- **1749** — major rebuild under [[qianlong-emperor|Qianlong Emperor]] (Qing): the Circular Mound Altar (*Yuanqiu*) enlarged, the white-marble triple-terrace given its present form.
- **1889** — Hall of Prayer destroyed by lightning; rebuilt under [[guangxu-emperor|Guangxu Emperor]] in the present form (the wooden hall standing today is the 1890 reconstruction, faithful to the 1545 / 1749 design).
- **1914** — the **last imperial sacrifice** performed by Yuan Shikai during his abortive bid to revive monarchy in the early Republic. After 1914, the altar is no longer used for state sacrifice; it has been a public park since 1918.

## Architectural form — the cosmological programme

The complex is organised around three principal structures on the **north–south axis**:

| Structure | Form | Cosmological reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Circular Mound Altar (*Yuanqiu Tan*, 圜丘壇)** — southern end | Three concentric circular white-marble terraces, open to the sky | The altar of the actual sacrifice. Round = Heaven. Number-symbolism: the top terrace's central paving-stone is surrounded by **nine** stones, then **eighteen** (2×9), then **twenty-seven** (3×9) ... up to **eighty-one** (9×9). Nine is the yang-maximum, the imperial number. |
| **Imperial Vault of Heaven (*Huangqiongyu*, 皇穹宇)** — between | Round single-eave wooden hall on a circular base | Houses the **spirit-tablets of Heaven and the ancestral emperors** between sacrifices. Surrounded by the famous **Echo Wall**. |
| **Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests (*Qiniandian*, 祈年殿)** — northern end | Three-eaved circular wooden hall on a triple-terraced circular white-marble platform; ~38 m high | The most photographed building of the complex. Three roof-tiers = the three realms (Heaven, Earth, Mortals). Twenty-eight interior pillars: **four central pillars** (the four seasons) + **twelve middle pillars** (the twelve months) + **twelve outer pillars** (the twelve two-hour periods of the day). The whole interior is a **calendric-cosmological diagram**. |

**Cardinal alignment**: the entire complex is on a single **N–S axis** continuous with the Forbidden City's central axis ~3 km to the north. The emperor processed south from the palace, performed the sacrifice at the southern altar at the winter solstice (the moment of cosmic re-beginning), and processed back north.

**Round-Heaven / square-Earth (*tian yuan di fang*)**: the entire **enclosure** of the precinct is a giant **diagrammatic embodiment of the cosmological doctrine** — the southern wall is square (Earth), the northern wall is rounded (Heaven). The visitor crosses from the square south to the round north as they approach the heart of the precinct. This is **architectural cosmology at the precinct-plan scale**, not merely at the building scale.

## The ritual

The annual **winter-solstice sacrifice** (*dongzhi jisi tian*) was the **single most important ritual of the imperial year**, performed only by the **emperor in person**. Preparation required three days of fasting and abstinence. On the dawn of the solstice, the emperor processed south, mounted the Circular Mound, and offered the **burnt sacrifice of a young bullock** (the *suiniu*) along with jade, silk, soup, wine, and ritual music. The protocol is preserved in exhaustive detail in the [[da-ming-huidian|*Da Ming Huidian*]] and [[da-qing-huidian|*Da Qing Huidian*]].

The sacrificing emperor stood at the **centre of the top terrace** of the Circular Mound — the **acoustic and cosmological centre of the building** — and addressed the High Lord of Heaven (*Shangdi* / *Huangtian Shangdi*) directly. **No other figure in the empire could perform this sacrifice.** It is the architectural-ritual signature of the Confucian-imperial doctrine that **the emperor mediates between Heaven and the realm**.

## MASSIVE WIN — round-Heaven / square-Earth as architectural diagram

The Temple of Heaven is the **most articulate built expression in any tradition of a society's cosmological doctrine translated directly into architectural plan**.

The doctrine: **Heaven is round, Earth is square** (*tian yuan di fang*) — found in the *Zhou Bi Suan Jing* (~2nd c. BCE), the *Lüshi Chunqiu*, and woven through classical-Confucian and Daoist cosmology.

The architectural translation:
- **Plan of the whole precinct** — northern enclosure rounded, southern enclosure squared.
- **Plan of the principal altar** — circular (Circular Mound).
- **Plan of the Hall of Prayer** — circular hall on circular triple-terraced base.
- **Number-system of the paving** — multiples of 9 (the yang-maximum), in circular concentric rings.
- **Cardinal axis** — N–S, continuous with the Forbidden City.
- **Seasonal-calendric pillar-count** of the Hall of Prayer interior — 4 (seasons) + 12 (months) + 12 (hours).

The result is **a complete cosmogram you can walk through**. The pilgrim moves from square Earth (south outer entrance) to round Heaven (north precinct), past the calendric interior of the Hall of Prayer, ends at the open-sky altar of Heaven where the sacrifice is performed. The form *is* the doctrine.

This is a structural cognate of [[borobudur-stupa-mandala|Borobudur]]'s instantiation of the [[tradition-mahayana-buddhism|Mahayana]] *Trailokya* in stepped-mandala form, of [[pyramid-of-khufu|Khufu]]'s instantiation of the Egyptian solar-resurrection programme in geometric pyramid form, of [[hagia-sophia|Hagia Sophia]]'s instantiation of Byzantine theology in dome-and-pendentive form. The Chinese case is **the clearest single example of round-vs-square as architectural diagram** in world religious building.

## State-Confucian ritual architecture across East Asia

The Temple of Heaven is the **apex** of a network of **state-Confucian ritual buildings** that includes:

- **[[temple-of-confucius-qufu|Temple of Confucius at Qufu]]** (China) — the ancestral cult-centre of Confucius and his lineage.
- **[[jongmyo-shrine-seoul|Jongmyo Shrine]]** (Seoul, Joseon Korea) — Confucian royal ancestral shrine.
- Smaller Confucian temples (*wenmiao*) in every Chinese prefecture and in the Joseon Korean ritual programme.

Together these form the **architectural infrastructure of the Confucian state-cult** across China and the Confucian Sinosphere (Korea, Vietnam, partially Japan). The Ming–Qing Temple of Heaven is the topmost rung: the emperor's altar to Heaven itself.
