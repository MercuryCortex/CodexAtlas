# Pantheon (Rome)

## What it is

The Roman **Pantheon** — *templum omnium deorum*, "the temple of all the gods" — is the **best-preserved surviving classical Roman building** and the **architectural ancestor of every major domed religious building in the Western and Islamic traditions**. Built by [[hadrian-emperor|Hadrian]] ~113–125 CE on the site of an earlier Pantheon built by [[marcus-vipsanius-agrippa|Marcus Vipsanius Agrippa]] (~25 BCE, destroyed by fire 80 CE), it has been **in continuous architectural use for 1900 years**.

The inscription on the portico reads:

> **M·AGRIPPA·L·F·COS·TERTIVM·FECIT**
> ("Marcus Agrippa, son of Lucius, consul for the third time, built [this]")

Hadrian — the building's actual builder — **deliberately re-instated Agrippa's inscription on his rebuild**. This is one of the most-quoted architectural-historical paradoxes: the Pantheon Hadrian built is the **third** Pantheon on this site, and Hadrian's inscription credits Agrippa.

## Form — the architectural synthesis

The Pantheon is **two architectures bolted together**:

- **The portico**: a **Greek-classicising frontal façade** — eight monolithic Corinthian granite columns (each 12 m tall, ~60 tons, quarried at Mons Claudianus in Egypt), pediment, two rear rows for an eight-column façade.
- **The rotunda**: an **entirely Roman concrete-and-brick interior** — a 43.3 m diameter sphere inscribed in a 43.3 m cube, capped by a coffered concrete dome with a single 9 m oculus admitting light from above.

The portico promises a classical Greek temple. Then the visitor passes through the bronze doors and encounters the **rotunda**, which is unlike any Greek architectural experience — a **vast hemispherical interior space**, light streaming from a single hole in the ceiling.

This is the architectural-historical hinge. The Greek temple is an **exterior object** — peripteral, sculptural, designed to be seen from outside. The Roman Pantheon, while continuing the Greek-temple language on its façade, **invents the temple as monumental interior space**. Every later monumental domed religious building — Hagia Sophia, the Dome of the Rock, Florence Duomo, St. Peter's — descends from this Roman interior-spatial conception.

## The dome

The Pantheon dome is:

- **43.3 m in diameter** — the **largest unreinforced concrete dome ever built**, and it has held this record for 1900 years.
- **Coffered** with five rings of 28 coffers each — partly structural (reducing weight), partly visual (the rhythmic coffering directs the eye to the oculus).
- **Variable concrete recipe through the dome's depth** — Roman engineers used heavier aggregate (travertine + tuff) at the dome's base and progressively lighter aggregate (volcanic scoria, pumice) toward the top. The dome **gets lighter as it rises**. This is sophisticated structural-engineering that medieval-and-Renaissance builders had to **re-discover** centuries later.
- **The oculus** — a 9 m diameter circular opening at the apex, open to the sky. Rain enters; drains in the floor (with a 3 cm dish to channel water to bronze rain-drains at the perimeter) carry it away. The oculus is the building's only natural-light source — and as the day passes, the sun's beam **traces a circle around the interior**, the building's only "clock-hand."

## "All the gods" — function and theological reading

The name *Pantheon* ("all the gods") is ancient but its exact ritual function is **debated**. Cassius Dio (69.7.1, c. 230 CE) records the building was called Pantheon "because among the images that decorated it there were statues of many gods, including Mars and Venus." Modern scholarship (Ziolkowski 1994; Hetland 2007) is cautious — the building's specific cult-programme is poorly documented in surviving sources.

What is consensus: the Pantheon was an **imperial monument**, integrated into the [[tradition-imperial-cult|Roman imperial cult]] (Hadrian held audiences there), and the **interior spatial programme of dome + oculus** has been read since antiquity as **cosmological** — the dome as the celestial vault, the oculus as the sun-eye. Cassius Dio: "the vaulted roof resembles the heavens."

## The Christian conversion (609 CE)

In **609 CE**, the Byzantine emperor Phocas gave the building to Pope [[pope-boniface-iv|Boniface IV]], who consecrated it as a Christian church under the dedication **Santa Maria ad Martyres** ("St. Mary and the Martyrs"). The conversion is one of the cleanest temple-to-church transitions in the Mediterranean: no major architectural alteration, just a re-dedication and the relocation of martyr-relics from the catacombs into the building.

The Christian consecration is **why the building survives**. Many Roman temples were systematically demolished from the 4th c. onwards (cf. [[event-destruction-of-serapeum-391]]); the Pantheon's consecration as a Christian church in 609 secured it for the medieval and Renaissance eras. The vault wreathed-bronze coffer ornaments were stripped (much of the bronze was melted for [[bernini]]'s baldachin at St. Peter's by Pope Urban VIII in 1626 — Pasquino's famous Roman pasquinade: *"Quod non fecerunt barbari, fecerunt Barberini"*, "What the barbarians did not do, the Barberini did").

[[raphael-sanzio|Raphael]] is buried in the Pantheon (1520), with Pietro Bembo's epitaph on the tomb. Several Italian kings and queens of the Savoy dynasty are buried in side chapels.

## MASSIVE WIN — the Pantheon as origin-text of the domed religious building

The Pantheon is one of the most consequential single buildings in the history of architecture because **almost every monumental domed religious building since 600 CE descends typologically from it**:

> Pantheon Rome (~125 CE) — 43.3 m concrete dome
> → Hagia Sophia (537 — pendentive-dome solution; structurally different but conceptually the heir)
> → Dome of the Rock (691 — Islamic centralised-domed-shrine; Krautheimer's reading places it in this typological line)
> → Cordoba mosque interior dome (10th c.), Suleymaniye, Selimiye (Sinan)
> → Florence Duomo (Brunelleschi, 1436 — double-shell engineering rediscovered)
> → St. Peter's Basilica (Michelangelo's plan, 1546)
> → Les Invalides, Capitol Dome Washington, every major domed religious building of the modern era

The **typological influence is universally acknowledged**. The Pantheon is the building that taught the architectural-religious imagination of the West and the Islamic world what a monumental domed sacred interior is.
