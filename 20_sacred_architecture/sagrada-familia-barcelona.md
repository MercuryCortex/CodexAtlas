# Sagrada Família (Barcelona)

## What it is

The **Sagrada Família** (formally *Basílica i Temple Expiatori de la Sagrada Família* — "Basilica and Expiatory Temple of the Holy Family") is the architectural masterwork of [[antoni-gaudi|Antoni Gaudí]] (1852–1926) and one of the most-visited churches-under-construction in the world. Begun **1882** and projected for completion **2034** (currently delayed from the original 2026 target after COVID-19 disruptions), it is a **142+ year project** — making it one of the longest single-architect-conceived buildings in human history.

The basilica is a **modernist-organic reinterpretation of the European basilica tradition**, deploying Gaudí's unique architectural vocabulary of:
- **Naturalistic forms**: columns shaped like trees with branching capitals; vaults using double-hyperboloid + paraboloid + helicoidal surfaces
- **Catenary-arch structural geometry**: derived from Gaudí's hanging-chain models, where the form of an inverted hanging chain represents the optimal compressive curve
- **Polychrome stained glass**: extending the High Gothic tradition into a uniquely modernist palette
- **Iconographic-narrative façades**: each of the three principal façades narrates a distinct theological-narrative cycle (Nativity, Passion, Glory)

When completed, Sagrada Família will have **18 spires**:
- 12 spires for the Apostles (~98–125 m)
- 4 spires for the Evangelists (~135 m)
- 1 spire for Mary (~138 m)
- 1 central spire for Jesus Christ (~172.5 m) — making it the **tallest church building in the world**

It is part of the UNESCO Works of Antoni Gaudí World Heritage Site (1984, extended 2005 to include the **Nativity Façade + crypt** of Sagrada Família — the parts completed in Gaudí's lifetime). The post-Gaudí continuation of the project is NOT covered by the World Heritage inscription.

## History

**1872** — [[josep-maria-bocabella|Josep Maria Bocabella]] founds the *Asociación Espiritual de Devotos de San José* (Spiritual Association of Devotees of St. Joseph). The Association raises funds for an **expiatory temple** — a church funded only by alms, never by government or institutional funds, as collective lay-devotion act.

**1882** — Foundation stone laid 19 March (St. Joseph's day). [[francisco-de-paula-del-villar|Francisco de Paula del Villar]] is the initial architect; designs a neo-Gothic plan.

**1883** — Del Villar resigns over technical-aesthetic disputes. **[[antoni-gaudi|Antoni Gaudí]]**, age 31, takes over as chief architect. Gaudí progressively transforms the project from neo-Gothic to a wholly new architectural vocabulary; over the next 43 years, the project becomes his life work and the principal vehicle of his architectural-theological vision.

**1883–1893** — Gaudí completes the crypt (begun by del Villar) and begins the apse.

**1894–1925** — Construction of the Nativity Façade (the eastern façade, oriented to the rising sun, depicting the birth of Christ). Gaudí lavishes attention on the figural programme: every figure has a specific theological-narrative role; the iconographic programme is meticulously researched and executed.

**1914** — Gaudí closes his architectural practice to other commissions and dedicates himself entirely to Sagrada Família. He lives in a small workshop on the construction site for the rest of his life. He becomes increasingly ascetic, devoting himself to the project's theology and construction.

**1925** — Gaudí completes the bell-tower of St. Barnabas (the first of the 18 spires).

**7 June 1926** — Gaudí is struck by a tram in central Barcelona. Carrying no identification (he had become indifferent to his appearance and was mistaken for a destitute), he is taken to a charity hospital. He is only identified the following day. He dies 10 June 1926. He is buried in the crypt of the Sagrada Família.

**1926–1936** — Construction continues under Gaudí's collaborators (Domènec Sugrañes, etc.), following Gaudí's plaster models and drawings.

**20 July 1936** — During the Spanish Civil War, **anarchist arson destroys Gaudí's workshop**. Most of Gaudí's drawings, the principal plaster models, and his archive are burned. **This is one of the most destructive single archival losses in 20th-century architectural history.** The surviving plaster-model fragments and Gaudí's published statements are the principal evidence for the design.

**1939–1952** — Project largely halted by the Civil War aftermath + post-war austerity.

**1952–1960** — Restoration of the salvaged plaster fragments + reconstruction of working models begin under Francesc Quintana.

**1960s–1980s** — Slow but steady progress. Major controversy over whether to **continue the project** or **preserve what existed** as a Gaudí monument. The continuation faction wins; project continues using Gaudí's surviving models as guide, complemented by structural analysis of the underlying geometric system.

**1985–2012** — Jordi Bonet i Armengol as chief architect. Major acceleration of construction using CAD-CAM technology, parametric structural analysis, and computer-controlled stone-cutting.

**1992** — Olympic Games in Barcelona: Sagrada Família becomes globally visible as Barcelona's principal architectural icon.

**2010** — **Papal consecration** by Pope Benedict XVI on 7 November 2010. The basilica is now formally consecrated and the interior is in active liturgical use.

**2012–present** — Jordi Faulí as chief architect. The project has been accelerated using parametric design and modern construction methods; the originally projected 2026 completion was the centenary of Gaudí's death, but COVID-19 has pushed this to 2034.

**2024** — Construction of the Evangelist towers continues.

**2034 (projected)** — Completion of the central spire of Jesus Christ.

## Architectural form — the modernist-organic basilica

Sagrada Família deploys an architectural vocabulary essentially without precedent:

**Plan**:
- **Latin cross plan** with five-aisle nave + three-aisle transepts
- **Length**: ~90 m
- **Width at transepts**: ~60 m
- **Interior area**: ~4,500 m²
- **Nave height**: ~45 m (excluding the central spire)
- **Capacity**: ~9,000 seated

**The structural-geometric vocabulary**:
1. **Catenary arches**: Gaudí used hanging-chain models (suspended chains under their own weight, with weights attached at specific points) to derive optimal compressive curves; inverting these curves gave him the structurally optimal arch-and-vault forms. The interior columns follow these forms.
2. **Hyperboloid / paraboloid surfaces**: the vaults are constructed on double-curved geometric surfaces (hyperboloids and paraboloids) — geometrically precise forms that handle structural loads with minimum material.
3. **Branching columns**: the interior columns rise from the floor and **branch out near the vault** like trees, distributing the vault's load through multiple branches. The interior of the basilica is a **forest of stone trees** — Gaudí's signature naturalist conception.
4. **Geometric proportional system**: every dimension is governed by a strict proportional system involving the dimensions of the human body, the Trinity, the four evangelists, the twelve apostles, etc.

**The three principal façades**:
1. **Nativity Façade (Gaudí 1894–1925, completed posthumously)** — eastern, oriented to the rising sun. Depicts the Nativity, Adoration of the Magi, Annunciation. Three portals: Faith, Hope, Charity. **The most Gaudí-controlled façade**; UNESCO-listed.
2. **Passion Façade (1954–present, by Josep Maria Subirachs from 1986)** — western, oriented to the setting sun. Depicts the Passion of Christ. Architecturally stark and angular, in deliberate contrast to the Nativity Façade's organic warmth. The Subirachs figures are themselves controversial — many critics argue they depart from Gaudí's intent.
3. **Glory Façade (2002–present)** — southern, the principal future entrance. Will depict the Last Judgment + Glory of God. Architecturally still incomplete as of 2024.

**The 18 spires** (when completed):
- 12 for the Apostles (98–125 m)
- 4 for the Evangelists (~135 m) — currently under construction
- 1 for Mary (~138 m) — completed 2021 with the 12-pointed-star illumination
- 1 central for Jesus Christ (~172.5 m) — projected completion 2026, now 2034

**Stained glass**: extensive Gaudí + post-Gaudí stained glass programme; the interior takes on different luminous qualities depending on time of day and season — Gaudí designed the colour distribution carefully (cooler blues + greens on the east; warmer reds + oranges on the west).

## Theological / cosmological function

Sagrada Família is conceived as:
- **A basilica dedicated to the Holy Family** (Jesus, Mary, Joseph)
- **An expiatory temple** — a collective lay-devotion act, funded only by alms
- **A modernist cathedral-for-the-modern-world** — Gaudí explicitly conceived the project as a Catholic response to the modernist crisis, deploying contemporary architectural vocabulary in service of perennial theological content

The basilica's iconographic programme is unusually complete and theologically dense:
- **Nativity Façade** = Incarnation
- **Passion Façade** = Crucifixion
- **Glory Façade** = Last Judgment + Resurrection-Glory
- **18 spires** = Apostles + Evangelists + Mary + Christ
- **Forest-interior columns** = Tree of Life / Garden of Eden / Christ-as-Tree-of-Life

The **central spire of Jesus Christ** will be the cosmic axis — the *axis mundi* of the basilica — rising ~172.5 m above the floor, crowned with a great cross. The 12 apostle-spires + 4 evangelist-spires + 1 Mary-spire + 1 Christ-spire form a **vertical-architectural Christology** of the New Testament.

## MASSIVE WIN — modernist religious architecture and the long-duration project

Sagrada Família establishes the **modernist religious architecture** category in a way no other building does:

> **The first major modernist building conceived from the outset as a Christian religious sacred space.**

Earlier modernist religious architecture exists (Antonio Sant'Elia's Futurist church drawings; Wright's Unity Temple, 1908; Saarinen's MIT Chapel, 1955) but is mostly small-scale or modernist-functional. Sagrada Família is **modernist-monumental-religious** — the only building in this category at the scale of the great medieval cathedrals.

The architectural-cultural position of the project:
- **A bridge** between the medieval Gothic tradition (long narrow nave, stained glass, spires, vertical cosmography) and the modernist tradition (organic naturalism, computed geometric surfaces, structural optimisation)
- **A 142+ year project** — one of the very few buildings whose construction has spanned three architectural-cultural eras (late-19th-c. modernism → 20th-c. modernism → contemporary parametric / digital architecture)
- **An expiatory-temple-as-collective-lay-act** — uniquely sustained as a Catholic lay devotional project for 142+ years, never receiving government or Vatican institutional funding

The **long-duration project** pattern is the unique architectural-cultural feature: Sagrada Família joins the small cluster of buildings whose construction has actively spanned multiple generations:
- **[[cologne-cathedral|Cologne Cathedral]]** — 632 years (1248–1880)
- **Milan Cathedral** — 579 years (1386–1965)
- **Notre-Dame de Paris** — ~180 years construction + ongoing reconstruction
- **Florence Cathedral** — 140 years (1296–1436)
- **Sagrada Família** — 142+ years and still building

What Sagrada Família uniquely adds: **the long-duration project as a deliberate religious-devotional choice**, not the result of historical interruption. The other long-duration projects became long-duration through historical contingency (war, economic decline, political change). Sagrada Família is long-duration **because the lay-devotional funding model intrinsically requires patience** — and because Gaudí himself, asked about the slow progress, famously replied: *"My client is not in a hurry."*
