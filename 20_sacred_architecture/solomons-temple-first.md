# Solomon's First Temple

## What it is

**Solomon's First Temple** (Hebrew *Beit HaMikdash HaRishon*) was the **central Yahwistic cult-temple** of the united Israelite kingdom and (after the split) the southern kingdom of Judah. Built ~966–957 BCE according to the biblical chronology of 1 Kings 6, it stood for ~380 years until its destruction by [[nebuchadnezzar-ii|Nebuchadnezzar II]] of Babylon in **586 BCE** (the *Churban Bayit Rishon*, "destruction of the First House").

It is one of the most influential buildings in religious history — and one of the most **archaeologically inaccessible**. The Temple Mount has not been excavated due to Islamic-custody constraints; the entire reconstruction is based on:

- **Biblical text** (1 Kings 6–8, 2 Chronicles 3–7, Ezekiel 40–48 — the latter being a visionary architectural programme, possibly idealised).
- **Archaeological parallels** at related Iron-Age Levantine temples (especially **Ain Dara** in Syria, which provides the closest surviving architectural cognate).
- **Inscriptional and iconographic evidence** from Iron-Age Levantine sacred architecture.

There is no physical surviving fragment that can be confidently attributed to the First Temple. The **Western Wall** (Kotel) is from the [[solomons-temple-second|Second Temple]] (Herodian rebuilding) — not the First.

## The textual building-account

**1 Kings 6** gives the principal building-account:

- **Dimensions**: 60 cubits long × 20 wide × 30 high (~30 × 9 × 14 m).
- **Tripartite plan**: *Ulam* (porch / vestibule) → *Hekal* (main hall / nave) → *Devir* (Holy of Holies / inner sanctuary).
- The *Devir* was a **20-cubit cube** containing the **Ark of the Covenant** between two **15-cubit-tall olive-wood cherubim** plated with gold.
- **Materials**: stone construction (foundation + walls of dressed stone, "no hammer was heard at the building"); **cedar of Lebanon** for the roof and interior panelling; **gold leaf** overlaying interior surfaces.
- **Two bronze pillars at the porch entrance**: [[jachin-and-boaz]] (1 Kings 7:21) — cast by [[hiram-abi|Hiram-Abi]] of Tyre; ~18 cubits tall; surmounted by lily-and-pomegranate capitals.
- **The Bronze Sea**: a massive bronze basin holding ritual water, resting on twelve bronze oxen (three facing each compass direction) — 1 Kings 7:23–26.
- **Side chambers**: three storeys of side rooms around the *Hekal* and *Devir* (1 Kings 6:5–6).

**Construction timeline (1 Kings 6:1, 6:38)**: begun in the 4th year of Solomon's reign (~966 BCE), completed in the 11th year (~957 BCE) — seven years.

## Phoenician craftsmen and Levantine architectural form

The biblical account is unembarrassed that the Temple was built with **Phoenician materials and craftsmen**:

- **[[hiram-of-tyre-king|Hiram of Tyre]]** supplied cedar (1 Kings 5).
- **[[hiram-abi|Hiram-Abi]]** (a Tyrian metalworker, distinct from the king) cast the bronze fittings (1 Kings 7:13–14).
- The architectural-form is **classical Iron-Age Levantine**: the closest surviving parallel is the **temple at Ain Dara** in Syria (~10th–8th c. BCE) — tripartite plan, similar proportions, similar cherub iconography (Monson 2000, *BAR*). The Solomonic Temple is not a unique Israelite invention; it is a **first-rank Levantine royal-temple in a regional architectural vocabulary**.

This matters theologically: the **innovation** of Solomon's Temple is **not its architectural form** (which is regional) but its **religious programme** — the centralisation of Yahwistic cult in a single building, in the king's capital, with the Ark of the Covenant in the Holy of Holies.

## The Foundation Stone and the Holy of Holies

The Holy of Holies (*Devir*) housed the **Ark of the Covenant** flanked by two great cherubim, atop what later Jewish tradition identifies as the **Foundation Stone** (*Even haShetiyya*) — the bedrock from which (per Talmud Yoma 54b) "the world was created." The Holy of Holies was entered **only by the High Priest, only on Yom Kippur, only once a year** (Leviticus 16) — the most architecturally-restricted access of any space in Iron-Age religion.

The Ark itself was lost during or shortly after the destruction of the Temple (586 BCE); its fate is the subject of multiple traditions (the [[tradition-ethiopian-tewahedo|Ethiopian]] Kebra Nagast tradition holds it was taken to Aksum during Solomon's reign by Menelik I; the 02_documents 2 Maccabees 2:4–8 tradition holds Jeremiah hid it on Mount Nebo; modern critical scholarship treats it as destroyed in 586). The empty Holy of Holies of the [[solomons-temple-second|Second Temple]] is one of the most theologically loaded architectural absences in religious history.

## Destruction and afterlife

**586 BCE** — [[nebuchadnezzar-ii]] of Babylon destroys Jerusalem; the Temple is burned; the gold cult-implements are taken to Babylon (later restored in part by Cyrus per Ezra 1). The destruction triggers the [[event-babylonian-exile-586-538-bce|Babylonian Exile]] and the **theological-architectural crisis** out of which much of the Hebrew Bible takes shape: how is the Yahwistic cult continued when its sole legitimate sacrificial-architectural locus is destroyed? The answers — Ezekiel's visionary architectural programme, Deuteronomistic theology of centralised cult, eventually Rabbinic Judaism's transformation of the Temple's daily liturgy into prayer + Torah study — together define the **post-Temple turn** in Jewish religious history.

## MASSIVE WIN — the temple's afterlife

Solomon's First Temple has **no surviving architectural fragments**, but it has the densest afterlife of any building in religious history:

- **[[solomons-temple-second|Second Temple]] (516 BCE – 70 CE)** — rebuilt by Zerubbabel; massively expanded by Herod ~20 BCE.
- **[[dome-of-the-rock-jerusalem|Dome of the Rock]] (691 CE)** — built over the Foundation Stone where the Holy of Holies stood.
- **Christian biblical typology** — the Temple is read as type of the Church (1 Cor 3:16; the body-as-temple), of Christ's body (John 2:19–21), of the heavenly Jerusalem (Rev 21:22 — where there is *no* temple, because the Lamb is the temple).
- **[[tradition-knights-templar|The Knights Templar]] (1119)** — named for the Temple of Solomon (al-Aqsa, their HQ, identified as Templum Salomonis).
- **[[tradition-freemasonry|Freemasonry]] (1717 onwards)** — Masonic ritual is built around an elaborate mythologised history of Solomon's Temple, [[hiram-abi]] (Masonic "Hiram Abiff"), and the building's geometry. The Temple is the principal mythic-architectural reference in modern Freemasonry.
- **Mormon temple architecture (1830s onwards)** — Joseph Smith's Nauvoo and Salt Lake temples deliberately re-instantiate Solomonic temple symbolism.

One building, never excavated, lost for ~2600 years — and one of the **most architecturally consequential buildings in human history** through its textual and theological afterlife.
