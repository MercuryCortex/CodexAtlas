# Temple Church, London

## What it is

The **Temple Church** is the English headquarters church of the [[tradition-knights-templar|Knights Templar]], consecrated **10 February 1185** by Patriarch [[heraclius-of-jerusalem]] of Jerusalem during a state visit to King [[king-henry-ii-of-england|Henry II]]. Its **Round Church** — the surviving 12th-c. nave — is the most-cited Templar building in the English-speaking world and one of the clearest surviving examples of the Templar round-church programme of architectural *imitatio* of the [[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre-jerusalem|Holy Sepulchre]].

The complex consists of:
- **The Round Church** (1162–1185) — the original Templar nave, modeled on the Anastasis Rotunda.
- **The Chancel** ("the Oblong") (1220–1240) — extended in early-English-Gothic for King [[king-henry-iii-of-england|Henry III]] as a planned royal burial-place (he later chose Westminster Abbey).

The two phases — round nave + rectangular chancel — make Temple Church one of the most legible single-building documents of the **transition from Romanesque-Templar architectural austerity to Gothic English ecclesiastical fashion**, within the same century.

## History

**1119** — [[hugues-de-payens]] and his eight companions found the Templars at the al-Aqsa complex in Jerusalem.

**1128** — The Council of Troyes formally recognises the Order; [[bernard-of-clairvaux]] composes the *De Laude Novae Militiae* (*[[phase-5-037-bernard-de-laude-novae-militiae|In Praise of the New Knighthood]]*), the theological charter for the Templar vocation.

**~1147** — The Templars establish a London house in Holborn ("the Old Temple").

**~1162** — The Templars relocate to a new precinct between Fleet Street and the Thames ("the New Temple") and begin building the Round Church.

**10 February 1185** — Patriarch Heraclius of Jerusalem consecrates the Round Church during his English tour soliciting military aid for the Kingdom of Jerusalem (a tour that, by some accounts, foreshadowed Saladin's eventual recapture of Jerusalem in 1187).

**1199–1216** — King [[king-john-of-england|John]] often uses the Temple as a residence; [[william-marshal|William Marshal]], 1st Earl of Pembroke, joins the Templars on his deathbed in 1219 and is buried in the Round Church. His Purbeck-marble effigy is one of nine 12th–13th c. effigies that survived (one shattered) the 1941 Blitz.

**1215** — The negotiations preceding [[magna-carta]] involve the Templars as financial intermediaries between the Crown and the barons; tradition (Selden, *Titles of Honour*) holds parts of the meetings happened at the Temple.

**1220–1240** — The Oblong chancel is built in early-English-Gothic for Henry III.

**1307–1312** ([[event-suppression-of-templars-1307-1312]]) — The Order is suppressed; the property passes first to the Knights Hospitaller and eventually (after the Reformation dissolution) becomes the freehold of the Crown, leased to lawyers (the modern Inner and Middle Temple, two of the four Inns of Court — the legal precinct of barristerial England takes its name from the Templar building it surrounds).

**1841** — Major Victorian restoration by Smirke + Burton + Cottingham.

**10 May 1941** — A Luftwaffe incendiary raid destroys the chancel roof, gutting the interior. One of the Templar effigies is shattered. The post-war reconstruction (Walter Godfrey, 1958) restores the building but the original wood-work and paintwork are not recoverable.

## The Round Church — architectural reading

The Round Church is approximately **17 m in diameter**. It consists of:
- An outer wall of **six bays** (so the plan is not octagonal but a more complex polygonal-with-buttresses).
- An inner ring of **six clustered marble piers** supporting a clerestory and an internal arcade.
- A radiating ambulatory between inner and outer.
- A central focal point (in the medieval programme, the Templar altar; today, the nine surviving effigies arranged in the centre).

The form is modeled on the **Anastasis Rotunda** of the [[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre-jerusalem|Holy Sepulchre]] (Krautheimer's "iconographic copy" — same type, not literal geometric copy). The transmission relay is the Templars' occupation of the [[dome-of-the-rock-jerusalem|Dome of the Rock]] as *Templum Domini* (1119–1187), where they actually worshipped and from which they imported the rotunda-form back to Europe.

Six bays / six piers is unusual; most Templar round-naved cognates (Tomar's Charola, the Round Church Cambridge, Neuvy-Saint-Sépulcre, Charroux) use eight or sixteen. Wilson (2010) reads the six-bay London Round as an English regional variant within the Anglo-Norman architectural vocabulary, not a deliberate iconographic divergence.

## The effigies — Templar memorials of stone

Nine 12th–13th c. effigies of Templar-associated knights survive in the Round Church (one severely damaged in the 1941 bombing). The most famous is **[[william-marshal|William Marshal, 1st Earl of Pembroke]]** (d. 1219) — regent of England under the minority of Henry III, victor of the Battle of Lincoln (1217), and arguably the most celebrated knight of the High Middle Ages. His Purbeck-marble effigy in mail and surcoat is one of the canonical images of medieval English chivalry.

The effigies are not Templar tombs in the strict sense (one is William Marshal's two sons — never Templars; the others are mostly knights with Templar association). The arrangement converts the Round Church into a chivalric memorial — a sacred space that doubles as a hall of fame of the Plantagenet military aristocracy associated with the Order.

## The Inns of Court legacy

When the Templars were suppressed (1307–1312), their London precinct passed to the Knights Hospitaller (1324), then was Crown-acquired after the Reformation. The Crown leased the property to societies of lawyers. The **Inner Temple** and **Middle Temple** — two of the four Inns of Court — derive their names from the Templar precinct around the Round Church.

This is why every English barrister's professional address contains "Temple" — the medieval Templar architectural footprint has become the topographical scaffold of English jurisprudence. A direct line from 1162 Templar foundation to modern English bar.

## MASSIVE WIN — the English node of the round-church transmission

Temple Church is the most-visible English example of the Anastasis-imitatio chain:

> [[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre-jerusalem|Anastasis Rotunda]] (Jerusalem, 4th c.)
> → [[dome-of-the-rock-jerusalem|Dome of the Rock]] (Jerusalem, 691; Templar HQ 1119–1187)
> → **Temple Church Round** (London, 1162–1185)
> → English Inns of Court (1324 → present — secular afterlife)

One of the densest documents of the medieval *imitatio Jerusalemii* programme. England's principal contribution to the architectural transmission of the Holy Sepulchre type.
