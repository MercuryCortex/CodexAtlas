# Bighorn Medicine Wheel

## What it is

The **Bighorn Medicine Wheel** is a roughly circular arrangement of stones, ~24 m in diameter, on a ridge at ~2,940 m elevation on **Medicine Mountain** in the Bighorn Mountains of northern Wyoming. It consists of a **central stone cairn**, **six smaller cairns** arranged around the perimeter, and **28 radial stone spokes** running between the central cairn and the outer circle. The wheel is one of ~135 documented **Plains medicine wheels** distributed across Wyoming, Montana, Alberta, and Saskatchewan — but **Bighorn is by far the most elaborate and most-studied**.

Construction dates are debated but the best evidence places the principal construction phase at **~1650–1750 CE**, with some elements possibly older. The site has been **in continuous ritual use by multiple Plains-Native peoples** — Crow, Cheyenne, Arapaho, Lakota, Shoshone, Blackfoot, and others have all used the site for vision-quests, prayer, and ceremonial purposes. **The site remains in active religious use today**, with documented vision-quest practice and ceremonial visits by descendant communities; managed since 1996 under a co-management agreement between the US Forest Service and a council of tribal cultural representatives.

The Bighorn Medicine Wheel was the subject of [[john-eddy]]'s 1974 *Science* paper demonstrating that the cairns are deliberately aligned to:
1. **The summer-solstice sunrise** (the central cairn → easternmost peripheral cairn line).
2. **The summer-solstice sunset** (the central cairn → another peripheral cairn).
3. **The heliacal (pre-dawn) rises of Aldebaran (α Tauri), Rigel (β Orionis), and Sirius (α Canis Majoris)** — the three brightest stars in the late-summer to mid-autumn pre-dawn sky.

The site is the **most archaeo-astronomically-complex pre-modern monument in North America's Great Plains** and one of the cleanest single examples of stellar-alignment architecture anywhere in the world.

## History

### Plains medicine wheel tradition

Medicine wheels are a Plains religious-architectural form — stone arrangements on prominent landscape features (ridgetops, hilltops, exposed plateaus) using **stone-cairn-and-spoke patterns**. Of the ~135 documented examples, most are smaller and simpler than Bighorn; many lack the precise astronomical alignments. Bighorn is the most elaborate; **Majorville Cairn** in Alberta (~5,000+ years old at its earliest layer) is among the oldest.

The Plains medicine-wheel tradition stretches across multiple Plains-Native cultural groups (Crow, Cheyenne, Lakota, Blackfoot, Arapaho, Shoshone, Plains Cree, Stoney). It is **trans-cultural within the Plains region**, similar to how certain ritual forms (the Sun Dance, the pipe ceremony) are shared across multiple distinct linguistic-tribal groups in the same culture-area.

### Construction at Bighorn

The dating of the Bighorn Medicine Wheel is partly resolved through:
- **Radiocarbon dating** of wood fragments and charcoal in association with the construction layers (~1700 ± 100 CE).
- **Lichen dating** of stones (lichen growth rates suggest ~250 years for the largest patches — pointing to construction ~1750 CE).
- **Dendrochronology** of wood from nearby possibly-related sites.

The construction sequence may include:
- Earlier components (the central cairn, possibly) dating to several centuries earlier.
- The major Bighorn-wheel configuration constructed ~1650–1750 CE.
- Possibly ongoing additions or refurbishments through the 1800s.

Some scholars argue for older origins — Eddy himself entertained the possibility of construction back to ~200 CE — but the bulk of the evidence supports the 17th–18th c. dating for the major form.

### Eddy's 1974 archaeoastronomical analysis

[[john-eddy]], then a solar astronomer at the High Altitude Observatory (Boulder), surveyed the wheel in 1972 with his wife and colleagues. He measured the angular positions of:
- The **central cairn** to each of the six peripheral cairns.
- The directions of these lines compared to known astronomical events of the relevant epoch (~1700 CE; with precession corrections).

His findings (Eddy 1974, *Science*):
- **Cairn-1 (central) → Cairn-2 (E)** aligns with the summer-solstice sunrise.
- **Cairn-1 → Cairn-3 (W)** aligns with the summer-solstice sunset.
- **Cairn-1 → Cairn-4 (NE)** aligns with the heliacal rise of **Aldebaran** (α Tauri).
- **Cairn-1 → Cairn-5 (E)** aligns with the heliacal rise of **Rigel** (β Orionis).
- **Cairn-1 → Cairn-6 (E)** aligns with the heliacal rise of **Sirius** (α Canis Majoris).

The three stellar rises (Aldebaran, Rigel, Sirius) occur in the late-summer to early-autumn pre-dawn sky and form a sequence of helical risings spaced ~28 days apart — possibly used by Plains observers as a **post-solstice calendrical sequence** marking the approach of late summer + the start of autumn.

The **28 radial spokes** of the wheel may correspond to the **28-day lunar month** (the time between successive lunar appearances + the human female menstrual cycle, both of which are 28-day cycles attested in Plains-Native calendars). The "28 spokes for 28 days / one lunar month" interpretation is widely held but debated.

### Continuing ritual use

The Bighorn Medicine Wheel is **not** a purely-archaeological site — it is in **active religious use by multiple contemporary Plains-Native communities**. Visitors come for:
- **Vision-quests** (the multi-day fasting-prayer-isolation ceremony for personal spiritual revelation).
- **Pipe ceremonies**.
- **Prayer offerings** (sage, sweetgrass, tobacco, ribbons tied to the perimeter trees).
- **Solstice ceremonies** (most concentrated around the summer-solstice dates).

The site is managed under a **1996 cooperative agreement** between the US Forest Service and a council of representative tribes. The Forest Service maintains the trail + facilities; the tribes guide cultural-religious management decisions. Visitor access is sometimes restricted during ceremonial periods. The wheel itself is enclosed by a low fence (visitors view from outside the wheel).

## Architectural form

### Dimensions and structure

- **Outer diameter:** ~24 m (~80 feet).
- **Central cairn:** ~3.7 m diameter, ~0.6 m tall when first documented; smaller now due to weathering + visitor handling.
- **Six peripheral cairns:** smaller (~1 m diameter), arranged irregularly around the perimeter.
- **28 radial stone spokes** running from the central cairn outward to the perimeter circle.
- **Construction:** local limestone cobbles, set on the ground surface (not embedded in soil; just placed). The arrangement is therefore vulnerable to disturbance + has been incrementally modified over centuries.

The site is at the **summit ridge of Medicine Mountain** (2,940m elevation) — exposed, with sweeping views to the east + west horizons. The high-altitude exposure makes the site an excellent **horizon-marking astronomical platform** — the rising and setting positions of celestial events against the surrounding mountain horizon are precisely fixed and easily observed.

### Alignments (after Eddy 1974)

| Cairn pair (centre → peripheral) | Aligns with |
|---|---|
| 1 → 2 (E) | Summer-solstice sunrise (~21 June) |
| 1 → 3 (W) | Summer-solstice sunset (~21 June, evening) |
| 1 → 4 (NE) | Heliacal rise of Aldebaran (~1 month after solstice) |
| 1 → 5 (E) | Heliacal rise of Rigel (~2 months after solstice) |
| 1 → 6 (E) | Heliacal rise of Sirius (~3 months after solstice) |

The sequence is a **calendar of the late-summer / early-autumn cycle** — the period after the summer solstice when Plains peoples were on the high-mountain ranges hunting + gathering + preparing for the autumn return to the lowland winter camps. The alignments are functional for **timing the seasonal cycle** in a region where the brief Plains summer is the critical economic-religious period.

The **28-spoke + central-cairn pattern** has been interpreted variously:
- **28 days of the lunar cycle.**
- **28 ribs of a buffalo** (the buffalo being the central animal of Plains religion + economy).
- **28 days × cycles = annual solar calendar.**
- The **lodge-pole framework of a Plains tipi** (also has 28 poles in some configurations).

Multiple interpretations are not mutually exclusive — the form likely encoded **multiple converging cosmological-calendrical meanings**.

## Theological / cosmological function

### Vision quest site

The Bighorn Medicine Wheel is one of the **principal vision-quest sites** for multiple Plains-Native traditions. The vision quest:
- The seeker travels to a sacred high-elevation site (often a mountain or hill).
- Fasts and prays for **2–4 days alone in a small enclosed space** (a vision pit, often).
- Receives a **spiritual vision** that becomes the source of the seeker's adult identity, name, or sacred purpose.

The high-elevation exposed setting, the panoramic horizon-views, and the established multi-tribe sanctity all make Bighorn a particularly powerful vision-quest location. **Documented contemporary use is continuous**.

### Solar-stellar calendrical observation

The wheel's astronomical alignments make it an **integrated calendrical observatory** for the solar year (solstices) + the late-summer stellar cycle (Aldebaran-Rigel-Sirius heliacal rises). The combined system is **functionally similar to** [[stonehenge-sacred-site|Stonehenge]] + the [[fajada-butte-sun-dagger|Chaco Sun Dagger]] + [[el-castillo-chichen-itza|El Castillo]] + [[machu-picchu|Machu Picchu]]'s solar alignments + [[abu-simbel-temple|Abu Simbel]] — all astronomical-calendrical sacred-architectural systems.

### Trans-tribal Plains-religious importance

Unlike many sacred sites that belong to a single ethnic-religious tradition, Bighorn is **trans-tribally significant** across multiple Plains-Native peoples (Crow, Cheyenne, Arapaho, Lakota, Shoshone, Blackfoot). This **trans-tribal Plains religious geography** — sacred sites shared across many ethnic groups in the same cultural area — is structurally similar to:
- **Hindu pan-Indian pilgrimage circuit** ([[mount-kailash]], [[varanasi]], [[char-dham]]) — shared across all Hindu denominations.
- **Buddhist pan-Asian pilgrimage circuit** (Lumbini, Bodh Gaya, Sarnath, Kushinagar) — shared across all Buddhist traditions.
- **Christian Holy Land** ([[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre-jerusalem]]) — shared across all Christian denominations.

The pattern: **a sacred site that transcends sub-tradition boundaries within a larger cultural-religious continuum.**

## MASSIVE WIN — the Stonehenge-Bighorn 4000-year solstice-circle convergence

**The cleanest single case of cross-civilizational architectural-religious convergence in world religion may be the Stonehenge ↔ Bighorn Medicine Wheel parallel.**

Both are:
- **Circular stone constructions.**
- **In the open landscape on a prominent ridge or plain.**
- **Aligned to the summer-solstice sunrise** (and sometimes the sunset).
- **Incorporating additional astronomical alignments** (Stonehenge to lunar standstills; Bighorn to bright-star rises).
- **In active religious use across multiple successive cultural groups** (Stonehenge's Bronze Age + later veneration; Bighorn's multi-tribe Plains use).

Time gap: **4,000 years**. Spatial gap: **8,500 km**. Cultural contact: **zero**. The two monuments are pure convergent emergence of **the solstice-aligned stone circle as religious-architectural type** in mutually-uncontacting Neolithic British and 17th-century North American Plains societies.

**Broader cross-civilization convergent-astronomical-architecture set:**

- **[[stonehenge-sacred-site|Stonehenge]]** (~2500 BCE, Britain) — summer-solstice sunrise.
- **[[newgrange-passage-tomb|Newgrange]]** (~3200 BCE, Ireland) — winter-solstice dawn.
- **[[gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]]** (~9500 BCE, Anatolia) — possibly Sirius or other stellar alignments (still debated).
- **[[karnak-temple-complex|Karnak]]** (~1500 BCE, Egypt) — winter-solstice axis.
- **[[abu-simbel-temple|Abu Simbel]]** (~1265 BCE, Egypt) — biannual sanctuary illumination.
- **[[el-castillo-chichen-itza|El Castillo]]** (~1000 CE, Maya) — equinox feathered-serpent shadow.
- **[[fajada-butte-sun-dagger|Sun Dagger]]** (~1000 CE, Chaco) — solstices + equinoxes + lunar standstills.
- **[[machu-picchu]]** (~1450 CE, Inca) — June-solstice sunrise + multiple stellar alignments.
- **[[bighorn-medicine-wheel|Bighorn Medicine Wheel]]** (~1700 CE, Plains) — summer solstice + bright-star rises.

**Nine+ mutually-uncontacting civilizations across 11,000 years independently constructed precise sacred-architectural astronomical instruments.** No documented contact between most. The convergent religious-architectural use of celestial events as **cosmic-cyclical legitimation** is one of the strongest cases in human religion of **genuine universal religious-architectural patterns**.

The Plains-Native + the Neolithic British + the ancient Egyptian + the Chaco Ancestral Puebloan + the Inca + the Maya all arrived at **the same conceptual solution**: encode the solar year in stone, build a permanent architectural calendar that observes the cosmos's own cycles, demonstrate the religious-political authority's competence to know cosmic time.

**Continuity of religious use:** The Bighorn Medicine Wheel is **unique among the major astronomical-alignment sites in being in continuous active religious use today**. Most of the others (Stonehenge, Newgrange, Karnak, El Castillo, the Sun Dagger, Machu Picchu) are archaeological / tourist sites; their religious functions ceased centuries or millennia ago. Bighorn continues its religious function uninterrupted from at least 1700 CE to the present, by the lineal cultural descendants of the people who built it. This is one of the strongest cases anywhere of **religious-architectural continuity from pre-modern through contemporary use**.
