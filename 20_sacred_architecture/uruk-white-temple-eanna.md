# Uruk — White Temple and Eanna Precinct

## What it is

**Uruk** — modern Warka, in southern Iraq — was the largest city in the world c. 3500–3000 BCE, and its two great religious precincts (the **Kullaba** terrace with its **White Temple** dedicated to the sky-god An, and the **Eanna** precinct of [[inanna-sumerian|Inanna]]) are the **earliest examples of fully monumental sacred architecture in human history**, post-dating only the much smaller pre-pottery prehistoric sites like [[gobekli-tepe|Göbekli Tepe]] and [[catalhoyuk-sacred-site|Çatalhöyük]].

The White Temple — a tripartite cella standing on a 12-m-high mud-brick platform, finished in lime-plaster (hence "white") — is the **architectural type-specimen of the elevated platform-temple** that becomes, a millennium later, the formal stepped ziggurat ([[ziggurat-of-ur]]) and, two and a half millennia later, the great Babylonian [[etemenanki]] that the Hebrew tradition reframes as the [[tower-of-babel]].

Uruk is also the city of [[phase-1-004-gilgamesh-old-babylonian|the Epic of Gilgamesh]]'s eponymous king, and the Epic's opening lines praise the city's walls and the **Eanna precinct** — the temple precinct is **the city's identity** in Mesopotamian self-understanding.

## History

The German Archaeological Institute (Deutsches Archäologisches Institut, DAI) has excavated Uruk **continuously since 1912**, with the major campaigns interrupted only by the two World Wars and the post-2003 Iraq War period. The excavation, longer than any other in Mesopotamian archaeology, has produced a continuous architectural sequence from the Late Ubaid (~4000 BCE) through the Seleucid period (~150 BCE) — over 4,000 years of construction.

**Key phases of Uruk's temple precincts:**

- **Late Ubaid (~4000 BCE)** — Earliest monumental constructions on the Kullaba terrace; precursor temples to the later White Temple.
- **Uruk IV (Late Uruk, ~3500–3200 BCE)** — The **city's apogee** as a monumental settlement. The **White Temple** is built ~3500–3300 BCE atop the accumulated **Kullaba terrace** (~12 m above plain level). The **Eanna precinct** to the south is monumentally rebuilt in a series of vast structures (Temple C, Temple D, the Limestone Temple, the Stone-Cone Temple) decorated with the famous Uruk **cone mosaics** — coloured clay cones inserted into plastered walls to make geometric polychrome patterns.
- **Jemdet Nasr / Early Dynastic (~3100–2900 BCE)** — Continued monumental rebuilding at Eanna; transition into the form Sumerian temple-economy takes for the next two millennia.
- **Third Dynasty of Ur (~2100 BCE)** — [[ur-nammu]] formalises the **Anu Ziggurat** at Uruk atop the Kullaba mound — paralleling his ziggurats at Ur, Eridu, and Nippur. The Anu Ziggurat is now an Ur-III standardised three-terrace ziggurat in the same form as [[ziggurat-of-ur]].
- **Old Babylonian (~1800 BCE)** — Continued cult; Gilgamesh tradition is being codified during this period.
- **Neo-Babylonian (~600 BCE)** — Nebuchadnezzar II rebuilds Eanna; the Inanna-Ishtar cult continues.
- **Seleucid (~300–150 BCE)** — Late temple rebuilds (the Bīt Rēš, the Irigal); Uruk remains an important religious centre into the Hellenistic period before gradually being abandoned to the desert.

## Architectural form

**The White Temple** (Anu Ziggurat, Uruk IV phase, ~3500–3200 BCE):

- **Platform**: ~12 m above plain level, accumulated through earlier construction phases; the platform is **the building's principal architectural statement** — the temple is small compared to the immense platform.
- **Plan**: tripartite (the canonical Mesopotamian cella plan inherited from Eridu) — long central nave with altar at one end and offering-table at the other, side-rooms flanking.
- **Dimensions**: ~17.5 × 22.3 m at platform level — a substantial but not enormous building, sized for ritual use, not for crowds.
- **Surface**: lime-plastered white (the source of the "White Temple" name) — making the temple visible across the alluvial plain for many kilometres, a deliberate landscape-scale architectural gesture.
- **Approach**: a long ramp (rather than later ziggurat-style stairs) ascending the platform from the SE.

**The Eanna precinct** (Inanna's temenos): a sprawling complex of monumental structures across ~9 hectares — **Temple C** (a large tripartite hall, ~22 × 54 m), **Temple D** (similar but larger), the **Limestone Temple** (one of the earliest stone-built structures in Mesopotamia, in a region almost entirely lacking stone), the **Stone-Cone Temple**, the **Pillared Hall**, and the **Square Temple**. The precinct as a whole is **without parallel in the contemporary world for size and concentration of monumental architecture**.

**The cone mosaic** — small fired-clay cones (red, black, white) pressed into wet plaster to make geometric polychrome patterns — is the **earliest monumental architectural decoration in human history**, predating Egyptian painted tomb decoration. The cone mosaic disappears after the Uruk period and is not used again.

## Theological / cosmological function

Uruk had a **twin-cult**: An (the older sky-god, the *abstract heaven*) on the Kullaba precinct, and [[inanna-sumerian|Inanna]] (the goddess of love, war, and the morning/evening star — i.e. Venus) on the Eanna precinct.

**Eanna** means "**House of Heaven**" in Sumerian — the architectural-theological proposition is that the temple **is** the place where heaven becomes locally inhabited. Inanna's relationship to *heaven* (her father An / Anu being the sky) is built into the precinct's name: Inanna's house is itself the house of heaven, because she is heaven's daughter.

The Mesopotamian theology that emerges at Uruk and is fully attested by the late 3rd millennium:

- **The temple is the god's literal dwelling.** The cult-image inside the temple is the god's body. The god is fed, clothed, perfumed, entertained with music, taken on processional trips to other temples.
- **The temple is the city's pact with the god.** Without the temple, the city has no god; without the god, the city has no charter to exist.
- **The temple economy** ([[temple-economy]]) is the city's central institution. The temple owns land, herds, granaries, workshops, and slaves; the temple administers the redistribution that makes urban life possible. The earliest cuneiform writing — pictographic tokens evolving into the cuneiform script — emerges at Uruk in the Eanna precinct, almost certainly as **temple-accounting record-keeping**. The invention of writing is, in its first attestation, a *temple administrative technology*.

This last point is one of the deepest theological-architectural claims of Mesopotamian civilisation: **writing was invented in the temple, for the temple**. Civilisation's most consequential technology emerges as a side-effect of the architectural-theological problem of running a god's household.

## MASSIVE WIN — the moment monumentality and writing emerge together, in the same building

Uruk's Eanna precinct is the **single physical location** at which two of the most consequential developments in human history happen **at the same time, in the same building**:

1. **The first fully monumental sacred architecture** — the White Temple platform and the Eanna precinct's vast temple buildings are an order of magnitude larger than anything previously built for religious purposes.
2. **The invention of writing** — proto-cuneiform tablets, the earliest known writing system, are found in the Eanna precinct in temple-accounting contexts, c. 3300 BCE.

These are not two unrelated developments that happened to coincide. **They are causally linked.** The administrative pressure of a temple-economy at Uruk's scale — managing the redistribution of grain, livestock, textiles, and labour for tens of thousands of city-dwellers — required record-keeping that exceeded the capacity of memory and tokens. Writing emerges as the **technological response to the administrative pressure of the monumental temple-economy**.

This is one of the most consequential architectural-civilisational facts ever recovered by archaeology: **the building-form that becomes the ziggurat, and the writing-system that becomes cuneiform, are co-emergent at Uruk in the same century, in the same precinct, for the same theological-economic reason.**

Downstream: the Sumerian temple-architectural tradition produces the [[ziggurat-of-ur]] → [[etemenanki]] → [[tower-of-babel]] lineage; the Sumerian writing tradition produces cuneiform → Akkadian → Old Persian → ultimately, via complex chains, the **alphabet itself**. **Both lineages begin at Eanna.**
