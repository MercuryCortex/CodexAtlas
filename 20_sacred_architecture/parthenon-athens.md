# Parthenon

## What it is

The **Parthenon** on the Athenian Acropolis is the most-iconic surviving classical Greek temple and the **type-specimen** of the **peripteral Doric temple**. Built **447–432 BCE** under [[pericles|Pericles]] by the architects [[iktinos|Iktinos]] and [[kallikrates|Kallikrates]] with [[phidias|Phidias]] supervising the sculptural programme, it housed the colossal chryselephantine cult-statue of **Athena Parthenos** ("Athena the Maiden") and served as both:

- The principal **religious centre** of the cult of Athena in 5th-century Athens.
- The architectural **monument of the Athenian Empire** at its apex — funded substantially by Delian-League tribute relocated to the Athenian treasury.

## Form — the canonical Greek temple

- **Peripteral plan** — columns surround all four sides (8 × 17 columns, an unusual proportion enriching the building's apparent length).
- **Doric order** — the heavier, simpler order; baseless columns with simple capitals.
- **Pediments** at the east and west ends — triangular gables with sculpted figures: east, the **birth of Athena from Zeus's head**; west, the **contest of Athena and Poseidon for Athens**.
- **Metope frieze** (high-relief square panels on the outer entablature) — 92 metopes depicting the **Gigantomachy (East), Amazonomachy (West), Centauromachy (South), and Trojan War (North)**: the cosmic order's victory over chaos, four times across the building's exterior.
- **Ionic inner frieze** — running 160 m around the *cella* exterior — depicting the **Panathenaic Procession**: the four-yearly civic ritual that brought a new robe (*peplos*) to the goddess. This is one of the most-studied 160 m of sculpture in art history.
- **Architectural refinements** — entasis on the columns (slight bulge counteracting the optical illusion of concavity), curvature of the stylobate (slightly domed to drain rainwater AND counteract the optical illusion of sagging), inward inclination of the columns, varied corner-column thickness. The Parthenon's geometric perfection is **constructed of deliberate optical-correction asymmetries**.

## The Athena Parthenos

[[phidias|Phidias]]'s **colossal chryselephantine statue** of Athena Parthenos stood ~12 m tall in the *cella*. Materials: gold (for the dress, ~1100 kg of gold leaf, removable for emergencies) + ivory (for the flesh) over a wooden core. The goddess held a winged Nike in her right hand and stood on a base depicting the birth of Pandora. The original statue was lost in late antiquity (probably moved to Constantinople and destroyed there); Roman-era copies (the Varvakeion Athena, the Lenormant Athena) preserve the iconography. The Parthenon's interior was **designed around the statue** — the *cella*'s proportions, lighting, and entrance sequence stage its presentation.

## The afterlife — temple → church → mosque → ruin → national symbol

The Parthenon's post-classical history is one of the densest religious-architectural palimpsests in the Mediterranean:

- **~600 CE** — converted to a **Christian church**, dedicated to the Theotokos (the Virgin Mary). The east end was reordered for a Christian apse; the cult-image of Athena Parthenos was removed (probably to Constantinople); much of the interior was restructured.
- **1204** — after the Fourth Crusade, becomes a Latin (Catholic) church; renamed Notre-Dame d'Athènes.
- **~1460** — after the Ottoman conquest of Athens (1456), converted to a **mosque**; a minaret was added.
- **26 September 1687** — **catastrophic destruction**: a Venetian artillery shell (Francesco Morosini's siege of the Acropolis during the Morean War) ignited Ottoman gunpowder stored in the Parthenon-as-mosque. The central section of the building exploded. The 1687 explosion is the **most consequential single event** in the Parthenon's physical history — what survives today is essentially the post-1687 ruin.
- **1801–1812** — Lord Elgin removed about half the surviving sculpture, including most of the Ionic frieze, with Ottoman firman of disputed authority. The Elgin Marbles / Parthenon Marbles are now in the British Museum; the Greek state has formally requested return since 1983.
- **1832** — Athens becomes the capital of the new Greek state; the Parthenon becomes the **architectural emblem of Greek national identity**.
- **1975–present** — major ongoing restoration project: anastylosis of fallen elements, reinforcement, removal of Roman concrete repairs.

The Parthenon's **2400-year continuous architectural-religious-political relevance** is unique among ancient Mediterranean temples. Few buildings have been read by so many traditions for so long.

## What the Parthenon transmits

The Parthenon is the architectural **type-specimen** of the classical Greek peripteral temple. Its formal lessons — the Doric order, the optical refinements, the proportional system (based on a 4:9 ratio appearing repeatedly in plan and elevation), the integration of sculpture into the architectural programme — were absorbed into:

- **The Roman temple tradition** ([[pantheon-rome|Pantheon]] and other) — though Romans selectively combined Greek decoration with their own (Etruscan-Italic) ground-plan.
- **Renaissance and Neoclassical architecture** — Palladio's *Quattro Libri* (1570), and the 18th-c. revival via Stuart and Revett's *Antiquities of Athens* (1762), made the Parthenon the **canonical reference of Western architectural classicism**.
- **Civic/government architecture** of the modern era — from Schinkel's Altes Museum (Berlin) to the US Supreme Court — through deliberate stylistic quotation of the peripteral Doric type.

The Parthenon is the building that the Western classical-architectural tradition keeps returning to as **the source-text of architectural classicism**. It is religious-architectural transmission at the deepest possible level.
