# Mount Meru (Sumeru)

## What it is — and what it is not

Meru is **not a place**. It is the load-bearing member of a cosmography: the mountain at the centre of the world-disc, around which the sun, moon and planets circle, on and above whose summit the gods live, ringed by concentric mountain-walls and seas, with the human continent Jambūdvīpa lying to its south. Sanskrit *Meru* / *Sumeru*; Pali *Sineru*; Tibetan *Ri-rab*; Chinese *Xūmí-shān* 須彌山; Japanese *Shumisen*.

No tradition places it on a map you could walk to. It is the frame the map is drawn on — which is why it survives translation into Tibetan, Chinese and Javanese without ever needing a location, and why it can be **built**: a cosmography with a centre can be modelled, and the whole architectural history in [[theme-cosmic-mountain-as-temple-form]] follows from that.

**When it appears.** Late. Meru is absent from the Ṛgveda. The earliest brahmanical mention is a brief allusion at *Taittirīya Āraṇyaka* 1.7.1–3, naming Mahāmeru without any cosmological system around it (Satinsky 2015; Mabbett 1983 notes a single late-Vedic allusion). The developed cosmography belongs to the post-Vedic literature: the epics, the Purāṇas, Buddhist Abhidharma and the Jain cosmographic texts. This node is therefore dated `B7` — a cosmological-only place — and the earlier proposal in its own scaffolding to redate it to a "-1200 Vedic first-mention" has been **withdrawn as unsupportable**.

## Disambiguation — three things Meru is often confused with

- **Meru ≠ Mandara.** The mountain used as the churning-staff in the *samudra-manthana* (churning of the ocean of milk) is **Mandara**, not Meru. The two are routinely conflated in popular accounts and in Angkor Wat's own relief programme discussions.
- **Meru ≠ Kailāsa.** [[mount-kailash]] is a real Himalayan peak, treated in several traditions as Meru's earthly correlate — but the traditions do not agree that it *is* Meru, and Jains identify the same massif as Aṣṭāpada, where [[rishabha-jain]] attained mokṣa. Different claim, different mountain.
- **Meru ≠ the modern Mount Meru in Tanzania**, which is an unrelated volcano bearing a coincidental name.

## The three cosmographies — where they agree and where they diverge

| | **Hindu (Purāṇic)** | **Buddhist (Abhidharmic)** | **Jain (karaṇānuyoga)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name | Meru / Mahāmeru / Sumeru | Sumeru / Sineru | Meru / Mandara |
| Height | 84,000 yojanas above the earth's surface, 16,000 below (*Viṣṇu Purāṇa* 2.2) | 80,000 yojanas above the water, 80,000 below to the golden ground (*Abhidharmakośa* III) | 100,000 yojanas, the figure matching Jambūdvīpa's own diameter |
| Profile | Broader at the summit than at the base — a lotus seed-cup | Square in section; hourglass-waisted in the Kośa's account | Tapering |
| Surroundings | Concentric ring-continents and ring-oceans; four buttress mountains | Seven concentric golden mountain-ranges with seven seas, then the outer ocean holding four continents, closed by the Cakravāḍa iron ring-wall | Jambūdvīpa a disc 100,000 yojanas across, divided by six ranges into seven *varṣas* |
| Who is on it | Brahmā's city at the summit; the Lokapālas at the cardinal points | Cāturmahārājika heaven on the slopes; Trāyastriṃśa — the Thirty-Three, with Śakra ([[indra]]) — on the summit; the formless heavens above | Not a permanent divine residence in the Hindu sense; the site of the Tīrthaṅkaras' birth-anointing |
| Function beyond cosmography | Astronomical pole-reference in siddhāntic astronomy | Structural index for the whole system of thirty-one realms | Fixed reference in an uncreated, eternally-structured *loka* |

**The disagreement is the evidence.** Three traditions that share a language, a region and centuries of argument give three different measurements for the same mountain. That is not what independent invention looks like; it is what happens when a received frame is re-measured inside three separate doctrinal systems. Kirfel 1920 laid the three side by side from the sources and the comparison has held.

## What Meru does in each system

**Hindu.** The Purāṇas ([[phase-4-109-vishnu-purana]], [[phase-5-050-bhagavata-purana]]) and the epics ([[phase-3-095-mahabharata]], [[ramayana]]) make Meru the golden axis of Jambūdvīpa: the gods' seat, the pivot the heavenly bodies turn around, and the geometric origin of the world's quartered arrangement. [[brahma]]'s city stands at the summit with the guardians of the directions ranged around it; [[vishnu]] and [[shiva]] are located in relation to it.

**Buddhist.** Sumeru is not decorative — it is *structural*. The entire Abhidharma account of the thirty-one realms is indexed to the mountain: hells below, the human continents in the outer ocean, the Four Great Kings on Sumeru's flanks, the Thirty-Three at its summit, the form and formless heavens stacked above. [[vasubandhu]]'s [[phase-4-077-abhidharmakosa]] is the systematic statement; [[buddhaghosa]] carries the parallel Theravāda commentarial version ([[philosophy-abhidharma]]). Later Vajrayāna revises the scheme rather than abandoning it ([[kalachakra]], [[kalachakra-symbol]]) — and the [[mandala-circle]] in its developed form is Sumeru's world-system drawn from above, which is why the Tibetan maṇḍala-offering rite offers Meru and its continents heap by heap.

**Jain.** Meru sits at the centre of Jambūdvīpa in the Madhyaloka, the middle world of an **uncreated, eternal** cosmos — no creator, no cosmogonic moment, so Meru is a permanent fixture rather than a product ([[philosophy-jain-darshana]]; [[tradition-jainism]]). Its distinctive ritual role has no Hindu or Buddhist equivalent: at the birth of every Tīrthaṅkara, [[indra]] carries the infant to Meru's summit for the *janmābhiṣeka*, the birth-anointing — from [[rishabha-jain]] through [[mahavira-jain]]. Meru is where Jain sacred biography *begins*, twenty-four times over.

## Meru built

The reason this node lives in `20_sacred_architecture/` rather than in the motif lens: Meru is the most-built cosmology in human history.

- **The stupa.** Dome (*aṇḍa*) as the mountain-mass, *harmikā* as the summit palace, *yaṣṭi* as the axis running through it, tiered *chattrāvalī* as the heavens above — [[sanchi-great-stupa]] is the Indian type-specimen, and clockwise circumambulation walks the cosmic circuit (Snodgrass 1985; Mus 1935). See [[theme-stupa-as-cosmic-mountain]].
- **The Hindu temple.** *Garbhagṛha* ("womb-chamber") as the cave in the mountain's heart, *śikhara* / *vimāna* as the peak. The identification is written into the builders' manuals: the śilpa and vāstu treatises name a *Meru* class of prāsāda outright ([[vastu-shastra]]; the *Samarāṅgaṇasūtradhāra* lists it at the head of its prāsāda classes; ground-plan at [[vastu-purusha-mandala]]). Built: [[kandariya-mahadeva-khajuraho]], [[brihadeeswarar-temple-thanjavur]], [[meenakshi-amman-temple-madurai]], [[konark-sun-temple]], and in rock-cut form [[ellora-cave-complex]].
- **Borobudur.** [[borobudur-stupa-mandala]] maps the Mahāyāna *Trailokya* onto its three vertical zones so the pilgrim's ascent *is* the cosmological ascent — the most architecturally explicit Meru anywhere.
- **The Khmer temple-mountain.** [[angkor-wat-temple-complex]] under [[suryavarman-ii]]: five towers as Meru with its buttress peaks, the moat as the encircling cosmic ocean; continued at [[angkor-thom-bayon]] under [[jayavarman-vii]] ([[khmer-empire]]; [[place-angkor-wat]]).
- **Burma and the Buddhist mainland.** [[bagan-temple-plain]]; and east through [[bulguksa-temple-and-seokguram-grotto]] and [[todaiji-daibutsuden]].
- **Bali.** The tiered shrine-tower of Balinese Hinduism is still called, simply, a *meru*.
- **The altar.** In Chinese and Japanese Buddhist architecture the whole mountain survives at furniture scale as the **Sumeru pedestal** — *xūmízuò* 須彌座 / *shumidan* 須弥壇 — the waisted platform an icon is set on, so that every Buddha image sits on the world-mountain. This is the motif's smallest and most widely reproduced descendant.

## Meru and observational astronomy

Meru is also where Indian cosmography collides with Indian mathematical astronomy. The siddhāntic tradition ([[astronomy-surya-siddhanta]]; [[aryabhata-astronomer]]; [[astronomy-aryabhatiya]]) places Meru at the terrestrial north pole and reasons from that position about the length of day at the poles and the apparent motion of the heavens — a cosmographic postulate doing real computational work. The friction between the Purāṇic Meru and the spherical-earth geometry the siddhāntas actually calculate with is one of the more interesting unresolved seams in premodern Indian science, and the traditions handled it by keeping the two registers largely apart rather than reconciling them.

## Transmission and parallel — the classification for this node

Per the project CODEX §III, stated explicitly so a reader can check the call:

**TRANSMISSION.** Hindu ↔ Buddhist ↔ Jain Meru (common Indic descent plus continuous mutual borrowing). India → Sri Lanka, Java, Cambodia, Burma, Tibet, China, Korea, Japan (attested by translated texts, inscriptions, named donors and an unbroken form-lineage). These are terracotta wires.

**PARALLEL.** Meru ↔ the Mesopotamian ziggurat; Meru ↔ Mesoamerican and Andean mountain-pyramids. Teal wires.

**Catalogued and declined.** There is a real published hypothesis — von Heine-Geldern 1942, Quaritch Wales 1953 — deriving the Asian cosmic mountain specifically from the Mesopotamian ziggurat, via Achaemenid conquest of Babylon, Seleucid contact with Mauryan India, and the Persianate style visible in Aśokan-period art. The contact is genuine; the derivation is not entailed by it. Lambert 1975 removes the Mesopotamian end of the chain (the cosmic-peak cosmology the hypothesis needs is not securely reconstructable from the cuneiform sources); Gombrich 1975 shows Vedic India already had indigenous resources for the schemes in question; Mabbett 1983 concludes the derivation is open to question while explicitly declining to reject diffusion in principle, and Mus had argued as early as 1935 that Meru's Indian development was substantially home-grown. The vault records the hypothesis at T2 with its rebuttal attached, and does **not** wire it as transmission.
