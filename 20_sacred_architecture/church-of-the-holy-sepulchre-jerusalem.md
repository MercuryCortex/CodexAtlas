# Church of the Holy Sepulchre

## What it is

The **Church of the Holy Sepulchre** is the building that, by Christian tradition since the 4th century, covers the site of Christ's **Crucifixion** (Golgotha) and **Resurrection** (the empty tomb). It is one site, two events, one architectural complex — and its **rotunda over the tomb**, the *Anastasis*, is **the architectural archetype** that propagates outward through a millennium of Christian sacred building.

Hagia Sophia is the dome that ate Byzantium. The Holy Sepulchre is the rotunda that ate medieval Europe.

## Why it is the centre of this lens

This single building is the proximate or distal *imitatio*-source for:

- **The [[dome-of-the-rock-jerusalem|Dome of the Rock]]** (691 CE) — the first monumental Islamic building, built as an Umayyad rotunda over the Foundation Stone, in deliberate visual dialogue with the Anastasis (Grabar 1959).
- **Hundreds of round / polygonal naved churches across medieval Europe** — Charroux, Neuvy-Saint-Sépulcre, Cambridge Round Church, Northampton Round Church, Bologna's Santo Stefano "Hierusalem", the Templars' [[temple-church-london|Temple Church London]] (1185), the [[convent-of-christ-tomar|Charola at Tomar]] (~1160).
- **[[lalibela-rock-hewn-churches|Lalibela]]** (12th–13th c.) — Ethiopian King Lalibela's New-Jerusalem programme of eleven rock-hewn churches, with site-names mapping the Holy Sepulchre topography (the stream renamed "Jordan", churches named for Golgotha, the Virgin Mary, etc.).
- **Templar architectural identity itself** — the Order's vocation was protection of pilgrims to *this* building; every Templar round-nave they built in Europe is an architectural memorial of the Holy Sepulchre (Barber 1994; Nicholson 2001).

## History — paradox of preservation

The site has a paradoxical preservation history. After the [[event-destruction-of-second-temple-70|destruction of Jerusalem in 70 CE]] and the Bar-Kokhba revolt (132–135), Emperor [[hadrian-emperor|Hadrian]] built a Temple of Venus / Capitoline complex over the area as part of his refounding of Jerusalem as *Aelia Capitolina*. This Hadrianic pagan overlay, almost certainly intended to suppress Christian memory, paradoxically **fixed the location** of Christian memory under a recognisable landmark.

When [[constantine-the-great|Constantine]] commissioned [[macarius-of-jerusalem|Bishop Macarius]] to excavate the site around **326 CE**, the Hadrianic structures were demolished and a tomb was uncovered which was identified as that of Christ. Constantine's mother [[helena-empress|Helena]] is traditionally credited with finding the [[true-cross]] there (Eusebius, *Life of Constantine* III.25–40).

Constantine's original complex consisted of:
1. **The Anastasis Rotunda** — circular building covering the tomb.
2. **The Martyrium** — basilica covering Golgotha.
3. An open courtyard between them.

The complex was consecrated **335 CE**. It was sacked by the Sasanians in 614, restored by [[modestus-of-jerusalem]], razed by the Fatimid caliph [[al-hakim-bi-amr-allah]] in 1009 (a casus belli the Latin West never forgot), partially rebuilt by Byzantine Emperor Constantine IX Monomachos in 1048, and consolidated into roughly its current form by the [[tradition-knights-templar|Crusaders]] after their capture of Jerusalem in 1099 (re-consecrated 1149).

## The Anastasis Rotunda — what it transmits

The Rotunda's elements that propagate as architectural type:

- **Centralised plan over a sacred locus** (the tomb).
- **Surrounding ambulatory** of columns supporting a domed roof.
- **Apse, semicircular niches, opening to the sky/upper-level windows**.
- **Programme of pilgrimage circumambulation** around the relic-locus.

When a medieval builder built a "Holy-Sepulchre church", they were not aiming at literal geometric copy — Krautheimer's foundational 1942 study (*Introduction to an Iconography of Mediaeval Architecture*) shows that *imitatio* in medieval architecture meant **iconographic resemblance + dedication**, not surveyor's reproduction. A round nave + a dedication to the Holy Sepulchre = an architectural "copy". This is how the Anastasis multiplied.

## Custody and the Status Quo

Today the building is shared between six Christian communions under the 1852 **Status Quo** (Ottoman firman, never revised): the Greek Orthodox, Latin/Franciscan, [[tradition-armenian-christianity|Armenian Apostolic]], [[tradition-egyptian-coptic-christianity|Coptic Orthodox]], [[tradition-ethiopian-tewahedo|Ethiopian Tewahedo]], and Syriac Orthodox. The keys are held by two Muslim families (Joudeh and Nuseibeh) — a 1187 Saladin arrangement preserved through Ottoman and modern eras. The "immovable ladder" on the façade is the textbook image of the Status Quo's pathological stability.

## MASSIVE WIN — the architectural transmission chain

Christianity → older sources is the project's prize. But Christianity also **transmits outward**, and the Holy Sepulchre is the densest transmitter of architectural form in religious history:

> **Anastasis Rotunda (Jerusalem, 326–335 CE)**
> → [[dome-of-the-rock-jerusalem|Dome of the Rock]] (Jerusalem, 691 — Umayyad response in Islamic architectural language)
> → [[convent-of-christ-tomar|Tomar Charola]] (Portugal, ~1160) + [[temple-church-london|Temple Church]] (London, 1185) + hundreds of European round-naved churches (Templar + secular)
> → [[lalibela-rock-hewn-churches|Lalibela]] (Ethiopia, c. 1200 — rock-cut New Jerusalem)

One building, three faith-traditions, four continents-of-influence, eight centuries of architectural offspring. The Anastasis is the architectural Logos of medieval sacred building.
