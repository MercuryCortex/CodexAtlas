# Swabian Jura Aurignacian Figurines and Flutes (c. 40,000–33,000 BCE)

## What happened
Excavations across four limestone caves of the Swabian Jura in Baden-Württemberg, southwest Germany — Hohle Fels (Ach Valley), Hohlenstein-Stadel (Lone Valley), Vogelherd (Lone Valley), and Geißenklösterle (Ach Valley) — have produced a coherent assemblage of Aurignacian-age (~42,500–33,000 BP) figurative ivory carvings and bone musical instruments that constitute Europe's earliest documented portable representational art and the world's earliest known musical-instrument tradition. Key objects:

- **Löwenmensch / Lion-Man of Hohlenstein-Stadel** — a ~31 cm carved mammoth-ivory statuette of a standing figure with the head and front limbs of a cave lion and the body and legs of a human. Excavated in 1939 (Wetzel); initially mis-reconstructed; substantially completed by re-excavation 2008–2013 (Kind et al. 2014). Currently dated ~40,000 BP, making it **the earliest known anthropomorphic-zoomorphic (therianthropic) sculpture** anywhere in the human record.
- **Venus of Hohle Fels** — a ~6 cm mammoth-ivory female figurine with exaggerated breasts, abdomen, and vulva, and a carved-loop suspension ring where a head would otherwise be. Excavated 2008; published Conard 2009 *Nature*; dated to the basal Aurignacian ~35,000–40,000 BP. **The earliest unambiguous depiction of the human form**, predating the eastern European "gravettian Venuses" (Willendorf, Dolní Věstonice) by 5,000–10,000 years.
- **Hohle Fels flute** — a near-complete bone flute carved from the radius of a griffon vulture, five finger-holes, V-notched mouthpiece. Plus ivory flute fragments from Geißenklösterle. Published Conard, Malina, Münzel 2009 *Nature*; dated ~40,000 BP. **The earliest known musical instruments**.
- **Vogelherd zoomorphic figurines** — mammoth, horse, bison, cave lion, and other animal carvings (the "Vogelherd horse"), excavated by Riek 1931 and re-evaluated by Conard's team. Dated to the early Aurignacian.

The whole assemblage clusters in the Ach and Lone valleys within a few dozen kilometers of each other and is now inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage ("Caves and Ice Age Art in the Swabian Jura," 2017).

## Religious consequences
- **The Löwenmensch is the earliest material evidence of supernatural-hybrid imagery in the European record**, and (until the Sulawesi 2019 discoveries pushed the figurative-narrative threshold earlier) was the world's earliest known therianthrope. Whatever its specific local meaning, a 31 cm ivory composite that took an estimated 400+ hours to carve indicates institutionalized symbolic activity, not personal whim — i.e., shared belief or shared narrative.
- **The Venus of Hohle Fels** is the earliest known figural representation of the human body. The exaggerated reproductive features and the missing head-replaced-by-loop (designed for wearing or suspension) have been variously interpreted (fertility object, self-portrait genre, social-bonding token, didactic image of pregnancy). Mellars 2009 in the same *Nature* issue cautions against assuming "Mother Goddess" continuity to later European Gravettian Venuses — a caution that has held up.
- **The Hohle Fels and Geißenklösterle flutes** are evidence that music — and therefore some form of patterned acoustic communal practice — was part of the Aurignacian behavioral package at first contact with Europe. Whether the flutes had ritual, social, or aesthetic function (these are not mutually exclusive) cannot be inferred from the instruments themselves.
- The Swabian assemblage as a whole is load-bearing in the "human revolution" / "behavioral modernity" debate: by ~40,000 BP, *Homo sapiens* entering Europe possessed figurative art, body-image representation, supernatural-hybrid imagery, and musical instruments — the full cognitive package conventionally associated with religious capacity.

## Disputes
- **Dating.** The Hohle Fels Venus's date (~35,000–40,000 BP) depends on the basal Aurignacian layer's radiocarbon chronology, which Higham et al. 2012 revised; the Löwenmensch's date depends on associated charcoal AMS dates and stratigraphic reanalysis. The figures' ages are now widely accepted within ~40,000 BP ± 2,000 years; finer resolution is contested.
- **Authorship attribution.** The Aurignacian is conventionally identified with *Homo sapiens*; the Châtelperronian (a slightly earlier transitional industry in nearby France) is contested between *Homo sapiens* and late Neanderthal authorship. The Swabian figurines are securely *Homo sapiens*; the question is whether comparable cognitive capacity in adjacent late-Neanderthal populations is also attested (red-stencil dating in Iberia, La Roche-Cotard finger flutings; the issue remains active).
- **Interpretation.** "Therianthrope = shamanic transformation" (Lewis-Williams 2002, applied to the Löwenmensch) is one interpretive frame, not consensus. Alternatives: mythological hybrid, totemic ancestor, narrative-illustrative figure (a character in a story whose specifics are lost), social-emblem object. Conard himself has consistently been cautious about specific religious interpretation in publication.
- **"Earliest" claims** are inherently subject to revision. Each of the Swabian "earliest" titles has been contested or pushed earlier by subsequent finds (Sulawesi 2019/2024 narrative scenes, Bornean 2018 figurative cattle, Iberian Neanderthal-attributed marks). The reliable claim is that the Swabian Jura is the earliest known *European* assemblage with this full range of behaviors.
