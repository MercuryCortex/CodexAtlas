# New Atheism (2004–2010)

## What happened
A burst of bestselling, sharply polemical books arguing against religious belief, concentrated in the 2004–2007 window:
- Sam Harris, *The End of Faith* (Aug 2004) — published in the immediate post-9/11 climate, opens the wave; argues that religious moderation is a permissive enabler of religious extremism.
- Richard Dawkins, *The God Delusion* (Oct 2006) — over 3 million English-language copies sold; the most-discussed of the wave; argues that the God-hypothesis is empirically testable and fails.
- Daniel Dennett, *Breaking the Spell* (Feb 2006) — the most academically restrained: argues that religion should be studied scientifically as a natural phenomenon.
- Christopher Hitchens, *God Is Not Great* (May 2007) — the most rhetorically incendiary; long subtitle "How Religion Poisons Everything"; substantial influence in journalism and cultural commentary.

These four authors were rebranded (somewhat self-consciously by 2007) as "the Four Horsemen," and the package was given the label "New Atheism" by writer Gary Wolf in *Wired* magazine (Nov 2006). The wave was reinforced by Victor Stenger, A.C. Grayling, Lawrence Krauss, Jerry Coyne, and others.

By 2010–2012 the cultural moment had substantially passed. Hitchens's 2011 death from esophageal cancer was a symbolic endpoint. The movement's energy migrated through several channels: (1) "Atheism+" / social-justice-atheism (2012, brief and contested); (2) public-secularism advocacy (Center for Inquiry, Freedom From Religion Foundation, Sunday Assembly 2013); (3) cultural-conservative atheism (Sam Harris solo, Steven Pinker, Bret Weinstein etc.); (4) "intellectual dark web" (2018) — partial successor formation including some New-Atheism-adjacent figures alongside religious conservatives like [[jordan-peterson]]. Dennett died in 2024.

## Religious consequences
- **Cultural permission for non-belief.** Major effect: New Atheism made non-belief publicly speakable in American (and to a lesser extent British) middle-class culture. The rise of nones ([[event-rise-of-nones-2007-present]]) overlaps the New Atheism period and is plausibly mutually-reinforcing, though most "nones" are not New-Atheism-style militant atheists.
- **Religious counter-mobilization.** Substantial Christian (especially Catholic and evangelical) and Muslim counter-response: theological replies (Plantinga, [[david-bentley-hart]] 2009 *Atheist Delusions*, Eagleton 2009), apologetic publishing wave (William Lane Craig, John Lennox), Catholic intellectual responses.
- **Specific anti-Islam emphasis.** Particularly in Harris's and Hitchens's work, the post-9/11 anti-Islam emphasis was prominent and controversial; the movement was repeatedly accused of Islamophobia by religious-studies scholars and progressive religious writers.
- **Methodological-philosophical critique.** Academic philosophers of religion (Plantinga, Swinburne, Feser) and theologians (Eagleton, Hart, McGrath) argued the New Atheist treatments of religion were philosophically and theologically unsophisticated — engaging with popular religious fundamentalism rather than serious religious thought.

## Disputes
- **Sophistication of the religious targets.** The principal recurring critique: New Atheism attacks a "straw" version of religion (the fundamentalist literal-creationist version) rather than serious religious philosophy (Aquinas, classical theism, Augustinian and Reformed serious religious thought). Hart 2009 and Eagleton 2009 are the principal book-length statements of this critique. Defenders (Dawkins, Coyne) argue that fundamentalist religion *is* the politically and socially consequential version and is appropriately the target.
- **Causal contribution to "nones."** Disputed how much New Atheism specifically (vs. broader cultural shift, religious-right backlash, internet, sexual-abuse scandals) drove non-affiliation.
- **Political trajectory.** Several New-Atheist or New-Atheist-adjacent figures (Harris, Pinker, Maajid Nawaz, Ayaan Hirsi Ali) moved in the late 2010s into political positions increasingly distant from progressive politics, generating a sub-controversy about whether New Atheism's "rationalist" stance was in tension with its early progressive image. Hirsi Ali's 2023 announcement of Christian faith was a particularly noted defection.
