# Azusa Street Revival (1906–1909)

## Overview
The three-year revival at 312 Azusa Street, Los Angeles, California, led by [[william-seymour]], is the founding event of global Pentecostalism. Beginning in April 1906, the meetings at a former African Methodist Episcopal church turned stable ran continuously — often 24 hours a day — for months on end. Participants spoke in tongues, prophesied, fell prostrate, reported healings, and wept. What made the revival historically singular was its interracial character: in Jim Crow America, Black, white, Latino, and Asian worshippers gathered together in sustained shared ecstatic worship under a Black pastor. The revival became the transmission node from which Pentecostal experience dispersed worldwide within months.

## The building and early meetings
The building at 312 Azusa Street had been built as a First African Methodist Episcopal church, later used as a livery stable, then a tombstone shop. [[william-seymour]] held the first meetings in the home of Richard and Ruth Asberry at 214 N. Bonnie Brae Street in early April 1906. When crowds exceeded the house's capacity, the group moved to the dilapidated Azusa Street building. Services began on April 14, 1906.

## The *Los Angeles Times* report and national spread
On April 18, 1906 — the same morning as the San Francisco earthquake — the *Los Angeles Times* published a mocking front-page report headlined "Weird Babel of Tongues: New Sect of Fanatics Is Breaking Loose." The paper described the scenes with ridicule: "They cry and make howling noises all day and into the night. They run, jump, shake all over, shout to the top of their voice, spin around in circles, fall out on the sawdust blanketed floor jerking, kicking and rolling all over it." The juxtaposition with the earthquake gave the story enormous national reach. The ridicule had the opposite of its intended effect: it publicized the meetings nationally.

## Racial desegregation through Spirit-possession
The sociological significance of Azusa Street is as striking as its religious significance. [[william-seymour]] was the son of freed slaves from Louisiana. He had studied under [[charles-parham]] in Houston — but Parham, operating in the South, had seated Seymour outside the classroom door due to segregation. Yet in Los Angeles, under Seymour's leadership, the shared experience of Spirit-possession created a community that temporarily dissolved the racial hierarchy enforced by law across the American South. The white Pentecostal pioneer Frank Bartleman wrote: "The colour line was washed away in the Blood." This was not permanent — racial divisions reasserted themselves within Pentecostalism within a decade — but the founding moment was genuinely interracial in a way that had few parallels in early 20th-century American religious life. See [[possession-ritual]] for the cross-tradition structural parallel: Spirit-possession as a social leveling mechanism that crosses enforced social boundaries.

## Global spread (1906–1910)
Within months of April 1906, missionaries who visited Azusa Street carried the experience worldwide:
- **Scandinavia**: Thomas Ball Barratt (Norway) visited in 1906; his meetings in Christiana (Oslo) launched Scandinavian Pentecostalism (1907).
- **Britain**: Alexander Boddy in Sunderland; the Apostolic Faith Church (1908).
- **India**: Pandita Ramabai had already been experiencing revival phenomena at her Mukti Mission (Maharashtra, 1905) independently; Azusa Street cross-confirmed the movement.
- **Chile**: Willis Hoover's Methodist church in Valparaíso experienced Pentecostal revival (1909), the origin of Chilean Pentecostalism — now one of the largest Protestant bodies in South America.
- **West Africa**: multiple missionaries.

## Parham's rejection of the revival
[[charles-parham]] — whose Topeka, Kansas teaching had formulated tongues as the initial evidence — visited Azusa Street in October 1906 and rejected it. He was scandalized by what he called "hypnotism" and "wild bedlam" and by the interracial character of the worship. He attempted to take over leadership and was rebuffed by Seymour. Parham's subsequent marginalization (compounded by a 1907 arrest on charges later dropped) meant that Seymour, not Parham, became the primary apostolic figure for the global Pentecostal movement.

## Legacy
Azusa Street is the transmission node for global Pentecostalism in the same way the Council of Nicaea is for Trinitarian orthodoxy — the event that fixed the shape of what followed. From a dozen founding figures, a movement of 600–700 million adherents grew within a century. The tradition node is [[tradition-pentecostalism]].

## MASSIVE-win edge
The simultaneous dissolution of racial hierarchy and the outbreak of ecstatic Spirit-possession at Azusa Street makes this event the clearest historical demonstration of the cross-tradition thesis: [[possession-ritual]] (structured communal Spirit-possession) consistently disrupts conventional social hierarchies. The same structural dynamic appears in Vodou's preserving African identity through French colonial oppression (see [[tradition-vodou-haitian]]), and in Dionysian religion's role in transgressing gender and class boundaries in archaic Greece (see [[tradition-dionysian-mysteries]]). These are independent developments of the same pattern.
