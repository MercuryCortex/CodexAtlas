# Cyrus the Great Conquers Babylon (539 BCE) — and the Edict of Return

## What happened

In **October 539 BCE**, the army of **[[cyrus-the-great]]** — Persian-Zoroastrian king of the Achaemenid Empire — **conquered Babylon**, ending the Neo-Babylonian Empire of Nabonidus (whose son Belshazzar was killed in the conquest). Cyrus entered Babylon to widespread popular acclaim — having cultivated a reputation for religious tolerance and respect for native cult-traditions. The **Cyrus Cylinder** (a clay-cylinder inscription, found at Babylon in 1879, now in the British Museum) records Cyrus's restoration of native cult-images and his proclamation of religious toleration. **Among the populations Cyrus released were the Jewish exiles** deported by Nebuchadnezzar II in 587 BCE; the **biblical "Edict of Cyrus"** (preserved in Ezra 1:2-4, 6:3-5, 2 Chronicles 36:23) authorizes the return of the Jewish exiles to Yehud and the rebuilding of the Jerusalem Temple. **This event inaugurates the Persian period (-539 to -332) of Jewish religious-political history — the period during which the foundational theological-textual transformations of Second-Temple Judaism occur**, almost certainly under substantial Zoroastrian religious-cultural influence (Boyce 1982, Hultgård 1998, Berquist 1995).

## Religious consequences

### THE MASSIVE-WIN: Cyrus as the textual moment of Zoroastrian-Jewish theological contact

The Persian period (-539 to -332) is the period during which **the distinctive theological vocabulary of Second-Temple Judaism** emerges:

1. **Cosmic dualism** (good-and-evil as cosmic principles, as opposed to the more monistic First-Temple Israelite theology where YHWH is the sole-cause of weal-and-woe — cf. Isaiah 45:7 "*I form light and create darkness, I make weal and create woe*" — itself a polemical-rejoinder against emerging dualism).

2. **Angelology** (the named hierarchy of angels — Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, etc. — emerges in the Persian and early Hellenistic period in texts like Daniel and Tobit; precedented by pre-exilic Israelite *mal'akh* but transformed into a structured hierarchy resembling Zoroastrian yazata-theology).

3. **Demonology** (a hierarchy of named demons — Asmodeus, Belial, Sammael, etc. — emerges in the same Persian-Hellenistic period, paralleling Zoroastrian daeva-theology. See [[aeshma]] for the Aeshma → Asmodeus etymological chain.)

4. **Resurrection of the dead** (Daniel 12:1-3 — the only Hebrew Bible passage clearly affirming bodily resurrection of individual dead persons — appears in the late Persian / early Hellenistic period; not present in earlier strata of the Hebrew Bible).

5. **Eschatological future-Messiah figure** (the Messianic theology of late-Second-Temple Judaism develops in the Persian-Hellenistic period, with structural parallels to Zoroastrian [[saoshyant]]).

6. **Final judgment and cosmic renovation** (the Daniel + 1 Enoch + Qumran apocalyptic eschatology has structural parallels with Zoroastrian [[frashokereti-cosmic-renovation]]).

**Boyce 1991 vol. 3 + Hultgård 1998 treat these developments as substantially Persian-influenced.** The conservative-evangelical counter-position (Yamauchi 1990) resists the influence-claim; most current scholarship accepts substantial Persian influence.

### The Cyrus Cylinder
The **Cyrus Cylinder** (British Museum BM 90920, ~-539) is a clay-cylinder inscription in Akkadian-cuneiform proclaiming Cyrus's restoration of native cults across the Babylonian empire. The cylinder is **the principal primary documentary evidence** for Cyrus's religious-tolerance policy. It has been famously (and somewhat anachronistically) called "the first declaration of human rights" — the inscription explicitly concerns the restoration of *cult-images and religious practice*, not modern human-rights concepts, but the religious-tolerance dimension is real.

### The Edict of Cyrus and the return
The biblical "Edict of Cyrus" (Ezra 1:2-4, 6:3-5) authorizes the Jewish return to Yehud and the rebuilding of the Jerusalem Temple. The authenticity of the biblical text-form is debated; the **historical reality of a Persian-imperial authorization for the Jewish return is uncontested**. The first wave of returnees comes under Sheshbazzar (-538); the second wave under Zerubbabel (-520); the third wave under Ezra and Nehemiah (mid-5th c.). The Second Temple is dedicated in -515.

### The Isaiah 44:28-45:7 "Cyrus as Messiah" passage
**The most-extraordinary cross-tradition theological passage in the entire Hebrew Bible**: Second Isaiah (~-540, just before or just after the conquest) has YHWH explicitly call Cyrus **"my anointed" (*māšīḥî*, the Hebrew technical term that gives English "Messiah")** — Isaiah 45:1: *"Thus says YHWH to his anointed, to Cyrus..."*. This is **the only place in the entire Hebrew Bible where a non-Israelite figure is called *māšīaḥ***. The passage represents a moment of **theological-cultural-political crisis** in Jewish religious imagination — the recognition that the Persian-Zoroastrian king is the divinely-appointed agent of YHWH's purposes. **The structural-and-historical roots of cross-tradition Messianic theology run through this passage**.

## Documents in play
- **Cyrus Cylinder** (~-539, BM 90920) — the principal primary-documentary evidence of Cyrus's religious-tolerance policy.
- [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]] — Isaiah 40-66, including Isaiah 44:28-45:7 ("Cyrus my anointed"); composed in the exilic-and-immediately-post-exilic period.
- [[phase-2-018-hebrew-bible-p-source]] — the Priestly source of the Pentateuch, conventionally placed in the Persian period; reflects post-exilic priestly-theological perspective.
- **Ezra 1, 6** + **2 Chronicles 36** — the biblical narrative-frames of the Edict and Return.
- [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — composed in the Persian-Hellenistic period; reflects intensive engagement with Persian religious-political-historical material (Daniel 5 has Belshazzar; Daniel 6 has Darius; Daniel's apocalyptic-eschatology is structurally Persian-influenced).

## Disputes
- **Authenticity of the biblical Edict.** Whether Ezra 1:2-4 and 6:3-5 preserve the actual text of Cyrus's edict (in Hebrew translation), or whether they are post-exilic theological-narrative constructions placing the historical-policy-reality into a religiously-shaped form, is debated. Most scholars accept the **historical reality** of a Persian-imperial authorization for the Jewish return while remaining cautious about the textual-authenticity of the specific biblical wording.
- **Zoroastrian content of Cyrus's religion.** Whether Cyrus was a **strict Zoroastrian** or a **broadly-Iranian-religious king** with eclectic religious policy is debated. The Cyrus Cylinder explicitly mentions Marduk (the Babylonian high-god) as legitimating his rule — which some scholars treat as political-religious-strategic accommodation, others as evidence of Cyrus's non-strict-Zoroastrian religious profile. Boyce 1982 argues Cyrus was substantially Zoroastrian; revisionists argue more eclectic.
- **Scope of Zoroastrian influence on Second-Temple Judaism.** See [[saoshyant]], [[frashokereti-cosmic-renovation]] for the larger debate.
