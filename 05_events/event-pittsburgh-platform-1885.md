# Pittsburgh Platform (1885)

## What happened
A conference of fifteen American Reform rabbis, convened in November 1885 at the Concordia Club in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, under the leadership of Isaac Mayer Wise (president of Hebrew Union College in Cincinnati). Kaufmann Kohler drafted the principal eight-plank declaration. The Platform articulated the "Classical Reform" position of late-19th-century American Reform Judaism: (1) Judaism as a religion (not a nation), (2) Bible as God-conscious record (rather than literal revelation), (3) Mosaic legislation as historically conditioned (especially the ritual-ceremonial provisions abrogated), (4) dietary, priestly-purity, and dress laws no longer binding, (5) modernity and science welcomed, (6) Reform Jews not awaiting return to Palestine or restoration of sacrificial cult, (7) immortality of the soul affirmed (without bodily resurrection), (8) social justice as central religious obligation.

## Religious consequences
The Pittsburgh Platform institutionalized a far-reaching theological-ritual position that distinguished American Reform Judaism from European Orthodox Judaism and from Positive-Historical / Conservative Judaism (Solomon Schechter, Jewish Theological Seminary). The non-Zionist plank #6 was reversed in 1937 (Columbus Platform); the universalist-non-particularist tone was significantly moderated in the 1999 *Statement of Principles for Reform Judaism* (Pittsburgh II), which restored ritual practices and Hebrew language to a more central place. The Pittsburgh Platform is the canonical statement of "Classical Reform" Judaism (~1885–1937 in particular).

## Documents in play
The Pittsburgh Platform itself; the subsequent Columbus Platform (1937), San Francisco Platform (1976), and Pittsburgh II (1999).
