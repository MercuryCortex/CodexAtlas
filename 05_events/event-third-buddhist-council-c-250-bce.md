# Third Buddhist Council at Pāṭaliputra (~-250 BCE)

## What happened
Under the patronage of [[asoka-maurya|Aśoka the Great]], a Buddhist council was convened at Pāṭaliputra (modern Patna), the Mauryan capital, in approximately the 17th year of his reign (~-250). The council, lasting ~9 months under the presidency of the elder Moggaliputta-Tissa, addressed (according to the Pali / Sri Lankan tradition that records it) doctrinal heresies that had crept into the monastic community during the period of Aśokan patronage — when monastery-supports had become so generous that non-Buddhist ascetics were entering the order to enjoy them. The council expelled non-orthodox monks, established the Vibhajjavāda position as orthodoxy (the lineage that becomes Theravāda), produced the *Kathāvatthu* ("Points of Controversy") refuting 219 heretical positions, and — most consequentially for the global spread of Buddhism — dispatched missionaries to nine regions, including Mahinda's mission to Sri Lanka and missions to the Hellenistic kings recorded epigraphically in [[phase-3-030-asokan-edicts|Aśokan Major Rock Edict XIII]].

## Religious consequences
- **Canonical consolidation**: the Pali Tipiṭaka in essentially its final form is associated with this council in Theravāda tradition. The Kathāvatthu — refuting Sarvāstivāda, Mahāsāṅghika, Pudgalavāda, and other early-school positions — is added to the Abhidhamma-piṭaka as its seventh and last book.
- **Sectarian definition**: the Vibhajjavāda ("Doctrine of Analysis") position is established as Aśokan orthodoxy. The Vibhajjavāda lineage becomes the ancestor of the Theravāda school.
- **[[event-asokan-dhamma-missions-c-250-bce|The dhamma-missions]]**: the most consequential outcome. Nine missions dispatched per the Pali Dipavaṃsa and Mahāvaṃsa:
  1. Mahinda → **Sri Lanka** (Aśoka's son or junior contemporary; founds Anurādhapura Mahāvihāra; the founding of Sinhalese Theravāda)
  2. Majjhantika → Kashmir-Gandhāra
  3. Mahādeva → Mahishamandala (modern Mysore/Karnataka region)
  4. Rakkhita → Vanavāsa (coastal Karnataka)
  5. Yonaka Dhammarakkhita → Aparantaka (western India)
  6. Mahā-Dhammarakkhita → Mahārāṣṭra
  7. Mahā-Rakkhita → Yonaka-loka (the Greek lands — i.e., the Hellenistic kingdoms named in Rock Edict XIII; the Mahāvaṃsa account corroborates the epigraphic Aśokan claim)
  8. Majjhima → Himavanta (Himalayan region)
  9. Soṇa and Uttara → Suvarṇabhūmi ("Land of Gold" — modern Burma / Thailand / Lower Burma)
- **Schism-prevention edict**: Aśoka's contemporary Schism Edict (Sānchi, Allahabad-Kosam, Sārnāth, see [[phase-3-030-asokan-edicts]]) prohibits monastic schism — a state intervention into Saṅgha discipline that is the foundational moment of *cakravartin*-king patronage of Buddhism.

## Documents in play
- ***Kathāvatthu*** — produced at this council per Theravāda tradition; the foundational Theravāda systematic refutation of competing school-positions.
- **[[phase-3-030-asokan-edicts]]** — particularly the Schism Edict and Major Rock Edict XIII, which provide contemporary epigraphic evidence of Aśoka's saṅgha-disciplinary intervention and his dhamma-missions to the Hellenistic kings.
- **Pali Dīpavaṃsa and Mahāvaṃsa** (4th-6th c. CE Sri Lankan chronicles) — the principal narrative sources for the council; partly hagiographic but with substantial historical core.

## Disputes
- **Historicity**: the Third Council is attested only in the Theravāda / Sri Lankan tradition. The Sarvāstivāda tradition does not recognize it as a "council" of canonical significance (and counts only two universal councils: First Rājagṛha, Second Vaiśālī). Modern scholarship (Gombrich 1996; Frauwallner 1956) accepts the broad outline — Aśokan period saṅgha-reform plus dispatch of missionaries — while remaining skeptical of the more elaborate Theravāda canonical claims (e.g., that the *full* Pali Tipiṭaka was redacted at this council). The dhamma-missions themselves are epigraphically attested by Rock Edict XIII, which is the strongest single piece of evidence.
- **Date**: Aśoka's 17th regnal year ~-250 is the standard reconstruction; precise dating depends on Aśoka's accession year (-268 per Hultzsch; refined by Falk 1998).
- **Mahinda's relationship to Aśoka**: Pali tradition says son; some Sanskrit sources say junior contemporary or brother; the Sri Lankan claim is foundational charter for Sinhalese Theravāda regardless of its precise historicity.
