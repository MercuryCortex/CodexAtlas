# Expulsion of the Hyksos (c. 1550 BCE)

## What happened

The Theban 17th Dynasty under Seqenenre Tao II initiated military conflict with the Hyksos 15th Dynasty. The campaign was continued by Kamose (last king of the 17th Dynasty), whose two stelae at Karnak — primary sources of exceptional value — record the campaign rhetoric and the siege of Avaris itself. Kamose's second stele refers to an intercepted letter between the Hyksos king Apepi and the Nubian king of Kush, indicating a planned diplomatic encirclement of Thebes. Ahmose I (r. c. 1550–1525 BCE), founder of the 18th Dynasty, completed the expulsion: his troops sacked Avaris and pursued the Hyksos into Canaan, besieging the fortress city of Sharuhen in the southern Negev for three years.

The autobiography of Ahmose son of Ebana — a military officer whose account is inscribed on the walls of his rock-cut tomb at El-Kab — is the only surviving first-person eyewitness account. He describes fighting at Avaris both on land and from ships on the Delta waterways, receiving "gold of valor" decorations for taking enemy captives, and the subsequent pursuit to Sharuhen.

After the expulsion, a garrison was established at Avaris absorbed into the priesthood of Set — the cult the Hyksos had developed and which now passed to their Egyptian conquerors. The Set-Baal syncretism survived the expulsion; Ramesses I's family (19th Dynasty) came from the Avaris district with deep Set-cult ties.

## Religious consequences

The 18th Dynasty's suppression of Hyksos memory was not complete. The Set-Baal syncretism ([[theme-baal-set-syncretism]]) the Hyksos had established at Avaris survived into the Ramesside period. The **Year 400 Stela of Ramesses II** (discovered at Tanis/San el-Hagar, 1863; Cairo Museum) commemorates a 400-year anniversary of a Set-cult event — a deliberate Hyksos-period memory-making by the Ramesside state. Seti I and Setnakht bear theophoric names from Set.

## Documents in play

- Kamose Stelae (Luxor Museum) — primary sources contemporary with the reconquest.
- Autobiography of Ahmose son of Ebana (El-Kab; trans. Lichtheim 1976) — first-person military account.
- Manetho *Aegyptiaca* in Josephus *Contra Apionem* I.82–106 — Ptolemaic-era narrative reconstruction.

## Disputes

The central dispute is whether the Hyksos expulsion is the historical kernel of the Exodus narrative: see [[theme-exodus-hyksos-thesis]]. The Hyksos were expelled rulers, not slaves, and exited in organized military groups rather than under pursuit — the Exodus narrative's sociology (slaves fleeing, pharaoh pursuing) is the inverse. The consensus is that the Hyksos expulsion is at most a contributing strand to the Exodus tradition, not its direct prototype.
