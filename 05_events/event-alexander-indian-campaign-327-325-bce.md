# Alexander's Indian Campaign (327-325 BCE)

[[alexander-the-great|Alexander III]]'s eastward push beyond the Hindu Kush into the Indus basin (327-325 BCE) — the most ethnographically and philosophically consequential phase of the Asian campaign. Key engagements: Battle of the Hydaspes (326 BCE, against King Porus of the Paurava kingdom — the elephant-cavalry battle whose narrative dominates Arrian's account); the mutiny at the Hyphasis (Beas) when the Macedonian troops refused to advance further east; the punishing return through Gedrosia (~325). The campaign produced lasting cultural-political consequences far disproportionate to its temporal duration: the Hellenistic states in Bactria and the Indus basin (Greco-Bactrian Kingdom; Indo-Greek Kingdom of Menander) that emerged in the Diadochi period; the Gandharan substrate that produced Greco-Buddhism; and the Indian-philosophical encounter of [[pyrrho-of-elis|Pyrrho]] (who travelled with Alexander) which produced Pyrrhonism on his return to Greece.
