# First Fitna (656-661 CE)

## What happened
**The first Islamic civil war (*al-fitna al-kubrā*)** — a five-year sequence of escalating political-religious-military conflict that **transforms the early Muslim *umma* from a unified consensus-community into a permanently divided sectarian landscape** (Sunni / Shīʿī / Khārijite / Ibādī). The principal sequence:

**1. Assassination of [[uthman|ʿUthmān]] (June 656).** ʿUthmān, the third Rashidun caliph, was killed in his Medinan house by rebels (a coalition of Egyptian, Iraqi, and Hijazi opponents of his administrative policies — especially the nepotistic appointment of Umayyad relatives to key gubernatorial posts, the centralization of the Quranic codex, and the alienation of provincial elites). ʿUthmān's body was left unburied for three days. The assassination marks the **end of the consensus-Rashidun period**.

**2. ʿAlī's caliphate begins (June 656).** [[ali-ibn-abi-talib|ʿAlī]] was acclaimed caliph in Medina by the Anṣār + the rebel coalition. The acclamation was contested by **(a) the [[aisha-bint-abi-bakr|ʿĀʾisha]] + Ṭalḥa + al-Zubayr faction** (charging that ʿAlī had not pursued ʿUthmān's killers vigorously enough), and **(b) the [[muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Muʿāwiya]] faction in Syria** (Muʿāwiya, ʿUthmān's kinsman as fellow-Umayyad, refused to give *bayʿa* to ʿAlī until ʿUthmān's killers were brought to justice).

**3. Battle of the Camel (December 656).** ʿĀʾisha + Ṭalḥa + al-Zubayr marched on Basra; ʿAlī pursued them; the resulting battle near Basra was fought around ʿĀʾisha's camel-litter at the center of the rebel army (hence the name). ʿAlī's army won; Ṭalḥa and al-Zubayr were killed; ʿĀʾisha's camel-litter was disabled by ʿAlī's archers killing the bodyguard's camels; ʿĀʾisha was returned to Medina with honor. The rebel faction was defeated; the Muʿāwiya threat in Syria remained.

**4. Battle of Ṣiffīn (July 657).** ʿAlī's army (mostly Iraqi-Kūfan) and Muʿāwiya's army (Syrian-Arab) met at Ṣiffīn on the Euphrates. After days of inconclusive fighting and substantial casualties (the engagement was the largest intra-Muslim battle to that date), Muʿāwiya's strategist **ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ** ordered the Syrian troops to **raise Qurans on the tips of their lances**, calling for the dispute to be resolved by Quranic arbitration (*taḥkīm*). ʿAlī's army was demoralized by the religious symbolism; ʿAlī reluctantly accepted arbitration.

**5. The *taḥkīm* and the Khārijite secession (657-658).** Two arbitrators — Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī (for ʿAlī) and ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ (for Muʿāwiya) — were appointed to deliberate. The arbitration deadlocked (the published outcome is disputed across the sources but appears to have been a Muʿāwiya political win regardless of the substantive result). **A faction of ʿAlī's army rejected the arbitration outright** with the slogan *"lā ḥukma illā li-llāh"* ("no judgment except God's") — arguing that ʿAlī had sinned by accepting human arbitration in a matter that the Quran had already settled. This faction **seceded** from ʿAlī's army — the *Khawārij* ("Goers-Out") — and became the **first sectarian schism in Islam**. ʿAlī fought and largely destroyed the Khārijites at the Battle of Nahrawān (658), but the surviving Khārijite remnants would assassinate him three years later. The Khārijite movement persists into the modern period as the **[[tradition-islam|Ibādī]] branch of Islam** (especially Oman, Zanzibar, North Africa).

**6. Muʿāwiya proclaimed caliph at Jerusalem (660).** Muʿāwiya's faction proclaimed him caliph in Jerusalem in 660 — the first formal Umayyad caliphal proclamation, before ʿAlī's death.

**7. Assassination of [[ali-ibn-abi-talib|ʿAlī]] (January 661).** ʿAlī was killed by the Khārijite ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn Muljam at the Great Mosque of Kūfa during morning prayer (19 Ramaḍān 40 AH / January 28, 661 CE). His son [[hasan-ibn-ali|al-Ḥasan]] was acclaimed caliph in Kūfa but, after a short caliphate (~6 months) facing Muʿāwiya's military advance, **abdicated in favor of Muʿāwiya** (Treaty of al-Ḥasan-Muʿāwiya, summer 661 — with the terms that Muʿāwiya would not designate a hereditary successor; Muʿāwiya later broke this term by designating Yazīd I c. 676, leading directly to [[event-karbala-680|Karbala 680]]).

**8. End of the Rashidun period and inauguration of the Umayyad caliphate (661).** With al-Ḥasan's abdication, Muʿāwiya became sole caliph, founding the Umayyad dynasty (661-750) from Damascus.

## Religious consequences
1. **Permanent Sunni-Shīʿī divide.** The First Fitna **establishes the foundational Sunni-Shīʿī fault-lines** that have persisted for 1,400 years. Sunni reading: Abū Bakr → ʿUmar → ʿUthmān → ʿAlī were all legitimate Rashidun caliphs; Muʿāwiya was a legitimate caliph after ʿAlī; the conflict was a regrettable but politically-understandable dispute among Companions. Shīʿī reading: ʿAlī was the divinely-designated successor from Ghadīr Khumm onward; the three caliphs who preceded him sidelined his legitimate succession; Muʿāwiya was a usurper; the *Ahl al-Bayt* (ʿAlī, Fāṭima, al-Ḥasan, al-Ḥusayn, the subsequent Imams) carry the legitimate imamic succession.
2. **Khārijite / Ibādī secession.** The First Fitna produces the **first sectarian schism in Islam** (the Khārijites) and establishes the **Ibādī branch** as a continuing minority tradition. The Ibādī branch is the principal religious tradition of Oman (where it remains the state religion) and significant populations in Zanzibar, Algerian Mzāb, and Libyan Jabal Nafūsa.
3. **Umayyad imperial transformation.** The First Fitna ends the Medinan-consultative Rashidun pattern and inaugurates the **Damascus-based Arab-imperial Umayyad caliphate** (661-750). The political-religious paradigm shifts decisively from the Hijazi-tribal-consultative model to the Mediterranean-imperial monarchical model.
4. **Karbala 680 follow-on.** Muʿāwiya's c. 676 designation of his son [[husayn-ibn-ali|Yazīd I]] as successor (breaking the al-Ḥasan-Muʿāwiya treaty) leads directly to the foundational Shīʿī catastrophe at [[event-karbala-680|Karbala (October 680)]] — the killing of al-Ḥusayn ibn ʿAlī by Yazīd's army at Karbala, Iraq. Karbala is the **foundational martyrological event of Shīʿī religious-political identity**.

## Documents in play
- **[[phase-4-034-quran]]** — Quran-on-lances at Ṣiffīn; Quran 49:9-10 ("if two parties of believers fight each other, make peace between them") is the canonical Quranic-political-text for civil-war reconciliation.
- **[[phase-5-007-sahih-al-bukhari]]** + **[[phase-5-042-sahih-muslim]]** — extensive hadith material on the First Fitna; Sunni and Shīʿī isnād-tradition diverges on key hadith.
- **[[phase-5-043-ibn-ishaq-sirat-rasul-allah]]** — concludes with Muhammad's death; the First Fitna is the immediate sequel, narrated by Ṭabarī's *Tārīkh al-Rusul wa-l-Mulūk* (~915) as the canonical Sunni historical narrative.

## Disputes
- **ʿAlī's responsibility for ʿUthmān's death.** Did ʿAlī culpably fail to prevent the assassination? Sunni-Umayyad-rooted tradition: yes, indirectly. Mainstream Sunni: no, ʿAlī was not involved. Shīʿī: no, ʿAlī actively tried to protect ʿUthmān.
- **Legitimacy of the *taḥkīm* arbitration.** Sunni: legitimate political negotiation. Khārijite (then and now): impermissible substitution of human judgment for divine. Shīʿī: ʿAlī's acceptance was reluctant and forced by Muʿāwiya's tactics.
- **Outcome of the arbitration.** Multiple source-traditions; the most common Sunni narrative has Abū Mūsā agreeing to depose both ʿAlī and Muʿāwiya, after which ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ declared only ʿAlī deposed — a political win for Muʿāwiya. Other sources have different outcomes.
- **Al-Ḥasan's abdication (661).** Sunni: legitimate political pragmatism. Shīʿī: under duress.
