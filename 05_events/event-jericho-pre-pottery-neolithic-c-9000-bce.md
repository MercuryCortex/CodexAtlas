# Jericho (Tell es-Sultan) PPNA Tower and PPNB Plastered Skulls (c. 9,500–6,500 BCE)

## What happened
Tell es-Sultan, a low mound on the western edge of the Jordan Valley fed by the perennial 'Ain es-Sultan spring, is one of the oldest continuously occupied locations in the world, with a stratigraphic sequence from the Natufian (~12,000 BP) through PPNA, PPNB, Pottery Neolithic, Chalcolithic, Early–Middle–Late Bronze Age, Iron Age, and historic periods. The site was systematically excavated by Kathleen Kenyon (1952–1958) with stratigraphic methods that became standard for Near Eastern archaeology; her work substantially revised the chronological framework for the Levantine Neolithic and decoupled the archaeological Jericho from the biblical Joshua narrative (the Late Bronze Age city Joshua's army would have destroyed appears to have been already abandoned or in serious decline by the conventional date of Joshua's campaign).

**Two religious-archaeologically significant features of the PPNA / PPNB sequence:**

1. **The PPNA Tower and Wall** (~9,500–8,000 BCE). A circular stone tower ~8.5 m tall and ~9 m in diameter, with an internal stairwell of 22 steps, abutting an enclosure wall ~3.6 m high and ~1.8 m thick. Kenyon interpreted this as a fortification system — "the world's first city wall and tower." Bar-Yosef 1986 (*Current Anthropology*) reinterpreted the wall as a flood-defense / silt-trap structure protecting the settlement from seasonal wadi runoff, with the tower having an unspecified communal-ritual function. Barkai & Liran 2008 argued for a solstitial-alignment ritual-astronomy interpretation of the tower. Current consensus accepts the tower as a major communal construction (requiring ~11,000 person-days of labor) whose specific function (defensive, hydrological, ritual, astronomical, or several at once) is not definitively resolved.

2. **The PPNB Plastered Skulls** (~7,500–6,500 BCE). A regionally widespread practice in which selected human skulls were defleshed, plastered with lime-and-mud, modeled to restore facial features (sometimes with inset cowrie or shell eyes), painted, and curated — often kept above ground in domestic spaces or buried in clusters separate from the rest of the body. Jericho is the type-site (Kenyon recovered nine plastered skulls including the famous "Jericho A" example in 1953), but the tradition spans 'Ain Ghazal (Jordan, the largest single cache: ~80 plastered skulls and figurines), Beisamoun, Tell Aswad, Tell Ramad, Yiftahel, and Kfar HaHoresh. Bonogofsky 2003 *BASOR* is the comprehensive review.

The Natufian → PPNA → PPNB mortuary sequence at Jericho and adjacent southern Levantine sites is one of the most-securely-attested deep-prehistory religious-practice continuities (Kuijt 2008 *Current Anthropology*): intramural burial → skull removal a generation or so post-burial → curation and plastered-reconstruction of selected skulls → deposition.

## Religious consequences
- **Plastered skulls** are the most direct material evidence for any kind of *ancestor cult* (in a loose, non-technical sense) in the deep-Neolithic Southwest Asian record. The selection of specific individuals for plastering (the demographic profile is biased toward mature adults of both sexes, with subadults underrepresented), the labor-intensive modeling, and the curation of these objects in domestic spaces strongly imply differentiated memorial treatment of recognized social-ritual personae.
- The **Natufian → PPNB continuity** is significant for Religious-Studies-as-History: it is one of the few cases where a documented archaeological sequence shows a *practice trajectory* (treatment of the dead) elaborating in a single region over ~5,000 years toward what looks, materially, like *organized ancestor commemoration*. This pattern then articulates with later Levantine bone-collection ossuary practice and (with substantial caution) with the much later concept of *avot* / patriarchs in Israelite religion (no documented continuous transmission, but typological recurrence in the same landscape).
- The Jericho PPNA tower, whatever its specific function, is the earliest known monumental construction at this scale outside Göbekli Tepe. Together they mark the emergence of *large-scale communal construction projects* — a precondition for and material trace of organized social-ritual labor.
- **Decoupling from the Joshua narrative.** Kenyon's chronological work definitively established that the archaeological Jericho destruction-horizons do not align with a Late Bronze Age conquest narrative as conventionally dated for Joshua. The Atlas treats the biblical Jericho narrative and the archaeological Jericho as *separate phenomena* requiring separate evaluation — the early Israelite emergence in the central highlands is a topic for later phases.

## Documents in play
- None at Jericho itself (~5,000+ years before writing reaches the southern Levant). The Hebrew Bible Joshua-Jericho narrative is a far later (Iron Age / post-exilic) literary composition; see [[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]] and related Pentateuch-source nodes for that material.

## Disputes
- **The "fortification" framing of the PPNA tower-and-wall.** Kenyon 1957's "world's first city wall" is rhetorically powerful but archaeologically contestable. Bar-Yosef 1986 argues for flood-defense; Naveh 2003 and others have raised storage / silo possibilities. The tower could have served multiple functions simultaneously. The "world's first fortifications" claim should be retained with the caveat that "fortification" is one reading among several.
- **The "ancestor cult" framing of the plastered skulls.** Bonogofsky 2003 critiques the over-confident application of "ancestor cult" terminology — the practice is *demonstrably* differentiated mortuary treatment, *demonstrably* curated, and *demonstrably* involved facial-feature modeling; whether the social-symbolic content is best described as *ancestor* veneration, *trophy* display, *enemy* memorialization, *founder-figure* commemoration, or some combination cannot be determined from the material evidence alone. Cross-cultural ethnographic analogies are heterogeneous.
- **The Barkai-Liran astronomical interpretation of the tower** (2008) is interesting but at the speculative end. The proposed solstitial alignment is suggestive; whether this was the *intent* of the construction or a fortuitous geometric outcome is not securely demonstrable.
- **Joshua narrative correlation.** Garstang 1930s argued for archaeological correlation with a Joshua-conquest dating; Kenyon's stratigraphic work overturned this. A minority of "biblical archaeology" tradition (e.g., Bryant Wood) has continued to argue for a Late Bronze Age date for City IV's destruction matching Joshua; mainstream Levantine archaeology (Finkelstein, Mazar, Killebrew) rejects this. The Atlas adopts the mainstream stratigraphic consensus.

## Connections
- [[event-natufian-burial-traditions-c-12500-9500-bce]] — **direct continuity**, well-established. The Natufian intramural-burial tradition flows into the PPNA intramural-burial tradition at Jericho and adjacent sites, then elaborates into the PPNB skull-removal and plastered-skull tradition. Kuijt 2008 is the canonical synthesis. **This is the strongest documented case of deep-prehistory religious-practice continuity in this set of events.**
- [[event-gobekli-tepe-c-9500-bce]] — *shared-milieu*; both are PPNA-PPNB monumental-construction sites of the same broad horizon.
- [[event-catalhoyuk-c-7400-6200-bce]] — *parallel-motif*; the Çatalhöyük intramural burial and bucranial-display traditions are typologically continuous with the Jericho plastered-skull practice in broad cultural-region terms, with the caveat that Çatalhöyük's specific practices have their own particularities.
- [[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]] and the Joshua tradition — *anachronism warning*. The Iron Age / post-exilic Joshua narrative is a *literary* phenomenon ~7,000 years after the Tell es-Sultan PPNA tower; no documented transmission of memory of the PPNA-PPNB Jericho exists. The connection is *spatial coincidence and later mytho-historical reframing*, not religious-practice continuity.
