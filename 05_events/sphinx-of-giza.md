# Great Sphinx of Giza

## Physical description

The **Great Sphinx** is a monolithic limestone figure — the largest surviving ancient monolithic statue — carved directly from the Giza bedrock. It depicts a recumbent lion with a human head, facing due east toward the sunrise.

**Dimensions:**
- Length: ~73 m
- Height: ~20 m
- Head width: ~4 m
- Carved from a single knoll of limestone left by quarrying operations

The Sphinx has been partially buried by sand for most of its recorded history. It was cleared by **Thutmose IV** (~1401 BCE) after a dream in which the Sphinx promised him the kingship if he cleared its body (the *Dream Stele* stands between its forepaws and records this). It was cleared again by Émile Baraize (1925–1936), then by Zahi Hawass and the Egyptian Antiquities Organization in the 1980s–1990s.

## Conventional attribution: Khafre

The Sphinx is conventionally attributed to **[[khafre|Khafre]]** (~2558–2532 BCE) on several grounds:
- The causeway of Khafre's pyramid aligns with the Sphinx's east-facing axis
- The **Valley Temple** immediately south of the Sphinx shows identical limestone construction and was clearly part of the same building project
- A diorite statue of Khafre was found in the Valley Temple showing the same facial features scholars associate with the Sphinx's damaged face
- The Sphinx Stele of Thutmose IV (Dream Stele) contains a partially legible cartouche that may read "Khafre" — though this reading is disputed

**The alternative: Khufu's reign?** Some Egyptologists argue for Khufu's reign based on proportional analysis of the causeway geometry. This remains a minority academic position but is distinct from the Schoch hypothesis below.

## The water erosion hypothesis (Schoch 1992)

**Robert Schoch**, a Boston University geologist, studied the Sphinx enclosure walls in 1992 and argued that the pattern of weathering on the limestone is inconsistent with wind and sand erosion — it shows deep vertical fissuring characteristic of **precipitation and water runoff**. The last time Giza received sufficient rainfall to produce this weathering, by climatological consensus, was approximately **7000–5000 BCE** — roughly 2,000–4,000 years before Khafre.

**What this would imply:** A Sphinx carved during the Egyptian pre-dynastic or early Neolithic period, by a civilization that left no other monumental trace — a proposition that mainstream Egyptology finds implausible and archaeologically unsupported.

**The Egyptological response:**
- The head (which is clearly in the Khafre style) has been re-carved; the original head was larger and older
- Alternatively, the Sphinx enclosure's weathering could be explained by groundwater seepage, burial-induced moisture, or differential rock strata
- The absence of any pre-dynastic monumental tradition makes the Schoch hypothesis very difficult to accept even if the geology is correct

**John Anthony West** popularized the Schoch hypothesis in *Serpent in the Sky* (1987) and a 1993 NBC documentary. He framed it within the Schwaller de Lubicz tradition — that Egypt preserved wisdom from a much older civilization.

## The Hall of Records hypothesis

**Edgar Cayce** (1877–1945), an American psychic, gave hundreds of "readings" about Atlantis in which he consistently described a **Hall of Records** — a library of Atlantean history — buried beneath the right paw of the Sphinx. Cayce predicted it would be discovered around 1998.

The Sphinx contains several known cavities:
- A large vertical shaft behind the head (blocked; Hawass found no chambers)
- A cavity between the forepaws (natural limestone void)
- A tunnel beneath the body (known; unexplored in full)

**Schor Foundation investigations (1990s):** With Zahi Hawass's permission, American researchers conducted seismic surveys. They detected anomalies beneath the Sphinx — possible cavities — but Hawass revoked permissions before definitive investigation and has maintained that no Hall of Records exists.

**Archaeological status:** No Hall of Records has been found. The cavities detected by remote sensing remain unexplored. Egyptological consensus is that the anomalies are natural features of the karstic limestone.

**[[theme-hall-of-records-hypothesis]]** documents this claim in full, including its Theosophical origins (Blavatsky → Cayce) and the archaeology of the searches.

## Stellar alignment: the Leo hypothesis

In the **[[theme-orion-correlation-theory|Orion Correlation Theory]]** framework (Bauval & Hancock 1996), the Sphinx faces due east — toward the constellation Leo as it appeared on the eastern horizon at the vernal equinox in approximately **10,500 BCE** (the "Age of Leo"). The three Giza pyramids simultaneously mirrored Orion's Belt at its lowest point in the precessional cycle. This would place the Sphinx's "construction date" (or at least its "design date") at 10,500 BCE.

**Mainstream position:** The east-facing alignment is simply toward sunrise, which had solar theological meaning in the 4th Dynasty. The precession argument depends on Bauval's contested interpretation of the pyramid-Orion alignment.

## The Sphinx as Hermetic symbol

In **[[tradition-hermeticism]]**, the Sphinx is the guardian of gnosis — the animal body (nature) surmounted by the human head (intellect/spirit) facing east toward the sun. This symbolism enters Hermeticism via the Renaissance *prisca theologia* tradition and becomes codified in 19th-century occultism: Lévi's *Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie* (1855) uses the Sphinx as the emblem of the four magical elements. The Tarot Wheel of Fortune card in the Rider-Waite deck (1909) shows the Sphinx atop the wheel — transmitting this symbolism into mass culture.

The historical Egyptian Sphinx was named *Hor-em-akhet* ("Horus on the Horizon") and had no connection to the Greek riddle-sphinx. The symbol-conflation is entirely post-classical.
