# Yavneh / Jamnia Rabbinic Consolidation (c. 70-100 CE)

## What happened
After the destruction of the Second Temple in 70 CE (see [[event-destruction-of-second-temple-70]]), Pharisaic-rabbinic leadership re-established a center for Jewish religious-academic life at **Yavneh (Jamnia)** on the Judean coast. Per rabbinic tradition, [[yohanan-ben-zakkai]] secured Roman permission from Vespasian to establish the academy. Over the next generation (~+70 to ~+130, ending with Bar Kokhba), Yavneh under successive leaders ([[yohanan-ben-zakkai]], then [[gamaliel-ii]]) re-centered Judaism around **Torah-study, synagogue worship, and rabbinic halakhic decision** in place of the now-impossible Temple cult.

The traditional historiography characterized this period as a "**Council of Jamnia**" that (a) closed the Hebrew Bible canon and (b) expelled the Christians via the *Birkat ha-Minim*. Both of these elements have been **substantially revised by modern scholarship** (Lewis 1964; Boyarin 2004; Becker-Reed 2003): there was no formal canon-closing council; the *Birkat ha-Minim* targeted *minim* (heretics generally), and the *notzrim* (Christians) clause is probably a later addition.

## Religious consequences
- **Foundation of rabbinic Judaism**: the institutional transition that made post-Temple Judaism survivable.
- **Curricular and liturgical reorganization**: the *Amidah* (Eighteen Benedictions) standardized in this period; oral Torah formalized into the precursor materials of the Mishnah.
- **Jewish-Christian separation**: a *gradual, geographically uneven, and centuries-long* process — not the sharp ecclesiastical break of older scholarship. The Yavneh period is one early stage in a long arc.
- **Birkat ha-Minim**: ~+90 traditional attribution; the *notzrim* clause is probably later (perhaps 2nd–3rd c.).

## Documents in play
- Precursor materials of *[[phase-4-031-mishnah]]* (compiled ~+200 by [[judah-ha-nasi]]).
- The *Amidah* / *Shemoneh Esrei* (Eighteen Benedictions).
- The *Birkat ha-Minim* (Twelfth Benediction).

## Disputes
- **Council of Jamnia as canonical-defining event**: rejected by mainstream scholarship since Lewis 1964.
- **Date and authorship of the *Birkat ha-Minim***: traditional under Gamaliel II ~+90; modern scholarship more cautious.
- **Christian-Jewish separation**: Boyarin 2004 and Becker-Reed 2003 emphasize gradualism; older "parting of the ways" framing is now considered too sharp.
- **Scope of Yavnean authority**: probably more localized than later rabbinic tradition presents.
