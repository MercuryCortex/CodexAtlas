# Neronian Persecution of Christians (64 CE)

## What happened
On the night of July 18-19, 64 CE, a fire broke out near the Circus Maximus in Rome and burned for nine days, devastating ten of the city's fourteen districts. **Tacitus** (*Annales* 15.44, ~+115/+120) reports that rumors blaming Emperor Nero himself for setting the fire (to clear ground for his planned Domus Aurea) circulated freely, and that Nero — to deflect popular suspicion — fastened blame on the Christians of Rome, "a class hated for their abominations." A large number of Christians were arrested and executed in spectacular fashion in Nero's gardens — burned alive as human torches, mauled by wild beasts wearing animal skins, crucified.

The persecution was **city-of-Rome-specific**, did not extend through the Empire, and likely lasted only a few weeks of intensive activity, though Christians remained legally vulnerable in Rome throughout the remainder of Nero's reign (64–68). Christian tradition traces the martyrdoms of [[peter-apostle]] and [[paul-of-tarsus]] to this period.

## Religious consequences
- **The First Persecution paradigm**: Nero becomes the archetype of the demonic-emperor in Christian memory.
- **Martyrdom theology**: 1 Clement 5–6 (~+95) and the wider Christian martyrological tradition trace foundational typologies to this event.
- **Beast-666 identification**: the Beast of Revelation 13 is most plausibly Nero (per mainstream critical reading), drawing on the numerical value of *Neron Caesar* in Hebrew transliteration. If Revelation is Neronian-dated (~+68/+69, a substantial minority view), this is direct response; if Domitianic-dated (~+95, the mainstream), it is retrospective.
- **The traditional founding of Roman Christian martyrdom**: Peter and Paul both traditionally martyred ~+64/+67.

## Documents in play
- **[[tacitus]] *Annales* 15.44** — the principal external source.
- **[[suetonius]] *Nero* 16.2** — independent corroboration.
- **1 Clement 5–6** — indirect Christian reference.
- **Revelation 13:18 (the *Beast 666*)** — possibly Neronian.

## Disputes
- **Scale**: Moss (2013) and other recent scholarship argue that the Christian tradition substantially exaggerated the scope and intentional pattern. Mainstream view: limited but real persecution in Rome, exaggerated by later tradition.
- **Date of Peter and Paul's deaths**: traditionally during the Neronian period (~+64/+67) but not securely datable.
- **Revelation dating**: Neronian (~+68) vs. Domitianic (~+95) — Domitianic is mainstream.
- **Tacitus's source**: archival, oral, or a Christian source? Mainstream: archival and oral Roman tradition.
