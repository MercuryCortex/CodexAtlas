# Second Council of Constantinople (553)

## What happened
Convened by the emperor Justinian I after a decade of intense theological maneuvering. Two distinct (though related) doctrinal projects:
1. **The Three Chapters** — condemnation of (a) the person and works of [[theodore-of-mopsuestia]], (b) the anti-Cyrillian writings of Theodoret of Cyrrhus, (c) the *Letter to Mari* of Ibas of Edessa. The political purpose: to conciliate the Miaphysite (anti-Chalcedonian) East by retroactively distancing Chalcedon from the Antiochene theologians it had not condemned. Pope Vigilius, present in Constantinople essentially under imperial duress, vacillated, fled to Chalcedon, was eventually forced into assent.
2. **Anti-Origenist condemnation** — 15 anathemas condemning Origen, Evagrius, Didymus the Blind, and a catalogue of (broadly Evagrian) Origenist propositions: pre-existence of souls, *apokatastasis* (universal restoration), the original henad of intellects, the resurrection body as spherical, etc.

The conciliar status of the 15 anti-Origenist anathemas is **disputed**: they appear in the conciliar collections, but Brian Daley 1991 argues persuasively (with earlier work by Diekamp) that they were issued by a **pre-conciliar synod under Justinian's authority in 543**, condemning Origenism, with the 553 council *receiving and ratifying* the earlier action but not formally legislating it. Ilaria Ramelli (2013) extends this argument: the 553 council's *strictly conciliar* doctrinal action concerns the Three Chapters; the Origenist anathemas are an emperor-driven synodal decision that the council acknowledged rather than itself defined.

## Religious consequences
- **The Three Chapters condemnation caused a major schism in the Latin West**: the bishops of northern Italy (Aquileia, Milan, Genoa) refused communion with Rome, claiming Vigilius had betrayed Chalcedon. The "Three Chapters Schism" lasted until 698 in Aquileia.
- **Theodore of Mopsuestia's Greek works were largely destroyed** after his posthumous condemnation; what survives is in Syriac. The greatest Antiochene exegete of the patristic age is recoverable only fragmentarily.
- **Origenism is officially anathematized.** The condemnation effectively ends the open transmission of Origenist-Evagrian Christian Platonism in the Greek East — though the spiritual-ascetic core survives under "safe" attributions (Evagrius as Nilus; sanitized scholia). The 15 anathemas become the medieval standard against Origenist *apokatastasis* and pre-existence of souls.
- **Justinian's caesaropapism is on full display.** The 553 council is the high-water mark of imperial direction of conciliar theology in the Christian Roman empire.

## Substrate connections
- The Origenist tradition condemned in 553 was the **principal channel by which Christian Platonism — including pre-existence of souls, the soul's pre-bodily fall, *apokatastasis* / universal restoration — was articulated**. The condemnation amounts to a partial closure of the Christian Platonist option that had run from Origen through the Cappadocians through Evagrius. Christian Neoplatonism survives — but in the apophatic Dionysian register, not in the Origenist-cosmogonic register.
- Behind the Origenist propositions condemned in 553 stand:
  - Plato's *Phaedrus* (pre-existence and fall of the soul).
  - Middle Platonism (the soul's ascent / descent through the cosmic levels).
  - Plotinus (the *nous* as the soul's true home).
  - The Hellenistic *apokatastasis* tradition (Stoic universal restoration, picked up via Origen for the universalist Christian *apokatastasis*).
- The 553 closure forces the survival of these substrate-Platonist motifs into Eastern monasticism (where they continue under attribution to safer figures) and into the Dionysian / Maximian / Eriugenian mystical tradition (which preserves the structure without the explicit propositions). Ramelli 2013: 740–822.

## Documents in play
- **Conciliar acta** — preserved in Latin in Atto's collection and the Acta Conciliorum Oecumenicorum (Schwartz IV).
- **Justinian's *Edict on the True Faith*** (~551) — the imperial theological intervention preceding the council.
- **Justinian's earlier *Letter on Origen*** (~543) — the basis for the 15 anti-Origenist anathemas.
- **Vigilius's *Constitutum*** (553) — his initial refusal to condemn the Three Chapters; later reversed.

## Disputes
- **Conciliar status of the 15 anti-Origenist anathemas.** Daley 1991 and Ramelli 2013 argue they are a 543 imperial-synodal action received but not legislatively re-promulgated by the 553 council proper. The standard Catholic conciliar tradition treats them as conciliar.
- **Was Origen actually condemned at 553?** Origen is named in Anathema 11 of the proper conciliar canons (anti-heretical canon listing heresiarchs). Whether the 15 specifically-Origenist anathemas count as conciliar is the live question.
- **Three Chapters condemnation and Chalcedonian fidelity.** The Latin opposition argued the council overturned Chalcedon by condemning theologians whom Chalcedon had not condemned. The Eastern position: the council clarified, did not overturn.
