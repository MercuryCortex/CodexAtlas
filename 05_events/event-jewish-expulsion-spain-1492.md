# Jewish Expulsion from Spain (1492)

## What happened
On 31 March 1492, Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile — the Catholic Monarchs whose marriage had united Christian Spain and who had completed the *Reconquista* by capturing Granada on 2 January 1492 — issued the Alhambra Decree, ordering all Jews in their kingdoms to convert to Christianity or leave Spain by 31 July (subsequently extended to 9 August). Approximately 200,000+ Spanish Jews were affected; perhaps 50,000–80,000 converted (becoming *conversos* / "New Christians," many of whom remained crypto-Jewish and were subsequently subject to Inquisitorial prosecution); the rest emigrated, mainly to Portugal (where they faced forced conversion in 1497), the Ottoman Empire (welcomed by Sultan Bayezid II), North Africa, Italy, and the Netherlands. The expulsion ended one of the world's most ancient continuous Jewish communities, established at least since Roman times.

## Religious consequences
- **Lurianic Kabbalah.** The Scholem thesis: the catastrophic Sephardic experience of 1492 (and the Portuguese 1497 follow-on) provided the theological-experiential matrix out of which Isaac Luria's Kabbalistic system (tzimtzum / shevirat ha-kelim / tikkun) emerged at Safed in 1570–1572. The cosmos itself was in exile; redemption was a cosmic-historical *tikkun*. Idel and others have complicated the directness of the causal link, but the centrality of the expulsion to the Sephardic theological imagination of the 16th century is consensus.
- **Sabbateanism.** The 17th-c. Sabbatean messianic movement (1665–1666) drew on the eschatological tension that the expulsion had intensified; Shabbatai Tzvi was himself the descendant of Sephardic refugees in Smyrna.
- **The Safed Kabbalistic renaissance.** Safed in the 16th century became a Sephardic refugee center; the figures of Joseph Karo, Solomon Alkabetz, Moses Cordovero, and Isaac Luria all worked in or descended from the Sephardic diaspora that 1492 had created.
- **Ottoman Jewish flowering.** Sultan Bayezid II's welcome of Sephardic refugees rebuilt the Ottoman Jewish communities (Constantinople, Salonica, Safed, Tiberias) that became the principal centers of Sephardic civilization for the next several centuries.
- **Dutch and Italian Sephardic communities.** Smaller but intellectually consequential: the Amsterdam Portuguese community (1593+) produced figures like Menasseh ben Israel and Baruch Spinoza; the Italian (Venice, Ferrara, Livorno) communities preserved Kabbalistic and humanist Jewish-Christian dialogue.
- **Inquisition and converso prosecution.** The Spanish Inquisition (founded 1478) focused its energies on conversos after 1492; the Portuguese Inquisition (1536+) followed. Converso prosecutions continued into the 18th century.

## Documents in play
- The Alhambra Decree itself.
- Sephardic post-expulsion responsa literature.
- The Safed Kabbalistic corpus (see [[phase-6-011-cordovero-pardes-rimmonim]] and [[phase-6-015-luria-vital-etz-chayyim]]).
- The 1492 expulsion historiography (Abraham Zacuto's *Sefer Yuhasin*, Solomon ibn Verga's *Shevet Yehudah*, Samuel Usque's *Consolação às Tribulações de Israel*).

## Disputes
- **Was the expulsion *necessary* for Lurianic Kabbalah?** Scholem's strong thesis (yes) has been refined by Idel's argument that proto-Lurianic elements predate 1492 and that the time-lag (~80 years) is substantial. The current consensus accepts the expulsion as the principal cultural-experiential matrix while granting that the theological development was complex.
- **Scale of conversions vs. emigration.** Estimates of the converso/emigrant ratio vary widely; both populations were substantial.
