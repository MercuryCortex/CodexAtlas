# Fall of Acre

## What happened
The Mamluk Sultanate, under al-Ashraf Khalil, besieged and on 18 May 1291 captured Acre — the last major Latin Christian city in the Holy Land. The siege lasted approximately six weeks (April-May 1291). Templar Grand Master Guillaume de Beaujeu was killed defending the breached northern wall; the Templar Quarter held out under the Marshal Theobald Gaudin until 28 May before its evacuation by sea (the Hospitallers similarly evacuated wounded). Within months the remaining Latin coastal towns — Tyre, Sidon, Beirut, Haifa, Athlit / Château Pèlerin — were abandoned. The Crusader Kingdom of Jerusalem (1099–1291), and with it nearly two centuries of Latin Christian Outremer presence, ceased to exist on the mainland.

## Religious consequences
- **End of the Latin Christian Holy Land.** Cyprus becomes the new headquarters of the surviving military orders and the titular Kingdom of Jerusalem.
- **Strategic-purpose crisis for the [[tradition-knights-templar|Templars]].** With no Holy Land to defend, the Order's *raison d'être* is suddenly absent. Sixteen years later, this crisis is the political-rhetorical context in which Philip IV moves against the Order ([[event-trial-of-templars-1307-1314]]).
- **Pivot of crusading energy.** Subsequent crusading shifts toward (a) the Reconquista in Iberia, (b) the Baltic Crusades under the [[tradition-teutonic-knights|Teutonic Knights]], (c) the *failed* projects to retake Jerusalem (continually planned and continually unrealized through the 14th c.).
- **Hospitaller pivot.** The [[tradition-hospitallers|Hospitallers]] take Rhodes (1307–1310) and reinvent themselves as a Mediterranean naval power.
- **End of sustained Latin contact with Eastern Christian, Mandaean, Druze, and Sufi communities.** Whatever theological transmission was possible during the Crusader-period contact zone, the institutional channel closes here.

## Documents in play
- The anonymous *De excidio urbis Acconis* (Latin lament).
- Abu al-Fida (Arabic, contemporary, a Mamluk officer participating in the siege).
- The Templar of Tyre — *Gestes des Chiprois* — anonymous Latin Outremer chronicle written ~1320.
