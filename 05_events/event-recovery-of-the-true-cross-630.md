# Recovery of the True Cross (630)

On 21 March 630 [[heraclius-byzantine-emperor|Emperor Heraclius]] personally restored the relic of the True Cross to Jerusalem, eight years after its capture by the Sasanian Persians during the 614 sack of the city ([[event-byzantine-sasanian-war-602-628|see Last Byzantine-Sasanian War]]). The ceremony was the political-religious capstone of Heraclius's improbable Persian victory: his counter-offensive into the Sasanian heartland (Nineveh 627; treaty 628) had recovered the Roman East and the relic. The event is liturgically commemorated as the **Exaltation of the Cross** in Eastern and Western Christianity (14 September). The Christian recovery proved brief: within eight years Jerusalem fell to the Rashidun caliphate ([[event-arab-conquest-of-jerusalem-638]]), this time permanently for over four centuries. Heraclius's restoration is the **first Roman imperial campaign framed in explicitly religious / holy-war terms** (Kaegi 2003), inaugurating the conceptual ancestry of the later medieval crusades.
