# Founding of Alexandria (331 BCE)

## What happened
Alexander the Great founded **Alexandria** on the Mediterranean coast of Egypt in 331 BCE during his Egyptian campaign (-332 to -331). He chose the site personally, near the small Egyptian village of Rhakotis at the western mouth of the Nile Delta, with a natural harbor protected by the offshore island of Pharos. The plan — a grid of broad streets oriented to catch the Mediterranean breeze, designed by the architect Dinocrates of Rhodes — anticipated a city far larger than any previous Greek foundation. Alexander left immediately after the founding (heading to the Siwa Oasis oracle of [[amun|Zeus-Ammon]] and then back to the campaign against Darius), and the city's institutional buildup was carried out over the following decades by **[[ptolemy-i-soter]]** and his successor **[[ptolemy-ii-philadelphus]]**: the **Library**, the **Museum** (research institution attached to the Library), the **Pharos lighthouse**, the **Serapeum**, the **Royal Quarter (*Brucheion*)**, the **Soma** (tomb of Alexander, brought from Memphis to Alexandria under Ptolemy I).

## Religious consequences
Alexandria becomes, within a century of its founding, **the principal religious-intellectual capital of the eastern Mediterranean**, with extraordinary downstream consequences for the Codex Atlas mission:

1. **Greek-Egyptian theological synthesis.** [[ptolemy-i-soter]] institutes the **[[serapis]] cult** at Alexandria as deliberate Greco-Egyptian state religion (~-300); the cult-statue at the **Serapeum of Alexandria** becomes one of the most famous religious images in the ancient world (destroyed by Christian mob in +391 — see [[event-destruction-of-serapeum-391]]). The Hellenistic Isis cult spreads from Alexandria across the Mediterranean.

2. **Hellenistic Judaism and the Septuagint.** Alexandria becomes the principal site of **Hellenistic Jewish intellectual life**. Under [[ptolemy-ii-philadelphus]] the **[[phase-3-006-septuagint]]** is translated — the Greek translation of the Hebrew Bible that becomes the principal Old Testament of early Christianity. The Alexandrian-Jewish philosophical-theological tradition culminates in [[philo-of-alexandria]] (~-25 to +50), whose synthesis of Platonist philosophy with biblical exegesis directly shapes Christian theology via [[clement-of-alexandria]] and [[origen]].

3. **Hermetic literature.** The earliest Hermetic texts ([[phase-3-021-hermetic-corpus-earliest]], [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]], [[phase-4-012-asclepius]]) are composed in Greco-Egyptian Alexandria in the early Roman imperial period (1st–3rd c. CE). The **Greek-Egyptian fusion** that produces Hermes Trismegistus / Thoth is an Alexandrian phenomenon.

4. **Alexandrian Christian theology.** The **Catechetical School of Alexandria** under [[clement-of-alexandria]] and [[origen]] (2nd–3rd c. CE) is the principal site of **Christian-Platonist** theological development. [[athanasius]] of Alexandria, the dominant 4th-c. defender of Nicene Christology, is the Alexandrian Christian Patriarch. The Alexandrian-Christian intellectual lineage runs continuously from the Library-Museum institutional infrastructure Alexander and Ptolemy founded.

5. **Egyptian dynastic continuity.** Alexandria becomes the new capital of Egypt for the entire Ptolemaic and Roman period; the old Egyptian capitals (Memphis, Thebes) decline; the geographical-religious center of Egypt shifts to the Mediterranean coast — **a religious-cultural reorientation visible to this day**.

## Disputes
- **Exact founding date.** Conventionally April 7, -331, on the basis of Arrian's narrative chronology. Some scholarship prefers slightly later in -331. The year is uncontested.
- **Alexander's personal involvement.** Plutarch (*Life of Alexander* 26) and Arrian (*Anabasis* 3.1–2) report Alexander personally chose the site and traced the city plan in barley grain (which birds then ate — interpreted variously as good or bad omen). Whether these are legend or fact is debated; the broad outline is accepted.
- **Pre-existing Egyptian Rhakotis.** The Egyptian fishing-village of Rhakotis pre-existed Alexandria's founding by some centuries; how much continuity (Egyptian-religious, demographic) there is between Rhakotis and Alexandria proper is debated. Some Egyptian-religious continuity is real.
