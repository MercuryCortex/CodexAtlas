# Fourth Crusade and Sack of Constantinople

## What happened
The Fourth Crusade (1202–1204), called by Pope Innocent III for the conquest of Jerusalem, was diverted by Venetian financial leverage (the Crusaders had contracted for naval transport they could not afford) first to the conquest of Christian Zara (1202) and then, in pursuit of payment promised by the pretender Alexios IV, to the sack of Christian Constantinople in April 1204. The Crusaders established the short-lived Latin Empire of Constantinople (1204–1261), divided Byzantine territory among themselves and Venice, and transferred enormous quantities of Greek manuscripts and artistic treasures westward (the bronze horses of Saint Mark's, the relics, and crucially for our concerns, the manuscript libraries). The Byzantine Empire was substantially restored under the Palaiologos dynasty from 1261 but never recovered its earlier power.

## Religious consequences
- Devastated Byzantine ecclesiastical institutions; deepened the East-West breach beyond any chance of organic healing.
- Transferred massive Greek manuscript collections to the Latin West — this is the largest single manuscript-transmission event prior to the 1453 fall of Constantinople. Aristotelian, Neoplatonic, and patristic texts that had been continuously preserved in Constantinople began circulating in Italy; the eventual reception of the Greek philosophical corpus by Western scholastics drew substantially from these post-1204 transfers (the dominant flow was via the Sicilian and Spanish Arabic-Latin translation channels, but the post-1204 Greek-direct channel supplemented and corrected).
- The trauma is foundational for Orthodox-Catholic ecclesial memory; the 1204 sack is, in modern Orthodox consciousness, a deeper wound than the 1054 mutual excommunication.

## Documents in play
- No specific document produced; but the manuscript-transfer consequences feed forward into the Latin scholastic reception of Aristotle (William of Moerbeke's direct-Greek Latin Aristotle translations ~1260–1270 use post-1204 manuscript witnesses) and into the Renaissance Greek-recovery (which accelerates after 1453 but begins with the post-1204 manuscript movement).

## Disputes
- [[pope-innocent-iii|Pope Innocent III's]] responsibility — he condemned the sack but had not effectively prevented it; modern scholarship is divided on his actual ability to control the Crusader expedition.
- **[[tradition-knights-templar|Templar]] and [[tradition-hospitallers|Hospitaller]] non-participation.** Neither military-religious order participated meaningfully in the Fourth Crusade — they remained committed to the Outremer defense rather than to the Constantinople diversion. This is one of the data-points sometimes invoked in their *de facto* alignment against the more egregious Crusader misuse-of-purpose.
