# Evangelical Political Realignment in the USA (1979–present)

## What happened
The mobilization of conservative American Protestants (Southern Baptists, broader evangelicals, and Pentecostal/charismatic streams) into a self-conscious political bloc aligned with the Republican Party. Standard milestones:

- **1971**: U.S. *Green v. Connally* federal court ruling denying tax-exempt status to racially segregated schools. Balmer 2014/2021 argues this — not Roe v. Wade (1973) — was the actual catalyst that activated white-evangelical institutional politics, by threatening tax-exempt status for the network of "segregation academies" (e.g., Bob Jones University) that had emerged in response to *Brown v. Board* (1954).
- **1979**: Jerry Falwell founds the Moral Majority, the prototype institutional vehicle. Reagan's 1980 campaign explicitly courts evangelicals; Falwell's "Listen, America!" articulates the program.
- **1980s**: Religious Roundtable (Edward McAteer), Family Research Council, Focus on the Family (Dobson), Concerned Women for America (LaHaye) build infrastructure.
- **1989**: Christian Coalition founded by Pat Robertson and run by Ralph Reed; principal institutional vehicle of 1990s religious-right.
- **1990s–2000s**: George W. Bush's 2000 and 2004 campaigns mobilize evangelical voters explicitly; "compassionate conservatism" and faith-based-initiative policy.
- **2016**: 81% of self-identified white evangelicals vote for Donald Trump. This is the principal break in the historical pattern: a thrice-married, profane, non-church-going candidate whose personal life flatly contradicts the religious-right's stated criteria nevertheless wins overwhelming evangelical support, defended primarily on policy-transactional grounds (Supreme Court appointments, abortion, religious-liberty cases) and increasingly on identitarian/cultural grounds.
- **2017–2025**: Continued white-evangelical / Trump alignment through 2020 election denial, January 6 2021 Capitol attack (with strong Christian-nationalist iconography), and the 2024 election. Internal evangelical critique (Russell Moore, David French, Beth Moore, Tim Keller pre-2023) and exit ("exvangelical" movement, post-evangelical migration into Anglican and Orthodox communions) accelerates.

## Religious consequences
- Substantial change in what "evangelical" *means* as a religious-sociological category. Pew, GSS, and PRRI data show non-attending and non-belief-self-identifying "evangelicals" rising — "evangelical" has become partly a *political-identitarian* rather than (only) religious-creedal label.
- Schism in the Southern Baptist Convention and other denominations between "Christian-nationalist" and "traditional conservative-evangelical" wings (e.g., Russell Moore's 2021 departure from the SBC Ethics & Religious Liberty Commission).
- Christian nationalism as a distinct contested category — a 2022 PRRI/Brookings survey found a substantial minority of white-evangelicals endorse explicit Christian-nationalist statements (USA as Christian nation, biblical law as basis for civil law, etc.). Whitehead & Perry's *Taking America Back for God* (2020) is the principal scholarly synthesis.
- Effect on global evangelicalism mixed: in Brazil (Bolsonaro), parts of Africa, Eastern Europe (Orbán's Hungary) — American-evangelical-style political-religious mobilization has international counterparts; in other parts of global evangelicalism (UK, much of African Anglican evangelicalism, Korean evangelicalism) the American pattern is regarded as a cautionary case.

## Disputes
- **Cause of the realignment.** Roe v. Wade abortion-as-catalyst (older popular narrative) vs. *Green v. Connally* tax-exempt-segregation (Balmer 2021) vs. broader cultural anxiety (FitzGerald 2017) vs. economic-resentment / "deaths of despair" (Case/Deaton) — multiple framings.
- **Reading of 2016+.** Continuity (Du Mez 2020 — same patriarchal-warrior masculinity has run through evangelicalism for decades, Trump is the natural endpoint) vs. rupture (some traditional-evangelical voices — David French, Russell Moore — argue Trump-alignment is a betrayal of historic evangelical theology and ethics).
- **"Christian nationalism" as a useful category.** Defenders (Whitehead/Perry 2020, Stewart 2020) vs. skeptics (some on the right who see it as a smear-term; some scholars who note historical-American religiosity has always had a civic-religion strand).
