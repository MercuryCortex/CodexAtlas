# Pyramid of Khafre (Giza)

## Overview

The **Pyramid of Khafre** is the second of the three Giza pyramids and the **visual centerpiece** of the Giza plateau — appearing taller than the Great Pyramid when viewed from certain angles because it sits on higher ground and retains its original limestone casing stones at the apex. It is slightly smaller than the Great Pyramid in absolute height but creates a more imposing profile.

**Dimensions (Petrie 1883):**
- Base: ~215 m per side
- Original height: ~143.5 m (now ~136.4 m)
- Angle: 53°7'48" (steeper than the Red Pyramid; close to the Great Pyramid's 51°50')
- **Original casing stones** still intact at the upper ~30 m — the white Tura limestone casing that once covered all Giza pyramids (and was stripped for Cairo's medieval construction) survives here, making Khafre's pyramid the best example of what all three Giza pyramids originally looked like

## Internal chambers: simpler than Khufu's

The interior of Khafre's pyramid is architecturally simpler than the Great Pyramid — no Grand Gallery, no relieving chambers, no complex series of shafts:

**Two entrance passages:** One at ground level on the north face; one higher up on the north face (unusual feature). Both descend and eventually connect to the same passage system.

**Single burial chamber:** A pit-shaped chamber cut partly into the bedrock (~14.15 m × 5 m × 6.8 m) with a gabled ceiling. **Giovanni Belzoni** (1818) forced the entrance open and found the chamber empty except for a granite coffer (lidless) sunk into the floor — and inside it, scattered animal bones (possibly an intrusive later burial; possibly ancient robbers' leavings).

**No shafts or secondary chambers:** Unlike the Great Pyramid, Khafre's pyramid has no known shafts, no secondary chambers, and no relieving chambers above the burial room.

## The Valley Temple and causeway

The **Valley Temple of Khafre** — connected to the pyramid by a 494-m causeway — is one of the best-preserved Old Kingdom temples in Egypt and the most sophisticated building on the Giza Plateau outside the pyramids themselves.

**Construction:** Built of massive local limestone blocks (some 100+ tonnes) faced with polished Aswan granite — a combination of brute scale and precise finish that Khafre's architects inherited from his father Khufu's quarry teams.

**Interior:** The T-shaped hall contained **23 diorite statues of Khafre** arranged against the walls. Only one has been found intact — the famous diorite statue of Khafre with the Horus falcon behind his head, now in the Cairo Museum. The other 22 were destroyed or removed in antiquity.

**The Sphinx connection:** The Valley Temple is adjacent to the Sphinx Temple and the **[[sphinx-of-giza|Great Sphinx]]** itself. The Sphinx sits at the eastern end of the causeway — whether it was carved as part of the Khafre complex or pre-dates it is the central question in Sphinx chronology.

## The Orion Correlation

In **[[theme-orion-correlation-theory|Robert Bauval's Orion Correlation Theory]]** (1994), the three Giza pyramids mirror the three stars of Orion's Belt (*Mintaka*, *Al Nitak*, *Al Nilam*) with:
- Menkaure's pyramid = *Mintaka* (smaller, offset)
- Khafre's pyramid = *Al Nitak* (middle star)
- Khufu's pyramid = *Al Nilam* (brightest)

The correspondence requires explaining the slight offset in the three-pyramid alignment (they are not perfectly collinear, matching the Belt's curvature) as intentional. The Milky Way corresponds to the Nile. Mainstream Egyptology regards the alignment as partial and the overall correlation as a pattern-match rather than evidence of deliberate celestial cartography.

## Why Khafre's pyramid appears taller

Khafre placed his pyramid on a **4.5 m higher bedrock platform** than Khufu's pyramid — a site engineering decision that means the apex of Khafre's pyramid (143.5 m above bedrock) ends up at nearly the same absolute elevation as Khufu's (146.5 m above bedrock, but on lower ground). When photographed from the east, Khafre's pyramid often appears as tall as or taller than Khufu's. This deliberate visual parity between father's and son's monuments is either a remarkable engineering coincidence or an intentional architectural statement.
