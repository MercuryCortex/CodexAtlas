# Armenian Genocide

Stub. See [[tradition-armenian-apostolic]] and [[alphabet-armenian]] for the religious and linguistic dimensions of this event.

The genocide (1915–1923) resulted in the deaths of approximately 1–1.5 million Armenian Christians and the destruction of the entire Anatolian Armenian world. Ottoman policy specifically targeted Armenian-language education, clergy, and press — recognizing that the Armenian script and church were the twin pillars of Armenian national identity. The script survived in diaspora communities worldwide and became a central resistance vector. The Armenian genocide is widely recognized as the first modern genocide and was explicitly cited at Nuremberg as a historical precedent.
