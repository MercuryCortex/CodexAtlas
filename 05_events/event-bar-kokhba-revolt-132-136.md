# Bar Kokhba Revolt

## What happened
The third major Jewish-Roman war (132–136 CE), led by Simon bar Kokhba (Shimon bar Kosiba — the "Bar Kokhba" name, meaning "son of the star," was a messianic title applied by his supporters from Numbers 24:17). Triggered by Hadrian's plans to refound Jerusalem as the Roman colony *Aelia Capitolina* (with a temple of Jupiter on the Temple Mount) and possibly by a ban on circumcision. The revolt established a Jewish administration over much of Judaea for ~2.5 years (coinage with "Year 1 of the Redemption of Israel" survives). Hadrian eventually committed massive Roman forces under his best general, Sextus Julius Severus, who reduced the rebellion by attrition.

## Religious consequences
- *Catastrophic Jewish defeat.* Cassius Dio reports 580,000 Jewish dead and 50 fortresses + 985 villages destroyed (figures probably high but evidencing scale). The Jewish population of Judaea was decimated; many sold into slavery.
- *Hadrian's renaming.* The province of Judaea was renamed *Syria Palaestina* — the act that introduced "Palestine" as a Roman-era place-name; Jerusalem became *Aelia Capitolina*, and Jews were banned from entering the city.
- *End of messianic political activism in Judaism.* The revolt's failure under a charismatic messianic figure produced lasting rabbinic ambivalence about messianic politics; Akiva's celebrated declaration of Bar Kokhba as the messiah was later disowned in the Talmud (Y. Ta'an. 4:5).
- *Acceleration of the Jewish-Christian divide.* Christian sources (Justin Martyr, *First Apology* 31) report Jewish Christians refusing to acknowledge Bar Kokhba as messiah and being persecuted by his supporters — a key moment in the parting of the ways.

## Documents in play
- *Bar Kokhba letters.* Discovered by Yigael Yadin's 1960–61 expeditions in the Cave of Letters in the Judaean Desert — Aramaic, Hebrew, and Greek letters between Bar Kokhba and his commanders, giving direct primary evidence of the revolt's administration.
- Cassius Dio's *Roman History* 69.12–14 is the principal Roman literary source.

## Disputes
- **Akiva's support.** Whether Akiva actually declared Bar Kokhba the messiah (per Y. Ta'an. 4:5) or whether this is a later rabbinic projection.
- **Casualty figures.** Cassius Dio's numbers are probably exaggerated.
