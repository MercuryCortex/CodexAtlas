# El Castillo (Chichen Itza)

## Overview

**El Castillo** ("The Castle") is the dominant pyramid of **Chichen Itza** (Yucatán, Mexico), dedicated to the feathered serpent deity **Kukulkan** (equivalent to the Aztec Quetzalcóatl). Built approximately 900–1200 CE by the Itza Maya (with possible Toltec cultural influence), it is the most visited Maya monument and one of the New Seven Wonders of the World (2007).

**Dimensions:**
- Base: ~55 m per side (each of 4 sides)
- Height: ~30 m including the summit temple
- 9 terraced levels
- 4 staircases of 91 steps each + 1 top platform step = **365 steps** total (the solar year)
- Each face has **52 panels** (the 52-year Maya Calendar Round cycle)

## The astronomical calendar encoded in stone

El Castillo is among the most precisely calendar-encoded monuments in the ancient world:

**The equinox serpent:** On the **spring and autumn equinoxes**, the afternoon sun creates a series of triangular shadows on the northwest balustrade of the north staircase — a pattern of light-and-shadow that appears to create an undulating serpent body descending the staircase toward the serpent head carved at the base. This effect lasts approximately **3–4 hours** on each equinox day.

Whether this was intentional (designed into the pyramid's geometry) or a coincidental side effect of the staircase angle and the Maya preference for equinox observation is debated. Modern archaeoastronomers generally support intentionality: the effect requires precise angle calculation, and the Maya were among history's most accurate naked-eye astronomers.

**The numbers:**
- 91 steps × 4 sides = 364 + 1 platform = **365 days** (solar year)
- 52 panels per side = **52-year Calendar Round** (the cycle when the 365-day Haab' and the 260-day Tzolk'in calendars realign)
- 9 terraces × 2 faces = 18 = the Maya month count (18 months of 20 days + 5 extra)
- The 4-staircase symmetry mirrors the 4 cardinal directions

## The inner pyramid: a pyramid within a pyramid

INAH archaeologists in the 1930s discovered that El Castillo was built **over an earlier, complete pyramid** — a smaller structure at its core. This "inner El Castillo" is accessible via a narrow passage from the north staircase base.

**Inner pyramid dimensions:**
- Height: ~17 m
- Base: ~33 m per side
- Its summit temple contains two significant objects: a **Chac Mool** figure (a reclining deity sculpture used as a sacrificial offering table) and a **jaguar throne** — a stone jaguar painted red and encrusted with jade spots (72 jade discs) and obsidian eyes. The jaguar's mouth is open and an intact jade and mosite jade-mosaic disc sits on its seat.

The jaguar throne is associated with the Maya practice of enthronement: the king sat on the jaguar throne to receive divine power. The colour red and jade spots are attributes of rulership and the sun.

**A second inner pyramid:** 2015–2016 UNAM geoelectrical surveys detected a possible **third nested structure** — a pyramid within the pyramid within the pyramid — though this has not been excavated and the data are not conclusive.

## The cenote connection

**Chichen Itza** means "At the mouth of the well of the Itza" — the site is named for the **Sacred Cenote** (a limestone sinkhole ~60 m in diameter, 27 m deep) located 300 m north of El Castillo via the White Road (*sacbe*). The cenote was used for ritual sacrifice: gold, jade, copal incense, pottery, and human remains were thrown in as offerings to the rain deity Chaac. Edward Thompson dragged the cenote (~1900) and found these offerings, many of which are now in the Peabody Museum at Harvard.

**El Castillo's alignment toward the cenote:** The Sacred Cenote is positioned precisely due north of El Castillo. The north staircase — the one that receives the equinox serpent shadow — is the staircase that faces the cenote. Whether El Castillo was sited specifically in relation to the cenote, or the cenote's north axis is coincidental, is unresolved.

## Maya vs. Egyptian pyramid: parallel and divergence

| Feature | El Castillo | Great Pyramid |
|---|---|---|
| Calendar encoded in structure | Yes — 365 steps, 52 panels | Yes — but more debated |
| Astronomical phenomenon | Equinox serpent shadow | Stellar shaft alignments |
| Inner pyramid / nested structure | Yes — at least one inner structure | No (but Big Void above Grand Gallery) |
| Purpose | Temple (ritual use at summit) | Tomb (or ritual; debated) |
| Underground water feature | Sacred Cenote nearby | Subterranean Chamber in bedrock |
| Access to interior chambers | Via internal passage | Via internal passages |

El Castillo and the Great Pyramid represent two fully independent traditions of pyramid building that share the human instinct to encode cosmological order in monumental architecture.
