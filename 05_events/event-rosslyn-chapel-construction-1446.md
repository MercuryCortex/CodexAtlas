# Construction of Rosslyn Chapel

## What Happened
**[[william-sinclair-rosslyn]]** (1st Earl of Caithness) began construction of the **Collegiate Church of St Matthew** at Roslin, Midlothian in 1446. The project was never completed — only the choir was built of what was intended to be a full cruciform collegiate church. Construction continued until Sinclair's death in 1484. The unfinished state contributes to the building's aura: the East wall is unfinished stone, the full nave was never built.

## The Building

Rosslyn Chapel is **12.8 × 6.7 meters internally** — small enough to walk around in minutes, dense enough to take years to decipher. Its stone carvings are among the most extraordinary in Britain:

**The Apprentice Pillar**: The chapel's most famous feature — a column of extraordinary sculptural complexity, with spiraling foliage and eight intertwining dragons at its base. Its legend: a master mason left for Rome to study the original design; in his absence, his apprentice created this pillar from a dream vision. The master, returning and finding the apprentice's work superior to anything he could produce, murdered the apprentice in a fit of jealousy. This is **structurally identical** to the **Hiram Abiff legend** of Freemasonry: a master craftsman murdered by those who want his secret; a substitute kept in place of the lost truth. Whether the Apprentice Pillar legend predates the Hiram Abiff legend, or was retroactively Masonicized, is debated — but the structural identity is exact.

**The Green Man**: Approximately 110 carved Green Man heads throughout the chapel — a pagan vegetation deity in a Christian church. This is one of the highest concentrations of Green Man imagery in any British religious building.

**Templar crosses**: Multiple Templar cross pattée carvings throughout the building; the Sinclair family had Crusader connections (Henri de Saint-Clair joined the First Crusade, 1096).

**Possible pre-Columbian plant carvings**: Certain carvings have been identified as possibly depicting aloe vera, maize (Indian corn), and other New World plants — predating Columbus by ~46 years. If accurate, this would imply pre-Columbian transatlantic contact. The identification is debated among botanists and architectural historians.

**Danse macabre**, Seven Deadly Sins, Seven Cardinal Virtues, angel musicians (including an angel playing bagpipes), and what some identify as encoded cryptographic patterns in the ceiling cubes.

## The Freemasonic Connection

Rosslyn Chapel is the **symbolic heart of Scottish Freemasonry's origin mythology** — not because it was used as a Masonic lodge (it wasn't) but because:

1. The **Sinclair family** held hereditary Grand Mastership of Scottish operative masons for ~300 years
2. The **Apprentice Pillar legend** is structurally identical to the core Masonic Third Degree drama
3. The **Templar iconography** supports Ramsay's Crusader-origin thesis for Freemasonry
4. A **later William St. Clair** (~1736) transferred the Sinclair hereditary claim into the Grand Lodge of Scotland — the institutional founding of Scottish speculative Freemasonry

When the Da Vinci Code (Dan Brown, 2003) placed the Holy Grail's final resting place at Rosslyn Chapel, it was drawing on a decades-old esoteric tradition that had made the chapel the most symbolically loaded building in the Masonic-Templar imaginary.
