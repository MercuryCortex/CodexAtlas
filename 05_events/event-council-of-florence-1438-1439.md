# Council of Florence (1438–1439)

The ecumenical council at which the Latin and Greek churches formally reunited (bull *Laetentur Caeli*, 6 July 1439) — in exchange for Greek concessions on the *filioque*, papal primacy, purgatory, and azymes, and in expectation of Latin military aid against the advancing Turks. The Union was bitterly contested in Greek territory (Mark of Ephesus dissented at the Council itself; the post-1439 Greek public largely rejected it) and ultimately failed to produce the promised Western aid (Constantinople fell to Mehmed II in 1453). Two collateral consequences proved more historically consequential than the Union itself: (1) **Bessarion of Nicaea** converted definitively to Catholic obedience, eventually becoming a cardinal and bequeathing his Greek manuscript collection to Venice (the future Biblioteca Marciana); (2) **Gemistus Plethon's Florentine lectures on Plato** (1438–1439) so impressed Cosimo de' Medici that he commissioned [[marsilio-ficino]] to translate the Hermetica (1463) and the complete Plato (1484) — the foundational moment of [[tradition-renaissance-hermeticism|Renaissance Hermeticism]].
