# World's Parliament of Religions (Chicago, 1893)

## What happened
The first formal interfaith gathering in the modern Western world, held 11–27 September 1893 as part of the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago. Over 4,000 attendees heard speakers from Christianity (Protestant, Catholic, Orthodox), Judaism, Islam, Hinduism (Vivekananda, Mozoomdar, Vivekananda's senior fellow Brahmin Pratap Chandra Mozoomdar), Buddhism (Sinhalese Theravada via Anagarika Dharmapala; Japanese Rinzai Zen via Soyen Shaku), Jainism (Virchand Gandhi), Zoroastrianism (Jinanji Modi), Confucianism, Shinto, Bahá'í (the first public US presentation), Spiritualism, Theosophy, and others. The 5,000-seat Hall of Columbus filled for major sessions.

## Religious consequences
- **Vivekananda's emergence**: a near-unknown Bengali sannyasi opened with "Sisters and Brothers of America" on 11 September 1893; the standing ovation was reportedly two minutes. Vivekananda's subsequent US tour (1893–1896) established the Vedanta Society and inaugurated the modern Hindu-modernist presence in the West.
- **Soyen Shaku and Japanese Zen**: Shaku's Chicago presence led, through his disciple D.T. Suzuki, to the 20th-century Western Zen reception.
- **Anagarika Dharmapala**: Dharmapala's presence advanced the late-19th-century international Buddhist revival (he had been Olcott's protégé in Ceylon).
- **Bahá'í public emergence**: the Parliament was the first public introduction of the Bahá'í Faith to the United States.
- **Christian-pluralist framing**: the Parliament's framers (Barrows, others) intended it within a Christian-inclusivist framework; the actual effect — making non-Christian traditions visible and authoritative on their own terms — exceeded the framing.
