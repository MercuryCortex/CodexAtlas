# Rise of the "Nones" (2007–present)

## What happened
The dramatic rise of religiously-non-affiliated adults in the United States and parallel rises across other historically-Christian societies. The "nones" are survey respondents who answer "none" / "nothing in particular" / "atheist" / "agnostic" to questions about religious affiliation.

US trajectory (Pew Research Religious Landscape Studies; General Social Survey data):
- 1972 GSS: ~5% religiously non-affiliated.
- 1990 GSS: ~7% religiously non-affiliated.
- 2007 Pew Landscape Study: ~16% religiously non-affiliated.
- 2014 Pew Landscape Study: ~23% religiously non-affiliated.
- 2024 Pew Landscape Study: ~28–29% religiously non-affiliated.
- Among under-30 American adults, non-affiliation runs ~35–40% (varies by survey).

The 2007 Pew study is identified as the inflection point that made the trend a major topic in religious-sociology and journalism. The trend accelerated through the 2008–2015 period (often called the "great decline" by Bullivant 2022), slowed somewhat in the late 2010s, and appears to be plateauing by the mid-2020s, with Pew 2024 finding the non-affiliated share roughly stable from 2019–2024.

Parallel international trends (mostly faster than the US): UK religious-non-affiliation moves from ~30% to ~50%+ by 2021 census; Netherlands becomes majority non-religious by 2017; Australia majority non-Christian (though not non-religious) by 2021 census; substantial declines across continental Western Europe except for Muslim immigration counter-trend.

## Religious consequences
- **Mainline Protestant collapse and Catholic decline.** Mainline Protestant share of US adults: ~17% (2007) → ~11% (2024). Catholic share: ~24% → ~19%. Evangelical share more stable but declining among younger cohorts. The nones are disproportionately ex-mainline-Protestant and ex-Catholic.
- **"Believing without belonging" partial reality.** Older sociology-of-religion frames (Davie's "believing without belonging") found imperfect support: many nones do retain some spiritual/supernatural beliefs but increasingly fewer.
- **Political consequences.** The "religious right" / "white evangelical" alignment (see [[event-evangelical-political-realignment-usa-1979-present]]) is, by Putnam & Campbell 2010 and Margolis 2018, *itself* a substantial cause of the rise of nones — younger socially-liberal Americans dissociated from "religion" because they had come to associate it with conservative politics. The causal arrow runs both ways.
- **"Spiritual but not religious" / SBNR.** The non-affiliated are not uniformly secular. A substantial fraction identify as "spiritual but not religious" — drawing from contemplative practice, mindfulness, [[tradition-buddhism]], [[tradition-new-age]], astrology, paganism (cf. [[tradition-wicca-modern-pagan]]), psychedelic spirituality, etc. The American religious-marketplace transforms rather than collapses.
- **Internal differentiation of "nones."** Atheists are a minority (~5% of adults); agnostics ~5%; "nothing in particular" ~18%+. The "nothing in particular" group is the largest and the most internally varied — many retain belief in God or higher power; a minority attend services occasionally; many are not actively anti-religious. Bullivant 2022 distinguishes "nonverts" (active converts away from religion) from "cradle nones" who never had a tradition.

## Disputes
- **Drivers.** Multiple proposed: (a) Putnam-Campbell — over-association of religion with conservative politics drove the young away in the 1990s–2000s; (b) Smith — broader cultural shift toward "moralistic therapeutic deism" and consumer religion; (c) Burge — institutional-religious failure (sexual-abuse scandals, internal hypocrisy); (d) Bullivant — secularization-as-cohort-replacement; (e) social-media and information-access effects. No consensus.
- **Whether the trend is reversing.** Some 2022–2024 data suggests young-adult religious non-affiliation has stabilized rather than continuing to rise. Whether this is a plateau, a cohort-floor, or a reversal is too early to tell.
- **International comparability.** "None" surveys do not map cleanly across cultures. The 28% American non-affiliation is structurally different from 50%+ UK non-affiliation, which is in turn different from 75%+ Czech non-affiliation, because the surrounding religious cultures differ substantially.
