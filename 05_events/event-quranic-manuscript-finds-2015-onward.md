# Qur'anic Manuscript Finds and Studies (2015 onward)

## What happened
A cluster of late-20th- and early-21st-century early Qur'anic manuscript discoveries and (newer) high-resolution analyses, with 2015 as a public-watershed year:

- **Sana'a palimpsest** (discovered 1972 in the loft of the Great Mosque of Sana'a, Yemen, during repair work; ~12,000 fragments). Lower script is a partially-erased earlier Qur'an, written in Hijazi script, with substantive variants from the standard Uthmanic *rasm*. Major scholarly study began in the 2000s; Sadeghi & Goudarzi 2012 (*Der Islam* 87, "The Codex of a Companion of the Prophet…") is the definitive analysis of one set of folios, dating the lower-text codex to the first decades after the Prophet's death (c. 632–670 CE), arguing it represents a *Companion codex* (i.e., a variant pre-Uthmanic recension of the kind the canonical sources name). Hilali 2017 offers a contrasting interpretation (later scribal exercise / training text). Radiocarbon dating of one Sana'a palimpsest folio (Stanford/Oxford 2014–2015): 95% confidence range c. 578–669 CE.
- **Birmingham folios** (Mingana Collection, Cadbury Research Library, U. Birmingham). Two parchment folios (parts of Surat al-Kahf, Maryam, Taha) in Hijazi script, radiocarbon-dated July 2015 to 568–645 CE (95% confidence; mean ~605 CE). The 2015 announcement received massive global press attention; date overlaps with the Prophet's lifetime (570–632 CE), though manuscript scholars caution that the radiocarbon-dating is of the *parchment*, not the *ink*, and the upper bound of the range is the salient figure for actual production.
- **Tübingen Ma VI 165** (German radiocarbon study 2014): radiocarbon-dated 649–675 CE (95% confidence).
- **Corpus Coranicum project** (Berlin-Brandenburg Academy of Sciences, 2007 onward, under Angelika Neuwirth and Michael Marx): high-resolution digital corpus and critical-apparatus project for the early Qur'anic manuscript tradition. Ongoing.
- **Codex Mashhad** (manuscript at the Imam Reza shrine, Mashhad, studied 2010s onward): a substantial early codex with substantive variants from the standard Uthmanic *rasm*.

## Religious consequences
The early-Qur'anic-manuscript field has transformed substantially in 2010–2025. Three principal effects:

1. **Earlier physical-witness chain.** The radiocarbon-anchored Hijazi-script manuscripts (Birmingham, Sana'a, Tübingen, Mashhad) push the physical-witness chain back to within a few decades of the Prophet's death. This is closer-in-time-to-origin than any major comparable religious-text tradition.
2. **Documented variants from the Uthmanic *rasm*.** The Sana'a lower-text confirms what Sunni hadith and Shia traditions had already documented about pre-Uthmanic Companion codices: that there were multiple recensions, that Caliph Uthman's canonization (c. 650 CE) was a real editorial moment, and that some Companion codices had readings now lost. This both confirms the broad outline of the traditional Islamic account and complicates simple claims about the Qur'an's purely-oral transmission and Uthmanic uniqueness.
3. **Methodological reorientation.** Qur'anic studies is shifting toward a manuscript-based, paleographically and radiocarbon-anchored historical-critical approach, comparable to what Hebrew Bible studies underwent in the late 19th c. and what New Testament studies has long had. The field is methodologically much less developed than the parallel Hebrew Bible / New Testament fields, partly because of historical access difficulty.

## Disputes
- **Reading the Birmingham radiocarbon date.** Sympathetic Muslim and some scholarly readers interpret the 568–645 CE range as evidence the folios are *Prophet-era* manuscripts, possibly from the Companions. More cautious readers note that (a) the radiocarbon dates the parchment, not the ink (which could be later); (b) the 95% CI upper bound is 645 CE, comfortably consistent with Uthmanic-era (~650 CE) production; (c) Hijazi script is plausibly a few decades wider than this range. The folios are early on any reading; the question is *how* early.
- **Sana'a lower-text interpretation.** Sadeghi/Goudarzi (Companion-codex recension) vs. Hilali (scribal exercise). Substantive ongoing dispute.
- **Wider revisionist Qur'anic studies.** A separate revisionist school (Wansbrough 1977, Crone & Cook 1977 *Hagarism*, more recent variants) has argued for a substantially later Qur'anic redaction (8th–9th c.). The new manuscript dates substantially constrain this revisionist position toward the traditional Islamic dating, though some revisionist positions remain defensible regarding *editing* (vs. *origin*) of the text.
