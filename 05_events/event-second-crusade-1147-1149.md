# Second Crusade

## What happened
A papally-sanctioned crusade (1147–1149) called by Pope Eugenius III (*Quantum praedecessores*, December 1145) in response to the fall of Edessa to Zengi (1144) — the first of the Crusader states to fall. Preached most influentially by [[bernard-of-clairvaux]] across northern France and the Rhineland. Two royal armies marched east — Louis VII of France (with Eleanor of Aquitaine) and Conrad III of Germany — both savaged in Anatolia. The remnants combined at Acre and chose to attack Damascus rather than aim for Edessa or Aleppo; the siege of Damascus (July 1148) failed disastrously after four days. The Crusade is broadly judged a strategic failure.

The Crusade encompassed three theatres simultaneously:
1. **Levantine theatre** — the main royal expeditions; the disaster at Damascus.
2. **Iberian theatre** — the **Siege of Lisbon** (1147) — an Anglo-Flemish-German fleet, *en route* to the Holy Land, paused to help Afonso I of Portugal capture Lisbon from the Almoravids; one of the few clear Crusade successes.
3. **Baltic / Wendish theatre** — the **Wendish Crusade** (1147) against the pagan Polabian Slavs of north-eastern Germany — the inaugural moment of the Northern Crusades that the [[tradition-teutonic-knights|Teutonic Knights]] will later institutionalize.

## Religious consequences
- **Major embarrassment for Bernard of Clairvaux and the crusading idea.** Bernard's *De consideratione* (~1150) defends the crusade-theology against the obvious objection that God did not grant victory.
- **Templar prominence.** The Templars provide critical military support to Louis VII's army; the Order's reputation rises as a result, despite the strategic failure.
- **Rhineland Jewish protection.** Bernard intervenes to prevent a repetition of the 1096 anti-Jewish massacres — a significant point in the medieval Latin Christian–Jewish relationship and in Bernard's biography.
- **Initiation of the Northern Crusades concept.** The 1147 Wendish Crusade extends the crusading framework to *pagan European* targets — a development with two-century consequences in the Baltic.

## Documents in play
- Odo of Deuil's *De profectione Ludovici VII in Orientem* — eyewitness clerical account of Louis VII's army.
- Otto of Freising's *Gesta Frederici* — German imperial perspective.
- *De expugnatione Lyxbonensi* — anonymous, the Lisbon-siege account.

## Disputes
- **Damascus rationale.** Why attack a Muslim power that had been *allied* to Jerusalem against Zengid Aleppo? William of Tyre's later account suggests treachery; Phillips 2007 ch. 9 argues for a Latin strategic miscalculation, not betrayal.
