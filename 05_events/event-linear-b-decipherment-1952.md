# Decipherment of Linear B (Michael Ventris, 1952)

## What happened
In June 1952 Michael Ventris, an English architect and amateur philologist, announced in a BBC Third Programme broadcast (and shortly afterward in a co-authored paper with John Chadwick, *Journal of Hellenic Studies* 1953) that he had deciphered the Linear B script — a syllabic writing system used on clay tablets from Mycenaean Greek palace sites (Knossos on Crete, Pylos, Mycenae, Thebes, Tiryns, Khania) dating to ~-1450 to -1180 BCE. The decipherment revealed Linear B as recording **an early form of Greek**, roughly 500 years older than the earliest previously known Greek (the alphabetic inscriptions of the 8th c. BCE).

The decipherment was the culmination of work that included Alice Kober's grid-based statistical analysis (1940s, prematurely ended by her 1950 death), Arthur Evans's earlier publication of the Knossos tablets (which he had wrongly believed recorded a pre-Greek "Minoan" language), and Ventris's combinatorial-cryptographic insights, refined and tested with Chadwick's philological backing. Ventris died in a car accident in 1956 at age 34; Chadwick continued the work for decades.

## Religious consequences
The decipherment was the **single largest "compose-date pushback" event in the historiography of Greek religion**. Where pre-1952 scholarship treated the Olympian pantheon as essentially a post-Dark-Age (Iron Age, ~-1000 BCE onward) construction, possibly with Mycenaean traces, the deciphered tablets disclosed that the major Olympians were *already named and receiving cult* in 13th-c. BCE palace administration:

- **di-we / Diwo (= Zeus)** — Knossos, Pylos.
- **e-ra (= Hera)** — Pylos, Thebes.
- **po-se-da-o (= Poseidon)** — Pylos especially, more prominent in the Pylian cult-rosters than Zeus himself.
- **a-ta-na po-ti-ni-ja ("Mistress Athena") (= Athena)** — Knossos. (Some debate over whether "Athena" or simply a "Mistress of Athana" is meant; consensus reading is the former.)
- **e-ma-a2 (= Hermes)** — Pylos.
- **a-ti-mi-te / a-te-mi-to (= Artemis)** — Pylos.
- **di-wo-nu-so (= Dionysus)** — Pylos, Khania (Crete). **This is the major surprise** — Dionysus had been treated by older scholarship (Erwin Rohde, Walter F. Otto) as a "late arrival" in Greek religion from Thrace or Phrygia, but Linear B attests him in the Mycenaean palace cult itself.
- **pa-ja-wo (probably = Paean, an epithet later attached to Apollo)** — Knossos. Whether this attests "Apollo" specifically in the Mycenaean period is contested; "Apollo" as such is not securely attested in Linear B (the proposed *a-pe-ro₂* reading is heavily disputed).
- **e-nu-wa-ri-jo (= Enyalios, war-epithet later attached to Ares)** — Knossos.
- **pa-si-te-o-i ("to all the gods")** — a generic offering-formula, attesting an organized pantheon-concept.

Plus deities and divine epithets that did *not* survive into the Iron Age Olympian system (or survived only as obscure local cults) — e.g., the prominent Pylian *Potnia* ("Mistress"), apparently a high goddess; *Mnasa* and *Marineu*; the divinity *Diwija* (a feminine of Zeus, attested at Pylos).

The pantheon is thus minimum ~800 years older than Homer + Hesiod. Greek religion is no longer an Iron Age construction but a Bronze Age inheritance with deep continuity through the Mycenaean palaces.

## Documents in play
- **Documents re-contextualized.** [[phase-2-008-homeric-epics]] and [[phase-2-009-hesiod-theogony-works-and-days]] are now read as the *literary apex* of a continuous tradition with attested Bronze Age roots, not as Iron Age innovations.
- **Documents produced.** Chadwick, *The Decipherment of Linear B* (1958); Ventris & Chadwick, *Documents in Mycenaean Greek* (1956; 2nd ed. 1973); Chadwick, *The Mycenaean World* (1976).
- **Cult-substrate texts.** The Linear B tablets themselves are administrative-economic records (offerings to deities, rations to personnel, herd-counts) rather than religious literature, but they attest the *system of cult* operating in the palace economy.

## Disputes
- **Reading of specific theonyms.** The decipherment of the script is uncontested. Specific identifications remain debated:
  - **Apollo's Mycenaean attestation** is heavily contested. *Pa-ja-wo* (Paean) is a divine name later treated as an Apollonian epithet, but whether the Mycenaean Paean *is* Apollo (or rather a precursor figure later absorbed into Apollo) is open.
  - **Athena's reading.** *a-ta-na po-ti-ni-ja* is consensus-read as "Mistress Athena" (the Knossos tablet KN V 52), but a minority reading takes it as a toponym ("Mistress of Athana / Athens") with an unspecified mistress. Most scholars accept the Athena reading.
  - **Ares.** *e-nu-wa-ri-jo* corresponds to the later Apollonian / Aresian epithet Enyalios; whether "Ares" as a separate deity is attested in Linear B is unsettled (some readings of *a-re* are debated).
- **Continuity vs. rupture across the Greek Dark Age.** That Mycenaean theonyms reappear in Homer is uncontested. Whether the *cult content* — myth, ritual, theological conceptions — is continuous, or whether only the names survive as templates filled with new content after the Dark Age, is debated (Burkert 1985 §1.3 surveys positions; he leans toward significant continuity in cult).
- **Dionysus.** The Linear B attestation of Dionysus was deeply surprising and overturned a major prior scholarly consensus (Rohde, Otto, Nilsson) that treated Dionysus as a late importation from Thrace or Phrygia. The attestation is *secure* (Pylos tablet Xa 102, Khania tablet KH Gq 5); but how to integrate it into the broader picture of Dionysiac cult is still being worked out.
