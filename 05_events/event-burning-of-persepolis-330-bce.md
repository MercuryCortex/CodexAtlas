# Burning of Persepolis (330 BCE)

[[alexander-the-great|Alexander III]]'s destruction of the Achaemenid ceremonial capital of Persepolis (Pārsa) in the spring of 330 BCE — the symbolic-political climax of the Macedonian conquest of Persia. The ancient sources (Arrian, Plutarch, Diodorus, Quintus Curtius) divide on motive: deliberate Greek revenge for Xerxes's 480 BCE burning of the Athenian Acropolis (the Persian-Wars revenge frame), drunken caprice incited by the Athenian courtesan Thaïs (the Plutarchan story), or controlled symbolic destruction of the Achaemenid royal-religious center (Briant 2002's more political reading). The Apadana, the Treasury, the residential palaces were burned; archaeological evidence (visible burn-layer; melted bronze; collapsed columns) confirms the literary tradition. The destruction is the proximate symbolic event of the Achaemenid Persian Empire's political end and remains a load-bearing trauma in Persian (and especially Zoroastrian) cultural memory across the next 2,300 years.
