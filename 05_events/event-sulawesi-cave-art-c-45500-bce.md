# Sulawesi Cave Art (c. 45,500 BCE — and possibly earlier)

## What happened
A sequence of Pleistocene-age painted panels in limestone karst caves of South Sulawesi (Maros-Pangkep region, Indonesia), dated by uranium-thorium (U-Th) series on overlying coralloid speleothems ("cave popcorn") by a Griffith University–Indonesian BRIN team led by Maxime Aubert and Adam Brumm. Key dated panels:

- **Leang Tedongnge** — a near-life-size red-ochre rendering of a Sulawesi warty pig (*Sus celebensis*), with at least one additional pig and hand-stencils on the same panel. Minimum U-Th age 45,500 BP for the principal pig figure (Brumm et al. 2021 *Science Advances*). On publication this was the *world's earliest dated figurative art*.
- **Leang Bulu' Sipong 4** — a composite hunting scene depicting therianthropic (part-human, part-animal) figures pursuing wild pigs and anoa (Sulawesi dwarf buffalo) with what appear to be spears or ropes. Minimum U-Th age 43,900 BP (Aubert et al. 2019 *Nature*). On publication this was the *world's earliest dated narrative-figurative scene*.
- **Leang Karampuang** — a 2024 publication (Oktaviana et al. *Nature* 631) using laser-ablation U-series microsampling reported a minimum age of ~51,200 BP for a three-figure pig-and-human-hunter panel, pushing the threshold for known narrative art back further still and challenging earlier European-centric models that placed the cognitive emergence of figurative art in the European Aurignacian.

The Sulawesi finds, together with Bornean panels of comparable age (Aubert et al. 2018 *Nature*, the Lubang Jeriji Saléh figurative cattle), now anchor a Southeast Asian Pleistocene figurative-art horizon that *precedes or matches* the earliest European cave art (Chauvet ~36,000 BP; Hohle Fels mobiliary ~38,000 BP; El Castillo and other Iberian non-figurative ~64,000 BP red discs and stencils).

## Religious consequences
- **Therianthropic imagery at this depth is the principal religious-cognitive finding.** The Leang Bulu' Sipong 4 hunting scene depicts what appear to be human bodies with animal heads or animal-feature extensions (a beak, a tail) — figures that are *neither human nor animal* — engaged with prey. Such hybrid figures are conventionally interpreted in the cross-cultural ethnographic record as supernatural agents, ancestors, spirits, or shamanic transformations (Lewis-Williams & Pearce 2005). The Sulawesi panels therefore push back the documented presence of *non-naturalistic supernatural imagery* in human cognition to at least ~44,000 BCE — far earlier than the Aurignacian Löwenmensch of southwest Germany (~38,000 BCE) which had previously held that distinction. Caveat: "therianthrope = shaman" is an interpretive frame, not an unmediated reading.
- The narrative-scene composition (multiple figures in coordinated action toward a single goal) is the earliest dated evidence of pictorial *storytelling* — a precondition for and material trace of myth.
- The very-similar age of figurative art on opposite sides of Eurasia (Sulawesi ~46,000–51,000 BP; Iberia ~40,000+ BP) is now thought to reflect *parallel emergence* rather than diffusion from a single source, consistent with figurative cognition being a baseline capacity of behaviorally modern *Homo sapiens* by the Out-of-Africa wave c. 60,000–70,000 BP.

## Disputes
- **U-Th dating of overlying speleothems gives a *minimum* age** for the underlying paintings — the actual paintings could be older. Some critics (Slimak et al. 2018 critique of the Iberian Neanderthal-art dating using the same method) have argued for potential contamination and recommended caution in single-sample dates; the Sulawesi team has addressed this with multiple samples per panel and methodological refinements (laser ablation in the 2024 paper). The current consensus accepts the Sulawesi sequence as broadly Pleistocene but treats specific date assignments as minimum-age estimates pending wider sampling.
- **Whether the therianthropic figures are "supernatural" in any meaningful sense vs. costumed hunters wearing animal disguises** is interpretively underdetermined by the imagery alone. The Lewis-Williams shamanism framework (see [[event-chauvet-cave-paintings-c-32000-bce]]) is one reading; alternatives include hunting-disguise representation, totemic identification, and mythological-ancestor depiction. The investigation-posture position: the *category* "therianthrope" is securely attested; specific *interpretations* are not.
- **Authorship.** All Sulawesi Pleistocene art is currently attributed to *Homo sapiens*, but Sulawesi was occupied by other hominin populations earlier (Talepu stone tools ~194,000–118,000 BP, hominin uncertain); no skeletal remains have yet been directly associated with the painted panels.

## Connections
- [[event-chauvet-cave-paintings-c-32000-bce]] — *parallel-motif* (figurative cave art with therianthropic elements); explicitly *not* a direct connection. Sulawesi and Chauvet are ~12,000 km apart and ~12,000 years apart. The shared imagery indicates convergent or baseline human-cognitive figurative capacity, not transmission.
- [[event-lascaux-cave-paintings-c-17000-bce]] — *parallel-motif*; the Lascaux Shaft "Bird-Man" composite figure is a therianthrope in the same broad category as the Sulawesi hunters, separated by ~28,000 years and continents. Convergence of imagery, not lineage.
- [[event-hohle-fels-figurines-c-35000-bce]] — *parallel-motif*; the Löwenmensch of Hohlenstein-Stadel is a portable-art therianthrope (lion-headed human figure). Sulawesi pushes the painted-therianthrope horizon ~8,000+ years earlier than Hohlenstein-Stadel.
