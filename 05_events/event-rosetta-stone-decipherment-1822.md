# Rosetta Stone Decipherment

## What happened
The Rosetta Stone — a granodiorite stele bearing a decree issued at Memphis in 196 BCE under Ptolemy V, in three scripts (hieroglyphic, demotic, and Ancient Greek) — was found in July 1799 by French soldiers of Napoleon's Egyptian campaign at Fort Julien near Rosetta (Rashid). Captured by the British in 1801 (Treaty of Alexandria) and shipped to the British Museum (where it has remained from 1802). Thomas Young (1814–1819) partially deciphered the demotic and identified the royal cartouches. Jean-François Champollion (1790–1832) achieved the full hieroglyphic decipherment, announced in his 27 September 1822 "Lettre à M. Dacier" — recognizing that hieroglyphic was a mixed phonetic-ideographic system, not the purely symbolic-philosophical script that Renaissance and early-modern Egyptologists (drawing on Horapollo, Athanasius Kircher) had imagined.

## Religious consequences
Champollion's breakthrough opened the entire Egyptian religious literature — Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts, Book of the Dead, hymns to Amun-Ra and the Aten, the Book of Gates, the Book of Amduat — to systematic philological reading. Within a generation, Egyptology produced editions of these religious corpora that would shape (a) comparative-religion's understanding of ancient Mesopotamian-Egyptian religious-mythological substrates, (b) the reception of the Pyramid Texts and Book of the Dead as Phase-1 religious-literary witnesses for the Codex Atlas vault. The 19th-century Egyptology revolution is the foundational *modern-recovery* event for Phase 1 Egyptian material in this vault.

## Documents in play
All Phase 1 Egyptian documents (Pyramid Texts, Coffin Texts, Book of the Dead, Great Hymn to the Aten, Amarna Letters Egyptian-language portions) entered modern legibility via the Champollion-onward decipherment.

## Disputes
- **Young vs. Champollion priority**: a long-running scholarly dispute. Young made critical contributions (especially on demotic and the recognition of foreign-name cartouches); Champollion made the decisive hieroglyphic breakthrough. The British-French priority dispute had a national-rivalry element. Modern consensus: both contributed, Champollion's was the decisive systematic decipherment.
