# Spanish Conquest of Tenochtitlan (1519–1521)

## What happened
Hernán Cortés landed at Veracruz in April 1519 with ~500 Spaniards, marched inland accumulating indigenous allies (especially Tlaxcalans, enemies of the Mexica), entered Tenochtitlan in November 1519 as Motecuhzoma's guest, took the tlatoani hostage, was driven out in the *Noche Triste* (June 1520), regrouped with Tlaxcalan support and besieged Tenochtitlan, taking the city block by block from May to August 1521. The last Mexica resistance under Cuauhtémoc fell on 13 August 1521. The city, the temple precinct, and the cultic infrastructure were destroyed; Mexico City was built on the ruins of Tenochtitlan with the cathedral atop the demolished Templo Mayor.

## Religious consequences
- **Destruction of the Mesoamerican state cult**: the Templo Mayor (the great twin-pyramid of Huitzilopochtli and Tlaloc) and its priesthood were destroyed; the codex libraries of central Mexico were burned in the immediate aftermath and through the 16th-c. Franciscan-Dominican missionizing campaign.
- **Missionary documentation**: Bernardino de Sahagún (Franciscan, in Mexico from 1529) recognized that the destruction was erasing the ethnographic record and embarked on the long collaborative project that became the *Florentine Codex* — Nahuatl-Spanish encyclopedia of Mexica life — preserving substantial pre-conquest religious material under colonial conditions.
- **Syncretism**: surviving Mesoamerican religious patterns syncretize with Spanish Catholicism (Virgin of Guadalupe = Tonantzin; cult of the saints integrating local deity-functions; calendrical and ritual continuities).
- **Periodization**: 1521 is the conventional end-date for pre-Columbian Mesoamerican religion as a self-sustaining state-supported tradition.

## Disputes
- The "smallpox plus steel plus Tlaxcalan-alliance" causation of Cortés's victory.
- The Sahaguntine "ethnographic vs. evangelizing" tension in the Florentine Codex's preservation of indigenous material.
