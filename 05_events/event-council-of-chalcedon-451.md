# Council of Chalcedon (451)

## What happened
The fourth ecumenical council, convened by Emperor Marcian and Pulcheria, attended by ~520 bishops (the largest ancient council). The council adopted the *Chalcedonian Definition* — Christ is "one *prosopon* (person) in two natures (*physeis*), divine and human, without confusion, change, division, or separation." The Definition rejected both the radical Antiochene position (separating the two natures) and the radical Alexandrian position (fusing them into one *physis*, hence "Mono-physite").

## Religious consequences
- Stabilized the Christological formula for what would become Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant Christianity ("Chalcedonian" Christianity).
- *Triggered the Oriental Orthodox schism* — the Coptic, Syriac (Syrian Orthodox), Armenian, and Ethiopian churches rejected the Definition (preferring Cyril of Alexandria's "one nature of God the Word incarnate") and formed the parallel "Non-Chalcedonian" / Miaphysite tradition that persists to today. This is the *first lasting major schism* in Christianity, anticipating the East-West schism of 1054 and the Reformation of the 16th century.
- The Council also issued Canon 28 elevating the see of Constantinople to a status equal with Rome — the seed of the long East-West Roman-Constantinopolitan rivalry.

## Disputes
- **Reception.** Chalcedon was contested for centuries within the East; multiple imperial attempts at compromise (Henotikon of Zeno, 482; Three Chapters condemnation, 553) failed to restore unity.
