# Stonehenge Construction (c. 3000–1600 BCE)

## What happened
Stonehenge on Salisbury Plain in Wiltshire is the most-studied prehistoric monument in the world and was constructed in a long, multi-phase sequence spanning ~1,400 years. Current phasing (Parker Pearson 2012 and subsequent Stonehenge Riverside Project publications; broadly consistent with Cleal et al. 1995 *Stonehenge in its Landscape* and Darvill 2006):

- **Stage 1 (c. 3000–2900 BCE).** Circular ditch and bank enclosure (~110 m diameter), 56 Aubrey Holes around its inner circumference (some apparently containing the earliest bluestone settings; Parker Pearson et al. 2009), and substantial cremation-burial deposits (~150–240 individuals over a few centuries) — Stonehenge in this phase is the **largest known late-Neolithic cremation cemetery in Britain**.
- **Stage 2 (c. 2900–2600 BCE).** Continued cremation burial; possible timber settings.
- **Stage 3 (c. 2600–2400 BCE).** First substantial stone setting: the bluestones from the Preseli Hills of western Wales (~230 km away — the most distant stone source for any megalith of its date in Europe) arranged in an arc or oval configuration. Source-specific quarry identification at Carn Goedog and Craig Rhos-y-felin (Parker Pearson et al. 2015, 2019).
- **Stage 4 (c. 2500–2400 BCE).** The great sarsen-stone phase — the iconic outer sarsen circle (originally 30 uprights with 30 lintels, mortise-and-tenon and tongue-and-groove jointed) and the inner sarsen trilithons (five horseshoe-arranged trilithons, the tallest >7 m). Sarsens sourced to West Woods near Marlborough, ~25 km north (Nash et al. 2020 *Science Advances*).
- **Stage 5 (c. 2400–2200 BCE).** Bluestone rearrangement into the inner-bluestone-circle and bluestone-horseshoe configurations.
- **Stage 6 (c. 2200–1600 BCE).** Final refurbishments of bluestones; the Y and Z holes (never filled with stones — possibly a planned but abandoned modification); accumulation of Bronze Age burials in surrounding barrow cemeteries.

The Aubrey-hole and ditch-bank cremation deposits were the discovery of the Stonehenge Riverside Project (2003–2009) that fundamentally re-framed the monument: Stonehenge is not principally a temple, observatory, or healing-site — although it has elements of each — but is *primarily a cemetery*, and specifically a cemetery of a small elite kindred over centuries. Mid-2010s ancient-DNA work (Snoeck et al. 2018 *Scientific Reports*) found that some of the cremated individuals likely originated in west Wales — the same Preseli region as the bluestones.

The famous *solstitial alignment* — the axis from the entrance through the Heel Stone aligned to the summer-solstice sunrise (and conversely the winter-solstice sunset along the reverse axis) — is well-established and is part of the broader Salisbury Plain monumental landscape (Durrington Walls is reciprocally oriented, with its Avenue aligned to the winter-solstice sunrise). The current best reading (Parker Pearson 2012) is that Stonehenge was a *place of the dead* and Durrington Walls a *place of the living*, ritually linked by the Avenue and the River Avon, with the winter-solstice axis as the principal sacred-temporal organizer rather than the summer solstice (a substantial revision of the older "Druidic summer-solstice ceremony" tradition, which in any case is a 17th–18th-c. Stukeley invention not an ancient practice).

Inscribed UNESCO World Heritage 1986 (jointly with Avebury).

## Religious consequences
- **Stonehenge is principally a mortuary monument.** The pre-stone-circle Stonehenge of c. 3000–2500 BCE is one of the largest cremation cemeteries in the Neolithic British record. The stone-monument Stonehenge that succeeds it incorporates and monumentalizes the earlier cremation-burial significance. The recurrent motif of *ancestor-and-place* documented in the Natufian → PPNB sequence (see [[event-natufian-burial-traditions-c-12500-9500-bce]], [[event-jericho-pre-pottery-neolithic-c-9000-bce]]) is reattested in independent form on the other side of the European continent, ~5,000 years later — not as continuous transmission, but as a recurring human-cultural pattern in pre-state societies.
- **The bluestone transport from Preseli to Salisbury Plain** is one of the most-discussed feats of organized labor in the prehistoric record. Whatever its specific symbolic meaning (and many have been proposed: ancestral homeland of part of the population; the healing-spring association of the Preseli bluestones in Darvill & Wainwright 2008's reading; political-territorial integration via long-distance procurement; symbolic mountain-brought-to-plain), the bluestone movement evidences that the Stonehenge community was capable of, and committed to, ~230-km-distance long-term coordinated material procurement.
- **The solstitial-axis orientation** materially evidences astronomical-temporal-ritual practice. The specific religious content of that practice is not recoverable from the monument alone; the post-medieval Druidic association is an 18th-c. Stukeley fabrication unrelated to Neolithic religion. (The original Iron Age Druids documented in classical sources [Caesar, Tacitus, Strabo] postdate Stonehenge by ~1,500+ years.)
- **Stonehenge anchors a wider landscape** of henges (Avebury, Durrington Walls, Marden, Mount Pleasant), barrows, the Avenue, the Cursus, and other earthworks; the monument is one node in a managed *sacred landscape*, not a standalone shrine.

## Documents in play
- None (Stonehenge construction substantially predates writing reaching Britain; writing arrives only with Roman conquest c. 43 CE, ~1,600+ years after the last Stonehenge construction phase).

## Disputes
- **The "Druids built Stonehenge" tradition** is an 18th-century antiquarian fabrication (John Aubrey's speculation, elaborated by William Stukeley) that has no basis in archaeology. The Druids are an Iron Age phenomenon (~500 BCE–100 CE) whose religious-cultural identity postdates Stonehenge construction by ~1,500+ years. Modern Druidic and pagan groups use Stonehenge as a ritual site (especially at the summer solstice) — this is a 20th-c. revival phenomenon, not an Iron Age or Neolithic continuity.
- **Astronomical-observatory interpretations.** Gerald Hawkins 1965 *Stonehenge Decoded* and Fred Hoyle 1972 *From Stonehenge to Modern Cosmology* argued for sophisticated astronomical-calculator functions (predicting lunar eclipses, etc.). Most current archaeology accepts the major solstitial alignments but is skeptical of the more elaborate eclipse-prediction claims; the monument's primary function is not principally astronomical.
- **The Darvill-Wainwright "healing place" reading.** Darvill & Wainwright 2008 (*Antiquity* 82) argued that the bluestones were brought from Preseli because they were locally regarded as healing stones, and that Stonehenge was a Neolithic-Chalcolithic equivalent to a healing shrine (citing the high proportion of trauma-and-pathology evidence in Stonehenge-region burials, including the "Amesbury Archer" with severe knee infection). Parker Pearson and the Riverside Project team are skeptical of the specific healing-shrine reading, instead emphasizing the place-of-the-dead interpretation. Both readings have evidence; neither is fully established.
- **Bluestone transport mechanism.** Whether the bluestones were transported by humans (the conventional view, supported by recent quarry-identification work at Preseli), by glacial-erratic transport in the Pleistocene with later human gathering (the Kellaway 1971 hypothesis, revived periodically), or some combination, is debated. Current consensus is *human transport* but the glacial-erratic alternative retains some support.
- **Pseudo-archaeological accretions.** Stonehenge has the longest and most varied pseudo-archaeological literature of any prehistoric monument (von Däniken's "ancient astronauts," various Atlantis-derivative readings, the popular ley-lines tradition from Alfred Watkins 1925 onward, etc.). The Atlas treats these as separate reception-history phenomena belonging to modern alternative-history, not as evidence about the monument's construction and use.

## Connections
- [[event-malta-temples-c-3600-2500-bce]] — *parallel-motif*; both are European Neolithic / Chalcolithic megalithic ritual traditions. The Maltese temples are ~800 years older than the earliest Stonehenge stone phase. No documented direct connection; the two traditions are typologically distinct (Maltese: enclosed apsidal-multi-lobed temples; Stonehenge: open-air circular henges with standing-stone settings).
- [[event-natufian-burial-traditions-c-12500-9500-bce]] and [[event-jericho-pre-pottery-neolithic-c-9000-bce]] — *parallel-motif* on the recurring human-cultural pattern of *ancestor-and-place* mortuary monument-making. ~6,000–8,000 years and ~3,000 km separate the Salisbury Plain cremation cemetery from the southern Levantine intramural-burial / plastered-skull tradition. No documented continuity.
- [[event-thera-eruption-c-1600-bce]] — *parallel-milieu* only in the broad sense (both are Late Bronze Age phenomena ~1600 BCE; Stonehenge's final phase ends roughly when Thera erupts; no direct connection).
- **Later European Iron Age sanctuaries and Druidic / Celtic religion** ([[tradition-celtic]]) — *parallel-motif* of pre-Roman British ritual landscape, with explicit caveat that the 18th-c. Druidic-Stonehenge association is *not* an attested continuity but an antiquarian invention.
