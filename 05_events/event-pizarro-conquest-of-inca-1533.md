# Pizarro's Conquest of the Inca Empire

## What happened
In 1532, a Spanish expedition of about 168 men led by Francisco Pizarro arrived in the Andean highlands during the aftermath of a major Inca civil war (between the half-brothers Huáscar and Atahualpa). At Cajamarca, November 16, 1532, the Spaniards captured the victorious Inca emperor Atahualpa in a surprise attack on a ceremonial meeting; held him for ransom (an enormous quantity of gold and silver); and despite receiving the ransom, executed him on July 26, 1533. The Spaniards then marched on Cusco (entered November 1533), installed a puppet Inca (Manco Inca Yupanqui, who later revolted 1536), and progressively consolidated control over Tawantinsuyu. The neo-Inca state of Vilcabamba (founded by Manco Inca's faction) held out in the eastern Andes until 1572, when the last Inca emperor Túpac Amaru was captured and executed.

## Religious consequences
- *Inca state cult destroyed.* The Coricancha (Temple of the Sun) in Cusco was stripped of gold and converted into the Convent of Santo Domingo; the *Punchao* (golden disk of Inti) was melted down. The imperial Sun cult ended.
- *Andean *huaca* religion suppressed.* Local Andean religious tradition — densely organized around local *huaca* (sacred-being) cults — was targeted for systematic destruction in the extirpation campaigns initiated by the Third Council of Lima (1582–83) and accelerated under Francisco de Ávila and others from ~1608 onward. The Huarochirí Manuscript is the principal Indigenous-language document of pre-conquest Andean religion to survive — preserved *because* Ávila commissioned it as ammunition for his extirpation campaigns.
- *Catholic missionary expansion.* Dominicans, Franciscans, Augustinians, Jesuits arrived in succession. The Cuzcan Catholic establishment combined Inca-religious-political symbolism with Catholic devotion in complex syncretic forms; the Virgen del Cerro / Pachamama-Mary syncretism is one of the most-studied cases.
- *Loss of *quipu* records.* The Inca administrative-religious system used knotted-string *quipu* for record-keeping, including religious-ritual records; the vast majority of these were destroyed in the conquest period. Only ~600 quipu survive.
- *Indigenous chronicling.* Mestizo and Indigenous chroniclers Felipe Guaman Poma de Ayala (*Nueva corónica y buen gobierno*, ~1615) and Garcilaso de la Vega *El Inca* (*Comentarios reales*, 1609) preserved Indigenous historical-religious memory under colonial conditions.

## Documents in play
- Produced: Huarochirí Manuscript; Guaman Poma; Garcilaso; the Cronistas (Cieza de León, Polo de Ondegardo, Cobo, Acosta, Molina, Murúa).
- Affected: Inca *quipu* records; pre-conquest oral cosmogonic and dynastic tradition.

## Disputes
- **Demographic collapse magnitude.** Andean population decline 1532–1700 is variably estimated as 50%–90%+ from disease, war, forced labor (especially mita-system silver mining at Potosí), and social disruption.
- **Atahualpa's execution.** The legal-religious justification for executing the captive Inca emperor after the ransom had been paid has been a focus of moral-historical reflection from Las Casas onward.
