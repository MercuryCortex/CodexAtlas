# Pike et al. 2018: Neanderthal-attributed cave art at La Pasiega, Maltravieso, Ardales

## What happened
Hoffmann, Standish, García-Diez, Pike and colleagues published in *Science* (22 February 2018) uranium-thorium dates on carbonate crusts overlying painted imagery at three Iberian cave sites — **La Pasiega** (Cantabria; red linear sign), **Maltravieso** (Extremadura; hand stencil GS3b), and **Ardales** (Andalusia; red curtain-like flowstone painting). All three returned minimum ages ≥ 64,800 BP. Anatomically-modern humans arrived in Iberia ~45,000–42,000 BP; therefore, on these dates, *the imagery must have been produced by Neanderthals*. The same team also published a companion paper (*Science Advances*) reporting pierced, pigmented marine shells from Cueva de los Aviones at ~115,000 BP — Neanderthal symbolic-object use ~70,000 years before the cave art.

If correct, the result establishes Neanderthal symbolic behavior independent of contact with *Homo sapiens* — a major reframing of pre-*sapiens* cognition. If incorrect, it is a cautionary tale about carbonate-crust dating methodology.

## Religious consequences
- **Origins of religious / symbolic cognition.** The shamanism hypothesis (see [[shamanism-hypothesis]]) is built primarily on *sapiens* Upper Paleolithic evidence; if Neanderthals were producing painted symbols ≥ 64,800 BP, the cognitive prerequisites for ritual / symbolic / mythopoeic thought are at least 20,000 years older than the earliest *sapiens* cave art at Sulawesi (~45,500 BP — see [[event-sulawesi-cave-art-c-45500-bce]]).
- **Anthropological boundary problem.** The clean division between *sapiens* symbolic modernity and Neanderthal cognitive limitation — once a foundational assumption of comparative-religion historiography — becomes untenable on these dates.
- **Reception:** if the dates hold, every theological / philosophical claim that grounds religion in "uniquely human" cognition needs adjustment to "uniquely *hominin*."

## Disputes
This is **methodologically contested** and the user should know that before citing the dates.
- **Slimak et al. 2018** challenge the U-Th methodology on the grounds that carbonate crusts above pigment do not reliably bracket the pigment's age — uranium loss or detrital thorium contamination can yield artificially old crust ages.
- **Pearce & Bonneau 2018** raise additional concerns about sample selection and the assumption that the crust is depositionally continuous with the painting underneath.
- **Hoffmann et al. 2018 (reply)** defended the methodology and the result.
- As of the cutoff: the dates are *published, peer-reviewed, and not retracted*, but they are *not consensus*. The proper register is "if the Pike et al. 2018 dating holds, then …" rather than "Neanderthals painted at 64,800 BP."
