# Léo Taxil Anti-Masonic Hoax (1885–1897)

## What happened
A **twelve-year sustained literary-and-publishing hoax** by the French author **Léo Taxil** (born Marie-Joseph-Antoine-Gabriel Jogand-Pagès, 1854–1907), constituting **the single most spectacular Catholic anti-Masonic propaganda campaign in modern history** — and concluding with **Taxil's public confession on 19 April 1897 at the Salle de la Société de Géographie in Paris** that the entire campaign had been a deliberate fabrication intended to demonstrate Catholic credulity.

## The hoax structure

1. **1885** — Taxil, previously a militant anti-Catholic free-thought polemicist, publicly converts to Catholicism in highly-publicized fashion.
2. **1886 onward** — Taxil publishes the *Mystères de la Franc-Maçonnerie* and a stream of subsequent anti-Masonic exposés, **claiming inside knowledge** of Masonic Satanic practices.
3. **1887** — Pope Leo XIII grants Taxil a private papal audience.
4. **1891 (death of Albert Pike)** — Taxil intensifies the campaign, focused on the late **Albert Pike** (Supreme Commander of the Scottish Rite, Southern Jurisdiction, 1859–1891). Taxil claims Pike had founded a secret Palladium Lodge in Charleston, South Carolina, devoted to Luciferian worship of the "Light-Bearer." **Pike's actual *Morals and Dogma* (1871)** contained passages on *light-as-symbol* that Taxil quoted out-of-context to support the Palladium-Lodge fabrication.
5. **1892–1894** — *Le Diable au XIXe siècle* (multi-volume) — ostensibly by "Dr. Bataille," a Taxil pseudonym. Lurid Satanic-Palladium-Lodge accounts.
6. **1895** — *Mémoires d'une ex-Palladiste* — ostensibly by *Diana Vaughan*, a Catholic convert from Satanism whom Taxil claimed had previously been the high-priestess of the Palladium Lodge and Pike's spiritual heir. **Diana Vaughan was entirely fictional.**
7. **1895–1896** — Taxil organizes an anti-Masonic Congress in Trent and arranges for "Diana Vaughan" to make public appearances (impersonated by a typist).
8. **19 April 1897** — Taxil convokes a public meeting at the Salle de la Société de Géographie in Paris, **ostensibly to introduce Diana Vaughan in person**. Instead, **Taxil publicly confesses the entire 12-year campaign as a deliberate hoax**, naming his methods, his motivations (to embarrass the Catholic Church), and his collaborators. The meeting ends in uproar.
9. **Post-confession (1897 onward)** — Catholic anti-Masonic apologetics is severely damaged but does *not* abandon the underlying anti-Masonic posture. Taxil dies in 1907.

## Religious-and-cultural consequences

- **Severe damage to Catholic anti-Masonic literature.** The Taxil hoax remained the most-cited Catholic anti-Masonic source for 12 years (1885–1897); its sudden revelation as fabrication retroactively discredited a generation of European Catholic anti-Masonic apologetics.
- **The Pike-Palladium narrative survived the hoax.** Despite the 1897 confession, the Pike-Palladium-Lodge-Luciferian narrative entered subsequent **conspiracy literature** — including some 20th-21st-c. far-right and Catholic-traditionalist literature still citing Pike-Palladium claims as fact.
- **Established the modern conspiracy-literature template** — sustained fabrication, public credulity, retroactive embarrassment. The Taxil pattern recurs in subsequent constructions: the *Protocols of the Elders of Zion* (1903 — the most-consequential subsequent hoax in the genre); the [[event-priory-of-sion-fabrication|Priory of Sion *Dossiers Secrets*]] (1956 onward).
- **Baphomet-and-Templar-and-Pike contagion.** Taxil's narrative recycled [[joseph-von-hammer-purgstall|Hammer-Purgstall]] / [[eliphas-levi|Lévi]] Baphomet-Templar material, projecting it forward onto Pike and the Scottish Rite. The hoax thereby substantially contributed to the late-19th-c. mass-cultural conflation of *Templars + Masonry + Baphomet + Lucifer-worship* that subsequently feeds 20th-c. conspiracy literature.

## Documents in play
- Taxil's own publications (1885–1897), all in the hoax.
- The 19 April 1897 confession-speech (recorded; published in subsequent reportage and historical scholarship).
- Contemporary Catholic-press coverage (mostly credulous through 1896; rapidly reversing post-confession).

## Disputes
- **Was Taxil ever sincerely Catholic?** Almost certainly not. His 1885 conversion was probably pre-planned as part of the hoax.
- **Did Taxil have collaborators?** Several — including the journalist Charles Hacks ("Dr. Bataille") who actually wrote substantial portions of *Le Diable au XIXe siècle*. The 1897 confession named some collaborators.
- **Why the Vatican accepted Taxil's claims.** Late-19th-c. Catholic-anti-Masonic apologetics was substantially predisposed to credulity about Masonic Satanism; Taxil exploited the predisposition with skill.
