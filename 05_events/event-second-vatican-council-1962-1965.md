# Second Vatican Council (1962–1965)

## What happened
Twenty-first ecumenical council of the Roman Catholic Church, convoked by Pope John XXIII in October 1962 and concluded under Pope Paul VI in December 1965. Four working sessions (autumn 1962, 1963, 1964, 1965) produced four constitutions, three declarations, and nine decrees. Major outcomes: (1) liturgical reform allowing vernacular Mass (replacing the previously universal Latin Tridentine Mass); (2) recognition of the Church as the "People of God" (*Lumen Gentium*) — collegial rather than purely monarchical-papal ecclesiology; (3) revised teaching on divine revelation (*Dei Verbum*) — Scripture and Tradition together rather than Scripture-against-Tradition or Tradition-above-Scripture; (4) declaration on religious freedom (*Dignitatis Humanae*) — official Catholic recognition of state non-establishment as the right model; (5) declaration on non-Christian religions (*Nostra Aetate*) — explicit rejection of antisemitism, recognition of truth-elements in Judaism, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism.

## Religious consequences
The most consequential Catholic event of the 20th century. Reshaped Catholic liturgy worldwide; opened Catholic theology to Protestant and Eastern Orthodox dialogue; transformed Catholic-Jewish relations (*Nostra Aetate* §4 substantially repudiated long-standing Catholic anti-Judaic teaching, especially the "deicide" charge); opened Catholic philosophy to dialogue with non-Christian religions; created enduring intra-Catholic conservative-progressive tensions (the "spirit of Vatican II" vs. "continuity hermeneutic" of Pope Benedict XVI).

## Documents in play
The 16 documents of Vatican II (4 constitutions, 9 decrees, 3 declarations) are the principal output.

## Disputes
- **Reception and interpretation**: ongoing intra-Catholic dispute over the nature and extent of Vatican II's discontinuity with prior Catholic teaching. "Continuity hermeneutic" (Ratzinger / Benedict XVI) vs. "rupture / spirit of Vatican II" (progressive theologians) is the dominant interpretive frame.
- **Traditionalist rejection**: the Society of St. Pius X (Marcel Lefebvre, 1970 onward) rejected major Vatican II documents, especially on religious freedom and ecumenism; the resulting schism remains partially unresolved.
