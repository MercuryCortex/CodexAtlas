# Martyrdom of Perpetua and Felicity (203 CE)

## What happened
On **March 7, 203 CE** — the birthday of Caesar Geta, son of [[septimius-severus]] — six Christian catechumens were executed at Carthage in the arena: **Vibia Perpetua** (a 22-year-old well-educated noblewoman, nursing infant), her enslaved companion **Felicity** (eight months pregnant when arrested; she gave birth in prison two days before execution), and four male companions (Saturus, Saturninus, Revocatus, Secundulus). They had been arrested some weeks before as catechumens, baptized in prison, and refused multiple offers to recant.

The execution proceeded by mauling by wild beasts (a wild cow tossed Perpetua and Felicity; Saturus was bitten by a leopard) followed by *iugulatio* — beheading or throat-cutting — by gladiators. The *Passio Perpetuae* records Perpetua, the noblewoman, helping to guide the unsteady sword of the young, frightened gladiator who could not finish her execution cleanly.

## Religious consequences
- **The *Passio Perpetuae et Felicitatis***: produced in immediate response (~+203/+210), preserving Perpetua's own prison diary — the **earliest extant Christian text known to be written by a woman** and one of the most theologically rich martyrologies of the early period.
- **North African Christian formation**: the cult of Perpetua and Felicity is foundational to Latin African Christianity; Augustine preached on their feast day annually.
- **Severan persecution context**: contemporary with the (probably-spurious) Severan rescript banning conversion to Christianity and Judaism (HA *Severus* 17.1); the Carthaginian episode is the most documented persecution event under Severus.
- **Women's authority debate**: Perpetua's diary and visions, the *Passio*'s elevation of women martyrs, the New-Prophecy-friendly framing — all data points for the early-3rd-c. negotiation of women's religious authority in North Africa.

## Disputes
- **Date**: March 7, 203 — the *Passio*'s internal datings make this secure.
- **Severan-rescript context**: the HA *Severus* 17.1 rescript is contested for historicity.
- **Theological character of the *Passio***: New-Prophecy-friendly (Heine 1989) vs. proto-orthodox-redaction (Trevett 1996, Heffernan 2012).
- **Authorship of the framing narrative**: Tertullian (older view) vs. anonymous (modern mainstream).
