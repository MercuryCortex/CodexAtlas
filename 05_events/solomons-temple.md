# Solomon's Temple — First Temple Jerusalem

**Stub.** Solomon's Temple (1 Kings 5–8; 2 Chronicles 2–7), traditionally dated c. 970–960 BCE, was the first permanent Jewish Temple in Jerusalem, constructed by Solomon according to plans said to have been received by his father David through divine revelation (1 Chronicles 28:19: "All this, in writing, from the hand of the Lord, He made clear to me — all the work of this plan").

**The architecture-language connection**: The Temple is described as built entirely "according to the word of the Lord" — every measurement, proportion, and material specified through divine instruction transmitted in words. The Temple is not merely a building for divine worship; it is the physical instantiation of divine speech. Every dimension is a frozen word of God. The Temple is a text made of stone.

This makes Solomon's Temple the most prominent ancient example of the letter-as-cosmos pattern (see [[alphabet-letter-as-cosmos]]): architecture as scripture, building as divine speech made permanent.

**Egyptian architectural parallels**: Solomon's Temple incorporates features (proportions, cedar wood from Lebanon, Phoenician craftsmen) that show Egyptian and Phoenician architectural influence — the Temple's construction was an international project. This connects to the vault's [[transmission-amnesia]] pattern: the physical structure of Hebrew sacred architecture carries Egyptian and Canaanite technical traditions into the Israelite context.

**Destruction**: The First Temple was destroyed by Nebuchadnezzar II of Babylon in 586 BCE, an event that generated the major theological crisis documented in Lamentations, Psalms 137, and Ezekiel.
