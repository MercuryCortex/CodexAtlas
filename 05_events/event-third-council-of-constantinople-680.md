# Third Council of Constantinople (680–681)

The Sixth Ecumenical Council (per Eastern and Latin reckoning), convened by Emperor Constantine IV. The council **condemned [[doctrine-monothelitism|Monothelitism]]** (the one-will Christology promulgated by [[heraclius-byzantine-emperor|Heraclius]]'s *Ekthesis* of 638) and affirmed **Dyothelitism** — two wills and two operations in Christ, corresponding to his two natures per [[event-council-of-chalcedon-451|Chalcedon]]. The council posthumously anathematized **Pope Honorius I** for his initial endorsement of Monothelitism — a fact that produced enormous controversy in later Roman Catholic theology (notably at Vatican I 1870, where Honorius's case was the principal historical counter-example invoked against papal infallibility doctrine). The council vindicated the theological position of [[maximus-the-confessor|Maximus the Confessor]] and Pope Martin I, both of whom had been imperially persecuted under Heraclius's grandson Constans II for resisting Monothelitism. The council marks the **end of the Christological controversies** that had run since the 4th century.
