# Council of Vienne

## What happened
The **15th ecumenical council** of the Catholic Church, convened by Pope [[pope-clement-v]] at Vienne (south-eastern France) from October 1311 to May 1312. The principal item on the agenda was the suppression of the [[tradition-knights-templar|Order of the Temple]], on charges accumulated through the four-year trial process ([[event-trial-of-templars-1307-1314]]). The Council ultimately *declined* to formally condemn the Order as heretical — the proceedings lacked sufficient evidence convincing to the assembled bishops, especially the non-French majority. Clement V, under direct pressure from Philip IV (who attended in person in March 1312), bypassed the council's reluctance and suppressed the Order administratively by apostolic provision (*per modum provisionis*, not *per modum condemnationis*) in the bull ***Vox in excelso*** (22 March 1312). The bull's careful language acknowledged that the Order had not been *legally* convicted but suppressed it on grounds of public scandal and incapacity to recover its reputation.

Other Council business:
- **Beguines and the Free Spirit movement** — *Ad nostrum* condemned the *secta libertatis spiritus* (the "Heresy of the Free Spirit"), eight allegedly Beguine-Beghard antinomian theses. Marguerite Porete had been burned in Paris in 1310; *Ad nostrum* generalized the condemnation.
- **Cistercian-Franciscan apostolic-poverty dispute** — *Exivi de paradiso* moderated the Franciscan position.
- **Crusade planning** — a six-year tithe levied for a new crusade (never executed).
- **Theological condemnations** — including the propositions of Peter John Olivi (provisional).

## Religious consequences
- **End of the [[tradition-knights-templar|Templars]] as an institutional order.** Asset transfer to the [[tradition-hospitallers|Hospitallers]] (*Ad providam*, 2 May 1312), with significant royal-treasury skim in France. Iberian holdings preserved as the **Order of Christ** in Portugal (1319) and the **Order of Montesa** in Aragón (1317).
- **Demonstration of royal-over-papal power.** The Council's reluctance and Clement's circumvention of it demonstrate the political weakness of the Avignon papacy under French pressure.
- **Condemnation of the Free Spirit.** *Ad nostrum* and *Cum de quibusdam mulieribus* (the latter restricting Beguines) accelerate the suppression of the Beguine movement — Marguerite Porete's burning (1310) becomes paradigmatic. The Free Spirit's "becoming-God-through-self-annihilation" mysticism has resonances with Eckhart's later (and contested) language and with [[tradition-gnosticism|Gnostic]] gnosis-as-divinization motifs — though direct genealogical connection is not demonstrable.

## Documents in play
- *Vox in excelso* — the suppression bull.
- *Ad providam* — the asset-transfer bull.
- *Ad nostrum* — condemnation of the Free Spirit.
- The Vienne acts and decrees subsequently incorporated into the *Clementinae* (1317) — part of the *Corpus Iuris Canonici*.

## Disputes
- **Was the suppression valid in canon law?** Contemporary canonists were uneasy; *Vox in excelso* explicitly bypassed the requirement of conciliar conviction. The political-but-not-juridical character of the suppression has been a perennial subject of speculative-historical Templarism.
- **Was Marguerite Porete a "Free Spirit"?** Lerner 1972 *The Heresy of the Free Spirit* argued that the *secta libertatis spiritus* was largely an inquisitorial construct rather than an organized movement. *Ad nostrum's* eight theses do, however, identify specifiable doctrines.
