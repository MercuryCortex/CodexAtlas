# Massacre of Thessalonica (390)

In April 390 the population of Thessalonica rioted against the local Gothic garrison; the *magister militum* Buthericus was killed. [[theodosius-i|Emperor Theodosius]] ordered massive reprisal — variously reported as ~7,000 civilians killed in the hippodrome over several days. [[ambrose-of-milan|Bishop Ambrose of Milan]] then refused Theodosius communion until he publicly confessed and did penance. Theodosius eventually submitted, performing public penance in the cathedral of Milan, and was re-admitted to communion at Christmas 390. The episode is the **canonical late-antique precedent for episcopal authority over the Christian emperor** — invoked across the next millennium of medieval Latin-Christian church-state struggles (Henry IV at Canossa 1077, the Investiture Controversy, Becket vs Henry II, etc.). McLynn 1994 reassessed the older heroising narratives but confirmed the political-symbolic significance.
