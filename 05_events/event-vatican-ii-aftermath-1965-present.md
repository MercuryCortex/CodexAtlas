# Vatican II Aftermath (1965–present)

## What happened
The Second Vatican Council ([[event-second-vatican-council-1962-1965]]) concluded on 8 December 1965. The substantive event for the religious-historian, however, is not the four-year council but its sixty-year reception — the contested *implementation* of its sixteen documents across global Catholicism, the reform of the liturgy (the Pauline Missal of 1969 replacing the Tridentine Missal of 1570), the rise and partial-rapprochement of the traditionalist refusal (Marcel Lefebvre's Society of Saint Pius X founded 1970; 1988 episcopal consecrations without papal mandate triggering excommunication; partial 2009 lifting; ongoing canonical irregularity), the long succession of postconciliar popes (Paul VI 1963–1978, John Paul I 1978, John Paul II 1978–2005, Benedict XVI 2005–2013, Francis 2013–) each interpreting and implementing the council differently, and a series of major postconciliar documents from *Humanae Vitae* (1968) through *Laudato Si'* (2015) to *Traditionis Custodes* (2021).

## Religious consequences
- **Liturgical revolution and traditionalist backlash.** The Pauline Missal (1969) substantially restructured the Roman-rite Mass; the Tridentine Mass became a permitted exception. The SSPX (Lefebvre 1970), the FSSP (1988 split from SSPX), the broader "traditionalist" Catholic movement, and various Eastern Catholic and Anglican-ordinariate developments form the conservative response. Benedict XVI's *Summorum Pontificum* (2007) greatly expanded permission for the old Mass; Francis's *Traditionis Custodes* (2021) substantially reversed that expansion. The intra-Catholic conflict is ongoing.
- **Ecumenical and inter-religious opening.** *Nostra Aetate*'s declaration on non-Christian religions and *Unitatis Redintegratio*'s on ecumenism produced a half-century of substantive Catholic-Jewish, Catholic-Orthodox, Catholic-Protestant, Catholic-Muslim, and broader Catholic-non-Christian dialogues. The 1965 mutual lifting of the 1054 mutual excommunications between Rome and Constantinople (Athenagoras I–Paul VI) is the dramatic symbolic moment.
- **Hermeneutic of continuity vs. rupture.** Joseph Ratzinger ([[joseph-ratzinger-benedict-xvi]]), as Cardinal and as Pope Benedict XVI, articulated the "hermeneutic of reform in continuity" — Vatican II as in-continuity-with-prior-tradition. Progressive theologians (and the *Concilium* school) read the council as substantive rupture. The interpretation is the principal intra-Catholic theological dispute of the period.
- **Ressourcement legacy.** The mid-20th-c. *nouvelle théologie* / *ressourcement* movement ([[henri-de-lubac]], Daniélou, [[hans-urs-von-balthasar]], Congar, Rahner) provided much of the theological scaffolding for Vatican II and continues to shape postconciliar Catholic theology, both in its *Communio* (Balthasar / de Lubac / Ratzinger) and *Concilium* (Rahner / Küng / Schillebeeckx) wings.
- **Liberation theology and Latin-Americanization.** The Medellín CELAM conference (1968) applied Vatican II to Latin America; liberation theology (Gutiérrez, Boff, Sobrino) was a major postconciliar development sharply contested under John Paul II and now somewhat rehabilitated under [[pope-francis]] (himself a Latin American with Argentine *teología del pueblo* roots).
- **Sexual-abuse crisis (2002 Boston / *Spotlight*; 2010 Murphy/Ryan Irish reports; 2018 Pennsylvania grand jury; 2018 McCarrick scandal; ongoing).** Not directly a Vatican II consequence but emerges as a major late-20th-c./21st-c. Catholic event during the council's aftermath; transformed Catholic-laity relations and ecclesiology in unanticipated ways.

## Disputes
- **Continuity vs. rupture.** The principal hermeneutic frame for evaluating Vatican II's reception. Benedict XVI's continuity hermeneutic vs. progressive rupture-reading is unresolved.
- **Liturgy.** Old Mass / New Mass remains intensely contested; *Summorum Pontificum* / *Traditionis Custodes* whipsaw under successive popes signals that no settlement is yet established.
- **Synodality.** Francis's 2021–2024 Synod on Synodality is a major recent reorientation of Catholic governance toward more consultative-collegial process; conservative readers see it as further rupture; progressive readers as overdue Vatican-II realization. Disputed.
