# Solomonic Restoration of 1270

## What happened
In 1270 CE, **Yekuno Amlak**, a Shewan-Amhara claimant asserting direct Davidic-Solomonic descent through the legendary [[menelik-i-legendary]] (son of Solomon and Sheba), overthrew the last Zagwe king Yetbarak and assumed the throne of Ethiopia. The new dynasty styled itself the *Solomonic restoration* — framing the Zagwe rulers (1137–1270) as illegitimate Agaw-Cushitic usurpers and claiming continuity with a "true" Israelite-Solomonic royal lineage reaching back to King Solomon of Israel through Menelik I. The restoration was theologically grounded in (the still-developing) **[[phase-8-008-kebra-nagast]]** tradition, which would receive its surviving Ge'ez redaction by 1322 under Nebura'ed Yeshaq of Aksum but whose underlying narrative complex was already structurally present and politically operative.

Ethiopian tradition assigns a mediatory role in the transition to the monastic founders [[takla-haymanot]] and Iyasus Mo'a of Hayq Estifanos — particularly the *Kidan* ("Covenant") in which the church received one-third of Ethiopia's land in compensation for facilitating the transition. The historicity of this specific *Kidan* arrangement is disputed (Kaplan 1984; Taddesse Tamrat 1972) but the church *did* benefit materially from royal patronage in the Solomonic-restoration period.

## Religious consequences

1. **The Solomonic-genealogical-political complex is institutionalized.** Yekuno Amlak's claim to David-Solomon-Menelik descent becomes the constitutive legitimating frame for Ethiopian monarchy for the next 700 years — formalized as such in the 1955 Constitution under [[wolega-tafari-makonnen-haile-selassie]], which made the Solomonic descent claim a legal constitutional fact.

2. **The Kebra Nagast crystallizes.** The Ge'ez redaction of the *[[phase-8-008-kebra-nagast]]* in 1314–1322 is the textual codification of the Solomonic-restoration ideology. The text presents Ethiopia as the *new* chosen people (with the Ark of the Covenant transferred from Jerusalem to Aksum by Menelik I), the Ethiopian emperor as the Davidic-Solomonic descendant, and Ethiopian Christianity as the legitimate successor to Israelite election.

3. **A flowering of Ethiopian Christian-literary production.** The Solomonic period (1270–1527) is the great literary-theological flowering of medieval Ethiopia: [[giyorgis-of-sagla]]'s *Mashafa Mistir*, the *Fetha Nagast*, [[zara-yaqob-emperor]]'s extensive theological corpus, the Sinkessar's Ge'ez redaction, the development of indigenous saint-hagiographies (Gadla Lalibela, Gadla Takla Haymanot, Gadla Ewostatewos), the *Te'amre Maryam* miracle compendium.

4. **The southern frontier christianizes.** Takla Haymanot's Debre Libanos network in Shewa and Ewostatewos's networks in the north drive the Christianization of regions southward and eastward from the highland core.

5. **The institutional tension between Coptic Abuna and Ethiopian Ichege.** The Abuna (metropolitan, Coptic-appointed) and the Ichege (head of all Ethiopian monasteries, indigenous-elected) become the two heads of the Ethiopian church under Solomonic-period institutional development. The structure persists until autocephaly in 1959.

## Documents in play
- **Produced:** Solomonic royal chronicles; the Kebra Nagast Ge'ez redaction (1314–1322); the Ge'ez Fetha Nagast (~mid-15th c.); the Sinkessar Ge'ez redaction (~early-15th c.); all the great medieval Ethiopian theological-hagiographic literature.
- **Affected:** existing Zagwe-period charters preserved in monastic archives.

## Disputes
- **Historicity of the Davidic-Solomonic descent.** Legendary; constitutive ideology rather than verifiable history. The Zagwe-Solomonic transition is *politically* real but the *genealogical* claim is mythologically constituted.
- **Role of Takla Haymanot and Iyasus Mo'a in mediating the transition.** Traditional. Modern scholarship (Kaplan 1984) sees the role as hagiographically elaborated and probably post-hoc rather than contemporary with 1270.
- **The *Kidan* (Covenant) one-third-land grant.** Disputed historicity; probably hagiographic. The institutional pattern of substantial church land-holding in the Solomonic period is real, but the specific *Kidan* claim is post-hoc legitimation.
