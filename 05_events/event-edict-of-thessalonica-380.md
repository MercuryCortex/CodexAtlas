# Edict of Thessalonica (380)

## What happened
On 27 February 380, the joint emperors Theodosius I, Gratian, and Valentinian II issued from Thessalonica the edict *Cunctos populos*, addressed to the people of Constantinople, declaring Nicene Christianity (specifically the faith taught by Pope Damasus of Rome and by Bishop Peter of Alexandria — i.e., consubstantialist orthodoxy) the official religion of the Roman state. The edict commands that all peoples ruled by the emperors "should practice that religion which the divine Peter the Apostle transmitted to the Romans" and labels dissenters "demented and insane." The edict is preserved as Theodosian Code 16.1.2.

## Religious consequences
- Christianity transitioned from a *favored* religion under Constantine to *the state religion* under Theodosius.
- Subsequent legislation (391–392) closed pagan temples, banned sacrifice, ended the Olympic Games (393), and removed traditional Greco-Roman public religion from civic life.
- Heretical Christian sects (Arians, Macedonians, Eunomians, Photinians) were progressively legally suppressed alongside paganism.
- The Edict's framework — "the religion of the Apostle Peter as held by Damasus and Peter of Alexandria" — locates orthodoxy by reference to specific bishops, an ecclesiastical-political move that prefigures medieval papal claims.

## Disputes
- **Practical effect timing.** The edict was issued from Thessalonica during Theodosius's recovery from illness en route to Constantinople; its immediate practical effect on the still-Arian-dominated Constantinopolitan church was limited until the Council of Constantinople (May–July 381) ratified its theology.
