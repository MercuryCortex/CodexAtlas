# Bent Pyramid of Sneferu (Dahshur)

## Overview

The **Bent Pyramid** is Sneferu's first attempt at a true smooth-sided pyramid at **Dahshur** (~2614–2600 BCE), and it is the most informative pyramid in Egypt from an architectural-process perspective — because it shows a major construction change in progress. The lower portion rises at **54°31'** (a steep angle); approximately halfway up, the angle abruptly changes to **43°21'** — producing the distinctive "bent" profile visible from kilometers away.

**Dimensions:**
- Base: ~188 m per side
- Height: ~105 m (reduced from ~130 m if the original angle had been maintained)
- Still retains **most of its original Tura limestone casing stones** — the only large pyramid to have preserved the outer casing to near its full extent. The surface gleams white. This is the closest modern visitors can get to seeing what the Giza pyramids looked like originally.

## Why the angle changed: three competing explanations

1. **Structural failure mid-construction (most accepted):** The steep 54° angle created excessive inward stress. Cracks appeared in the casing stones and interior chambers — visible today — and the builders reduced the angle to stabilize the structure. This is the consensus explanation and is supported by evidence of emergency repairs: the chambers were packed with cedar logs and gypsum mortar as internal buttressing.

2. **Time pressure:** Sneferu was old or ill and needed to finish quickly; reducing the angle allowed faster completion.

3. **Deliberate "double slope" design:** A minority position; not supported by the evidence of distress in the masonry.

**Significance for the Great Pyramid debate:** The Bent Pyramid demonstrates empirically that Egyptian pyramid builders did change designs mid-construction — this directly supports the "abandoned plan" interpretation of the [[subterranean-chamber-great-pyramid|Subterranean Chamber's]] unfinished state.

## Internal chambers: an unusual double-entry design

The Bent Pyramid has **two separate entrance passages** — one in the north face (conventional) and one in the west face — leading to two separate chamber systems that were later connected:

**North entrance system:**
- Descending passage → Antechamber → Lower chamber (corbelled ceiling; 6.5 m high)
- Corbelled vault still standing intact; one of the best-preserved examples in Egypt

**West entrance system:**
- Separate descending passage → Upper chamber (also corbelled)
- Connected to the lower chamber via a short passage

The double-entry design is unique among Egyptian pyramids and may reflect an early exploration of double-tomb theology (Upper/Lower Egypt duality; *ka* and *ba* having separate chambers) or simply a design revision.

## The satellite pyramid and valley temple

The Bent Pyramid is accompanied by:
- A small **satellite pyramid** to the south (subsidiary structure)
- A **valley temple** connected by a causeway — the best-preserved valley temple in Egypt, with relief-carved panels showing Sneferu receiving offerings and the personifications of his estates

The valley temple panels are significant because they show Sneferu in the same processional relief style that becomes canonical in later pyramid complexes — establishing the ideological program of the pyramid complex as a complete theological machine, not just a burial chamber.

## Sneferu's building program

Sneferu is the most prolific pyramid builder in Egyptian history by volume: he built or completed **four pyramids** — the Meidum pyramid (begun by Huni; Sneferu completed it and possibly converted it from step to true form), the Bent Pyramid, and the **[[red-pyramid-sneferu-dahshur|Red Pyramid]]**. His total construction volume exceeds that of the Great Pyramid itself.

This program of learning-through-failure — Meidum's outer casing collapsed in antiquity, the Bent Pyramid buckled, and only the Red Pyramid achieved full smooth-sided success — is the direct foundation from which **[[khufu|Khufu]]** and his architect **Hemiunu** launched the Great Pyramid with accumulated knowledge of what worked and what failed.
