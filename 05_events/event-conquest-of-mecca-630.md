# Conquest of Mecca / *Fatḥ Makka* (630 CE, 8 AH)

## What happened
In **Ramadan of 8 AH (January 630 CE)** Muhammad led an army of approximately 10,000 from Medina toward Mecca, claiming that the Quraysh had violated the **Treaty of Hudaybiyya (628 / 6 AH)** by supporting the Banu Bakr against the Khuzāʿa (a tribe allied with Muhammad). The Meccan Quraysh, militarily exhausted from the earlier engagements (Badr 624, Uḥud 625, the Trench 627) and unable to mount effective resistance, **largely surrendered without battle**. Abū Sufyān ibn Ḥarb — leader of the Umayyad faction of the Quraysh and previously Muhammad's principal Meccan opponent — converted to Islam at the conquest (per Sira-tradition memory, immediately before the entry into Mecca) and declared all Meccans who entered Abū Sufyān's house or shut their own doors to be safe.

Muhammad **entered Mecca largely peacefully**, declared a general amnesty (sparing the lives of all but a small named list of Meccan opponents), and **proceeded directly to the Kaaba** to perform the *ṭawāf* (circumambulation) and oversee the **destruction of the cult-images inside and around the Kaaba** — including the principal cult-image [[hubal]] and the multiple lesser idols (Islamic-tradition memory counts 360, likely stylized). The smashing of the Kaaba idols is the canonical foundational act of Islamic monotheism. Muhammad recited Quran 17:81 — *"Truth has come and falsehood has vanished; falsehood ever vanishes"* — as he destroyed the idols.

The conquest was followed shortly by additional destruction-expeditions: **Khalid ibn al-Walid destroyed the [[al-uzza]] sanctuary at Nakhla**; **Saʿd ibn Zayd al-Ashhalī destroyed the [[manat]] sanctuary at Qudayd**; **al-Mughīra ibn Shuʿba destroyed the [[allat]] sanctuary at Taif** (after the Battle of Taif, 8 AH); **Khalid ibn al-Walid destroyed the [[wadd]] cult-image of the Banu Kalb at Dūmat al-Jandal**. Within the same year (8 AH) the principal pre-Islamic Hijazi sanctuary system was effectively dismantled.

## Religious consequences
1. **End of pre-Islamic Arabian organized polytheism in the Hijaz.** The destruction of the principal Hijazi sanctuaries (Kaaba-Hubal, Nakhla-ʿUzzā, Qudayd-Manāt, Taif-Lāt, Dūmat al-Jandal-Wadd) ended pre-Islamic Hijazi religious infrastructure as a functioning system. The [[tradition-pre-islamic-arabian|pre-Islamic Arabian]] religion persists in residual / nomadic / South-Arabian forms after 630, but its principal centers are eliminated.
2. **Re-foundation of the Kaaba as Islamic monotheist sanctuary.** The Quranic narrative of Abraham and Ishmael's foundation of the Kaaba (Quran 2:127, 3:96-97 — "the first house established for humankind, at Bakka") is enacted in the post-conquest cleansing; the Kaaba becomes the *qibla* (direction of prayer) — already redirected from Jerusalem to Mecca in 624 / 2 AH — and the destination of the *ḥajj* pilgrimage.
3. **The "Year of the Delegations" (9 AH, 630-631 CE).** After the conquest of Mecca, tribes across the Arabian peninsula sent delegations to Medina to negotiate submission to Muhammad's authority. The peninsular Arabian unification under Islamic political authority is largely accomplished in 9-10 AH.
4. **Conversion of the Umayyad / Abū Sufyān faction.** The Umayyad clan — Muhammad's principal Meccan opponents at Badr, Uḥud, and the Trench — converts at the conquest. Abū Sufyān's son Muʿāwiya (later [[muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|the founder of the Umayyad caliphate]]) is among the conversions of this period. This sets up the post-632 political dynamics that will produce the First Fitna ([[event-first-fitna-656-661]] — this batch).
5. **The general amnesty as foundational precedent.** Muhammad's amnesty at the conquest of Mecca — sparing the lives of long-standing political-military opponents — is a foundational precedent in Islamic political ethics; widely cited in subsequent Islamic legal-political theory.

## Documents in play
- **[[phase-4-034-quran]]** — *Sūrat al-Naṣr* (110, "the Victory" — three verses, "when the help of God comes and the victory, and you see the people entering God's religion in throngs...") is traditionally read as referring to the conquest of Mecca. Sūrat al-Fatḥ (48, "the Victory" — longer surah) likely refers to the Hudaybiyya treaty as proximate trigger, though some readings extend it to the conquest itself. Various verses on apostasy / idol-destruction were revealed in this period.
- **[[phase-5-043-ibn-ishaq-sirat-rasul-allah]]** — the canonical Islamic-tradition Sira-narrative of the conquest, including the Abū Sufyān conversion episode and the idol-smashing.
- **Ibn al-Kalbi *Kitāb al-Aṣnām*** — the principal Islamic-tradition reconstructive source for the destruction-of-sanctuaries narratives. Tier 4 source-critically per Hawting 1999.

## Disputes
- **Source-critical reliability of the detailed idol-smashing narratives.** Hawting 1999: the detailed Ibn al-Kalbi descriptions of each destroyed sanctuary are later Islamic-tradition reconstructive material with strong polemical interests. The historical core (conquest of Mecca in 8 AH; destruction of the Kaaba cult-images; subsequent peninsular submission) is secure; the iconographic and ritual destruction-details should be read source-critically.
- **Historicity of the Abū Sufyān conversion-narrative.** The detailed conversion-vignette of Abū Sufyān immediately before the entry into Mecca, with al-ʿAbbās interceding for him, is canonical Sira-narrative; modern critical scholarship treats the broad outline (Umayyad-clan conversion at the conquest) as secure but the conversation-details as legendary.
- **The list of named persons exempted from amnesty.** Sira-tradition gives different lists; the principle (amnesty with narrow exceptions for personal-attack opponents) is secure, the specifics vary.
