# King's Chamber (Great Pyramid of Khufu)

## Physical description

The **King's Chamber** is the uppermost and most elaborate of the Great Pyramid's three known chambers. Located in the upper third of the pyramid, it is built entirely of **red Aswan granite** — the only chamber in the Great Pyramid not constructed of local limestone.

**Dimensions (Petrie 1883):**
- Length: 10.46 m (20 Egyptian Royal Cubits)
- Width: 5.23 m (10 cubits; exactly half the length — a **double-square** proportioned room)
- Height: 5.84 m (11.18 cubits)
- 100 granite blocks, precisely fitted, with horizontal courses

**Coffer (sarcophagus):**
A lidless red granite box. Its interior volume has been noted as remarkably close to the Coffer of the Ark of the Covenant's specifications (as given in Exodus) — a correlation first noted by Robert Menzies (1865) and repeated in sacred geometry literature. The coffer is **slightly wider than the Ascending Passage** — it could only have been placed during construction, not carried in later. It was empty when Al-Ma'mun's workmen reached it in the 9th century CE.

## The Ascending Passage and Antechamber

Access is via the **Grand Gallery** into a low corridor and then the **Antechamber** — a granite vestibule with three portcullis slots (the granite leafs that sealed the passage; all found in the raised position or broken by ancient robbers). The granite leaf partitions divided the Antechamber into four compartments.

## The five Relieving Chambers

Above the King's Chamber are **five hidden chambers** — stacked construction voids that distribute the pyramid's enormous weight away from the King's Chamber ceiling. They are:
1. Davison's Chamber (discovered 1765 by Nathaniel Davison via the Grand Gallery ramp)
2. Wellington's Chamber (blasted open by Vyse 1837)
3. Nelson's Chamber (Vyse 1837)
4. Lady Arbuthnot's Chamber (Vyse 1837)
5. Campbell's Chamber (Vyse 1837) — where the **Khufu cartouche** was found, painted in red ochre by ancient workers

The cartouche discovery is the primary hieroglyphic evidence connecting the Great Pyramid specifically to Khufu. Its authenticity has been occasionally disputed (most notably by Zecharia Sitchin and Egyptologist Rainer Stadelmann's critics) but is accepted by the vast majority of scholars.

## The two air shafts

Two narrow shafts (~20–23 cm wide) rise from the north and south walls of the King's Chamber to exit through the exterior casing:

**South shaft (→ Orion's Belt / Al Nitak):** At its designed angle of ~45°, this shaft pointed directly at the lower culmination of **Al Nitak** (ζ Orionis), the southernmost star of Orion's Belt, c. 2500 BCE. Orion was identified with Osiris in Egyptian theology — the god of resurrection and the dead. The pharaoh's *ba* (soul) was understood to ascend to rejoin Osiris.

**North shaft (→ Thuban):** The north shaft pointed to **Thuban** (α Draconis), the pole star c. 2787 BCE — the circumpolar star that "never sets" and represented eternal, imperishable existence. The *akh* (transfigured spirit) aspired to become circumpolar — to circle the pole forever without descending below the horizon.

These alignments are not disputed by mainstream Egyptology (Bauval 1994, Trimble 1964). What is disputed is whether they were deliberate navigational/astronomical targeting or coincidental results of the structural angles chosen for other reasons.

## Acoustic properties

The King's Chamber has unusual acoustic properties. Researcher Paul Devereux documented an infrasound resonance at approximately **120 Hz** — a frequency that can induce altered states of consciousness, visual phenomena, and disorientation. The chamber's granite walls are among the most resonant materials available. Whether the acoustic properties were intentional or incidental is unknown; Egyptologists generally regard them as incidental. The acoustic research prompted investigation of similar properties in other ancient megalithic spaces (Newgrange, Hal Saflieni Hypogeum in Malta).

The granite coffer, when struck, resonates at a frequency consistent with its dimensions — it has been described as a "granite bell" by acoustic researchers.

## The empty coffer: what it means

**The orthodox position:** The chamber was a royal tomb, possibly used for burial and then robbed in antiquity. The absence of a mummy does not prove the chamber was never used — the looting of royal tombs was systematic in ancient Egypt. The empty coffer is simply the result of ancient robbery.

**Alternative positions (Tier 3/4):**
- **Initiation chamber:** The earliest attestation of the pyramid-as-initiation-space is in the Rosicrucian/Masonic tradition (~17th c. onward); there is no contemporary Egyptian evidence for this function. The coffer as "initiatory sarcophagus" where the candidate underwent symbolic death and rebirth is a post-medieval construction.
- **Empty from the start:** Some researchers (including Alford 2003) argue the chamber was never intended as a burial — that the pyramid was a functional theological machine rather than a tomb. The Pyramid Texts' language of "the king ascending to become Re" supports a transformation rather than burial framing.
- **Repository for the Ark of the Covenant:** Hancock (*The Sign and the Seal*, 1992) and others have noted dimensional coincidences between the coffer and the Ark's specifications. No archaeological basis.
