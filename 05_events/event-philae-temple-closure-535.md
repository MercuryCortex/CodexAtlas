# Closure of the Philae Temple of Isis by Justinian I (~ +535-537)

The **terminal moment of pharaonic Egyptian religion as a continuously-practiced institutional tradition**. Justinian I (reigned +527-565) closed the Philae temple of Isis ~ +535-537, ending a religious tradition that had been continuously practiced for approximately **3,800 years** (~ -3300 predynastic origins → +535 closure). This is one of the longest-running continuous religious traditions in human history, and Philae's closure is the precisely-datable institutional end.

## Background: how Philae survived 150 years longer than other Egyptian cults

Egyptian temple-religion was systematically dismantled in the 4th-c CE under Theodosius I and his successors. The principal Egyptian sanctuaries (Karnak, Luxor, Dendera, Edfu, Abydos, the Memphite Serapeum, the Alexandrian Serapeion) were closed, destroyed, or converted to churches between ~ +380 and ~ +400. The Alexandrian Serapeion is destroyed by mob action in +391 (described by Rufinus, Sozomen, Socrates Scholasticus); other sanctuaries followed.

**Philae survived because of the southern frontier treaty**. The Roman southern border was unstable; the Blemmyes (a desert-tribe of Nubia) and the Nobatae (the Nubian kingdom centered at Nobatia / Faras) were periodically raiding the Egyptian Nile valley. The treaty of [diocletian-roman-emperor] (~ +298) required Rome to:

1. Pay an annual subsidy to the Nubian kings
2. **Permit the Nubian royal house and people to continue worshipping at the Philae Isis temple**
3. **Permit them periodically to bring the Isis cult-statue south to their own territory for ritual visits**

This treaty preserved the Philae cult through the Christianisation of Egypt — the Roman emperors found it politically expedient to honour the treaty rather than provoke Nubian raids. The Philae priesthood served a *quasi-international* constituency: Egyptian-Christian surroundings, but Nubian-Meroitic-Blemmye pilgrims and patrons.

The **last datable hieroglyphic inscription** in human history is at Philae: a graffito by the priest **Esmet-Akhom** dated 24 August +394 — written ~150 years after the formal Christianisation of the empire. The continuity is real: the Esmet-Akhom inscription is contemporaneous with the destruction of the Alexandrian Serapeion, but the Philae cult continues.

## Justinian's decision (~ +535)

Justinian I came to the throne in +527 with an aggressive religious-uniformity programme. His policy on pagan institutions had three principal moves:

1. **+529 — Closure of the Platonic Academy of Athens**: Justinian forbade the teaching of philosophy by pagans at Athens; the last seven Neoplatonic philosophers (Damascius, Simplicius, Priscianus, and four others) leave Athens for the Sasanian court of Khosrau I
2. **+529 — *Code of Justinian* Books 1.5 and 1.11**: codifies anti-pagan legislation, mandating death penalty for sacrificing to pagan gods
3. **+535 — Abrogation of the Nubian treaty and closure of the Philae cult**: Justinian sends General **Narses** (not the later Narses of the Italian campaign, an earlier Persian-eunuch general) to the southern frontier; Narses dismantles the cult, removes the cult-statue, sends the priests into exile, and converts the temple to a Christian church dedicated to St. Stephen

The closure is described in detail by **Procopius**, *De Bello Persico* I.19 — the principal contemporary historical source. Procopius mentions the Philae cult-statue's removal explicitly, indicating he understood the theological significance.

## Significance

The Philae closure marks:

1. **The end of pharaonic Egyptian religion** as a continuously-practiced living tradition (~ -3300 to +535 = ~3,800 years)
2. **The end of the longest-running continuous writing system** (Egyptian hieroglyphic, ~ -3300 to +394 = ~3,700 years; the script outlives the immediate cult by ~140 years because Philae's priesthood maintains literate continuity)
3. **The institutional end of Mediterranean paganism** when paired with Justinian's closure of the Platonic Academy in +529
4. **The Christianisation of the Egyptian southern frontier**, paving the way for Nubian Christianisation in the 6th-c CE (the Nubian kingdoms of Nobatia, Makuria, and Alodia are Christianised between +543 and +580)

The cult's Mediterranean-export form (the Hellenistic-Roman Isis cult; the Isis mysteries; the Isis-Lactans iconography) had already been Christianised or absorbed by the 4th-5th centuries. The terminal-Philae moment is the closure of the *home* of the tradition.
