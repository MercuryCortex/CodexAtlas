# Cologne Mani Codex Identified

## What happened
A miniature parchment codex (38 × 45 mm — among the smallest known ancient codices), originally produced in Greek in Egypt c. 400 CE, was acquired by the University of Cologne Papyrus Collection from the Asyut antiquities market. Albert Henrichs and Ludwig Koenen identified the codex's content in 1969–1970 as a Manichaean autobiographical-hagiographical work — the first substantial first-person Manichaean text discovered, providing critical information about Mani's youth, his break with the Elchasaite Jewish-Christian baptist community in which he was raised (~3rd century Mesopotamia), and his early prophetic call. Critical edition Koenen & Römer 1985–1988; complete English translation Cameron & Dewey 1979.

## Religious consequences
The CMC transformed Manichaean studies. Mani's connection to a Jewish-Christian baptist community ("Elchasaites") had been suggested by some scholars but was now confirmed and richly documented; the early phase of Mani's prophetic mission became visible; the Manichaean self-understanding (Mani as the seal of prophets in succession Adam-Seth-Enosh-Enoch-Noah-Abraham-Jesus-Mani) became readable from inside the tradition for the first time outside the Coptic Manichaica (Medinet Madi, 1929) and Turkic Manichaica (Turfan, 1902–1914).
