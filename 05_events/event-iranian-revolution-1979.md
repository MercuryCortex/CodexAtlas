# Iranian Revolution (1978–1979)

## What happened
A mass popular uprising in 1977–1978 against Mohammad Reza Shah Pahlavi's regime culminated in the Shah's departure (16 January 1979), the return of Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini from his Paris exile (1 February 1979), the collapse of the monarchy (11 February 1979), and the establishment of the Islamic Republic of Iran (formally proclaimed 1 April 1979 by referendum). The new constitution (December 1979) institutionalized the doctrine of *vilāyat-i faqīh* (guardianship of the jurist) — that during the occultation of the Twelfth Imam [[muhammad-al-mahdi-person]], supreme political authority rests with the most qualified Islamic jurist (initially Khomeini as *Supreme Leader*).

## Religious consequences
The first successful modern revolution founded explicitly on Islamic-theological grounds. *Vilāyat-i faqīh* was a major innovation in Twelver Shi'a political theology — traditional Shi'a quietism (refusal to recognize sovereign political authority during the Twelfth Imam's occultation; see [[muhammad-al-mahdi-person]]) was reversed in favor of clerical political rule. The Iranian Revolution decisively demonstrated that political Islam was a major late-20th-century force, accelerated parallel Sunni political-Islamist movements (the Salafi-jihadist trajectory, the Muslim Brotherhood's expansion), and reshaped Middle Eastern geopolitics. Khomeini's theological writings (especially *Hukumat-i Islami*, 1970 — his Najaf lectures) had laid the doctrinal groundwork that the Revolution actualized.

## The Karbala paradigm
The Revolution's ideological-emotional engine was explicitly the *Karbala paradigm* — the structural opposition of the righteous-oppressed (the people, the clergy, Husayn) against the illegitimate-tyrant (the Shah, Yazid). Khomeini's slogan *"Every day is Ashura, every land is Karbala"* directly redeploys [[event-karbala-680]] as the structural template of modern Shi'i revolutionary politics. Ali Shariati's pre-Revolution readings of [[husayn-ibn-ali]] as revolutionary archetype, and the mass Ashura processions of December 1978 that became anti-Shah demonstrations, were the immediate ideological-ritual antecedents.

## Documents in play
Khomeini's *Hukumat-i Islami* (1970) and the 1979 Constitution of the Islamic Republic.

## Disputes
- **Vilāyat-i faqīh** is itself a contested doctrine within Twelver Shi'a Islam. The "quietist" school (Najaf, Sistani) does not accept Khomeini's expansive version; the Iranian "activist" school (Qom-centered) does. The dispute is theologically substantive and politically consequential.
