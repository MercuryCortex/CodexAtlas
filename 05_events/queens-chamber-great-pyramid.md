# Queen's Chamber & Shaft Doors (Great Pyramid of Khufu)

## The chamber

The **"Queen's Chamber"** is a misnomer — the name is Arabic convention applied to any chamber with a **gabled (pitched) ceiling** (as opposed to the flat granite ceiling of the King's Chamber). No queen was buried here; it was almost certainly intended for the pharaoh himself before the design was changed.

**Dimensions:**
- Length: 5.75 m (east-west)
- Width: 5.23 m (north-south)
- Height to gable ridge: 6.26 m
- Limestone construction (not granite)

**The niche:** The east wall contains a recessed corbelled niche ~4.67 m high — the largest niche in any Egyptian pyramid. It may have held a statue of the king, a canopic chest, or (in some interpretations) the "King's Ka statue." It was found empty and vandalized.

## The sealed air shafts: one of the most active archaeological investigations in the world

Unlike the **King's Chamber shafts** (which exit through the exterior), the two Queen's Chamber shafts extend approximately **65 m** into the pyramid's body — and end approximately **20 cm short of the exterior** without piercing it. They were plastered over and not intended to be found.

**Timeline of discoveries:**

**1872:** Engineers Waynman Dixon and James Grant discover the shafts by probing the walls after noticing a crack. They open both and send a metal rod 14 m into each. A cedar-wood rod and a stone ball are found in the north shaft — their purpose unknown. The objects disappear from the British Museum (the rod rediscovered 2020; the stone ball still missing).

**1993 — Gantenbrink's discovery:** German engineer **Rudolf Gantenbrink** designs and deploys **Upuaut-2** — a 35×28×25 cm robot with a camera and laser. It travels 65 m up the south shaft and encounters a **blocking stone** (a limestone slab with two copper fittings/handles). *This is the first significant unknown feature found inside the Great Pyramid in over a century.* The Egyptian antiquities authority delays further exploration for 9 years.

**2002 — National Geographic's Pyramid Rover:** The Egyptian authorities commission a National Geographic robot to drill through Gantenbrink's door. Behind it: a small space (~20 cm), then another limestone blocking stone. The second stone has a **carved loop on its inner face** — mirroring the copper handles on the front of the first door.

**2011 — Djedi Project (Leeds University):** The team passes a miniature camera through the drilled hole and around the edge of the second door. Findings:
- **Red ochre symbols** on the floor of the cavity behind the first door — numbers? hieratic script? The meaning is disputed.
- The remnants of the copper handles from the first door, folded back.
- The second door's inner face, with its carved loop, photographed.

The north shaft has not been as thoroughly explored. Its route bends (unlike the south shaft), and Upuaut-2 could not navigate the bend. The north shaft's terminal "door," if it has one, has not been reached.

## The investigation status

As of 2026, **nobody knows what (if anything) is behind the second blocking stone in the south shaft.** The two doors, their symbols, and the copper handles are among the most genuinely unexplored features of any major ancient monument. Explanations range from:
- **Sealing plugs** placed to close off the shaft during construction (most parsimonious)
- **False doors** — a symbolic architectural feature common in Egyptian tombs (the *ka* door through which the spirit passes)
- **Sealed chambers** containing objects — papyri, ritual objects, the canopic equipment originally intended for this chamber

**[[theme-hall-of-records-hypothesis]]**: The sealed Queen's Chamber shafts are a prime candidate in theories about hidden documents or ritual objects inside the pyramid. No archaeological evidence supports this — but no archaeological investigation has yet reached behind the second door.

## Stellar alignment of the shafts

Like the King's Chamber shafts, the Queen's Chamber shafts pointed to specific stars c. 2500 BCE (Bauval & Gilbert 1994):
- **South shaft** (angle ~38–39°): pointed to **Sirius** (Sopdet — identified with Isis; herald of the Nile inundation). Since Osiris/Orion is associated with the King's Chamber south shaft, the Isis/Sirius targeting of the Queen's shaft is consistent with the theological pairing.
- **North shaft** (angle ~37–38°): pointed to **Kochab** (β Ursae Minoris) — a circumpolar star of the "Imperishable Ones."

The shafts end before reaching the exterior — which means, if the stellar alignment is intentional, the symbolic axis is internal (king's *ba* aligned toward the stars *within* the pyramid) rather than physical (the star visible through the shaft from inside).
