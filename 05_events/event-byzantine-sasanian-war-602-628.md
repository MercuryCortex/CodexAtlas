# Last Byzantine-Sasanian War (602–628)

The 26-year war between the Byzantine and Sasanian Persian empires that **mutually exhausted both** and structurally prepared the ground for the astonishing Arab-Muslim conquest of 634–651. Triggered by the Sasanian Shahanshah Khosrow II's invasion in 603 (using the 602 murder of his protector Emperor Maurice as casus belli), the war saw Sasanian armies overrun the entire eastern Mediterranean — **Jerusalem sacked 614, the True Cross seized, Egypt conquered 619, Persians camped at Chalcedon facing Constantinople 626** — before [[heraclius-byzantine-emperor|Emperor Heraclius]]'s daring counter-offensive into the Persian heartland (Nineveh 627; treaty 628) restored the antebellum frontier. The True Cross was returned to Jerusalem by Heraclius in person on 21 March 630. Both empires emerged victorious-on-paper but materially shattered; within a decade both lost their eastern provinces — Byzantium permanently, Sasanian Persia entirely — to the Rashidun caliphate. The war is the political-military hinge between late antiquity and the medieval Near East.
