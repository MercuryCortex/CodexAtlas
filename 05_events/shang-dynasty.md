# Shang Dynasty

The Shang Dynasty (c. 1600–1046 BCE) was the second Chinese dynasty by traditional reckoning, and the first for which we have written historical records — the oracle-bone inscriptions from the final Shang capital at Anyang (Yīn). Shang civilization was characterized by:

- **Royal ancestor cult**: Kings communicated with royal ancestors through ritual divination using oracle bones; the entire state apparatus centered on maintaining relationships with the dead
- **Oracle-bone writing**: The earliest form of Chinese writing (jiǎgǔwén 甲骨文) — ancestor of all modern Chinese script — developed to record divination questions and answers; see [[alphabet-chinese-oracle-bones]]
- **Bronze ritual vessels**: The Shang produced the most technically sophisticated bronze casting in the ancient world; vessels were used in ancestral ritual offerings
- **Human sacrifice**: Large-scale sacrificial rituals accompanied royal burials (pit burials at Anyang contain sacrificed retainers, horses, chariots)
- **The high god Di**: Shang theology centered on the high deity Di (帝), to whom the king's ancestors had access, and through them the king

The Shang were overthrown by the Zhou in 1046 BCE at the Battle of Muye; Zhou legitimacy was articulated through the "Mandate of Heaven" (Tianming) concept — a new idea that divine favor was conditional on just rulership.
