# Natufian Burial Traditions and the Hilazon Tachtit "Shaman" Burial (c. 12,500–9,500 BCE)

## What happened
The Natufian (named by Dorothy Garrod 1928 after Wadi an-Natuf where the type-site el-Wad was located) is the Epipalaeolithic / terminal Pleistocene–earliest Holocene culture of the southern Levant, c. 15,000–11,500 cal BP (~13,000–9,500 BCE), spanning the Bølling-Allerød warming and the Younger Dryas cooling. It is the immediate cultural predecessor of the Pre-Pottery Neolithic and a critical bridge from mobile hunting-gathering to sedentary village life. Major sites include 'Ain Mallaha (Eynan), el-Wad Terrace, Hayonim Cave, Hilazon Tachtit Cave, Raqefet Cave, Nahal Oren, and Wadi Hammeh 27.

**Key religious-ritual features of the Natufian record:**

- **Intramural burial** (burial of the dead *within* and beneath the floors of habitation structures). This is one of the earliest and most consistent attestations of intramural-burial practice in the human record. Over 400 Natufian burials are known; many include grave goods (perforated dentalium and shell beads, bone pendants, ground-stone implements). The pattern of *the dead remaining in the village* is itself a religious-cultural statement and forms the typological precursor to the PPNB plastered-skull "ancestor" tradition.

- **The Hilazon Tachtit "Shaman" Burial** (Grosman, Munro, Belfer-Cohen 2008 *PNAS*). A Late Natufian (~12,000 cal BP) cave burial of an elderly, severely deformed woman (~45 years old, with multiple skeletal pathologies indicating she had been highly visible and remarkable in her community), interred in a deliberately prepared pit with an exceptional grave-good assemblage: 50 complete tortoise carapaces, a complete human foot from another individual, a wing-tip of a golden eagle, the pelvis of a leopard, the forearm of a wild boar, two stone-marten skulls, and a cow tail. The animal-part assemblage is interpreted by Grosman et al. as ritually charged in a way that the cross-cultural ethnographic record associates with practitioner-of-the-supernatural roles ("shaman-like" — with appropriate scare-quotes around the ethnologically over-applied term).

- **Raqefet Cave flower-lined graves** (Nadel et al. 2013 *PNAS*). Late Natufian graves with phytolith evidence for deliberate placement of fresh sage, mint, and figwort flowers in burial pits. **This is the earliest *securely demonstrated* evidence of floral burial offerings** — and is relevant to the disputed Shanidar 4 floral-burial claim (Natufian floral graves are 50,000 years later than Shanidar; the practice cannot be evidence of Neanderthal-to-Natufian continuity, but it is independently the *securely-attested earliest* case).

- **Feasting deposits** (Munro & Grosman 2010 *PNAS*; documented at Hilazon Tachtit and other Natufian sites). Concentrations of butchered tortoise and aurochs remains with patterns indicating large-group consumption events around burials — material evidence for *funerary feasting* as a social-ritual institution, also a typological precursor to later Levantine and ancient Near Eastern mortuary practice.

## Religious consequences
- The Natufian establishes, in the southern Levant specifically, a **continuous tradition of intramural burial with grave goods, individuated mortuary treatment, and funerary feasting** that flows directly into the Pre-Pottery Neolithic A and B. Kuijt 2008 (*Current Anthropology* 49) argues this is a documented continuous-development sequence — Natufian intramural burial → PPNA continued intramural burial → PPNB skull-removal, skull-curation, and plastered skulls (Jericho, 'Ain Ghazal, Beisamoun, Tell Aswad). **This is one of the few cases in deep prehistory where a direct-continuity claim is empirically supportable.**
- The Hilazon Tachtit burial is the most-discussed pre-agricultural "specialist religious practitioner" candidate in the archaeological record. Whether the individual was a "shaman" in the strict sense (an ethnographically-defined role with specific trance-and-spirit-communication practices) is interpretively underdetermined; what is securely attested is **differentiated mortuary treatment indicating a recognized social-ritual role**, and a grave-good assemblage emphasizing animal-part symbolism.
- The Raqefet Cave flower-lined graves are *securely* dated and *securely* deliberate (the phytolith and pollen evidence is methodologically robust), and they materially establish floral burial offerings ~12,000 years ago — a useful counter-anchor in any discussion of the contested Shanidar 4 claim.

## Disputes
- **"Shaman" terminology.** Grosman et al. use the term *shaman* heuristically and with appropriate caution; some critics (e.g., a Mithen response in the literature) note that *shaman* is a Tungus-derived ethnographic term whose specific content cannot be transported intact to the Late Natufian. The *evidence* (anomalous burial, specialist-role differentiation) is widely accepted; the *term* is conventional, not technical.
- **Sedentism debate.** Whether the Natufian was fully sedentary (year-round occupation) at sites like 'Ain Mallaha, or seasonally semi-sedentary with central-place residential bases, is contested (Boyd 2006, Hardy-Smith & Edwards 2004 on the 'house-of-the-dead' question). The burial-as-ancestor-presence interpretation depends in part on the sedentism question.
- **The Younger Dryas as a cultural-ritual driver.** Bar-Yosef and others have argued that the Younger Dryas cold-snap (~12,900–11,700 BP) stressed Late Natufian populations and accelerated both the move into sedentism-with-cultivation and the elaboration of ritual practice. This is one synthesizing framework; not all scholars accept the specific causal claim.

## Connections
- [[event-shanidar-neanderthal-burials-c-60000-bce]] — *parallel-motif* with critical caveat. The Raqefet Cave floral-burial evidence (Natufian, ~12,000 BP, secure) is sometimes invoked to *support* the contested Shanidar 4 floral-burial claim (Neanderthal, ~60,000 BP, contested). The Atlas position: the Raqefet evidence is *securely* the earliest demonstrated case of deliberate floral burial; it does not retrospectively validate the Shanidar claim, which is evaluated on its own evidence (see Disputes there).
- [[event-jericho-pre-pottery-neolithic-c-9000-bce]] — **direct continuity**, ~3,000 years, well-established sequence. Kuijt 2008 (*Current Anthropology* 49) is the canonical reference for the Natufian → PPNA → PPNB mortuary continuity, including intramural burial → skull removal → plastered-skull treatment. This is *the* documented case of long-deep-prehistory religious-practice continuity in the southern Levant.
- [[event-gobekli-tepe-c-9500-bce]] — *shared-milieu* in the broad sense (terminal-Pleistocene / earliest-Holocene Southwest Asia); the Late Natufian and the earliest PPNA Göbekli Tepe layers are roughly contemporary; whether Natufian-mobile-foragers and Anatolian-monument-builders interacted directly is undocumented but the broad cultural milieu is the same.
