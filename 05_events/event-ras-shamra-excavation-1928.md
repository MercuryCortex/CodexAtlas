# Ras Shamra (Ugarit) Excavation

## What happened
In March 1928, a Syrian farmer named Mahmoud Mella az-Zir, plowing his field near the coast at Minet el-Beida, struck a stone slab covering a tomb. Local antiquities authorities notified French Mandate officials, who organized an exploratory mission. Excavations under Claude F.A. Schaeffer began at the nearby tell of Ras Shamra in 1929 and identified it as ancient Ugarit, a major Late Bronze Age Canaanite kingdom (~1450–1180 BCE). The Royal Palace archives yielded thousands of clay tablets in multiple languages and scripts; the most consequential were tablets written in a previously-unknown alphabetic cuneiform script (the **Ugaritic alphabet** — 30 wedge-form signs, the earliest fully alphabetic script known). Charles Virolleaud, with Hans Bauer and Édouard Dhorme working independently, deciphered the Ugaritic script with remarkable speed (1929–1930) by recognizing it as a West-Semitic language cognate with biblical Hebrew.

## Religious consequences
The Ras Shamra texts contained extensive Canaanite mythological literature — the *Baal Cycle* (KTU 1.1–1.6), the *Aqhat Epic*, the *Kirta Epic*, ritual texts, god-lists — providing the first direct primary access to Canaanite religion (previously known largely through Hebrew Bible polemics and a few Phoenician inscriptions). The impact on biblical studies was transformative: many obscure biblical phrases, divine epithets, and poetic conventions were illuminated; the YHWH-Baal polemic in the Hebrew Bible became readable as a specific theological contestation within a shared Canaanite cultural-religious milieu; the Ugaritic divine council (with El at its head and Baal as the storm-warrior) provided the most direct comparative context for Hebrew divine-council theology (Ps 82, Job 1–2, 1 Kings 22, Isa 6).

## Documents in play
The Baal Cycle, Ugaritic ritual texts, the Aqhat and Kirta epics — all Phase-1 documents entered modern legibility through this 1928–1929 discovery and the rapid 1929–1930 decipherment.
