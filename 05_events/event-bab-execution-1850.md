# Execution of the Bab (July 9, 1850)

## What Happened
On **July 9, 1850**, in the public square of **Tabriz, Iran**, **Siyyid Ali-Muhammad Shirazi** — known as **the Bab** ("the Gate") — was executed by firing squad after six years of imprisonment. The execution sequence has become one of the most theologically significant narratives in Bahá'í history:

1. The Bab was suspended from a nail in the wall of a barracks by ropes, together with one of his disciples, Muhammad Ali Zunuzi (Anis)
2. A regiment of **750 Christian Armenian soldiers** fired simultaneously
3. When the smoke cleared: **the Bab was missing.** The ropes had been severed by bullets, but both men were unharmed. The Bab was found in his cell, completing a conversation with his secretary that had been interrupted when soldiers arrived
4. He was returned to the square and suspended again
5. A **Muslim regiment** — unwilling to risk the miracle — was ordered to fire
6. The second volley killed the Bab and his companion; their bodies were described as so intermingled that they could not be separated

Bahá'í tradition regards the first regiment's failure as miraculous. Secular historians have proposed various explanations (the angle, the ropes, smoke concealing an exit). The British diplomatic dispatch from Tabriz (preserved in the Momen anthology) confirms the bare event: the Bab was executed, the first volley's result was unusual, a second volley was required.

## The Bab's Mission and Claim
Siyyid Ali-Muhammad had proclaimed in **1844** (the year that Bahá'ís consider the beginning of a new prophetic cycle) that he was the **Bab** — the "Gate" to the Hidden Imam awaited by Shia Islam — and subsequently made progressively stronger claims, ultimately presenting himself as an independent Manifestation of God in the Shia mahdi tradition.

His six-year ministry (1844–1850) while imprisoned:
- He composed approximately **five million words** — letters, tablets, books (including the *Bayán*, his primary theological work)
- He gathered tens of thousands of followers (*Bábís*)
- His movement precipitated significant social disruption and three major armed conflicts in Iran (Shaykh Tabarsi, Nayriz, Zanjan — where Bábís were massacred)

The Qajar government's decision to execute him was driven by both religious and political considerations: the ulama regarded his claims as heresy; the government feared the social disruption his movement caused.

## The Theological Significance

**For the Bahá'í Faith**: The Bab's execution is the hinge event of the faith's history. The Bab had explicitly prophesied the coming of a greater figure — "He Whom God Shall Make Manifest" — whom Bahá'ís identify as **Bahá'u'lláh**. The Bab's martyrdom is thus not a terminus but a pivot: it makes Bahá'u'lláh's emergence narratively necessary and theologically prepared. The dying-founder here is explicitly a *forerunner* who dies so that the tradition's actual center can be revealed.

**The disproportion**: A young merchant from Shiraz, imprisoned for 6 years, executed at 30, having never commanded an army or held political power — yet his followers number over 5 million globally in 2025, and the Bahá'í Faith is one of the world's fastest-growing religions. The gap between execution conditions and subsequent global reach is the dying-founder paradox at maximum intensity.

**Bahá'u'lláh's response**: Bahá'u'lláh, who would later be imprisoned and exiled for 40 years and compose the Bahá'í Faith's foundational texts *in captivity*, was in Tehran when the Bab was executed. According to Bahá'í sources, this is the moment he received his own prophetic awareness. The chain runs: Bab martyred → Bahá'u'lláh's mission accelerated → Bahá'í Faith's scriptures composed in prison.

## The Bábi Massacres — Scale of the Founding Persecution
The Bab's execution was the apex of a broader persecution:
- **1844–1852**: An estimated **20,000 Bábís killed** in various regional conflicts and massacres
- Shaykh Tabarsi (1848–49): Bábí community besieged for 7 months; ~300 killed after a promise of safe conduct was violated
- Nayriz (1850): 2 separate episodes; hundreds killed
- Zanjan (1850): approximately 1,800 Bábís killed after a 9-month siege

The founding persecution of the Bahá'í Faith is, in absolute numbers per capita of the movement's size at the time, one of the most intense in modern religious history.

## Connections to Dying-Founder Pattern
The Bab's execution follows the dying-founder paradigm with unusual precision:
- **Claim to divine authority** → **religious establishment trial** → **state execution** → **tradition founded around martyrdom**
- The executed founder composed his major texts **in prison** — making the tradition's scriptures literally products of imprisonment, matching Bahá'u'lláh's later pattern
- The **miraculous first-volley narrative** parallels other founding-martyrdom miraculous elements (the darkness at Jesus's crucifixion; al-Hallaj's reported smile)
