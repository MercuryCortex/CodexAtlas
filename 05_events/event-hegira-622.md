# Hegira (Hijra)

## What happened
In September 622 CE, Muhammad and a group of his Meccan followers migrated from Mecca to Yathrib (about 350 km north), escaping intensifying persecution by the dominant Meccan clans. Yathrib — which became known as *Madinat al-Nabi*, "the City of the Prophet," and then simply Medina — had reached out to Muhammad to arbitrate its internal tribal conflicts. The migration began the Muslim community as an organized polity.

In 638, Caliph Umar made the Hegira year (622–623 CE) the start of the Islamic calendar (Anno Hegirae), so all subsequent Islamic dating is reckoned from this event.

## Religious consequences
- *Foundation of the* umma. The Muslim community at Medina became the first organized Islamic polity, with the *Constitution of Medina* (Sahifat al-Madinah) establishing a multi-tribal pact between Muslims, Medinan Jewish clans, and Arab clans.
- *Calendar.* The Hegira as Year 1 of the Islamic calendar marks the religious significance the early Islamic community attached to the event — not Muhammad's birth, not the first revelation, but the founding of the community.
- *Geopolitical pivot.* The Hegira shifted Islam from a persecuted minority religion in Mecca to the organizing principle of a polity at Medina; the Muslim military, legal, and theological development that followed (Battle of Badr 624; Battle of Uhud 625; Battle of the Trench 627; [[event-conquest-of-mecca-630|Conquest of Mecca 630]]) culminated in the unification of the Arabian peninsula under Muslim authority by Muhammad's death in 632 ([[event-death-of-muhammad-632]]).

## Documents in play
- The Constitution of Medina, preserved in Ibn Ishaq's *Sira*, is the first surviving Islamic legal-political document.
- Quranic Medinan suras (later in the canonical order despite being later chronologically) reflect the post-Hegira community's situation.
