# Council of Troyes

## What happened
A French church council convened on 13 January 1129 at Troyes (Champagne), under the presidency of Cardinal Matthew of Albano (papal legate of Honorius II). [[hugues-de-payens|Hugues de Payens]] and a delegation of Templar brothers attended to seek formal ecclesiastical recognition of their decade-old Jerusalem confraternity. [[bernard-of-clairvaux|Bernard of Clairvaux]] — already the most influential Latin churchman of his generation — was the moving voice of the council. The principal output was the **Latin Rule of the Templars** ([[phase-5-036-rule-of-templars-latin]]), a 72-article rule modeled substantially on the Cistercian / Benedictine framework but adapted to a military religious life: combined monastic vows (poverty, chastity, obedience) with explicit provisions for combat, military discipline, and the organized life of a fighting community.

The council also marks **the institutional birth of the [[tradition-knights-templar|Knights Templar]]** as a recognized religious order of the Latin Church.

## Religious consequences
- **Theological innovation: the *miles Christi* in arms.** Combining monastic vow and military profession was unprecedented in the Latin tradition (the closest precedent was the late-Roman *milites Christi* metaphor, but never institutionalized). The Rule, paired with Bernard's separate companion treatise [[phase-5-037-bernard-de-laude-novae-militiae|*De Laude Novae Militiae*]] (~1129–1136), inaugurates the medieval military-religious-order tradition.
- **Direct precedent for the militarized [[tradition-hospitallers|Hospitallers]] (~1130s) and the [[tradition-teutonic-knights|Teutonic Knights]] (1198).**
- **Two-century scaffolding.** From 1129 to 1312 the Latin Rule (with later French additions, the *Retraits / Statutes* and the *Penances*) governs the operational life of the Templars across Latin Christendom and Outremer.

## Documents in play
- [[phase-5-036-rule-of-templars-latin]] — surviving in multiple medieval manuscript witnesses; the standard modern edition is Upton-Ward 1992.
