# Council of Clermont

## What happened
A church council convened by Pope Urban II at Clermont (Auvergne) in November 1095. On 27 November 1095, in an outdoor sermon at the Place Delille, Urban II called Latin Christendom to a *bellum sacrum* — a holy war — to aid Byzantine Emperor Alexios I Komnenos against Seljuk Turkish advances in Anatolia and to "liberate" Jerusalem and the holy places. Urban offered participants a **plenary indulgence** (remission of penance for sins confessed) — a theological innovation with vast subsequent significance. The crowd response, reportedly, was the spontaneous cry *Deus vult!* ("God wills it!"). The First Crusade was effectively launched.

## Religious consequences
- **Theological invention of crusading.** The plenary indulgence as motivation for armed pilgrimage is a Clermont-and-after creation. Subsequent crusading theology (Bernard of Clairvaux for the Second, Innocent III for the Fourth, the entire later medieval indulgence economy that Luther will protest at Wittenberg 1517) descends from Clermont.
- **Direct cause of the [[event-first-crusade-1095-1099|First Crusade]].** Without Clermont, no First Crusade, no Crusader Kingdom, no Outremer, no [[tradition-knights-templar|Templars]] or [[tradition-hospitallers|Hospitallers]].
- **The first major Latin Christian → Byzantine Christian → Islamic-world contact event** since the seventh-century Arab conquests. Reshapes Latin Christianity's geographic horizon.
- **Pivotal moment in Latin–Eastern Christian relations.** Already in schism since 1054 ([[event-great-schism-1054]]), Latin–Byzantine relations are formally a crusade-alliance from 1095 — though the alliance breaks down by [[event-fourth-crusade-1204|1204]].

## Documents in play
- **Urban's sermon survives in five non-verbatim accounts.** Each reflects its author's later interpretation — Robert the Monk emphasizes Holy-Land liberation, Fulcher of Chartres emphasizes Byzantine assistance, etc. Riley-Smith 1986 reconstructs the most defensible historical content.
- **The Decrees of Clermont** themselves (32 canons) — preserved separately; most are unrelated to the Crusade and concern Truce of God, simony, etc.

## Disputes
- **Did Urban primarily intend a defensive Byzantine-aid expedition, or a Jerusalem-liberation campaign?** Older scholarship favored Byzantine-aid; Riley-Smith 1986 and Asbridge 2004 argue Jerusalem was central from the start.
- **Was *Deus vult!* the original or a literary embellishment?** Probably literary; Asbridge 2004 ch. 1.
