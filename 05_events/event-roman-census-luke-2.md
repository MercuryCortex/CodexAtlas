# The Lucan Roman Census (Luke 2:1–3)

The empire-wide enrollment "decreed by Caesar Augustus" that [[phase-3-018-luke-acts|Luke 2:1–3]] uses to anchor the Christian Nativity in Augustan imperial chronology. Historicity is contested: Quirinius's actual Syrian governorship dates to 6 CE (Josephus *Antiquities* 18.1–2), ten years after Herod the Great's death in 4 BCE — and the Lucan narrative requires Jesus's birth to fall within Herod's reign. The mainstream critical consensus (Brown 1993, Fitzmyer 1981) treats the Lucan framing as **theological-historical** rather than strictly chronicled history: the point is to date the Nativity inside Augustan imperial time and inside an enrollment-event that subjects the holy family to imperial authority. Whether or not the specific Quirinius-census can be harmonized with Herodian chronology (various apologetic two-census models exist), the load-bearing point for Christianity's foundational text is that the new dispensation is dated by the *Pax Romana* it eventually transforms.
