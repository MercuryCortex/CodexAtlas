# Stobaeus compiles his Anthologion (c. 500 CE)

## What happened
A learned Macedonian Christian scholar, **John of Stobi** ("Stobaeus" — from his hometown Stobi in northern Macedonia), in roughly the late 5th / early 6th century CE compiled an enormous four-volume *Anthologion* — a commonplace-book of selected passages from Greek pagan philosophical, religious, ethical, and gnomic literature, organized topically. The work was assembled for the education of his son Septimius. The original four-book structure survives only in the breviated form preserved through Byzantine manuscript transmission; the standard modern critical edition (Wachsmuth-Hense, Berlin 1884–1923) runs to 5 volumes.

What makes the Anthologion historically consequential is that it preserves substantial excerpts from many Greek philosophical-religious works that **otherwise survive only fragmentarily or not at all**. Specifically:

- **The Stobaean Hermetica** — 29 excerpts (SH I–XXIX in Nock-Festugière) including the long [[phase-4-076-stobaean-hermetica-kore-kosmou|Kore Kosmou]] (SH XXIII) — material from now-lost Greek Hermetic books. Without Stobaeus, the entire Isis-Horus revelatory branch of Hermetic literature would be unknown.
- **Pre-Socratic fragments** — many Anaximander, Anaximenes, Heraclitus, Empedocles, and Democritus quotations are preserved through Stobaeus alongside Aristotle, Theophrastus, Plutarch, and Diels.
- **Neopythagorean material** — substantial excerpts from the Pythagorean *Hieroi Logoi* and from later Pythagoreanizing authors.
- **Stoic and Middle-Platonist philosophy** — material from Chrysippus, Posidonius, Numenius, and others.

Stobaeus made no theological argument; he assembled the material as ethical-philosophical *paideia*. The cross-cultural absurdity is striking: a 5th–6th-c. Christian Macedonian copying out for his son enormous chunks of pagan Hermetic, Pythagorean, and Stoic religious literature — material that the contemporary Christianized Roman state was actively suppressing in cult contexts.

## Religious consequences
**Without Stobaeus, late-antique Greek pagan religious philosophy would be much more fragmentary than it actually is.** Specifically:
1. The **Stobaean Hermetica** would be lost. The Greek Corpus Hermeticum (Byzantine transmission) and the Latin Asclepius (continuous Latin transmission) would survive, but the Isis-Horus dialogues including Kore Kosmou would be lost. **An entire branch of Hermetic literature owes its survival to Christian compilation.**
2. The **astrological-decan Hermetica** (the technical-religious Hermetic material on the 36 ten-degree zodiacal divisions) survive almost entirely through Stobaeus.
3. The **pre-Socratic philosophical-religious tradition** would be substantially impoverished.
4. The **Christian-pagan intellectual transmission edge** of late antiquity would be much harder to document.

The Anthologion is a load-bearing example of the larger pattern by which **Christian compilers in the 4th–7th centuries preserved pagan religious literature** — Eusebius's *Praeparatio Evangelica* (which preserves much pre-Socratic and Hellenistic religious material as polemical citation), Cyril of Alexandria's *Contra Julianum* (which preserves Hermetic citations), the Coptic monastic codex collectors at Nag Hammadi who bundled Hermetic with Christian texts. The Christian Roman state was suppressing pagan religion in cult contexts but Christian intellectuals were preserving pagan religious literature in textual contexts — a paradox that defines late-antique cultural history.

## Documents in play
- **Texts produced because of this event**: the Anthologion itself; the Stobaean Hermetic Excerpts as extracted by modern editors (Nock-Festugière, Budé vols. 3–4, 1954).
- **Texts preserved because of this event**: the Hermetic Kore Kosmou (SH XXIII); ~28 other Hermetic excerpts; pre-Socratic and Pythagorean fragments cited under Stobaeus's topical headings.
- **Texts cross-confirming Stobaeus**: Cyril of Alexandria's *Contra Julianum* (433 CE) cites Hermetic material that overlaps with the Stobaean excerpts, providing independent confirmation that Stobaeus was preserving real circulating Hermetic text.

## Disputes
- **Date of Stobaeus.** Generally placed late 5th or early 6th c. CE based on internal evidence and Photius's notice (codex 167). Some place him as early as 400 CE; others as late as 600.
- **Religious identity.** Generally taken as Christian based on his cultural context; the *Anthologion* itself contains no explicit Christian theological commitment, only the implicit choice to preserve pagan philosophical-religious material for the education of his son. A minority view treats him as a crypto-pagan; the majority treats him as a Christian humanist-compiler.
- **Original structure.** The four-book original is partially reconstructible; the surviving manuscript tradition has reorganized and abridged the material.
