# Parinirvāṇa of the Buddha (~-400 BCE)

## What happened
At approximately age 80, [[siddhartha-gautama-buddha|the Buddha]] died at Kuśinagara (modern Kushinagar, Uttar Pradesh) after having lain down between two *sal* trees, given his final teachings to the assembled monks (the *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta*'s narrative-frame), and entered final nirvāṇa (*parinirvāṇa* / Pali *parinibbāna*) — the final extinction of craving and the exit from the cycle of rebirth. The canonical narrative gives his last meal as offered by Cunda the smith (food variously identified as truffles, pork, or a mushroom dish — the Pali *sūkaramaddava* is disputed) and reports that the meal caused his terminal illness. His final words were: "*Vayadhammā saṅkhārā, appamādena sampādetha*" — "Conditioned things are subject to decay; with diligence strive on." His body was cremated; the relics were distributed in eight portions to representatives of the kingdoms and republics of the Gangetic plain, plus one portion of the embers and one portion of the urn — the foundation of the *stūpa* veneration cult that became the principal lay religious practice of Buddhist Asia.

## Religious consequences
- **Institutional foundation**: the Buddha's death precipitated the immediate question of monastic continuity, leading to the First Buddhist Council at Rājagṛha (~-400) convened by [[mahakasyapa]] with [[ananda-disciple]] as the canonical reciter of the suttas and Upāli as reciter of the Vinaya. This council is the foundational event of the Pali Tipiṭaka and of all subsequent Buddhist canonical literature.
- **Stūpa cult**: the eight-fold relic distribution (plus the embers and urn portions = ten Aśokan-era *stūpa*s, of which Aśoka's tradition holds he excavated seven and redistributed the relics into 84,000 *stūpa*s) institutes the *stūpa* veneration practice that becomes the principal layfollower religious activity of Buddhist Asia for the next two millennia.
- **Chronological anchor**: the parinirvāṇa is the baseline for all Buddhist chronology. Traditional Theravāda dating: ~-483 BCE. "Corrected long chronology" (Bechert et al. 1991-1997): ~-400 / -380. Most current scholarship favors the later dating.
- **The "Buddha-absence problem"**: how does Buddhism continue without the Buddha? The canonical answer is that the Dhamma is now the teacher (the Buddha's exhortation in the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta: "Be islands unto yourselves, with the Dhamma as your island, with the Dhamma as your refuge"). This question generates the entire subsequent Buddhological literature — from the early-Buddhist *arahant*-as-final-figure model to the Mahāyāna *trikāya* (three-bodies) doctrine to the Pure Land *Sambhogakāya* and Vajrayāna deity-yoga.

## Documents in play
- **Mahāparinibbāna Sutta** (Dīgha Nikāya 16, with Sanskrit and Chinese parallels) — the principal canonical death-narrative; one of the longest Pali suttas. Walshe 1995 is the standard English version.
- **[[phase-2-016-early-buddhist-suttas]]** — the broader early Pali stratum was recited at the First Council triggered by the parinirvāṇa.
- **[[phase-3-030-asokan-edicts|Aśokan Lumbinī pillar]]** (~-249) — ~150 years post-parinirvāṇa, Aśoka in person commemorates the Buddha's birthplace, providing the strongest single piece of independent epigraphic attestation for the Buddha's historicity (see [[siddhartha-gautama-buddha]]).

## Disputes
- **Date**: Traditional Theravāda dating (~-483) vs. corrected long chronology (~-400). Bechert et al. 1991-1997 established the consensus shift toward the later dating, with the parinirvāṇa now most commonly placed ~-400 to -380 BCE. The shift was driven by reanalysis of the Sri Lankan Pali chronologies and the Aśokan synchronisms.
- **Cause of death**: the *sūkaramaddava* dish: pork-derivative or mushroom/truffle? Pali tradition is ambiguous; the term literally means "soft pig-thing" but could refer to a mushroom that pigs root for. Theravāda commentarial tradition (Buddhaghosa) leans toward truffle; Mahāyāna tradition often assumes pork. Doctrinally irrelevant; biographically uncertain.
- **Authenticity of recorded last words**: the *vayadhammā saṅkhārā* formula is found across recensions; its historicity as the actual final utterance is unrecoverable but its function as canonical valediction is uncontested.
- **Eight-fold relic distribution**: hagiographic? Modern archaeological excavation (e.g., the Piprahwa stupa relic-casket inscription, "of the Sākyas of the Buddha the Blessed One") has yielded relic-deposits that are early but cannot be unambiguously dated to the original distribution.
