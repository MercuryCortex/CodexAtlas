# Decian Persecution (250-251 CE)

## What happened
In late 249 or early 250 CE, the new emperor [[decius-emperor]] issued an edict requiring **every inhabitant of the Roman Empire to sacrifice to the gods**, taste the sacrificial food, and obtain a written certificate (***libellus***) of compliance. The edict was not explicitly anti-Christian, but its universal scope and the requirement of *pagan* sacrifice made it *de facto* a comprehensive test that Christians (and to a lesser extent Jews, exempted via local privilege) were uniquely unable to pass. The edict produced:

(a) **Martyrdoms** of those who refused to sacrifice — most notably **Pope Fabian** at Rome (January 20, 250), **Babylas of Antioch**, **Alexander of Jerusalem** (died in prison), and many others;

(b) **Mass *lapsi*** (lapsed Christians) — Christians who sacrificed, obtained false certificates from sympathetic officials, or otherwise compromised;

(c) **Refugees** — clergy and laity who fled, including [[cyprian-of-carthage|Cyprian]] himself, who left Carthage for safety and remained in contact by letter throughout the crisis.

The persecution ended abruptly with **Decius's death in battle at Abrittus (Bulgaria) in June 251**.

## Religious consequences
- **First empire-wide systematic persecution**: distinct from previous local or court-driven episodes. Set precedent for [[valerian-emperor|Valerian]] (257–258) and [[diocletian-emperor|Diocletian]] (303–311).
- **The *lapsi* crisis**: post-persecution, the question of how to handle Christians who had sacrificed split the church into rigorist (Novatian at Rome, Felicissimus's separate Carthaginian faction) and moderate (Cyprian, Pope Cornelius) parties. The crisis produced foundational pastoral-ecclesiological texts: [[cyprian-of-carthage|Cyprian]]'s ***De lapsis*** and ***De ecclesiae unitate*** (both ~+251).
- **Confessor authority**: those who suffered for the faith but survived (*confessores*) acquired enormous spiritual authority and could (per their own claim) grant *libelli pacis* (peace-certificates) restoring lapsed Christians to communion — bypassing episcopal authority. Cyprian's correspondence wrestles with this confessor-authority crisis.
- **Origen's death**: imprisoned and tortured under the persecution; survived but died shortly after, possibly from after-effects (~+253/+254).
- **Schism**: Novatian at Rome breaks from Pope Cornelius over rigorist treatment of the lapsi, producing the long-lasting Novatianist schism.

## Documents in play
- ~45 physical *libelli* recovered from Egypt — direct documentary evidence.
- [[cyprian-of-carthage|Cyprian]]'s *De lapsis*, *De ecclesiae unitate*, and letters (especially Epp. 1–66).
- Eusebius *HE* 6.39–46.

## Disputes
- **Intent**: targeted anti-Christian policy or universal religious-conformity measure with Christians as principally affected? Mainstream: universal, with Christians as the principal victims by structural necessity.
- **Scale of mortality**: traditional Christian accounts probably exaggerated; estimates of named martyrs are in the low hundreds, with the great majority lapsing.
- **Date of the edict**: probably winter 249/250.
