# Order of the Solar Temple mass suicides (1994–1997)

**Critical-tiering note:** This node documents a **modern criminal religious-violence event** in which 74 people died across three coordinated mass-suicide-and-murder incidents in 1994–1997. Per Codex Atlas methodology on contested-bridge documentation, the entry treats the OTS soberly as **a documented late-20th-c. neo-Templarist apocalyptic group whose theology drew on the post-1750 Templar-revival tradition** ([[tradition-strict-templar-observance|Strict Templar Observance]] → [[bernard-raymond-fabre-palaprat|Fabré-Palaprat]] → 19th-c. occult Templar literature → 20th-c. esoteric continuation), and whose ideational lineage is documented in the broader [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis]].

## What happened
The **Ordre du Temple Solaire (OTS)** was a Swiss/French/Canadian neo-Templarist apocalyptic-esoteric group founded **~1984 in Geneva by Joseph Di Mambro and Luc Jouret**, drawing on the modern Western neo-Templarist tradition that descends institutionally and ideationally from the **18th-c. Masonic Templar-revival** ([[tradition-strict-templar-observance|von Hund]] 1751), the **19th-c. Fabré-Palaprat Larmenius-Charter tradition** (1804), the **late-19th-c. Theosophical milieu** (Blavatsky, Annie Besant), and various **20th-c. French and Quebecois esoteric organizations**. Membership reached ~150 active adherents at peak across French-speaking Switzerland, France, and Quebec.

The OTS produced an integrated doctrine combining:
- **Templar succession-claim** (with the broader post-Fabré-Palaprat continuity-of-Templarism construction).
- **Apocalyptic millennialism** (an imminent ecological-catastrophe + Christ-return scenario).
- **Theosophical-Hermetic-Rosicrucian** content (drawn from 19th-20th-c. esoteric tradition).
- **Salvific-passage-to-Sirius doctrine** (Di Mambro taught that members' deaths would translate them to the star Sirius for cosmic regeneration).

The **mass-suicide-and-murder events** occurred in three coordinated waves:

1. **5 October 1994 — Cheiry (Switzerland)** + **Salvan (Switzerland)**: **53 dead** (mix of suicides and murders, including a 3-month-old infant; including Di Mambro and Jouret).
2. **16 December 1995 — Vercors (France)**: **16 dead** (mix of suicides and murders).
3. **22–23 March 1997 — Saint-Casimir, Quebec**: **5 dead** (the final wave; coordinated with the spring-equinox-and-Hale-Bopp-comet-passage).

Total: **74 dead** across the three events. The mass-suicide-and-murder pattern is one of the highest-casualty examples of late-20th-c. religious-violence and was investigated by Swiss, French, and Canadian police, and academically by Massimo Introvigne's CESNUR center.

## Religious-and-cultural consequences

- **One of the most-consequential modern religious-violence events** in Western Europe and North America.
- **Documented modern example of how the 19th-c. Templar-revival tradition migrated into 20th-c. apocalyptic-cult settings.** Di Mambro and Jouret had been members of various 1970s-80s European Rosicrucian and Templar-revival groups (Rosicrucian Fellowship; AMORC adjacency; various neo-Templar bodies) before founding the OTS.
- **Substantial impact on Swiss, French, and Quebec public-policy** regarding new-religious-movement regulation post-1994.
- **The OTS is the principal late-20th-c. case-study in the academic-study-of-religion literature** on neo-Templarism and the broader cult-violence phenomenon — Introvigne 1995/1999; Hall, Schuyler & Trinh 2000; Lewis 2006.

## Documents in play
- **Testament of the OTS** (~3,000 pages; delivered by mail to French media October 1994; the principal doctrinal document).
- Swiss, French, and Canadian police investigation reports.
- CESNUR (Center for Studies on New Religions) research documentation.
- Di Mambro and Jouret's pre-1994 lectures and writings.

## Disputes
- **The mix of suicide and murder.** Police investigations established that **not all deaths were suicides**; some adult members and infants were killed by other members. The "mass suicide" framing is partly inaccurate.
- **Di Mambro's and Jouret's personal motivations.** Investigations suggest Di Mambro was substantially driven by financial-and-control-of-followers motives alongside any ideational belief; Jouret may have been more ideationally-committed. The precise mix is unrecoverable.
- **The Sirius-passage doctrine's relation to OTS practice.** Documented in surviving OTS materials; integrated with broader Theosophical-cosmic-evolution metaphysics.
