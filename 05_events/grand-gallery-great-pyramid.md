# Grand Gallery (Great Pyramid of Khufu)

## Physical description

The **Grand Gallery** is the most architecturally extraordinary feature of the Great Pyramid — and possibly of all ancient Egyptian architecture. It is a corbelled limestone corridor that rises at the same angle as the Ascending Passage (~26.5°) for 47 metres before leveling off into the Antechamber and then the King's Chamber.

**Dimensions (Petrie 1883):**
- Length: 47.09 m along the floor
- Width at base: 2.09 m
- Height: **8.74 m** (the corbelling rises through 7 successive courses of limestone, each course overhanging the one below by ~7.5 cm per side, until the ceiling reaches its apex)
- Floor: a sunken central channel (~0.52 m wide, ~0.22 m deep) flanked by two raised limestone ramps with 27 pairs of **niches** cut into their outer edges

The corbelled vault is a masterpiece of structural engineering — the overlapping courses distribute the compression force laterally rather than downward, creating a stable arch-like structure without a true arch. No mortar; friction and precise fitting carry the load.

## The 27 pairs of niches

The most archaeologically puzzling feature: 27 pairs of rectangular slots cut into the ramps flanking the central channel. Each slot is approximately 53 cm × 17 cm. They served some mechanical purpose — the universal interpretation is that they held wooden beams or runners used in the final lowering of the granite blocking plugs that sealed the Ascending Passage after the burial. But 27 seems excessive for this function; some researchers propose the niches held a counterweight system, a hauling mechanism for the relief chambers' granite beams, or a completely different unknown device.

The central channel on the floor (not the ramp niches) is also unexplained — it may have been a conduit for builders' tools, or a drainage channel, or part of the slot-mechanism.

## Sacred geometry claims

The Grand Gallery's dimensions have attracted attention from proportional analysts:
- **Its height-to-width ratio** encodes a near-perfect expression of the **Royal Cubit** and the Pythagorean 3:4:5 triangle (the angle of the Ascending Passage, 26.5°, is close to arctan(1/2), implying a 1:2 rise:run)
- The **total length** has been proposed to encode a precessional constant (the ~26,000-year precession of the equinoxes expressed in Pyramid Inches — a unit invented by John Taylor and Charles Piazzi Smyth in the 1860s; rejected by mainstream metrology)
- Petrie explicitly debunked Piazzi Smyth's "Pyramid Inch" measurements, noting they relied on selective measurement of ambiguous features and were not replicable

**[[theme-sacred-geometry-giza]]** documents both the legitimate proportional observations (Royal Cubit encoding is genuinely present in the structure) and the debunked Pyramid Inch tradition.

## The "Big Void" above the Grand Gallery

The 2017 **ScanPyramids muon tomography** discovery reported a large void directly above the Grand Gallery — at least 30 m long, with a similar cross-section to the Grand Gallery itself. This cavity was unknown to all previous investigations. Its purpose, whether it contains anything, and whether it is intentionally constructed or a natural feature of the limestone, remain completely open as of 2026.

The discovery proves that even one of the most studied monuments in the world contains unexamined spaces.

## Approach and theology

The Grand Gallery is the **ascent** — the architectural embodiment of the king's soul rising from the horizontal world of the living (the subterranean and Queen's chambers) toward the celestial transformation (the King's Chamber with its Orion and Thuban shafts). The dramatic height (8.74 m from a passage barely 1 m high) is a deliberate architectural revelation — the ceiling vaults away above the entrant in sudden darkness.

In the **[[theme-pyramid-as-resurrection-machine]]** framework: the Subterranean Chamber is the chthonic/Osirian realm; the Queen's Chamber is an intermediate station (associated with Isis/Sirius); the Grand Gallery is the transitional ascent; the King's Chamber is the solar transformation (*akh*). The pyramid is not a building that happened to contain spaces — it is a spatial narrative of royal death-and-resurrection.
