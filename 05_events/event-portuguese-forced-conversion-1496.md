# Manoel I's Forced Conversion Decree (1496–1497)

## Event
In December 1496, Manoel I of Portugal issued a decree ordering all Jews and Moors in Portugal to convert to Christianity or leave within ten months. Unlike the 1492 Spanish Expulsion (which allowed Jews to leave), the Portuguese decree was designed to *prevent departure* while forcing conversion: when Jews arrived at the ports to embark, their children were seized for baptism and scattered across the country, compelling parents to submit to baptism rather than abandon their children. In March 1497, mass forced baptisms occurred at Lisbon's Rossio square. The community that had survived the Spanish Expulsion — many of whom had fled to Portugal with their assets — was absorbed by force into the Christian population.

## Distinction from the 1492 Spanish Expulsion
The 1492 Castilian/Aragonese expulsion gave Jews a choice (leave or convert) and honored departures. The 1496 Portuguese decree was structurally more coercive: it was calculated to produce mass conversion by making departure practically impossible (child-seizure policy). This produced a distinct New Christian community that was, by definition, composed of involuntary converts — a legally Christian population with strong motivation to maintain Jewish practice covertly. The Portuguese New Christians (sometimes called *cristãos-novos* or *Marranos*, a derogatory term) became one of the most religiously complex communities in European history: publicly Catholic, privately varying from genuine assimilation to decades-long underground Judaism.

## Cross-tradition significance

**The Inquisition as response**
The Portuguese New Christian community was sufficiently large and visibly dual-practicing that pressure mounted for an inquisition. The Portuguese Inquisition was formally established in 1536 under João III; it operated until 1821 and focused primarily on New Christians suspected of *judaizing* (covert Jewish practice). The Portuguese Inquisition, unlike the Spanish (founded 1478), was established specifically to surveil the converso community created by the forced conversion. The covert Judaism of the New Christian community is the context for Iberian crypto-Kabbalistic practice and represents one of history's most sustained examples of religious underground persistence.

**Connection to Lurianic Kabbalah**
The Spanish Expulsion (1492) and Portuguese forced conversion (1497) together expelled or forcibly converted the entire Iberian Jewish community — the most intellectually and economically developed Jewish community in the world. Within 70 years, the spiritual descendants of this community were in Safed producing Lurianic Kabbalah. Gershom Scholem's thesis: the Lurianic doctrines of *tzimtzum* (divine contraction), *shevirat ha-kelim* (cosmic shattering), and *tikkun* (repair) are the Iberian catastrophe written in metaphysical code — an attempt to explain and give cosmic meaning to the greatest Jewish disaster since the Temple's destruction. The connection between this event and Isaac Luria's Safed circle (see [[isaac-luria]]) is the vault's strongest link between a historical catastrophe and a mystical theology.

**New Christians and the Atlantic world**
Portuguese New Christians became major participants in the Atlantic trade networks (Brazil, West Africa, the spice trade). Their social and economic position — legally Catholic, culturally Jewish, economically mobile, subject to ongoing Inquisition pressure — made them a uniquely cross-cultural community. New Christian merchants were among the primary carriers of printed books (including Hebrew books and Kabbalistic texts) across the Atlantic world. The Sabbatean movement of 1665–1666 received disproportionate support from Portuguese New Christian diaspora communities (Amsterdam, Livorno, Salonika) — communities descended from those forcibly converted in 1497.
