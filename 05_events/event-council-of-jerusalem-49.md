# Council of Jerusalem (c. 49 CE)

## What happened
A meeting at Jerusalem ~+49 CE between the **Antiochene Christian delegation** ([[paul-of-tarsus|Paul]], [[barnabas]], and others — per Paul, [[titus]] as well) and the **Jerusalem leadership** ([[james-brother-of-jesus|James]], [[peter-apostle|Cephas/Peter]], and John). The core question: should Gentile converts to Christianity be required to undergo circumcision and Torah-observance, or are they admitted to the community by faith alone?

We have **two surviving accounts** that significantly diverge:
- **Galatians 2:1–10** (Paul, ~+56/+57): Paul presented his Gentile gospel privately to the "pillars" (James, Cephas, John); they accepted his mission without modification; Titus was not compelled to be circumcised; the only condition was remembering the poor.
- **Acts 15** (Luke, ~+85/+95): a public council convened; Peter speaks first endorsing Gentile inclusion; James delivers the formal decision; the *Apostolic Decree* (abstain from meat sacrificed to idols, from blood, from things strangled, from sexual immorality) is issued.

The two accounts cannot be straightforwardly harmonized. The Galatians silence on the Apostolic Decree is critically important — most likely it was a later Antiochene/Jerusalem development that Acts has retrojected.

## Religious consequences
- **Foundation of Gentile-inclusive Christianity**: the meeting essentially decided that Christianity would be a movement for Jews *and* Gentiles, with Gentile-converts not required to undergo full Torah-observance. Without this decision Christianity would likely have remained a Jewish sect.
- **The Antioch incident** (Gal 2:11–14): shortly after, at Antioch, Peter withdrew from table-fellowship with Gentile Christians under pressure from "men from James," provoking Paul's public rebuke. The Jerusalem meeting did not resolve the practical food-fellowship question.
- **Pauline Gentile mission**: the meeting legitimated Paul's continuing Gentile mission throughout the Mediterranean.
- **Long-term consequences**: the *Apostolic Decree* (in Luke's framing) became a kind of minimal-law for Gentile Christians and influenced subsequent church discipline.

## Documents in play
- [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] Galatians 1–2 (Paul's account)

## Disputes
- **Date**: ~+49 standard; ~+47–51 range.
- **Number of meetings**: some scholars (Lüdemann, others) propose the Galatians and Acts accounts describe *different* meetings — Paul's private consultation (Gal 2) and a later public Jerusalem council (Acts 15).
- **Historicity of the Apostolic Decree**: Paul's silence on it suggests it post-dates his Galatians composition; possibly an Antiochene-Jerusalem agreement from the early 50s reflected in Luke retrospectively.
- **James's role**: Paul has him as one of three pillars; Acts has him deliver the decision — Acts probably reflects the actual ecclesiastical authority of James in the 50s/60s as it crystallized.
