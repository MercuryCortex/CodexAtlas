# Battle of Gaugamela (331 BCE)

The decisive engagement (1 October 331 BCE) in which [[alexander-the-great|Alexander III]] destroyed the Achaemenid Persian field army of Darius III Codomannus and ended the [[darius-i-the-great|Darius I]]-founded Achaemenid Empire as a political entity. The battle is the **political-military terminus of the Persian period (539-331 BCE)** that had begun with [[cyrus-the-great|Cyrus the Great]]'s 539 BCE conquest of Babylon. Alexander's victory delivered the Near East — including the Levant, Mesopotamia, and Iran — into Hellenistic political dispensation; Darius III was murdered by his own satrap Bessus the following year (330 BCE), ending the Achaemenid royal line. The political-cultural consequences are enormous: the Hellenistic world-system that becomes the substrate of New Testament Christianity emerges from the post-Gaugamela arrangement.
