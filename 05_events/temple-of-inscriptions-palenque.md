# Temple of Inscriptions (Palenque)

## The monument

The **Temple of Inscriptions** is a nine-level step pyramid at **Palenque** (Chiapas, Mexico), built ~683–692 CE as the funerary monument of the great Maya ruler **K'inich Janaab' Pakal I** ("Pakal the Great"). It is the most important funerary pyramid in the Maya world and the site of one of the most significant archaeological discoveries of the 20th century: a hidden royal tomb inside the pyramid, found intact.

**Dimensions:**
- Height: ~27 m (nine levels)
- Summit temple: 5 chambers; the interior walls carry the three great hieroglyphic panels (1,500+ glyphs) that give the temple its name — recording Pakal's genealogy and the mythology of royal rulership

**Why nine levels:** The nine-tiered form mirrors the **nine levels of Xibalba** — the Maya underworld. The pyramid is a cosmological map: the king descends into the underworld at death and must pass through all nine levels before resurrection. The pyramid is literally the path of the descent.

## The discovery: Alberto Ruz Lhuillier (1949–1952)

In **1949**, Mexican archaeologist **Alberto Ruz Lhuillier** noticed that the floor of the summit temple had a peculiar slab with finger holes cut into it. Lifting it, he found a rubble-filled staircase descending into the pyramid. It took **four excavation seasons** (1949–1952) to clear the staircase of the rubble deliberately packed by the ancient builders.

On **June 15, 1952**, Ruz reached a triangular blocking stone at the bottom, removed it, and entered the **Tomb of Pakal** — a vaulted limestone chamber 9 m × 4 m × 7 m deep within the pyramid's base, still intact after 1,268 years.

**Contents:**
- A massive limestone sarcophagus (~5 m × 3.8 m × ~3.8 m, weighing ~20 tonnes) carved from a single block, with a carved lid
- Inside: **Pakal's skeleton** wearing a jade mosaic death mask, jade jewelry, red cinnabar powder (ritual coloring for the dead), and jade burial offerings
- Nine figures in stucco relief on the chamber walls: the Nine Lords of the Night (Bolontiku), each presiding over a level of the underworld
- A **psychoduct** (thin hollow tube) running from the sarcophagus up the staircase to the summit temple, allowing Pakal's spirit to communicate with the living

The sarcophagus was too large to have been brought down the staircase; it was placed in the tomb chamber during the pyramid's construction, and the pyramid was built around it.

## The sarcophagus lid: Mayanist interpretation vs. von Däniken

The carved limestone lid of Pakal's sarcophagus (2.4 m × 3.6 m) is one of the most analyzed single objects in ancient art.

**What it actually depicts (Mayanist consensus):** Pakal at the moment of death, falling backward down the World Tree (Wacah Chan, the Maya axis mundi) into Xibalba, the underworld. Above him rises the Celestial Bird (Itzam-Ye). Around the border, the bones and skeletal jaws of death frame the scene. This is standard Maya royal death-and-resurrection iconography.

**What Erich von Däniken claimed (1968):** In *Chariots of the Gods*, von Däniken reproduced the lid and stated it showed an astronaut at the controls of a spaceship — the figure's posture (reclining, hands forward) resembles a pilot; the "machinery" below is the rocket engine. He provided no hieroglyphic or iconographic analysis.

**Why this matters to the Atlas:** The "Pakal astronaut" is one of the most widely reproduced ancient-aliens claims and a textbook case of **decontextualized iconography** — removing an image from its cultural grammar and substituting an external interpretive framework. It has been exhaustively debunked but continues to circulate. The iconographic literature (Schele & Miller 1986; Martin & Skidmore 2012) provides the definitive reading.

## Cross-pyramid theology: Palenque vs. Egypt

The Temple of Inscriptions and the Great Pyramid share remarkable structural parallels as independently developed cross-tradition patterns:

| Feature | Temple of Inscriptions | Great Pyramid |
|---|---|---|
| Hidden descending passage | Yes — sealed staircase, rubble-filled | Yes — Descending Passage |
| Burial in pyramid body | Yes — sarcophagus placed during construction | Debated |
| Sarcophagus wider than entrance | Yes — placed first, pyramid built around it | Yes — coffer wider than Ascending Passage |
| Vertical cosmological narrative | Yes — nine levels = nine underworld levels | Yes — subterranean/Queen's/King's sequence |
| Astronomical alignment | Yes — sunset at equinoxes; the sun descends into the temple | Yes — stellar shaft alignments |
| Spirit communication device | Psychoduct to summit | Possibly: shafts as *ba* conduits |

These parallels are **convergent independent development** — Maya and Egyptian pyramid theology arose separately — but they illuminate common patterns in how humans map death-and-resurrection cosmology onto built space.
