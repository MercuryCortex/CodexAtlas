# Death of Muhammad and the Saqīfa Banī Sāʿida succession (632 CE / 11 AH)

## What happened
**Muhammad died in Medina on Monday, 12 Rabīʿ al-Awwal 11 AH (June 8, 632 CE)** in the chamber of his wife [[aisha-bint-abi-bakr|ʿĀʾisha]], after a short final illness. He was buried in ʿĀʾisha's chamber (now the *Rawḍa al-Sharīfa* within the Masjid al-Nabawī in Medina, alongside the later graves of [[abu-bakr|Abū Bakr]] and [[umar-ibn-al-khattab|ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb]]).

**The succession was not unambiguously settled at the time of death.** Per the canonical Sunni Sira-tradition (Ibn Isḥāq, Ṭabarī), Muhammad had not formally designated a successor — though the canonical narrative emphasizes that Muhammad had asked [[abu-bakr|Abū Bakr]] to lead prayers during his final illness, which Sunni tradition reads as an implicit succession-designation. Per the Shīʿī tradition, Muhammad had **explicitly designated [[ali-ibn-abi-talib|ʿAlī]] as successor** at the Ghadīr Khumm sermon (~March 632, shortly before Muhammad's death — Quran 5:67 and *ḥadīth al-ghadīr* "man kuntu mawlāhu fa-ʿAlī mawlāhu" [whoever I am his patron, ʿAlī is his patron]). The Sunni-Shīʿī divergence on the meaning of the Ghadīr Khumm declaration is foundational.

While ʿAlī was occupied with washing Muhammad's body and burial preparations (the *Ahl al-Bayt* funeral duties), **a meeting of the Anṣār (the Medinan Helpers) convened at the Saqīfa Banī Sāʿida (the roofed-courtyard of the Banū Sāʿida clan)** to deliberate succession. The Anṣārī candidate was the Khazraji leader **Saʿd ibn ʿUbāda**; the meeting reflected the Medinan Helpers' concern that the leadership of the Muslim *umma* not pass entirely to the Meccan Muhājirūn (Emigrants). When [[abu-bakr|Abū Bakr]] and [[umar-ibn-al-khattab|ʿUmar]] were informed (with Abū ʿUbayda ibn al-Jarrāḥ), they rushed to the Saqīfa and intervened: ʿUmar drove the discussion toward selecting Abū Bakr as caliph (citing Abū Bakr's status as Muhammad's closest companion and prayer-leader during the final illness), and the meeting **elected Abū Bakr by acclamation** with ʿUmar leading the *bayʿa* (oath of allegiance). The Anṣārī compromise was that succession was settled among the Quraysh-Muhājirūn rather than going to a Medinan-Helper candidate.

ʿAlī and the *Ahl al-Bayt* learned of the Abū Bakr selection only after the fact. ʿAlī's response is disputed across the source traditions: **the Sunni narrative** (Ṭabarī, Bukhārī) has ʿAlī giving *bayʿa* to Abū Bakr after some delay (most commonly placed at six months, after Fāṭima's death); **the Shīʿī narrative** has ʿAlī refusing for the duration of Abū Bakr's caliphate and giving *bayʿa* only under duress or only after ʿUmar's death. The dispute also includes the **Fāṭima-Fadak controversy** — Fāṭima's claim against Abū Bakr for inheritance of Muhammad's Fadak-estate, which Abū Bakr denied on the basis of the hadith *"naḥnu maʿāshira l-anbiyāʾ lā nūrithu, mā taraknāhu ṣadaqa"* (Prophets are not inherited from; what we leave is alms).

## Religious consequences
1. **Foundation of the Sunni-Shīʿī divide.** The Saqīfa is the **foundational fault-line of Sunni-Shīʿī sectarian divergence**. Sunni tradition: the Saqīfa was legitimate consultative succession (*shūrā*); Abū Bakr's selection was consistent with Muhammad's implicit indications. Shīʿī tradition: the Saqīfa was an illegitimate sidelining of ʿAlī, whose succession was divinely-designated at Ghadīr Khumm.
2. **Establishment of the *shūrā* / consultative-selection paradigm.** The Saqīfa pattern (consultation among senior Companions, *bayʿa* / oath of allegiance, public acclamation) becomes the **Rashidun-period succession model** (Abū Bakr → ʿUmar via Abū Bakr's designation; ʿUmar → ʿUthmān via the six-member *shūrā* council; ʿUthmān → ʿAlī via Medinan public acclamation after ʿUthmān's assassination). The model breaks down under the Umayyad hereditary-succession transition ([[muawiya-ibn-abi-sufyan|Muʿāwiya]] → Yazīd I designation c. 676).
3. **Ridda wars (632-634).** Multiple Arabian tribes treated their political-religious obligation as personal to Muhammad and refused to continue paying *zakāt* (alms) to Abū Bakr; Abū Bakr's first-year caliphate was occupied with the *Ḥurūb al-Ridda* (Apostasy Wars) restoring Arabian-peninsular Muslim political authority. This **fixed the Quraysh-Muhājirūn central-authority pattern** and prepared the way for the Rashidun conquests under ʿUmar.
4. **Quranic canonization.** Abū Bakr's caliphate (632-634) saw the **first major effort at Quranic canonical compilation** under Zayd ibn Thābit's leadership, in response to the loss of Quran-reciters at the Battle of Yamāma (632) during the Ridda wars. This is the first stage of the canonization-process completed under [[uthman|ʿUthmān]] ~650.
5. **End of revelation.** Muhammad's death **closes the period of divine revelation** in Islamic theology. Subsequent religious authority is mediated through the Quran (as canonically fixed) and the Sunna (as preserved in the hadith corpus) and the consensus of the Companions, with no further direct prophetic revelation. The closure-of-revelation doctrine is foundational for the Islamic-tradition distinction from continuing-revelation traditions ([[tradition-baha-i|Bahaism]], [[tradition-mormonism|Mormonism]], etc.).

## Documents in play
- **[[phase-4-034-quran]]** — Quran 5:3 (Farewell Pilgrimage final revelation per traditional reading) and 33:40 (*"Muhammad is the Messenger of God and the Seal of the Prophets"* — the foundational text for the closure-of-prophethood doctrine).
- **[[phase-5-043-ibn-ishaq-sirat-rasul-allah]]** — the canonical Sira-narrative of Muhammad's death and the Saqīfa.
- **[[phase-5-007-sahih-al-bukhari]]** + **[[phase-5-042-sahih-muslim]]** — extensive hadith on Muhammad's death, Abū Bakr's prayer-leadership during the final illness, the Saqīfa narrative, the Fāṭima-Fadak controversy. Sunni and Shīʿī isnād-tradition diverges on key hadith.

## Disputes
- **Did Muhammad designate ʿAlī at Ghadīr Khumm?** Shīʿī tradition: yes, explicitly. Sunni tradition: the Ghadīr Khumm *hadith* (*"man kuntu mawlāhu fa-ʿAlī mawlāhu"*) is preserved in Sunni hadith but is read as an honorific declaration of friendship-and-loyalty rather than as an explicit succession-designation. Madelung 1997 surveys the source-critical issues sympathetically toward the Shīʿī reading while preserving the Sunni alternative.
- **Length of ʿAlī's delay in giving *bayʿa* to Abū Bakr.** Sunni: six months. Shīʿī: extended or under duress.
- **Authenticity of the Fāṭima-Fadak hadith.** *"naḥnu maʿāshira l-anbiyāʾ lā nūrithu"* — Sunni: authentic, supports Abū Bakr's denial. Shīʿī: contested, sometimes treated as Sunni-political-fabrication.
- **Date of Muhammad's death.** 12 Rabīʿ al-Awwal 11 AH is the canonical Sunni date; Shīʿī tradition has 28 Ṣafar 11 AH as alternative.
