# Proto-Sinaitic — The First Alphabet

## What it is
Proto-Sinaitic is the earliest known alphabetic writing — the ancestor, by a single line of descent, of very nearly every alphabet used on earth today. It appears around 1850–1700 BCE in graffiti carved by West Semitic ("Canaanite") workers in the Egyptian turquoise mines of Serabit el-Khadim in Sinai, and at Wadi el-Hol in Egypt itself. Its roughly two dozen signs are simplified pictures borrowed from Egyptian hieroglyphs, but used on a radically new principle: not one sign per word or syllable (as in Egyptian or cuneiform), but one sign per consonant.

## The acrophonic leap
The genius of Proto-Sinaitic is **acrophony**: a picture stands for the first sound of the name of the thing pictured. The Egyptian hieroglyph of a *house* (Semitic *bayt*) is reused to write the sound **b**; an *ox-head* (*ʾalp*) writes the glottal **ʾ** (aleph); *water* (*mayim*) writes **m**. A speaker of a Semitic language, in which roots are carried by consonants, could thus capture speech with only ~22–27 signs that any worker could learn — instead of the hundreds of signs that took Egyptian and Mesopotamian scribes years to master. Writing ceased to be the monopoly of a professional class.

## Context
- **Who invented it.** Not Egyptian scribes but Semitic-speaking laborers and miners in Egyptian service — a rare case of a world-changing technology arising from the bottom of a society, "the invention of the alphabet by illiterates" (Goldwasser).
- **Religious milieu.** Several Sinai inscriptions invoke the goddess "Baʿalat" (the Lady, an Egyptian-Canaanite Hathor/Asherah figure), tying the first alphabet to West Semitic cult.
- **Transmission.** Proto-Sinaitic → Proto-Canaanite (linear scripts of Bronze Age Canaan) → the standardized **Phoenician** abjad (~1050 BCE).

## Themes and motifs
- The democratization of writing — a learnable script versus a scribal monopoly.
- Acrophony — the picture-to-sound principle that made the alphabet possible.
- Levantine-Egyptian contact — the cultural seam where the alphabet was born.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **One invention behind almost every alphabet on earth — and the letters still carry their Bronze Age names.** Proto-Sinaitic is the single root from which the Phoenician, Greek, Latin, Cyrillic, Aramaic, Hebrew, Arabic, and (via Aramaic) Brahmic and even Mongolian scripts all descend. Its fingerprints are still visible: the Hebrew letter-names *aleph* (ox), *bet* (house), *gimel* (camel/throwstick), *dalet* (door) are the Semitic words for the things the original Sinai pictures depicted, frozen into the alphabet ~3,800 years ago and inherited — as *alpha, beta, gamma, delta* and then *A, B, C, D* — by every Western reader since. That the inventors were West Semitic workers in the Egyptian orbit places the birth of the alphabet in exactly the Levantine-Egyptian world the Bible remembers as the matrix of Israel's origins (Sass 1991; Hamilton 2006; Goldwasser 2010).
