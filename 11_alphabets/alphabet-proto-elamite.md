# Proto-Elamite — The World's Oldest Undecoded Writing

## Identity

Proto-Elamite is the **oldest undecoded writing system** in the world. Approximately **5,000 clay tablets** survive, primarily from the excavations at **Susa** (modern Shush, Khuzestan Province, Iran) — the ancient capital of Elam — and from Anshan (Tal-i Malyan), Chogha Mish, and Tepe Yahya. The script was in use c. **3200–2900 BCE**, making it contemporary with — or possibly slightly older than — the earliest Sumerian cuneiform from Uruk.

The script uses approximately **1,000 distinct signs** (substantially more than early Sumerian cuneiform's ~700). It has **never been decoded**. Unlike Linear A, Proto-Elamite does not have a known decoded descendant that could serve as a phonetic crib. Unlike Mayan glyphs, it has no surviving bilingual document. It is an archive without a key.

## The Priority Dispute — Was Writing Invented Once or Twice?

The most important unresolved question about Proto-Elamite is whether it was invented **independently** of Sumerian cuneiform, or whether it developed under Sumerian influence.

The conventional history of writing goes: writing was invented once, in Sumer (southern Iraq), c. 3200 BCE, for economic record-keeping at Uruk, and then diffused outward. This narrative has Proto-Elamite as a secondary development, invented by the Elamites after contact with the Sumerian system.

The problem: **the dates are uncomfortably close**. The earliest Proto-Elamite tablets from Susa are dated c. 3200 BCE — approximately the same horizon as the earliest Uruk cuneiform tablets. Some analyses place the earliest Proto-Elamite **before** the earliest cuneiform. The two scripts share **no sign resemblances** — they use completely different visual vocabularies, unlike scripts that typically show derivation by gradual sign modification.

The current scholarly consensus is divided: some argue for Sumerian influence (economic contact between Susa and Uruk is documented in material culture before the scripts appear); others argue for **parallel independent invention** — that writing emerged in the same cultural horizon in two adjacent but culturally distinct societies simultaneously, the way fire was independently discovered at multiple sites.

If the independent-invention hypothesis is correct, it transforms the history of human literacy: **writing is not a single discovery but a cognitive capacity that emerges predictably when societies reach a certain complexity threshold** — a conclusion with major implications for how we understand language, cognition, and civilization.

## The Archive — What the Tablets Record

The content of the Proto-Elamite tablets is partially legible in terms of **function** (if not language):

- **Numerical sequences** are identifiable — the system uses a sexagesimal (base-60) framework similar to Sumerian, suggesting shared accounting conventions
- **Logograms for commodities** — signs that appear to represent grain, livestock, and oil in count-and-commodity format, parallel to Uruk-era administrative tablets
- **Personal names or institutional designations** — signs that may record the names of individuals, officials, or administrative units

The **content** is almost certainly **administrative** — inventory records, rations, livestock counts, grain distributions. This parallels the earliest cuneiform, which was also administrative before it became literary or religious. The tablets are the accounting records of a Bronze Age palace economy.

But we cannot read them. We cannot verify whether any tablet carries what might be a religious formula, a divine name, a royal decree, or a purely practical ration list. Every tablet is simultaneously a potential key to Elamite theology and an incomprehensible column of numbers.

## Elam — The Civilization Behind the Script

The Elamite civilization (c. 3200 BCE – 539 BCE) was one of the great powers of the ancient Near East. Its heartland was Khuzestan (modern southwest Iran), centered on Susa, one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities. Elam was the eastern rival of Mesopotamia across the entire span of ancient Near Eastern history:

- The **Elamites sacked Ur** (~2004 BCE), ending the Ur III dynasty — the subject of the famous Lamentation over the Destruction of Ur
- **Elamite kings** controlled Susa and Anshan across multiple dynasties spanning 2,000 years
- The **Middle Elamite period** (c. 1500–1100 BCE) produced significant religious art — the Untash-Napirisha temple complex at Chogha Zanbil is one of the best-preserved ziggurats in the world
- Elam was also the context for the **Book of Daniel**: Susa = **Shushan** (Daniel 8:2), the city where Daniel received visions; the Tomb of Daniel in Susa is a pilgrimage site for Jews, Christians, and Muslims

The script that preceded all this history — the script in use at the founding of Susa, at the earliest phase of this civilization — is unreadable.

## The Destruction of Elam

The Elamite civilization was effectively ended by the **Assyrian king Ashurbanipal**, who sacked Susa c. **647 BCE** and looted its temples and archives. Ashurbanipal left a boastful account:

> *"Susa, the great holy city, abode of their Gods, seat of their mysteries, I conquered. I entered its palaces, I opened their treasuries... I destroyed the ziggurat of Susa. I smashed its shining copper horns. I reduced the temples of Elam to naught; their gods and goddesses I scattered to the winds."*

The Elamite script tradition had already ceased c. 2900 BCE — Proto-Elamite was replaced by a later Linear Elamite script (also largely undecoded), and eventually by Old Elamite (partially decoded). But Proto-Elamite, the oldest stratum, had no descendant that preserved its readings. When Ashurbanipal destroyed Susa's archival tradition, whatever oral memory might have explained the oldest tablets was effectively extinguished.

The result: a city and civilization that survived 2,500 years left a foundational archive of 5,000 tablets that no one can read.

## MASSIVE WIN — The World's Oldest Unheard Archive

Proto-Elamite is the **negative space of the world's writing history**. Every other major ancient writing system has been decoded:

- Sumerian cuneiform ✓ (decoded via Akkadian bilingual)
- Egyptian hieroglyphs ✓ (Rosetta Stone)
- Mayan glyphs ✓ (Knorosov + phonetic grid)
- Linear B ✓ (Ventris 1952)
- Ugaritic ✓ (1929, via Semitic family)
- Old Persian ✓ (Behistun bilingual with Babylonian)

Proto-Elamite alone, among major archives with thousands of surviving inscriptions, **has never yielded**. The archive exists. The clay survived 5,000 years in the Khuzestan soil. The signs are recoverable. The language beneath them is not.

This is the clearest demonstration that decipherment depends not on survival of the material record but on survival of the **human chain of understanding** — the oral tradition, the bilingual contact, the continuous use that keeps a script readable. When Elam was absorbed and destroyed, the human chain broke. The archive went silent. The tablets became beautiful, durable, perfectly preserved statements that no one can hear.

## The Simultaneous-Invention Implication

If Proto-Elamite was independently invented (the evidence is genuinely ambiguous), then the world's first writing was invented **at least twice** in the same century, in adjacent societies with documented trade contact but no apparent script transfer.

This would mean:
1. Writing is not a single genius invention (like the wheel or agriculture) but a **predictable emergent technology** that complex palace economies generate when they reach a certain administrative complexity
2. The **cognitive architecture** for symbolic representation of spoken language was already present in human minds 5,200 years ago and would activate independently wherever the social conditions required it
3. The absence of writing is not evidence of cognitive limitation but of social organization — nomadic societies, small-scale agricultural societies, and oral-transmission-based cultures chose not to write (or found no institutional need to do so), not because they could not

This is a conclusion with implications across linguistics, cognitive science, and anthropology — and it hinges partly on whether the 5,000 unread tablets of Proto-Elamite turn out to represent an independent invention or a derivation.
