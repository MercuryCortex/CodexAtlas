# Yoga — Eight-Limbed Ritual Technology

Classical yoga is not a fitness practice. It is a ritual technology for the systematic deconstruction and reconstruction of consciousness, culminating in a state (*samadhi*) indistinguishable from what other traditions call mystical union, enlightenment, or divine absorption. [[patanjali-yoga-sutras-author]]'s *Yoga Sutras* (c. 400 CE) — [[phase-3-023-yoga-sutras-of-patanjali]] — define the practice with unprecedented philosophical precision: "Yoga is the cessation of the fluctuations of the mind" (*yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ*, 1.2). The cessation is the goal; the eight limbs are the path.

## The Eight Limbs (Ashtanga)

The staged structure is the key: (1) *Yama* — five ethical restraints: non-violence (*ahimsa*), truthfulness (*satya*), non-stealing (*asteya*), celibacy/continence (*brahmacharya*), non-possessiveness (*aparigraha*). (2) *Niyama* — five personal observances: purity (*shaucha*), contentment (*santosha*), austerity (*tapas*), self-study (*svadhyaya*), surrender to God (*Ishvara pranidhana*). (3) *Asana* — physical posture: stable, comfortable, with relaxed effort. (4) *Pranayama* — regulation of breath. (5) *Pratyahara* — withdrawal of the senses from external objects. (6) *Dharana* — concentration: fixing the mind on a single point/object. (7) *Dhyana* — meditation: unbroken flow of attention toward the object. (8) *Samadhi* — absorption: the meditator and the object of meditation become indistinguishable.

The structure is a staged initiation: ethical preparation (limbs 1–2) → physical preparation (limb 3) → energetic preparation (limb 4) → sensory preparation (limb 5) → consciousness transformation in three stages (limbs 6–8). Each limb is a prerequisite for the next.

## The Terminal State: Samadhi

*Samadhi* — the eighth limb — is the dissolution of the subject-object boundary. In *savikalpa samadhi* (with form), the meditator merges with the object of meditation but retains some awareness of the process. In *nirvikalpa samadhi* (without form), even that residue dissolves: pure consciousness without content. The *Yoga Sutras* further distinguish *samprajnata samadhi* (consciousness directed with a support) from *asamprajnata samadhi* (consciousness without any support). At the apex, the *purusha* (pure consciousness) recognizes itself as permanently distinct from *prakriti* (all matter including thought): this is *kaivalya* — aloneness, liberation.

## Patanjali's Buddhist Debt

The *Yoga Sutras*' meditation terminology is almost entirely shared with Buddhist *dhyana* literature: *dharana* corresponds to Buddhist *dharana*, *dhyana* corresponds to Buddhist *jhana/dhyana*, *samadhi* is shared, the four *jhanas* of Buddhist meditation map directly onto Patanjali's ascending samadhi states. The *Yoga Sutras* appear to be a Brahminical reframing of Buddhist meditation practice, imposing Samkhya metaphysics (the *purusha/prakriti* dualism) onto techniques that Buddhist texts had already formalised. Patanjali is synthesizing Buddhist praxis with Hindu metaphysics — he is writing the Hindu response to Buddhist meditation literature.

## Cross-Tradition

**The Staged Mystical Path**: Every major mystical tradition specifies a staged path with the same three-phase structure: (1) Ethical/purgative phase; (2) Illuminative/contemplative phase; (3) Union/transformative phase. Western Christian mysticism calls these *via purgativa / via illuminativa / via unitiva* (Pseudo-Dionysius, 5th c.). Sufi *maqamat* (stations) begin with *tawba* (repentance) and end with *fana* (annihilation of self) and *baqa* (subsistence in God). Buddhist path structure: *sila* (ethical conduct) → *samadhi* (concentration) → *prajna* (wisdom). Daoist internal alchemy: ethical cultivation → *jing* (essence) refinement → *qi* (breath) refinement → *shen* (spirit) refinement. The eight limbs of yoga map onto every one of these.

**Samadhi and Its Cognates**: The terminal state — consciousness dissolving into or merging with its object — appears across traditions: Sufi *fana* (annihilation of the nafs/ego in God; the self ceases and only God remains); Christian *unio mystica* (Bernard of Clairvaux: "the soul which loves God is so filled with God that it becomes God"; Meister Eckhart's *Abgeschiedenheit*); Buddhist *nirodha-samapatti* (the cessation of perception and feeling — the closest parallel to *nirvikalpa samadhi*); Daoist *wu-wei* absorption; Kabbalistic *devekut* (cleaving to God). Six traditions, six names, structurally indistinguishable terminal states.

**The Body as Preparation**: Only yoga (and its tantric variants) systematically includes *physical* preparation (asana, pranayama) as ritual prerequisites for consciousness work. The insight — that the body's state determines the mind's accessibility to transformation — is most fully developed here, but appears in partial form elsewhere: Sufi sama (the body's movement releasing *hal*); Hesychast breath-coordinated prayer in Byzantine Christianity; Buddhist kinhin (walking meditation); Tibetan tsa-lung (channel and wind exercises in [[tradition-vajrayana]]).

## Surprising Find

[[patanjali-yoga-sutras-author]]'s *Yoga Sutras* synthesize [[tradition-samkhya-yoga]] dualism (*prakriti/purusha*) with Buddhist meditation techniques. The text is almost certainly *responding to* Buddhist meditation literature that had already established the vocabulary and staged structure. Classical yoga is a Hindu reframing of Buddhist praxis with Samkhya metaphysics — making it a *counter-tradition* built on the technology of its rival. This is one of the most documented cross-tradition syntheses in ancient South Asian religious history: the Hindu tradition absorbed Buddhist meditation and redeployed it within a non-Buddhist metaphysical framework that the Buddha himself had explicitly rejected (the permanent self / *atman* that yoga calls *purusha*).

The irony: the most widely exported Hindu spiritual technology globally (modern yoga) was originally a Hindu appropriation of Buddhist technique.
