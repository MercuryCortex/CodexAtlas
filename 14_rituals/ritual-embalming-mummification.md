# Egyptian Embalming and Mummification

Egyptian mummification is a 70-day ritual process — not merely a preservation technique — performed by priests of [[anubis]] to prepare the body for its role in the afterlife. The earliest evidence is from c. 3500 BCE (pre-Dynastic) in Upper Egypt; the most elaborate form is attested in the New Kingdom (c. 1550–1070 BCE) and documented by Herodotus (*Histories* II.86–90, c. 440 BCE) and the Rhind Papyrus.

The process: 40 days of natron desiccation (the salt-dry of the desert) + canopic organ removal + linen wrapping + amulet placement + funerary mask installation + ritual prayers for each limb. The body must be preserved because the *ka* (vital force/spiritual double) returns to the body periodically for nourishment. Destroy the body and you destroy the ka's home; the person ceases to exist.

## The 70-Day Process

**Days 1–4 — Death, washing, and the ibu (purification tent)**:
- The body is washed with Nile water (Nilotic ritual purity)
- Transported to the *ibu* (tent of purification) set up near the river
- Initial prayers are performed; the deceased is introduced to the ritual space

**Days 5–10 — Brain removal and organ extraction**:
- The brain is removed through the left nostril using a long hooked instrument — the brain was considered unimportant (the heart was the seat of intellect and emotion in Egyptian physiology)
- The abdomen is opened with an obsidian knife (obsidian because stone, not iron, which was polluting to the dead)
- Liver, lungs, stomach, and intestines are removed and placed in four *canopic jars* protected by the four sons of Horus:
  - **Imsety** (human head) — liver
  - **Hapy** (baboon head) — lungs
  - **Duamutef** (jackal head) — stomach
  - **Qebehsenuef** (falcon head) — intestines
- The heart is left in place — it will be weighed against Maat's feather; it cannot be removed

**Days 10–40 — Natron desiccation**:
- The body is packed inside and out with natron (a naturally occurring salt mixture: sodium carbonate + sodium bicarbonate)
- 40 days of complete desiccation in the dry air; all moisture is removed
- The body shrinks, darkens, and becomes leather-hard

**Days 40–70 — Wrapping and amulet placement**:
- The body is anointed with oils and resins — cedar oil, juniper oil, myrrh, frankincense
- Each limb is wrapped separately in linen strips; specific amulets are placed at specific anatomical locations:
  - Heart scarab (often inscribed with Chapter 30B of the Book of the Dead: "O my heart...do not stand against me as a witness")
  - Djed pillar at the spine — stability
  - Wadjet eye at the chest — protection
  - Golden finger-stalls and toe-stalls for royalty
- The face is covered with a funerary mask (cartonnage for nobles, gold for royalty — Tutankhamun's gold mask, 11 kg)

**Days 70 — Funeral procession and Opening of the Mouth**:
- The wrapped mummy is installed in its coffin(s)
- [[ritual-opening-of-the-mouth]] is performed on the mummy
- The mummy is carried in procession to the tomb
- The tomb is sealed

## The 70-Day Period as Sacred Number

The 70-day duration is not arbitrary. It corresponds to:
- The period of Sirius's (*Sopdet's*) disappearance below the horizon before its heliacal rising — the astronomical event that signals the Nile flood and the new year. The mummification period thus mirrors Sirius's "death" and "resurrection."
- 70 is a sacred number in multiple ancient Mediterranean traditions: 70 elders of Israel (Exodus 24:1), 70 languages of the world (Genesis 10 — the "Table of Nations"), the 70 weeks of Daniel's prophecy, the 70 years of Babylonian exile (Jeremiah 25:11). The resonance across traditions with zero contact points to a shared astronomical or cosmological significance that may predate any of the individual traditions.
- Compare: Jesus fasts 40 days in the desert; Noah's flood lasts 40 days; Moses spends 40 years in the wilderness. The 40-day natron desiccation occupies the same "sacred trial period" slot.

## MASSIVE WIN: Preservation vs. Exposure as Two Complete Solutions to the Same Theological Problem

**The problem**: what must happen to the body after death to ensure the best possible outcome for the soul?

**Solution 1 — Preserve the body** (Egyptian, later Christian):
- The body must remain intact because the soul (ka/resurrection-body) returns to it or needs it for the afterlife
- Egyptian: the ka visits the body periodically; the body is the soul's permanent house
- Christian burial (from 1st century): the body will be physically resurrected; destroying it is theologically problematic; hence Christian resistance to cremation until the Second Vatican Council (1963)
- Both traditions treat the body as irreplaceable — it is not a temporary garment but a permanent address

**Solution 2 — Destroy/expose the body** (Zoroastrian, Buddhist sky burial, Hindu cremation):
- The body is corrupted matter; keeping it intact would pollute the pure elements (earth, fire, water in Zoroastrian theology; it would burden the elements with impurity)
- **Zoroastrian *dakhma*** (Tower of Silence): the body is exposed on a stone tower for birds of prey to consume; bones are bleached by the sun; the three pure elements (earth, fire, water) remain unpolluted. [[ritual-zoroastrian-tower-of-silence]] documents this in full.
- **Buddhist sky burial** (*jhator* in Tibetan): the body is cut and fed to vultures; this is an act of generosity (dana) — the last gift of the dying person's body as food. [[ritual-tibetan-sky-burial]] documents this in full.
- **Hindu cremation**: fire (Agni) purifies and releases; the smoke carries the soul upward; the body's physical matter is returned to the elements from which it came

**The theological matrix**:
| Solution | Tradition | Body logic | Soul logic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Preserve** | Egyptian | body = soul's permanent home | ka returns; resurrection requires intact anchor |
| **Preserve** | Christian | body will be resurrected | resurrection is bodily; the same flesh |
| **Expose/destroy** | Zoroastrian | body = dangerous impure matter | soul flies free; body must not pollute creation |
| **Expose/destroy** | Buddhist sky burial | body = final gift to sentient beings | karma travels; no bodily resurrection; dana releases merit |
| **Burn** | Hindu cremation | body = Agni's fuel | atman is immortal; physical form is illusion |

**The MASSIVE WIN**: Egyptian mummification and Christian burial are the same theological answer (preserve the body for future use by the soul), and Zoroastrian dakhma + Buddhist sky burial are the same opposite answer (destroy/expose the body to prevent it polluting the cosmos or burdening the soul). Two complete and opposite solutions to an identical problem, both with multi-tradition attestation, across a 3,500-year timespan.

## The Anubis Mask

Embalmers — particularly the chief embalmer, the *controller of mysteries* (*imy-khent*) — wore a jackal mask of [[anubis]] during the mummification. This is not theatrical costuming but identity replacement: during the rite, the priest IS Anubis; he performs Anubis's role in the Osirian myth (where Anubis embalmed Osiris after his murder by Set). Every mummy is Osiris; every embalmer is Anubis.

This costuming-as-identity-replacement has a near-universal parallel: shamanic animal-spirit donning, the Mass (the priest acts *in persona Christi*), the Zen transmission of the robe (the dharma-heir becomes the teacher). The ritual performer does not merely represent the divine agent — they become that agent for the duration.
