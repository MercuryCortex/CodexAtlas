# Aztec Autosacrifice (Mexica Bloodletting)

## Overview
Aztec autosacrifice — the ritual letting of one's own blood — is the most personal form of the Mexica sacrificial complex. While large-scale human sacrifice (*nextlahualli*, the debt payment) is the most discussed Aztec ritual technology, autosacrifice was practiced by every Mexica, from the *tlatoani* (ruler) to the commoner, as the daily-ritual baseline. The gods created humans from their own blood; humans owe blood in return; the cosmos depends on this exchange.

The instruments: stingray spines (*huitzitzillin*), obsidian blades (*itzli*), and maguey thorns. The sites: tongue, ear, thigh, calf, and — for penitential sacrifice by priests — the penis. The blood was collected on paper (*amatl*) or grass bundles and burned as offering. The rising smoke was the blood's translation into a form the gods could receive.

## The Theological Foundation

**The Fifth Sun creation myth:** [[quetzalcoatl]] descended to Mictlan (the underworld) and retrieved the bones of the dead from the previous sun. He let his own blood on the bones; the other gods let their blood; and from this divine autosacrifice the humans of the current world were made. Humans are therefore *made of divine blood*. This is not merely symbolism — it is the foundational ontological fact.

**Blood debt (*nextlahualli*):** Because humans are made from the gods' blood, they owe blood. The cosmos — specifically the sun — moves because it is fed by blood. [[tonatiuh]] (the Fifth Sun) requires blood to complete its circuit. Without blood offerings, the sun stops; the world ends. Every Mexica ritual is therefore a contribution to keeping the universe running. Autosacrifice is each individual's contribution to this cosmic maintenance.

**The penitential calendar:** The *tonalpohualli* (260-day ritual calendar) specified when autosacrifice was required. Major calendar ceremonies required it universally. Aztec festivals documented in the [[phase-8-004-florentine-codex-sahagun]] describe priests emerging from extended solitary retreat with their blood dried across their faces and bodies from days of continuous autosacrifice.

**Priestly intensity:** Priests practiced autosacrifice continuously during fasts. The Florentine Codex records priests piercing their bodies at midnight, at the call of a conch trumpet, drawing blood that was then rubbed on the faces of temple idols. The temple itself was anointed with blood as part of its consecration and maintenance.

## Instruments

- **Stingray spine (*huitzitzillin*):** The most prestigious piercing instrument; stingray spines were traded inland from the Gulf Coast specifically for ritual use; their use by elites and priests in autosacrifice is extensively documented in codices. The barbed tip produces a wound that bleeds freely.
- **Obsidian blade (*itzli*):** Mass-produced; the utilitarian autosacrifice tool for commoners; obsidian blades are among the most archaeologically abundant objects at Aztec sites; many show the characteristic blood-staining of sacrificial use.
- **Maguey thorn:** The everyday instrument; the maguey plant (*metl*) is associated with [[mayahuel]], goddess of the maguey, and its thorns carry ritual charge from this association.

## Archaeological Record

Caches at the Templo Mayor (excavated by Eduardo Matos Moctezuma from 1978) include stingray spines with human blood residue, obsidian blades in ritual arrangements, and sculptural representations of autosacrifice. The Tlaltecuhtli monolith (2006) and numerous other finds document the sacrificial complex archaeologically. The Florentine Codex (Sahagún, 1577) and the Codex Borgia provide the most detailed textual descriptions.

## MASSIVE WIN — Body-as-Sacrifice Across Five Traditions

Autosacrifice is the Aztec node in the cross-tradition body-sacrifice cluster. See → [[ritual-sun-dance]] for the full comparative table. The Aztec version is distinctive in its:

1. **Cosmogonic necessity:** Blood debt is not devotional — it is existential. Without it, the sun literally stops.
2. **Universality:** Every Mexica, every day, across every class and gender, participated. This is not specialist priestly knowledge — it is the daily practice of a civilization.
3. **Self-directed:** Unlike the large-scale human sacrifice of captives, autosacrifice is the self given as the offering. The priest's own blood, from his own body, is the most direct form of gift.

**The fundamental theological question:** What does the divine require from humans? Across traditions, blood is the oldest answer. The Aztec system makes this explicit as a cosmic-mechanical necessity. The Christian wound-mysticism tradition (stigmatics; Catherine of Siena drinking pus from wounds as her blood-offering; the Flagellants' public bloodletting) arrives at the same logic through the imitatio Christi framework. The Lakota Sun Dance (→ [[ritual-sun-dance]]) arrives at it through the logic of the self-gift. Hindu Thaipusam kavadi piercing arrives at it through vow fulfillment to [[murugan]]. Four traditions, same answer: the body's blood is the currency of the sacred.

## The Conquest Rupture

The Spanish prohibition of autosacrifice and human sacrifice (1521 onward) was the destruction of the cosmological operating system, not just the elimination of "cruel practices." Without blood debt being paid, the Fifth Sun would end — this was what Aztec theology predicted. The persistence of syncretic forms (Christ's blood in the Eucharist substituting for the blood offering; the integration of Quetzalcoatl with Christ) represents the Mexica attempt to find a blood-debt theology within Christianity.
