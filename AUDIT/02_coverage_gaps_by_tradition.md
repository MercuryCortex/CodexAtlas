# Coverage Gaps by Tradition

Found 2026-05-14 by external audit. Organised by tradition so an agent can take one section at a time. Each section lists what's missing, with one-line scholarly source recommendations so the implementing agent can satisfy the source-integrity policy on first write.

Priority ordering within each section: most-load-bearing first.

## 1. 🇨🇳 Chinese textual canon — LARGEST GAP

The Phase 2 entries are Daodejing and Analects only. For a global atlas this is severely undersized.

### Pre-imperial / classical (Phase 1–2)

- **Yi Jing (I Ching) / Book of Changes** — Zhou-layer (~9th c. BCE), Ten Wings (Warring States – Han). Foundational divination text and metaphysical source for all later Chinese thought.
  - Source: Richard J. Smith, *Fathoming the Cosmos and Ordering the World* (U Virginia Press, 2008).
- **Zhuangzi** — Inner Chapters by Zhuang Zhou (~4th c. BCE); Outer/Mixed Han redactional layers. Co-foundational Daoist text. Daoism without Zhuangzi is decapitated.
  - Source: A.C. Graham, *Chuang-Tzu: The Inner Chapters* (HackettAllen, 1981/2001); Brook Ziporyn, *Zhuangzi: The Essential Writings* (Hackett, 2009).
- **Mengzi (Mencius)** — Confucian elaboration; human-nature-is-good thesis.
  - Source: Bryan W. Van Norden, *Mengzi* (Hackett, 2008).
- **Xunzi** — Confucian elaboration; human-nature-is-bad / ritual-as-corrective. Long underrepresented in West; recovered in last 30 years.
  - Source: Eric L. Hutton, *Xunzi: The Complete Text* (Princeton UP, 2014).
- **Mozi** / Mohism — Warring States universal-love + consequentialist critique of Confucianism. Lost in imperial period but recovered in modern scholarship.
  - Source: Ian Johnston, *The Mozi: A Complete Translation* (Columbia UP, 2010).
- **Han Feizi** — Legalism. The actual political theology of the Qin and Han imperial systems.
  - Source: Burton Watson, *Han Fei Tzu: Basic Writings* (Columbia UP, 1964).
- **Doctrine of the Mean (Zhongyong)** + **Great Learning (Daxue)** — the rest of the Four Books canonized by Zhu Xi.
  - Source: Daniel K. Gardner, *The Four Books* (Hackett, 2007).
- **Shujing (Book of Documents)**, **Shijing (Book of Songs)**, **Liji (Book of Rites)**, **Chunqiu (Spring and Autumn Annals)** — the Five Classics (with Yijing). Backbone of 2000 years of East Asian civilization.
  - Sources: James Legge translations (Tier 1, public domain via sacred-texts.com); Michael Nylan, *The Five "Confucian" Classics* (Yale UP, 2001) for modern scholarship.

### Religious Daoism (Phase 3–5)

- **Huainanzi** — Han syncretic cosmology (Liu An, ~139 BCE).
  - Source: John S. Major et al., *The Huainanzi* (Columbia UP, 2010).
- **Liezi** — Daoist-philosophical (~4th c. CE redaction of older material).
  - Source: A.C. Graham, *The Book of Lieh-tzu* (Columbia UP, 1990).
- **Baopuzi** — Ge Hong (~320 CE) — alchemical-Daoist.
  - Source: James R. Ware, *Alchemy, Medicine, and Religion in the China of A.D. 320* (MIT Press, 1966).
- **Cantong qi (Zhouyi cantong qi)** — root text of internal alchemy / *neidan*. The Chinese parallel to Western alchemical literature; major cross-tradition opportunity.
  - Source: Fabrizio Pregadio, *The Seal of the Unity of the Three* (Golden Elixir Press, 2011).
- **Daozang** (Daoist canon, finalized 15th c.) — as a tradition-level node + reference; full enumeration not feasible but the canon's structure deserves description.
  - Source: Kristofer Schipper & Franciscus Verellen (eds.), *The Taoist Canon* (U Chicago Press, 2004).

### East Asian Mahāyāna (Phase 4–5)

- **Lotus Sūtra (Saddharmapuṇḍarīka)** — most-influential Mahayana text in East Asia (Tendai, Nichiren, Tiantai). **Egregious absence.**
  - Source: Burton Watson, *The Lotus Sutra* (Columbia UP, 1993); Jacqueline Stone, *Original Enlightenment and the Transformation of Medieval Japanese Buddhism* (U Hawaii Press, 1999).
- **Avataṃsaka Sūtra (Huayan Jing)** — keystone of Huayan / Kegon. Cosmological vision of mutual interpenetration (the Indra's-net metaphor).
  - Source: Thomas Cleary, *The Flower Ornament Scripture* (Shambhala, 1993).
- **Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra** — keystone of Chan/Zen; cited by Bodhidharma.
  - Source: D.T. Suzuki, *The Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra* (Routledge, 1932) — Tier 1 primary translation; Florin Sutton, *Existence and Enlightenment in the Laṅkāvatāra-Sūtra* (SUNY, 1991).
- **Pure Land sutras** — Larger and Smaller *Sukhāvatīvyūha* + *Amitāyurdhyāna Sūtra*. Amitabha is in your deities; the canonical sutras must be too.
  - Source: Luis O. Gómez, *The Land of Bliss* (U Hawaii Press, 1996).
- **Yogācāra texts** — Asaṅga's *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*; Vasubandhu's *Triṃśikā* and *Viṃśatikā*. Asaṅga and Vasubandhu are in persons; their texts must be docs.
  - Source: Stefan Anacker, *Seven Works of Vasubandhu* (Motilal Banarsidass, 1984); John P. Keenan, *The Summary of the Great Vehicle* (BDK, 1992).

### Modern / political religious China

- **Taiping rebellion (1850s)** as an event — Christian-millenarian rebellion; ~20–30 million dead; largest religious war in human history.
  - Source: Jonathan Spence, *God's Chinese Son* (Norton, 1996).
- **Falun Gong / qigong movements** as a tradition node (Phase 7).
  - Source: David Ownby, *Falun Gong and the Future of China* (Oxford UP, 2008).

## 2. 🇮🇳 Indic — depth gaps in puranic, tantric, and modern layers

### Phase 2–3

- **Bhagavad Gītā as own node.** Currently folded into Mahabharata oral-layers. The most-cited Hindu text in the West deserves its own Phase 2 or Phase 3 node.
  - Source: R.C. Zaehner, *The Bhagavad-Gītā* (Oxford UP, 1969); Patton, *The Bhagavad Gita* (Penguin Classics, 2008).

### Phase 4–5 (Puranas)

The 18 Mahāpurāṇas span 3rd–10th c. CE and define classical Hinduism. None are currently full nodes except partial via traditions. At minimum:

- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** — ~9th–10th c. CE. Single most-influential Vaishnava text; theological backbone of bhakti.
  - Source: Edwin Bryant (ed./trans.), *Krishna: The Beautiful Legend of God: Srimad Bhagavata Purana Book X* (Penguin Classics, 2003).
- **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** — ~4th–5th c.
  - Source: H.H. Wilson, *The Vishnu Purana* (London 1840, public domain); Wendy Doniger, *Hindu Myths* (Penguin, 1975) for context.
- **Śiva Purāṇa**, **Skanda Purāṇa**, **Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa** (contains the Devī Māhātmya, which *is* already in Phase 5), **Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa**.
  - Source: Cornelia Dimmitt & J.A.B. van Buitenen, *Classical Hindu Mythology* (Temple UP, 1978).

### Phase 5 (Tantric)

- **Vajrayāna root tantras** — Guhyasamāja, Hevajra, Cakrasaṃvara, Kālacakra. (Kālacakra is a deity-node already.) These are the actual textual foundation of Tibetan Vajrayana.
  - Source: David Snellgrove, *The Hevajra Tantra* (Oxford UP, 1959); Christopher Wallis, *Tantra Illuminated* (Mattamayura, 2013) for survey.
- **Tibetan canon (Kanjur/Tanjur)** as a tradition-level reference node.
  - Source: Paul Harrison, "A Brief History of the Tibetan bKa' 'gyur" in Cabezón & Jackson, *Tibetan Literature* (Snow Lion, 1996).

### Jain canon

- **Jain Āgamas** — Acharanga Sutra, Sutrakritanga, Sthananga, Tattvārtha Sūtra of Umāsvāti. Jainism tradition exists, Mahavira is in persons, but the canon is unrepresented.
  - Source: Paul Dundas, *The Jains* (Routledge, 2nd ed. 2002); Nathmal Tatia, *That Which Is: Tattvārtha Sūtra* (HarperOne, 1994).

### Theravāda commentarial layer

- **Buddhaghosa's Visuddhimagga** — Theravadin systematics (~5th c. CE). Buddhaghosa missing from persons entirely.
  - Source: Bhikkhu Ñāṇamoli (trans.), *The Path of Purification* (BPS, 1956; reprint Pariyatti 2003).
- **Milindapañha** — Theravadin dialogue, ~1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE.
  - Source: Bhikkhu Pesala, *The Debate of King Milinda* (BPS, 1991).

### Tamil canon (non-Sanskrit South Indian)

- **Tirukkuṟaḷ** — early Tamil ethical compendium, c. 5th c. CE.
  - Source: V.V.S. Aiyar, *The Kural* (Tiruchirapalli, 1916; archive.org); G.U. Pope, *The Sacred Kurral of Tiruvalluvar* (1886, public domain).
- **Tēvāram** — Tamil Shaiva devotional poetry.
  - Source: Indira Peterson, *Poems to Śiva* (Princeton UP, 1989).
- **Tiruvāymoḻi** of Nammāḻvār — Tamil Vaishnava (Nammāḻvār already in persons).
  - Source: A.K. Ramanujan, *Hymns for the Drowning* (Princeton UP, 1981).

### Modern Indian religion

- **B.R. Ambedkar — *The Buddha and his Dhamma*** (1957). Founder of Navayāna / Neo-Buddhism; engineered mass Dalit conversion. Critical modern South Asian religious development with major caste-and-religion politics.
  - Source: Christopher Queen, "Dr. Ambedkar and the Hermeneutics of Buddhist Liberation" in Queen & King (eds.), *Engaged Buddhism* (SUNY, 1996).
- **Iqbāl — *Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam*** (1930) — pivotal South Asian Islamic modernist text. Should be Phase 7.
  - Source: Mustansir Mir, *Iqbal* (I.B. Tauris, 2006).

## 3. 🕌 Islamic canon and modern Islam

### Modern Islam — political

## 5. ✝️ Early Christianity — Apostolic Fathers and apocryphal Gospels/Acts

### Apostolic Fathers — TOTAL ABSENCE

This is structurally severe: the bridge between Paul (~60 CE) and Justin Martyr (~150 CE) is the Apostolic Fathers. Without them Phase 3 → Phase 4 has a 90-year textual void.

- **Didache** — late 1st c., earliest extant Christian community handbook.
- **1 Clement** — ~96 CE, Roman church to Corinth, earliest extant post-NT Christian writing.
- **Ignatius of Antioch — seven letters** — ~107–110 CE, en route to Roman martyrdom; first explicit Christology of the "one bishop" model.
- **Shepherd of Hermas** — ~140 CE, apocalyptic-prophetic; nearly canonical in some 2nd-c. communities.
- **Epistle of Barnabas** — ~130 CE, anti-Jewish typological exegesis.
- **Polycarp — Letter to the Philippians** + **Martyrdom of Polycarp**.
- **2 Clement** — ~150 CE, earliest extant Christian sermon.
- **Papias** (fragments via Eusebius). Papias already in persons.

**Source for all**: Bart D. Ehrman, *The Apostolic Fathers* (Loeb Classical Library, 2 vols., 2003) — Tier 1 facing-page Greek/English critical edition; Michael Holmes, *The Apostolic Fathers in English* (Baker, 2007).

### Apocryphal Gospels (additional)

- **Protoevangelium of James** (~150) — *enormously* influential for Marian theology (Joachim & Anna, Mary's virginity *in partu*, etc.). Larger consequence than several Nag Hammadi texts already in the vault.
  - Source: Bart D. Ehrman & Zlatko Pleše, *The Apocryphal Gospels* (Oxford UP, 2011).
- **Gospel of Peter** — fragmentary Akhmim Codex; docetic Passion.
- **Gospel of the Hebrews** / **Gospel of the Egyptians** / **Gospel of the Nazaraeans** — Jewish-Christian gospel traditions.
- **Gospel of Nicodemus / Acts of Pilate** — Harrowing of Hell mythology.
- **Gospel of Judas** (Tchacos Codex, 2006 publication). Dead-link in your vault.

### Apocryphal Acts — ESSENTIALLY ABSENT

- **Acts of Thomas** — Syrian Christian; Hymn of the Pearl (massive Gnostic-poetic text).
- **Acts of Paul** (incl. Acts of Paul and Thecla, which *is* in your Phase 4).
- **Acts of Peter**.
- **Acts of John** — docetic Christology; Round Dance of the Cross.
- **Acts of Andrew**.

**Source**: J.K. Elliott, *The Apocryphal New Testament* (Oxford UP, 1993) — single-volume scholarly translation, gold standard.

## 6. 🏛️ Hittite / Hurrian — TOTAL GAP

This is severe for the comparative-mythology mission. The Kumarbi Cycle is the *direct source* for Hesiod's Theogony's succession-myth (Ouranos → Kronos → Zeus mirrors Anu → Kumarbi → Teshub). The Illuyanka myth feeds chaoskampf.

### Deities to add (Phase 1)

- **Teshub** — Hittite-Hurrian storm-god; Anatolian counterpart of Baal/Marduk.
- **Hebat** — consort of Teshub.
- **Sun Goddess of Arinna** — chief Hittite goddess.
- **Telipinu** — vanishing-god / fertility (the disappearing-god motif Frazer foregrounded).
- **Kumarbi** — succession-myth father-god.
- **Illuyanka** — chaos-serpent.

### Documents (Phase 1)

- **Kumarbi Cycle** (Song of Kumarbi / Song of Ullikummi / Song of Hedammu).
  - Source: Harry A. Hoffner Jr., *Hittite Myths* (SBL, 2nd ed. 1998); Mary R. Bachvarova, *From Hittite to Homer* (Cambridge UP, 2016) — the latter explicitly traces the transmission.
- **Telipinu Myth** — vanishing-and-returning fertility god.
- **Illuyanka Myth** — chaoskampf.
- **Hittite Prayers** (Kantuzzili, Muršili II's plague prayers).
- **Hittite ritual texts** (Anatolian-Hurrian rituals — Tunnawiya, etc.).

### Events

- **Boğazköy / Ḫattuša archive discovery** (Hugo Winckler, 1906) — made the Kumarbi-Hesiod link demonstrable.
  - Source: Trevor Bryce, *The Kingdom of the Hittites* (Oxford UP, new ed. 2005).

This addition alone produces dozens of new high-quality cross-tradition edges (Hittite ↔ Greek, Hittite ↔ Hurrian, Hurrian ↔ Mesopotamian, Hittite ↔ Hebrew Bible chaoskampf).
