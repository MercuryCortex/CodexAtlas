## 1. Ancient — pre-200 CE

### 1.1 Plato (c. 428–348 BCE) — Athenian philosophy

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Republic* (Books II + X on religion + the Forms); *Timaeus* (cosmology as religious-mythic philosophy); *Symposium* (Diotima's ascent — mystical philosophy); *Euthyphro* (the divine-command dilemma — foundational text for philosophy of religion).
- **Significance:** Plato's *Euthyphro* — "Is the holy holy because the gods love it, or do the gods love it because it is holy?" — is the founding text of philosophy of religion in the West. *Timaeus* gives the Demiurge as philosophical-cosmological deity, distinct from Olympian polytheism.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `03_deities/zeus`, `03_deities/the-one`/`platonic-good`, `06_themes/divine-command-theory`, `06_themes/forms-and-participation`, `21_theology/doctrine-of-eternal-forms`-class material.
- **SEP entry:** "Plato" + "Plato's Ethics" + "Ancient Theories of Religion" (related entries).
- *Notes:* Plato straddles religious-mystery (mystery-cult dimensions of *Symposium*) and rationalist philosophy. The 29_philosophy node carries the philosophical-method side; the 04_persons biographical node carries the life. **Paired-batch counter per PHIL-1:** Critias-the-sophist on religion-as-political-invention (early-naturalist counter-tradition).

### 1.2 Aristotle (384–322 BCE) — Lyceum / Peripatetic

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Metaphysics* Λ (Lambda — Book XII, the Unmoved Mover); *Nicomachean Ethics* X.7–8 (contemplation as the highest activity); *De Anima*.
- **Significance:** The Unmoved Mover as a philosophical-rational deity reached by cosmological argument — direct ancestor of Aquinas's Five Ways. Establishes the "Pure Act" model that runs through medieval theology into Plantinga + contemporary classical-theism debates.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/unmoved-mover`, `06_themes/contemplation-as-highest-activity`, `21_theology/doctrine-of-divine-simplicity` (Aristotelian roots), cross-link to Aquinas philosophical node.
- **SEP entry:** "Aristotle" + "Aristotle's Metaphysics" + "Aristotle's Theology".

### 1.3 Plotinus (c. 204–270 CE) — Neoplatonism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Enneads* (compiled by Porphyry) — especially V.1 ("On the Three Primary Hypostases"), VI.9 ("On the Good or the One"), I.6 ("On Beauty").
- **Significance:** Founder of philosophical Neoplatonism; the One / Nous / Soul tripartite hypostasis; mystical-union (henosis) with the One as the telos of philosophical practice. Direct ancestor of Christian mystical theology (Pseudo-Dionysius), Sufi metaphysics, and the Western mystical-philosophy tradition through Eckhart.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `03_deities/the-one`, `06_themes/emanation`, `06_themes/mystical-union`, `21_theology/doctrine-of-divine-simplicity`, the `neoplatonism-philosophical` school node.
- **SEP entry:** "Plotinus".

## 2. Late antique / medieval — 200–1500

### 2.1 Augustine of Hippo (354–430) — Latin Christianity / Neoplatonic-Christian synthesis

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works (philosophical side):** *Confessions* Book XI (philosophy of time); *De Trinitate* X–XV (the proto-cogito + philosophy of mind); *De Libero Arbitrio* (free will + problem of evil); *City of God* (philosophy of history).
- **Significance:** The first major thinker whose work decisively straddles theology and philosophy of religion. Privation theory of evil — evil as the absence of good rather than a positive being — is a philosophical thesis still defended in contemporary analytic theodicy (Stump, Adams). Augustine's *Confessions* Book XI on the philosophy of time prefigures Husserl-on-internal-time-consciousness.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `03_deities/yahweh-elohim`, `06_themes/time-and-eternity`, `06_themes/free-will`, `21_theology/augustine-theology` (paired sibling node per spec §3.2), `problems/problem-of-evil-evidential`.
- **SEP entry:** "Saint Augustine".
- *Notes:* Receives the suffix-disambiguation treatment per spec §3.2 — `augustine-philosophical` in 29_philosophy/thinkers/ + `augustine-theology` in 21_theology/, cross-linked via `cross-lens-pair:`.

### 2.2 Boethius (c. 480–524) — Latin Christian Neoplatonism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Consolation of Philosophy* (philosophical theodicy in dialogue form); *Theological Tractates*.
- **Significance:** Bridges classical philosophy and medieval Christian thought; the *Consolation* gives the foundational medieval formulation of God-eternity-vs-foreknowledge problem (Book V) — the problem reanimated by Plantinga and Stump in contemporary analytic philosophy of religion.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/divine-foreknowledge`, `06_themes/free-will`, `problems/problem-of-evil-evidential`.
- **SEP entry:** "Anicius Manlius Severinus Boethius".

### 2.3 Anselm of Canterbury (1033–1109) — Latin medieval

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Proslogion* (the ontological argument — "that than which nothing greater can be conceived"); *Cur Deus Homo* (sits in 21 — atonement theology).
- **Significance:** Founder of the ontological argument tradition; the *Proslogion* is the philosophical-argument-bearing text whose downstream reception spans Descartes → Leibniz → Kant (the critique) → Plantinga (modal version) → Gödel (formalization). Anselm's "*fides quaerens intellectum*" coins the methodological self-description that PHILOSOPHY of religion sometimes inhabits and theology always inhabits.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `03_deities/yahweh-elohim`, `problems/existence-of-god-ontological-argument`, `21_theology/anselm-theology` (paired sibling for the atonement-theology side).
- **SEP entry:** "Saint Anselm".

### 2.4 Maimonides (Moses ben Maimon, 1138–1204) — medieval Jewish philosophy

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works (philosophical):** *Guide for the Perplexed* (negative theology + religious-language + problem of evil); *Eight Chapters*.
- **Significance:** The classical Jewish philosophical-theology synthesis; *Guide* I.50–60 on negative theology / "via negativa" is foundational for religious-language philosophy. Influences Aquinas's analogy doctrine directly.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/apophatic-theology`, `problems/religious-language-problem`, `21_theology/maimonides-theology` (paired sibling for *Mishneh Torah* halakhic-theology side).
- **SEP entry:** "Maimonides" + "Maimonides: Philosophy of Religion".

### 2.5 Ibn Sina (Avicenna, c. 980–1037) — Islamic philosophy

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Kitāb al-Shifāʾ* (The Healing) — esp. the Metaphysics; *Kitāb al-Najāt*; the "Floating Man" thought experiment.
- **Significance:** The Necessary-Being argument (*wājib al-wujūd* — the being whose existence is necessary in itself), foundational for the Islamic-philosophical-theology synthesis and decisive influence on Aquinas's contingency argument (Five Ways, third way). The Floating Man is philosophy-of-mind / philosophy-of-soul whose religious dimensions concern the survival of the soul.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/existence-of-god-cosmological-argument` (the contingency/Necessary-Being version), `06_themes/soul`, `21_theology/ibn-sina-theology-light` (less material than philosophical).
- **SEP entry:** "Ibn Sina's Metaphysics" + "Arabic and Islamic Philosophy of Religion" + "The Influence of Arabic and Islamic Philosophy on the Latin West".

### 2.6 Al-Ghazali (Abu Hamid Muhammad al-Ghazali, 1058–1111) — Islamic philosophy + Sufism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works (philosophical):** *Tahāfut al-Falāsifa* (The Incoherence of the Philosophers) — the classical Sunni-orthodox critique of Avicennan philosophy; *al-Munqidh min al-Ḍalāl* (Deliverer from Error) — philosophical autobiography of his epistemic crisis.
- **Significance:** The *Incoherence* is a polemical-philosophical text that closes much of the Islamic-philosophical Aristotelian project — Ibn Rushd responds with *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* (Incoherence of the Incoherence). Al-Ghazali's epistemic-crisis narrative in the *Munqidh* prefigures Descartes's *Meditations* method-of-doubt.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/religious-epistemology-evidentialism` (paired-counter), `problems/religious-experience-as-evidence` (Sufi-experience as epistemic ground), `21_theology/al-ghazali-theology` (paired sibling for the *Ihya'* devotional-theology side).
- **SEP entry:** "al-Ghazali".

### 2.7 Ibn Rushd (Averroes, 1126–1198) — Andalusi Islamic philosophy

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* (Incoherence of the Incoherence) — response to al-Ghazali; *Faṣl al-Maqāl* (Decisive Treatise) — harmonization of philosophy and religion; the Aristotle commentaries.
- **Significance:** The harmonization-program: philosophy and revealed religion as two routes to the same truth, with philosophical proof-reading method for tensions. Decisive influence on Aquinas (the "Commentator" of medieval Latin tradition).
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/double-truth` (the medieval-Latin-reception version), `problems/religious-language-problem`, `21_theology/ibn-rushd-theology` (the *Decisive Treatise* harmonization side).
- **SEP entry:** "Ibn Rushd's Natural Philosophy" + "Ibn Rushd's Metaphysics".

### 2.8 Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274) — Latin medieval / Christian Aristotelianism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works (philosophical):** *Summa Theologiae* I q.2 a.3 — the Five Ways (treated as natural-theology accessible without revelation); *Summa contra Gentiles* (apologetic-philosophical work for non-Christian addressees); the *De Ente et Essentia*.
- **Significance:** The most influential medieval Christian philosopher of religion. The Five Ways remain the canonical formulation of cosmological + teleological arguments — engaged today by Craig, Pruss, Feser on the defending side and Hume, Mackie, Oppy on the critical side. Aquinas's analogy doctrine (univocal / equivocal / analogical predication of "God is good") is the canonical medieval treatment of religious language.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `03_deities/yahweh-elohim`, `problems/existence-of-god-cosmological-argument`, `problems/existence-of-god-teleological-argument`, `problems/religious-language-problem`, `21_theology/aquinas-theology` (paired sibling for the Trinity-Incarnation-sacraments-faith-internal side).
- **SEP entry:** "Saint Thomas Aquinas".

### 2.9 Meister Eckhart (c. 1260–1328) — Rhineland mystical philosophy

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works (philosophical side):** the Latin works *Opus Tripartitum*; the German sermons (especially "Sermon 52" on poverty of spirit).
- **Significance:** The most philosophically-precise medieval Christian mystic; Eckhart's "ground of the soul" / "godhead beyond God" / *abegescheidenheit* (detachment) anticipate apophatic + comparative-mystical philosophy. Engaged today in comparative-mysticism literature (Loy, Smith, Forman) as a Western parallel to non-dual Buddhist and Advaita formulations.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/apophatic-theology`, `06_themes/mystical-union`, `problems/mystical-experience-perennialism` (Eckhart frequently invoked by Stace + Forman as a perennialist test-case), `21_theology/eckhart-theology` (paired sibling).
- **SEP entry:** "Meister Eckhart".

## 3. Eastern classical — Indian, Buddhist, Chinese

### 3.1 Nāgārjuna (c. 150–250 CE) — Madhyamaka Buddhism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* (Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way); *Vigrahavyāvartanī* (Dispeller of Disputes).
- **Significance:** Founder of Madhyamaka philosophy; the *catuṣkoṭi* (four-cornered logic — affirmation, negation, both, neither) and the doctrine of *śūnyatā* (emptiness) are foundational for Buddhist philosophy of religion + comparative philosophy (Garfield, Priest, Westerhoff). Direct philosophical-method engagement with religious-doctrinal claims via dialectical refutation.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/emptiness-shunyata`, `06_themes/dependent-origination`, `problems/comparative-emptiness-and-being`, `madhyamaka-philosophy` school node.
- **SEP entry:** "Nāgārjuna".

### 3.2 Vasubandhu (4th–5th c. CE) — Yogācāra Buddhism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Abhidharmakośa* (Sarvāstivāda-Sautrāntika treatise); *Triṃśikā-vijñaptimātratā* (Thirty Verses on Consciousness-Only); *Viṃśatikā* (Twenty Verses).
- **Significance:** Foundational Yogācāra ("consciousness-only") philosophy. Engaged today in philosophy of mind comparison with phenomenology (Tzohar 2018) + idealism debates.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/consciousness-only`, `problems/comparative-self-and-no-self`, the `yogacara-philosophy` school node (sibling of madhyamaka).
- **SEP entry:** "Vasubandhu".

### 3.3 Dharmakīrti (c. 600–660 CE) — Buddhist epistemology

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Pramāṇavārttika* (Commentary on the Means of Knowledge); *Nyāyabindu*.
- **Significance:** Buddhist epistemology — *pramāṇa* (means of valid cognition) theory; foundational for Indian + Tibetan Buddhist philosophy of religion. Engaged comparatively today by Matilal, Mohanty, Tillemans.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/religious-epistemology-reformed` (paired-counter — Dharmakīrti's pramāṇa-theory is the Buddhist analogue to Reformed-Epistemology debates on basic-belief structure), `06_themes/perception-and-inference`.
- **SEP entry:** "Dharmakīrti".

### 3.4 Śaṅkara (c. 788–820 CE) — Advaita Vedānta

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works (philosophical side):** *Brahmasūtra-Bhāṣya* (commentary on the Brahma Sutras); *Upadeśasāhasrī*; (the *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* — attribution disputed).
- **Significance:** Foundational Advaita Vedānta philosophy. The doctrine that Brahman is non-dual (*advaita*) — that the world's multiplicity is *māyā* and Atman-Brahman identity is the ultimate philosophical truth — engaged comparatively today as a counterpart to Eckhart and to Buddhist non-dualism (Loy 1988).
- **Cross-lens connections:** `03_deities/brahman`, `06_themes/maya`, `06_themes/non-duality`, `problems/comparative-emptiness-and-being`, `advaita-vedanta-philosophy` school, `21_theology/sankara-theology` (paired sibling).
- **SEP entry:** "Sankara" + "Advaita Vedanta" (broader entry).

### 3.5 Rāmānuja (c. 1017–1137) — Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Śrī Bhāṣya* (commentary on the Brahma Sutras); *Vedārthasaṃgraha*.
- **Significance:** Founder of qualified non-dualism (*viśiṣṭādvaita*) — Brahman as personal-with-attributes; theistic Vedānta in opposition to Śaṅkara's impersonal-absolute. Engaged philosophically as the canonical alternative-position within Vedānta debate.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `03_deities/vishnu`, `06_themes/personal-vs-impersonal-absolute`, `21_theology/ramanuja-theology` (primarily theological — paired sibling thin).
- **SEP entry:** "Rāmānuja" (limited; broader coverage in "Indian Philosophy" entry).
- *Notes:* PHIL-1 paired counter to Śaṅkara — Vedānta's internal philosophical debate.

### 3.6 Mulla Sadra (c. 1571/2–1640) — Iranian Islamic philosophy

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *al-Asfār al-Arbaʿa* (The Four Journeys); *al-Mashāʿir*.
- **Significance:** Founder of the Transcendent Theosophy (*Ḥikma muta'āliya*) — the most ambitious Islamic-philosophical synthesis of Avicennan philosophy, Ishrāqī illuminationism, and Sufi metaphysics. Foundational for modern Iranian philosophy of religion (Soroush, Nasr).
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/being-and-existence`, `06_themes/existence-precedes-essence-shia`, `problems/comparative-emptiness-and-being` (paired with Heidegger via comparative scholars), Mulla-Sadra-as-Iranian-philosopher cross-link to Iranian-reformist-Shia (politics-lens).
- **SEP entry:** "Mulla Sadra".

### 3.7 Tsongkhapa (1357–1419) — Tibetan Gelug Madhyamaka

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Lam rim chen mo* (Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path); *drang nges legs bshad snying po* (Essence of True Eloquence — Madhyamaka treatise).
- **Significance:** Founder of the Gelug school and the most influential Tibetan philosopher of religion; the Madhyamaka prāsaṅgika synthesis. Engaged today in comparative work by Garfield, Thurman, Newland, Cabezón.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/emptiness-shunyata`, `madhyamaka-philosophy` school, comparative-Madhyamaka literature.
- **SEP entry:** Limited (broader coverage in "Tibetan Buddhist Philosophy" entries).

## 4. Early modern — 1500–1800

### 4.1 Baruch Spinoza (1632–1677) — Dutch rationalism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Tractatus Theologico-Politicus* (1670); *Ethics* (1677, posthumous).
- **Significance:** *Deus sive Natura* — God-or-Nature — the philosophical-monist identification that became foundational for naturalist + pantheist philosophy of religion. *TTP* pioneered historical-critical scripture analysis. Spinoza's biblical-criticism is a precursor to the secularization-thesis tradition.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/secularization-thesis` (precursor), `06_themes/pantheism`, `problems/religious-language-problem` (TTP on scriptural metaphor).
- **SEP entry:** "Baruch Spinoza" + "Spinoza's Philosophical Theology".

### 4.2 Blaise Pascal (1623–1662) — Jansenist apologetics + philosophical

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Pensées* (posthumous fragments); the wager argument (Pensée 233/418 in Lafuma).
- **Significance:** The Wager is one of the earliest decision-theoretic arguments in philosophy of religion and is engaged today by analytic philosophers (Hájek, Saka). Pascal's "reasons of the heart that reason knows not" prefigures religious-epistemology debates on non-evidential warrant.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/religious-epistemology-evidentialism` (paired-counter), `21_theology/pascal-theology` (paired sibling for the Jansenist-apologetic dimension).
- **SEP entry:** "Pascal's Wager" + "Blaise Pascal".

### 4.3 Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716) — German rationalism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Theodicy* (1710); *Monadology* (1714); *Discourse on Metaphysics* (1686).
- **Significance:** Coined "theodicy"; the "best of all possible worlds" defense of theism against the problem of evil. The cosmological argument's "principle of sufficient reason" formulation is Leibniz's; engaged today by Pruss + Koons in the contingency-argument literature.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/problem-of-evil-logical` (Leibniz's classical-theodicy is the foil for Hume's *Dialogues* and Voltaire's *Candide*), `problems/existence-of-god-cosmological-argument`.
- **SEP entry:** "Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz" + "Leibniz on the Problem of Evil".

### 4.4 David Hume (1711–1776) — Scottish empiricism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion* (posthumous, 1779); *The Natural History of Religion* (1757); "Of Miracles" (Section X of *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*, 1748).
- **Significance:** The *Dialogues* are the most important early-modern critical engagement with natural theology. Hume's critiques of the cosmological, ontological, and teleological arguments and his argument against miracles set the agenda for analytic philosophy of religion's critical side ever since.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/existence-of-god-cosmological-argument` (critique), `problems/existence-of-god-teleological-argument` (critique), `problems/religious-language-problem`, `works/hume-dialogues` (primary work-node).
- **SEP entry:** "David Hume" + "Hume on Religion".
- *Notes:* The canonical PHIL-1 paired-counter to Aquinas + Leibniz + Paley natural-theology lineage.

### 4.5 Immanuel Kant (1724–1804) — German critical philosophy

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781/1787) — the Transcendental Dialectic on God-proofs; *Critique of Practical Reason* (1788) — moral argument for God; *Religion within the Bounds of Bare Reason* (1793).
- **Significance:** The *Critique of Pure Reason* contains the most influential critique of the ontological argument (the famous "existence is not a predicate"); the *Practical Reason* offers the moral argument as a postulate of pure practical reason. *Religion within the Bounds* is a foundational work for rational-religion / liberal-Protestant theology.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/existence-of-god-ontological-argument` (critique), `problems/moral-argument-for-god`, `21_theology/kant-as-protestant-rationalist` (light theological reception side — Kant is more straightforwardly philosophical).
- **SEP entry:** "Immanuel Kant" + "Kant's Philosophy of Religion".

## 5. Nineteenth century

### 5.1 Friedrich Schleiermacher (1768–1834) — German Romantic / liberal Protestant

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works (philosophical side):** *On Religion: Speeches to Its Cultured Despisers* (1799).
- **Significance:** Founder of modern liberal theology AND of the religion-as-feeling philosophical tradition. "Religion is the feeling and intuition of the infinite" — anticipates Otto's *Das Heilige* by 120 years. The *Speeches* is the founding philosophical-argument-bearing text of the philosophy of religion as a feeling/experience-based discipline.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/religious-experience-as-evidence` (early modern roots), `phenomenology-of-religion-school` (ancestor figure), `21_theology/schleiermacher-theology` (paired sibling for *The Christian Faith*).
- **SEP entry:** "Friedrich Daniel Ernst Schleiermacher".

### 5.2 G.W.F. Hegel (1770–1831) — German Idealism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Lectures on the Philosophy of Religion* (1827); *Phenomenology of Spirit* (1807, "Religion" chapter); *Lectures on the Philosophy of History*.
- **Significance:** The most systematic 19th-century philosopher of religion. Religion as the Absolute Spirit's self-knowledge in representation (*Vorstellung*) ascending to Concept (*Begriff*) in philosophy. The *Lectures* propose a developmental-typology of world religions that prefigures phenomenology-of-religion.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `phenomenology-of-religion-school` (precursor), `problems/religious-language-problem` (Vorstellung-Begriff distinction), the comparative-religion taxonomies.
- **SEP entry:** "Georg Wilhelm Friedrich Hegel" + "Hegel's Philosophy of Religion".

### 5.3 Søren Kierkegaard (1813–1855) — Danish existentialism / Christian

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Fear and Trembling* (1843); *Concluding Unscientific Postscript* (1846); *Sickness Unto Death* (1849).
- **Significance:** The founding figure of religious existentialism. *Fear and Trembling* on Abraham's "teleological suspension of the ethical" is one of the most-engaged philosophical works on the relation between religion and morality. Kierkegaard's "subjective truth" and the leap-of-faith become foundational for 20th-century existentialist + continental philosophy of religion (Tillich, Buber, Marcel).
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/akedah-binding-of-isaac`, `problems/religious-epistemology-evidentialism` (paired-counter — subjective-truth as alternative), `21_theology/kierkegaard-theology` (paired sibling for the Christian-edifying-discourse side).
- **SEP entry:** "Søren Kierkegaard" + "Kierkegaard's Philosophy of Religion".

### 5.4 Friedrich Nietzsche (1844–1900) — German philology / philosophy

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *The Gay Science* (1882, 1887) — "God is dead" (§125, §343); *On the Genealogy of Morals* (1887); *The Antichrist* (1888); *Twilight of the Idols* (1888).
- **Significance:** The most consequential modern critique of Christianity-as-philosophy + the analyst of religion as ressentiment (slave-morality genealogy). Nietzsche's "God is dead" is not a metaphysical claim but a diagnosis of the modern secular condition — directly influencing the secularization-thesis literature (Taylor, Gauchet).
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/secularization-thesis` (foundational text), `06_themes/death-of-god`, `06_themes/genealogy-of-morality`, paired-counter to Schleiermacher + Kierkegaard.
- **SEP entry:** "Friedrich Nietzsche".

### 5.5 William James (1842–1910) — American pragmatism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *The Varieties of Religious Experience* (1902 — Gifford Lectures); *The Will to Believe* (1896).
- **Significance:** The founding text of psychology + philosophy of religious experience. *Varieties* establishes the descriptive-typological + pragmatic-evaluative method that Otto, James-Pratt, Stace, and Forman all extend. *The Will to Believe* offers the classical pragmatist defense of religious commitment under evidential underdetermination.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/religious-experience-as-evidence` (foundational), `06_themes/conversion`, `06_themes/mystical-experience` — paired with Otto + Stace + Katz.
- **SEP entry:** "William James".

## 6. Twentieth century — phenomenology of religion

### 6.1 Rudolf Otto (1869–1937) — Marburg phenomenology of religion

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Das Heilige* / *The Idea of the Holy* (1917); *Mysticism East and West* (1926); *India's Religion of Grace and Christianity Compared and Contrasted* (1930).
- **Significance:** The single most-cited 20th-century work in phenomenology of religion. *Das Heilige* defines the "numinous" — the *mysterium tremendum et fascinans* — as the irreducible category of religious experience. *Mysticism East and West* is an early comparative-mysticism work (Eckhart + Śaṅkara).
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/phenomenology-of-the-holy` (foundational), `06_themes/mysterium-tremendum`, `phenomenology-of-religion-school` (founder), comparative-mysticism literature.

### 6.2 Gerardus van der Leeuw (1890–1950) — Dutch phenomenology of religion

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Phänomenologie der Religion* / *Religion in Essence and Manifestation* (1933).
- **Significance:** The most systematic phenomenology of religion in the descriptive-typological mode. Establishes the "essence-and-manifestation" structure that Eliade later extends.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `phenomenology-of-religion-school`, `06_themes/sacred-and-profane` (precursor), Eliade-as-direct-heir.

### 6.3 Mircea Eliade (1907–1986) — Chicago history of religions / phenomenology

- **Tier:** T1 (with mandatory PHIL-1 paired-counter: Wasserstrom 1999 + McCutcheon 1997)
- **Key works:** *The Sacred and the Profane* (1957); *Patterns in Comparative Religion* (1958); *The Myth of the Eternal Return* (1949); *Shamanism: Archaic Techniques of Ecstasy* (1951).
- **Significance:** The most widely-read 20th-century scholar of religion. Eliade's universalism — "homo religiosus" as a trans-historical type, hierophany as the universal structure of religious manifestation — is the most-engaged form of phenomenological perennialism in religious studies. Foundational for popular and academic comparative religion alike.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/phenomenology-of-hierophany` (foundational), `06_themes/sacred-and-profane`, `06_themes/cosmic-axis`, `06_themes/eternal-return`, broad cross-link to deities + traditions.

- *Notes:* **PHIL-1 paired-counter MANDATORY** — Wasserstrom 1999 *Religion after Religion* + McCutcheon 1997 *Manufacturing Religion* document Eliade's methodological perennialism as a position with named opponents in current religious studies. Both critical works ship in the same batch as the Eliade node.

### 6.4 Ninian Smart (1927–2001) — comparative phenomenology of religion

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *The Religious Experience of Mankind* (1969); *Worldviews: Crosscultural Explorations of Human Beliefs* (1983); *Dimensions of the Sacred* (1996).

## 7. Twentieth century — analytic philosophy of religion

### 7.1 Alvin Plantinga (1932–) — Reformed Epistemology / analytic

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *God and Other Minds* (1967); *The Nature of Necessity* (1974) — Ch. X modal-ontological argument + free-will defense to the logical problem of evil; *Warranted Christian Belief* (2000).
- **Significance:** Founder of Reformed Epistemology — the school holding belief-in-God can be "properly basic" without inferential evidence. Plantinga's free-will defense is widely held to have closed the logical problem of evil in analytic philosophy. The modal ontological argument is the contemporary heir of Anselm.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/problem-of-evil-logical` (foundational defense), `problems/existence-of-god-ontological-argument` (modal version), `problems/religious-epistemology-reformed` (founder), `reformed-epistemology` school.

### 7.2 Richard Swinburne (1934–) — analytic / Oxford

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *The Coherence of Theism* (1977); *The Existence of God* (1979/2004 2nd ed.); *Faith and Reason* (1981); *The Resurrection of God Incarnate* (2003).
- **Significance:** Premier analytic natural theologian; argues cumulatively for theism via Bayesian probability. The contemporary heir of Paley's design argument (in updated fine-tuning version) + the cosmological-argument tradition.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/existence-of-god-cosmological-argument`, `problems/existence-of-god-teleological-argument` (fine-tuning version), `problems/religious-experience-as-evidence`.

### 7.3 John Hick (1922–2012) — Pluralist analytic

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Evil and the God of Love* (1966 — Irenaean theodicy); *An Interpretation of Religion* (1989 — pluralist hypothesis).
- **Significance:** The "soul-making" theodicy (Irenaean — evil as instrumental to soul-development) is one of two canonical contemporary theodicies. *An Interpretation of Religion* is the foundational text of religious pluralism: the major religions are culturally-conditioned responses to the same noumenal Real (Kantian framing).

### 7.4 William P. Alston (1921–2009) — Reformed Epistemology / religious-experience

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Perceiving God: The Epistemology of Religious Experience* (1991); *Divine Nature and Human Language* (1989).

### 7.5 J.L. Schellenberg (1959–) — hiddenness argument

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Divine Hiddenness and Human Reason* (1993); *The Hiddenness Argument: Philosophy's New Challenge to Belief in God* (2015); *The Wisdom to Doubt* (2007).

The hiddenness argument is the most-engaged new argument-against-theism of the past 30 years.

## 8. Twentieth-century philosophy of mysticism

### 8.1 W.T. Stace (1886–1967) — perennialist

- **Tier:** T1 (with mandatory PHIL-1 paired-counter Katz; per PHIL-3 §4 perennialism-discrimination)
- **Key works:** *Mysticism and Philosophy* (1960); *The Teachings of the Mystics* (1960).
- **Significance:** Articulated the perennialist hypothesis in its most rigorous form: mystical experience has a "common phenomenological core" (the pure consciousness event — PCE) across traditions, despite divergent doctrinal interpretations.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/mystical-experience-perennialism` (founder of the contemporary form), paired-counter to Katz 1978.
- **SEP entry:** "Mysticism" (cites Stace centrally as one of two foundational positions).

### 8.2 Steven T. Katz (1944–) — contextualist

- **Tier:** T1 (current academic-dominant per SEP "Mysticism" entry)
- **Key works:** *Mysticism and Philosophical Analysis* (ed., 1978); *Mysticism and Religious Traditions* (ed., 1983); *Mysticism and Sacred Scripture* (ed., 2000); *Mysticism and Language* (ed., 1992).
- **Significance:** The contextualist counter to Stace: "There are NO pure (i.e., unmediated) experiences" — all mystical experience is shaped by the mystic's tradition-specific conceptual framework. The strongest argument against any naïve form of perennialism.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/mystical-experience-contextualism` (founder), paired-counter to Stace + Forman.
- **SEP entry:** Cited centrally in "Mysticism" + "Religious Experience" entries.

### 8.3 Robert K.C. Forman (1948–) — neo-perennialist

- **Tier:** T1 (per PHIL-3 — live academic position in tension with Katz)
- **Key works:** *The Problem of Pure Consciousness: Mysticism and Philosophy* (ed., 1990); *The Innate Capacity: Mysticism, Psychology, and Philosophy* (ed., 1998); *Mysticism, Mind, Consciousness* (1999).
- **Significance:** The most rigorous post-Katz defense of a weaker perennialism centered on the "PCE" (Pure Consciousness Event) — a contentless awareness reportable across traditions.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/mystical-experience-perennialism`, paired-counter to Katz.
- **SEP entry:** Cited in "Mysticism".

### 8.4 Wayne Proudfoot (1939–) — constructivist

- **Tier:** T1

- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/mystical-experience-contextualism`, `problems/religious-experience-as-evidence` (constructivist counter-position).
- **SEP entry:** Cited in "Mysticism" + "Religious Experience".

### 8.5 Richard H. Jones (1951–) — comparative philosophy of mysticism

- **Tier:** T1

## 11. Twentieth-century overlap / process / dialogical

### 11.1 Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947) — process philosophy

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Process and Reality* (1929); *Religion in the Making* (1926); *Adventures of Ideas* (1933).
- **Significance:** Founder of process philosophy and process theology's philosophical-substrate. Whitehead's "dipolar God" — primordial nature + consequent nature — is the philosophical formulation that Hartshorne and Cobb extend.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `process-philosophy` school (founder), `21_theology/whitehead-process-theology` (paired sibling), `problems/process-philosophy-of-god`.
- **SEP entry:** "Alfred North Whitehead".

### 11.2 Charles Hartshorne (1897–2000) — process theism / neoclassical theism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *The Divine Relativity* (1948); *Anselm's Discovery* (1965); *A Natural Theology for Our Time* (1967).
- **Significance:** Premier process theist; revived and refined the modal ontological argument; foundational figure for process theology's philosophical respectability.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `process-philosophy`, `problems/existence-of-god-ontological-argument` (modal version), `21_theology/process-theology` (cross-link).
- **SEP entry:** "Charles Hartshorne".

### 11.3 Paul Tillich (1886–1965) — existentialist + philosophical theology

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works (philosophical side):** *The Courage to Be* (1952); *Dynamics of Faith* (1957).
- **Significance:** Major 20th-century straddler. *The Courage to Be* engages existentialist anxiety philosophically; "God as the ground of being" is philosophical-theology that has had influence in both philosophy of religion and continental theology.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/being-and-ground`, `problems/religious-language-problem`, `21_theology/tillich-theology` (paired sibling for *Systematic Theology*).
- **SEP entry:** "Paul Tillich" (limited).

### 11.4 Martin Buber (1878–1965) — dialogical philosophy / Jewish

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *I and Thou* (1923/1937 Eng.); *Between Man and Man* (1947); *Eclipse of God* (1952).
- **Significance:** Dialogical philosophy — the I-Thou vs I-It distinction — is the foundational text of relational philosophy of religion and of post-Holocaust Jewish philosophy.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `06_themes/i-thou-relation`, `06_themes/dialogical-personhood`, `21_theology/buber-theology` (paired sibling for *Two Types of Faith*).
- **SEP entry:** "Martin Buber".

### 11.5 Abraham Joshua Heschel (1907–1972) — Jewish philosophical theology

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works (philosophical):** *Man Is Not Alone* (1951); *The Prophets* (1962).
- **Significance:** *Man Is Not Alone* is a phenomenology of religious wonder; the "radical amazement" is Heschel's phenomenological-philosophical formulation of the encounter with the holy.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/phenomenology-of-the-holy` (Jewish-tradition formulation), `06_themes/radical-amazement`, `21_theology/heschel-theology` (paired sibling).
- **SEP entry:** Limited.

### 11.6 Edith Stein (1891–1942) — phenomenology + Carmelite mysticism

- **Tier:** T1
- **Key works:** *Finite and Eternal Being* (posthumous, 1950); *The Science of the Cross* (posthumous, 1950).
- **Significance:** Husserl's student who later entered the Carmelite order; *Finite and Eternal Being* attempts a phenomenological-Thomistic synthesis. Important figure for the phenomenology-Catholic-mysticism bridge.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `continental-philosophy-of-religion`, `06_themes/mystical-union`, Carmelite cross-link.
- **SEP entry:** "Edith Stein".

## 13. T2 / T3 / T5 — the perennialism cluster (per Rule PHIL-3, lens-spec §4)

These figures are cataloged with explicit tier-discrimination per the lens-spec §4 PHIL-3 rule. They are flagged but **not omitted** — CODEX v1.1's always-collect-never-discard rule applies.

### 13.1 Aldous Huxley (1894–1963) — perennial philosophy popularizer

- **Tier:** T2 (contextually defended — culturally-historically influential, methodologically informal)
- **Key works:** *The Perennial Philosophy* (1945); *The Doors of Perception* (1954).
- **Significance:** Popularized "perennial philosophy" in 20th-century anglophone culture. Bridges Vedanta + comparative-mysticism + early psychedelic-perennialism.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `problems/mystical-experience-perennialism` (popular reception), psychedelic-perennialism cluster.
- **SEP entry:** No direct entry.
- *Notes:* T2 per PHIL-3. Engaged as a historical-intellectual figure; not engaged as peer-reviewed academic philosophy of mysticism. Sedgwick 2004 documents Huxley's reception within the broader Traditionalist orbit.

### 13.2 René Guénon (1886–1951) — Traditionalist School founder

- **Tier:** T3 (alternative-school per PHIL-3 + CODEX v1.1)
- **Key works:** *Introduction to the Study of the Hindu Doctrines* (1921); *The Crisis of the Modern World* (1927); *The Reign of Quantity and the Signs of the Times* (1945).
- **Significance:** Founder of the Traditionalist School (perennialism as a coherent intellectual movement); foundational figure for Schuon, Coomaraswamy, Lings, Burckhardt.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `traditionalist-perennialism-school` (founder), `problems/mystical-experience-perennialism` (alternative-school position).
- **SEP entry:** No direct entry.
- *Notes:* T3 per Sedgwick 2004 *Against the Modern World* (Oxford). The lens engages Guénon on T3 alternative-school terms — documented as a recognized intellectual movement with its own claims and reception, not engaged as peer-reviewed analytic philosophy of mysticism.

### 13.3 Ananda K. Coomaraswamy (1877–1947) — Traditionalist + art history

- **Tier:** T1 in his art-history work (he was curator at MFA Boston); T3 in his Traditionalist-philosophical work
- **Key works:** *The Transformation of Nature in Art* (1934); *Hinduism and Buddhism* (1943).
- **Significance:** The most academically-respectable Traditionalist; his art-history work is mainstream-T1 (engaged on equal terms in art history) while his Traditionalist-philosophical writing is T3.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `traditionalist-perennialism-school`, art-history cross-link.
- **SEP entry:** No direct entry.
- *Notes:* Two-zone tier per PHIL-3. The lens carries both dimensions explicitly.

### 13.4 Frithjof Schuon (1907–1998) — Traditionalist perennialism + racial-drift

- **Tier:** T3 (perennialism-broad dimension); **T5 with mandatory political-risk-flag** (post-1981 racialist-drift dimension — "essential races" speculation)
- **Key works:** *The Transcendent Unity of Religions* (1953); *Esoterism as Principle and as Way* (1981, later perennialism); various later essays on the "essential races".
- **Significance:** The most influential post-Guénon Traditionalist; popularized perennialism via mainline-press translations. The post-1981 racialist material is documented by Sedgwick 2004 as the school's politically-problematic drift.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `traditionalist-perennialism-school`, paired with Schuon-racialist-drift sub-node (T5 — see spec §7 consolidated risks table).
- **SEP entry:** No direct entry.
- *Notes:* Mandatory two-node treatment per PHIL-3. The broad perennialism stays T3 (alternative-school); the racialist-drift sub-claim is T5 with political-risk-flag. Both nodes cite Sedgwick 2004 as the academic-history anchor.

### 13.5 Seyyed Hossein Nasr (1933–) — Traditionalist / Islamic philosophy bridge

- **Tier:** T2 in his Islamic-philosophy work (mainline-academic — held endowed chair at GWU); T3 in his Traditionalist-perennialism dimension
- **Key works:** *Ideals and Realities of Islam* (1966); *Knowledge and the Sacred* (1981); *Islamic Philosophy from Its Origin to the Present* (2006).
- **Significance:** The academically-respectable contemporary Traditionalist; bridge figure between Traditionalist-perennialism and Islamic-philosophical-theology. His Islamic-philosophy work is mainline; his Traditionalist work is alternative-school.
- **Cross-lens connections:** `traditionalist-perennialism-school`, Islamic-philosophy cluster.
- **SEP entry:** No direct entry; cited in Islamic-philosophy entries.
- *Notes:* Two-zone tier per PHIL-3.

### 13.6 Julius Evola (1898–1974) — Traditionalist + fascist political-theology

- **Tier:** T5 (disclaimer-required, mandatory political-risk-flag)
- **Key works:** *Revolt Against the Modern World* (1934); *Men Among the Ruins* (1953); *Ride the Tiger* (1961).
- **Significance:** Italian Traditionalist who applied perennialism to fascist + post-fascist politics; foundational for European neo-fascist intellectual culture (Goodrick-Clarke 2002).
- **Cross-lens connections:** wire ONLY via `polemic-against` / `polemic-inversion` edges. Atomic with `30_politics/julius-evola-traditionalist-political-theology` (T5 node, already flagged in politics-lens spec).
- **SEP entry:** No direct entry.
- *Notes:* T5 per CODEX v1.1, with mandatory citation to Goodrick-Clarke 2002 *Black Sun* (NYU) + Hansen 2002. Renders OFF by default. Cataloged for intellectual-history completeness; the lens documents what scholarship documents about the political-fascist appropriation of Traditionalism.
