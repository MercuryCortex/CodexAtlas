# Canonical Deity Colors — academic reference

**Date:** 2026-05-23
**Handle:** opus-colors-table-1
**Status:** PROPOSAL — research compilation for the "Canonical Colors" wheel-theme; awaits John's greenlight.

---

## Scope

For each major deity in the listed traditions, document the **tradition-attested canonical color(s)** with primary source. Where multiple colors are canonical, list all with the function/aspect each represents.

**Source-tier convention** (matches vault's existing T1-T4 system):

- **TI** = TRADITION-INTERNAL primary source (a Purana naming Vishnu blue; *Devi Mahatmya* describing Kali; an iconographic *dhyana* verse; the Roman Catholic Missal specifying liturgical colors). The tradition itself attests the color.
- **SE** = SCHOLARSHIP-EXTERNAL secondary source (modern academic iconographic surveys, museum catalogues, art-historical monographs). Modern scholarship documents the convention.

Both are valid for vault use; the distinction is preserved for honesty about what kind of evidence anchors each claim.

**Exclusion rule:** no New-Age "color symbolism" dictionaries, no chakra-color-correspondence pop literature, no mystical-rainbow encyclopedias. Either a tradition-internal primary text OR a peer-reviewed academic source.

## 2. Buddhist Five Families (Pancha-Tathagata)

The most highly-systematized color-deity correspondence in any tradition. The Vajrayana Five-Buddha schema is **load-bearing across the entire mandala system**.

| Buddha | Color | Direction | Element | Wisdom | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vairocana** | White | Center | Space (*ākāśa*) | Dharmadhātu Wisdom (mirror of reality) | **TI**: *Mahāvairocana Tantra* (*Mahāvairocana-abhisaṃbodhi-sūtra*, 7th c.); *Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha* (8th c.). **SE**: David Snellgrove, *Indo-Tibetan Buddhism: Indian Buddhists and Their Tibetan Successors* (1987); Yamasaki, *Shingon: Japanese Esoteric Buddhism* (1988) |
| **Akshobhya** | Blue | East | Water | Mirror-like Wisdom | **TI**: *Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha*. **SE**: Snellgrove 1987; Alex Wayman, *The Buddhist Tantras* (1973) |
| **Ratnasambhava** | Yellow / gold | South | Earth | Wisdom of Equality | **TI**: same. **SE**: Snellgrove 1987 |
| **Amitabha** | Red | West | Fire | Discriminating Wisdom | **TI**: *Sukhāvatī-vyūha-sūtra* + the Vajrayana mandala texts. **SE**: Snellgrove 1987; Galen Amstutz, *Interpreting Amida* (1997) |
| **Amoghasiddhi** | Green | North | Air / wind | All-Accomplishing Wisdom | **TI**: *Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha*. **SE**: Snellgrove 1987 |

**Important variant:** In later Vajra-family-dominated tantras (esp. Tibetan), Akshobhya and Vairocana swap the center (Akshobhya goes to center and becomes white; or Vairocana stays center but turns blue). Snellgrove (1987) explains this as the shift accompanying the rise of the *vajra* family.

The Five Buddhas extend to **Five Buddha-mothers** (Locanā, Māmakī, Pāṇḍarā, Tārā, Vajra-dhātviśvarī) carrying the same color schema, and to **Five Wisdom Dakinis** in highest-yoga-tantra material.

## 3. Egyptian pantheon

Egyptian iconographic color is heavily systematic. The general principle: **the flesh of gods is gold** (*nbw* — gold, "the flesh of the gods"). Deviations from gold carry specific theological meaning. Erik Hornung's *Conceptions of God in Ancient Egypt: The One and the Many* (orig. 1971; Eng. trans. Cornell 1982) is foundational. Richard Wilkinson's *Reading Egyptian Art* (1992) and *Symbol and Magic in Egyptian Art* (1994) are the standard iconographic surveys.

| Deity | Color | Source | Aspect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ra / Re** | Gold (flesh) + red disk (sun-disc *Aten*-glyph) | **TI**: Coffin Texts, *Book of the Dead*. **SE**: Hornung 1982; Wilkinson, *The Complete Gods and Goddesses of Ancient Egypt* (2003) | Solar fire; the visible sun |
| **Amun-Ra** | Often painted **blue** (sky-blue) skin in New Kingdom representations | **TI**: tomb paintings + temple reliefs (e.g., Karnak, Deir el-Bahari). **SE**: Wilkinson 2003; Hornung 1982 | "Hidden" Amun (the name = "the hidden one") shown blue = sky / invisible-air |
| **Osiris** | **Green** skin (most common) or **black** skin | **TI**: *Book of the Dead* vignettes; New Kingdom tomb paintings. **SE**: Hornung 1982; J. Gwyn Griffiths, *The Origins of Osiris and His Cult* (1980) | Green = vegetation, regeneration, the rebirth of crops; black = fertile Nile silt + underworld kingship |
| **Anubis** | **Black** (jackal form) | **TI**: Pyramid Texts; *Book of the Dead* mummification vignettes. **SE**: Wilkinson 2003 | Black = embalming-resin + Nile silt = fertility-through-death |
| **Set** | Reddish-brown / red | **TI**: the "Sethian" creature with red skin in some depictions; Late Period polemics call followers "red ones". **SE**: H. Te Velde, *Seth, God of Confusion* (1967) | Red = desert (*deshret* = "red land"); chaos; foreign |
| **Hathor** | Gold (flesh) + her **cow** form often shown with specific cosmetic / *menat* color codings | **TI**: tomb paintings. **SE**: Hornung 1982; Geraldine Pinch, *Magic in Ancient Egypt* (1994) | Gold = solar daughter-of-Ra |
| **Isis** | Generally gold (divine flesh) in her primary mode; **black** in mourning-Osiris episodes; **blue** in later Greco-Roman *Isis Pelagia* (sea-Isis) | **TI**: *Pyramid Texts*; the *Lamentations of Isis and Nephthys*. **SE**: R.E. Witt, *Isis in the Ancient World* (1971); Pinch 1994 | Color shifts by ritual context |
| **Nephthys** | Like Isis — gold or **dark** in mourning | **SE**: Wilkinson 2003 | Funerary mourner; Isis's sister |
| **Thoth** | Sometimes blue-grey (ibis form) | **SE**: Patrick Boylan, *Thoth, the Hermes of Egypt* (1922; still cited) | Lunar wisdom |
| **Nun (primordial waters)** | Blue (the cosmic waters) | **TI**: *Coffin Texts* spell 76; cosmogonic depictions. **SE**: Hornung 1982 | The pre-creation chaos-waters |
| **Bes** | Often shown with distinctive red-brown skin (unlike most gods) | **SE**: Wilkinson 2003 | Liminal household-protector deity; foreign / Nubian origin reflected in color |

## 4. Christian liturgical colors

This is a **liturgical** (not iconographic) system — the colors belong to the *vestments* and *altar cloths* of the church year, not to portraits of the persons of the Trinity. The system has unusual sourcing strength because the *primary text codifying it* is itself a single document: **Lothar of Segni (later Pope Innocent III), *De sacro altaris mysterio*, c. 1195, book I chapter 65**. Innocent III named four colors (white, red, green, black); the system was then expanded and made binding for the universal Church by the **post-Tridentine Roman Missal of 1570 (Pius V)**.

| Color | Liturgical use | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **White / gold** | Christmas, Easter, feasts of Christ (non-passion), feasts of Mary, feasts of non-martyr saints, baptisms, weddings, funerals (post-Vatican-II option) | **TI**: Innocent III, *De sacro altaris mysterio* I.65; *Missale Romanum* 1570 + 1970 Vatican-II revision; Catholic Encyclopedia (Catholic University of America Press) | Joy, glory, resurrection, purity |
| **Red** | Passion Sunday, Good Friday, Pentecost, feasts of martyrs, feasts of apostles (excluding non-martyred), Holy Spirit feasts | same | Blood (martyrs) + fire (Holy Spirit) |
| **Green** | Ordinary Time (Sundays after Epiphany + after Pentecost) | same | Hope, life, growth |
| **Violet / purple** | Advent, Lent (penitential seasons); funerals (traditional / pre-Vatican-II) | added in 16th-c additions to Innocent's scheme; codified post-Trent | Penitence, preparation, mourning |
| **Rose / pink** | Gaudete Sunday (3rd of Advent), Laetare Sunday (4th of Lent) — penitential seasons "lightened" | post-Trent codification | Joy breaking through penance |
| **Black** | Funerals (traditional); All Souls (traditional) | Innocent's original 4-color set; still permitted post-Vatican-II as a funeral option | Mourning |
| **Gold** | Often substituted for white on high feasts as "any color may be replaced by gold" per current rubrics | **TI**: 1970 *Missale Romanum* general instruction | Glory |

The Eastern Orthodox color scheme overlaps but diverges (e.g., **dark red / burgundy** for Lent in many Russian traditions, **white** more broadly for Pascha and the entire 50 days of Bright Week). Primary source: Hugh Wybrew, *The Orthodox Liturgy* (SVS Press 1989).

**Note on Christ's iconographic color:** unlike the Hindu and Buddhist systems, Western Christianity did NOT canonicalize a "skin color of Christ" — the figure is rendered with the prevailing local pigment range. In iconography, however, **Christ Pantocrator's robes** are highly canonical: red inner tunic (humanity / blood) + blue outer himation (divinity / heaven). Source: Robin Cormack, *Painting the Soul: Icons, Death Masks and Shrouds* (1997). **The Virgin Mary's robes** invert this: blue inner tunic + red outer mantle (in Byzantine tradition) — see Maria Vassilaki ed., *Mother of God: Representations of the Virgin in Byzantine Art* (2000).

## 5. Mesoamerican (Aztec + Maya)

The Aztec and Maya systems are **directional-color** systems. Each cardinal direction has its color, and gods associated with that direction inherit it. Foundational source: Mary Miller & Karl Taube, *An Illustrated Dictionary of the Gods and Symbols of Ancient Mexico and the Maya* (Thames & Hudson 1993). Companion: Karl Taube, "The Symbolism of Jade in Classic Maya Religion" (*Ancient Mesoamerica* 2005).

### 5.1 Maya directional colors

| Direction | Color | Maya term | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| East (*lak'in*) | Red (*chak*) | sunrise / rebirth | **TI**: *Madrid Codex*; *Dresden Codex* almanacs; *Popol Vuh* color-direction references. **SE**: Miller & Taube 1993; Aveni, *Skywatchers of Ancient Mexico* (rev. 2001) |
| North (*xaman*) | White (*sak*) | celestial / ancestral | same |
| West (*chik'in*) | Black (*ik'*) | sunset / underworld | same |
| South (*nohol*) | Yellow (*k'an*) | underworld-fertility | same |
| Center (*yax*) | Green / blue-green | living center; the *yaxche* world-tree | same |

### 5.2 Aztec deities — the Four Tezcatlipocas

The four directional-Tezcatlipocas split the cosmic year and the four world-quarters. Primary source for the four-color schema: *Codex Vaticanus A (3738)*; *Codex Tudela*; Sahagún's *Florentine Codex*. SE: Miller & Taube 1993; Davíd Carrasco, *Religions of Mesoamerica* (3rd ed. 2014); Eduardo Matos Moctezuma works.

| Deity | Direction | Color | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tezcatlipoca (Black)** | North / sky | Black (with horizontal yellow stripes on face) | **TI**: *Florentine Codex* descriptions; *Codex Borgia* iconography. **SE**: Miller & Taube 1993 (face-bands link him to the jaguar) |
| **Quetzalcoatl (White Tezcatlipoca)** | West | White | **TI**: *Florentine Codex* book 3; *Anales de Cuauhtitlán*. **SE**: David Carrasco, *Quetzalcoatl and the Irony of Empire* (rev. 2000) |
| **Huitzilopochtli (Blue Tezcatlipoca)** | South | Blue (hummingbird-feather armor and helmet) | **TI**: *Florentine Codex* book 1, 2; Hymn of Huitzilopochtli. **SE**: Miller & Taube 1993; Inga Clendinnen, *Aztecs: An Interpretation* (1991) |
| **Xipe Totec (Red Tezcatlipoca)** | East | Red (or flayed-skin-red of the *tlacaxipehualiztli* festival) | **TI**: *Florentine Codex* book 2 festival cycle. **SE**: Miller & Taube 1993 |

## 6. Mesopotamian

Mesopotamian deity-color is **less systematic** than the Hindu/Buddhist/Mesoamerican cases — it works through *attributes* and *materials* (especially lapis lazuli) rather than painted skin. Primary source: Jeremy Black & Anthony Green, *Gods, Demons and Symbols of Ancient Mesopotamia* (British Museum 1992; the standard reference). Also Wayne Horowitz, *Mesopotamian Cosmic Geography* (1998); Kathryn Stevens, "Bel-Marduk's Celestial Dais: Lapis Lazuli and the Materiality of Mesopotamian Cosmic Order" (Academia/AOAT).

| Deity | Associated color / material | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Marduk** | Gold + lapis-blue (the celestial dais of Marduk is built of lapis lazuli) | **TI**: *Enuma Elish* tablets IV-V (Marduk's elevation); royal inscriptions of Nebuchadnezzar II describing the *Esagila* temple. **SE**: Stevens (Academia 2021); Black & Green 1992 |
| **Inanna / Ishtar** | Red and lapis-blue (the dual aspect: love=red, war=blue/lapis); white in some hymns | **TI**: *Inanna's Descent to the Netherworld* (her lapis necklace); *Exaltation of Inanna* (Enheduanna). **SE**: Black & Green 1992; Diane Wolkstein & Samuel Noah Kramer, *Inanna: Queen of Heaven and Earth* (1983) |
| **Enlil** | (no strong color attestation); associated with the *kingship-aura* (*melammu*) | **SE**: Black & Green 1992 | The "shining aura" *melammu* of kingship is a brightness, not a single color |
| **Anu** | Lapis-blue (heavens) | **SE**: Horowitz 1998 |
| **Ea / Enki** | Often shown with flowing-water streams (no fixed flesh-color) | **SE**: Black & Green 1992 |
| **Shamash** | Bright / radiant (the sun); the rayed disk | **TI**: Shamash hymns. **SE**: Black & Green 1992 |
| **Sin / Nanna** | White / silver (moon) | **TI**: Sin hymns; royal inscriptions of Nabonidus. **SE**: Black & Green 1992 |
| **Nergal** | (no strong color attestation; underworld-associated) | **SE**: Black & Green 1992 |

**Mesopotamian summary: lapis-blue is the heavily-loaded color** (heavenly dais, divine ornament, royal beard). Gold is the second principal divine color. Red is feminine/war/Inanna. Most other deities are characterized by **attribute-symbols** (weapon, animal mount, headdress style) rather than skin color.

## 7. Yoruba Orisha

The Yoruba system is one of the **most rigorously color-coded** living religious systems. Colors are encoded in **eleke (bead necklaces)** worn by initiates of each orisha. The primary tradition-internal evidence is the bead-color pattern itself, transmitted within Ifá and Lukumí lineages. Academic sources: William Bascom, *Ifa Divination* (1969); Margaret Thompson Drewal, *Yoruba Ritual* (1992); Joseph Murphy, *Santería: African Spirits in America* (1988); Robert Farris Thompson, *Flash of the Spirit* (1983).

Note: there is **regional + lineage variation** (Lukumí / Santería in Cuba; Candomblé in Brazil; Yoruba homeland practice) — colors are nearly consistent across, but a given orisha may have secondary colors in one lineage and not another. The table below gives the most consistently-attested primary colors.

| Orisha | Primary color(s) | Domain | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Eshu / Eleguá** | Red + black | Crossroads, messenger, opener of paths | Bascom 1969; Thompson 1983; Murphy 1988 |
| **Ogun** | Green / dark green (sometimes black + green) | Iron, warfare, technology | Bascom; Thompson |
| **Oshosi** | Blue + amber | Hunting, justice | Murphy 1988 |
| **Obatala** | White (pure white) | Creator orisha, cool wisdom, purity, "owner of all heads" | Bascom 1969; Thompson 1983; Drewal 1992 |
| **Shango / Changó** | Red + white | Thunder, lightning, kingship | Thompson 1983; Bascom 1969; Brian Brazeal field studies |
| **Yemoja / Yemayá** | Blue + white | Ocean, motherhood, the seas | Thompson 1983; Murphy 1988 |
| **Oshun / Oxum** | Yellow / gold + amber | Fresh waters, love, beauty, wealth | Joseph Murphy & Mei-Mei Sanford eds., *Òṣun across the Waters* (2001); Thompson 1983 |
| **Oya** | Maroon / brown + 9 colors (the *iku-meji* "nine colors" of her wind) | Wind, storms, cemetery gate, transformation | Judith Gleason, *Oya: In Praise of an African Goddess* (1987); Thompson 1983 |
| **Babaluaiyé / Omolu** | Black + white (sometimes with red speckles) | Disease and healing, esp. smallpox | Thompson 1983 |
| **Orunmila (Ifá)** | Yellow + green | Wisdom, divination | Bascom 1969 |
| **Ibeji (twins)** | Red + blue / red + white (varies by lineage) | The sacred twin orisha | Pemberton & Drewal |

## 8. Additional traditions (brief)

### Norse / Germanic

No strong canonical color-system for the Aesir/Vanir comparable to the systems above. Color attributions in Norse iconography are mostly inferred from saga descriptions (Thor's red beard; the *einherjar* in gold) rather than codified. **Recommendation: do not force color-attribution for Norse deities.** Cite Hilda Ellis Davidson, *Gods and Myths of Northern Europe* (1964) for the general principle that Norse iconography was attribute-based rather than color-coded.

### Greek / Roman classical

Same as Norse: Greek deities are characterized by attribute (Athena's owl + aegis, Apollo's lyre + bow) rather than codified skin color. *However:* the "Gods in Color" exhibition + Vinzenz Brinkmann's polychromy research has revealed that classical statues themselves were heavily painted, often in conventional patterns (Athena's peplos with specific border designs). Brinkmann + Koch-Brinkmann + Dreyfus, *Gods in Color: Polychromy in the Ancient World* (Prestel 2017) — this is the place to wire painting-on-marble polychromy, *not* a deity-color schema. Liebieghaus Polychromy Research Project (Frankfurt) is the institutional home.

### Celtic / Insular

Minimal canonical color attestation. The Tuatha Dé Danann are sometimes given "shining" or "fair" generic descriptors. The exception is the **threefold goddesses** (Morrígan, Macha, Badb) sometimes attributed crow-black. Cite Proinsias Mac Cana, *Celtic Mythology* (1968) for the general iconographic poverty of pre-Christian Celtic representation.

### Zoroastrian

White is the dominant ritual color (Zoroastrian priests wear white; the sacred fire's smoke is white). The named Yazatas are not systematically color-coded. Cite Mary Boyce, *A History of Zoroastrianism* (3 vols., Brill 1975-91) for ritual-color context.

### Shinto

Color in Shinto attaches to **shrine architecture and offerings** (vermilion *torii*, white *shide* paper streamers) rather than to kami. Amaterasu's solar identity implies golden / brilliant-white but is not iconographically codified the way Vishnu's blue is. Cite Sokyo Ono, *Shinto: The Kami Way* (Tuttle 1962) and Helen Hardacre, *Shinto: A History* (Oxford 2017).

## 9. Color-coding integrity

For the visualization-theme wheel, this is the practical sorting:

### UNDISPUTED canonical color (paint with confidence)

- All **Hindu primary pantheon** (Vishnu, Krishna, Rama, Shiva, Brahma, Devi, Kali, Lakshmi, Saraswati, Ganesha, Hanuman, Surya)
- All **Buddhist Five Families** (Vairocana/Akshobhya/Ratnasambhava/Amitabha/Amoghasiddhi); Green Tara, White Tara, Mahakala
- Most **Egyptian primary gods** (Ra gold, Amun blue, Osiris green/black, Anubis black, Set red, Nun blue)
- All **Christian liturgical seasons** (white/red/green/violet/rose/black + gold)
- All **Aztec Four Tezcatlipocas** (black/white/blue/red) + Tlaloc blue + Chalchiuhtlicue jade
- **Maya directional palette** (red/white/black/yellow/green)
- All major **Yoruba Orisha**

### DISPUTED or VARIANT (paint with a primary but flag the variant in node body)

- **Shiva**: white (ash) is most primary; blue throat is local; full-blue depictions are regional Bengali / Vaishnava-Shaiva fusion zones
- **Akshobhya / Vairocana center-swap** in late-tantric mandalas
- **Isis**: gold primary; black in mourning episodes; blue in late Greco-Roman Pelagia form
- **Inanna / Ishtar**: red AND lapis-blue both canonical (dual love/war aspect)
- **Brahma**: red is most attested; iconography sometimes shows the four faces as four different colors (a derivative claim, weaker sourcing)

### NO STRONG CANONICAL COLOR (do not force; leave neutral on the wheel)

- Most **Norse, Celtic, classical Greek/Roman, Zoroastrian, Shinto** deities
- **Most minor / regional Mesopotamian gods** (Enlil, Nergal, Anzu)
- **Most Mesoamerican gods outside the Tezcatlipoca-quartet and Tlaloc** (Mictlantecuhtli, Tonatiuh, Coatlicue, etc. have *some* color in *some* sources but no universally-canonical attribution)

**Recommendation for the Atlas wheel:** when a deity has no strong canonical color, **paint by tradition cluster** instead (e.g., all Norse = a single tradition-tint; all classical Greek = another) rather than inventing color-attributions. Document the convention in a wheel-legend.
