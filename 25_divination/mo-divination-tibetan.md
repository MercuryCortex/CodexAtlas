# Mo — Tibetan Buddhist Dice/Bead Oracle

Tibetan Buddhist divination system using thrown dice or beads. The dominant standardized system — *Manjushri Mo* — was codified by Jamyang Khyentse Wangpo (1820-1892) in the 19th century and is the most widely practiced Tibetan-Buddhist divinatory institution today. Operates by throwing three six-sided dice (or moving three beads on a mala) to produce a code that maps to a fixed text of answers + interpretive verses.

## How it works

1. **Setup** — the consultant or diviner recites the *Manjushri* mantra (*Om Ah Ra Pa Tsa Na Dhih*) for the wisdom-bodhisattva who is patron of the divination
2. **Throw** — three dice thrown simultaneously yield a 3-digit code; OR three beads on a 108-bead mala are moved to produce the equivalent code
3. **Lookup** — the code identifies a section of the canonical Mo text; the text provides interpretive verses on the question domain (illness, business, journey, marriage, etc.)
4. **Interpretation** — the diviner contextualizes the text-output for the consultant's specific situation

The system is *text-mediated* + *deity-invoked* (the Manjushri patronage frames the consultation theologically) + *expert-mediated* (a competent diviner is required to apply the answer to the specific question).

## Cross-tradition position

Tibetan Mo sits at three cross-tradition junctures:

1. **Throwing-cast cross-tradition family** — parallel-form with Yoruba opèlè (chain), Roman *sortes*, Norse rune-casting, Mongolian bone-throwing. The cross-cultural pattern: *cast multiple objects; their pattern is interpretable as an oracular code*.

2. **Vajrayana deity-invocation framing** — the Manjushri patronage embeds the divination within a specifically Vajrayana-Buddhist theological framework. This is the distinctive Tibetan contribution: divination is not theologically-neutral (as in much pre-Christian oracular practice) but explicitly mediated by a wisdom-bodhisattva.

3. **Text-divination synthesis** — like [[bibliomancy-sortes]] (random-scripture-passage), Mo combines a randomization-mechanism with a canonical text-output. The combination of *random-physical-throw + fixed-text-lookup* is structurally cognate with the Sortes Sanctorum tradition and with the Yi Jing's hexagram-to-text mapping.

The 19th-century codification by Jamyang Khyentse is itself a methodological case: a major figure of the *rimé* (non-sectarian) movement systematized the divination, making it interpretively standardized across Tibetan-Buddhist lineages. The Mo system as practiced today is thus a relatively recent (post-1850) standardization of a much older practice-cluster.
