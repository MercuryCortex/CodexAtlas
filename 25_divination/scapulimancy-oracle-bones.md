# Scapulimancy — Heat-Crack Bone Divination

Ritual divination by applying heat to a prepared bone (typically an ox shoulder-blade or tortoise plastron) and reading the resulting cracks as oracular response. The Chinese **Shang dynasty oracle-bone corpus** (~-1500 to -1046; ~150,000+ excavated bones from Yinxu/Anyang) is the most-elaborated documented instance + the principal source for Shang religion, state ritual, and the earliest substantial corpus of Chinese writing (*jiaguwen* oracle-bone script).

## The Shang practice

The Shang king + court diviners performed scapulimancy for state-religion purposes:
- Questions concerned **sacrifice, military campaigns, royal hunts, weather, harvests, illness, dream interpretation, ancestor-consultation**
- Bones were ritually prepared (drilling depressions to thin); heated with a poker; cracks read for yes/no or short narrative answer
- The question + the answer + sometimes the outcome-verification were *inscribed on the bone* in oracle-bone script — making Shang divination simultaneously the largest single source for ancient Chinese religion AND the founding corpus of Chinese writing
- See Keightley 1978 for the canonical scholarly treatment

## MASSIVE-WIN — cross-cultural parallel-form convergence

Scapulimancy is independently documented in at least seven cultures with no plausible Bronze-Age contact:

1. **Chinese Shang dynasty** (~-1500-) — the most elaborated; oracle-bone script
2. **Korean** (Goguryeo + Silla periods; documented continuity from Chinese influence + possibly older indigenous practice)
3. **Mongolian** (continuous practice into the modern period; shoulder-blade reading)
4. **Tibetan** — bone-throwing + scapulimancy in folk-Buddhist contexts
5. **Inuit** — caribou scapulimancy for hunt-divination (documented Boas + later anthropological surveys)
6. **Native American (Naskapi + others)** — caribou scapulimancy
7. **Greek (Homeric)** — *Iliad* 2.299-300; Hesiod fragment; Boeotian tradition

The convergence is structurally striking: every culture using burning-bone divination uses *scapulae* (shoulder-blades) — a flat thin bone that cracks reliably under heat in patterns readable as figurative oracles. The cross-cultural reach is too geographically distributed (East Asia + Arctic + sub-Arctic + Aegean) for transmission to be the explanation across all cases; independent invention is the dominant scholarly view (Cooper 2007 surveys the convergence).

The structural finding: **scapulimancy is one of the best-documented cases of independent religious-technical convergence on a particular divination-form across geographically and culturally separated populations**. The pattern presumably reflects (a) widespread availability of scapulae from butchered livestock + hunted game; (b) reliable physical-property that scapulae crack predictably under heat; (c) the cracks producing visually readable patterns. Three structural-physical conditions + religious-divinatory motivation → convergent emergence.

## Cross-tradition position

Scapulimancy is the **convergent-emergence pole** of cross-tradition divination — paired with the **transmission pole** instantiated by extispicy → haruspicy ([[extispicy-mesopotamian]] + [[haruspicy-etruscan-roman]]). Both poles use animal bodies / parts for divination; the difference is that extispicy/haruspicy is a documented transmission chain (Mesopotamia → Etruria) while scapulimancy is independent emergence across separated cultures.

The two cases together provide one of the cleanest cross-tradition methodology-discipline demonstrations in the vault: **the same practice-class (bodily-divination) can be both transmitted AND independently emergent in different geographic-temporal frames**.
