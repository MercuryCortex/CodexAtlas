# Istikhara — Islamic Prayer-Divination

Islamic divination-by-prayer: a two-rakat (two-cycle) sunna prayer followed by a canonical supplication asking God to guide the consultant toward what is best in a specific decision. The practice is **Prophetic-established** (canonical hadith) and **operationally distinct** from the oracular-medium and lot-casting divinations of pre-Islamic + non-Islamic traditions: istikhara is **prayer + listening for divine guidance** rather than **manipulation of a sign-producing apparatus**.

## The canonical hadith

Bukhari 1162 (and parallels in Tirmidhi, Abu Dawud, Ibn Maja, Ahmad) records Jabir ibn Abdullah transmitting from the Prophet Muhammad: *"If one of you intends to do something, let him perform two rakat other than the obligatory prayer, then say: 'O God, I seek your guidance through your knowledge ... if you know that this matter is good for me in my religion, my livelihood, and the consequence of my affair (or: my present and my future), then ordain it for me and make it easy for me and bless me in it. If you know that this matter is bad for me in my religion, my livelihood, and the consequence of my affair (or: my present and my future), then turn it away from me and turn me away from it, and ordain good for me wherever it may be, and make me content with it.'"*

The hadith is the foundational establishing-text. Every subsequent Islamic istikhara practice grounds itself in this Prophetic precedent.

## How interpretation works

The interpretation methods are several + somewhat contested:

1. **Dream-interpretation** — after the prayer, sleep on the question; interpret dream-signs (white / green / clear water / lawful animals = favorable; black / red / unlawful = unfavorable). Used widely but considered Tier-2 by some scholars (no canonical hadith specifies dream-interpretation as the istikhara method)
2. **Settling-of-heart interpretation** — after the prayer, the heart inclines to one decision; that inclination is the guidance. The dominant interpretation in many modern legal schools
3. **Sign-interpretation** — events following the prayer (facilitation or obstruction) are read as divine response
4. **Quran-istikhara variant** — opening the Quran at random after the prayer; the passage encountered is read as guidance. Considered controversial in some legal schools (Hanafi + Shafi'i more permissive; Salafi-Wahhabi more restrictive); see [[bibliomancy-sortes]] for the broader cross-tradition bibliomancy pattern this connects to

## Cross-tradition position

Istikhara represents a distinctive Islamic articulation within the cross-tradition divination cluster:

1. **Prayer-as-divination structure** — uncommon in cross-tradition divination (most divinatory practices use apparatus + procedure; istikhara uses prayer + listening). Structurally cognate with Christian discernment-prayer traditions (Ignatian *Spiritual Exercises*; Quaker discernment; *lectio divina* attentive-reading)
2. **Bibliomantic variant** — the Quran-istikhara variant connects to the cross-tradition [[bibliomancy-sortes|bibliomancy / sortes]] practice
3. **Sufi extension** — Sufi orders developed elaborate dream-interpretation + 40-day retreat (*erbieh*) practices building on the istikhara framework; Schimmel 1975 documents the Sufi adaptations
4. **Modern practice** — istikhara remains one of the most-practiced Islamic devotional rituals in contemporary Muslim life across all Sunni + Shia + Sufi communities; the practice survives + thrives in the global Muslim diaspora

## Cross-tradition position summary

Within the divination cluster:
- *Pre-Islamic Arabian divination* (lot-casting at the Kaaba, *azlam* arrows) was condemned by Quran 5:90; istikhara provides the Islamically-licit alternative
- *Within Islam*: istikhara is the dominant licit divinatory practice; geomancy (raml), astrology (ahkam al-nujum), and other forms exist but are more contested theologically
- *Cross-Abrahamic comparison*: parallels with Christian discernment-prayer (Ignatian + Quaker) + Jewish goralot/lot-casting are structurally striking; istikhara is the prayer-pole of the cross-Abrahamic divine-guidance practices
