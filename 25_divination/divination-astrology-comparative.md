# Astrology — Comparative Spine (Celestial Divination Across Traditions)

The organizing spine for the **astrology** strand of the Atlas's divination lens — the "queen" of the divinatory arts and, by historical weight, the most consequential. The lens holds the individual astrology nodes (the Mesopotamian celestial-omen series, the Hellenistic textbooks, the Indian rasi and praśna, the Arabic and Latin manuals, the critique), but no node that lays out how they relate. This node supplies that frame. Like its siblings [[divination-typology-comparative]] (the whole lens) and [[astronomy-zodiac-comparative]] (the zodiac as a coordinate system), it is a **comparative spine, not a practice**: it does not describe a way of divining but maps the relationships among the ways already documented.

Its single most important claim is the one the history of science actually supports: horoscopic astrology is **not** a thing every culture independently invented. The mainstream version practiced from Baghdad to London to Varanasi descends, by a **documented, named, datable chain of texts**, from one origin in Mesopotamian celestial-omen science. The systems that genuinely arose independently — Chinese and Mesoamerican astral divination — are different objects, and the comparative work consists largely in keeping the one transmission apart from the several convergences, and both apart from the recurring charge that the whole enterprise is a pseudo-science.

## The four branches of astrology

Before the comparison, the object needs defining. "Astrology" in the lens spans four sub-genres, all present already in the Hellenistic corpus and recurring downstream:

| Branch | The chart is cast for… | Type-cases on the spine |
|---|---|---|
| **Mundane / omen** | the state of the sky as a portent for king and land | [[phase-1-019-enuma-anu-enlil]] (the Babylonian celestial-omen series) |
| **Genethlialogy (natal)** | the moment of a person's **birth** | the Hellenistic horoscope; the Indian rasi-chart |
| **Katarchic / electional** | a chosen **moment to begin** an action | [[phase-3-040-dorotheus-carmen-astrologicum]] (Dorotheus) |
| **Interrogational / horary** | the moment a **question** is asked | [[divination-jyotisha-prashna-horary]] (praśna); [[phase-6-054-lilly-christian-astrology]] (Lilly) |

The **omen** branch is the oldest and is the bridge to the hub [[divinatory-omen-reading]]; the **natal** branch (a chart with Ascendant, twelve houses, planets in zodiac signs, and aspects) is the Hellenistic innovation that makes "astrology" in the strict sense; the **electional** and **horary** branches share that apparatus and turn it from reading-a-life to choosing-a-moment or answering-a-question.

## The documented transmission chain (Babylon -> Greece -> India -> Islam -> Europe)

This is the canonical case, and the reason astrology is one of the best-documented transmissions in the history of science. The evidence is not typological hand-waving but concrete: shared technical apparatus, sign-names that translate one-to-one across five languages, and explicit source-attributions in the receiving cultures (Pingree 1997; Rochberg 2004).

- **Babylon.** The celestial-omen series **[[phase-1-019-enuma-anu-enlil|*Enuma Anu Enlil*]]** reads eclipses, planetary phases, and weather as state portents in the *summa* ("if…then") form. This is mundane omen-astrology, not horoscopy. Once the uniform [[astronomy-babylonian-zodiac|12-sign zodiac]] (c. -500) and mathematical ephemerides make a chart-for-a-moment computable, **personal horoscopy is born** — the earliest surviving cuneiform horoscope is dated -410. Rochberg's thesis: horoscopic astrology grows *out of* the omen science, not from a separate impulse.
- **Hellenistic Greece (from c. -200).** Greek astronomy adopts the Babylonian zodiac and degree-arithmetic wholesale and adds the **horoscopic apparatus**: the Ascendant, the twelve houses, planetary rulerships, the system of aspects. **[[phase-3-040-dorotheus-carmen-astrologicum|Dorotheus]]** (1st c. CE) supplies the electional and horary doctrine; **[[phase-3-038-ptolemy-tetrabiblos|Ptolemy's *Tetrabiblos*]]** (+150) is the surviving theoretical codification and the conduit onward.
- **India (c. +200 onward).** The 12-sign **rasi** chart enters via Greek-derived material; **[[phase-3-041-yavanajataka|the *Yavanajataka*]]** ("Greek horoscopy") attributes the system to a "Yavana" (Greek) source, and its sign-names are direct translations of the Greek. It is layered *on top of* the indigenous nakshatra ([[astronomy-lunar-mansions|lunar-mansion]]) system, giving the dual frame that defines [[tradition-jyotisha-indian-astrology|jyotisa]] to this day — with the Indian horary art **[[divination-jyotisha-prashna-horary|praśna]]** as one fruit.
- **The Islamic world (8th-9th c.).** The ʿAbbasid translation movement gathers the Greek, Sasanian, and Indian material; **[[phase-5-064-abu-mashar-great-introduction|Abu Maʿshar]]** (787-886) systematizes it and gives it an Aristotelian physical rationale. The Arabic astrologers are the indispensable middle term across the fall of antiquity.
- **Latin Europe (12th c. onward).** The 12th-c. translation movement carries the Arabic system into Christendom; **[[phase-5-066-bonatti-liber-astronomiae|Bonatti]]** (c. 1277) synthesizes it in Latin, and **[[phase-6-054-lilly-christian-astrology|Lilly's *Christian Astrology*]]** (1647) surfaces the ~2,000-year chain in a printed **English** manual still in use. The whole continuous arm is what the [[tradition-western-astrology]] node names in its title.

What travels here is a **specific artefact**, not a vague resemblance — the same houses, the same aspect-geometry, the same horary rules (perfection, prohibition, translation of light), and zodiac sign-names that map one-to-one Akkadian -> Greek -> Sanskrit -> Arabic -> Latin. That one-to-one correspondence, plus the receiving cultures' own attributions, is the decisive evidence that this is **transmission, not convergence**.

## The independent systems (convergence, not transmission)

Set firmly apart from the chain are astral-divinatory systems that arose with **no documented contact** with the Old-World line:

- **Chinese astrology** — *ziwei doushu* (Purple Star astrology) and *bazi* (the Four Pillars / Eight Characters) — is built on Chinese calendrics (the stem-branch sexagenary cycle, the lunar lodges), not the ecliptic zodiac, and develops in its own textual tradition. (It has no dedicated Atlas node yet and is named here in prose only.)
- **Mesoamerican astrology** — the **[[tonalpohualli-mesoamerican|tonalpohualli]]**, the 260-day divinatory count (20 day-signs x 13 numbers), fixes the *tonalli* (fate/soul-character) of a day and of those born on it. A genuinely astral-divinatory system on a wholly different mathematical base (260 days, not the 360° ecliptic), of separate origin, with no possible Old-World contact (Aveni 2001; Boone 2007). It is the **cleanest counter-case** to any "all astrology is one thing" claim.

These are not weaker members of the chain; they are evidence of **independent convergence** on the deep human impulse to read fate in time and sky — the same convergence the parent spine [[divination-typology-comparative]] documents for cleromancy and omen-reading generally.

## Cross-tradition significance (transmission vs. convergence)

The comparative payoff is that this node separates three distinct epistemic situations that the casual "every culture has astrology" claim collapses into one:

- **Secure transmission.** Babylon -> Greece -> India -> Islam -> Europe for **horoscopic astrology** is about as solid as cultural transmission in antiquity gets — concrete shared apparatus, one-to-one translatable sign-names, explicit source-attributions, an unbroken textual relay (Dorotheus survives *only* through its Pahlavi-then-Arabic versions). Pingree (1997), Rochberg (2004), Neugebauer (1975), and Barton (1994) are unanimous on the direction of flow. The occasional nationalist argument for indigenous Indian origin of the rasi is not accepted in the mainstream.
- **Independent convergence.** Chinese and Mesoamerican astral divination are **not** the transmitted system and were not in contact with it. Their existence shows that the *impulse* to astral divination is near-universal even though the *specific* horoscopic technique is a single inheritance. To call the tonalpohualli or bazi "the Chinese/Aztec zodiac" is a modern convenience that this node exists to resist.
- **A theme that recurs, an apparatus that does not.** The honest summary mirrors the zodiac spine's: **one horoscopic astrology, transmitted across five cultures; several independent astral-divinatory systems that share the impulse but not the apparatus.** The recurrence of star-reading elsewhere is not evidence that those elsewhere-systems are the transmitted art.

## Disputes

- **Science or superstition — and is that even the right question?** The science-vs-superstition critique is **not** a modern import; it runs the length of the chain. Cicero (*De Divinatione* II) ridicules the 'twins' problem (same horoscope, different fates) already in -44; **Augustine** (*City of God* V.1-7) rejects astral fatalism on free-will and providence grounds; and **[[phase-6-053-pico-disputationes-adversus-astrologiam|Pico della Mirandola]]** (1496) mounts the most influential premodern refutation — crucially, an *internal* one, from inside the Christian-Neoplatonic tradition that produced the spine. Historians of science (Tester 1987; Curry 1989; Vanden Broecke 2003) treat astrology as a once-mainstream intellectual technology that **lost** its epistemic standing in the 17th century, not as a perennial superstition — a stance this node adopts: astrology is reported as a historical system of knowledge-claims and its empirical failure is stated plainly, without either anachronistic ridicule or endorsement.
- **Where astronomy ends and astrology begins.** For most of the chain the two were a single enterprise (Ptolemy wrote both the *Almagest* and the *Tetrabiblos*). The Atlas separates them deliberately — [[astronomy-zodiac-comparative]] holds the observational coordinate system, this node the divinatory art — so that the genuine mathematical astronomy is never used to launder the divinatory claims, and the divinatory claims are never credited to the mathematics. The separation is an editorial decision, not a claim that the ancients drew the line.
- **Is "astrology" one category at all?** As with divination generally (see [[divination-typology-comparative]] Disputes), a minority position holds that "astrology" is a family-resemblance grouping rather than a natural kind — the transmitted horoscopic art and the independent Chinese and Mesoamerican systems may share little beyond reading-fate-in-the-sky. On that view this spine's value is heuristic (a map of the lens) rather than ontological. This node takes the heuristic-but-real position: the transmission chain is a hard historical fact; the broader "astrology" umbrella over the independent systems is a useful comparative convenience whose seams are stated.
