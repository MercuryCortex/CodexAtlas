# Tarot de Marseille and the Modern Occult Tarot

The **tarot** is a 78-card deck used both as a card game (the original use) and as a divinatory instrument (a post-1781 invention reframing playing cards as a Hermetic mystery). The relationship between the two functions is one of the most-documented cases of an early-modern playing-card pattern being retrofitted with esoteric symbolism.

## Historical reality vs. Egyptian-origin myth

**Historical reality (Decker / Depaulis / Dummett 1996; Dummett 1980)**: tarot cards originated as a 15th-century Italian playing-card pack, itself built on the ordinary four-suit pack that reached Europe from the **Mamluk sultanate** in the 1370s. The earliest surviving hand-painted tarot decks are the **Visconti-Sforza decks** from Milan, ~1440-1460, commissioned by the Visconti and Sforza ducal courts — see [[visconti-sforza-tarocchi-deck]]; the earliest secure documentary reference to *carte da trionfi* is the Este court ledger at **Ferrara, 1442**. The cards were played as the game *tarocchi*, a trick-taking card game popular across Renaissance Italy and France, and still played today. The 22-trump structure (Major Arcana) + 4-suit 56-card deck (Minor Arcana) is a 15th-c. Italian playing-card structural innovation, not a survival of ancient mystery teaching.

**The Egyptian-origin myth**: in 1781, the French Protestant pastor + Freemason **Antoine Court de Gébelin** published volume 8 of *Le Monde Primitif* containing an essay claiming the tarot was a survival of the lost **Book of Thoth** — an ancient Egyptian sacred text encoded in playing-card form. This thesis has **no factual basis** — Egyptian hieroglyphics had not yet been deciphered (Champollion 1822 was 41 years away), no evidence connects Egyptian religion to playing cards, and the tarot's documented 15th-c. Italian origin is incompatible with the claim. But the Egyptian thesis caught fire and became the foundational mythology of modern occult tarot.

Shortly after Court de Gébelin, **[[etteilla-jean-baptiste-alliette]]** (1738-1791) published the first systematic cartomantic interpretation of the tarot (*Manière de se récréer avec le jeu de cartes nommées tarots*, 1783-1785), establishing card meanings + reversed-card meanings + spread methods, and then issued the *Livre de Thot* (c. 1788-1789), the first deck ever designed for divination rather than play. Etteilla's system is the **first occult divinatory tarot**.

The chronology matters and is usually told backwards. Etteilla had been reading **ordinary 32-card piquet packs professionally since 1770** — eleven years before Court de Gébelin's essay. French card divination did not descend from an Egyptian book; it was an existing popular practice that acquired an Egyptian credential thirteen years into its commercial life, and then extended itself to the tarot pack. See [[divination-cartomancy-playing-card]].

## The Hebrew-alphabet retrofit — dated 1781, systematized 1854

The correspondence is routinely credited to Lévi. **It is not his.** In the *same* 1781 volume of *Le Monde Primitif*, printed beside Court de Gébelin's Egyptian essay, the **[[comte-de-mellet]]** (Louis-Raphaël-Lucrèce de Fayolle, 1727-1804) published *Recherches sur les Tarots*, which for the first time in recorded history maps the **22 trumps onto the 22 letters of the Hebrew alphabet** — and gives the earliest published tarot reading-method (Decker, Depaulis & Dummett 1996).

The transformative *systematizer* was **[[eliphas-levi]]** (Alphonse Louis Constant, 1810-1875), whose *Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie* (1854-1856) took de Mellet's letter-mapping and built it out into the full **22 trumps ↔ 22 Hebrew letters ↔ 22 paths on the [[sefirot-tree-of-life]]** architecture, with the four suits assigned to the letters of the Tetragrammaton and the four Kabbalistic worlds. This linkage has **no historical basis in either tarot or Kabbalah**: the trumps number 22 because a 15th-c. trick-taking game wanted 21 ranked trumps plus an unnumbered excuse card; the letters number 22 because of the Northwest Semitic consonantal script (see [[alphabet-hebrew-aleph-bet]], [[alphabet-sefer-yetzirah]]). No Kabbalistic source before 1781 mentions cards. What Lévi's construction did achieve was to make the tarot a **portable Kabbalah**, usable by European occultists who read no Hebrew — and on that basis it became the organizing structure of every subsequent occult tarot system.

Lévi's framework was inherited and re-cut by the **Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn** (founded 1888), whose [[samuel-liddell-macgregor-mathers|Mathers]]-authored *Book T* moved the Fool to *aleph* and swapped Strength and Justice to fit the zodiacal order — producing attributions **incompatible** with the French school's (Aleph → the Magician), which [[papus]] codified in 1889. Two rival letter-schemes, produced in the same decade from the same inheritance, is itself evidence that the correspondence was being constructed rather than transmitted (Decker & Dummett 2002). Golden Dawn members Arthur Edward Waite, Aleister Crowley and others produced the dominant modern decks.

## The Waite-Smith deck (1909)

In 1909 the publisher Rider released the **Rider-Waite-Smith deck**, designed by [[arthur-edward-waite]] (a Golden Dawn member) and drawn by **[[pamela-colman-smith]]** (1878-1951) — an artist born in London to American parents, raised partly in Jamaica and Brooklyn, trained at the Pratt Institute, initiated into the Golden Dawn in 1901 and a follower of Waite's rite from 1903. Its decisive innovation: **full pictorial scenes on all 56 Minor Arcana cards**. Pre-Waite decks showed only repeated pip patterns on the minor arcana, without narrative scenes. The Waite-Smith pictorial minors made every card readable as a small symbolic tableau — vastly easier for novice cartomancers, and readable from intuition rather than memorized correspondences. That single design decision, more than any doctrine, is why the Waite-Smith deck is the dominant modern English-language tarot; it also made the entire Kabbalistic superstructure optional in practice. Smith was paid a flat fee, went uncredited on the box for most of the twentieth century, and died in poverty in Cornwall in 1951.

## Tarot de Marseille as the alternative tradition

Within the post-1909 ecosystem, the **Tarot de Marseille** persists as the alternative European tradition: pip-style minor arcana, trump iconography descended from the 17th-19th c. French printer-engravers (Jean Noblet 1650, Nicolas Conver 1760), and a reading style that resists Golden Dawn correspondences. The Marseille school (Alejandro Jodorowsky, Philippe Camoin, Yoav Ben-Dov) reads the cards as a French Renaissance hermetic image-language, ignoring the Lévi/Golden Dawn Hebrew-Kabbalah overlay.

## Cross-tradition position

**Cross-tradition finding — invented-tradition divination as a case study**: tarot is the most-documented modern instance of an **invented esoteric tradition**. The components are:
1. A pre-existing material culture object ([[visconti-sforza-tarocchi-deck|15th-c. Italian playing cards]]) without esoteric content
2. A speculative origin-claim ([[antoine-court-de-gebelin|Court de Gébelin]] 1781) projecting ancient-wisdom origin
3. A systematized correspondence scheme (de Mellet 1781, [[eliphas-levi|Lévi]] 1854) linking the object to an existing prestigious esoteric tradition (Kabbalah)
4. A standardized visual canon ([[pamela-colman-smith|Waite-Smith]] 1909) embedding the correspondences in card art
5. Cumulative living practice that gradually loses awareness of the invention sequence

The same five-stage pattern produces:
- **Modern [[runes-divination-norse|rune divination]]** — Tacitus's lot-casting → Hávamál Odin myth → Ralph Blum 1982 systematization
- **Modern Wiccan ritual** — Margaret Murray's witch-cult thesis → Gardner 1954
- **Theosophical "ancient wisdom"** — Blavatsky's *Isis Unveiled* (1877) + *Secret Doctrine* (1888)
- **The Petit Lenormand** — Hechtel's German parlour race-game *Das Spiel der Hoffnung* (Nuremberg, c. 1799) → rebranded after Marie Anne Lenormand's death in 1843 as the deck of a fortune-teller who never used it; see [[divination-cartomancy-playing-card]]

Tarot is the cleanest case because the documentary record is unusually complete. Methodology-discipline requires distinguishing what is documented (modern living practice = real and meaningful) from what is claimed (ancient Egyptian origin = false).

**Position within the divination spectrum**: tarot is one node in the broader **card-cleromancy** family — and, specifically, a branch of ordinary European [[divination-cartomancy-playing-card|playing-card cartomancy]] rather than its root. The family and its structural neighbours:
- Chinese fortune-stick (*Kau Cim* / *qiuqian*, Buddhist + Daoist temple oracle) — see [[divination-omikuji-kau-cim-lottery-oracle]]; a formal parallel, not a source
- **[[bibliomancy-sortes|bibliomantic lot books]]** (medieval European *Sortes Sanctorum*) — the practice cartomancy structurally replaces
- Piquet-pack cartomancy (Etteilla 1770) — the immediate parent
- Petit Lenormand (German c. 1799, renamed post-1843)
- Modern oracle decks (post-1970s neopagan + New Age proliferation)

Tarot's specific contribution is the **22-trump narrative arc** (the Fool's Journey) which Waite-Smith made canonical and which gives tarot a quasi-mythological coherence absent in most other card-cleromancy systems.
