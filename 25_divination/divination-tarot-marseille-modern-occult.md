# Tarot de Marseille and the Modern Occult Tarot

The **tarot** is a 78-card deck used both as a card game (the original use) and as a divinatory instrument (a post-1781 invention reframing playing cards as a Hermetic mystery). The relationship between the two functions is one of the most-documented cases of an early-modern playing-card pattern being retrofitted with esoteric symbolism.

## Historical reality vs. Egyptian-origin myth

**Historical reality (Decker / Depaulis / Dummett 1996)**: tarot cards originated as a 15th-century Italian playing-card pack. The earliest surviving hand-painted tarot decks are the **Visconti-Sforza decks** from Milan, ~1440-1460, commissioned by the Visconti and Sforza ducal courts. The cards were played as the game *tarocchi*, a trick-taking card game popular across Renaissance Italy and France. The 22-trump structure (Major Arcana) + 4-suit 56-card deck (Minor Arcana) is a 15th-c. Italian playing-card structural innovation, not a survival of ancient mystery teaching.

**The Egyptian-origin myth**: in 1781, the French Protestant pastor + Freemason **Antoine Court de Gébelin** published volume 8 of *Le Monde Primitif* containing an essay claiming the tarot was a survival of the lost **Book of Thoth** — an ancient Egyptian sacred text encoded in playing-card form. This thesis has **no factual basis** — Egyptian hieroglyphics had not yet been deciphered (Champollion 1822 was 41 years away), no evidence connects Egyptian religion to playing cards, and the tarot's documented 15th-c. Italian origin is incompatible with the claim. But the Egyptian thesis caught fire and became the foundational mythology of modern occult tarot.

Shortly after Court de Gébelin, **Jean-Baptiste Alliette / Etteilla** (1738-1791) published the first systematic cartomantic interpretation of the tarot, establishing card meanings + reversed-card meanings + spread methods. Etteilla's system is the **first occult divinatory tarot**.

## Éliphas Lévi and the Kabbalah-tarot correspondence

The transformative figure was **Éliphas Lévi** (Alphonse Louis Constant, 1810-1875), whose *Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie* (1854-1856) established the **22 trump cards ↔ 22 Hebrew letters ↔ 22 paths on the Kabbalistic Tree of Life** correspondence. This linkage, which has no historical basis in either tarot or Kabbalah, became the organizing structure of every subsequent occult tarot system. It made the tarot a **portable Kabbalah**, accessible to non-Hebrew-reading European occultists.

Lévi's framework was inherited and elaborated by the **Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn** (founded 1888), whose members Arthur Edward Waite, Aleister Crowley, and others produced the dominant modern decks.

## The Waite-Smith deck (1909)

In 1909 the publisher Rider released the **Rider-Waite-Smith deck**, designed by Arthur Edward Waite (a Golden Dawn member) and illustrated by **Pamela Colman Smith** (a Theosophist artist of Jamaican-British descent). Its decisive innovation: **full pictorial scenes on all 56 Minor Arcana cards**. Pre-Waite decks showed only repeated pip patterns on minor arcana without narrative scenes. The Waite-Smith pictorial minors made every card readable as a small symbolic tableau, vastly easier for novice cartomancers + reading from intuition rather than memorized correspondences. The Waite-Smith deck is the dominant modern English-language tarot.

## Tarot de Marseille as the alternative tradition

Within the post-1909 ecosystem, the **Tarot de Marseille** persists as the alternative European tradition: pip-style minor arcana, trump iconography descended from the 17th-19th c. French printer-engravers (Jean Noblet 1650, Nicolas Conver 1760), and a reading style that resists Golden Dawn correspondences. The Marseille school (Alejandro Jodorowsky, Philippe Camoin, Yoav Ben-Dov) reads the cards as a French Renaissance hermetic image-language, ignoring the Lévi/Golden Dawn Hebrew-Kabbalah overlay.

## Cross-tradition position

**Cross-tradition finding — invented-tradition divination as a case study**: tarot is the most-documented modern instance of an **invented esoteric tradition**. The components are:
1. A pre-existing material culture object (15th-c. Italian playing cards) without esoteric content
2. A speculative origin-claim (Court de Gébelin 1781) projecting ancient-wisdom origin
3. A systematized correspondence scheme (Lévi 1854) linking the object to an existing prestigious esoteric tradition (Kabbalah)
4. A standardized visual canon (Waite-Smith 1909) embedding the correspondences in card art
5. Cumulative living practice that gradually loses awareness of the invention sequence

The same five-stage pattern produces:
- **Modern [[runes-divination-norse|rune divination]]** — Tacitus's lot-casting → Hávamál Odin myth → Ralph Blum 1982 systematization
- **Modern Wiccan ritual** — Margaret Murray's witch-cult thesis → Gardner 1954
- **Theosophical "ancient wisdom"** — Blavatsky's *Isis Unveiled* (1877) + *Secret Doctrine* (1888)

Tarot is the cleanest case because the documentary record is unusually complete. Methodology-discipline requires distinguishing what is documented (modern living practice = real and meaningful) from what is claimed (ancient Egyptian origin = false).

**Position within the divination spectrum**: tarot is one node in the broader **card-cleromancy** family. The family extends back through:
- Chinese fortune-stick (*Kau Cim* / *qiuqian*, Buddhist + Daoist temple oracle, ~6th c. onward)
- **[[bibliomancy-sortes|bibliomantic lot books]]** (medieval European *Sortes Sanctorum*)
- Lenormand cards (post-1845 French petit Lenormand)
- Modern oracle decks (post-1970s neopagan + New Age proliferation)

Tarot's specific contribution is the **22-trump narrative arc** (the Fool's Journey) which Waite-Smith made canonical and which gives tarot a quasi-mythological coherence absent in most other card-cleromancy systems.
