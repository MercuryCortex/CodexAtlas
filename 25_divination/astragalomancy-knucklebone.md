# Astragalomancy — Knucklebone Divination

The Greco-Roman divinatory practice of casting astragaloi — the four-sided anklebones of sheep or goats — and reading the resulting combination against a published oracle-key. One of the most-archaeologically-documented divinatory systems of the ancient Mediterranean: ~21 oracle-inscriptions surviving on temple walls + city-gate stones in Pisidia + Pamphylia (modern southwestern Turkey), each keying every possible five-bone throw to a verse-oracle in Greek hexameter.

## How it works

The astragalus is naturally near-four-sided (the other two "sides" — the rounded ends — almost never land up, so the bone effectively has four stable landing positions). In Greek convention the four faces were assigned the values **1, 3, 4, 6** (the values 2 + 5 were skipped, producing the characteristic Greek astragalus number-set).

The canonical inscriptional oracle-method:
1. **Five astragaloi** cast simultaneously
2. **The combination of upturned values** read in unordered form — there are 56 distinct unordered combinations of five draws from {1, 3, 4, 6}, and the canonical Pisidian oracle-walls list exactly these 56
3. **Look up the verse-oracle** on the inscribed wall corresponding to the combination
4. **Interpret** the hexameter verse in the context of the consultant's question

The published oracle-walls — at Termessos, Adada, Sagalassos, Selge, Cremna, and other Pisidian + Pamphylian sites — survive partially or substantially intact; Nollé 2007 catalogs them. They are the most-complete archaeological evidence in the Greco-Roman world of an *operational* divinatory system: we have both the mechanism (the bones, in funerary + domestic deposits across the empire) AND the keys (the inscribed verse-walls).

## The proto-dice — astragalus → cubic die

The astragalus is the archaeological + technological ancestor of the cubic die. The transition from naturally four-sided bone to artificially six-sided cube is documented across the Iron Age + Classical Mediterranean:
- **Earliest astragali** as divinatory + gaming objects: 2nd millennium BCE Levant + Greece (worked, polished, sometimes filled with metal to bias throws)
- **First cubic dice (*kyboi*)**: appear in archaeological record c. -700 onward, in parallel with continuing astragalus use
- **Roman *tali* (= astragali)** + *tesserae* (= cubic dice) coexist throughout the Imperial period; *tali* retain the divinatory + ceremonial connotation, *tesserae* are more purely gaming objects

This is one of the cleanest cases in the archaeology of probability of a divinatory technology becoming a gaming technology becoming a probability-theoretic primitive (the cubic die is the archetypal randomizing object in mathematical probability — but its ancestor is a divinatory bone).

## Augustus's Venus-throw at Apollonia (Suetonius)

Suetonius *Augustus* 94 records the most-cited Roman astragalus-oracular episode: the young Octavian (the future Augustus), studying at Apollonia in -45 BCE, consulted the astragalus-oracle of the local sanctuary. He threw the *Venus* — four bones each showing a different face, the rarest + most-favorable combination, named for Aphrodite/Venus as goddess of luck. The oracle was interpreted as predicting his rise to supreme power. Suetonius's narrative treats the episode as straightforwardly prophetic. The episode functions as a documented historical instance of an astragalus-oracle being consulted by a major political figure + recorded as prophetically validated.

## Why this matters cross-tradition

Astragalomancy sits at three cross-tradition junctures:

1. **The throwing-cast divination cluster** — structurally parallel-form with Norse rune-casting, Tibetan Mo dice-throwing, Yoruba opèlè chain-casting, Roman sortes lot-drawing. Cross-cultural pattern: a randomizing mechanism produces a code that maps to fixed symbolic / textual content. Astragalomancy with its published verse-walls is the most-completely-documented instance.

2. **The archaeology of probability** — the astragalus → die transition is the material-culture origin of the cubic die which becomes the archetypal randomizing object in mathematical probability theory (Cardano, Pascal, Fermat). The probability theory of the 16th-17th c. CE has divinatory bones in its archaeological lineage.

3. **The oracle-inscription archaeology** — the Pisidian + Pamphylian oracle-walls are the most-completely-preserved *operational* divinatory installation in the ancient world. Most divinatory systems we reconstruct from indirect literary testimony (the Delphic oracle, the Sibylline books); astragalomancy at these sites we can reconstruct from both the throwing-mechanism (archaeological astragali) AND the verse-key (inscriptional oracle-walls), making it a uniquely complete case study.
