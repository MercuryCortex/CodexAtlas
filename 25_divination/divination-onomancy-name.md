# Onomancy / Onomatomancy — Divination from Names

## Identity

Onomancy (Greek *ónoma*, "name" + *manteía*, "divination"; more strictly **onomatomancy**, from the stem *onomat-*) is divination that reads a person's character, fate, or the outcome of an action from their **name**. It is not a single technique but a fused pair of them. The **semantic strand** treats the name itself as a portent — *nomen omen*, "the name is an omen" — so that a fortunate-sounding name foreshadows a fortunate life, and names of good omen are deliberately chosen or given ritual precedence. The **arithmological strand** converts the name into a number: each letter is read at its numerical value (Greek isopsephy, Hebrew gematria, Arabic abjad), the values are summed to a **name-number**, and that number — usually reduced by the "rule of nine" (the *pythmēn*, casting out nines to a base digit) — is read for meaning or set against a diagram to yield a prognosis.

In the Atlas, onomancy is placed in the divination lens as a specific **application** that overlaps the letter-number sciences ([[alphabet-isopsephy-greek|isopsephy]], [[mathematics-gematria|gematria]], [[alphabet-ilm-al-huruf|abjad]]) but is distinct from them: those are general arithmetics of the alphabet, while onomancy is the **name-focused divinatory** use of them. Its home is the Greco-Roman Hellenistic world, where the two strands are first found fused; its number-substrate is [[tradition-pythagorean|Pythagorean]] arithmology; and it recurs, on a shared alphabetic scaffold, in Jewish, Islamic, and modern practice.

## In Greco-Roman practice

**The name as omen.** The oldest documented strand is the reading of a name as a sign. Plautus already plays on it — *nomen atque omen* (*Persa* 625, c. -200) — and by Cicero's day it is an institution: at a levy the first soldier enrolled, at a census the first citizen, and at the founding of a colony the man who leads, should bear a **name of good omen** (*bona nomina*) so that the act begins under a favorable word (*De Divinatione* 1.102). Cicero the augur reports the practice straight in book 1 and demolishes it in book 2, making onomancy one of the many targets of the ancient world's most sustained skeptical treatise on divination.

**The name as number.** On the Greek side the name was also a **number**. Because every Greek letter carried a numerical value ([[alphabet-isopsephy-greek|isopsephy]]), a personal name had a total, and that total could be read. The habit surfaces even in daily life: Pompeian graffiti encode a beloved by her isopsephic value — *"φιλῶ ἧς ὁ ἀριθμὸς φμε"*, "I love her whose number is 545" — naming a woman by a number only the initiate can invert. Turned to prognosis, the name-number drives the family of diagrams known as the **"Sphere of life and death"** — attributed to Petosiris, to Democritus, or to Pythagoras, and preserved in Greek magical-astrological papyri and in Byzantine and Latin medieval manuscripts. The procedure: take the isopsephic value of the querent's (or a contestant's, or a sick person's) name, add the day of the lunar month, divide by a fixed number (commonly 30, sometimes 9 or 7), and read the remainder in the diagram's **upper register** (life, victory) or **lower register** (death, defeat). To predict which of two gladiators or athletes would win, one compared their reduced name-numbers. This is onomancy as a working prognostic machine, built directly on the isopsephy substrate and the Pythagorean conviction that number is the deep structure of things.

## Cross-tradition significance (transmission vs convergence)

Onomancy is a clean test case for the Atlas's central distinction, and the honest reading is **mixed** — part shared inheritance, part independent convergence, part modern reinvention.

**Shared scaffold (the number-strand).** The name-number recurs in Greek isopsephy, Hebrew [[mathematics-gematria|gematria]], and Arabic [[alphabet-ilm-al-huruf|abjad]] because all three activate the **same inherited Phoenician letter-order** — the fact developed at [[alphabet-isopsephy-greek]]: three traditions each noticed that their alphabet was also a counting system and built a name-numerology on it. So the Jewish reading of name-changes as numerical events (Abram אברם = 243 → Abraham אברהם = 248, the added *he* = 5), the value of divine and personal names in Kabbalah (Scholem 1965), the abjad of a person's and mother's names for compatibility and talismans in Islamic practice, and the Greek name-number are not one borrowed from another but **parallel activations of a common Semitic-alphabetic scaffold**. The most famous single instance sits at the root of Christian scripture: the name-number "of the beast," *Neron Kaisar* (נרון קסר) = **666** (Revelation 13:18) — onomancy applied to an emperor's name.

**Convergence (the omen-strand).** The *nomen omen* habit — reading a personal name as ominous — needs no transmission at all. Naming a child for a fortunate word, or flinching at an ill-omened name, is a near-universal cognitive reflex; it appears wherever names carry meaning, and its Greco-Roman institutionalization (Cicero) is one culturally specific formalization of a convergent human tendency, not a diffused technique.

**Modern reinvention.** The onomancy most people meet today — the **"name number"** or "expression/destiny number" of popular numerology, in which A-Z are assigned 1-9 cycling and the full birth-name is summed and reduced to a single digit — presents itself as ancient Pythagorean wisdom but is in fact an **early-20th-century construction**, systematized by L. Dow Balliett (*The Philosophy of Numbers*, 1908) and her pupil Juno Jordan, with a rival "Chaldean" assignment popularized by Cheiro. As Underwood Dudley shows (*Numerology, or What Pythagoras Wrought*, 1997), its 1-9 letter-values are **not** the ancient isopsephic or gematric values, and its attribution to Pythagoras is spurious. It is a genuine survival of the *idea* that a name is a number that discloses fate — but a modern re-engineering of the *method*, not an unbroken inheritance of it.

## Disputes

- **Terminology.** "Onomancy" is a contracted (and etymologically loose) form of the more correct **onomatomancy**; older sources also fold the number-of-a-name method into **arithmomancy** (divination by numbers generally). The boundary between "onomancy" and "isopsephy/gematria" is a matter of framing: this node treats the alphabetic arithmetics as the substrate and reserves *onomancy* for their **name-focused divinatory** use, but the sources do not police the line consistently.
- **The "Pythagorean" label.** The attribution of name-numerology to Pythagoras — ancient (the prognostic "Sphere of Pythagoras") and modern (the "Pythagorean system") alike — is **pseudepigraphic**. Pythagorean arithmology supplied the cosmological conviction that number underlies reality; it did not supply the specific name-reduction tables that trade under Pythagoras's name (Dudley 1997).
- **Efficacy.** Like all divination, onomancy has no demonstrated predictive validity; the scholarship (Bouché-Leclercq 1879-82; Dornseiff 1925; Johnston 2008) treats it as a real and historically important **cultural technology**, embedded in the letter-number cosmology of its milieu, not as a working instrument.
