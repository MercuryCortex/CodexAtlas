# Binary-Permutation Divination Systems (Yi Jing · Ifá · Geomancy)

## Identity

This node is the **mechanism-family spine** for the divination lens's most mathematically distinctive group: the systems that produce an oracle by reading a random process as a **fixed-length string of independent binary choices** and looking that string up in a **closed table of 2^n named figures**, each figure carrying its own memorized text. It is a comparative frame, not a practice — like [[divination-astrology-comparative]] (the celestial arm) and under [[divination-typology-comparative]] (the whole-lens spine) — and it holds three principal members that keep their own nodes:

| Member | Binary depth | Figures | Home |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[geomancy-raml-cross-tradition]] | 4 rows | 2^4 = **16** figures | Islamic N. Africa → Latin Europe |
| [[yi-jing-divination]] | 6 lines (2 trigrams) | 2^6 = **64** hexagrams | China |
| [[ifa-odu-divination]] | 8 marks (2 tetragrams) | 2^8 = **256** Odù | Yorubaland |

The family's defining feature is that its members are **the same kind of machine**: a binary randomizer, a closed permutation address-space, and a corpus indexed by address. That is a stronger and more specific claim than "these are all forms of lot-casting" — it is why the group has a standing place in comparative divination studies (the "Yi-Jing–Ifá problem") and why it drew the attention of a mathematician, [[leibniz|Leibniz]], rather than only of ethnographers.

## The shared structure (the mathematics)

Strip away the imagery and the three members run the same three steps:

1. **Binarize chance.** Each system forces a physical randomizer to emit one bit at a time. The Yi Jing counts fifty yarrow stalks (the *da yan* rite, forty-nine used) or throws three coins per line, six lines ([[divination-i-ching-yarrow-stalk-method]]); Ifá throws the eight-half **opèlè** chain — eight bits in a single cast — or manipulates sixteen **ikin** palm-nuts, marking odd/even in *iyèrosùn* powder on the *opón Ifá*; geomancy strikes rows of dots in sand *without counting* and reads each row odd or even (one dot or two).
2. **Index a closed table.** The binary word is not interpreted freely; it names one address in a **fixed set** — 16, 64, or 256 figures. The outcome-space is finite and enumerated in advance.
3. **Read the attached corpus.** Every address owns text: hexagram judgments, line-statements and Ten Wings (Yi Jing); the *ese Ifá* verses, hundreds to thousands per Odù, that a *babaláwo* memorizes over years (Ifá); figure-meanings overlaid on the astrological houses (European geomancy).

Several members add a **combinatorial second layer** that operates *on* the figures. Geomancy is the clearest: from four randomly generated "Mothers" it derives four "Daughters," four "Nieces," two "Witnesses" and a final "Judge" by **row-wise XOR** — an algebraic transform, not a new throw (Savage-Smith & Smith 1980). Ifá pairs two four-bit columns into the 16×16 = 256 grid; the Yi Jing's "moving lines" flip specific bits to transform one hexagram into a second. Marcia Ascher's *Mathematics Elsewhere* (2002) makes the case at book length that such systems — she analyses the Malagasy **sikidy**, a raml derivative — are genuine formal mathematics, which is the warrant for treating the group as a **mechanism** family defined by combinatorics rather than a loose thematic grouping.

The ascending bit-depth (2^4 → 2^6 → 2^8) is the tidiest way to hold the three in mind, and it is also where the history gets interesting: the two systems that share a bit-depth — Ifá's 16 primary figures and geomancy's 16 — are the two whose independence is *not* secure.

## Cross-tradition significance (transmission vs convergence)

This family is one of the Atlas's headline cross-tradition cases, but the honest reading is **not** a clean three-way independence — and this node deliberately refines the flatter "no documented borrowing between any of them" statement carried by the individual member nodes.

**The secure convergence: China vs the rest.** The **Yi Jing is independent** of the African and North-African systems on strong grounds: there is no plausible contact route in its formative period (Western Zhou, c. -1000), and its structure is *different in kind* — 6-bit hexagrams built by stacking two of eight trigrams, a construction with no counterpart in Ifá or raml. That the Chinese and West-African/North-African traditions both arrived at binary-permutation divination is therefore a real case of **independent structural convergence** — arguably the cleanest in world divinatory history, and the reason the pairing is called the "Yi-Jing–Ifá problem." The convergence is best read on plausible structural grounds (binary is the minimal way to force a randomizer to yield an enumerable, indexable outcome), not as evidence of contact.

**The genuinely open case: Ifá vs raml-geomancy.** Here independence is **contested**, and the node should not launder the dispute. Ifá's 16 primary figures and Islamic geomancy's 16 figures are *identical in bit-structure* (both 2^4, four binary positions), the two traditions are **geographically adjacent across the Sahara**, and Islamic geomancy was demonstrably present in West Africa. Ron Eglash (*African Fractals*, 1999) argues for a historical **trans-Saharan diffusion** of binary/geomantic divination; the fact that raml **did** travel — into Latin Europe (documented) and into the Malagasy **sikidy** (documented) — proves the technology is transmissible and weakens any a-priori claim that Ifá must be independent. Against this, **William Bascom** (*Ifa Divination*, 1969), the standard authority, documents the resemblance but argues Ifá is an **indigenous Yoruba institution** with its own deity ([[ifa-odu-divination|Òrúnmìlà]]), corpus, and ritual logic, not a borrowing. The defensible verdict: **Yi Jing ↔ (Ifá / raml) is convergence; Ifá ↔ raml is unresolved.**

**The documented transmissions inside the family.** Two lines are not parallels at all but datable chains: raml-geomancy → **Latin Europe** (Hugh of Santalla c. 1145 → Agrippa 1531 → the 19th-c. occult revival), and raml → the **Malagasy sikidy** via Islamic Indian-Ocean contact (Ascher 2002). The family therefore contains, in one frame, both the Atlas's best convergence case and a textbook travelled-technique case — which is exactly why it is worth isolating from the general [[cleromancy-lot-casting|cleromancy]] node.

## The Leibniz recognition (stated accurately)

The family's most famous cross-tradition episode is mathematical. **Gottfried Leibniz invented binary arithmetic c. 1679.** In 1701 the Jesuit missionary **Joachim Bouvet**, at the Kangxi court, sent him a diagram of the 64 hexagrams in binary-sequential order; Leibniz saw his own system in it and published the correspondence as *Explication de l'Arithmétique Binaire* (1703). Two corrections that the popular version usually drops, and that this node keeps:

- **The direction is one-way.** The Yi Jing did **not** give Leibniz binary; he recognized his *already-invented* binary in the hexagrams and read them as independent confirmation. The common "ancient China invented binary computing" framing inverts the actual history.
- **The ordering he matched is medieval, not ancient.** The binary-sequential ("Fu Xi" / *xiantian*) arrangement Bouvet sent is the one systematized by the Song Neo-Confucian **Shao Yong (1011-1077)**, not an arrangement of the Zhou text (Ryan 1996, *Philosophy East and West*). The hexagrams **are** a binary symbol-set of great antiquity; their binary-*numerical ordering* — the thing that let Leibniz read 0–63 straight off — is an eleventh-century contribution. Leibniz's own claim that "Fu Xi more than 3,000 years ago" had his arithmetic rests on the traditional (mythic) attribution to [[fuxi|Fu Xi]].

Told this way the episode remains a striking cross-tradition recognition — a European rationalist finding his binary in a Chinese divinatory diagram — without the overreach.

## Disputes

1. **Is Ifá independent of geomancy?** The central open question (above): identical 2^4 base + Saharan adjacency + proven transmissibility of raml (Eglash 1999) vs Bascom's (1969) indigenous-institution thesis. Unresolved; this node marks it open rather than asserting independence.
2. **Is "binary-permutation" a real family or a modern imposition?** The grouping is defined by a *mathematical* property (fixed-length binary word → closed 2^n table) that no premodern practitioner would have named as such — it is Ascher's and the comparativists' analytic, read back onto the practices. Defensible as a **mechanism** description (the combinatorics are objectively there), but it is an etic category, and the members' own theologies (Zhou cosmology, Òrúnmìlà's wisdom, Islamic and Hermetic cosmology) are mutually alien.
3. **The Leibniz overreach.** As above: repeated claims that the Yi Jing is "ancient binary computing" conflate the antique symbol-set with the medieval Shao Yong ordering and reverse Leibniz's actual direction of discovery (Ryan 1996).

This node takes the mechanism-family position: the binary-permutation grouping is a **real and specific** refinement of cleromancy, its China-vs-rest convergence is secure, its Ifá-vs-raml link is genuinely open, and the Leibniz recognition is important precisely when told without the myth.
