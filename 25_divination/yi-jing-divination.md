# Yi Jing (I Ching) Divination System

Chinese binary-permutation divination system; one of the oldest continuously-operating divination institutions in human history (~3,100 years from earliest attestation to the present). Operates on a structure of **64 hexagrams** (6-line figures of yin or yang lines) generated by combination of **8 trigrams** (3-line figures), themselves generated by 3-bit binary combinations of yin (broken line) + yang (solid line).

## How it works

The consultant generates a hexagram via the yarrow-stalk method (older + more elaborate; 48 stalks, sevenfold division producing line-by-line variation including "moving lines" that signal transformation) OR the simpler three-coins method (each coin produces yin/yang; three coins yield one line; six throws yield a hexagram). The resulting hexagram + any moving lines are looked up in the Yi Jing text, which provides judgments (King Wen) + line-statements (Duke of Zhou) + Ten-Wings commentary (attributed to Confucius). The consultant interprets the text-output in the context of the question asked.

## MASSIVE-WIN — the Ifá parallel (structural cross-tradition convergence)

The single most-striking divination cross-tradition parallel in world religious history: the Yoruba **[[ifa-odu-divination]]** system (256 *odu*, derived from 4-bit binary × 4 — a 16×16 grid) is structurally cognate to the Yi Jing's 64-hexagram system (3-bit binary × 2 = 64). Both are millennia-old binary-permutation divination systems built on the same underlying mathematical-combinatorial logic, on opposite sides of the world, with no documented contact. Bascom 1969 documents the Ifá system; Smith 1991 documents the Yi Jing. The structural-cognation question is one of the principal open problems in comparative divination studies.

## MASSIVE-WIN — Leibniz binary correspondence (1701-1703)

Gottfried Leibniz independently discovered binary arithmetic ~1679. In 1701 the Jesuit missionary Joachim Bouvet (working in the Kangxi court in Beijing) sent Leibniz a diagram of the 64 hexagrams arranged in binary order. Leibniz recognized immediately that the ancient Chinese had developed the same binary system 2,700+ years before him. He published *Explication de l'Arithmétique Binaire* (1703) noting the correspondence. This is one of the most-documented cross-tradition mathematical-philosophical recognitions in early-modern science. See [[leibniz]].

## Cross-tradition position

The Yi Jing sits at three cross-tradition junctures:
1. **Divinatory**: parallel-form with [[ifa-odu-divination]] (probably independent emergence)
2. **Mathematical**: binary-arithmetic precursor recognized by Leibniz (1701-1703)
3. **Psychological**: Jung's synchronicity framework (preface to Wilhelm-Baynes 1950) makes the Yi Jing the principal Western depth-psychology engagement with a non-Western divination system
