# Bone-Throwing Divination — Southern African (Sangoma / Nyanga)

**Bone-throwing** (Zulu *ukubhula*, Sotho-Tswana *go laola*, Shona *kushopera*) is the principal divinatory practice of Southern African Bantu-speaking peoples. The diviner — Sangoma (Zulu, Ndebele) / Nyanga (some Nguni traditions, often distinguishing herbalist from diviner) / Inyanga / Iggira (Xhosa, often *amagqirha*) / Selaodi (Sotho-Tswana) / Mungome (Venda) — casts a heterogeneous set of small objects (bones, shells, tablets, dice, stones, coins) onto cloth or earth, then reads the resulting configuration as the ancestors' communicated answer to the consultant's question.

## The set and its composition

A diviner's set (*ditaola* in Sotho-Tswana, *amathambo* in Zulu, *zinyanga* in some Shona traditions) is built up over years. It is **never identical between diviners** — each Sangoma's set reflects their own training-lineage, ancestral guidance, and accumulation. Common components:

- **Astragali (knucklebones)** — most often goat, sheep, or antelope; sometimes pig or wild bovid. The astragalus is the universal divination-bone of the ancient world (cross-tradition parallel below).
- **Tortoiseshell pieces, cowry shells, vertebrae** — animal-derived elements signaling specific symbolic roles
- **Hand-carved wooden tablets** (the **Venda tablets**, four flat oblongs with carved faces) — a distinctive Venda development; each tablet is "male" or "female" and "elder" or "younger," producing 16 possible up/down configurations analogous to the Ifá 4-tablet odu structure
- **Cowries, beads, dominoes, dice, coins** — modern additions in 20th-21st c. sets
- **Stones, fossils, archaeological finds** — significant objects that come to the Sangoma through dreams or ancestor-instruction

Each element has fixed symbolic identity within the diviner's system — typically encoding categories like *male/female ancestor*, *witchcraft attack*, *type of illness*, *recovery*, *travel*, *body part*. The reading combines fixed element-meanings with positional relationships (close/distant, above/below, touching/separate) + which objects fall face-up vs face-down.

## The Sangoma's calling and training

Becoming a Sangoma is not chosen but **received** — the calling (*ukuthwasa* in Zulu) manifests as **ancestor-sickness**: unexplained illness, dreams of ancestral instruction, voices, sometimes serious mental-health crisis. The illness is interpreted as the ancestors selecting the individual; only initiation into Sangoma practice resolves it. Training takes 1-5 years under an established Sangoma (*gobela* in Zulu) and includes:
- Drumming + dance trance protocols
- Plant pharmacopoeia (*muthi* in Zulu) for healing
- Reading the bones
- Ancestor-communication protocols
- Customary law + community mediation

The completed Sangoma is simultaneously diviner, herbalist, ritual specialist, community mediator, and ancestor-medium. Modern South African law (Traditional Health Practitioners Act 22 of 2007) recognizes the Sangoma as a regulated healing profession.

## Cross-tradition position

**Cross-tradition finding — universal astragalus divination**: the goat/sheep astragalus is one of the most-universally-attested divination instruments in human history. Documented uses:
- **Greek [[astragalomancy-knucklebone]]** — astragali used as 4-sided dice in Greek + Roman divination; results read against pre-set astragalus-tables
- **Mesopotamian** — astragali from -3000 onward in temple deposits; precursor to dice
- **Mongolian *shagai***: traditional 4-side astragalus dice
- **Tibetan *mo* divination** ([[mo-divination-tibetan]]) — incorporates dice + astragalus elements
- **Roman tali** (knucklebones) — both game + divination
- **West African Ifá / Geomancy** — uses palm-nuts but the binary-quadripartite logic parallels astragalus 4-side outcomes

The Southern African Sangoma bone-set embeds the astragalus as one element of a much larger heterogeneous-object set. The cross-tradition pattern is that astragali — the small, durable, four-sided ankle-bone of even-toed ungulates — are the most natural physical analog of a 4-state random generator before the invention of dice. Their persistence across Indo-European, Mediterranean, African, and Central-Asian traditions makes them one of the strongest examples of **convergent technology of randomness** in human divinatory practice.

**Ancestor-mediation as the divinatory ontology**: unlike Western tarot (random mechanism + projection psychology) or I Ching (cosmological correspondence), Sangoma divination is unambiguously **ancestor-mediated** — the bones fall as they do because the ancestors arrange them to answer the consultant's question. This is the same ontology as:
- **West African Ifá** ([[ifa-odu-divination]]) — Òrúnmìlà mediates Olódùmarè's answer
- **Haitian Vodou divination** — the loa speak through cast objects
- **Tibetan Buddhist *mo* divination** — the deity's blessing arranges the dice

The cross-tradition pattern: ancestor / deity / spirit mediation as the divinatory ontology distinguishes traditional-religious divination from secular-Western divination's psychological framings. The Sangoma bone-throw is the principal Southern-African instance.

**Living tradition + medical-pluralism context**: bone-throwing divination is currently practiced by an estimated 200,000+ registered Sangomas in South Africa alone (Department of Health figures, 2018), with extensive parallel practice across Zimbabwe, Zambia, Mozambique, Botswana, Lesotho, and Swaziland. It coexists with biomedical care in modern South African medical pluralism — the Sangoma may diagnose witchcraft-attack as the meaning of an illness the hospital diagnoses as tuberculosis, with both diagnoses simultaneously valid in different explanatory frames.
