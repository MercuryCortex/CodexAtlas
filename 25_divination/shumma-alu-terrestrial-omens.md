# Šumma Ālu — Mesopotamian Terrestrial-Omen Compendium

The canonical Mesopotamian omen-series for everyday + terrestrial phenomena: ~120 cuneiform tablets organized as ~10,000 conditional "if X then Y" protasis-apodosis pairs covering urban features, animal behavior, plant growth, domestic events, household occurrences, and bodily marks. Standardized by ~-1100 (the Esagil-kīn-apli editorial tradition); copied + transmitted continuously through Neo-Babylonian times; principal source is the Library of Ashurbanipal at Nineveh (7th c. BCE).

## The conditional-omen structure

Each entry in *Šumma ālu* follows the canonical Mesopotamian omen-form:

> **Protasis** (the observed condition) — *"Šumma ālu ina mēlê šakin..."* — "If a city is set on a height..."
>
> **Apodosis** (the predicted outcome) — *"the people who dwell in that city will be in distress"* OR *"that city will prosper"* OR similar

Tablets are organized topically: tablets 1-10 = urban omens (city placement, gate-orientation, wall-condition); tablets 11-21 = snake-and-animal omens within the house; tablets 22-40 = wild animals; tablets 41+ = domestic-animal omens (cattle, sheep, dogs, pigs), insect omens, plant omens, fire-and-water omens, etc. The full corpus is encyclopedic — virtually every conceivable everyday observation is keyed to a divinatory reading.

## Use — the *āšipu* and *bārû*

The Mesopotamian scholarly diviner-priests had distinct specialties:
- **bārû** — the liver-omen + extispicy specialist (see [[extispicy-mesopotamian]])
- **āšipu** — the exorcist-diagnostician; reads *Šumma ālu* + *Sa-gig* (medical-diagnostic omens) to identify the demonic / divine cause of an illness or misfortune and prescribe ritual remedy
- **ṭupšarru Enūma Anu Enlil** — the astronomical-omen scribe; reads the celestial-omen series for state-relevant heavenly events

For an observed terrestrial omen — a snake entering the house, an unusual animal behavior, a strange domestic event — the *āšipu* would locate the phenomenon in the relevant tablet of *Šumma ālu* and read the corresponding apodosis. If the apodosis concerned the king or the state, an official report was forwarded to the royal court; the Ashurbanipal correspondence preserves dozens of such scholarly reports (Hunger 1992, *Astrological Reports to Assyrian Kings*).

## MASSIVE-WIN — divination as the origin of systematic classification

Jean Bottéro's principal thesis (*Mésopotamie* 1987) treats the Mesopotamian omen-series as **the earliest documented systematic-rational classificatory + inferential corpus in human intellectual history**. The argument:

1. The omen-corpora — *Šumma ālu*, *Enūma Anu Enlil*, *Bārûtu*, *Šumma izbu*, *Sa-gig* — collectively constitute ~30,000+ conditional statements organized topically + indexed systematically
2. The form is rigorously *propositional* — every entry is a conditional "if-then" linking an observable phenomenon to a predicted outcome
3. The corpora are *exhaustive* in coverage (the scribes attempted to enumerate every possible variation of a phenomenon)
4. The corpora are *transmitted with editorial discipline* — successive recensions standardize the text + extend coverage
5. The cognitive operations involved — observation, classification, inference, generalization, systematic record-keeping — are the operations of *proto-science*

Bottéro's claim is not that the divinatory predictions were accurate, but that the *cognitive machinery* the omen-corpora developed — the conditional form, the systematic classification, the inferential chain — is the documentary origin of the systematic-classificatory reasoning that becomes Greek logic + science. *The world's first encyclopedias are divinatory*.

This is one of the headline cross-tradition findings in the comparative history of divination: divinatory practice is not merely "primitive irrationalism" being supplanted by science; *divinatory practice is one of the lineages out of which systematic rationality emerges*. The conditional-form of *Šumma ālu* is the cuneiform predecessor of every "if A then B" statement in mathematics + logic + science.

## Cross-tradition transmission chain

*Šumma ālu* + the broader Mesopotamian omen-corpus is the *parent-tradition* of multiple downstream divinatory institutions:

- **Hittite + Hurrian omen-texts** (~-1500-1200) — the Hattuša archives include omen-texts directly modeled on Babylonian originals; the Hittites adapted the Mesopotamian system for their own use
- **Aramaean + Phoenician omen-texts** (~-900-500) — fragmentary; show continued Mesopotamian influence
- **Etruscan + Roman augury + haruspicy** — the *Šumma ālu* bird-and-animal sections + the *Bārûtu* liver-omens are documented (Bottéro, Wardle) as parts of the chain that ends in Etruscan augury + Roman haruspicy (see [[haruspicy-etruscan-roman]] + [[augury-roman-bird-omens]])
- **Hellenistic astrology** — the *Enūma Anu Enlil* celestial-omen series is the documented source-stream for Hellenistic astrology (Rochberg 1998 *Babylonian Horoscopes*; Furley + Gysembergh 2015 — Greek extispicy preserves direct Mesopotamian transmission)
- **Hebrew Bible** — multiple omen-references (the *teraphim*, Joseph's silver-cup divination, the Urim and Thummim) reflect a Levantine omen-cultural milieu shaped by Mesopotamian institutional precedent

## The institutional archaeology

The *Šumma ālu* corpus survives primarily through:
- **The Library of Ashurbanipal at Nineveh** (~-650) — Ashurbanipal's deliberate state-funded omen-library; the most-complete surviving copies
- **Late-Babylonian scholarly archives** at Babylon, Borsippa, Uruk (5th-3rd c. BCE) — continued copying + scholarly transmission into the Hellenistic period
- **Mari archives** (~-1800) — earlier Old Babylonian fragments showing the system's antiquity
- **Hattuša archives** (~-1400) — Hittite adaptations confirming transmission westward

Sally Freedman's three-volume critical edition (1998, 2006, 2017) is the modern definitive reconstruction of the canonical series.

## Cross-tradition position

*Šumma ālu* is the most-systematically-developed canonical terrestrial-omen corpus surviving from the ancient world + the principal documentary source for understanding institutional divinatory-rational reasoning in early human intellectual history. Its position in the comparative history of divination is roughly analogous to the Yi Jing's position in the comparative history of binary-permutation divination: the most-fully-developed example of its type, with millennia of continuous practice + a substantial surviving textual corpus.
