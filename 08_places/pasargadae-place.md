# Pasargadae

**Pasargadae** is the **founding capital of the Achaemenid Persian Empire**, built by **Cyrus the Great** c. -546 in the Fars region of southern Iran (modern Pārs, ~90 km north-east of Shiraz). The site contains the **Tomb of Cyrus** — the architectural anchor of Persian cultural memory across the entire Achaemenid + Hellenistic + Sasanian + Islamic + modern Iranian sequence — and is the **architectural archetype of the Persian quadripartite *čahār-bāgh* garden** that propagates downstream into the cross-tradition paradise-imagery of every major Eurasian religious tradition.

## Founding context

Cyrus the Great defeated the Median king Astyages at the battle of Pasargadae (-550), inaugurating the Achaemenid dynasty. He commemorated the victory by building a capital on the site, c. -546. Pasargadae remained the political capital until Darius I moved the administration to **Persepolis** (~-518), but it continued as a sacred site — the place of royal coronation rituals — through the Achaemenid + Sasanian periods.

## The Tomb of Cyrus

The freestanding stepped-platform tomb in the southwest sector (~12 m height; six stepped courses + a gabled-roofed chamber) is **one of the best-preserved Achaemenid monuments**. The architectural form fuses **Persian + Lydian + possibly Mesopotamian ziggurat** influences. Alexander the Great visited the tomb during his conquest of Persia (-330) and is reported by Arrian (Anabasis 6.29) + Strabo (15.3.7) to have honored Cyrus by ordering its restoration after finding it desecrated. The Greek + Hellenistic + Roman testimony makes the Tomb of Cyrus one of the most-continuously-attested ancient monuments. Through the Islamic period, the site was popularly identified as **"the Tomb of the Mother of Solomon"** (preserving its sacredness across the religious-tradition shift).

## MASSIVE-WIN: Pasargadae and the origin of "paradise"

The quadripartite *čahār-bāgh* ("four-garden") layout of Palace P at Pasargadae — **walled garden with cross-axis water channels dividing it into four quadrants** — is the architectural archetype of the Persian **paridaida** ("walled garden"), the Old Persian noun that becomes:

- **Greek *paradeisos*** (loaned via Xenophon, Anabasis 1.2.7, describing Cyrus the Younger's gardens).
- **Hebrew *pardes*** (Song of Songs 4:13; Ecclesiastes 2:5; Nehemiah 2:8).
- **Aramaic *pardesa*** (Targumic + rabbinic).
- **Christian-Latin *paradisus*** (Vulgate; the Garden of Eden becomes "paradise").
- **English "paradise"** + parallel terms across most European languages.

The walled-garden-as-cosmic-image originates at Pasargadae and propagates through Hellenistic + Jewish + Christian + Islamic + Mughal traditions, becoming the cross-tradition standard image of the **post-mortem reward** and the **primordial perfect place**.

## MASSIVE-WIN: Pasargadae and the Persian-period injection

Pasargadae is the **founding capital of the empire that executes the Persian-period injection** — the Achaemenid transmission of Zoroastrian-derived theological elements (cosmic dualism, archangelology, demonology, resurrection-eschatology, Saoshyant-messianic-template) into Second-Temple Judaism (Boyce 1991 vol. 3; Cohn 1993; Hultgård 1998). The empire that takes the Jewish exiles back to Jerusalem under **Cyrus's edict** (Ezra 1:1-4, Isaiah 45:1-7 "Cyrus is the LORD's anointed") is the same empire founded here. The architectural + political memory anchors the entire cross-tradition transmission event at this single founding-place.
