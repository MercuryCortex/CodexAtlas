# Naqsh-e Rustam

**Naqsh-e Rustam** ("the Picture / Image of Rustam," in modern Persian; the modern name preserves a later popular identification with the legendary hero Rustam of the *Shāhnāmeh*) is **a sandstone cliff in the Marvdasht plain of Fars** (c. 12 km north-west of Persepolis) that holds **two of the most-important monumental complexes of ancient Iran**:

1. **The Achaemenid royal necropolis** — the cliff-tombs of **Darius I, Xerxes I, Artaxerxes I, and Darius II** (-486 to -404).
2. **The Sasanian rock-relief pantheon** — eight reliefs (3rd-4th c. CE) celebrating Sasanian kings + their relationship to the Zoroastrian divine order.

The site is functionally the **axis-mundi of imperial Persian Zoroastrianism** across a 1000-year span — the principal monumental document of how the Persian state articulated its relationship to Ahura Mazda's cosmic order.

## The Achaemenid royal tombs

The four cliff-tombs are carved into the vertical face of the cliff, c. 20 m above ground level, in a cross-shaped façade (the so-called "Persian cross" form). The **Tomb of Darius I** (-486) is the prototype + the only one with surviving identifying inscriptions. Its **DNa inscription** — Old Persian + Elamite + Babylonian — is one of the most-important primary documents of Achaemenid royal-Zoroastrian ideology:

> *"A great god is Ahuramazda, who created this earth, who created yonder sky, who created man, who created happiness for man, who made Darius king."*

The opening declaration is the canonical formulation of Achaemenid royal theology: **the king as Ahuramazda's chosen instrument**.

## The Sasanian rock-relief pantheon

In the 3rd-4th c. CE, the Sasanian dynasty (224-651) commissioned monumental rock-reliefs at Naqsh-e Rustam to articulate their **revival of Achaemenid + Zoroastrian imperial-religious continuity** after the four centuries of Parthian rule. The eight reliefs include:

- **Ardashir I's investiture by Ahura Mazda** (224 CE): the founding image of the Sasanian dynasty. Ardashir and Ahura Mazda face each other on horseback; Ahura Mazda hands Ardashir a *cydaris* (royal diadem). The image is the **canonical scene of theocratic legitimation** that propagates downstream into Byzantine + Islamic ruler-iconography.
- **Shapur I's triumph over the Roman emperor Valerian** (260 CE): commemorating the Sasanian capture of the Roman emperor at Edessa. Valerian kneels in submission before Shapur on horseback.
- **Hormizd II's equestrian battle + Bahram II's court reliefs** (3rd-4th c.).

## The Ka'ba-ye Zardosht

At the foot of the cliff stands the **Ka'ba-ye Zardosht** ("Cube of Zoroaster") — a square tower-like Achaemenid structure (~12 m height) of disputed function: Zoroastrian fire-tower? Royal-dynastic treasury? Dynastic archive? The structure dates to the Achaemenid period (c. -500), but its walls were **inscribed by Sasanian kings + priests in the 3rd c. CE**:

- **ŠKZ inscription** (Šābuhr I's Ka'ba inscription, ~262 CE): a trilingual (Middle Persian + Parthian + Greek) royal proclamation that names + locates the Sasanian fire-temples + chronicles Shapur's victories over Rome.
- **Kartīr's inscriptions** (~270s-280s CE): the inscriptions of the high-priest Kartīr, the principal Sasanian Zoroastrian state-orthodoxy ideologue. Kartīr's inscriptions narrate his rise + his **persecution of Manichaeans, Christians, Jews, Buddhists, and other religious-minorities** under Sasanian state Zoroastrianism — one of the most important primary documents of 3rd-c. religious-history.

## MASSIVE-WIN: the Sasanian investiture iconography

The **Ardashir I investiture relief** is the canonical image of Zoroastrian sacred-kingship — the king-receives-divine-glory directly from Ahura Mazda. The image is the source-template for:

- **Byzantine Christian** ruler-iconography (the emperor crowned by Christ).
- **Umayyad + Abbasid Islamic** ruler-iconography (the caliph as God's deputy, *khalīfat Allāh*).
- **Sasanian-influenced Mughal + Persianate Islamic** ruler-portraiture.

The trans-Mediterranean propagation is documented by Canepa 2009 *The Two Eyes of the Earth*. The Sasanian Naqsh-e Rustam reliefs are arguably the **single most-consequential monumental site for the development of cross-tradition sacred-kingship iconography** in the 1st millennium CE.
