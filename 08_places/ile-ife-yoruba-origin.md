# Ile-Ife — Yoruba Cosmic Origin Point

## Identity
The sacred city of Ile-Ife in southwestern Nigeria is the cosmological origin-point of Yoruba creation theology — the place from which Oduduwa (in some traditions) or Obatala (in others) descended to begin creation, where the waters were separated from the dry land, where the first orishas gathered, and from which the human race spread to the world. It is also a historical-archaeological reality: classical Ife (c. 1100–1450) was a major artistic and political center whose bronze and terracotta heads are among the most-celebrated sculpture in world art history. Drewal (ed.) *Dynasties and Divinity* (2009) is the standard recent academic treatment.

## The cosmogonic narrative
In the canonical Yoruba creation account (with variation across lineages):
1. **Olódùmarè** (the supreme being) decided to create the world; the cosmos initially consisted only of sky above and water below.
2. **Obatala** (in one version) or **Oduduwa** (in another) was sent down with a snail shell containing earth and a chicken to scatter the earth.
3. The earth was scattered; the chicken scratched it into hills and valleys; dry land emerged.
4. This first emergence happened at **Ile-Ife** — literally "the house of Ife" or "the wide place."
5. The orishas descended from the sky on a chain at Ile-Ife; the human race was created there.
6. From Ile-Ife humans dispersed to populate the world.

The Obatala-vs-Oduduwa-as-creator dispute is itself theologically significant: the two cosmogonic accounts are associated with different priestly lineages and different political factions in classical Ife. Bascom (1969) catalogues the variations; Idowu (1962) presents the most theologically synthetic Yoruba account.

## Historical archaeology
Classical Ife (c. 1100–1450 CE) was a major political and artistic center. Archaeological work since the 1950s (Frank Willett's excavations especially) has documented an urban site with elaborate stone-pavement plazas, palace structures, and the famous Ife bronze and terracotta heads — naturalistic life-size portrait heads in cast brass and modeled terracotta, of an artistic sophistication that astonished European art historians when they first became known to the West in 1910 (Frobenius's expedition).

The Ife bronzes / brasses are among the most famous sculpture of medieval Africa, the technical equal of contemporaneous Renaissance bronze-casting. Their existence was, for some early 20th-c. European observers, so theologically problematic to colonial-racial assumptions that Frobenius proposed Atlantean origin — an early example of the racial-pseudoarchaeology pattern documented by Chidester (1996 *Savage Systems*).

The Ife political-religious complex declined c. 1450 with the rise of nearby Oyo Empire; Ile-Ife remained the religious-cosmogonic center even as political power shifted to Oyo and other Yoruba states.

## MASSIVE WIN — Cosmogonic-origin-place archetype
Ile-Ife belongs to the cross-tradition archetype of the COSMOGONIC ORIGIN PLACE — the specific physical site identified with the moment of creation:

| Place | Tradition | Cosmogonic role |
|---|---|---|
| Ile-Ife | Yoruba | Site of first dry land; descent of orishas |
| Jerusalem (Foundation Stone) | Judaism, Islam | Site from which creation spread; *Even ha-Shtiya* |
| Mecca (Kaaba site, Zamzam) | Islam | Pre-Adamic Kaaba; Hajar's well; Adam's first prayer |
| Heliopolis / Iunu | Egyptian | Site of the Benben — primordial mound at creation |
| Eridu | Sumerian | Sacred-city of Enki; first city founded by gods |
| Mount Meru | Hindu, Buddhist | Cosmic-axis mountain (more mythological than place-specific) |
| Cuzco | Inca | Cosmic-navel of the empire |
| Tenochtitlan | Aztec | Founded at the place of Huitzilopochtli's sign |
| Delphi (omphalos) | Greek | Navel of the world per Zeus's two-eagles myth |

The cosmogonic-origin-place archetype is a documented cross-tradition pattern (Eliade 1958; J.Z. Smith *To Take Place* 1987 critiqued the archetype's universalization). Ile-Ife is the Yoruba/diaspora instance. The Atlantic-diaspora dimension is important: enslaved Yoruba in Cuba, Brazil, and Haiti maintained Ile-Ife as their cosmogonic point-of-origin reference even though they were displaced from it physically — making the diaspora-religion structure preserve the origin-place as memory and longing.

## Modern status
Ile-Ife is a city of ~500,000 in Osun State; the *Ooni* (king) of Ife continues as the spiritual head of Yoruba religion in Nigeria. Obafemi Awolowo University (formerly University of Ife) sits in the city. The royal palace preserves religious-political significance; the classical Ife archaeological zone includes preserved bronze-casting workshops, palace foundations, and the museum of Ife antiquities.
