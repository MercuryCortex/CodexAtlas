# Yazd Atash Behram

The **Yazd Atash Behram** is the **only Atash Behram of the highest sacred grade ("Behram" = "victorious") continuously burning in Iran** — a Zoroastrian sacred fire whose unbroken liturgical lineage extends from its initial consecration at **Pars-Karyan in the Sasanian heartland c. 470 CE** through multiple relocations to its present location in **Yazd**, where it has been continuously housed since **1474 CE**. It is one of only **9 Atash Behrams in the world** — 8 in India + this one in Iran.

## The continuous lineage

The lineage of the Yazd Atash Behram is one of the cleanest documented cases of an **unbroken liturgical-tradition lineage across a 1500+ year span**:

1. **Initial consecration** at Pars-Karyan (Sasanian Larestan) c. 470 CE.
2. **Transfer to Aqda** (~12th c.) following the Arab conquest of Iran (-651) + the gradual Islamicization of the surrounding region.
3. **Transfer to Ardakan** (~14th c.) for further preservation.
4. **Transfer to Yazd** (1474 CE; permanent relocation).
5. **Reconstruction of present temple** (1934, funded by Indian-Parsi philanthropist Homusji Khurshedji Mavalankar).

The fire has been **continuously fed by ordained priests in 4-hour rotations** for ~1550 years. The Arab conquest, Mongol invasion, Safavid Shi'ite consolidation, Qajar dynasty, Pahlavi period, and Islamic Republic have all passed; the fire continues to burn.

## The Atash Behram grade

Zoroastrian sacred fires exist in **three grades**:

1. **Atash Dadgah** — the lowest grade; a domestic-or-community fire that can be consecrated by a single priest.
2. **Atash Adaran** — the middle grade; requires fires from four occupational sources fused in the consecration.
3. **Atash Behram** — the highest grade; requires **16 different fires** (from different occupational + cosmic sources, including a fire kindled by lightning) fused through an elaborate consecration ritual lasting up to a year. The Atash Behram is the materially-most-sacred form of [[atar]]'s manifestation.

## MASSIVE-WIN: religious-persistence under conquest

The Yazd region preserved **one of the largest continuous Zoroastrian populations through the Arab + Mongol + Safavid + Qajar phases** of Iranian-Islamic history. Yazd remained a majority-Zoroastrian city through the 9th-10th c. + retained substantial Zoroastrian population through the modern era. The Yazd Atash Behram is the **principal liturgical anchor of this religious-persistence-under-conquest** — one of the cleanest documented cases of a **non-Abrahamic minority-tradition surviving 1300+ years of Islamic majority-rule with continuous liturgical lineage**.

(Choksy 1997 *Conflict and Cooperation*; Hinnells 2005 *Zoroastrian Diaspora*.)

## Connection to the Parsi diaspora

The 9 world Atash Behrams are linked by the **post-Sasanian Zoroastrian diaspora** that fled Iran after the Arab conquest (~7th-10th c.) for Gujarat. The **Iranshah Atash Behram in Udvada, Gujarat** (consecrated 1742 from older fires going back to the 8th-c. diaspora-flight) is the principal Indian-Parsi sacred fire; the Yazd Atash Behram is its **Iranian-counterpart-and-source-tradition**. The two represent the two surviving branches of post-Sasanian Zoroastrianism — Iranian-Zoroastrian (Yazd) + Parsi-Indian (Udvada).
