# Saqqara

The Old Kingdom royal necropolis of [[place-memphis-egypt|Memphis]] — a desert plateau on the west bank of the Nile, ~30 km south of modern Cairo. Saqqara is one of the most theologically loaded archaeological sites in human history: the **origin point** of monumental stone architecture, the **origin point** of the world's oldest substantial religious-text corpus, and the **origin point** of the Hellenistic Serapis cult. All three world-historical origins are anchored to this single site.

## The Step Pyramid — origin of pyramid evolution

[[djoser|Djoser]]'s Step Pyramid (~ -2650), designed by [[imhotep|Imhotep]], is the world's first monumental stone-cut architectural complex. Before Djoser, Egyptian royal tombs were mud-brick rectangular *mastabas*. The Step Pyramid is the first time the *mastaba* form is stacked vertically and translated into limestone — six superimposed *mastabas* of decreasing size, forming a stairway-shaped tomb-mountain. The entire surrounding complex (heb-sed court, mortuary temple, serdab, southern tomb) is also stone-cut and represents the canonical *birth of stone architecture*.

The Step Pyramid is the **morphological origin** of the pyramid form. Every subsequent Egyptian pyramid — Snefru's Meidum, Bent, and Red pyramids; [[khufu|Khufu]]'s Great Pyramid at Giza; Khafre's and Menkaure's pyramids; the 5th- and 6th-Dynasty pyramids with the Pyramid Texts inscribed inside; the Middle-Kingdom pyramids at Lisht, Dahshur, and Hawara — is a successor-form to the Saqqara prototype.

## The Pyramid Texts — origin of religious literature

Beginning with the pyramid of Unas (~ -2350, last king of the 5th Dynasty), Saqqara pyramids have their interior chamber walls inscribed with vertical columns of religious spells: the **Pyramid Texts**. These are the world's oldest substantial religious literature surviving in original form — older than the Hebrew Bible, the Vedas, the Mesopotamian temple hymns of Enheduanna, and the Homeric epics by ~1,000-2,000 years.

Allen 2005 demonstrates the textual continuity from the Saqqara Pyramid Texts (~ -2400 to -2200) through the Coffin Texts (Middle Kingdom, ~ -2000) and the Book of the Dead (New Kingdom, ~ -1550 onward) — a **2,500-year unbroken textual tradition** of Egyptian funerary spells, all originating in the Saqqara pyramid interiors of the late Old Kingdom. The Pyramid Texts are also the principal source for early Egyptian theology of the [[heliopolis-ennead|Heliopolitan Ennead]] and for the cosmological structure that shapes all subsequent Egyptian religion.

## Late-Period animal mummification

From the Saite period (~ -650) through the Ptolemaic and Roman periods (~ +200), Saqqara becomes a vast complex of animal-mummification catacombs. The **Serapeum** is the burial place of the sacred Apis bulls (one bull per generation, embalmed and interred in massive granite sarcophagi); the **Anubieion** contains millions of canine mummies; the **Bubasteion** holds cat mummies; other galleries are dedicated to ibis, baboon, and falcon mummies. The animal-cult industry at Saqqara was a major Late-Period economic and theological enterprise, supplying votive mummies to pilgrims who would present them at the corresponding deity's temple.

## Origin of the Serapis cult

The Saqqara Serapeum is the **theological origin point** of the [[serapis|Serapis]] cult. Ptolemy I Soter (reigned -305 to -282) commissioned a new syncretic god combining the Saqqara Osiris-Apis (Osor-Hapi → Serapis) with Greek Zeus/Hades iconography, designed as a uniting cult for the Greek and Egyptian populations of Ptolemaic Egypt. The cult's *theological* origin is anchored to the Saqqara Apis-bull catacombs, even though its principal *institutional* site became the great Serapeion of Alexandria. The Serapis cult dispersed throughout the Mediterranean — Athens, Delos, Rome, Pompeii, even Roman Britain — and was the principal vehicle of Egyptian religion's Greco-Roman expansion for ~700 years.

## Methodological note — site as theological origin

Saqqara is the single best documented example of a place that is *the origin* of multiple distinct world-historical religious phenomena. Most sites have *one* world-historical origin (Jerusalem for Christianity; Mecca for Islam; Bodh Gaya for Buddhism). Saqqara has at least three: the pyramid form, the religious-text tradition, and the Hellenistic syncretic-deity tradition. The site's geographic continuity (it has been an active sacred-burial precinct for over 3,000 years) is structurally unusual and is a function of its proximity to Memphis, the Old-Kingdom capital and the principal cult centre of [[ptah]].
