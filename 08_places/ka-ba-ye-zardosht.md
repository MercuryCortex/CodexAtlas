# Ka'ba-ye Zardosht (Cube of Zoroaster)

The **Ka'ba-ye Zardosht** ("Cube of Zoroaster") is a tower-like stone structure of the
Achaemenid period standing at the foot of the cliff at [[naqsh-e-rustam-place]], opposite the
rock-cut royal tombs. Its original function is disputed — a royal-Zoroastrian fire-tower, a
treasury, or a dynastic archive have all been proposed — but its historical importance rests
overwhelmingly on the **Sasanian-period inscriptions carved on its walls** in the 3rd century CE.

## Why it matters — the primary documents of Sasanian state religion

- **The ŠKZ** — the *res gestae* of **Šābuhr I** (the "Šābuhr Ka'ba-ye Zardosht"
  inscription), a trilingual (Middle Persian, Parthian, Greek) royal record of the Sasanian
  king's victories over Rome and the organization of his empire.
- **The Kartīr inscriptions** — the autobiographical inscriptions of the mowbed (high priest)
  **Kartīr**, the single most important primary source for 3rd-century Sasanian Zoroastrian
  state-orthodoxy and its programme against Manichaeans, Christians, Jews, and Buddhists.

The monument thus belongs to the cross-tradition **[[divine-kingship]]** template that
Naqsh-e Rustam monumentalizes — the king as the cosmic pivot between the divine and human
orders, an iconographic programme that Canepa (2009) traces forward into Byzantine, Umayyad,
and Mughal ruler-imagery. See [[naqsh-e-rustam-place]] for the wider site.
