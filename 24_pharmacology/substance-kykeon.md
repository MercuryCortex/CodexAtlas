# Kykeon

The barley-water-pennyroyal ritual drink consumed at the climax of the Eleusinian Mysteries. The textually attested recipe is recorded in the Homeric Hymn to Demeter: when the goddess Demeter, exhausted from her search for Persephone, refuses wine in the house of Metanira, she asks instead for *kykeon* — barley and water with pennyroyal. The ritual repetition of this preparation is the most prestigious moment of one of the most prestigious ritual sequences of the ancient Mediterranean world.

## The mystery and the suppression

The Eleusinian Mysteries ran continuously for some eighteen centuries — from the late Bronze Age (Mylonas's c. -1450) until Theodosius I's anti-pagan edict in 392 CE and the Visigothic sack of 396 CE. Initiates included Plato, Sophocles, Cicero, Marcus Aurelius. The secret of what occurred at the *telestērion* on the night of vision was protected by the death penalty; classical sources hint, never describe. Two textually-confirmed elements are repeated: the drinking of the kykeon, and the showing-forth (*epoptai*) of objects whose identity remains contested (a cut barley stalk? a phallus? a relic?).

## The pharmacology controversy

Wasson, Hofmann (the LSD chemist), and the classicist Carl Ruck proposed in 1978 that the kykeon contained ergot — *Claviceps purpurea*, the rust-fungus on barley that produces lysergic-acid amides. The argument: (a) ergot is endemic to Mediterranean barley; (b) water extraction would dissolve the psychoactive lysergic alkaloids while leaving the dangerous peptide alkaloids in the marc; (c) the Greek descriptions of the *epopteia* match a visionary state. The hypothesis is chemically defensible and remains the dominant non-classicist explanation, but the textual record is silent and most classicists treat it as unproven. The same hypothesis has been extended (in less rigorous form) to other mystery cults — see Muraresku 2020.

## What the texts allow

Whatever the pharmacology, the structural pattern of the kykeon is clear: a *prepared* ritual substance (not raw plant, not natural beverage); a *named recipe* attached to a foundational mythological scene; a *boundary-marking* function — drinking the kykeon is what defines crossing from the profane crowd into the mystai. This three-element structure — prepared substance + foundational myth + initiatory threshold — recurs across multiple traditions: [[substance-soma]], [[substance-haoma]], [[substance-ayahuasca]] (with its founding plant-teacher myths), the [[ritual-eucharist-communion|Eucharistic bread + wine]].

## Cross-tradition connections

- **[[substance-soma]] / [[substance-haoma]]**: the Indo-Iranian parallel — also a *prepared* substance, also a *deified* substance, also of contested botanical identity.
