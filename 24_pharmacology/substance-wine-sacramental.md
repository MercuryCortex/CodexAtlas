# Sacramental Wine

Wine is the most *domesticated* of the sacred psychoactives — the one intoxicant that major traditions took into the center of their liturgy rather than to the margins. This node holds the three registers in which wine is sacred, and the abstinence tradition that refuses it.

## Three registers

1. **Literal sacrament.** The Christian **Eucharist** — wine becomes (Catholic/Orthodox) or represents (most Protestant) the blood of Christ, "the fruit of the vine" of the Last Supper. Jewish **kiddush** and the four cups of the Passover seder sanctify time and memory over wine; the Temple **libation** (*nesekh*) poured wine at the altar.
2. **Ecstatic god-intoxication.** Greek **Dionysian** cult ([[dionysus]]) — wine as the god's own presence, drunk into possession (*enthousiasmós*); the Vedic **surā**; the symposium libation.
3. **Metaphorical love-wine.** Sufi and bhakti poetry — the **sharāb** / *may-e ʿishq*, the "wine of divine love" of Ḥāfiẓ, Rūmī, and Khayyām — intoxication as annihilation of the self in God, deployed precisely *against* the literal prohibition of **khamr** in Islam.

## The abstinence counter-tradition

The same substance is refused as strongly as it is embraced: Islamic prohibition of *khamr*, the Nazirite vow, Buddhist and some Christian temperance. Wine's sacredness and its danger are the same fact seen from two sides — which is exactly why the mystical poets could weaponize the forbidden cup as their central image.
