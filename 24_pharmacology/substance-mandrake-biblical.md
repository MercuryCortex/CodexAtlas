# The Mandrake

The **love-and-death root** of the Near East and Europe — a fertility charm in Genesis, a shrieking sorcerer's root in medieval folklore, and pharmacologically a deliriant nightshade.

## Two lives of one root

- **The biblical fertility-plant.** In [[document-genesis]] (30:14-16) Reuben finds **dudaim** — conventionally mandrake — in the field; Rachel trades a night with Jacob for them, hoping for children. The same plant returns as an erotic scent in Song of Songs 7:13. Here mandrake is love-and-fertility magic.
- **The magical root of Europe.** In classical and medieval lore the anthropomorphic mandrake root **shrieks when pulled** and kills the puller (hence the ritual of loosing a dog to draw it up); it belongs to sorcery, to the witches' ointment, and — in the Arabic world — is the *tuffāḥ al-jinn*, "apple of the jinn."

## The pharmacology

Mandrake is a **tropane nightshade** — scopolamine, hyoscyamine, atropine — the same deliriant chemistry as [[substance-henbane-witches]] and [[substance-datura-nightshade]]. Its place in the [[substance-entheogen-hypothesis]] is as fertility-and-sorcery magic, not lucid vision — and its mythic aura vastly exceeds any pleasant effect.
