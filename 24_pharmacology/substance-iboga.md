# Iboga (Tabernanthe iboga)

The root bark of *Tabernanthe iboga*, an understory shrub of the equatorial African rain forest, contains ibogaine and a complex of related indole alkaloids that together produce one of the longest entheogenic states known to ethnopharmacology — typically 24-36 hours of intense oneiric and life-review experience.

## Bwiti

The principal continuous-use context is the syncretic Bwiti religion of the Fang and Mitsogho peoples of Gabon, which crystallized in the late 19th to early 20th century from earlier Pygmy/Babongo ancestral substrate combined with elements of Catholic mission Christianity and Fang ancestor cult. The defining ritual is the *initiation* — an all-night ceremony in which the novice consumes massive doses of iboga root bark, experiences an extended visionary state interpreted as ancestor encounter and review of personal life and lineage history, and emerges as a member of the Bwiti community. Music (the *moungongo* harp, the chant cycles) and the *banzie* (the initiated dancers) frame the ritual.

## Ibogaine in pharmacology

Western pharmaceutical interest concentrated on the ibogaine alkaloid after 1960s-90s reports that a single dose can interrupt opiate (and to a lesser extent cocaine and alcohol) dependency in ways that resist standard receptor-pharmacology explanation. Ibogaine is illegal in the US (Schedule I) but legal or unscheduled in Mexico, Brazil, the Netherlands, New Zealand, South Africa, and others; an addiction-treatment subculture has grown around clinics in these jurisdictions. Cardiotoxicity (QT prolongation) is the principal medical concern.
