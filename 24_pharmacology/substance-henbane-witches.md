# Henbane + the Witches' Nightshades

The **deliriant behind the witch-flight** — the Old-World tropane nightshades whose transdermal delirium is the leading naturalistic account of the witches'-sabbath.

## The flying ointment

Early-modern witch-trial records describe a **salve** the accused rubbed on the body (or a staff) before "flying" to the sabbath. Its plausible pharmacological core is the **tropane nightshade complex** — **henbane** (*Hyoscyamus niger*), belladonna, mandrake, and datura — whose alkaloids (scopolamine, hyoscyamine, atropine) are **absorbed through the skin** and produce vivid delirium including the sensation of **flight**. Michael Harner (1973) made this the classic thesis: the flight was chemically real, the sabbath a shared delirium.

## Deeper roots

- **Germanic Bilsenkraut** — henbane in folk-magic and (by a contested etymology) linked to beer-brewing and place-names; a god's-plant in some readings.
- **Classical fumigation** — henbane is among the plants proposed for ancient oracular/fumigatory rites (a T3 claim; e.g. debates over the Delphic vapours reach for such candidates).

## What's secure and what's reconstructed

The **pharmacology is certain** — these are dangerous deliriants that can induce the sensation of flight. What is *reconstructed* is the exact recipe-and-rite, drawn largely from hostile trial testimony. So the node holds Harner's thesis as **strong-but-partly-inferred** — the [[substance-entheogen-hypothesis]] applied, honestly, to European witchcraft.
