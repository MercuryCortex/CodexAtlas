# Sacred Cannabis

Cannabis crosses more traditions as a sacred plant than almost any other, from a documented Iron-Age steppe rite to a living Hindu sacrament to a 20th-century new religion.

## The core cases

- **The Scythian rite.** [[herodotus]] (*Histories* 4.75) describes the Scythians vaporizing hemp-seed on hot stones in a closed tent, "howling with joy" — a funerary purification. Archaeologically confirmed by the **Pazyryk** frozen tombs (Rudenko 1970) and, chemically, by the **Jirzankal** braziers of the Pamirs (Ren et al. 2019, c. -500).
- **Hindu Shaivism.** **Bhāṅg** (cannabis ground into a milk-and-spice drink) and **gāñjā** are bound to [[shiva]] — the Mahāśivarātri and Holi drink, the ascetic's chillum; cannabis is "Shiva's plant." The Atharvaveda lists it among five sacred plants.
- **Rastafari.** Ganja as sacrament and "the wisdom weed" ([[tradition-rastafari]]) — a modern sacralization citing biblical warrant.

## The contested case

The Israelite **qĕneh-bośem** ("kaneh-bosm") in the holy anointing oil (Exodus 30:23) has been read as cannabis since Sula Benet (1936/1967); most translators render it *calamus/sweet cane*. Flagged here as a **T3 debate**, not a settled identification.
