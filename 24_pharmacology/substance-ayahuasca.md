# Ayahuasca

A preparation of two Amazonian plants — the vine *Banisteriopsis caapi* and a DMT-bearing leaf (typically *Psychotria viridis*, "chacruna") — boiled together into a brown decoction. The combination is pharmacologically extraordinary: the harmine and harmaline alkaloids in the vine inhibit monoamine oxidase, allowing the otherwise rapidly metabolized N,N-DMT in the leaf to reach the brain through oral ingestion. Neither component is psychoactive when consumed alone.

## The botanical-ritual problem

The two-plant combination is one of the most cited examples of indigenous pharmacological knowledge whose discovery resists ordinary explanation. Of the ~80,000 species in the Amazonian flora, fewer than a handful contain MAOI alkaloids in significant doses, and fewer than a handful contain DMT in sufficient concentration; the chance of trial-and-error pairing is extremely low. Indigenous explanations attribute the discovery to revelation from the plants themselves through dreams — the *plant-teacher* (planta maestra) model documented across dozens of Amazonian peoples.

## Traditions of use

- **Indigenous Amazonian**: hundreds of named cultural variants across Shipibo-Conibo, Asháninka, Cofán, Shuar, Tukano, and many other groups. Function ranges from medical diagnosis to hunting divination to inter-village warfare-counsel to initiatory training of shamans (*ayahuasqueros*, *vegetalistas*, *curanderos*).
- **Brazilian syncretic churches**: [[tradition-santo-daime]] (founded by Raimundo Irineu Serra in Rio Branco, c. 1930) and [[tradition-uniao-do-vegetal]] (founded by José Gabriel da Costa, 1961) fused indigenous ayahuasca with Catholic devotion, Kardecist spiritism, and Afro-Brazilian elements. Both are now legally protected religious uses in Brazil and (since the 2006 *Gonzales v. UDV* US Supreme Court decision) in the United States.
- **Mestizo vegetalismo**: the urban-mestizo ayahuasquero tradition documented by Beyer (2009) — a 20th-century hybrid drawing on indigenous practice + Catholic + spiritist elements.

## The icaros

In Shipibo and mestizo practice the song (*icaro*) is the operative ritual technology — the substance is the vehicle, but the icaro is what *moves* the experience. Each icaro is learned from a specific plant during apprenticeship-dieting (the *dieta*: weeks or months of isolated diet with a single plant). The Shipibo geometric textile patterns (kené) are described as visualized icaros made visible — a sound-pattern that becomes a visual-pattern that becomes woven cloth.

## Cross-tradition connections

- **[[substance-soma]] / [[substance-haoma]]**: ayahuasca shares the structural pattern of a *prepared* sacred substance — neither raw plant nor natural state, but a worked preparation requiring ritual knowledge to construct. Cf. also the Eleusinian [[substance-kykeon]].
- **[[substance-fly-agaric]]**: a different Siberian-shamanic entheogen with comparable cosmological function (vertical-axis travel, encounter with non-human teachers).
- **[[theme-plant-teacher]]**: the indigenous insistence that the plants themselves teach is one of the strongest cross-cultural ethnobotanical patterns — paralleled in Mazatec *santa María* (Salvia divinorum), Huichol peyote, and other entheogenic traditions.
