# Soma (the substance)

The pressed sacred drink at the center of Vedic ritual + theology, and one of the central unresolved botanical-historical questions of the study of religion. Soma is *both* a substance + a deity ([[soma|see deity node]]) in Vedic theology — the substance *is* the god in pressed-and-filtered form; the Vedic priests who press and consume soma are described as participating in the same divine substance that the gods drink in heaven.

## The ritual

The Vedic soma-yajña — described in technical detail in the Brāhmaṇa-prose corpus ([[phase-2-006-brahmanas-aranyakas]]) — involves:

1. **Procurement** — soma plants brought from a distant region (the texts mention mountain origins, sometimes associated with Mount Mūjavant, possibly the Pamirs/Hindu Kush region — see Witzel 2003 for the linguistic-geographic argument).
2. **Pressing** — the plant pressed between stones with a rhythmic + verbally-accompanied procedure (Rigveda 9 is largely a corpus of pressing-hymns).
3. **Filtering** — through wool filters; the *Pavamāna* ("purifying") form is the soma flowing through the filter, central to Maṇḍala 9.
4. **Mixing** — with milk + other substances.
5. **Libation + consumption** — offered to the gods (especially [[indra]], who is described as needing soma to muster the strength to slay [[vritra]]) + consumed by the participating priests.

## The botanical question

The question "what plant is soma" has been actively debated since the 19th century, with no scholarly consensus. The principal candidates:

| Candidate | Pharmacology | Principal advocate | Scholarly tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ephedra** (multiple spp.) | Ephedrine alkaloids (stimulant) | Falk 1989 | T1 — current institutional standard |
| **Amanita muscaria** (fly agaric) | Muscimol (psychoactive) | Wasson 1968 | T3 — historically influential, not now dominant |
| **Peganum harmala** (Syrian rue) | Harmine + harmaline (MAOI) | Flattery & Schwartz 1989 | T2 — academic-minority |
| **Cannabis sativa** | THC | various | T3 |

Falk's Ephedra position is the current institutional standard — it matches the linguistic + geographic + Avestan-haoma evidence best — but the question is not closed.

## The Indo-Iranian inheritance

The substance + the practice are inherited from the Proto-Indo-Iranian period ([[language-proto-indo-iranian]]). Sanskrit *soma* + Avestan *haoma* derive from the same root *sauma-* via regular sound change. The two cognate substances + their cognate rituals (Vedic soma-yajña + Zoroastrian haoma-rite, see [[substance-haoma]]) are one of the most carefully-reconstructed Indo-Iranian religious inheritances. The botanical identity question applies to both: whatever soma was, haoma was the same.
