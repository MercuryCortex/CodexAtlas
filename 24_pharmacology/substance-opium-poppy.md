# The Opium Poppy

The sacred narcotic of the ancient Mediterranean — the plant not of *vision* but of **sleep, oblivion, and death**, and so bound to the underworld and the mysteries of return.

## The ancient record

- **Sumer.** The poppy is written **ḫul gil**, conventionally read "joy plant," among the earliest possible textual references (Kritikos & Papadaki 1967).
- **Minoan Crete.** The **"Poppy Goddess"** of Gazi (c. -1300) — a terracotta figure crowned with incised poppy-capsules, read by some as a narcotic-cult image (a T3 claim).
- **Greece.** Poppies belong to [[demeter]] (Eleusis) and to [[hypnos]] and Thanatos (Sleep and Death); the Homeric **nēpenthes**, the drug "banishing grief," is often identified with opium.

## A narcotic, not a visionary

The poppy matters to the [[substance-entheogen-hypothesis]] precisely as a *counter-example*: its gift is anodyne oblivion, not vision. It marks one pole of the sacred-psychoactive spectrum (sleep/death) against the visionary pole of the psychedelics — a reminder that "sacred plant" is not a synonym for "hallucinogen."
