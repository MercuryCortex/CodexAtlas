# San Pedro / Huachuma

The **mescaline cactus of the Andes** — a visionary sacrament in ritual use for three thousand years, and the South-American twin of the Mexican peyote.

## The record

- **Chavín de Huántar.** At the great early cult centre ([[chavin-de-huantar]], c. -1200 to -500), a fanged supernatural is carved holding a columnar **San-Pedro** cactus; the labyrinthine galleries and roaring water-channels read as a setting for a visionary rite (Burger 1992).
- **Living curanderismo.** In the far north of Peru the *curandero* still brews huachuma for the all-night **mesa/mesada** healing-rite (Sharon 1978).

## The peyote parallel

San Pedro and [[substance-peyote]] are the two great **mescaline cactus sacraments** of the New World — Andean and Mexican, independent in tradition, identical in active principle. Together they mark the *visionary* Andean pole against the *stimulant* pole of [[substance-coca-andean]].
