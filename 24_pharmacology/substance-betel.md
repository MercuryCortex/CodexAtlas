# Betel quid

A composite stimulant-quid — areca nut (sliced or grated), betel leaf (the wrap), slaked lime (the activator) — chewed for several thousand years across South, Southeast, and Western Pacific Asia. The hospitality offering, the marriage rite, the temple offering, the diplomatic-exchange gift: across a region of roughly 600 million current consumers and at least four millennia of continuity, the *tāmbūla* / *pan* / *sirih* quid is one of the most pervasive ritual-and-social plant preparations in human history.

## Antiquity and distribution

Direct archaeological evidence of betel staining on human teeth: Spirit Cave (Thailand) c. -7600 BP; Vietnam Man Bac c. -2700; widespread early-Holocene to mid-Holocene across South and Southeast Asia. The distribution maps onto the natural ranges of *Areca catechu* (Wallace's Line tropical Asia/Oceania) and *Piper betle*, with cultural diffusion into adjacent regions (early-modern Indian Ocean trade carried betel to Madagascar and East African coastal markets).

## Ritual structure

- **South Asia**: *tāmbūla* offering is named in Vedic, Tantric, and Bhakti contexts; the betel-areca-spice preparation is a standard *upachara* (devotional service item) in Hindu temple ritual; auspicious presence at weddings, births, and naming ceremonies.
- **Southeast Asia**: the *sirih pinang* tray is the canonical ceremonial gift across Malay/Indonesian polities — opening adat (customary) negotiation, marking marriage proposal, sealing alliance.
- **Pacific Melanesia**: betel is the principal social-bonding substance across coastal New Guinea, the Solomons, and Vanuatu — overlapping into the [[substance-kava]] zone but with distinct social registers.

## Cross-tradition connections

- **[[substance-kava]]**: structural-functional parallel as the Pacific's other major ceremonial plant; non-overlapping principal ranges but contiguous.
- **[[substance-tobacco-amerindian]]**: comparative axis as continuously-shared stimulant-ritual substance with deep antiquity.
