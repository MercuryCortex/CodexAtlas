# Haoma (the substance)

The pressed sacred drink at the center of Zoroastrian Yasna ritual + theology, cognate with Vedic [[substance-soma|Soma]] and inherited from the common Indo-Iranian period (see [[language-proto-indo-iranian]]). As with soma, the substance haoma *is* the deity [[haoma-zoroastrian|Haoma]] in pressed form, and the pressing-and-consumption ritual is the central act of the Yasna liturgy.

## The ritual

The Yasna (modern + ancient) centers on the haoma-pressing performed by trained priests in a precisely-prescribed sequence:

1. **Preparation** — twigs of the haoma plant + pomegranate sprigs (added in the post-ancient ritual as a substitute / complement; the *hōm* of modern Parsi liturgy is Ephedra) + consecrated water.
2. **Pressing** — performed in a stone-and-metal apparatus, with precise verbal accompaniment from the Yasna text.
3. **Filtering** — through a hair filter, paralleling the Vedic wool filter.
4. **Consumption** — by the officiating priest, with portions offered to the sacred fire.

## Continuous tradition

What distinguishes haoma from soma in the comparative-religion record is that the haoma tradition has been *continuously practiced* from the Avestan period to the present. The Indian + Iranian Parsi communities have preserved the Yasna ritual + the haoma-pressing across nearly three millennia. This makes the modern *hōm* substance (Ephedra) the strongest single piece of evidence for the historical identity of haoma + soma — Boyce 1975 + Falk 1989 both treat the continuous-tradition evidence as substantially decisive in favor of the Ephedra identification.
