# Salvia divinorum

The **Mazatec visionary mint** — a living divinatory sacrament, Christianized in its very name, and a pharmacological outlier among the sacred psychoactives.

## The sacrament

Among the Mazatec of the Sierra Mazateca (Oaxaca), *ska María Pastora* — "the leaf of Mary the Shepherdess" — is taken as a rolled quid or a crushed-leaf infusion in the nighttime **divinatory-healing rite**, the tradition made famous through the curandera **María Sabina**. It serves as the visionary sacrament **when the sacred mushrooms are out of season** — an explicit ritual substitute for [[substance-psilocybin-mushroom]].

## The Christian name

Like much Mazatec sacred botany, the plant is wrapped in a Marian veil — "María Pastora," the Virgin as Shepherdess — a syncretism that let an indigenous sacrament survive inside a Catholic surface.

## The pharmacological outlier

Salvia's active principle, **salvinorin A**, is a potent **κ-opioid receptor agonist** — the first known non-nitrogenous, non-serotonergic natural psychedelic. It does *not* work on the 5-HT2A system of the classic psychedelics ([[substance-psilocybin-mushroom]], peyote, LSD). It is the vault's proof that [[substance-entheogen-hypothesis]] gathers several *unrelated* neuropharmacologies under one cultural category.
