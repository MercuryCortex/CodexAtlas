# Ololiuqui

The seeds of two morning-glory species — *Turbina corymbosa* (the climbing white-flowered vine of southern Mexico) and *Ipomoea violacea* — used in Aztec and Mazatec ritual practice as a visionary substance. Spanish colonial-era sources (Sahagún, Hernández, Ruiz de Alarcón) describe *ololiuhqui* as a divination medium consulted by priests under controlled circumstances; the Inquisition explicitly prohibited its use alongside [[substance-peyote]] in the 1620 edict. The Mazatec continued the practice into the modern ethnographic period.

The chemical identification in 1960 by Albert Hofmann — the LSD chemist — was historically dramatic: the active compounds in the New World seeds turned out to be structural cousins of the European ergot alkaloids (and of Hofmann's own laboratory LSD). One ergoline-alkaloid family supplied both the New World Aztec sacrament and the European ergot fungus implicated (controversially) in the Eleusinian [[substance-kykeon]].

## Cross-tradition connections

- **[[substance-psilocybin-mushroom]]**: the Mazatec used both ololiuhqui and *teonanácatl* mushrooms; both were Inquisition-suppressed.
- **[[substance-peyote]]**: parallel Mesoamerican entheogen with shared Inquisition history.
- **[[substance-kykeon]]**: structural-pharmacology link via ergot ergoline alkaloids — Wasson's intuition was that the same alkaloid family bridges Eleusis and the Aztec ritual.
