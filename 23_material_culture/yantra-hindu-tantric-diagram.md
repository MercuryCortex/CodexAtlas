# Yantra (Hindu Tantric Diagram)

## Identity

The **yantra** (Sanskrit यन्त्र, "instrument, device, machine") is a geometric sacred diagram used as the central focus of Tantric Hindu worship, meditation, and ritual identification. A yantra encodes a specific deity's emanative-cosmological structure in a geometric form: typically concentric arrangements of interlocking triangles, circles, lotus-petals, squares, and gateways arrayed around a central point (*bindu*) representing the deity's essential nature. The yantra is the **visual + geometric counterpart to the mantra** — where mantra is the deity-in-sound, the yantra is the deity-in-form; the two together (yantra + mantra + mūrti, the iconographic image) constitute the canonical Tantric tripartite cosmology.

The **Śrī Yantra** (श्रीयन्त्र, also Śrī Cakra) is the canonical case — the most-elaborated yantra in the entire tradition, central to the Śrīvidyā Tantric school. It consists of nine interlocking triangles (four pointing up = Śiva; five pointing down = Śakti) generating 43 sub-triangles arranged in concentric levels (the nine *cakras* or "circles"), enclosed within an eight-petaled and sixteen-petaled lotus, framed by three concentric circles, and contained within a square earth-citadel with four gateways. At the absolute center, the *bindu* point — the seed of the cosmos and the essential identity of the Goddess.

## History + canonical structure

The yantra-form crystallizes in the post-Gupta Tantric synthesis (6th-9th c. CE) alongside the broader development of Hindu Tantric ritual, mantra-doctrine, and the systematic deity-emanation theology of the Śaiva + Śākta + Vaiṣṇava Āgamas. The earliest detailed textual treatment is the *Saundarya Laharī* (8th c., attributed to Śaṅkara), which describes the construction of Śrī Yantra (v. 11) and the meditative method of mounting the yantra in identification-meditation. By the medieval period (10th-13th c.) — the great age of Indic Tantric synthesis — the major yantras of the principal deities are fixed:

- **Śrī Yantra** — the Devī / Tripura-sundarī yantra (Śrīvidyā tradition)
- **Kālī Yantra** — fierce-Goddess yantra (Kālīkula tradition)
- **Bagalāmukhī Yantra** — one of the ten Mahāvidyās' yantras
- **Sudarśana Yantra** — Vaiṣṇava yantra of Viṣṇu's discus
- **Mahā-Mṛtyuñjaya Yantra** — Śaiva yantra of death-conquering Rudra-Śiva
- **Gaṇeśa Yantra** — obstacle-removing deity-form

## Cross-tradition: yantra–mandala kinship (MASSIVE-WIN)

The Hindu yantra and Buddhist [[mandala-buddhist-cosmic-diagram]] are sister-forms emerging from the same post-Gupta Tantric Indic matrix. Both deploy concentric geometric structures to map deity-emanation onto a meditative-cosmic diagram. The two together represent the cleanest case of shared cross-tradition tantric sacred-geometry — emerging from the same religious milieu, employing the same geometric vocabulary, structurally distinguished primarily by the specific deity-pantheon mapped (Hindu Devī/Śiva-Śakti vs Buddhist vajradhātu/garbhadhātu Buddhas).
