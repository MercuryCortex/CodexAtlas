# Reliquary — Medieval Christian Relic-Container

The decorative container designed to hold + display + transport the physical remains (bone, blood, hair, vestment) of a saint or fragments of the True Cross / other relics of Christ + the apostles. Reliquaries developed from the late-Roman *capsa* (4th c.) into elaborately gem-ornamented gold-and-silver shrines through the Carolingian + Romanesque + Gothic periods. Standard forms: arm-reliquary, bust-reliquary, monstrance-reliquary, châsse (house-shaped), feretrum (portable shrine). Theological grounding in the Catholic + Orthodox doctrine that the bodies of saints retain divine grace (per 2 Kings 13:21 + Acts 19:11-12) and thus warrant veneration + pilgrimage. Comparable to (and one of the principal Western analogues of) the Buddhist [[theme-stupa-as-cosmic-mountain|stūpa]] relic-mound tradition.
