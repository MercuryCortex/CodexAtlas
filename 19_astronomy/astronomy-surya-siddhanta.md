# Sūrya Siddhānta

One of the principal Siddhāntic astronomical treatises of post-Hellenistic-transmission Indian astronomy. The text is *pseudepigraphic* — framed as Sūrya (the Sun) instructing the asura Maya — and is *composite*, with strata extending from roughly the 4th-6th c. CE down to the redaction we have in preserved MSS (c. 7th-10th c.). It is one of the Pañcasiddhāntikā ("Five Siddhāntas") epitomized by Varāhamihira in the 6th century, establishing a *post-quem* in the 4th-6th centuries.

## Key technical content

- **Sidereal year length** computed to 365 d 6 h 12 m 36.56 s (modern value: ~365 d 6 h 9 m 9.5 s — Sūrya Siddhānta's value is ~3 minutes long, very impressive for the period).
- **Trigonometric tables** — sine (*jyā*) + versine (*utkrama-jyā*) functions in tabular form; geometric derivation in chapter 2.
- **Eclipse computations** — geometric model treating eclipses as shadow phenomena (lunar = Earth's shadow on Moon; solar = Moon's shadow on Earth), with mathematical machinery for predicting timing + duration + magnitude. The *Rāhu* + *Ketu* mythological framework is preserved nominally (Rāhu = ascending lunar node, Ketu = descending node), but the *computation* is geometric not mythological.
- **Precession of the equinoxes** — recognized + computed via the *ayanāṃśa* (precessional offset). Crucially, Indian astronomy maintains a *sidereal* zodiac (fixed against the stars) while Hellenistic Greek astronomy maintains a *tropical* zodiac (fixed against the equinoxes), so the *ayanāṃśa* correction is the offset between the two systems — accumulated since their last alignment.
- **Geocentric epicyclic model** — structurally cognate with the Ptolemaic system, transmitted along with that system through the *Yavanajātaka* (Pingree 1971).

## Position in the transmission chain

The Sūrya Siddhānta + the [[astronomy-aryabhatiya|Āryabhaṭīya]] together represent the two principal Siddhāntic texts of post-Hellenistic-transmission Indian astronomy. Both feed downstream into the Islamic-Golden-Age tradition via al-Khwarizmi's *Zīj al-Sindhind* (early 9th c. CE), which combines Indian + Persian + Hellenistic + early-Islamic astronomical material. From there the line continues via the Maragha school → [[al-tusi-astronomer|al-Tusi]] → Copernicus's lunar model.
