# MUL.APIN

The principal Babylonian astronomical compendium — two tablets in cuneiform containing the systematic astronomical knowledge of Mesopotamian civilization at the end of its first long observational arc. MUL.APIN is the *start* of the cross-tradition astronomical-transmission chain that the Atlas is mapping: Babylonian observations + parameters feed forward into Hellenistic Greek astronomy (Hipparchus + Ptolemy), and from there into Indian + Islamic + European astronomy.

## What it contains

- **A star + constellation catalog** of ~71 named entries organized into three *paths* (paths of Anu / Enlil / Ea — corresponding roughly to equatorial / northern / southern celestial bands)
- **Heliacal risings + settings** of fixed stars + planets, dated against the calendar — the standard ancient observational + practical-calendrical method
- **Planetary periods** (Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Mercury) — substantially correct, derived from centuries of accumulated observation
- **The path of the Moon** — the 18 ecliptic-band constellations the Moon passes through, which becomes the predecessor of the 12-sign zodiac (the Babylonian transformation of "18 lunar stations" → "12 ecliptic divisions" happens c. -500, slightly after MUL.APIN's principal composition)
- **Intercalation rules** for the lunisolar calendar
- **Shadow-length tables + lengths of day + night** across the year
- **Weather omens** — Mesopotamian astronomy + meteorology overlap at the omen-prediction edge

## The Babylonian astronomy → Hellenistic transmission

MUL.APIN sits at the start of the transmission chain. Three forms of evidence link Babylonian astronomical knowledge to Hellenistic Greek astronomy:

1. **Observational records**. Ptolemy's *Almagest* directly quotes Babylonian eclipse observations from the 8th c. BCE onwards as calibration data for the lunar theory (Almagest IV.6 ff.). Without Babylonian observational records, Hipparchus's + Ptolemy's planetary + lunar theories would lack the long baseline of data they require.
2. **Numerical parameters**. The planetary-period values Ptolemy uses match Babylonian "System-A" + "System-B" parameters preserved on Late-Babylonian astronomical tablets — the same values, with the same characteristic rounding errors.
3. **Mathematical conventions**. The Greek (and now universal) division of the circle into 360° + the sexagesimal (base-60) degree-arithmetic conventions are direct Babylonian inheritances — preserved in the Greek tradition even though the Greeks otherwise used decimal arithmetic.
