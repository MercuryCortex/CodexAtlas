# Cheomseongdae

**Cheomseongdae** (첨성대 / 瞻星臺 "Star-Gazing Tower") is the **oldest extant astronomical observatory in East Asia** — built in 647 CE during the reign of Queen Seondeok of Silla (r. 632–647) in the Silla capital of Gyeongju. The structure is a 9.17 m bottle-shaped granite tower of 27 horizontal stone courses with a square top window for sighting, preserved continuously on-site for 1,400 years.

Cheomseongdae is the principal Korean monument of pre-modern East Asian state-astronomy + the earliest of the three great pre-modern Asian state-observatories (Cheomseongdae +647 / [[maragha-observatory|Maragha]] +1259 / [[samarkand-ulugh-beg-observatory|Samarkand]] +1424).

## The structure

- **Granite tower** 9.17 m tall, ~5.17 m diameter at base
- **27 horizontal courses** of cut granite (symbolically reading as Queen Seondeok's status as the 27th monarch of Silla; numerologies vary in interpretation)
- **365 stones** in the structure (echoing the days of the year; not all scholars accept this count as deliberate)
- **Square top window** for sighting
- **Internal cavity** above the entrance window for an observer to climb up
- **Cardinally-oriented base** (the four sides aligned to the compass)

## Function

Cheomseongdae operated within the **East Asian state-astronomy framework** inherited from Tang China — the imperial-cult tradition in which observation of celestial phenomena is integrated into political ideology (the "mandate of heaven" is read through astronomical-portent interpretation). The Silla royal astronomers (*cheonmunbaksa* 天文博士, "astronomical doctors") used the tower for:

- **Calendrical observation** — equinox + solstice timing, lunar-mansion (xiu) observation
- **Portent interpretation** — eclipses, comets, planetary conjunctions read for political-religious significance
- **Royal astrological consultation** — birth-charts + dynastic chronologies

The Silla Kingdom maintained the observatory until the Silla–Goryeo transition c. 935; the structure has been preserved on-site continuously since.
