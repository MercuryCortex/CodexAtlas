# Aryabhata (the astronomer)

The 5th-6th-century-CE Indian mathematician + astronomer whose *Āryabhaṭīya* (499 CE) is the principal early-medieval Indian astronomical treatise — the work that fixes the form of *Siddhāntic* astronomy that becomes the dominant Indian astronomical-mathematical tradition for the next millennium, and that feeds downstream into Islamic + (via the Maragha school) European Renaissance astronomy.

## Why this node

Aryabhata sits at a strategic junction in the cross-tradition astronomical transmission chain:

- **Upstream**: he inherits + extends a tradition that has already absorbed Hellenistic Greek planetary-model machinery (via the *Yavanajātaka*, c. 2nd-3rd c. CE).
- **Within India**: he is the principal alternative + interlocutor to the Sūrya Siddhānta tradition; subsequent Indian astronomers (Bhaskara I, Brahmagupta, Lalla, Vaṭeśvara, Munjala, Bhaskara II) all work in conversation with the Āryabhaṭīya.
- **Downstream**: his work is transmitted into Arabic via [[al-khwarizmi]]'s *Zīj al-Sindhind* (early 9th c. CE), where it joins the Hellenistic-Greek stream feeding the Islamic Golden Age — and from there continues into European astronomy via the Maragha school + [[al-tusi-astronomer|al-Tusi]] (see [[astronomy-zij-i-ilkhani]]) → Copernicus's lunar model.
