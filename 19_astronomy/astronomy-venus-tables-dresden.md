# Venus tables of the Dresden Codex

Pages 24 and 46-50 of the Dresden Codex preserve one of the most sophisticated pre-modern astronomical tables in any tradition: a multi-cycle table for the synodic period of Venus, organized around the planet's heliacal appearances and disappearances, designed to remain accurate across centuries through periodic adjustment.

## The synodic period

Venus's synodic period — the interval between successive same-phase appearances as seen from Earth — averages 583.92 days. The Dresden tables use an integer approximation of 584 days, but with a long-period correction scheme that brings the calendar back into alignment with observation. The principal structural cycle:

- 5 Venus synodic periods = 2920 days = 8 Haab' (the 365-day solar calendar): the *Venus-Sun resonance*.
- 65 Venus synodic periods = 37,960 days = 104 Haab' = 146 Tzolk'in (260-day calendar) = 2 Calendar Rounds: the great commensuration.

Within this great cycle the tables track the four phases of the Venus synodic period (morning star, superior conjunction, evening star, inferior conjunction) and assign specific intervals (236 + 90 + 250 + 8 = 584 days) to each phase. The intervals are not the exact astronomical durations but a deliberate adjustment that produces correct dates of heliacal rising over the cycle.

## Operational adjustment

The most impressive feature of the tables is the explicit *correction* mechanism. The 584-day approximation produces a cumulative error of 0.08 days per Venus cycle, which the cycle is structured to absorb in specific places. The tables thus remain accurate over hundreds of years of operation — a precision matched in the Old World only by the (later) Islamic astronomical zij tradition.

## Theological loading

Venus in Mesoamerican cosmology is not a neutral celestial object. The morning-star heliacal rising is associated with [[quetzalcoatl|Quetzalcóatl / Kukulkán]] (the feathered serpent in his Venus aspect); descents and ascents are loaded with sacrificial and military meaning. The tables identify specific dates as ominous (war-favourable, etc.). The mathematical and astronomical precision is in service of a fully theological ritual calendar.
