# Indian Nakshatras

The **nakshatras** are the 27 (or 28) **lunar mansions** of Indian astronomy — the asterisms along the lunar ecliptic-band through which the Moon passes during its sidereal month of ~27.32 days. The system is attested in late Vedic texts (the *Taittirīya Saṃhitā* and the *Atharva Veda*) and structures the indigenous Indian astronomical-ritual calendar from the Vedic period to the present.

The nakshatras are the **older Indian system** — the 12-sign rāśi zodiac arrives much later (c. -200 to +200 with Hellenistic-Greek transmission, see [[astronomy-babylonian-zodiac]]). Indian astronomical and astrological practice uses **both systems in parallel**: planetary positions are routinely given in both nakshatra-and-pada and rāśi-and-degree.

## The system

- **27 or 28 mansions** — the count varies because Abhijit (the bright star Vega) is sometimes counted as a separate small mansion and sometimes folded in
- **Each mansion is associated with a yoga-tārā (chief star)** — many of these are still-identifiable asterisms (Kṛttikā = the Pleiades, Rohiṇī = Aldebaran in Taurus, Mṛgaśiras = Lambda Orionis, Ārdrā = Betelgeuse, Maghā = Regulus, Hasta = the Cup of Corvus, Citrā = Spica, Svātī = Arcturus, Viśākhā = Alpha Librae, Jyeṣṭhā = Antares, Mūla = Lambda Scorpii, Śravaṇa = Altair, Revatī = Zeta Piscium, etc.)
- **Each mansion has a presiding deity** — e.g. Kṛttikā = Agni, Rohiṇī = Prajāpati, Mṛgaśiras = Soma, Jyeṣṭhā = Indra, Abhijit = Brahmā
- **Each mansion governs ritual + calendrical functions** — auspicious times for ceremonies, marriages, śrāddha rites, fire-altar construction (the agnicayana ritual is timed by the Kṛttikā nakshatra in the *Taittirīya Saṃhitā*)

## The 28-station Path of the Moon convergence

The structural analogues across cultures are striking:

- **Babylon (MUL.APIN, -1000)**: 18-constellation *path of the Moon* (the predecessor of the 12-sign zodiac)
- **India (Vedic, c. -1200 to -800)**: 27/28 nakshatras
- **China (pre-Han, attested by -400)**: 28 [[astronomy-chinese-28-mansions|xiu / lunar lodges]]

The Indian and Chinese 28-station systems share several anchor stars (e.g. Antares = Jyeṣṭhā = Xīn 心 "Heart"). Pingree 1989 argued for a common Mesopotamian origin; Needham + Sun-Kistemaker treat the convergence as independent invention reflecting the natural arithmetic of the 27.32-day sidereal lunar month. Either way, the **three independent lunar-mansion systems** are one of the most striking parallel-form patterns in the history of astronomy.

## The Babylonian/Hellenistic 12-sign overlay

The 12-sign zodiac arrives in India c. -200 to +200 with Hellenistic-Greek transmission (via Indo-Greek and Sasanian intermediation; see Pingree 1963, 1981). Indian astronomy assimilates the rāśi system **alongside** the older nakshatra system rather than replacing it:

- Indian Siddhāntic handbooks (the [[astronomy-aryabhatiya|Āryabhaṭīya]] + the [[astronomy-surya-siddhanta|Sūrya Siddhānta]]) routinely give positions in both frameworks
- Modern Indian astrology uses both — nakshatras govern the lunar-natal ritual framework (the janma-nakshatra); rāśis govern the planetary-natal horoscopic framework
- The Indian terms for the 12 signs are **direct translations of the Babylonian-via-Greek names** — Meṣa = Krios = Aries, Vṛṣabha = Tauros = Taurus, etc.
