# Claudius Ptolemy (as astronomer)

The 2nd-century-CE Hellenistic mathematician + astronomer working in Alexandria, author of the *Almagest* — the canonical astronomical treatise of antiquity that fixed the form of pre-modern Western + Islamic astronomy for the next ~1400 years. This node treats Ptolemy specifically as astronomer; his astrological *Tetrabiblos* belongs in the divination/astrology lens (overlapping practitioner — see the 19_astronomy/ README for the policy on "overlapping practitioners historically").

## Position in the cross-tradition chain

Ptolemy is the most consequential single node in the entire cross-tradition astronomical-transmission chain that the Atlas is mapping. Three downstream destinations:

1. **Islamic Golden Age** — the *Almagest* was translated into Syriac and then Arabic in the early Abbasid period (8th-9th c. CE) as *al-Majisṭī* ("the greatest [composition]" — from the Greek superlative *megístē*; this is where the word "almagest" comes from). It became the foundational astronomical text of Islamic astronomy for the next ~800 years. Every major Islamic-astronomical work (al-Khwarizmi, al-Battani, al-Bīrūnī, al-Tusi, Ibn al-Shatir, Ulugh Beg) presupposes the Almagest framework.

2. **European Latin tradition** — translated from Greek into Latin (by Gerard of Cremona, Toledo, 1175) + later directly from the Greek (1496 Latin edition of Trebizond's translation). It was the canonical European astronomical text from then until Copernicus's reforming *De Revolutionibus* (1543).

3. **Reform → Copernicus** — Copernicus's heliocentric reform is *not* a rejection of Ptolemy but a reformulation: most of the mathematical machinery (epicycles, deferents) is preserved; only the choice of coordinate-system center changes. Specific technical content (the lunar model, the model for Mercury) reaches Copernicus via the Maragha-school intermediate (Ibn al-Shatir, see [[al-tusi-astronomer|al-Tusi]] + [[astronomy-zij-i-ilkhani]]) — documented by Saliba 2007 as one of the cleanest cases of Islamic-Renaissance scientific transmission.
