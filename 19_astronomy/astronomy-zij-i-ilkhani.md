# Zīj-i Īlkhānī

The astronomical handbook produced under [[al-tusi-astronomer|al-Tusi]]'s direction at the Maragha observatory under Mongol-Ilkhanate patronage, completed c. 1272 CE. The Zīj is one of the most sophisticated Islamic astronomical-table works + the principal document of the Maragha-school Ptolemaic-reform program — the technical content that transmits via Renaissance Italy into Copernicus's *De Revolutionibus*.

## What a "zīj" is

A *zīj* (Persian *zīk*, from Pahlavi *zīg*, from Sanskrit *jya* "bow-string" / "chord/sine") is an Islamic astronomical handbook in standardized form: prefatory chronological + calendrical material, then trigonometric tables, then planetary-position tables computed from the underlying geometric models, then astrological derivations + auxiliary tables. The *zīj* genre runs from al-Khwarizmi's *Zīj al-Sindhind* (early 9th c. CE) to Ulugh Beg's *Zīj-i Sulṭānī* (mid-15th c.) + beyond. The *Zīj-i Īlkhānī* is one of the most influential of the genre — copied + commented for the next ~300 years across the Islamic world from India to Ottoman Anatolia.

## Position in the transmission chain

The Zīj-i Īlkhānī is the *technical embodiment* of the cross-tradition astronomical-transmission chain:

- **Inherits:** Ptolemaic geometric framework (Almagest, via 9th-c. Arabic translation); Indian Siddhāntic computational methods (via al-Khwarizmi); earlier Islamic-astronomy refinements (al-Battani, al-Bīrūnī).
- **Reforms:** replaces the Ptolemaic equant with Maragha-school Tūsī-couple constructions (preserving observational fit but restoring uniform-circular-motion).
- **Transmits:** the reformed planetary models reach Copernicus's *De Revolutionibus* (1543) — documented as one of the cleanest Islamic→Renaissance technical transmissions in the history of science (Saliba 2007, Roberts 1957).
