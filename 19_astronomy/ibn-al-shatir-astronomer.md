# Ibn al-Shāṭir

The timekeeper of the Umayyad Mosque in Damascus, and the author of the most thoroughly reformed planetary models produced anywhere before Copernicus. His lunar and Mercury models are mathematically identical to the ones in *De revolutionibus*, published a hundred and seventy years later. Whether that identity is a transmission or a coincidence is the single most argued question in the history of pre-modern astronomy, and this node is written to hold the argument rather than to settle it.

## The job, which explains the work

Ibn al-Shāṭir was a *muwaqqit* — a mosque astronomer whose salaried duty was to determine the five daily prayer times and the direction of Mecca, and to maintain the instruments that did so. He was orphaned young and reportedly trained first in ivory inlay, which is the usual explanation for his exceptional instrument-making; he travelled to Cairo and Alexandria to study astronomy and then spent his career in Damascus.

David King's work established that this profession, not court patronage, is the institutional base of late Islamic astronomy. Maragha had a Mongol khan paying for it; Damascus had a mosque payroll. The mosque outlasted the khanate. The most radical planetary theory of the fourteenth century was produced by a man whose job was telling a city when to pray.

The sundial he built for the northern minaret of the Umayyad Mosque in 1371 was among the finest of the Middle Ages; the surviving original fragments are in Damascus, with a replica in place on the minaret.

## What he actually did

The Maragha programme's target was Ptolemy's **equant** — a device that produces uniform angular motion about a point that is not the centre of the circle it governs, and so violates the principle that heavenly motions are uniform and circular about their own centres. Al-Ṭūsī and his colleagues built replacements out of combinations of uniform circular motions.

Ibn al-Shāṭir went further. His models discard not only the equant but the **eccentric** as well: every deferent is concentric with the Earth, and all the required inequality is produced by additional epicycles turning uniformly. The result is a system in which the offending device is gone entirely rather than replaced by an equivalent.

His **lunar model** is the most important. Ptolemy's lunar theory reproduces the Moon's longitude well but implies that the Moon's distance from Earth varies by nearly a factor of two, which would make its apparent diameter nearly double over the course of a month — something no one has ever seen. Ibn al-Shāṭir's model keeps the longitudes and removes the absurdity. This is not philosophical tidying; it is a fix to an observational failure.

Copernicus's lunar model is the same model.

## The contested question, stated honestly

**What is not in dispute:** the mathematical identity of Ibn al-Shāṭir's lunar and Mercury models with those in *De revolutionibus*. Roberts established it in 1957; Kennedy and Roberts extended it in 1959; Swerdlow and Neugebauer, working from the Copernican side in 1984, confirm it. Copernicus was not the first to build these constructions.

**The case for transmission.** The models are too specific for independent invention; Maragha-school material demonstrably reached Byzantium through named individuals, above all Gregory Chioniades, who studied at Tabriz around 1300 and translated Persian astronomical works into Greek; Byzantine Greek manuscripts containing Maragha-type material reached Italy, where Copernicus studied at Bologna and Padua in the 1490s; and Ragep argues that with this much circumstantial structure in place, the burden has moved to those who deny contact.

**The case against.** No Latin translation of the *Nihāyat al-suʾl* is known. Copernicus names al-Battānī, al-Zarqālī, Thābit and others, but never Ibn al-Shāṭir or al-Ṭūsī. No manuscript has been placed in his hands. Di Bono argued in 1995 that the relevant devices were reachable within the Italian tradition by other routes, and Blåsjö argued in 2014 that the inference from mathematical identity to historical contact has been made too easily.

**How the vault holds it.** The wire to [[copernicus]] is tiered **T2** — a serious, published, non-consensus position with its rebuttal cited alongside — and it should be read against the [[al-battani-astronomer]] wire, which is **T1** because Copernicus names al-Battānī in his own text. Two Islamic-to-Copernicus claims, two different evidentiary situations, two different tiers. That difference is the whole point of the tier system.
