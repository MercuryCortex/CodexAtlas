# Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī

The 13th-century Persian polymath who founded the Maragha observatory under Mongol-Ilkhanate patronage + author of the *Zīj-i Īlkhānī* + originator of the Tūsī couple. Al-Tusi is the central node of the Maragha-school revolution in Islamic astronomy, which produced the mathematical-geometric content that transmits directly into Copernicus's Renaissance reform of Ptolemaic astronomy.

## Biography in three phases

**1. Ismaili Khorasan + Alamut phase (~1220-1256).** Tusi grew up in Khorasan, was educated in the Twelver Shia tradition, but spent much of his early-mid career under Ismaili (Nizari) patronage at Quhistan + later at the Alamut fortress (the famous Ismaili stronghold). He produced significant philosophical + theological work in this period (the *Akhlāq-i Nāṣirī*).

**2. Mongol-Ilkhanate phase (1256-1274).** When Hulagu Khan's Mongol forces sacked Alamut in 1256 + Baghdad in 1258, Tusi survived (controversially — some accounts say he advised Hulagu to take Baghdad). He became Hulagu's chief astronomer + obtained massive Mongol patronage for the **Maragha observatory** (founded 1259 in northwest Iran). Maragha was the most well-funded observatory in the world at the time, attracted an international staff (Mu'ayyad al-Din al-'Urdi from Damascus, Qutb al-Din al-Shirazi, Chinese astronomers), and produced the *Zīj-i Īlkhānī* + the technical innovations associated with the Maragha school.

**3. Death 1274.** Buried in Baghdad. The Maragha school continued through his students for another century; Ibn al-Shatir (Damascus, c. 1350) is the last major figure in the Maragha tradition whose work is directly identifiable in Copernicus.

## The Tūsī couple (the load-bearing technical content)

Ptolemy's planetary models required the **equant** — a fictitious point offset from the Earth from which the epicycle center moves at *uniform angular speed*. The mathematical convenience was great; the philosophical problem was that the epicycle center then does *not* move at uniform speed with respect to its own deferent center — violating the ancient Aristotelian-Ptolemaic commitment to uniform circular motion at the center of each rotating circle.

The **Tūsī couple** is a geometric device: two circles, one of radius *r*, the other of radius *2r*, with the small circle rolling inside the large circle. A point fixed on the small circle traces out a *straight-line oscillation* along a diameter of the large circle. By combining uniform circular motions, you get linear oscillation — a result that allowed Tusi to *replace* the equant with mathematically-equivalent but philosophically-acceptable constructions.

Copernicus's *De Revolutionibus* (1543) uses the geometrically-identical construction for the same purpose (Saliba 2007 ch. 7; Roberts 1957). Copernicus's lunar model is mathematically equivalent to Ibn al-Shatir's (Maragha-school, c. 1350). Copernicus does not cite the Islamic sources, but the geometric-mathematical identity is too detailed to be coincidence — this is one of the cleanest documented Islamic→Renaissance technical-transmission cases in the history of science.
