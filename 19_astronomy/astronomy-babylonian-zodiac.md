# The Babylonian Zodiac

The 12-sign zodiac is a **Babylonian invention of c. -500**, the mathematical refinement of an older observational constellation belt into a uniform coordinate system. It is not Greek and not "ancient" in the sense of going back to the dawn of civilization — it is a Late-Babylonian scribal innovation, after the older [[astronomy-mul-apin|MUL.APIN]] compendium, that the Hellenistic world inherits wholesale and that becomes the foundational reference frame of Greek + Indian + Islamic + European astronomy + astrology.

## The MUL.APIN → 12-sign transition

MUL.APIN (c. -1000) lists the **path of the Moon** as 18 constellations of varying sizes — an observational catalog of star groups through which the Moon passes. By c. -500 the Babylonian scribal-astronomical tradition has transformed this into:

- **12 signs** (one per month of the Babylonian lunisolar calendar)
- **30° each** (equal mathematical divisions of the 360° ecliptic)
- **Coordinate system, not constellation belt** — positions can now be stated as "X° in sign Y" rather than "near star Z"

This is the load-bearing innovation: the conversion of an observational constellation system into a uniform mathematical coordinate system. It is the precondition for the whole subsequent mathematical-astronomical tradition.

## The earliest preserved horoscopes are Babylonian

Rochberg 1998 publishes the 28 surviving cuneiform horoscopes — the oldest dated -410, the latest -69. These are the world's earliest individual-natal horoscopes; they presuppose:

- The 12-sign zodiac as coordinate frame
- Sexagesimal arithmetic on degree-positions
- A standard horoscopic format (Sun + Moon + five planets + Lots, by sign + degree)

The Hellenistic Greek horoscopic tradition (Vettius Valens, Dorotheus, Ptolemy's *Tetrabiblos*) builds directly on this Babylonian substrate — the Greeks translate the sign-names (Krios = Aries, Tauros = Taurus, etc.) and inherit the mathematical framework intact.

## The transmission chain (load-bearing)

- **Babylon (c. -500)** invents the 12-sign zodiac + horoscopic format
- **Hellenistic Greece (c. -200 onwards)** inherits both, translates sign-names, develops Greek horoscopic-astrology literature
- **Ptolemy's Almagest (+150)** uses the 12-sign zodiac as the standard reference frame for all planetary positions
- **India (c. -200 to +200)** receives the 12-sign rāśi via Hellenistic transmission, layering it onto the indigenous [[astronomy-indian-nakshatras|nakshatra]] lunar-mansion system
- **Islamic astronomy (c. +800 onwards)** inherits via the Greek + Indian channels
- **Medieval + Renaissance Europe** inherits via the Islamic + Latin Greek channels
- **Modern Western astrology** uses the same 12-sign framework
