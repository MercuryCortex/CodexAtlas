# Egyptian Decans

The 36 **decans** are ten-day star groups whose heliacal risings mark the 36 ten-day "weeks" of the Egyptian civil calendar (3 weeks per month × 12 months = 36, with 5 epagomenal days added to make 365). They originate as a **Middle Kingdom Egyptian star-clock for the hours of the night** — the earliest preserved lists appear painted inside coffin lids c. -2100 to -1800, the so-called *diagonal star tables* or *coffin star clocks*.

In the Hellenistic period the decans are re-elaborated as the most distinctively Egyptian contribution to Greco-Egyptian astrology: each decan is given a tutelary daimon, made to govern 10° of an ecliptic zodiac sign, assigned a body part (melothesia), and inserted into horoscopic interpretation. This decanic horoscopy becomes one of the principal contents of the Hermetic astrological corpus and transmits through Arabic + Latin medieval astrology into modern Western astrology (where the decans survive as the "first/second/third face" of each sign).

## The Middle Kingdom origin: star clocks on coffin lids

The earliest preserved decanal lists are painted on the inside of wooden coffin lids of the 9th–12th Dynasties (c. -2100 to -1800). The arrangement is a *diagonal star table*:

- 36 columns (one per decan-week of the year)
- 12 rows (one per night-hour)
- A diagonal pattern showing which decan is rising on the eastern horizon at each hour of each week

This is a **practical astronomical instrument**, in painted form: a temporal-orientation aid for the deceased. The decans are stars (or close star groups) whose heliacal risings are separated by roughly 10 days as the Sun advances through them across the year. The Egyptians used them to tell time at night the way they used the Sun's shadow on a sundial to tell time by day.

The selected star groups include some still-identifiable asterisms (Sothis = Sirius is the principal decan; Orion is decan group "Sah") plus others that have eluded definite identification by modern astronomers (Neugebauer-Parker 1960 vol. I gives the full inventory + the identification problems).

## The Hellenistic re-elaboration: decanic astrology

By the 1st century BCE the decanic system is being redeployed as part of Greco-Egyptian horoscopic astrology. The transformation:

- **From star clock to ecliptic coordinate system**: each decan governs 10° of an ecliptic sign (3 decans × 12 signs = 36 decans = 360°)
- **Tutelary daimons**: each decan has a presiding spirit/god with iconography preserved in temple reliefs (e.g. Esna, Dendera) and in magical-papyrus material
- **Melothesia**: each decan governs a body part (decanic medical astrology — magic stones, decanic amulets)
- **Decanic horoscopy**: in casting a chart, the decan a planet occupies is read alongside its sign

This is the **decanic doctrine** that pervades the Hermetic astrological corpus (the *Liber Hermetis*, the Greek + Demotic astrological papyri from Oxyrhynchus, the Latin *Picatrix* via Arabic intermediation) and the most distinctive *Egyptian* contribution to Hellenistic astrology — the Babylonian contribution being the [[astronomy-babylonian-zodiac|12-sign zodiac]] itself. The synthesis (Babylonian signs × Egyptian decans = 36 ecliptic divisions) is the canonical Hellenistic astrological framework.

## Transmission chain

- **Egypt, Middle Kingdom (-2100 to -1800)**: decans as night-hour star clock on coffin lids
- **Egypt, New Kingdom + Late Period**: decans elaborated on temple ceilings (Dendera, Esna), tomb ceilings (Senenmut), and astronomical texts including the *Book of Nut*
- **Hellenistic Egypt (c. -200 to +200)**: decans re-elaborated as ecliptic-astrological doctrine in Greco-Egyptian Hermetic + magical literature
- **Roman + Late Antique world**: decans embedded in mainstream horoscopic astrology
- **Medieval Arabic + Latin tradition**: decans transmitted as the "faces" of each zodiac sign; decanic talismans systematized in the *Picatrix*
- **Renaissance + modern Western astrology**: decans survive as a standard subdivision of the 12-sign zodiac
