## The Mbira

The *mbira* (also *mbira dzavadzimu* — "voice of the ancestors") is a lamellophone: a wooden soundboard fitted with metal tines of varying lengths, played by plucking with the thumbs and one finger. In Shona tradition (Zimbabwe), it is not merely a musical instrument — it is a ritual technology specifically designed for one purpose: enabling communication with ancestral spirits (*vadzimu*).

The mbira is played at *bira* ceremonies — all-night ceremonies where the music is sustained for hours to create the conditions for ancestor spirits to speak through living participants. Without the mbira, the bira does not happen. The mbira is the mechanism by which the dead become present.

## The Acoustic Secret: Deliberate Overtone Buzz

Every mbira is fitted with additional resonating elements — traditionally shells or seed pods, now frequently bottle caps — attached to the soundboard and the gourd resonator (*deze*). These create a characteristic buzzing overtone cloud around every note. The buzz is not a side effect; it is designed in.

Berliner (1978) documents that Shona musicians explicitly value this buzz as essential: it is described as what makes the mbira "alive," as the quality that allows the ancestors to "ride" the sound into the present moment.

## The Bira Ceremony

A complete *bira* ceremony proceeds in stages:

1. **Gathering** — the extended family of the person seeking ancestral guidance assembles at night
2. **Sustained mbira performance** — two or three mbira players alternate in continuous performance through the night; the interlocking patterns create a layered, cyclical sound field
3. **Possession** — one or more participants enters trance (kusvikirwa) and the ancestral spirit speaks through them: diagnosing illness, resolving disputes, providing guidance, naming grievances that need resolution
4. **Return and integration** — the spirit is respectfully dismissed; the possessed person returns to ordinary consciousness; the guidance is interpreted by elders

The mbira music does not "set the mood" for possession. It is the active agent. Berliner documents that different mbira pieces (*kushaura* and *kutsinhira* interlocking parts) correspond to different spirits — the music names and summons a specific ancestor, not ancestral presence generally.
