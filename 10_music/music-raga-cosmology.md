## Raga Cosmology — The Sonic Map of Time

The Indian *raga* system is the most thoroughly developed music-cosmos homology in world religious history. Where the Pythagorean "music of the spheres" remained a mathematical-philosophical doctrine about celestial harmony, the raga system is a *performed* cosmological map: each raga is designated for a specific time of day, a specific season of the year, a specific deity, a specific emotional state (*rasa*), and specific natural phenomena. To perform a raga at the wrong time is not merely an aesthetic violation — it is a cosmological disruption.

## The Structure of the Raga-Cosmos Map

A raga is not simply a scale. It is a total musical personality comprising:

1. **Aroha / avaroha**: ascent and descent pattern (which notes are used, in what order)
2. **Vadi / samvadi**: the most emphasized note (*vadi*) and its secondary (*samvadi*), separated by a fourth or fifth
3. **Rasa**: the dominant emotional-spiritual quality (one of the 8 *rasa* defined in the Natya Shastra)
4. **Thaat**: parent scale classification (Bhatkhande's 10-parent-scale system, modern)
5. **Time designation**: specific prahar (3-hour period) or season
6. **Deity association**: which deity the raga invokes or expresses

This multidimensional specification makes each raga a cosmic address — a precise location in the intersection of time, emotion, and the divine.

## The Time Grid: Ragas of the 24-Hour Cycle

| Time Period | Sample Ragas | Mood / Rasa |
|---|---|---|
| Dawn (Brahma muhurta, ~4–6 AM) | Bhairav, Lalit, Todi | Solemn, devotional, awakening |
| Morning (6–9 AM) | Ahir Bhairav, Bilaskhani Todi | Contemplative, pastoral |
| Late morning (9 AM–noon) | Gaud Sarang, Shuddh Sarang | Serene, expansive |
| Afternoon (noon–3 PM) | Bhimpalasi, Multani | Languid, slightly melancholic |
| Late afternoon (3–6 PM) | Puriya, Marwa, Puriya Dhanashri | Yearning, restless, anticipatory |
| Dusk (Sandhiprakash, 6–7 PM) | Yaman, Kedar, Hameer | Romantic, devotional, liminal |
| Early night (7–10 PM) | Darbari Kanada, Bageshri | Majestic, deep, sorrowful |
| Midnight (10 PM–1 AM) | Malkauns, Bhairavi | Meditative, transcendent |
| Late night (1–4 AM) | Mian ki Malhar, Desi | Rain-evoking, mysterious |

The **Sandhiprakash ragas** (dawn and dusk) are considered most sacred — they express the *liminal* moment between night and day, when the cosmic veil is thinnest.

| Tradition | System | How Time Maps to Sound |

**All five traditions independently mapped musical time onto cosmic time.** The Indian raga system is the most granular: 8 three-hour divisions × multiple seasonal grids × deity associations = a multi-axis cosmic coordinate system expressed through melodic choice.

## The Seasonal Ragas

Beyond daily time, ragas map onto seasons (*ṛtu*):

- **Malhar family** (Mian ki Malhar, Gaud Malhar, Megh Malhar): Rain / Monsoon (*varsha*). Tradition holds that Tansen's performance of Megh Malhar literally summoned rain. This is not decorative legend — it reflects the doctrine that correct performance of the correct raga *activates* the corresponding natural phenomenon.
- **Bahar**: Spring (*vasanta*). Associated with new beginnings and the deity Saraswati.
- **Bhairavi**: Traditionally the raga that closes morning concerts and musical sessions, expressing emotional completion — it is not tied to one season but to the *completion* of time.

Selected raga-deity bindings (representing a small sample of the full taxonomy):

| Raga | Deity | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| Bhairav | Shiva (as Bhairava) | Dawn, destruction-of-illusion, terror-beauty |
| Bhairavi | Parvati/Bhairavi (Shakti) | Devotion, completion, bittersweet |
| Yaman | Saraswati | Evening, learning, beauty |

These associations are not metaphorical overlays — they reflect the Vedic *śruti* doctrine that the raga literally embodies the deity's quality, and that performance makes the deity present.

## The Physics Basis

The raga system's time-association is not arbitrary. Several modern researchers (including Daniélou) have noted that the dominant raga associations track the acoustic properties of different times of day:

- Morning and evening light (lower sun angle) produces more long-wavelength sound propagation — the ragas designated for these times emphasize deeper, more resonant intervals (the minor second in Bhairav; the flat sixth in Yaman)
- Midday heat affects atmospheric acoustics — midday ragas (Sarang family) tend toward brighter, higher-energy tessitura

Whether this is causal or analogical is debated. What is undisputed: the raga-time grid has been continuously performed for 2,000+ years, making it the longest-running music-as-cosmological-practice in documented history.

## The 22 Śruti Microtonal System

The raga system operates within the Vedic *śruti* tuning framework: 22 microtones per octave (vs. the Western 12 equal-temperament semitones; vs. the Arabic maqam's 24 quarter-tones). The 22 śruti are not equal-sized — they are derived from a complex ratio system that Bharata Muni documents in the Natya Shastra (chapters 28–33). This gives Indian music a tonal vocabulary nearly twice as expressive as Western equal temperament. Ragas do not simply select notes from this grid — they bend, approach, and leave notes in ways that encode emotional-cosmic information in microtonal shading.
