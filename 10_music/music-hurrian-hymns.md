## Hurrian Hymns

A collection of approximately 36 hymns inscribed on clay tablets discovered at Ugarit (modern Ras Shamra, Syria), dating to c. 1400–1200 BCE. They are the **oldest surviving notated music in the world** — the only ancient music for which both lyrics and performance instructions (tuning + rhythm) survive in the same document.

The most complete tablet — designated H.6 — is a hymn to Nikkal, the moon goddess and wife of the moon god. It is simultaneously the oldest surviving song with identifiable melody reconstruction, the oldest surviving sacred music text with notation, and one of the oldest surviving examples of polyphonic composition.

## What the Tablets Contain

The Hurrian tablets use a cuneiform notation system that specifies:
- The **text** of the hymn (words to be sung)
- The **string intervals** to be played (named positions on a nine-string harp)
- **Numerical values** indicating rhythmic duration of each interval

The notation is not intuitive to modern readers — it describes intervals between strings, not individual pitches. Scholars spent decades reconstructing what the notation means. Anne Kilmer's 1974 reconstruction is the standard; multiple competing reconstructions exist.

What is certain: the Hurrian system uses **a heptatonic (7-note) scale** organized around a **nine-string harp**, with **named tuning positions** that correspond to names also found in later Babylonian music theory texts. The system is a direct ancestor of the Greek tetrachord/modal system.

## The Babylonian Music Theory Connection

Before the Hurrian hymns were fully understood, scholars discovered a separate cache of Babylonian music theory tablets — abstract treatises on tuning, intervals, and scale theory — dating to c. 1800–1600 BCE. These include:

- Tuning procedures for a nine-string instrument using a cycle of fifths (same procedure as Pythagorean tuning)
- Named scales with the same interval names as in the Hurrian notation
- Mathematical procedures for interval calculation

This establishes that **systematic music theory — not just practice, but written theoretical analysis — existed in Mesopotamia 1,200 years before Pythagoras.** The Pythagorean discovery of harmonic ratios was either an independent rediscovery or (as ancient sources claim) learned during Pythagoras's alleged studies in Babylon.

## MASSIVE WIN: The Oldest Chain

The Hurrian hymns anchor the earliest point of a documented transmission chain:

**Hurrian/Babylonian theory** (c. -1800–1400, Mesopotamia) → **Greek modes** (c. -600–400, allegedly via Pythagoras's Babylonian studies) → **Arabic maqam system** (c. 800–1000 CE, via Greek-to-Arabic translation movement) → **Byzantine/Orthodox chant** (same Greek modes, different transmission) → **European church modes** (via Boethius and Gregory) → **Western tonal system**

A single theoretical framework — heptatonic scale organized around consonant intervals — in continuous (though transformed) use from 1800 BCE to the present. 3,800 years.
