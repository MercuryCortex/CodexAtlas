## The Sānfēn Sǔnyì Fǎ — Successive Fifths

The Chinese **十二律 (shí'èr lǜ, twelve standard pitches)** were derived by a method called **三分損益法 (sānfēn sǔnyì fǎ, "three-part subtract-and-add method")** first recorded in the *Guanzi* (c. -7th to -3rd century BCE) and the *Lüshi Chunqiu* (c. -239 BCE). The method is:

1. Start with the foundational pitch **Huangzhong (黃鐘, Yellow Bell)** — a bamboo tube of standard length
2. **Subtract one third** of the tube (multiply length by 2/3): produces a pitch a perfect fifth above
3. **Add one third** to that result (multiply length by 4/3): produces a pitch a perfect fourth below the previous step
4. Alternate these operations to generate all 12 pitches

This is mathematically **identical to Pythagorean tuning**, which generates the 12-tone chromatic scale by successive applications of the ratio 3:2 (perfect fifth). The Chinese method operates on tube length; the Greek method operates on string length. The physics is the same: the perfect fifth (ratio 3:2) is the second-strongest consonance in the natural harmonic series, and applying it successively generates all 12 Western/Chinese chromatic pitches.

## The Pythagorean Comma Problem — Discovered Twice

Both traditions discovered the same mathematical anomaly through independent investigation.

If you apply 12 perfect fifths (ratio 3:2) starting from any pitch, you expect to return to the same pitch 7 octaves higher (ratio 2:1 applied 7 times = 128:1). Instead you arrive at a pitch that is slightly higher by the **Pythagorean comma** (ratio 531,441:524,288 ≈ 23.46 cents). The 12 fifths overshoot the 7 octaves by a small but musically significant amount. This means you cannot have both perfect fifths AND a perfectly closed 12-tone chromatic scale at the same time.

**In the Greek tradition**: Pythagoras and his followers documented the comma and chose to preserve pure fifths at the expense of a closed circle (Pythagorean tuning). Various meantone and just-intonation systems followed, each trading off different intervals. Equal temperament (Bach's *Well-Tempered Clavier*, 1722) solved the problem by detuning every fifth slightly, closing the circle at the cost of acoustic purity.

**In the Chinese tradition**: The sānfēn sǔnyì fǎ produces the same 12 pitches with the same comma. The *Lüshi Chunqiu* records the problem without resolving it. The Marquis Yi bells (c. -433 BCE) show multiple regional pitch standards coexisting, each managing the comma differently. Chinese scholars debated tuning theory continuously from the Zhou dynasty to the Ming.

The resolution came in **1584 CE**, when the prince and mathematician **Zhu Zaiyu (朱載堉)** published a mathematical derivation of 12-tone **equal temperament** — dividing the octave into 12 perfectly equal semitones using the 12th root of 2 (²√12, the same formula adopted in Europe). Zhu's derivation used a 9x9 counting-rod computation to arrive at the exact ratio to nine decimal places — extraordinary precision for 16th-century mathematics anywhere in the world.

## The Priority Question

The Western attribution for equal temperament typically goes to Simon Stevin (c. 1585, Netherlands) or, for practical implementation, to the Baroque composers culminating in Bach. Zhu Zaiyu's 1584 publication predates Stevin by approximately one year. Whether either derived it independently of the other, or whether Zhu's work reached Europe via Jesuit channels (Matteo Ricci arrived in China in 1582), is a genuinely open historical question. Fritz Kuttner's 1975 paper and Kenneth Robinson's 1980 monograph argue for Zhu's independent priority. The consensus remains unsettled.

If Zhu's independence holds, equal temperament was discovered in **two separate civilizations** within one year of each other after 2,000 years of both traditions circling the same Pythagorean comma problem. This would be one of the most striking near-simultaneous independent discoveries in the history of science — Zhu's derivation from the Chinese bamboo-tube tradition, Stevin's from the Western string-instrument tradition, both arriving at the 12th root of 2 independently.

## The Cosmological Stakes

The twelve lü were not merely a tuning system. They were a **calendar-cosmological instrument**. Each lü was associated with a month of the year and a hexagram of the *I Ching*:

| Month | Lü name | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| 11 (winter solstice) | Huangzhong 黃鐘 (Yellow Bell) | North |
| 12 | Dalü 大呂 | North-Northeast |
| 1 | Taicou 太蔟 | Northeast |
| 2 | Jiazhong 夾鐘 | East-Northeast |
| 3 (spring equinox) | Guxian 姑洗 | East |
| 4 | Zhonglü 仲呂 | East-Southeast |
| 5 | Ruibín 蕤賓 | South |
| 6 | Linzhong 林鐘 | South-Southwest |
| 7 | Yize 夷則 | Southwest |
| 8 | Nanlü 南呂 | West-Southwest |
| 9 (autumn equinox) | Wuyi 無射 | West |
| 10 | Yingzhong 應鐘 | West-Northwest |

At the winter solstice, the Huangzhong pitch was sounded to "call back" the Yang energy buried underground. The pitch was both an acoustic frequency and a cosmological event-trigger. Losing the correct Huangzhong standard was understood as a cosmic emergency: a dynasty that could not maintain the correct foundational pitch was a dynasty losing its calibration with Heaven.

This makes the 12-lü system the clearest Chinese statement of the music-cosmos thesis: the mathematical structure of the harmonic series (encoded in the sānfēn sǔnyì fǎ ratios) and the mathematical structure of cosmic time (12 months, 12 tones, 12 hexagram associations) are the same structure. The acoustic physics of bamboo tubes is a direct mirror of the temporal physics of the cosmos.

## The Connection to Pythagoras

The *Guanzi* records the bamboo-tube derivation method. The *Lüshi Chunqiu* (c. -239 BCE) gives the fullest early account. Pythagoras's monochord experiments are documented by Nicomachus of Gerasa (c. 100 CE). The mathematical operation is identical — multiply by 3:2 (or equivalently, reduce tube/string length by one-third) to ascend a perfect fifth. Both traditions built cosmological systems on this one acoustic ratio. Neither was aware of the other.

What the vault's investigation has established: the perfect fifth (3:2) is the **strongest consonant interval after the octave** in the natural harmonic series. It is physically present in every vibrating body. Any civilization that develops advanced acoustic theory — by measuring strings, bamboo tubes, bell dimensions, or any vibrating medium — will discover this ratio empirically. The music-cosmos connection built on it by both Greece and China is not a mystical intuition. It is an extrapolation from genuine physical measurement.
