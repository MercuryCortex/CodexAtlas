# Low-trust depictions — 2026-05-22

These entries WERE added (image attached to the node) but the iconographic
match is uncertain. Worth John's human eye before they go in front of paying
users.

## LOW-trust

- `belenos` — Used the Gundestrup cauldron (plate b) as Celtic context.
  The cauldron isn't directly Belenos — it depicts a horned/antlered
  deity often read as Cernunnos, and the silver-craft style is Thracian
  not Gaulish. It establishes "Celtic Iron Age ritual context" only.
  Source: `Gundestrupkarret_F.I.7074b.jpg` via frwiki Belenos lead.

- `gugalanna` — Used a Sumerian Ea/Enki image. Gugalanna is "husband of
  Ereshkigal" and appears only in the Inanna's-Descent text; he is the
  Bull-of-Heaven slain by Gilgamesh. The Ea/Enki image is pure
  Mesopotamian-pantheon context, not Gugalanna himself.
  Source: `Ea_(Babilonian)_-_EnKi_(Sumerian).jpg` via frwiki Gugalanna
  lead — this is whatever Wikipedia's French-Wikipedia editors chose
  as a hatnote image.

## MED-trust (probably fine but worth a glance)

- `zorya` — Modern painting by Andrey Shishkin (CC BY-SA 4.0). Shishkin
  is a Russian Neopagan revivalist painter; the image is iconographically
  consistent with folk-tradition descriptions of Zorya (dawn maiden with
  star) but is 21st-century, not historical.
  Source: `Zvezda-Zirka_by_Andrey_Shishkin.jpg`.

- `hunab-ku` — Post-Conquest Maya cosmic-calendar symbol. The
  pre-Conquest historicity of Hunab Ku is contested (the deity may be a
  colonial-era Catholic-inflected reinterpretation). The image labels
  itself as "Maya calendar (Hunab-Ku)" — fine for the entry but the
  caption note acknowledges the colonial-formulation issue.
  Source: `Maya_calendar_(Hunab-Ku).svg`.

- `suwa` — Yemeni statuettes of seated women, al-Jawf, 3rd–1st c. BCE.
  Used as cultic-context for the pre-Islamic Arabian deity Suwāʿ. The
  statuettes are not specifically of Suwāʿ — they are general pre-Islamic
  South-Arabian female cult figures. Reasonable proxy.
  Source: arwiki سواع lead.

- `haumia-tiketike` — A composite chart of "Māori god-symbols" pulled
  from the frwiki Haumia article. Haumia-tiketike (god of uncultivated
  fern-root) appears in the chart but is not the sole subject. Better
  than nothing; iconographic context is correct.

## HIGH-trust but worth confirming culturally

- `wiraqocha` — The Wiener/Tintin "Dieu Soleil" line drawing is from a
  19th-c. archaeological publication and shows the Tiwanaku Gate-of-the-Sun
  central figure. Standard identification with Viracocha, but academia
  is split (some scholars call it Inti, others Pachacámac).

- `prithvi` — The image shows Pṛthu (the king from whom Pṛthivī takes
  her name) ploughing the earth. The deity-and-king pair is a Vedic
  motif; this is the canonical iconographic association, but readers
  who don't know the Pṛthu-Pṛthivī relationship may be confused.
