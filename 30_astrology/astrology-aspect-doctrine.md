# Aspect Doctrine

## What it is

The system of **angular relationships** between points in a chart. Two planets 120° apart are in trine; 90° apart, in square; 180°, in opposition; 60°, in sextile; 0°, in conjunction. Trine and sextile are read as easy, square and opposition as difficult, conjunction as intensifying whatever it joins. Together with [[astrology-planetary-dignities|the dignities]], this is what makes a chart *judgeable*: dignity says how strong each planet is, aspect says how the planets are wired to one another.

The five angles are not arbitrary and were never presented as such. They are the divisions of the circle by the small whole numbers — **2, 3, 4, 6** — and everything else (the 30° semisextile, the 150° quincunx) was excluded on the grounds that it does not divide the circle evenly. [[ptolemy-astronomer|Ptolemy]] makes the argument explicitly in *Tetrabiblos* I by analogy with **musical concord**: the aspects are the harmonious ratios, the rejected angles the dissonances. It is a Pythagorean argument, of a piece with the world-soul mathematics of the [[music-plato-timaeus-worldsoul|*Timaeus*]].

## The one piece the Babylonians did not supply

This node exists partly to hold a **limit** in place.

Almost every component of horoscopic astrology descends from Mesopotamia: the twelvefold zodiac, the planetary sequence, the degree arithmetic, the ephemerides, the exaltations. Say "astrology came from Babylon" and you are mostly right.

You are not right about the aspects. Babylonian celestial divination attends to planetary **phases and events** — heliacal risings, stations, retrogradations, occultations, conjunctions understood as meetings. It does not construct a geometry of angular relationship on the circle, and no antecedent for the aspect scheme has been found in the cuneiform material (Rochberg-Halton 1988; Rochberg 2004; Barton 1994).

The aspects are therefore the clearest single case of a genuinely **Greek** contribution to the apparatus — a mathematical layer, argued from harmonic ratio, laid over an inherited body of omen-derived planetary lore. Which is exactly the shape the honest account of the whole system takes: not "Babylonian" and not "Greek", but a specific list of components with specific origins.

## The vocabulary is a vocabulary of sight

The Greek terms for aspect are verbs of **looking** and **bearing witness**: planets *behold* one another, or give *marturia* — testimony. Latin rendered this as ***aspectus***, "a looking at". English inherits the Latin.

So the standard English technical term still carries the original metaphor: an aspect is one planet **seeing** another. The same image governs the Sanskrit, independently: the indigenous Indian scheme is *dṛṣṭi*, "sight" or "glance". Two traditions that do not share this layer of the apparatus reached for the same metaphor for it, which is worth noting precisely because the rest of the layer did not travel the same way.

## Kepler: the sceptic who extended it

The single substantive addition to the aspect doctrine in seventeen centuries came from **[[kepler|Johannes Kepler]]**, who rejected most of the rest of astrology.

Kepler threw out the twelve houses, most of the dignity scheme, and the bulk of sign-lore as unfounded accretion. He kept the **aspects**, because he believed their harmonic basis was real — that the soul responds to angular ratios of light as the ear responds to ratios of pitch. And in *Harmonices Mundi* (1619) he **extended** the scheme, admitting further harmonic divisions of the circle: the quintile (72°), biquintile, sesquiquadrate (135°) and others.

Those are the "minor aspects" of modern practice. The reform of a critic became doctrine — and the astrologer who uses a quintile today is using an addition made by the man who replaced the circular orbits the whole cosmology had assumed. See [[music-kepler-orbital-harmonics]].

## Disputes

- **Whole signs or degrees?** In the earliest Hellenistic stratum aspects hold between whole **signs** — a planet in Aries aspects any planet in Leo, regardless of degree. Degree-based aspects with tolerances ("orbs") are a later development, and the modern practice of narrow orbs is later still. The recovery of the whole-sign stratum is one of the substantive findings of the traditional-astrology revival (Brennan 2017), and it changes how the ancient texts read.
- **Is conjunction an aspect?** Strictly, in the Greek sources, no: an aspect is a *figure* (*schēma*) formed between separated points, and a conjunction is a co-presence, not a figure. The modern habit of listing it as the first of the aspects is a later convention.
- **Orbs.** There has never been agreement on how much inexactness an aspect tolerates, in any period. The variation is not a modern sloppiness; it is old.
- **What is claimed here.** That the aspect doctrine is Greek in origin, harmonically justified, absent from the Babylonian material, and extended by Kepler on harmonic grounds, is Tier-1 documented history. That planets in trine *produce* ease and in square *produce* difficulty is a claim internal to the tradition, reported here as its doctrine and not as a finding.
