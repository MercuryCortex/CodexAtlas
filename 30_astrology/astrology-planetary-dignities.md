# Planetary Rulerships and Dignities

## What it is

The scheme by which every degree of the zodiac is **owned** by planets, at five nested levels, so that a planet's position can be graded from "in its own house" to "in exile". It is the second of the two judgement systems that make a chart readable: [[astrology-aspect-doctrine|aspect]] says how planets are *connected*, dignity says how *strong* each one is where it stands.

| Level | Greek | What it assigns |
|---|---|---|
| Domicile / rulership | *oikos* | one (or two) whole signs per planet |
| Exaltation | *hypsōma* | one sign, and in the Hellenistic sources one degree, of maximum strength |
| Triplicity | *trigōnon* | day, night and participating rulers for each of the four elemental trines |
| Bound / term | *horion* | five unequal segments within each sign, one per non-luminary planet |
| Decan / face | *dekanos* | three ten-degree segments per sign |

The negatives are **detriment** (the sign opposite a planet's domicile) and **fall** (the sign opposite its exaltation).

## Why this node carries the transmission argument

Because the dignities are where the components come apart cleanly, and each component can be assigned to a different civilization.

### The exaltations are Babylonian

This is the load-bearing fact. Sun exalted in Aries, Moon in Taurus, Jupiter in Cancer, Saturn in Libra, Mars in Capricorn, Venus in Pisces, Mercury in Virgo — the same planet-to-sign assignments appear in Mesopotamian sources under the term ***bīt niṣirti***, the "secret house" or place of a planet's hidden power. Rochberg-Halton's 1988 paper in the *Journal of the American Oriental Society* is the standard demonstration, and it is as close to a smoking gun as the transmission question gets.

What the Greek tradition added was **precision and system**: exact exaltation degrees, and the folding of a set of inherited planet–sign affinities into a five-level graded hierarchy usable for quantitative chart judgement. The intuition is Babylonian; the machinery around it is Hellenistic. Stating only one half of that produces one of the two standard errors about astrology's origins.

### The decans are Egyptian

Level five is Egyptian time-keeping absorbed wholesale ([[astronomy-egyptian-decans]]), and it keeps its name at every border: *dekanos* → Sanskrit *dṛkāṇa* → Arabic *darījān*.

### The domicile scheme is Hellenistic geometry applied to a Babylonian planet-order

The rulership assignments are generated, not listed: the Sun takes Leo and the Moon Cancer, and the remaining five planets are assigned outward in both directions in the **Chaldean order** (Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon — the sequence of apparent orbital period). Mercury takes Gemini and Virgo, Venus Taurus and Libra, Mars Aries and Scorpio, Jupiter Sagittarius and Pisces, Saturn Capricorn and Aquarius. [[ptolemy-astronomer|Ptolemy]] sets out the reasoning in *Tetrabiblos* I; the scheme is symmetrical about the Cancer–Leo axis, which is a Greek kind of argument applied to a Mesopotamian planetary sequence.

The same Chaldean order, applied to the hours of the day instead of the signs of the zodiac, produces the **seven-day planetary week** ([[calendar-seven-day-week-origin]]) — which is why Saturday, Sunday and Monday still name Saturn, the Sun and the Moon in English, and *mardi / mercredi / jeudi / vendredi* still name Mars, Mercury, Jupiter and Venus in French. The dignity scheme and the week are two applications of one ancient list, and the week is the piece of the apparatus that survived into secular modernity unnoticed.

## Where the scheme went

- **India.** The full apparatus crosses with the rest of the horoscopic frame: *svakṣetra* (own-sign), *ucca* (exaltation), *nīca* (fall), *drekkāṇa* (decan). Indian astrology uses the **same exaltation signs** as the Greek sources, and in several cases the same degrees — so Babylonian material reaches South Asia by way of Greek ([[phase-3-041-yavanajataka]]; [[varahamihira|Varāhamihira]]).
- **The Islamic world.** *Bayt* (house/domicile), *sharaf* (exaltation), *wabāl* (detriment), *hubūṭ* (fall) — [[abu-mashar-al-balkhi|Abū Maʿshar]] systematizes, [[al-biruni|al-Bīrūnī]]'s *Tafhīm* tabulates.
- **Latin Europe.** *Domus*, *exaltatio*, *triplicitas*, *terminus*, *facies* — and thence into English as house, exaltation, triplicity, term, face, in [[phase-6-054-lilly-christian-astrology|Lilly's 1647 tables]], which a modern practitioner can still read without a glossary.

## Disputes

- **Whose bounds?** Three competing tables circulated in antiquity — "Egyptian", "Chaldean", and Ptolemy's own, which he introduced on the strength of an **ancient manuscript he claimed to have found**. Ancient and modern readers alike have been sceptical of that claim; the Egyptian bounds are the ones most used in practice, then and now. The dispute is a good specimen of how authority worked in the tradition: a new table needed an old source, so an old source was produced.
- **Whose triplicities?** [[dorotheus-of-sidon|Dorotheus]] and [[ptolemy-astronomer|Ptolemy]] give different triplicity rulers for the air and water trines. The Dorothean scheme is the one the Perso-Arabic tradition carried; the divergence is live in the modern traditional revival.
- **Why these assignments?** The tradition offers rationalizations — thermal qualities, symmetry about the solstitial axis, the planet's nature answering the sign's. Scholarship treats these as *post hoc*: the assignments are inherited, and the reasons were supplied afterward to make an inherited list look derivable (Barton 1994; Bouché-Leclercq 1899).
- **What is claimed here.** That this scheme exists, that its exaltations are Babylonian, its decans Egyptian, its systematization Greek, and that it travelled intact into Sanskrit, Arabic and Latin, is Tier-1 documented history. That a planet in its domicile *acts more strongly* is a claim internal to the tradition and is reported, not endorsed.
