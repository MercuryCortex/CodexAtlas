# Chi-Rho (Christogram) / Labarum

## Identity
The **Chi-Rho** (☧) is a Christian monogram formed by superimposing the first two letters of the Greek word for Christ (ΧΡΙΣΤΟΣ) — capital chi (Χ) and capital rho (Ρ). The form is one of the **two principal Christian identifying-symbols of the 4th-5th centuries** (alongside the [[latin-cross|Latin cross]]) and became inseparable from the **labarum** — the military-imperial standard of [[constantine-i|Constantine I]] following his pre-Milvian-Bridge vision (28 October 312 CE).

## Earliest attestation
- **c. 280 CE** — Christian catacomb funerary inscriptions in Rome (Catacombs of Priscilla, Domitilla, Callixtus). The Chi-Rho **predates Constantine's vision by approximately 30 years**, indicating it was an existing Christian sign Constantine adopted rather than invented. (Snyder 1985 ch. 4.)
- **28 October 312 CE** — Constantine's pre-Milvian-Bridge vision; the form (as later described) becomes the labarum military standard.
- **313 CE onward** — Christian funerary, ecclesiastical, and imperial-military iconography across the Roman Empire; the Chi-Rho rapidly becomes the principal universal Christian identifying-mark.
- **325 CE [[event-council-of-nicaea-325|Nicaea]]** — by the time of the Council, the Chi-Rho is universal across Christian episcopal and imperial-military iconography.
- **5th-6th c.** — gradually replaced as principal Christian emblem by the [[latin-cross|Latin cross]] as the latter loses its earlier penal-stigma association (crucifixion as a Roman penal practice was abolished by Constantine c. 337 CE).
- **Continuous Christian liturgical use** — the Chi-Rho remains a standard Christian decorative-and-liturgical motif from the 6th c. through to the present (vestments, altar-cloths, manuscript-decoration, ecclesiastical heraldry).

## Meaning(s) attested
- **Greek Christogram** — the first two letters of ΧΡΙΣΤΟΣ (*Christos*).
- **Constantine's vision (Eusebius VC 1.28)** — a heavenly sign with the Greek inscription *τούτῳ νίκα* ("conquer by this"), Latinized as ***In hoc signo vinces*** ("in this sign you will conquer"). The visionary form became Constantine's military standard.
- **Imperial-Christian-fusion emblem (4th-5th c.)** — Chi-Rho on coinage from c. 315 CE (Constantine's silver medallion of Ticinum), on imperial military gear, on the *labarum* itself. Signals Constantine's identification of Roman military success with Christ.
- **Catacomb funerary emblem (pre-Constantinian)** — abbreviation of *Christos* on burial markers; signals Christian identity in the persecution period without the politically-charged content of an explicit cross.

## Variants
- **Constantinian vision-form (312)** — described in Lactantius and Eusebius, with non-identical details.
- **Catacomb form (c. 280)** — predates Constantine.
- **Labarum (c. 315 onward)** — the actual imperial military standard.
- **Iota-Chi monogram (ΙΧ)** — parallel Greek-initials variant.
- **Alpha-and-Omega-flanking variant** — Chi-Rho with Α and Ω flanking, signifying Revelation 22:13 (Christ as beginning and end). Common from late-4th c. onward.

## Cross-family appearance
The Chi-Rho is **specifically Christian and does not migrate to other religious traditions**. It is one of the cleanest cases of a *Christian-internal* symbol in the vault. Subsequent appropriations are:
- **Modern military insignia** — some Christian-state and Christian-military insignias (e.g., the Royal Norwegian Air Force emblem, certain Catholic-military-order modern uses).
- **Modern Catholic ecclesiastical heraldry** — papal arms, episcopal arms, religious-order arms.
- **Modern Christian liturgical decoration** — vestments, altar-furniture, manuscript-decoration.

## Contested interpretations
- **The Constantinian vision itself** — Lactantius (writing 318, closer to event) and Eusebius (writing 337, after Constantine's death) give non-identical descriptions. Lactantius describes a dream-vision with a *staurogram-like sign* (a tau-rho ⳨ or chi-rho ☧ — debated). Eusebius describes a sky-vision with explicit Greek inscription. Modern scholarship (Drake 2000; Barnes 2011) treats the vision-narratives as somewhat-redacted but anchored on a real Constantinian conversion-event.
- **The chi-rho-or-staurogram question** — some scholars (Bardill 2012) argue the original Constantinian sign was a *staurogram* (tau-rho ⳨, abbreviation of *stauros* = cross), only later harmonized to the chi-rho. The debate is alive.
- **Catacomb-Chi-Rho dating** — the pre-Constantinian attestations are sometimes contested in their exact dating; Snyder 1985 defends c. 280 CE as the earliest secure dating.
