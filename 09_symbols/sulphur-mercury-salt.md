# Sulphur, Mercury, Salt (Tria Prima)

## Identity

The **Tria Prima** — the Three Philosophical Principles of Paracelsian alchemy — is the triadic ontological framework introduced by **[[paracelsus|Theophrastus Bombastus von Hohenheim (Paracelsus)]]** in his *Opus Paramirum* (1531) and elaborated throughout his natural-philosophical corpus ([[phase-6-008-paracelsus-corpus]]). The three principles are:

- **Sulphur (Sulfur)** — the **combustible principle**, associated with fire, activity, spirit (*Spiritus*), and the capacity for transformation. That which burns in any substance.
- **Mercury** — the **volatile / fluid principle**, associated with water, mediation, soul (*Anima*), and the messenger-function between fixed and volatile. That which vaporizes.
- **Salt** — the **fixed / stable principle**, associated with earth, inertia, body (*Corpus*), and the capacity to resist dissolution. That which remains.

The Tria Prima replaces the older Arab-derived binary theory (sulphur-mercury theory of metals, attributed to the Jabir corpus, c. 9th c. CE; [[phase-5-039-jabir-corpus-arabic-alchemy]]) with a three-principle framework that maps onto Christian theological anthropology (spirit-soul-body) and cosmology. Every substance in nature, for Paracelsus, is a compound of these three principles in varying ratios — including human beings, who are therefore microcosmic mirrors of universal ternary structure.

## Earliest attestation

- **c. 9th c. CE** — The *sulphur-mercury binary* (two-principle theory of metals) appears in the Jabir ibn Hayyan corpus ([[phase-5-039-jabir-corpus-arabic-alchemy]]). This is the direct predecessor Paracelsus knew and expanded.
- **1531** — Paracelsus, *Opus Paramirum*: first systematic statement of the Tria Prima as a three-principle ontological system.
- **c. 1530–1541** — The Paracelsian corpus as a whole develops the Tria Prima across multiple texts; *Philosophia Magna*, *De Philosophia ad Athenienses*, and related works elaborate the system.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Ontological stratum** — In Paracelsian natural philosophy, the Tria Prima are not "elements" in the Aristotelian sense (earth/water/air/fire) but *functional principles* present in all matter. Any substance can be analyzed into its sulphurous, mercurial, and saline components by distillation and calcination.
- **Anthropological** — The human being is a microcosm: body (Salt) + soul (Mercury) + spirit (Sulphur). Disease arises from imbalance; cure requires restoring the appropriate ratio.
- **Christian-theological** — Paracelsus explicitly maps the Tria Prima onto the Trinity: Sulphur = Son (the combustible, passible, Christ who suffers); Mercury = Holy Spirit (the mediating, pneumatic, universally present); Salt = Father (the fixed, eternal, foundation). This is documented in the Paracelsian corpus itself — not a later imposition.
- **Operative-alchemical** — In laboratory practice, the Tria Prima provides the framework for distillation, calcination, and sublimation operations: isolate the sulphur-fraction (oils), the mercury-fraction (volatile spirit/alcohol), the salt-fraction (ash/salt residue) from any substance.

## Cross-family appearance

The Tria Prima is explicitly European-alchemical in origin. However, it has two extraordinarily close independent parallels in non-Western traditions:

### Massive Win 1: Hindu Three Gunas (Triguṇa — Samkhya philosophy)
The Sanskrit **triguṇa** system — **Sattva** (luminous, harmonious, clarity), **Rajas** (active, energetic, fiery), **Tamas** (inert, heavy, fixed) — first systematized in Samkhya philosophy (attested in *Bhagavad Gita* c. 200 BCE; *Sāṃkhyakārikā* of Īśvarakṛṣṇa c. 4th c. CE) maps with striking structural fidelity onto the Tria Prima:

| Paracelsian Principle | Character | Hindu Guṇa | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sulphur | Active, fiery, transformative spirit | **Rajas** | Active, passionate, kinetic force |
| Mercury | Fluid, luminous, mediating soul | **Sattva** | Luminous, balancing, clarifying principle |
| Salt | Fixed, stable, inert body | **Tamas** | Inert, heavy, resistant to change |

This parallel is **certainly independent** — the Bhagavad Gita predates Paracelsus by ~1700 years, and there is no documented historical transmission of Samkhya guṇa-theory to 16th-century German-speaking alchemy. The parallel is structural: both traditions independently arrived at a three-principle ontology that triply divides the continuum of matter/substance by the axes of (1) activity/fire, (2) mediation/light, and (3) fixity/inertia. [[tradition-samkhya-yoga]] carries this framework.

### Massive Win 2: Chinese Three Treasures (Jing-Qi-Shen — Daoist internal alchemy)
The Daoist neidan (internal alchemy) tradition's **Jing** (essence/seed/body), **Qi** (vital energy/breath), **Shen** (spirit/consciousness) — attested in Tang-dynasty Daoist texts (c. 7th c. CE) and systematized by the Song-dynasty neidan masters — maps onto the Tria Prima:

| Paracelsian | Chinese Three Treasures |
|---|---|
| Salt (body, fixed) | **Jing** (essence, the material/seminal foundation) |
| Mercury (vital soul, mediating) | **Qi** (vital energy, the mediating life-force) |
| Sulphur (spirit, active) | **Shen** (spirit, the luminous consciousness) |

**No documented historical transmission** before the modern period. This is parallel-form between two independent triadic ontologies — both of which triply divide reality along the same functional axes. [[tradition-daoism]] carries the Jing-Qi-Shen tradition.

### Massive Win 3: Neoplatonic Triad (Nous/Psyche/Hyle)
**Plotinus** (*Enneads*, c. 253–270 CE) articulates the Neoplatonic triadic ontology of **Nous** (Intellect/spirit), **Psyche** (Soul/mediating), and **Hyle** (Matter/body) as the three strata of the emanated cosmos. [[plotinus]] is the Neoplatonic source; [[tradition-neoplatonism]] carries the framework. This is the *direct philosophical ancestor* (not independent parallel) of the Paracelsian Tria Prima, via the Renaissance Hermetic synthesis (Ficino, Pico, Agrippa). Transmission is documented: Paracelsus absorbed Neoplatonic cosmology through the Renaissance Hermetic context.

### Massive Win 4: Valentinian Gnostic Anthropological Triad
The Valentinian Gnostic tradition (2nd c. CE, attested in Irenaeus *Adversus Haereses*, the Nag Hammadi Tractates) articulates a tripartite anthropology: **Pneuma** (spirit, the divine spark), **Psyche** (soul, the mediating psychic stratum), **Hyle** (matter, the Demiurgic body). Structurally identical mapping to Sulphur/Mercury/Salt. [[tradition-gnosticism]] carries this framework. The Valentinian triad is historically prior and feeds into the Neoplatonic stream that reaches Paracelsus — probable *indirect* transmission via Neoplatonic intermediaries.

## Contested interpretations

- **Were the Tria Prima "real" substances or principles?** Scholarly debate between "exoteric" historians of chemistry (the Tria Prima as proto-chemistry) and historians of esotericism (the Tria Prima as spiritual philosophy in chemical vocabulary). Principe 2013 insists the Paracelsian corpus must be read in both registers simultaneously.
- **Does Paracelsus owe more to Hermetic-Neoplatonic sources or to Jabir's Arabic chemistry?** Both are clearly operative; the relative weighting of Arabic technical chemistry vs. Renaissance Hermetic philosophy in the Paracelsian system is debated.
- **The Hindu-alchemical parallel** — Is the Triguṇa/Tria-Prima parallel a genuine convergence or post-hoc imposition? The parallel is structural, not historical. Jung 1944 discusses the cross-cultural triadic patterning without claiming transmission; subsequent Jungian scholarship (von Franz, Edinger) develops the amplificatory reading. Historians of religion note the structural convergence while refusing transmissionist claims without evidence.
