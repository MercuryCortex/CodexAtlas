# Tau cross

## Identity

The tau cross (*crux commissa*) is a T-shaped cruciform: a vertical beam with a horizontal crossbar set at its top (no upward extension above the crossbar). The form predates Christianity by at least a millennium — the Greek letter Τ (tau) and the Phoenician taw share this geometry. Within Christianity the tau cross has been adopted at specific moments as an alternative to the Latin form: most prominently by Francis of Assisi (early 13th c.) and as the emblem of the Hospitaller Order of Saint Anthony.

## Earliest attestation

- **Phoenician taw** (c. 1500–800 BCE) — last letter of the Phoenician abjad, originally written as a simple X or cross-mark; later evolves into the T-shape via the Aramaic and Hebrew scripts.
- **Hebrew tav** — last letter of the Hebrew alphabet; Ezekiel 9:4 ("set a tav upon the foreheads of the men who sigh and groan over all the abominations") provides the prophetic-marking origin used by later Christian apologists.
- **Greek tau** (Τ) — adopted from Phoenician; attested in Greek inscriptions from c. -800 onward; used in numerological and Pythagorean contexts.
- **Christian appropriation** — Tertullian (*Against Marcion* 3.22, c. 207 CE) explicitly identifies the Greek tau with the shape of the cross of Christ. From the early 13th c., Francis of Assisi adopts the tau as his personal blessing-sign (his autograph survives in the Sacro Convento, Assisi).

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Phoenician / Hebrew** — last letter, semiotic of completion / closure. Ezekiel's *tav* is a protective mark distinguishing the righteous from those slated for destruction.
- **Greek Pythagorean** — sometimes associated with the cosmic axis or with numerological functions; less developed than Christian readings.
- **Christian apologetic (Tertullian, Justin Martyr, Origen)** — the tau is a typological pre-figuration of the cross; the bronze serpent on a pole (Numbers 21), the marking of the Israelites in Ezekiel 9, and the Greek letter tau are read as Christological foreshadowings.
- **Franciscan** — Francis interpreted Ezekiel 9 as a personal call to bear the tau; his Lateran blessing and the tau-mark on his cell wall make it the standing Franciscan iconographic signature. The Order continues to use it today.
- **Antonian** — the Hospitaller Order of Saint Anthony (founded 1095) used the tau on its habit because Antony the Great in the *Vita Antonii* by Athanasius is associated with the cross / staff form; the tau-crutch becomes the iconographic attribute of Antony in Western art.

## Variants

The Greek letter, Phoenician taw, Franciscan tau, and Antonian tau are body sub-sections here.

## Cross-family appearance

- **Egyptian** — the ankh base is structurally a tau; whether this constitutes documented transmission from Egyptian sandal-strap glyph → Phoenician taw → Greek tau is unresolved. The general scholarly position is **visual cognate, transmission unproven** (Gardiner 1957 on the ankh; Jensen 2017 on the cross broadly).
- **Christian Latin tradition** — tau is the older form within early Christianity; the Latin long-shaft cross emerges as the dominant form in the 4th–6th c., but tau-form Christian crosses persist throughout the medieval period.
- **Heraldry and modern reception** — the Antonian and Franciscan tau remain in active institutional use; the tau also appears in some modern occult and Theosophical sources, where it is conflated (often without scholarly basis) with the ankh.

## Contested interpretations

- The hypothesis that the Egyptian ankh is the ultimate ancestor of the Greek tau (and therefore of the Christian cross) is suggested by visual cognacy but lacks documentary evidence of transmission. Gardiner 1957 treats the ankh as a sandal-strap pictograph; a direct trace to Phoenician taw is not established.
- Whether the historical crucifixion stake was a tau-form or a Latin-form is contested (Hengel 1977 *Crucifixion*; Jensen 2017 ch. 1).
