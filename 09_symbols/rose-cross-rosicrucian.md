# Rose Cross (Rosicrucian)

## Identity
The **Rose Cross** is the central iconographic emblem of the **[[tradition-rosicrucianism|Rosicrucian]] tradition** — a **cross with a rose at its center** (or a rose with a cross springing from it), variously elaborated with Hermetic-alchemical-Kabbalistic emblematic elements. Anchored to the textual emergence of the Rosicrucian fraternity in the **[[phase-6-018-rosicrucian-manifestos|*Fama Fraternitatis* (1614) and *Confessio Fraternitatis* (1615)]]**, the visual emblem develops iconographically through **Andreae's *Chymical Wedding* (1616), Maier's *Themis Aurea* (1618), and Fludd's apologetic works (1616–1621)**. The Rose Cross becomes the principal Christian-Hermetic-alchemical synthesis-glyph of the 17th-21st centuries — institutionally vehiculated through (1) the **18th degree of the Scottish Rite of Freemasonry** (the *Knight of the Pelican and Eagle, Sovereign Prince Rose Croix*), (2) the **Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn** (1887 onward), and (3) the **modern Rosicrucian orders** (AMORC, Rosicrucian Fellowship, Lectorium Rosicrucianum, etc.).

The Rose Cross is **one of the flagship symbols of the Bibliotheca Philosophica Hermetica / Ritman Library** — directly relevant to the user's stated interest in that collection.

## Earliest attestation
- **1614–1615** — the [[phase-6-018-rosicrucian-manifestos|*Fama* and *Confessio*]] introduce the textual tradition. **No visual emblem in the original manifestos.**
- **1616** — Andreae's *Chymical Wedding of Christian Rosenkreutz* [[phase-6-021-andreae-chymical-wedding]]; Fludd's *Apologia Compendiaria*. Early visual elaboration begins.
- **1618** — Maier *Themis Aurea*; the visual Rose Cross iconography crystallizes.
- **1617–1621** — [[phase-6-022-fludd-utriusque-cosmi-historia|Fludd's *Utriusque Cosmi Historia*]] uses Rose-Cross-related emblems extensively.
- **18th c.** — Scottish Rite Freemasonry codifies the 18° *Rose Croix*.
- **1871** — Albert Pike *Morals and Dogma of the Ancient and Accepted Scottish Rite* gives the canonical 18°-Rose-Croix interpretation.
- **1887 onward** — Golden Dawn elaborates the Rose Cross with Kabbalistic + planetary + elemental correspondences.
- **1915 onward** — AMORC and other modern Rosicrucian orders.

## Meaning(s) attested
- **Original Rosicrucian (1614–1620)** — *Christian-Hermetic-alchemical synthesis*: the cross is *Christ's sacrifice*; the rose is *Hermetic-alchemical regeneration and the soul's transformation*. Together: *Christian theology integrated with Hermetic-alchemical natural philosophy as the path of integral spiritual-and-natural reform of European culture* (per the *Fama*'s *universal reformation* project).
- **Scottish Rite 18° Rose Croix** — *the cross-and-rose as the Christian-and-Hermetic synthesis* in Masonic initiatic-allegorical form. Paired with the [[pelican-in-piety|pelican-in-her-piety]] (the 18° is the *Knight of the Pelican and Eagle, Sovereign Prince Rose Croix*).
- **Golden Dawn** — Rose Cross as **maximally syncretic glyph**: 22 rose-petals = Hebrew alphabet; 12 outer petals = zodiac; 7 inner petals = planets; central elements = elemental correspondences. Ceremonial-magical *lamen* worn by adepts.
- **Modern Rosicrucian orders** — variously interpreted: mystical-Christian (AMORC, Rosicrucian Fellowship); Gnostic-Hermetic (Lectorium Rosicrucianum); psychological-developmental (Anthroposophical Rosicrucian descendant traditions via Rudolf Steiner).

## Cross-family appearance
The Rose Cross is **specifically Christian-Hermetic in origin** (1614–1620, Lutheran-Württemberg-Hesse). Its subsequent reception is *within* the broader Western Christian-Hermetic-esoteric tradition — Scottish Rite Freemasonry, Golden Dawn, Anthroposophy, modern Rosicrucian orders. It does **not** migrate to non-Western or non-Christian traditions.

## Contested interpretations
- **Did Christian Rosenkreutz exist?** No. The textual tradition explicitly presents the founder as legendary; modern scholarship treats Christian Rosenkreutz as a *literary figure* (the protagonist of Andreae's allegorical *Chymical Wedding*) rather than a historical person. Andreae himself, in his *Vita ab Ipso Conscripta* (autobiography), described the manifestos as *ludibrium* (a satirical or playful literary jest) — though scholars dispute how literally to take this.
- **Was there ever an actual Rosicrucian Brotherhood?** Probably not as a formal institution. Yates 1972 argued for a *real network* of like-minded Lutheran-Hermetic-alchemical scholars (Andreae, Hess, Besold, Maier, Fludd, with Frederick V of the Palatinate as patron); subsequent scholarship (Stevenson 1988, Hanegraaff 2012) is more skeptical of the *institutional* dimension while accepting the *intellectual-network* reading.
- **Did the Rosicrucian movement survive into Scottish-Rite Masonry?** Direct genealogical continuity is **historically unrecoverable**. The 18th-c. Scottish Rite 18° draws on the 1614–1620 textual tradition as *literary heritage*, not as documented organizational descent.
- **AMORC and other modern Rosicrucian orders' claim of continuous tradition.** Not historically defensible. Modern Rosicrucian orders are 20th-c. constructions building on the 17th-c. textual heritage.
