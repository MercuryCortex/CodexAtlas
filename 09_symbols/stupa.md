# Stupa (Sacred Mound / Cosmic Mountain)

## Identity

The **stupa** (Sanskrit: *stūpa* — heap, mound; Pali: *thūpa*; Tibetan: *chörten*, མཆོད་རྟེན — offering support; Chinese: *tǎ*, 塔; Japanese: *tō*) is the primary Buddhist monumental form — a hemispherical mound enclosing relics (*śarīra*) of the Buddha or enlightened masters, topped by a spire of cosmic significance. It is simultaneously three things: a **tomb** (enshrining bodily relics), a **cosmic map** (the universe in three-dimensional form), and an **axis mundi** (the world-pole connecting earth to heaven). No other religious monument so completely fuses these three functions into a single coherent form. The stupa is the earliest distinctively Buddhist architectural type, predating the seated Buddha image by several centuries.

## Earliest attestation

- **Historical earliest**: The eight original stupas built over the Buddha's remains immediately following his parinirvāṇa (c. 400 BCE, *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta*, Dīgha Nikāya 16), distributed among the eight claimant kingdoms. These are the founding instance.
- **Archaeological earliest**: The Great Stupa of Sanchi (Madhya Pradesh) in its earliest form dates to the reign of Ashoka (r. c. 269–232 BCE), who according to tradition redistributed the original relics into 84,000 stupas across his empire. The third Buddhist council (c. 250 BCE) coincides with Ashoka's stupa-building campaign — see `[[event-third-buddhist-council-c-250-bce]]`.
- **Textual canonical source**: The *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta* (Pali canon, Dīgha Nikāya 16) prescribes stupa construction at four pilgrimage sites and specifies the relic-distribution protocol. This is the founding charter for the stupa institution.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Relic as Buddha's dharma-body**: The physical relic (*śarīra* — bone, tooth, hair) = the Buddha's *dharmakāya* (truth-body) condensed into matter. The stupa = the field in which the relic radiates *puṇya* (merit) to all who circumambulate it. To circumambulate = to receive merit through proximity to the Buddha's presence.
- **Cosmic mountain**: The stupa's cosmological diagram: square earth-base → hemispherical dome (*aṇḍa*, mountain-body) → harmika (the gods' platform atop the mountain) → spire of 13 rings (the 13 heavens of Buddhist cosmology) → finial (the tip of the world-axis reaching the formless realm). This is Mount Meru in architectural form.
- **Cosmic egg / womb**: The *aṇḍa* dome = the brahmāṇḍa (cosmic egg). The relic = the seed. The stupa's merit-radiation = the creative energy released when the cosmic egg hatches. The stupa is both tomb and womb.
- **Circumambulation (pradakṣiṇā)**: Clockwise circumambulation of the stupa (keeping the right shoulder toward the sacred object) is the primary ritual act. This is simultaneously: (a) the devotee's orbit around the center of the cosmos, (b) homologized with the planets' orbits around Mount Meru, and (c) formally identical to the Ka'ba *tawāf* (Islamic circumambulation during hajj) — a remarkable cross-tradition convergence of circumambulatory worship at a centered sacred monument, without historical transmission.

## Variants

### The Man-Made Sacred Mountain Cross-Tradition Pattern

The stupa belongs to a larger architectural-theological complex that appears across independent traditions: the sacred space IS a mountain; building the sacred space = constructing the mountain:

- **Egyptian pyramid** (c. 2600 BCE): The pyramid's pyramidal form references the primordial *benben* mound (the first land to emerge from Nun). The pyramid = the primordial mound + the solar ray (the slope = the descending ray of Ra). The king's burial inside = placement within the axis of creation.
- **Mesopotamian ziggurat** (c. 2100 BCE): The *ziqqurrat* ("to build on a raised area") — a stepped mountain-temple. The *Etemenanki* of Babylon ("House of the Foundation of Heaven and Earth") = the Tower of Babel prototype in Genesis 11. The divine presence dwelt at the summit; priests ascended to meet the gods.
- **Hindu temple shikhara**: The tower (*śikhara*) of the South Indian temple = Mount Meru rising above the garbhagṛha (womb-house) containing the deity. The temple is the cosmic mountain architecturally; entering the temple = ascending the mountain to the god's presence.
- **Mesoamerican pyramid-temple**: The Aztec *teocalli* ("god-house") = a stepped mountain with a temple at the summit. Human sacrifice at the summit = sacrificial offering at the mountain-top, maintaining cosmic renewal. The Pyramid of the Sun at Teotihuacan (c. 100–250 CE) was built over a cave — mountain above the underworld cave = the full cosmic axis.
- **Gothic cathedral**: The vertical thrust of the Gothic nave (the soaring height of Chartres = 37 m, Cologne = 43 m interior) = architectural ascent toward heaven. The spire = the axis reaching upward. The function is identical though the aesthetic is transformed.

### Borobudur — The Stupa as Three-Dimensional Mandala

The Borobudur stupa (central Java, c. 800 CE, Śailendra dynasty) represents the most complete realization of the stupa's cosmological program. Its nine terraces correspond to the Buddhist cosmological realms; its 504 seated Buddha statues face the four cardinal directions from every terrace; its 72 latticed stupas on the upper three circular terraces each contain a Buddha statue (visible through the lattice, invisible at ground level). Pilgrims who walk the full circumambulatory circuit of all nine terraces (covering approximately 5 km) physically re-enact the bodhisattva's progression from the realm of desire to the realm of formlessness — a complete meditation curriculum in architectural form.

## Cross-family appearance

The circumambulatory worship of a sacred center-point is cross-traditional:

- **Ka'ba tawāf** (Islamic hajj): Counterclockwise circumambulation of the Ka'ba in Mecca — structurally identical to stupa *pradakṣiṇā* in its centering logic and bodily ritual. No historical transmission (the Ka'ba predates Islam; stupa and Ka'ba developed independently); structural parallel from the convergent logic of centering sacred space.
- **Kailash circumambulation**: Mt. Kailash (Tibet/Nepal) is circumambulated by Hindu, Buddhist, Jain, and Bon pilgrims alike — the mountain itself as stupa/axis mundi. The 52-km circuit (*kora*) is made by all four traditions as the supreme pilgrimage act.

## Contested interpretations

- **Stupa origin**: Pre-Buddhist Indian burial mounds (*mahāpāṣāṇa*) are often cited as the architectural precursor. The question is whether the Buddhist stupa derives from Vedic burial practice (śmaśāna) or is a Buddhist innovation. Snodgrass (1985) argues the cosmological program is specifically Buddhist, even if the mound-form has pre-Buddhist precedents.
- **Pagoda derivation**: The exact transmission pathway from Indian stupa to Chinese pagoda (via Gandhara, Central Asia, and the Silk Road) is documented in outline but the specific intermediary forms are debated. The Chinese multi-story tower had indigenous pre-Buddhist precedents (*lóu*, 樓) that merged with the incoming stupa form.
