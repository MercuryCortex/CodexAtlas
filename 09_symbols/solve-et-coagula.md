# Solve et Coagula

## Identity

**Solve et Coagula** — Latin: "Dissolve and Coagulate" — is the fundamental operative maxim of Western alchemy. It names the two-phase sequence at the heart of the **Great Work** (*Opus Magnum*): (1) **solve** — dissolve the substance (or the self, in spiritual alchemy) into its prima materia, stripping away all composite accretion to reach the primal undifferentiated substrate; (2) **coagula** — reassemble, precipitate, crystallize the dissolved matter into a new, purified, elevated form. The maxim encapsulates the alchemical theory of transformation: no genuine transmutation is possible without first returning to the formless beginning. Destruction is not the end but the necessary precondition of a higher recomposition.

Visually, the maxim is most iconic today through **[[eliphas-levi|Eliphas Lévi]]'s 1861 Baphomet image**, where "SOLVE" is inscribed on the right forearm and "COAGULA" on the left of the goat-headed androgynous figure — a deliberately synthesizing image that packs Paracelsian alchemy, Hermetic Neoplatonism, and Masonic ritual language into a single glyph.

## Earliest attestation

- **c. 8th–9th c. CE** — The **Jabir ibn Hayyan corpus** ([[phase-5-039-jabir-corpus-arabic-alchemy]]) contains the earliest systematic development of the dissolve-and-recompose principle in alchemical laboratory theory. The Arabic technical vocabulary for tahwil (transformation through dissolution) and tajmid (coagulation/fixation) is the operative ancestor of the Latin phrase.
- **13th c. CE** — The **pseudo-Geber** Latin corpus and **Albertus Magnus's** *Libellus de Alchimia* crystallize the Latin phrase *Solve et Coagula* as the definitive alchemical maxim.
- **c. 1470** — [[phase-6-032-rosarium-philosophorum|Rosarium Philosophorum]] (printed 1550 but circulated earlier in manuscript) deploys solve/coagula language in its allegorical Royal Marriage sequence — the sequence Jung analyzed most intensively.
- **1617** — [[phase-6-035-maier-atalanta-fugiens|Michael Maier, *Atalanta Fugiens*]] — the solve-coagula cycle governs the emblematic program of the 50 fugues.
- **1861** — Lévi's Baphomet image with SOLVE/COAGULA inscriptions — the most culturally influential modern carrier.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Operative-laboratory** — In physical alchemy: dissolve the metal/substance in acid (aqua regia, aqua fortis); filter; allow the purified material to crystallize/precipitate (coagulate). The cycle may be repeated multiple times — hence the spiral nature of alchemical operations (distillation loops, repeated calcinations).
- **Spiritual-alchemical** — The practitioner themselves undergoes solve (deconstruction of ego, habits, false self) and coagula (reformation of the soul into a purer, more luminous form). This is the dominant reading in Hermeticism and Rosicrucianism.
- **Masonic-ritual** — In Scottish-Rite Masonic language, the candidate's symbolic death and resurrection (especially in the 3rd-degree "death of Hiram Abiff" and the 18th-degree Rose Croix ritual) enacts solve et coagula in initiatic-dramatic form. [[tradition-scottish-rite-freemasonry]] vehiculates this reading explicitly (Pike, *Morals and Dogma*, 1871).
- **Jungian-psychological** — Individuation as nigredo (dissolution of the persona/ego-complex) leading to coniunctio (integration of shadow + anima/animus into the Self). [[tradition-jungian-depth-psychology]] is the modern institutional vehicle.

## Cross-family appearance

*Solve et Coagula* as a *named maxim* is specific to Western alchemy. But the structural principle — **cosmic dissolution followed by cosmic recomposition** — appears independently in multiple traditions:

### Massive Win 1: Shiva as Cosmic Destroyer-Recreator (Hinduism)
**[[shiva|Mahakāla-Shiva]]** is the lord of cosmic dissolution (*pralaya*): at the end of each kalpa (cosmic cycle), Shiva performs the *tāṇḍava* dance of destruction that dissolves the entire manifest cosmos back into the unmanifest — the solve phase at cosmic scale. **[[brahma|Brahmā]]** then recreates (*sṛṣṭi*) — the coagula phase. The Hindu cosmological cycle of **pralaya (dissolution) → srishti (creation)** is *Solve et Coagula* operating at the level of the cosmos itself. This parallel is independent — the Purāṇic pralaya cosmology is documented from the Mahābhārata (c. 3rd c. BCE) onward, antedating even the Jabir corpus. [[tradition-vedic-hinduism]] and [[tradition-kashmir-shaivism]] carry this framework.

### Massive Win 2: Osiris Dismembered and Reassembled (Egypt)
The **[[osiris|Osiris myth]]** — Set murders Osiris, dismembers his body into 14 (or 16) pieces and scatters them across Egypt (solve) — Isis reassembles the body (coagula) — Osiris is resurrected as Lord of the Dead — is **structurally identical** to *Solve et Coagula*. Scholars of Egyptian religion and Hermeticism (Garth Fowden, *The Egyptian Hermes*, 1986; Erik Hornung, *The Egyptian Book of Gates*, 1999) have documented that the Osiris narrative was one of the founding mythological models for Hellenistic-Egyptian alchemy: the alchemist dissolves and reassembles matter in explicit imitation of Isis reconstituting Osiris. **Historical transmission is documented** — this is not a parallel-form but an ancestor-of relationship. [[tradition-egyptian-religion]] and [[tradition-isis-mysteries]] are the originating traditions.

### Massive Win 3: Christian Baptismal Death-Resurrection (Paul, Romans 6)
The Pauline theology of **baptism as death-and-resurrection** maps precisely onto *Solve et Coagula*: "We were buried with him by baptism into death, so that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life" (Romans 6:4). The **old Adam** is dissolved (solve — the baptismal death), the **new Adam** is coagulated (coagula — the baptismal rebirth). This is *Solve et Coagula* in Christian theological vocabulary. The early-modern alchemists were fully aware of this parallel — it is explicit in Paracelsian and Rosicrucian writing where baptism is read as the *baptism of fire and water* that corresponds to the alchemical solve-coagula cycle. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]] and [[tradition-early-christianity]] carry the baptismal theology.

### Massive Win 4: Taoist Return to the Uncarved Block (Pu)
The Taoist principle of **pu** (朴, "the uncarved block") in the *Tao Te Ching* — the goal of returning through emptying and simplicity to the undifferentiated primal state — has structural kinship with the solve phase. Laozi Ch. 28: "Return to the state of the uncarved block." Zhuangzi's dissolution of boundaries (the Cook Ding passage, Ch. 3) enacts the solve phase at the level of perception and ego. The subsequent **coagula** in Daoist internal alchemy (neidan) is the *return to Heaven* (*fan ben*, returning to the source). [[tradition-daoism]] carries this framework. No documented historical transmission — structural parallel.

## Contested interpretations

- **Is the Baphomet figure originally Masonic or anti-Masonic?** Lévi's Baphomet was created as an esoteric synthesis symbol, not as a Masonic emblem per se. The SOLVE/COAGULA inscription was Lévi's addition. 19th-c. anti-Masonic polemic (Leo Taxil affair, 1880s) misread the Baphomet as a Masonic deity; Lévi's original intent was Hermetic synthesis. Modern Satanist appropriation (LaVey, 1966) adds a further reception layer.
- **Is the Osiris-alchemy connection historically documented or retrospective?** Fowden 1986 and Hornung's work document the Hermetic Isis-Osiris alchemical allegory in Greek-Egyptian papyri (Zosimos of Panopolis, c. 300 CE) — this is a Tier-1 documented claim, not retrospective imposition.
- **Authenticity of "early" Jabir corpus texts** — Holmyard and Kraus's scholarship on the Jabir corpus establishes that parts of the corpus are later pseudepigraphical compositions; the date of the earliest solve-coagula formulations in Arabic alchemy remains a matter of ongoing philological investigation.
