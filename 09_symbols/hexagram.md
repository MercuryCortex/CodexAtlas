# Hexagram

> **Methodology discipline note:** This entry surfaces the **independent** Jewish and Hindu attestations without conflating them. Per Scholem 1949 and Oegema 1996, the hexagram's status as Jewish ethnic-religious emblem is largely *post-medieval* — formalized in early-modern Prague and made universal only by the Zionist movement (1897) and the State of Israel (1948). The Hindu Shatkona is millennia older with continuous Tantric and Shaiva-Shakta use. Edge type between the two attestations is `visual-cognate`, NOT `ancestor-of`. The popular claim "the Star of David is ancient" conflates the medieval-magical Seal-of-Solomon lineage with the modern ethnic emblem — these are separate.

## Identity

A hexagram is the six-pointed star formed by two overlapping equilateral triangles (the *hexalpha*, "six-A", since it can be read as six conjoined alphas). Geometrically equivalent to a regular star polygon {6/2}; semiotically it is read by different traditions as: (a) the interlocking masculine-feminine cosmic principles (Hindu Shatkona); (b) a magical seal of authority over demons (Seal of Solomon, medieval magical traditions); (c) a Jewish ethnic-religious emblem (Magen David / Star of David, primarily post-medieval); (d) a Hermetic / alchemical figure of fire-water complementarity (Renaissance and after).

## Earliest attestation

- **Bronze Age incidental forms** — six-pointed star designs appear on Iron Age and earlier Mediterranean and Mesopotamian decorative objects, but these are not theologically loaded and are best read as ornamental.
- **South Asia (Hindu Shatkona)** — interlocking-triangle Shatkona forms attested in early Tantric and Shaiva-Shakta iconography from at least c. -500 onward; central to Sri Yantra mandala-iconography (Kramrisch 1981). Continuous ritual use to the present.
- **Roman / Byzantine ornament** — six-point star motifs in mosaics and tilework, mostly decorative.
- **Medieval magical literature** — *Sefer Raziel HaMalakh* (c. 13th c.), *Clavicula Salomonis* (Greek and Latin manuscripts c. 14th–16th c.), and Islamic *Khatim Sulayman* (Seal of Solomon) develop the hexagram as magical seal of Solomonic authority over demons. This lineage is multi-religious (Jewish, Christian, Islamic) and predates the modern Jewish-ethnic adoption.
- **Prague Jewish community (c. 14th–17th c.)** — the hexagram appears as a community emblem on the Old-New Synagogue and on community documents; Oegema 1996 traces this as the kernel of the modern Magen David tradition.
- **Zionist adoption (1897)** — the First Zionist Congress in Basel adopts the hexagram on its flag; thereafter universal in Jewish ethnic-political iconography.
- **Flag of Israel (1948)** — formal state adoption.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Hindu Shatkona** — the interlocking upward (Shiva / *purusha* / fire / consciousness) and downward (Shakti / *prakriti* / water / matter) triangles signify the cosmogonic union of masculine and feminine principles. Central to Sri Yantra. (Kramrisch 1981)
- **Medieval Seal of Solomon** — a binding magical seal: Solomon (1 Kings 4:29–34 wisdom + extra-biblical tradition of his power over demons) wields it to compel demons; appears in *Clavicula* operations and Arabic magical texts. (Davies 2009)
- **Modern Magen David** — Jewish ethnic and religious self-identification; the meaning is in the use, not in a fixed cosmological reading. (Scholem 1949; Oegema 1996)
- **Hermetic / alchemical** — fire (▲) + water (▽), with air and earth as triangles with horizontal bars; the union of opposites cosmically. (Agrippa, *De Occulta Philosophia* 1533; subsequent Hermetic and Theosophical reception.)

## Variants

The four major variants (Shatkona, Magen David, Seal of Solomon, alchemical hexagram) are documented in the YAML. Each has its own iconographic lineage; the medieval Seal of Solomon is multi-religious magical and partly bridges them.

## Cross-family appearance

The hexagram is attested in **at least four independent traditions** (Hindu, Jewish, Islamic-magical, Christian-Hermetic). Scholem's foundational argument is that these are *parallel* attestations rather than a single line of transmission:

- **Hindu Shatkona ↔ Jewish Magen David** — `visual-cognate`. No documented historical transmission. Scholem 1949 explicitly rejects derivation claims in either direction. The Hindu attestation is older but no transmission to Jewish use is evidenced.
- **Medieval magical traditions** — the *Clavicula Salomonis* (Greek, Latin, and later Hebrew versions) is shared across Jewish, Christian, and Islamic magical practitioners; here there IS documented cross-religious transmission, but the magical-grimoire context is distinct from the modern ethnic emblem.
- **Theosophy (Blavatsky)** — explicitly identifies Shatkona with Magen David and proposes a unifying esoteric meaning; this is post-1875 syncretist reception, Tier 4 in evidentiary weight.

## Contested interpretations

- Whether the Magen David's status as Jewish emblem has any pre-medieval continuous lineage. Scholem 1949 and Oegema 1996 say no, the medieval and modern lineage is largely discontinuous from any ancient use, with the partial exception of the medieval magical Seal-of-Solomon tradition that bridges Jewish and non-Jewish magical practice.
- Whether the Indus Valley Civilization seals with six-point-star motifs are theologically related to later Shatkona (the IVC connection is contested per `mystery-status` of the IVC corpus itself — see [[indus-valley-script]]).
- Whether Theosophical (Blavatsky) identification of Shatkona with Magen David has any evidentiary basis. Scholarship treats this as Tier-4 syncretist projection.

## Mystery / unknown variants

None significant; the medieval and modern lineages are well-documented even when contested.
