# Wheat / Grain

> **Methodology discipline note:** Frazer's *Golden Bough* "dying-and-rising god" / vegetation-deity comparative program is now widely scaled back (Jonathan Z. Smith 1987 "Dying and Rising Gods" in *Encyclopedia of Religion*; Mettinger 2001 *The Riddle of Resurrection*). The Tammuz / Dumuzi cycle is real and attested; whether it should be grouped with the Egyptian Osiris and the Phrygian Attis under a single comparativist category is contested. Cite Frazer for reception-history of the comparative-religion idiom, NOT for primary Mesopotamian / Egyptian theology.

## Identity

Wheat / grain is the iconographic-cultic vocabulary of cultivated-cereal-as-divine-vehicle across the Mediterranean, ancient Near East, and (via Christian eucharistic reception) the global Christian tradition. The earliest sustained attestation is the Mesopotamian Inanna-Dumuzi / Tammuz cycle (c. -2500 onward), with the dying-and-returning vegetation god. The classical Greek Eleusinian Mysteries center on Demeter and Persephone with wheat-sheaf iconographic vocabulary. Egyptian Osiris has a grain-aspect (the Osiris-bed ritual). Christian eucharistic theology inherits the bread-as-body element from Jewish Passover seder *matzah* but the iconographic vocabulary of grain / wheat / ears overlaps with the Greco-Roman environment.

## Earliest attestation

- **Mesopotamian Dumuzi / Tammuz (c. -2500 onward textual)** — Inanna-Dumuzi cycle. The descent of Inanna ([[phase-1-017-descent-of-inanna]]) ends with Dumuzi substituting for Inanna in the underworld; his sister Geshtinanna agrees to spend half the year for him. Seasonal grain / vegetation cycle.
- **Greek Demeter and Persephone (c. -1500 onward Mycenaean attestation; Homeric Hymn c. -700)** — Eleusinian Mysteries cult attested from at least c. -700 BCE; the Mysteries continue until 392 CE (Theodosius I's prohibition of pagan cults).
- **Egyptian Osiris-grain (c. -2300 onward)** — Pyramid Texts allusions; subsequent New Kingdom Osiris-bed rituals; Plutarch's *De Iside et Osiride* (c. 100 CE).
- **Hebrew Bible Tammuz polemic (Ezekiel 8:14, c. -593)** — Ezekiel's vision at the Jerusalem Temple sees women weeping for Tammuz at the gate; explicit Hebrew polemic against the cult. ([[phase-2-010-hebrew-bible-early-prophets]])
- **Roman Ceres (3rd c. BCE onward)** — *interpretatio romana* identification with Demeter; Cerialia festival.
- **Christian eucharist (1st c. CE onward)** — Pauline *Epistles*, Synoptic Gospels, *Didache* 9 (c. 100 CE).

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Mesopotamian Dumuzi / Tammuz** — vegetation cycle; the dying-and-returning grain-god; seasonal lament-and-rejoicing. (Jacobsen 1976.)
- **Greek Demeter-Persephone** — the agricultural cycle as cosmic-mythic frame; the mother-daughter separation-and-reunion; the climactic showing of a single grain in the Eleusinian Mysteries. (Foley 1994.)
- **Egyptian Osiris** — grain-aspect as one of several dimensions of Osiris; the Osiris-bed grain-sprouting ritual. Not the only Osiris-meaning; Osiris is also chthonic-judge and royal-ancestor.
- **Christian eucharistic** — bread = body of Christ; the wheat-grain-falling-to-the-ground-and-dying-to-bear-fruit (John 12:24) typology; the bread-of-life (John 6:35-58). (McGowan 2014.)

## Variants

The five attested variants (Demeter-Eleusis, Tammuz-Dumuzi, Ceres, Christian eucharistic bread, Osiris-grain) are documented in YAML.

## Cross-family appearance

- **Mesopotamian Dumuzi → broader Mediterranean dying-vegetation-god comparativist category** — the Frazerian grouping. Methodology-discipline flag: the category is now contested; Smith 1987 and Mettinger 2001 scaled it back. Individual attestations (Dumuzi, Osiris-grain, Demeter-Persephone) are well-attested in their own contexts; grouping them under a single category is interpretive.
- **Hebrew polemic against Tammuz (Ezekiel 8:14)** — `polemic-inversion`. The Hebrew Bible explicitly opposes the Tammuz cult.
- **Greek Demeter → Roman Ceres** — `ancestor-of` (via interpretatio).
- **Greco-Roman wheat-iconography → Christian eucharistic iconography** — `parallel-form`. Same comments as for vine-iconography at [[vine-grape]]: the Christian theological content is Jewish-Passover origin, the iconographic Mediterranean vocabulary overlaps.

## Contested interpretations

- The Frazerian dying-and-rising-god category. Mettinger 2001 *The Riddle of Resurrection* gives a careful case-by-case assessment; the category survives for SOME cases (Dumuzi/Tammuz, Adonis, partly Osiris) but not as a universal comparativist apparatus. Smith 1987 *Encyclopedia of Religion* article is the foundational scholarly critique. Methodology-discipline call: do NOT cite Frazer as authority on what Mesopotamian or Egyptian theology *says*; cite him for the history of comparative-religion idiom.
- The exact relationship of Osiris-grain to Osiris-chthonic-judge. Egyptologists (Hornung, Pinch) treat these as different dimensions of a complex deity, not separate gods.
- The historical-causal influence of Greco-Roman mystery-cult iconographic vocabulary on early Christian eucharistic iconography. Mainstream scholarly view (McGowan 2014): theological content from Jewish-Passover, visual vocabulary partly from Mediterranean environment. Older Reitzenstein-Bousset "Christianity-is-just-Hellenistic-mystery-cult" theses are now widely rejected.
