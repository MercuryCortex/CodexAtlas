# Scarab (Khepri)

## Identity
The scarab — specifically *Scarabaeus sacer*, the dung beetle that rolls a ball of dung across the desert, lays its eggs in the ball, and from which new beetles emerge — is **one of the two most-produced Egyptian amulet-symbols** (alongside the [[eye-of-horus-wedjat]]). It represents the deity **Khepri** (*ḫprr*, "the becoming one"), the **morning aspect of [[ra]]** — the self-emerging, self-generating, rising sun. The scarab is a **cosmic-cosmogonic symbol**: as the beetle rolls its dung-ball, so the divine beetle rolls the sun across the morning sky; as the new beetles emerge from the dung-ball with no apparent parentage, so the sun re-emerges each morning self-generated from the underworld. For the Codex Atlas mission the scarab is doubly load-bearing: (1) it is the **principal Egyptian cosmic-self-renewal symbol** in the funerary corpus (heart-scarabs, scarab-amulets in vast numbers, the entire scarab-iconographic-theological complex of Heliopolitan solar religion); (2) it is **appropriated by Augustine of Hippo as a Christ-symbol** in *Sermons* 119.4 — *"bonus scarabaeus meus,"* "my good scarab" — a startling and theologically dense piece of Latin Christian-Egyptian iconographic absorption that almost no Christian-tradition or Egyptology survey discusses, because it sits at exactly the cross-field boundary mainstream scholarship doesn't cross.

## Earliest attestation
- **Old Kingdom** ([[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]], c. -2400 onward) — Khepri attested as solar deity; scarab-hieroglyph (*ḫprr*, Gardiner L1) used logographically for "become / come into being."
- **Middle Kingdom** — scarab-amulets become common; carved-stone scarabs with seal-impressions on the underside used as both personal amulets and administrative seals.
- **New Kingdom** — the **heart-scarab** institutionalized: large stone scarab placed on the heart of the embalmed dead, inscribed with [[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]] Spell 30B (the prayer that the heart will not testify against the deceased at the weighing-of-the-heart in the Hall of Two Truths). Tutankhamun's coffin had a heart-scarab; thousands of New Kingdom mummies have them.
- **Late and Ptolemaic-Roman period** — scarab-amulet production reaches massive industrial scale; **tens of thousands surviving**, including extensive Greco-Egyptian magical-papyri context (Bonner 1950).
- **Roman-imperial Christian period** — Augustine's *Sermons* 119.4 (~+410) deploys the scarab as Christ-figure (see below).

## Meaning(s) attested

### Egyptian
- **Self-generation / coming-into-being.** The verb *ḫpr* ("become") is the scarab's name; Khepri is "the one who becomes." The scarab is **the paradigm symbol of cosmic self-emergence** — what self-generates without prior cause.
- **Solar morning-emergence.** Khepri = the morning sun rolling itself across the dawn sky; Ra = the midday sun; Atum = the evening sun. The three aspects compose the daily solar cycle.
- **Resurrection / posthumous renewal.** The heart-scarab prays for the deceased's vindication at judgment; the scarab is iconographically the **promise of cosmic-self-renewal that the deceased will share**.
- **Protective amulet.** Worn in vast numbers across all periods; standard Egyptian apotropaic-protective category alongside Wedjat.

### Augustinian Christian appropriation
**Augustine of Hippo *Sermons* 119.4** (~+410), preaching on the Resurrection of Christ:

> *"Christus in cruce ut leo, in sepulchro ut formica. **Bonus scarabaeus meus**, non eo tantum, quod unigenitus, quod ipse sui auctor mortalium speciem induerit, sed quod in hac nostra faece sese volutaverit et ex hac ipsa nasci voluerit."*

> "Christ on the cross is like a lion, in the tomb like an ant. **My good scarab** — not only because as the only-begotten he took on a mortal form by his own will, but **because he rolled himself in this our filth and was willing to be born from it**."

Augustine deploys the scarab — **specifically the dung-beetle rolling itself in waste-matter and engendering new life from it** — as a **dense theological metaphor for the Incarnation and Resurrection**:
1. **Self-generation** (Khepri's defining Egyptian meaning) → Christ as *unigenitus* (only-begotten / self-existent).
2. **Rolling-in-filth** → Incarnation, the divine entering human/material existence.
3. **Birth from filth** → Resurrection, new life emerging from the apparent waste of crucifixion-death.

The passage is **astonishing on multiple levels**: a North African Christian Father (himself only a few generations removed from the African pagan tradition that still venerated Khepri-Ra in Egyptian-Christian-syncretic religion at this exact period — Frankfurter 1998 documents extensive Late-Antique Egyptian Christianity-Egyptian-religion syncretism) **deploying the central Egyptian self-generating-solar deity-symbol as a Christological metaphor in the highest patristic-Latin theological register**.

## Cross-family appearance

### MASSIVE-win: the Augustinian appropriation as cross-field-boundary edge

The Augustinian scarabaeus passage is **the most-direct surviving piece of patristic-Latin appropriation of Egyptian-religious vocabulary** in the entire Christian Latin tradition. It is **almost entirely unknown to mainstream Christian-tradition surveys** (which compartmentalize Augustinian theology) and to **mainstream Egyptology surveys** (which compartmentalize patristic reception). The cross-field boundary makes this an exemplary gold-zone edge.

Related patristic engagements:
- **Ambrose** *Hexameron* 6.7 — scarab mentioned in natural-theology context.
- **Eucherius of Lyon** *Formulae spiritualis intelligentiae* — explicit scarabaeus-Christus typology.
- **Ado of Vienne** (9th c.) Carolingian transmission.
- Medieval and later — the scarabaeus-Christus typology persists in Latin scholastic-bestiary literature, fading by the later medieval period as the natural-history of dung-beetles becomes empirically unromantic.

### Other cross-tradition diffusions
- **Phoenician scarab-amulets** — scarab-form amulets manufactured by Phoenician traders for Mediterranean export; **largest non-Egyptian source of scarab-amulet finds in archaeological contexts across the Mediterranean** (Carthage, Sardinia, Cyprus, Etruria).
- **Etruscan scarab-rings** — the principal pre-Roman Italian scarab-tradition; carved Etruscan scarabs are a distinctive jewelry-and-amulet category.
- **Renaissance Egyptophilia** — Athanasius Kircher's *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652) revives scarab-symbolism in Hermetic-Renaissance context; subsequent Romantic and modern occult traditions intermittently revive it.

## Contested interpretations
- **Did Augustine know he was using Egyptian-religious vocabulary?** Augustine was a North African educated in Latin; his Egyptian-religious knowledge was filtered through Greco-Roman-Hellenistic literature (Plutarch, Diodorus, possibly Apuleius). Whether he was consciously appropriating Egyptian-Khepri-symbolism or treating the dung-beetle as a generic natural-history-derived metaphor is **debated**. The dominant view treats him as **substantially conscious** — Augustine's North African heritage and his use of multi-layered theological metaphor make it implausible he was ignorant of the Egyptian background.
- **The "rolled himself in our filth" emphasis.** The Augustinian passage's stress on the *self-rolling-in-mire* is theologically dense — it could be either pure-incarnation-metaphor or specifically-scatological-incarnation-emphasis. Patristic Latin liked stark metaphors; Augustine's choice is deliberately startling.
- **Scarab-as-Christ in non-Augustinian Latin Christianity.** How widely the scarabaeus-Christus typology spread is unclear — Augustine is the most-influential text, but later medieval bestiaries are spotty in their inclusion of the scarab as Christ-figure.
