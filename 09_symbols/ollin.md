# Ollin (Movement / Earthquake)

## Identity

**Ollin** (Nahuatl: ōllin — "movement," "earthquake," "motion") is the central glyph of the Aztec *Tonalpohualli* (the sacred 260-day ritual calendar) and the central element of the *Piedra del Sol* (Aztec Sun Stone / "Aztec Calendar Stone," c. 1427-1479 CE, now Museo Nacional de Antropología, Mexico City). The Ollin glyph — an X-shaped crossing of two curved, scrolled bands — is the symbol of **cosmic movement**, seismic energy, and the current Fifth Sun (*Nahui Ollin* — "Four Movement"): the cosmic age in which the Aztecs believed they lived and which they believed would end in catastrophic earthquakes (*ōllin*). The glyph encodes the Aztec theological understanding that the cosmos is not a static given but a **dynamic process requiring continuous maintenance** — specifically, human sacrifice to keep the sun moving. Without sacrificial blood, the sun stops; the earth moves catastrophically; the current age ends. Ollin = the symbol of the cosmos-in-motion-by-obligation.

## Earliest attestation

- **Tonalpohualli calendar system**: The 260-day sacred calendar (Tonalpohualli) is attested from the **early Postclassic period** (c. 900 CE onward) across multiple Mesoamerican cultures. The calendar is pan-Mesoamerican — Maya, Mixtec, Aztec, Zapotec all share cognate versions. The Ollin day-sign is the 17th of 20 day-signs across the system.
- **Aztec codices**: The Ollin glyph appears prominently in the **Codex Borgia** ([[phase-8-002-codex-borgia]]), c. 15th c. CE — a pre-Columbian ritual calendar screenfold manuscript, one of the most important Aztec ritual texts.
- ***Piedra del Sol* (Sun Stone)**: c. **1427-1479 CE** (Aztec imperial period, reign of Axayacatl). The Sun Stone was discovered in 1790 CE beneath the Zócalo (Mexico City's main plaza, built on Tenochtitlan's ruins), initially mistaken for a 'calendar' in popular reception.
- **Florentine Codex**: Sahagún's *Florentine Codex* ([[phase-8-004-florentine-codex-sahagun]]), compiled c. 1569 from Nahuatl-speaking indigenous informants, is the primary textual source for the Five Suns cosmology and the meaning of Nahui Ollin.

## Meaning(s) attested

**In Aztec theology (from primary sources including Sahagún and the codices):**

- **The Tonalpohualli day-sign**: Ollin is the 17th of 20 *tonalpohualli* day-signs, governing a specific day in the 260-day ritual calendar. Persons born under an Ollin day had fates connected to movement, seismic power, and cosmic forces. The day-sign was associated with the *trecena* (13-day period) ruled by specific deities (Klein 1976).

- **Nahui Ollin — the Fifth Sun**: The current cosmic age was created at **Teotihuacan** ("the place where gods are made") in mythological time, when the gods gathered in darkness and sacrificed themselves to create the sun. Two gods — Tecuciztecatl and Nanahuatzin — threw themselves into a sacrificial fire; Nanahuatzin (the humble, scabbed god) became the sun; Tecuciztecatl became the moon. But the sun would not move. The gods then had to sacrifice themselves *again* (giving their blood) to make the sun move across the sky. This is the mythological foundation of human sacrifice as **cosmic maintenance**: if the sun required divine self-sacrifice to move, it requires ongoing sacrifice of blood to keep moving.

- **The earthquake-destruction prophesy**: The date 'Four Ollin' (4 Ōllin) was prophesied as the date on which the current age would end — the sun would stop moving and catastrophic earthquakes (*ōllin*) would destroy the world. This is not a distant eschatology but an imminent possibility requiring constant ritual attention.

- **Seismic theology**: Tenochtitlan was built in the Valley of Mexico, a seismically active zone. Earthquakes were real, frequent, and experienced as the shaking of the cosmic foundation. The Ollin glyph encodes **lived geological reality as theology** — the earth moves (*ōllin*) because the cosmos is dynamic and fragile, sustained by human ritual obligation.

## Cross-family appearance

**The Five Suns and Hindu-Buddhist cosmic cycles**: The Aztec Five Suns cosmology — five successive cosmic ages, each destroyed by a different catastrophe (jaguar, wind, rain of fire, flood, earthquake) — is structurally parallel to the Hindu doctrine of *kalpas* (cosmic time-cycles of vast duration, each ending in destruction and renewed creation) and Buddhist cosmic cycles (*mahākalpa*). All three: (1) posit multiple successive worlds/ages; (2) include a Great Flood as one of the destructions; (3) posit an ongoing current age that will end catastrophically; (4) require some form of ritual/moral maintenance to sustain the current age. These are independent constructions of cyclical-cosmos theology with striking structural convergence.

**Sacrifice as cosmic maintenance**: The Aztec theology that human sacrifice is necessary to keep the sun moving is the most explicit version of a cross-cultural principle: the cosmos requires ongoing ritual maintenance or it will fail. Hindu *yajña* (sacrificial ritual) maintains *ṛta* (cosmic order) — without fire sacrifice, the sun would not rise (Vedic cosmology). Norse *blót* (blood sacrifice) sustains the gods' strength against the giants — without sacrificial blood, Odin and the Æsir would weaken. All three are: *sacrifice → cosmic maintenance → continued motion of the world*. The Aztec version is simply the most explicit and the most demanding.

**The 52-year Calendar Round and the New Fire**: The Aztec sacred calendar (Tonalpohualli, 260 days) interlocks with the solar calendar (Xiuhpohualli, 365 days) to produce the **Calendar Round** — a 52-year cycle (260 × 365 / GCD). At the end of each 52-year cycle, if the calendars did not successfully realign, the world would end. The New Fire ceremony (*Toxiuhmolpilia* — "binding of the years") was performed at **Hill of the Star** (Cerro de la Estrella) when all fires in Tenochtitlan were extinguished and new fire was drilled on the chest of a sacrificed captive at the moment the Pleiades reached the zenith. If the fire was successfully drilled, the world would continue; if not, the sun would not rise. This connects to: the **Jewish Jubilee** (50-year cycle of cosmic reset); **Zoroastrian cosmic cycles** (*Bundahishn*); the **Mayan Long Count** (ending 2012 CE = another form of calendar-end theology). All are: *the calendar reaching its limit = the cosmos in danger = ritual response required*.

**The Ollin X-form and the crossroads**: The Ollin glyph's X-shaped crossing has been compared structurally (not as a documented transmission) to: the Greek cross, the pre-Columbian solar cross, and the [[crossroads-symbol|crossroads]] as the symbol of intersection. The X = the mark of the crossing-point, the dynamic junction where forces meet. Whether this reflects a deeper geometric universal (the X as the archetype of dynamic intersection) or is pure coincidence is the structural question.

## Contested interpretations

- **The "Aztec Calendar Stone" misnomer**: The popular name "Aztec Calendar Stone" for the *Piedra del Sol* is misleading — the stone is not a functional calendar but a monumental cosmological statement about the Five Suns and the current age. Scholars (Klein 1976, Townsend 1979, Pasztory 1983) prefer *Piedra del Sol* or "Aztec Sun Stone."
- **The central figure's identity**: The face at the center of the Sun Stone has been identified as (a) Tonatiuh (the sun god), (b) Tlaltecuhtli (the earth monster), (c) a composite solar-earth deity. Klein (1976) argues for the earth monster; Townsend (1979) and Pasztory (1983) are more cautious. The identification affects the symbolic interpretation of the entire monument.
- **Human sacrifice necessity**: The doctrine that human sacrifice is cosmically necessary (not merely symbolically appropriate) is presented consistently in the primary sources. Some scholars (following Inga Clendinnen) argue this should be understood as genuine Aztec theological conviction, not propaganda. Others see the sacrifice system partly as political-imperial machinery. Both readings have evidence; they are not mutually exclusive.
- **Ollin-swastika comparison**: Some early 20th-c. scholars (particularly Tylor, Zelia Nuttall) argued for diffusionist connection between the Aztec Ollin rotating cross-form and Old World swastika traditions. This is now rejected; the visual similarity is considered independent convergence on the rotating cross as cosmic-motion symbol.
