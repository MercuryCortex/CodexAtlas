# Bodhi Tree

## Identity

The **Bodhi Tree** (Sanskrit: *bodhidruma* / *bodhivṛkṣa* — "tree of awakening") is the specific *Ficus religiosa* (sacred fig / peepal tree) under which [[siddhartha-gautama-buddha|Siddhartha Gautama]] attained *bodhi* (enlightenment) at Bodh Gaya, Bihar, India, c. -528 BCE (traditional date). The Bodhi Tree is simultaneously: (1) a **historical object** — a specific tree whose direct descendant still grows at Bodh Gaya and whose earliest cutting grows at Anuradhapura, Sri Lanka, since c. -236 BCE; (2) a **sacred symbol** — in the earliest Buddhist art (Bharhut, Sanchi) the tree *replaces* the human figure of the Buddha as the primary symbol of enlightenment; and (3) the **Buddhist axis mundi** — the geographic and theological center of the Buddhist world, the point of contact between the human and the ultimately real. The tree is distinct from the general *tree-of-life* category: it is the **specific** tree, the tree whose event-meaning (the moment of the Buddha's awakening) saturates it with singular significance.

## Earliest attestation

- **Indus Valley context**: The *Ficus religiosa* (peepal) appears in **Indus Valley Civilization seals** (c. -2500 BCE), where figures are depicted in meditation beneath its distinctive heart-shaped leaves. The tree's sacrality predates Buddhism by nearly 2000 years.
- **Hindu Ashvattha**: The peepal as *Ashvattha* appears in **Vedic literature** (c. -1000 BCE) as a sacred tree; the *Bhagavad Gita* (c. -200 to +200 CE, [[phase-2-027-bhagavad-gita]]) 10:26 has Krishna declare: "I am the Ashvattha among trees."
- **Buddhist earliest iconographic**: The **Bharhut stupa** (c. -125 BCE) and **Sanchi stupa** (c. -250 to -100 BCE) depict the enlightenment scene with an empty throne (*vajrasāna*) beneath the Bodhi Tree — the Buddha is NOT shown in human form. The tree IS the Buddha.
- **Sri Maha Bodhi planting**: Emperor [[asoka-maurya]]'s daughter Sanghamitta carries a cutting to Sri Lanka c. -236 BCE (per the *Mahāvaṃsa*). This is the **oldest historically documented living tree** in the world with continuous cultivation records.

## Meaning(s) attested

**In early Buddhist texts and art:**
- **Locus of enlightenment**: The tree is the site where dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*), the Four Noble Truths, and the Eightfold Path were "discovered" — the tree IS the discovery (Gethin 1998).
- **The *Vajrasāna* (Diamond Throne)**: The stone seat at the tree's base is indestructible (*vajra* = diamond) because the awakening that happened there cannot be undone. The tree's roots grip the *vajrasāna*; the *vajrasāna* grounds the tree.
- **Aniconic Buddha-substitute**: In early Buddhism, depicting the Buddha in human form was avoided; the Bodhi Tree (with the empty throne), the Dharma Wheel (*dharmachakra*), the footprints (*buddhapāda*), and the *stūpa* were the permitted icons of the absent presence. The tree = the Buddha = the Dharma.
- **Bodhi as "tree" and "awakening"**: The Sanskrit root *budh* (to awaken, to know) gives both *bodhi* (awakening) and the tree's name. The tree becomes a pun on awakening — the *bodhi*-tree is literally the "awakening-tree," and all sacred peepal trees in Buddhist countries inherit this meaning.

## Cross-family appearance

**The Eden parallel — inverted knowledge trees**: The *Genesis* Tree of Knowledge (*Etz haDa'at* — Tree of Knowledge of Good and Evil) and the Bodhi Tree are both "trees of knowledge" — one causes the Fall into self-consciousness and suffering; the other ends it. The structural opposition is exact: Adam and Eve eat and *lose* paradise; the Buddha sits and *gains* liberation. Both are trees under which the decisive cognitive transformation of human history occurs — one as descent, one as ascent. The comparison is one of the sharpest structural inversions in comparative religion.

**The Newton parallel — insight under a tree**: The (partly legendary) story of Newton's apple — sudden insight about gravity while seated under an apple tree — maps the same structural pattern: decisive cognitive transformation at/under a specific tree. Buddha under the Bodhi Tree → Newton under the apple tree → the tree as locus of illumination. This is not a claimed transmission but a pattern in how cultures narrate cognitive breakthrough.

**Yggdrasil parallel — wisdom through ordeal at the world tree**: Odin hangs on Yggdrasil for nine nights, wounded by a spear, and takes up the runes — enlightenment through suffering at the cosmic tree. The Buddha sits beneath the Bodhi Tree through the night of Mara's assault (the temptation/assault parallel to Odin's ordeal) and takes up the Dharma. Both: sole figure + cosmic tree + night of ordeal + wisdom received. No documented transmission; convergent cosmological patterning (see [[yggdrasil]]).

**Ashoka's world-tree network**: Emperor [[asoka-maurya]]'s propagation of Bodhi Tree cuttings across the Buddhist world (Sri Lanka, possibly Central Asia) is one of the earliest documented cases of sacred botany as missionary strategy — the tree itself *is* the message. The Sri Maha Bodhi at Anuradhapura (planted c. -236 BCE) is the oldest historically documented living tree with continuous cultivation records, predating all surviving European trees.

## Contested interpretations

- **Date of the enlightenment**: The traditional Theravāda date (-528/-563 BCE) and the "short chronology" date (-380/-368 BCE) place the Bodhi Tree event in different historical contexts. Both are scholarly working estimates; the original tree cannot be directly dated.
- **Continuity of the Bodh Gaya tree**: The original tree died (reportedly destroyed by the hostile Śaivaite king Puṣyamitra Śuṅga, c. -180 BCE, per tradition, though this is disputed). The current tree is a cutting-descendant; some scholars are skeptical of continuous direct lineage. The Sri Maha Bodhi's continuity is better documented.
- **Ashvattha identity**: The equation of the Buddhist Bodhi Tree (*Ficus religiosa* in its specific Bodh Gaya instance) with the Hindu Ashvattha (the general peepal species) is deliberate in the tradition but deserves scholarly nuance: all peepal trees are Ashvattha; not all Ashvattha trees are the Bodhi Tree.
