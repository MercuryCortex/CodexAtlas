## What a Sigil Is

From Latin *sigillum* (diminutive of *signum* — a seal, a sign). In magical tradition: a visual symbol of specific intent or identity, condensed to a pure graphic form for ritual use. The word covers two practices so different they deserve separate entries — but history has given them one name.

### The Grimoire Tradition: Received Seals (c. 800–present)

In the medieval and Renaissance grimoire tradition, a sigil is the true visual name of a specific spirit — a seal that identifies and binds it. These sigils are not invented; they are transmitted through magical literature. To hold the sigil of a spirit is to hold power over it; to compel it to appear, you trace its seal in the air or inscribe it on a laminate or parchment.

The *Ars Goetia* (the first book of the *Lemegeton* or *Lesser Key of Solomon*, 17th c.) provides 72 unique seals for 72 demonic hierarchs. These are among the most elaborately documented spirit-seals in the Western tradition. The Hebrew and Arabic magical traditions contributing to this synthesis include *Shemot* (divine and angelic names) theology, whereby names carry intrinsic power — a logic shared with the Tetragrammaton tradition.

### The Spare Tradition: Invented Sigils (1913–present)

Austin Osman Spare in *The Book of Pleasure* (1913) invented an entirely different method. The practitioner:
1. Writes a statement of intent ("I WISH TO FIND WHAT I NEED")
2. Removes repeated letters and all vowels
3. Rearranges the remaining letters into an abstract symbol
4. Charges the symbol (via orgasm, trance, laughter, exhaustion — any state of gnosis)
5. **Forgets the sigil** — this is the operative step. Conscious desire interferes with the unconscious process; the sigil must be placed in the unconscious and the conscious connection severed.

Each Spare sigil is unique to one intent. There is no library, no tradition of transmission — the method is the point, not the product. This is maximally individual and maximally unrepeatable.

### The Bridge: The LBRP

The Golden Dawn's Lesser Banishing Ritual of the Pentagram draws sigils of archangels in the air at the cardinal directions. These sigils are derived via the rose-cross lamen — a 22-Hebrew-letter grid on which spirit-names are traced as paths, producing a unique seal for each name. The LBRP thus holds both traditions: grimoire sigils (fixed, received, traditional angelic forms) activated through Spare-style charged gesture (will + breath + visualized flaming light). The modern ceremonial magician using the LBRP is working both methods simultaneously without necessarily knowing it.

### The Grant Morrison Moment

Morrison's *The Invisibles* (1994–2000) is the first mass-media sigil-magic manual. In the final issue, Morrison directed readers to perform a specific sigilization (drawn from Spare's method) during reading — the comic panels became the charging mechanism. An estimated 100,000+ readers participated in a collective chaos-magic working without being certain it was 'real.' This is the moment sigilization escaped the occult subculture and entered mainstream cultural production.
