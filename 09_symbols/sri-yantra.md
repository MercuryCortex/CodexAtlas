# Sri Yantra

## Identity
The **Sri Yantra** (Sanskrit: *श्री यन्त्र*, "the auspicious instrument") is the supreme yantra of the **Shakta Tantric tradition** — a cosmological diagram consisting of **nine interlocking triangles** radiating from a central Bindu point, surrounded by two rings of lotus petals (16-petal outer, 8-petal inner) and enclosed in three concentric square frames (the **Bhūpura**). Four triangles point upward (*Shiva*, masculine, fire, consciousness) and five point downward (*Shakti*, feminine, water, matter/energy); their interlocking produces **43 smaller triangular sub-regions** arranged in four concentric circuits. The figure is simultaneously a cosmological diagram of universal emanation, a bodily map of the goddess **Lalitā Tripurasundarī** ("the beautiful lady of the three cities"), a meditational yantra, and — in Tantric doctrine — the visual body of the Om syllable and the Panchadashi mantra.

## Earliest attestation
- **Textual substrate**: The yantra-system is continuous with the Vedic *maṇḍala* altar-geometry (the Sulbasutras, c. -800 to -600) and the Upanishadic *nada brahman* (sound-as-cosmos) doctrine ([[phase-2-012-brihadaranyaka-upanishad]], [[phase-2-013-chandogya-upanishad]]).
- **The Tantric Sri Yantra** as a named, distinct symbol is attested in the Srividya corpus from approximately **600 CE** onward.
- **The *Saundarya Laharī*** ("Wave of Beauty"), attributed to Adi Shankaracharya ([[shankara]], c. 788–820 CE), is the earliest named literary source explicitly describing the Sri Yantra as the body of the goddess; some dating pushes this to the 9th–10th c. CE regardless of attribution.
- **The *Paraśurāma-kalpasūtra*** (c. 10th–12th c. CE) gives the fullest ritual prescriptions for Sri Yantra construction and worship in the Srividya lineage.
- **Archaeological**: Inscribed examples on copper, silver, and gold plates survive from c. 8th–10th c. CE onward; the three-dimensional Meru form is documented in temple contexts from the same period.

## Meaning(s) attested
- **Cosmological emanation**: The Sri Yantra encodes the entire process of cosmic emanation from undifferentiated consciousness (the Bindu) through progressive differentiation into matter. Reading from center outward = the path of creation (*srishti*); reading from outside inward = the path of resorption (*pralaya*) and liberation. The 43 sub-triangles are identified with specific aspects of reality (the 43 aspects of Shakti).
- **Deity body**: In Srividya practice, the Sri Yantra IS the body of Lalitā Tripurasundarī — the goddess is not merely depicted in the yantra but IS geometrically present as the yantra's form. Each of the 43 triangular sub-regions is inhabited by a specific aspect of Lalitā as a sub-deity (*avarana-devata*).
- **Mantra-visual identity**: Tantric doctrine claims that the Panchadashi mantra and the Sri Yantra are the same reality in sonic and geometric registers respectively. The *mantra-yantra-murti* triad (sound / diagram / image) is the triple body of the goddess.
- **Bindu as absolute**: The central Bindu point represents the primordial undifferentiated state before differentiation — simultaneous consciousness (Shiva) and power (Shakti) in unity, equivalent to the *turiya* state beyond the three states of waking/dreaming/deep sleep.
- **Liberation instrument**: In Sri Vidya ritual, the adept meditates the yantra from circumference inward to the Bindu, enacting the reabsorption of the differentiated cosmos into the absolute — a geometric path of liberation (*mukti*).

## Variants
See YAML: the flat Bhupura form (standard meditation and worship), the three-dimensional Meru/Shumeru pyramid (preferred ritual form in Srividya), the Bindu-point as standalone contemplation object, and the mantra-visual-body doctrine.

## Cross-family appearance
The Sri Yantra is **specifically Shakta Tantric Hindu in origin**. Its doctrines of mantra-yantra identity and divine geometric emanation influenced the broader **Vajrayana Buddhist** mandala tradition (which shares the Tantric substrate, 6th–12th c. CE), but the Sri Yantra as such is not documented in Buddhist texts.

**Cross-tradition structural parallels (no documented transmission):**
- The **Star of David / hexagram** ([[hexagram]]) encodes the same two-interlocked-triangles masculine/feminine opposites-union logic, independently, in Jewish mystical and Islamic geometric contexts.
- **Gothic cathedral sacred geometry** (Chartres, Notre Dame) encodes the golden ratio φ through vesica-piscis and circle overlays — the same φ-structuring principle documented in the Sri Yantra's proportions (Lawlor 1982). No transmission.
- The **Kabbalistic Tree of Life** ([[sefirot-tree-of-life]]) is a parallel cosmological emanation diagram: Ein Sof → 10 sefirot as Sri Yantra's Bindu → 43 sub-regions. Both are simultaneously cosmological maps and meditational deity-body diagrams.

## Contested interpretations
- **Cymatic Sri Yantra**: A widely circulated modern claim holds that the sound of Om, visualized via cymatic apparatus (vibrating plate + fine powder), generates the Sri Yantra geometry. This claim was popularized by Yogani and the New Age reception (Tier-3/4). No peer-reviewed acoustic science has replicated it consistently. The Tantric texts DO claim the identity of sonic and visual form (*mantra = yantra*), but as a doctrinal assertion, not an acoustic-physics claim.
- **Dating the Sri Yantra**: The Saundarya Laharī attribution to Shankaracharya (788–820 CE) is contested; critical scholarship (Brooks 1992) assigns portions to later redactors. The *Paraśurāma-kalpasūtra* dating to the 10th–12th century is more secure.
- **Golden ratio claim**: The proportional encoding of φ in the Sri Yantra's geometry is documented by Lawlor (1982) and by independent mathematical analysis; the *extent* to which this was consciously intended by the constructors vs. emerging from the geometric construction remains debated.
- **Relation to Vedic altar geometry**: The continuity of the Sri Yantra with the Sulbasutra Vedic ritual altar geometry (which also encodes sacred proportions in two-dimensional diagrams) is debated. Zimmer (1946) argues for deep continuity; more recent Indological scholarship is cautious.
