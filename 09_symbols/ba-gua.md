# Bā Guà (Eight Trigrams)

## Identity
The **Bā Guà** (八卦 — "Eight Trigrams") are the eight fundamental symbolic units of the **[[phase-2-042-yi-jing-i-ching|I Ching]]** (易經, "Book of Changes") — the eight possible combinations of three broken yin (⚋) and unbroken yang (⚊) lines. They are: ☰ Qián (Heaven, 天), ☷ Kūn (Earth, 地), ☳ Zhèn (Thunder, 雷), ☴ Xùn (Wind/Wood, 風), ☵ Kǎn (Water, 水), ☶ Gěn (Mountain, 山), ☲ Lí (Fire, 火), ☱ Duì (Lake, 澤). Each trigram is simultaneously a natural phenomenon, a direction, a family member, a body part, an animal, a cosmic principle, and a moment in the cycle of transformation. Arranged as 64 hexagrams (all possible pairs of two trigrams), they form the complete vocabulary for mapping every possible state of the cosmos in transformation — a system that turns out to be structurally identical to 6-bit binary code.

## Earliest attestation
- **Legendary origin**: The trigrams are attributed to the culture-hero [[fuxi]] (c. -2800 BCE in tradition). The mythological frame is that Fuxi observed the patterns on the back of a tortoise emerging from the Yellow River (*hetu*, 河圖) and discerned the eight trigrams in those markings.
- **Historical attribution**: The 64-hexagram system and the core text of the *I Ching* are attributed to King Wen of Zhou (c. -1000 BCE; "Judgments") and the Duke of Zhou (his son; "Lines"). The philosophical commentaries (*Ten Wings*, 十翼) are attributed to Confucius (c. -500 BCE) in tradition; modern scholarship dates them c. -300 to -100 BCE.
- **Archaeological**: Oracle bone divination (jiaguwen, 甲骨文) from the Shang dynasty (c. -1200 BCE) attests to systematic divination and numerical notation at Anyang, but does not yet use the trigram system in its mature form.
- **First coherent text**: The received *Zhou Yi* (周易 — the "Changes of Zhou") with the Ten Wings commentaries: c. -300 to -100 BCE (Smith 2012).

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Cosmological map of change**: The primary meaning of the Bā Guà system is that the cosmos is fundamentally constituted by change (*yì*, 易) and that all possible states of change can be mapped onto the 64 hexagrams. The I Ching is not primarily a fortune-telling book but a **cosmological grammar** of transformation (Wilhelm & Baynes 1950 Introduction).
- **Yin and yang dynamics**: Each line is either yin (broken — receptive, feminine, moon, earth) or yang (unbroken — active, masculine, sun, heaven). The eight trigrams are the eight stable structures that emerge from the interaction of yin and yang in groups of three.
- **Directional cosmology**: In the Post-Heaven arrangement (King Wen), the eight trigrams are assigned to the eight compass directions and map cosmic energies onto space. This is the foundation of *feng shui* (風水, "wind-water") — the Chinese art of harmonizing human environments with cosmic directional forces.
- **Divination**: The hexagrams are generated by casting yarrow stalks (the classical method) or coins (the simplified modern method), recording which lines are yin, yang, "old yin" (changing to yang), or "old yang" (changing to yin). The resulting hexagram and its "changing lines" are then interpreted for the querent's situation.

## Cross-family appearance

### MASSIVE WIN 1 — Leibniz and Binary Code
In 1701, the French Jesuit missionary **Joachim Bouvet** sent Leibniz a diagram of the 64 hexagrams of the I Ching. Leibniz had already (independently) discovered the binary number system (0 and 1) by 1679. Upon seeing the hexagram diagram, Leibniz immediately recognized — and wrote to Bouvet in 1703 — that the 64 hexagrams were his binary system: the broken yin line (⚋) = 0; the unbroken yang line (⚊) = 1; every hexagram is a unique 6-bit binary number from 000000 (☷ Kūn, pure yin) to 111111 (☰ Qián, pure yang). Leibniz published this recognition in his **"Explication de l'Arithmétique Binaire"** (1703) in the *Mémoires de l'Académie Royale des Sciences* — a PRIMARY SOURCE documenting one of the most extraordinary convergences in intellectual history: the I Ching of c. -1000 BCE = the mathematical foundation of modern computing (Smith 2012, pp. 154-162). Binary code — the universal language of all digital computation — was independently discovered in Bronze Age China.

### MASSIVE WIN 2 — Yoruba Ifá Divination System
The **Yoruba Ifá divination system** (West Africa, tradition at least several centuries old before European contact) uses a binary notation system called *Odù Ifá*: the diviner casts palm nuts or a divining chain (*opele*) and records whether the result is odd (single mark: |) or even (double mark: ||) — binary. Eight binary pairs produce 256 Odù figures (2^8 = 256, compared to I Ching's 2^6 = 64 hexagrams). The **structural identity** is complete: binary notation → systematic casting → closed system of symbolic figures → cosmological and divinatory interpretation. No documented transmission between West Africa and China. Bascom (1969) documents the Ifá system; Eglash (1999) explicitly addresses the mathematical parallel as independent convergence. This is the same mathematical solution — binary notation for mapping all possible states of the cosmos — independently discovered in China and West Africa. UNESCO proclaimed the Ifá system an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (2005).

### MASSIVE WIN 3 — Quantum Complementarity and Niels Bohr
**Niels Bohr**, upon being awarded the Danish Order of the Elephant (1947), designed his own coat of arms incorporating the **yin-yang symbol (Taijitu)** with the motto *Contraria sunt complementa* ("Opposites are complementary"). Bohr explicitly understood his **principle of complementarity** (wave/particle duality — a quantum system cannot be observed as both wave and particle simultaneously; both descriptions are necessary, neither is sufficient alone) as structurally parallel to the Taoist philosophy underlying the Bā Guà. The yin-yang / I Ching principle that opposites are complementary aspects of a unitary dynamic — which the Bā Guà systematizes — became, via Bohr's self-interpretation, a canonical metaphor for quantum mechanics. (Smith 2012, p. 227; widely documented in Bohr scholarship.)

### MASSIVE WIN 4 — Carl Gustav Jung and Synchronicity
**C.G. Jung** wrote the foreword to the Wilhelm-Baynes English translation (1950) and co-developed (with physicist Wolfgang Pauli) the concept of **synchronicity** — the non-causal coincidence of meaningfully connected events — partly in dialogue with the I Ching. Jung used the I Ching experimentally for years and understood its divination procedure as a technology for registering synchronistic patterns invisible to causal analysis. The I Ching / synchronicity connection entered the psychoanalytic tradition via [[carl-gustav-jung]] and became a significant vector of I Ching reception in the 20th-century West.

### Feng Shui and Sacred Space Orientation
The Bā Guà's mapping of the eight trigrams onto the eight directions — the cosmological grammar of the Post-Heaven arrangement — connects to: the Buddhist **mandala**'s directional cosmology ([[mandala-circle]]); the compass rose as orientation symbol; medieval Christian *mappamundi* (maps oriented East toward Jerusalem); and the Islamic *qibla* (directional orientation toward Mecca). All are systems for encoding cosmic quality into spatial direction, though no transmission between the Chinese and Western forms is documented.

## Contested interpretations
- **Fuxi and mythological origins**: The attribution of the trigrams to the legendary culture-hero Fuxi (c. -2800 BCE) is traditional, not historical. Modern scholarship dates the I Ching system to the early Zhou dynasty (c. -1000 to -800 BCE) and understands the Fuxi legend as a retrospective cosmological myth.
- **Leibniz priority**: Some Chinese nationalist scholarship has claimed that Leibniz "stole" or "derived" his binary system from the I Ching via Bouvet. This is historically untenable: Leibniz's binary papers predate his receipt of Bouvet's diagram by 22 years. The correct formulation (Smith 2012) is **independent parallel discovery** + **Leibniz's recognition** of the convergence.
- **I Ching as divination vs. philosophy**: Western reception has divided between treating the I Ching primarily as a divinatory text (John Blofeld; popular use) and primarily as a philosophical classic (*The Great Treatise*, Xì Cí 系辭, the Ten Wings). Confucian tradition emphasized the philosophical dimension; the full text integrates both.
