# Spiral

> **Methodology discipline note:** The spiral is the case study for the discipline against naïve diffusionism. The single spiral is one of the most universally attested decorative-cosmological forms in human iconography — Newgrange, Maltese temples, Celtic Insular, Hopi sand-painting, Australian Aboriginal Dreaming-art, Andean geoglyphs. Most of these are **independent attestations**, NOT transmission. The default edge is `parallel-form` for related-megalithic-vocabulary cases (Newgrange ↔ Malta ↔ Celtic) and `visual-cognate` for clearly independent attestations (Pueblo, Aboriginal, Andean). NO `ancestor-of` across continents is documented for this symbol.

## Identity

The spiral is the curve traced by a point continuously moving away from (or towards) a centre while rotating around it. As iconographic form it appears in two main varieties: the **single spiral** (one continuous curve from centre outward) and the **double spiral** (two opposed spirals sharing a centre or meeting at a midpoint). Geometric variants — the meander (rectangular-spiral, the Greek-key), the koru (Māori unfurling-frond), the volute (architectural spiral in Greek Ionic capitals) — are related-but-distinct iconographic vocabularies. The spiral is one of the most-attested decorative-cosmological forms in human iconography; most attestations are independent inventions, not transmissions.

## Earliest attestation

- **c. -10000 onward** — incidental spiral and curvilinear forms in Upper Palaeolithic art (Mezin, Ukraine; Lascaux, France; cave-art generally). Not consistent enough to constitute a documented tradition.
- **c. -3600 to -2500 BCE** — Maltese temple complex (Ġgantija, Tarxien, Hagar Qim): elaborate spiral relief-carving as central decorative-cosmological vocabulary. Earliest sustained iconographic-tradition attestation.
- **c. -3500 to -3000 BCE** — Atlantic European megalithic art (Brittany Gavrinis, Newgrange, Knowth, Dowth in Ireland). The Newgrange entrance-stone (c. -3200) is the iconic exemplar.
- **c. -2000 onward** — Bronze Age and Iron Age European decorative metalwork inherits the spiral.
- **Minoan (c. -1700 to -1450)** — Minoan palace frescoes and pottery use the spiral as canonical decorative form.
- **Greek Geometric period (c. -900 to -700)** — the Greek meander crystallizes on Geometric-style pottery; closely related rectangular-spiral form.
- **Celtic La Tène (c. -500 onward)** — the spiral and triple-spiral are canonical motifs.
- **Native American — Hopi, Pueblo, Anasazi** — independent New World attestation; Hopi sand-painting and Pueblo rock-art use spiral forms continuously through ethnographic-present.
- **Andean (Chavín, Paracas, Nazca, c. -1200 to 600 CE)** — pre-Columbian Andean iconography includes spirals; the Nazca geoglyphs include large-scale geoglyphic spirals.
- **Australian Aboriginal** — concentric-circle and spiral conventions in Dreaming-art are documented in 19th- and 20th-c. ethnographic record; specific conventions (waterhole, camp, songline-marker) are tradition-specific. (Strehlow 1947; Morphy 1998.)

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Atlantic European Neolithic** — meaning irrecoverable in textual sense; functional contexts (passage-tombs, temple-thresholds) suggest cosmological / liminal / cyclic readings without proving specifics.
- **Maltese temples** — same caveat as Atlantic European; sustained temple-context implies sacred function without textual specification.
- **Celtic La Tène** — generic decorative-cosmological; later medieval Christian Insular use overlays Christian readings.
- **Greek meander** — generic decorative; sometimes read as labyrinth / river-flow / continuous-motion emblem.
- **Hopi / Pueblo** — journey / migration / emergence from the lower worlds (Hopi tradition; published with elder community consent).
- **Andean** — varies by culture; Nazca-line geoglyphs are debated in interpretation (ritual-procession path vs. astronomical alignment).
- **Australian Aboriginal** — tradition-specific conventional meanings (waterhole, camp, songline-marker) embedded in Country and Dreaming-tradition. Restricted-knowledge contexts apply.

## Variants

The six attested variants (Neolithic megalithic, Celtic La Tène, Greek meander, Hopi/Pueblo, Aboriginal, Andean) are documented in YAML. The double-spiral and triple-spiral are derivable from the single-spiral vocabulary.

## Cross-family appearance

The spiral is **multiply independently attested** across at least three macro-regions (Atlantic European Neolithic, New World, Australia / Pacific). The conservative reading is **independent invention** — the spiral is a natural product of decorative-vocabulary development and was reinvented many times. Transmission claims are documented only at intra-regional scale:

- **Atlantic European Neolithic → Celtic Iron Age → Insular Christian art** — `parallel-form` chain at the level of iconographic inheritance within Atlantic Europe.
- **Malta megalithic vs. Atlantic European megalithic** — `parallel-form`; possibly related via shared Neolithic Mediterranean / Atlantic seafaring contact but not document-specific.
- **Hopi vs. Aboriginal vs. Andean vs. Atlantic European** — `visual-cognate` only. NO documented transmission. These are independent attestations of a natural geometric form.

## Contested interpretations

- The Gimbutas (1989) interpretive thesis that Neolithic European spirals are evidence of a unified "Mother-Goddess" matristic civilization — **contested** per Meskell 1995 (*American Anthropologist*) and subsequent gender-archaeology critique. Gimbutas's iconographic catalog is more solidly grounded than her interpretive synthesis.
- Whether Neolithic spirals encode astronomical / solar / cyclic readings. The Newgrange winter-solstice illumination is documented; broader cosmological readings are interpretive.
- The relationship between the Greek meander and the spiral proper — formally distinct but iconographically related.

## Mystery / unknown variants

Most Neolithic spiral attestations have meanings irrecoverable in the absence of textual record. `mystery-status: documented` overall but with strong caveats on Neolithic interpretation.

## Cross-symbol edges (provisional)
