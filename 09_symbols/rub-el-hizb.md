# Rub el Hizb (Islamic Eight-pointed Star)

## Identity
The **Rub el Hizb** (Arabic: رُبْع الْحِزْب — "quarter of a section") is an **8-pointed star** formed by two overlapping squares rotated 45° against each other. It functions simultaneously as a **typographical glyph within the Quran** (marking the end of each quarter-section of the Quranic recitation-division system) and as one of the most recognizable **emblems of Islamic visual culture** — appearing on national flags, mosque ornaments, and across the *girih* geometric tile tradition.

The Rub el Hizb is the **bridge between two Islamic sacred symbol families**: the **textual/navigational** (Quranic division marks) and the **geometric/aesthetic** (*girih* tile geometry and Islamic architectural decoration).

## The Quranic Division System
The [[phase-4-034-quran|Quran]] is structured into layered divisions for systematic recitation by observant Muslims:

- **30 *juzʾ*** (أَجْزَاء — "parts") — the primary division; each *juzʾ* is approximately equal in length; reciting one per day covers the full Quran in 30 days (the length of Ramadan).
- **2 *ḥizb*** per *juzʾ* = **60 *ḥizb*** total.
- **4 *rub' al-ḥizb*** per *ḥizb* = **240 *rub' al-ḥizb*** total — the quarter-section marked by the Rub el Hizb glyph.

The glyph appears **240 times** in a complete Quran, enabling readers to track their position in the recitation cycle without interrupting the Arabic text. It is one of the earliest forms of **sacred typographic navigation** — the equivalent of a chapter-and-verse system, but rooted in recitation practice rather than content divisions.

## Geometry
The form — two squares, one rotated 45° relative to the other, overlapping at center — produces:
- **8 outer points** (the star-points)
- **A central octagonal space**
- **8 intersecting rhombic cells** at the intermediate positions

This is identical to the geometry of the **8-pointed star tile unit** in the [[arabesque-girih|*girih* tile system]] — the Islamic architectural geometric ornament tradition. The Rub el Hizb, the *girih* star-polygon, and the broader family of Islamic 8-pointed star ornaments (mosaic work, Quranic illumination borders, muqarnas vaulting cells) all derive from the same fundamental geometry: the *murabbaʿ* (square) doubled through 45° rotation.

## The 8-Pointed Star Cross-Tradition
The 8-pointed star is one of the most globally distributed star-forms in world religious iconography:

- **[[star-of-ishtar]]** (Mesopotamia, c. -2350 BCE): The oldest continuously-attested 8-pointed star — the emblem of Inanna/Ishtar, goddess of love and the planet Venus. The 8 points encode the Venus 8-year synodic cycle.
- **Rub el Hizb** (Islamic, 650 CE onward): The Islamic 8-pointed star in its typographic-navigational and decorative-geometric registers.
- **Star of Lakshmi** (Hindu): The *ashtakona* — an 8-pointed star form in Hindu yantra geometry representing the 8 forms of Lakshmi.
- **Christian octagram** (Early Christian baptisteries): The 8-sided baptismal font form expressing the "eighth day" — resurrection as the day beyond the 7-day week of creation; baptism as participation in the resurrection.

Each tradition arrives at the 8-pointed star independently through its own logic (astronomical observation; geometric tile mathematics; doctrinal numerology), yet converges on the same form — one of the most arresting cross-tradition convergences in sacred geometry.

## Islamic Heraldry and Flags
The Rub el Hizb and the broader 8-pointed star appear on the emblems and flags of multiple Muslim-majority countries. The 8-pointed star (alongside the [[crescent-moon-star]]) is the dominant geometric emblem form in Islamic heraldic tradition — more common than any other star-polygon. The visual vocabulary of Islamic sovereignty encodes Quranic geometry at the state level.

- ↔ [[star-of-ishtar]] — *parallel-form*. Both 8-pointed stars in Near-Eastern cultural space; no documented direct transmission but shared geometric form across the Mesopotamian-to-Islamic continuum.
