# Wampum

## Identity

Wampum (from Algonquian *wampumpeag*, "white string of shell beads") are cylindrical shell beads manufactured from two species native to Northeastern American coastal waters: white beads from the columella of the whelk (*Busycon carica*) and purple-black beads from the quahog clam (*Mercenaria mercenaria*). The beads are drilled, polished, and strung on hemp cord or woven into rectangular belts using a backstrap loom. The contrast between white (associated with peace, health, life) and purple-black (associated with mourning, seriousness, death) is the fundamental color-grammar of wampum's semiotic system.

Wampum functions simultaneously as: (1) sacred diplomatic document encoding treaty terms, alliance agreements, and governmental decisions; (2) ceremonial object used in the Condolence Ceremony to heal mourning nations and install new leaders; (3) mnemonic device for oratory — the belt's pattern guides the recitation of the associated oral text. The widespread equation of wampum with "Indian money" is a post-European-contact misreading: wampum became a colonial currency only because European demand for shell beads disrupted the symbol's sacred-diplomatic function, creating a market in objects whose original value was entirely non-monetary.

## Earliest attestation

- **Archaeological**: Shell-bead ornaments from Northeastern North American coastal sites date to approximately -1000 BCE (archaic-period shell middens); the specific cylindrical drilled form and belt-weaving technology appears in the late Woodland period (c. 1000–1300 CE).
- **Documentary**: European contact-period observations (16th–17th c. CE) provide the first written records; Samuel de Champlain (1615) and Roger Williams (*A Key into the Language of America*, 1643) provide early ethnographic descriptions. The wampum tradition was already ancient at European contact.
- **The Hiawatha Belt**: dates to the founding of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy, traditionally c. 1450 CE (see [[event-great-law-of-peace-haudenosaunee-c1450]]). The belt is currently held by the Onondaga Nation (the confederacy's "firekeepers") and is considered its living legal constitution.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **White beads**: peace, health, positive outcomes, clarity of mind, life — the "clear" state that diplomatic communication requires.
- **Purple-black beads**: mourning, seriousness, death, grief — the "clouded" state that the Condolence Ceremony must clear.
- **The Hiawatha Belt's iconographic program**: four white squares = the four founding nations (Mohawk / Oneida / Cayuga / Seneca); white diagonal connecting band = the path of communication and unity between nations; central figure at the Onondaga position = the Great Tree of Peace (a white pine, symbol of the confederation). The design encodes the entire political theology of the Great Law: unity from diversity, peace through confederation, with the Onondaga as the central fire.
- **Condolence strings**: individual wampum strings passed from one nation to another during the Condolence Ceremony "clear the eyes, ears, and throat" of the grieving nation — each string materializes one restorative speech act. The ceremony is both a mourning protocol and an installation ceremony: it "requickens" the dead leader's title by seating a new chief in his place.

## Variants

### Wampum Belt (Kaswentha / Two-Row Wampum)
The Two-Row Wampum Belt (*Tawagonshi Treaty*, 1613, though the date is debated by scholars) encodes the principle of coexistence between the Haudenosaunee and Dutch (later applied to all European-American relationships): two parallel rows of purple beads on a white background represent two canoes traveling the same river without interfering with each other. This is one of the earliest documented treaty frameworks based on mutual non-interference and separate sovereignties.

### Condolence Strings
At the death of a chief (sachem), the allied nations convene a Condolence Ceremony (*At-ha-wen-tsen-ton-ta*). The bereaved nation is considered spiritually disabled — unable to see, hear, or speak clearly because of grief. The requickening nation passes wampum strings while reciting three acts of restoration: wiping the tears (white strings), unstopping the ears (white strings), clearing the throat (white strings). The ceremony typically concludes with installing a new chief in the dead leader's title — the person literally becomes the title, continuing the line.

## Cross-family appearance

**Quipu comparison (Andean — parallel-form, no documented transmission)**: The Andean quipu (Quechua: *khipu*) is the closest functional parallel to wampum in the non-phonographic recording tradition. Quipu encode information through knot type, knot position, cord color, and cord grouping on a pendant-cord framework; trained quipu-readers (*quipucamayoc*) could decode census records, tribute accounts, and historical narratives from the physical arrangement. Like wampum, quipu were non-phonographic (they do not represent sounds); like wampum, they served governmental and sacred functions. Both represent independent indigenous American developments of the concept of encoding complex information in physical material arrangement that requires trained interpretive reading. **No documented transmission between Northeastern North America and Andean South America** — the parallel-form is convergent, not diffusionary.

**US Constitution scholarly debate (Tier-2 / contested)**: Scholars including Bruce Johansen (*Forgotten Founders*, 1982) and Donald Grinde and Bruce Johansen (*Exemplar of Liberty*, 1991) have argued that the Haudenosaunee Confederacy's governmental structure (balance of powers, representation, federal principle, consensus-building, Great Council as bicameral body) influenced American Founders including Benjamin Franklin (Albany Plan of Union, 1754) and Thomas Jefferson. In 1988, the US Senate passed Resolution 76 acknowledging Haudenosaunee influence on the Constitution. However, historian Elisabeth Tooker (*Ethnohistory*, 1988) disputes the direct-influence claim as overstated, while acknowledging Haudenosaunee governmental sophistication. This remains an active scholarly debate. The wampum record (Hiawatha Belt, Two-Row Belt) constitutes primary documentary evidence of the Haudenosaunee governmental philosophy regardless of its influence on American founders.

## Contested interpretations

- **Wampum-as-writing-system debate**: Some scholars (Haas 1976; Lainey 2004) argue wampum constitutes a "writing system" because it encodes information in graphic form that trained readers can decode; others (Mallery 1893; Gelb 1963) reserve "writing" for phonographic systems. The Codex Atlas position: wampum is a legitimate recording and communication technology. Whether it qualifies as "writing" depends on the definition, not on the cultural value of the information encoded.
- **Currency origins**: Colonial-era historians often described wampum as "Indian money" that Europeans adopted. Speck (1919), Ceci (1982), and Jennings (1984) have collectively demonstrated that the currency function was created by European demand disrupting the pre-existing sacred-diplomatic system — not the reverse. The original function is diplomatic-ceremonial, not monetary.
