# Pelican-in-her-Piety

## Identity
The **Pelican-in-her-Piety** (*pellicanus in pietate*; also called *pelican vulning*) is the Christian iconographic emblem of a **pelican piercing her own breast to feed her young with her own blood**. Originating in the [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles|early-Christian]] **Physiologus** bestiary tradition (c. 200 CE), the pelican-vulning becomes one of the **principal Christian Eucharistic and resurrection emblems** of medieval and early-modern Latin Christianity, and is subsequently adopted as one of the canonical animal-emblems of **Renaissance Rosicrucian-alchemical iconography** — paired with the [[phoenix-bennu|phoenix]] as the *self-sacrificial-regenerative* and *fiery-regenerative* animal pair of the alchemical emblem-book tradition.

## Earliest attestation
- **c. 200 CE** — **Physiologus** (anonymous Greek-Christian bestiary; Curley 1979). The pelican-vulning narrative as Christian Christological-Eucharistic typology.
- **c. 1200** — medieval Latin bestiaries (Aberdeen Bestiary, Cambridge Bestiary, etc.) — the pelican-iconography fixed.
- **c. 1300–1500** — pelican-in-her-piety on liturgical-and-ecclesiastical objects across Latin Europe: tabernacles, ciboria, monstrances, altar-frontals, vestments. The pelican on Eucharistic vessels signifies *the consecrated host as Christ's blood feeding the faithful*.
- **c. 1617** — [[phase-6-035-maier-atalanta-fugiens|Maier *Atalanta Fugiens*]] uses the pelican as alchemical regenerative-emblem.
- **c. 1750 onward** — Masonic higher-degree integration: the **18th degree of the Scottish Rite** is the *Knight of the Pelican and Eagle, Sovereign Prince Rose Croix*. Pelican-and-rose-cross iconography becomes definitive for the Rosicrucian-Masonic synthesis.
- **1812** — Louisiana adopts the pelican-in-her-piety as state seal (drawing on the medieval Christian iconography). The flag explicitly shows the pelican feeding young with her own blood — one of the rare modern political-secular adoptions of a medieval-Christian-Eucharistic emblem.

## Meaning(s) attested
- **Physiologus / medieval Christian** — *Christological typology*: as the pelican-mother sacrifices her blood to revive her young, so Christ sacrificed his blood to revive humanity. *Eucharistic typology*: as the pelican feeds her young with her blood, so Christ feeds the faithful with his blood in the Eucharist. *Resurrection typology*: the third-day revival of the young is a typological parallel to Christ's third-day resurrection.
- **Renaissance alchemical** — *Regenerative-self-sacrificial emblem*: the alchemist's substance must be wounded (calcined, dissolved) to be regenerated. The literal-mechanical *pelican* (a circulating distillation-vessel with two side-arms returning the condensate to the body) is named for the bird-emblem.
- **Rosicrucian-Masonic** — the *Knight of the Pelican and Eagle, Sovereign Prince Rose Croix* (Scottish Rite 18°) makes the pelican explicitly Christological-Rosicrucian — Christ-on-the-cross-as-pelican-feeding-faithful.
- **Modern secular (Louisiana state symbol)** — generalized *self-sacrifice* and *care-for-young*.

## Cross-family appearance
The pelican-in-piety is **specifically Christian in origin** (Physiologus, c. 200 CE). The subsequent **Rosicrucian-alchemical appropriation** is the principal cross-family migration — and is *internal* to the broader Western Christian-Hermetic synthesis rather than a cross-religion transmission. **The Louisiana state-symbol adoption is a rare secular survival** of a medieval Christian iconographic motif.

## Contested interpretations
- **Whether the Physiologus story has any pre-Christian source.** Some scholars argue for a Hellenistic-Greek natural-history source (Aelian's *De Natura Animalium*); Curley 1979 treats the Physiologus's pelican-narrative as a substantially-Christian construction with possibly-Greek-natural-history substrate.
- **The literal pelican.** Real pelicans do *not* wound themselves to feed their young; the iconography is mythological rather than zoological. The folk-belief may have arisen from the pelican's habit of bending its head to its chest while feeding chicks regurgitated fish — visually resembling self-piercing.

## Cross-symbol edges (provisional)
- ↔ [[phoenix-bennu]] — *parallel-form*. Both Christian resurrection-and-Eucharistic animal-emblems; both alchemical regenerative-emblems. The phoenix is *fiery* regeneration; the pelican is *blood-sacrificial* regeneration. Paired in emblem-books.
- ↔ [[fish-ichthys]] — *parallel-form*. Both early-Christian zoomorphic Eucharistic identifying-symbols.
- ↔ [[ouroboros]] — *parallel-form*. Both alchemical self-sacrificial regeneration emblems; the pelican (self-wounding) and the ouroboros (self-consuming) are conceptually paired as the two principal animal-regeneration symbols in the Hermetic emblem-book tradition (Maier 1617, Khunrath).
- ↔ [[sun-disk]] — *parallel-form*. In alchemical reading the pelican's blood is solar regenerative energy; Maier's *Atalanta Fugiens* (1617) pairs pelican imagery with solar-alchemical themes. The pelican as solar-sacrificial emblem in the Hermetic tradition complements its primary Christian Eucharistic register.
