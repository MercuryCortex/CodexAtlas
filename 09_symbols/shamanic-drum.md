# Shamanic Drum

## Identity

The shamanic drum is the most globally distributed religious object in human history, attested across Siberia, Central Asia, the Arctic and Subarctic, Korea, the Himalayan region, the Great Plains of North America, and sub-Saharan Africa — wherever shamanistic religion (the practice of specialist spirit-mediators who journey between cosmic levels on behalf of their communities) has been documented. The drum is not merely an accompaniment to shamanic ritual: it *is* the vehicle for the shaman's journey. The drum beat is the sonic form of the World Tree — the axis mundi — and the act of drumming opens the path between cosmic levels.

The drum functions as a **cosmological diagram, a spirit canoe, and a portable axis mundi** simultaneously. Many Siberian and Sámi shaman drums have their cosmological structure painted directly onto the drum head: the World Tree at center, the three levels of the cosmos (upper world of spirits and celestial powers; middle world of human life; lower world of the dead), the spirit-helpers, and the journey routes. The drum head IS the cosmos in miniature. When the shaman drums, they enact the cosmological journey acoustically and kinetically.

## Earliest attestation

- **Paleolithic evidence**: Cave art at sites including Altamira (c. 35,000–15,000 BCE) and Lascaux (c. 17,000 BCE) depicts figures that some researchers interpret as shamanic practitioners, and percussion marks on cave walls and bone objects suggest ritual drumming. This identification is contested — the cave art is certainly not all shamanic, and the percussion interpretation of some objects is disputed.
- **Most cautious early attestation**: The earliest unambiguous archaeological evidence for frame drums is from ancient Mesopotamia (Sumerian period, c. 3000 BCE). However, this does not preclude earlier use — skin drums do not preserve well archaeologically.
- **Siberian ethnographic record**: Tungus (Evenki) shamanism is the first fully documented shamanic tradition with detailed drum use. Russian observers recorded Tungus shamanic ritual from the 17th century CE; Radloff's field documentation (1860s–1880s) is the earliest systematic account.
- **Mircea Eliade's synthesis** (1951/1964, *Shamanism: Archaic Techniques of Ecstasy*): Eliade conducted the first comprehensive cross-cultural comparison of shamanic drumming, documenting structural parallels across Siberian, Central Asian, North and South American, Arctic, and Asian traditions. This work is the foundational Tier 1 source for the drum's axis-mundi function, though subsequent scholars have critiqued Eliade's model (see Contested Interpretations).

## Meaning(s) attested

- **The drum as vehicle (axis mundi)**: Eliade (1964) documents that across Siberian and Central Asian traditions, the drum is explicitly called a "spirit canoe," a "horse," or a "boat" — it is the shamanic journey vehicle. The shaman does not merely drum *while* journeying; the drumming *is* the journey. The monotonous beat alters consciousness (inducing shamanic trance states documented in ethnographic and neurological research), and this alteration is described indigenously as movement through cosmic levels.
- **The drum head as cosmological map**: Manker's (1950) definitive study of Sámi shaman drums documents that the cosmological paintings on drum heads are not decorative — they are functional maps of the spirit world that the *noaidi* uses to orient the journey. The positioning of spirit-helpers, dangerous zones, and destination points on the drum head corresponds to the shaman's reported journey route.
- **The drum as living entity**: In Tungus shamanism, the drum has its own spirit (*ongon*). The drum is "born" through the ritual of its creation (the shaman must identify the tree from which the frame is made, often the World Tree or a tree struck by lightning), "lives" through use, and "dies" with the shaman (a shaman's drum is broken or buried with them). This is not a metaphor — the drum is treated as a person, not a tool.
- **The four-direction structure**: In Plains Indian traditions (Lakota, Cheyenne, Arapaho), the drum's round face corresponds to the four directions of the medicine wheel. The drum beat represents the heartbeat of Grandmother Earth. Drumming a round drum is drumming the cosmos. This is indigenously documented in oral traditions and in the accounts of medicine people.
- **Trance induction**: Modern neurological research (Neher 1962; Jilek 1982; Harner 1980) has documented that drumming at approximately 4–7 beats per second (theta-wave frequency) reliably induces altered states of consciousness in a significant proportion of subjects. Indigenous accounts of shamanic trance correlate with these neurological observations. The drum is a neurologically effective axis-mundi technology.
- **African parallels — the ngoma**: The West African *ngoma* (drum tradition of the Bantu-speaking peoples, particularly in Central and East Africa) performs analogous functions: the drums call spirits, mark sacred time, mediate between human and spirit worlds, and are treated as having their own spiritual power. The *ngoma* tradition is documented as extending across Bantu Africa from the Great Lakes region to South Africa. No transmission from Siberian shamanism is documented or plausible; this is a structural convergence.

### African talking drum (*dùndún*)
The West African *dùndún* (Yoruba hourglass tension drum) is linguistically distinct from percussion-trance drums — it encodes tonal language and transmits messages across distances. However, it also carries sacred status: the *dùndún* "talks" because language is divine. In Yoruba cosmology, speech and drum-speech are both forms of *àṣẹ* (divine power). The talking drum is a voice of the divine, not merely a communication tool.

### Vedic *dundubhi*
The Vedic *dundubhi* (war/festival drum) is mentioned in the Rig Veda (c. 1200 BCE) and Atharva Veda in ritual contexts — it is beaten to accompany sacrifice, to summon rain, and to drive away evil. The *dundubhi* drum beaten in the Vedic *rajasuya* (royal consecration) ritual is explicitly described as a cosmic boundary marker. While not a shamanic drum in the strict sense (Vedic religion is not shamanism), the *dundubhi*'s ritual function — cosmic summons, apotropaic sound, sacred marking — overlaps structurally with shamanic drum function.

## Cross-family appearance

The shamanic drum's cross-traditional distribution is its defining feature. The structural thesis of the node is convergent, not diffusionist: the drum performs the axis-mundi function independently across traditions because the acoustic and kinesthetic properties of drumming — rhythm that alters consciousness, a circular form that maps the cosmos, a resonant surface that encodes cosmological diagrams — make it a naturally suited vehicle for spirit-journey cosmologies.

The exceptions to independent convergence are:
- **Northeast Asian transmission**: Korean *mudang* shamanism shows documented cultural exchange with Tungus/Siberian shamanic practice — they share spirit concepts, drum types, and some ritual structures. This is historical diffusion, not convergence.
- **Central Asian → North American possibility**: Some scholars propose pre-Columbian transmission of shamanic complex (including drum practices) from Northeast Asia to North America via the Bering Strait land bridge. This is plausible but not specifically documented for drum practices vs. the broader shamanic complex.

## Contested interpretations

- **Eliade's universalism**: Eliade's *Shamanism* (1964) is the foundational text but has been significantly critiqued for: (1) constructing a universal "shamanism" that flattens genuine cultural differences; (2) privileging Siberian shamanism as the "archaic" form from which others derive (a form of primitivism); (3) using problematic sources and occasionally misreading field evidence. Contemporary shamanism scholars (Harvey 2003; Kehoe 2000) caution against treating Eliade's synthesis as ethnographic data. His comparative framework is valuable; his universalist claims require qualification.
- **Neurological reductionism**: The opposite error: reducing shamanic drumming to "trance induction technology" (Harner 1980's neo-shamanism model) discards the indigenous cosmological meaning. The drum is not *just* a trance machine; it is a living entity in a specific cosmological system. Neuroscience documents that drumming can induce altered states; it cannot document whether those states constitute spirit journeys in any non-trivial sense.
- **Paleolithic shamanism**: The claim that Upper Paleolithic cave art (Lascaux, Altamira) represents shamanic practice and that drum use is therefore 30,000+ years old is speculative. David Lewis-Williams's entoptic-neuropsychological model (2002) provides a mechanism for cave art as altered-state vision, but does not establish drumming as the induction method.

## Mystery / unknown variants

The origin of the three-level cosmos (upper/middle/lower world) that the shamanic drum maps is undocumented. Whether this cosmological structure is a human cognitive universal (as Eliade argued), a Central Asian original that diffused across Eurasia and the Americas, or an independent development in each tradition remains unresolved. The structural parallels across Siberia, Korea, the Americas, and the Himalayan region are striking; their explanation is contested.

## Cross-symbol edges (provisional)

- **Parallel-form → `axis-mundi`**: The drum head maps and acoustically enacts the axis mundi; the shaman's drum is a portable, sonic form of the World Tree. Eliade (1964) documents this across multiple independent traditions.
- **Syncretic-fusion → `medicine-wheel`**: In Plains Indian traditions the drum's round face and four-direction structure are explicitly identified with the medicine wheel cosmogram.
- **Parallel-form → `yggdrasil`**: Sámi noaidi drums and the Norse Yggdrasil tradition share the three-level cosmos mapped onto a vertical axis; the degree of mutual influence vs. convergence is debated.
- **Parallel-form → `bodhi-tree`**: Both the Bodhi Tree and the World Tree encoded in the shamanic drum represent cosmic axis as breakthrough point between ordinary and altered/enlightened consciousness.
