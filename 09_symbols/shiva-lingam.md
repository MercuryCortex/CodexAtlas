# Shiva Lingam

## Identity

The Shiva Lingam (Sanskrit: *liṅga* — mark, sign, phallus; also glossed as "that by which one is recognised") is the **primary sacred image of Śiva** in Hindu temple worship: an upright cylindrical stone shaft set in a flat disc-shaped base with a drainage spout. The shaft is the **lingam** (representing Śiva); the base is the **yoni** (representing Śakti, the divine creative power). Together the form encodes the complete Hindu cosmological process: pure static consciousness (Śiva) united with dynamic creative energy (Śakti) = the manifest world. The Shiva lingam is the **most numerous sacred image in South and Southeast Asian religion** — every Śiva temple, from Nepal to Bali, contains one as its primary cult object. It is simultaneously a natural form (cylindrical stones), a sculpted form, and a metaphysical form (the infinite cosmic pillar of light).

## Earliest attestation

- **Archaeological earliest**: Cylindrical stone objects from **Mohenjo-Daro** (Indus Valley civilization, c. 2600–1900 BCE) have been interpreted as proto-lingams — among the oldest known religious objects in South Asia. Scholarly consensus is that these are likely lingam precursors, though the direct continuity between Indus Valley religion and historical Shaivism remains debated.
- **Textual earliest**: The *Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad* ([[phase-2-021-shvetashvatara-upanishad]], c. 400–200 BCE) identifies Śiva as the cosmic personal God (Īśvara) — foundational for later Shaiva theology but does not explicitly describe the lingam form.
- **Inscriptional earliest**: Lingam worship is attested in Kushan-period inscriptions and coin iconography (c. 1st–3rd century CE).
- **Narrative earliest**: The *Śiva Purāṇa* (c. 5th–11th century CE) contains the definitive jyotirlinga myth: Śiva manifests as an infinite pillar of light to settle a dispute between Brahma (who claims supremacy by ascending to find its summit) and Viṣṇu (who descends as a boar to find its base). Neither succeeds. Śiva's infinite light-pillar = the lingam as cosmic axis beyond measure.

## Meaning(s) attested

Hindu theological sources articulate multiple simultaneous meanings:

- **Cosmic axis**: The jyotirlinga (lingam of light) as a pillar extending infinitely through all cosmic levels — above heaven, below the underworld. This is the **axis mundi** function: the lingam is the still center around which all existence revolves.
- **Sāṃkhya cosmology encoded**: Lingam (Puruṣa — pure consciousness, the masculine static principle) + Yoni (Prakṛti — creative nature, the feminine dynamic principle) = saṃsāra (manifest reality). This is the **complete Sāṃkhya ontology** compressed into a single sculptural form. See Kramrisch (1981).
- **Aniconic divinity**: The lingam is explicitly preferred over anthropomorphic images in certain Shaiva texts as being *closer* to the formless (nirguṇa) Brahman — the cylinder as a form that gestures toward formlessness. The svayambhu (self-arisen) natural-stone lingams are the most sacred precisely because they were not shaped by human hands.
- **Mark / sign**: The primary Sanskrit meaning of *liṅga* is not "phallus" but "sign" or "mark" — that by which the invisible is recognised. Kashmir Shaivism (Abhinavagupta, *Tantrāloka*, [[phase-5-014-abhinavagupta-tantraloka]]) develops this: the lingam is the *mark of Shiva* in the world, the trace of the absolute in matter.

## Variants

**Jyotirlinga**: The twelve shrines across India where Śiva is said to have manifested as a spontaneous lingam of light (*jyotis*). Each is a natural geographical feature — the Kedarnath lingam is an irregular rock formation; the Somnath lingam is a sculpted stone. The twelve are listed in the *Śiva Purāṇa* and constitute the primary pilgrimage circuit for Shaiva devotees.

**Mukhalinga**: Lingam with Śiva's face (or up to five faces representing his five aspects) carved on the shaft. Prominent in Cambodian Khmer sculpture — the Phnom Bakheng temple (c. 900 CE) centers on a mukhalinga symbolizing Mount Meru. This form transmitted Shaiva cosmology throughout Southeast Asia.

**Svayambhu-linga (self-arisen lingam)**: Naturally occurring cylindrical rock formations venerated as Śiva manifesting without human intervention. The highest grade of lingam sanctity — the Narmada River's smooth cylindrical stones (Narmadeshvara shilas) are widely distributed as svayambhu lingams for home worship.

**Bāṇaliṅga**: Smooth, elliptical stones from the Narmada River — self-arisen, without yoni base, used in personal worship. Ancient tradition; not made by human hands.

## Cross-family appearance

The sacred upright-shaft as cosmic axis / boundary marker / divine power is attested across independent traditions:

- **Egyptian Min**: Min (attested from Predynastic Egypt, c. 3100 BCE onward) is always depicted with an erect phallus — the oldest continuously attested phallic deity in world religion. Min's cult center (Koptos) makes him god of roads, desert fertility, and male generative power. The erected phallus as divine creative power is the Egyptian parallel to the lingam's generative-cosmic function.
- **Egyptian Osiris**: Osiris's dismemberment myth has a specific phallic dimension: when Isis reassembles Osiris's body, she finds every piece except the phallus (eaten by a Nile fish). She fashions a **golden replacement phallus** — making the phallic substitute the pivot of the Osirian resurrection mystery. Osiris's golden phallus is the closest Egyptian parallel to the cosmic significance of the lingam.
- **Greek Herms**: The boundary-marker *hermés* (herm) is a rectangular stone pillar topped by a head (originally Hermes') bearing an erect phallus — used as road markers and boundary-stones throughout ancient Greece. The upright-shaft-with-phallus as marker/boundary/axis is the Greek parallel to the lingam's boundary-and-axis function.
- **Canaanite maṣṣēbôt**: The standing stone (*maṣṣēbâ*, plural *maṣṣēbôt*) of ancient Canaan — upright stone pillars set up at sacred sites (Bethel, Shechem) as markers of divine presence. The Hebrew patriarchal tradition records their use before their condemnation in Deuteronomistic reform. Functionally parallel to the svayambhu lingam.

No documented historical transmission connects these traditions; they represent **parallel-form attestations** of the upright-stone-as-axis-and-power across unconnected civilizations.

## Contested interpretations

The primary scholarly debate concerns the **relationship between the Sanskrit term liṅga and the phallic object**:

1. **Phallic-primary reading**: The lingam is fundamentally a phallic symbol, and the Puranic texts that deny this (e.g., "the lingam is not a phallus but a mark") are apologetic bowdlerization by later Brahminical tradition uncomfortable with explicit sexuality. Doniger O'Flaherty (1973) develops this reading.
2. **Aniconic-primary reading**: The lingam is primarily an aniconic mark of the formless absolute (*nirguṇa Brahman*), and phallic associations — while present — are secondary. Kashmir Shaivism consistently treats the lingam as a semiotic sign, not a body part. Kramrisch (1981) develops this reading.
3. **Both simultaneously**: The lingam operates on multiple semantic registers simultaneously — phallic, cosmological, aniconic — without these being contradictory. The Hindu tradition's comfort with multiple simultaneous meanings makes the either/or debate a Western imposition.

## Mystery / unknown variants

The **continuity question**: whether the Indus Valley cylindrical stones represent a proto-lingam tradition that survived into historical Shaivism, or whether the lingam as a Shaiva cultic object developed independently in the post-Vedic period, remains open. The Indus Valley script remains undeciphered, preventing direct access to whatever religious system the archaeological objects belonged to.

## Cross-symbol edges (provisional)

- **→ [[axis-mundi]]** (parallel-form): The jyotirlinga myth explicitly identifies the cosmic lingam as the infinite central pillar of the universe — this is not metaphor but the primary cosmological role. The lingam IS the axis mundi in Shaiva cosmology.
- **→ [[yoni]]** (syncretic-fusion): The inseparable pair. The lingam always stands in the yoni; together they encode Śiva + Śakti = manifest cosmos. The yoni node describes the feminine half of this equation.
- **→ [[sri-yantra]]** (syncretic-fusion): The Sri Yantra's geometric structure of interlocking upward and downward triangles encodes the same lingam-yoni cosmology in two-dimensional sacred geometry form.
- **→ [[obelisk]]** (parallel-form): Egyptian solar axis as upright pillar of stone, parallel to the cosmic light-pillar of the jyotirlinga. No documented transmission; visual-functional cognate across civilizations separated by 2,000 years and 3,000 miles.
- **→ [[djed-pillar]]** (parallel-form): Osiris's backbone-as-stability-column and the phallic lingam-as-axis are parallel encodings of the male upright body as cosmic structural principle. The Osirian resurrection-phallus golden substitute is the closest Egyptian mythological parallel.
- **→ [[trishula]]** (parallel-form): Shiva's trident and lingam are the deity's two primary iconographic attributes — axis and weapon, rest and motion, being and action.
