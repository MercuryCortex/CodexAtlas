# Pomegranate

## Identity
The pomegranate (*Punica granatum*) is a fruit native to the eastern Mediterranean / Persian-Levantine zone, with religious-iconographic significance across **Greek, Mesopotamian, Hebrew, Christian, and Persian** religious traditions, spanning **~4,500 years** of continuous attestation. Its iconographic-symbolic power derives from a single botanical feature: **the fruit's interior is densely packed with hundreds of seeds in a single rind** — making it a natural symbol for **fertility, many-in-one cosmic unity, the underworld's harvest, and (in Christian Marian appropriation) the Church-as-many-souls-in-one-body**. For the Codex Atlas mission the pomegranate is load-bearing because it is **one of the cleanest documentable cross-tradition iconographic transmissions** in the Mediterranean record: Greek-Persephone → Greek-Eleusinian-mystery → Hellenistic-Jewish → Christian-Marian, with each transition documented in primary sources.

## Earliest attestation
- **Bronze Age Levantine / Mesopotamian** (c. -2500 onward) — pomegranates depicted in Mesopotamian seal-impressions and Egyptian (post-Hyksos) tomb-paintings. The fruit is a Levantine import to Egypt c. -1500 (Hyksos period).
- **Hebrew Bible** — pomegranates on the **priestly robes** (Exodus 28:33–34, "*pomegranates of blue and purple and scarlet, around the hem of the robe, with bells of gold between them*"); on the **Temple bronze pillars Jachin and Boaz** (1 Kings 7:18–20, "*two hundred pomegranates in two rows*"); listed among the **seven species** of the Promised Land (Deuteronomy 8:8). Rabbinic tradition: each pomegranate has **613 seeds** = the 613 mitzvoth — a numerological harmonization treated as folk-rabbinic rather than literal botany.
- **Greek mythology** — Persephone's pomegranate consumption (preserved most fully in **Homeric Hymn to Demeter** 372, ~7th c. BCE; later treated by Ovid *Metamorphoses* 5.534) binds her to the underworld for the equivalent of 6 months a year — **the Eleusinian seasonal-cycle aetiology**.
- **Eleusinian Mysteries** ([[event-eleusinian-mysteries-c1500-bce-396-ce]]) — pomegranate is integral iconographic component; the **Eleusinian initiates were forbidden to eat pomegranates** during the festival (the cult-prohibition reinforces the symbol's underworld-binding power; cf. Kerényi 1967).
- **Christian Marian iconography** (12th–17th c.) — Botticelli's *Madonna of the Pomegranate* (~1487), Mantegna's *Madonna of the Pomegranate*, Hugo van der Goes — late-medieval and Renaissance European Madonna-with-Pomegranate panel paintings are a recognized iconographic category.

## Meaning(s) attested

### Greek (Persephone / Eleusinian)
- **Underworld-binding**. The 6 seeds Persephone eats bind her to Hades for half the year; the fruit is **the fruit of the underworld**.
- **Seasonal-cycle aetiology**. Persephone's annual descent/return = the agricultural cycle; the pomegranate is the **vehicle of the binding**.
- **Fertility / many-in-one**. The botanical fact (hundreds of seeds in one fruit) reads as fertility-and-abundance.
- **Cult-prohibition** at Eleusis — Eleusinian initiates abstained from pomegranates during the festival.

### Hebrew
- **Priestly robe ornament + Temple decoration**. Pomegranates on the hem of the high priest's robe + on the Solomonic Temple bronze pillars.
- **613 mitzvoth** — rabbinic-traditional numerological identification.
- **Promised Land abundance** — pomegranate among the seven species of the Land of Israel.

### Christian Marian appropriation
- **Resurrection-promise**. The pomegranate's many-seeds-from-one-fruit-after-being-broken reads as the Resurrection-pattern.
- **Mary as fertility-vehicle**. Mary-with-Pomegranate iconography emphasizes Mary's role as bearer-of-Christ.
- **Church-as-many-souls-in-one-body**. The fruit's structure (many seeds, one rind) is read as ecclesiological — the Church as one Body of Christ with many members (1 Cor 12:12).
- **Christ-the-broken-fruit**. Christ-child holding the pomegranate (open, revealing the seeds) is a recognized Marian-iconographic motif foreshadowing the Passion.

## Cross-family appearance

### MASSIVE-win: the Persephone-pomegranate → Marian-pomegranate iconographic chain

The pomegranate provides **one of the cleanest documentable cross-tradition iconographic transmissions** in the Mediterranean record:

1. **Greek-Persephone (Eleusinian) → Hellenistic-Roman**: Persephone-with-pomegranate is a standard iconographic identifier in Greek-Roman religion.

2. **Hellenistic-Roman pomegranate-religious-iconography** ↔ **Hellenistic-Jewish Temple-pomegranate-iconography**: the Hellenistic period sees Jewish Temple iconography (pomegranates on the priestly robes and Temple pillars) and Greek-Roman pomegranate-religious-iconography circulating in the same Eastern Mediterranean visual-vocabulary. Whether the Hebrew Temple-pomegranates have any Greek-religious inflection or are purely-indigenous is debated; **the visual-vocabulary overlap is real**.

3. **Early Christian appropriation**. Early Christianity inherits BOTH the Greek-Persephonean pomegranate (via the Hellenistic-Roman visual vocabulary) AND the Hebrew Bible's priestly-Temple-pomegranates. The convergence makes the pomegranate **doubly-authorized** for Christian iconographic adoption — it is both a Persephone-fertility-resurrection symbol (which Christians can re-read as a Christ-resurrection symbol) AND a Hebrew-Bible-Temple-decoration symbol (which Christians can claim as a typological prefiguring of the Church).

4. **Marian iconography (12th–17th c.)**. The full elaboration: Mary or Christ-child holding the pomegranate. Verdon 2004 and Schiller 1971 document the iconographic tradition. **The Greek-Persephone-pomegranate iconography is the principal pre-Christian source** that Marian iconography draws on (mediated through Hellenistic-Roman visual culture); the Hebrew-Bible-Temple-pomegranates provide scriptural-typological warrant.

This is exactly the kind of **cross-field-boundary edge** that mainstream scholarship misses: Christian-Marian art-history rarely connects to Eleusinian-Persephone mystery-cult; Greek-religion-history rarely connects to Renaissance Madonna-with-Pomegranate panel-painting. The pomegranate sits exactly on the boundary.

### Persian and Islamic
- **Persian** — pomegranate is a recurring religious-iconographic element from Achaemenid period through Sasanian into early Islamic Persian art (Mithraic-Iranian context).
- **Quranic** — pomegranate mentioned three times in the Quran (6:99, 6:141, 55:68) as a fruit of paradise; subsequent Islamic-Persian decorative-religious art makes extensive use.

## Contested interpretations
- **613-seeds rabbinic numerology.** Folk-rabbinic claim with no botanical basis; pomegranate seed-counts vary widely (~250–1400). The numerological identification is theological-symbolic, not literal.
- **Hebrew-Greek-religious-vocabulary overlap.** Whether the Hebrew Temple-pomegranates have any Greek-religious-mythological background (Bronze Age Mediterranean shared symbolic vocabulary) or are purely-indigenous Israelite is debated. Most scholars treat the iconography as substantially-shared Eastern-Mediterranean visual vocabulary with each tradition giving it tradition-specific theological readings.
- **Marian-pomegranate origin date.** When the Christian Marian-pomegranate iconographic tradition emerges — 6th c. Byzantine? 12th c. Romanesque? 13th c. Italian? — varies by scholar. Most agree it consolidates in 14th–15th c. Italian panel-painting.
