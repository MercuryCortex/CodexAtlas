# Asherah Pole (Sacred Tree / Cult Object of Asherah)

## Identity

The **Asherah** (Hebrew: אֲשֵׁרָה; Ugaritic: *ʾAṯiratu*) is simultaneously a **Canaanite mother goddess** and her **cult object** — a sacred wooden pole, living tree, or planted grove set up at high places (*bāmôt*) and, strikingly, inside the Jerusalem Temple itself. It is the **most politically charged suppressed symbol in the Hebrew Bible**, condemned in 40+ passages yet persistently present in Israelite religious practice across four centuries — until the Deuteronomic reform under Josiah (621 BCE) destroyed it.

The Asherah-pole is central to one of the most consequential discoveries in modern biblical archaeology: the **Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions** (c. 800 BCE, Sinai desert, excavated 1975-76), which preserve the blessing formula *brkt ʾtkm lyhwh šmrn wlʾšrth* — **"I bless you by Yahweh of Samaria and by his Asherah."** This is Tier-1 epigraphic evidence that popular Israelite religion understood Yahweh as having a divine consort. The Khirbet el-Qom inscription (c. 750 BCE) similarly reads "Yahweh and his Asherah."

## Earliest attestation

- **c. -1400 BCE** — Ugaritic texts (Baal Cycle, Ras Shamra / modern Syria): Athirat (*ʾAṯiratu*) as consort of El and mother of the divine assembly. The Ugaritic corpus, excavated 1929-1939 (KTU 1.1-1.6), is the foundational Canaanite textual background.
- **c. -1000 to -600 BCE** — Widespread Iron Age Levantine iconography: stylized "tree of life" flanked by caprids (ibexes) on pottery, seals, and ivories, identified as Asherah symbols.
- **c. -800 BCE** — Kuntillet Ajrud pithos inscriptions: "Yahweh of Samaria and his Asherah" — Tier-1 epigraphic evidence.
- **c. -750 BCE** — Khirbet el-Qom burial inscription: "Yahweh and his Asherah."
- **Continuous biblical condemnations** — 1 Kings 14:23 through 2 Kings 23 = 40+ passages spanning c. -930 to -621.
- **621 BCE** — Josianic destruction: the Asherah is removed from the Jerusalem Temple, burned in the Kidron Valley, ground to dust (2 Kings 23:4-15). Terminus of official Israelite cult.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Sacred tree / cosmic axis** — A wooden pole or living tree planted beside an altar or at a hilltop sanctuary; marks the sacred center (axis mundi) between earth and heaven.
- **Goddess cult object** — The physical representation or dwelling of the Asherah-goddess; women wove garments for her (2 Kings 23:7).
- **Divine consort** — Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom: popular religion treated "Yahweh and his Asherah" as a paired divine couple, the Israelite equivalent of the Canaanite El + Athirat pairing.
- **Official apostasy** — In the Deuteronomistic frame: the Asherah is polemic-condemned as idolatrous Canaanite practice that caused divine punishment.
- **Modern feminist theology** — The Asherah and the biblical Wisdom/Hokhmah (who is called "a tree of life" in Proverbs 3:18) are identified as reflexes of the same suppressed divine-feminine principle in Israelite religion.

## The Josianic suppression

**2 Kings 23:4-15** is the most detailed single account of systematic Israelite iconoclasm. Josiah's reform (621 BCE) destroys: the Asherah from the Temple, the vessels of Baal and Asherah, the *qedeshim* (cult personnel) associated with Asherah, the high places throughout Judah, the Asherah-poles at every gate. **The same reform also destroys [[nehushtan]]**, the bronze serpent of Moses — the two most ancient Israelite cultic objects suppressed together in a single centralizing reform. The Deuteronomic reform under Josiah is the historical event that creates strict Yahwist monotheism by systematically eliminating the pluralistic popular religion that had preceded it.

## Cross-family appearance

- **Canaanite-Ugaritic religion** (primary origin) — Athirat as consort of El; mother of the 70 gods
- **Israelite popular religion** (Iron Age, c. -1000 to -621) — persistently present in spite of official condemnation
- **Feminist biblical theology (20th-21st c. CE)** — recovered as evidence of a suppressed divine-feminine in Yahwism; connection to Wisdom/Hokhmah/Sophia tradition
- **Biblical archaeology (1975-76 onward)** — Kuntillet Ajrud and Khirbet el-Qom are the material pivot of the modern scholarly debate

## Axis mundi cross-tradition comparison

The Asherah-pole planted at a high place is the axis mundi of popular Israelite religion — a vertical wooden tree-form reaching between earth and heaven. The same structural function appears independently in:

- **Yggdrasil** (Norse — [[yggdrasil]]) — cosmic ash connecting nine worlds
- **Maypole / Irminsul** (Germanic-Saxon — Maypole and Irminsul: related forms of the same European cosmic pillar, with the Irminsul destroyed by Charlemagne in 772 CE)
- **Hindu *skambha*** (Atharva Veda 10.7-8, c. -1000) — cosmic pillar hymn
- **Inanna's sacred huluppu-tree at Eridu** (Sumerian, c. -2000) — the foundational sacred tree of Mesopotamian religion
- **Mayan ceiba tree** — great ceiba as World Tree / axis mundi

The cross-tradition pattern: every Bronze/Iron Age agricultural-pastoral culture independently generates a sacred tree or pole as the cosmic axis connecting human and divine realms. The Asherah-pole is the Levantine instance.

## Contested interpretations

- **Cult object vs. goddess** — Does "his Asherah" in the inscriptions refer to a cult object (pole) or to the goddess Asherah as a personal deity? The grammatical construction (possessive pronoun on a divine name) is the crux of the debate. Hadley 2000 reviews all positions; the trend since Kuntillet Ajrud is toward accepting a personalized goddess.
- **Yahweh's consort** — Dever 2005 argues the inscriptions, material culture, and prophetic polemics together confirm that popular Israelite religion in the 9th-8th centuries treated Asherah as Yahweh's consort; the Deuteronomistic History's condemnations are polemical reformulation, not ethnographic description.
- **Wisdom/Hokhmah connection** — Highly debated in feminist biblical scholarship; the linguistic and conceptual overlaps (Wisdom as tree-of-life, female personification of divine order, ancient Israelite background) are suggestive but not conclusive.
