## Wheel of Fortune

The Wheel of Fortune is one of the most durable symbols of cosmic indifference in Western and Asian religious thought: the great wheel that turns constantly, raising the low and crushing the high, regardless of merit. Its two major independent elaborations — the Roman/Medieval *Rota Fortunae* and the Buddhist *Bhavacakra* — converge on the same message: cyclic existence is inescapable; wisdom lies in transcending the wheel rather than riding it.

### Fortuna's Wheel (Roman)

The goddess Fortuna (*Tyche* in Greek) is depicted turning a great wheel to which human figures cling:
- At the **top**: the king with crown (*Regno* — "I reign")
- On the **ascending side**: the aspirant rising (*Regnabo* — "I shall reign")
- On the **descending side**: the fallen man slipping (*Regnavi* — "I reigned")
- At the **bottom**: the ruler crushed underfoot (*Sum sine regno* — "I am without reign")

The wheel turns constantly, regardless of merit. Fortuna is **blind** (*caeca Fortuna*) — she distributes fortune randomly. The Roman philosophical tradition used Fortuna's Wheel to articulate the unpredictability of fate: success is not permanent; virtue does not guarantee prosperity; the wise person prepares for the wheel's inevitable turn.

### Boethius (523 CE) — The Canonical Text

The *Consolation of Philosophy* by Anicius Manlius Severinus Boethius (written in prison in 523 CE while awaiting execution for treason) is the single most important text for the Western Wheel of Fortune tradition. T1 primary text.

Fortune herself speaks in the *Consolatio*: "This is my art, this the game I never cease to play. I turn the wheel that spins. I delight to see the high come down and the low ascend. Mount up, if you like, but only on condition that you don't think it a hardship to come down when the rules of my game require it."

The *Consolatio* was the most read Latin prose text of the Middle Ages (after the Bible). Boethius was translated into Old English by King Alfred the Great, into Middle English by Chaucer, into French by Jean de Meun. The Wheel of Fortune entered every European vernacular tradition through Boethius's prison text — arguably the most influential book written in a condemned cell in history.

The *Consolatio*'s argument: Fortune's Wheel reveals that worldly goods are not true goods; they are *bona fortunae*, goods of fortune, inherently unstable. True good (*summum bonum*) is found only in God, which Fortune cannot take. This is the philosophical framework through which the Wheel becomes not merely a symbol of fatalism but of liberation: once you understand what Fortune actually is, you cease to desire what she offers.

### Carmina Burana — The Medieval Reception

The medieval Latin lyric collection (*Carmina Burana*, c. 1230 CE, Benediktbeuern monastery) opens with "O Fortuna" — a direct address to Fortune's Wheel, the apex of goliardic resignation to fate. The poem is structured around the wheel's motion: *luna semper variabilis* (ever-changing moon), *status malus, vana salus* (evil status, vain health). Carl Orff set it as the opening and closing number of his cantata (1937); "O Fortuna" is now one of the most performed and recognized choral works in history — Fortune's Wheel speaking through a 1937 score derived from a 1230 monastery manuscript derived from a 523 CE prison text.

### The Tarot Wheel (Major Arcana X)

The Rider-Waite-Smith Tarot Wheel of Fortune (1909, designed by Pamela Colman Smith, instructed by Arthur Edward Waite) is a maximally syncretic image:

- The wheel bears **TARO/ROTA** — a palindrome, symbol of cyclic return (read in either direction, the same)
- The **four fixed signs of the zodiac** appear in the corners: human (Aquarius), lion (Leo), eagle (Scorpio), bull (Taurus) = the **Tetramorph** = Ezekiel's four *hayyot* — this same quaternary appears in Ezekiel's chariot vision, Christian gospel-symbol tradition, and here as the stable frame around the turning wheel
- The **Sphinx** sits atop the wheel — wisdom immune to fortune's turning; the Sphinx knows the answer to the riddle, but the wheel cannot move it
- **Anubis/Typhon** ascend and descend the sides — life and death in cyclic motion

The Tarot Wheel is a compressed encyclopedia of Western esoteric cosmology: Egyptian, Kabbalistic, astrological, Hermetic elements fused into a single turning image.

### Buddhist Bhavacakra (Wheel of Life)

The *Bhavacakra* ("wheel of becoming") is a painted circular diagram showing the six realms of Buddhist cosmology as wheel-segments:
- Gods (*deva*)
- Demi-gods (*asura*)
- Humans (*manushya*)
- Animals (*tiryak*)
- Hungry ghosts (*preta*)
- Hell beings (*naraka*)

The wheel is held in the jaws and claws of **Yama**, lord of death. At the wheel's hub: the three poisons — **pig** (ignorance), **cock** (greed), **snake** (hatred) — chasing each other in a circle, driving the wheel's rotation.

The Bhavacakra IS samsara — the cycle of rebirth. Outside the wheel, the Buddha points toward *nirvana* — liberation from the wheel entirely.

### MASSIVE WIN: Western Fortune's Wheel and Buddhist Bhavacakra

Western *Rota Fortunae* and Buddhist *Bhavacakra* are **independent** elaborations of the same symbol, separated by 5000 km and with no documented contact during their classical formulation periods:

| Feature | Rota Fortunae | Bhavacakra |
|---|---|---|
| Core image | Great wheel turning | Great wheel turning |
| Who turns it | Blind Fortune | Three poisons at hub |
| What is shown | Humans rising and falling | Beings cycling through realms |
| Who holds it | Fortuna | Yama (death) |
| Liberation | Philosophy / true good | Nirvana / Buddha's path |
| Message | Cyclic existence is suffering | Cyclic existence is suffering |

Both traditions independently arrived at the wheel as the symbol of inescapable cyclic existence, and both independently identified wisdom (Boethian philosophy; Buddhist nirvana) as the means of transcending the wheel rather than merely riding it better. This is one of the clearest instances of independent parallel symbol-formation in world religious history.

### Cross-Symbol Connections

- [[dharmachakra]] (parallel-form — the Dharma Wheel (eight-spoke wheel of the law) and the Bhavacakra (wheel of life) are Buddhist parallel wheels with opposite functions: the Dharma wheel liberates; the Bhavacakra binds; one is what you spin toward, the other is what is spinning you)
- [[tarot]] (syncretic-fusion — the Wheel of Fortune is Major Arcana X; it encodes the entire Western cyclic-fortune tradition in a single Tarot card, combining Egyptian, Kabbalistic, and Hermetic elements)
- [[ouroboros]] (parallel-form — both are symbols of cyclic return; the ouroboros self-consuming serpent and the turning fortune wheel both encode eternal recurrence; both contain within the cycle the promise of renewal)
