# Pentagram

## Identity

A pentagram is the five-pointed star formed by extending the sides of a regular pentagon — a regular star polygon {5/2}. Geometrically distinguished by the golden ratio (φ ≈ 1.618) recurring in every nested segment; Pythagorean mathematicians considered this proportion central to cosmic harmony. The form is semiotically read by successive traditions as: (a) Pythagorean emblem of the five elements (earth, water, air, fire, *aether* / spirit); (b) medieval Christian symbol of the Five Wounds of Christ; (c) Renaissance Hermetic microcosm-macrocosm figure (Agrippa); (d) post-1854 occult emblem with explicit point-up (spirit-over-matter) vs. point-down (inverted, "satanic") distinction (Eliphas Levi); (e) central 20th-c. Wiccan ritual emblem (Gardnerian reception).

## Earliest attestation

- **c. -3500 onward** — incidental five-point star forms appear on Mesopotamian, Egyptian, and other Bronze Age decorative objects; these are not theologically loaded.
- **Pythagorean** (c. -550 onward) — the *pentalpha* is the school emblem of the Pythagorean fellowship; the golden-ratio recursion in the pentagram is one of their signature mathematical discoveries. Lucian of Samosata (*Pro Lapsu inter Salutandum*, 2nd c. CE) attests the Pythagorean use as recognition-sign.
- **Medieval Christian** — *Sir Gawain and the Green Knight* (c. 14th c., Pearl Poet) gives the most explicit literary description: Gawain's gold pentangle (called *endeles knot* — endless knot) on red shield read as Five Wounds of Christ, Five Joys of Mary, five chivalric virtues. Continental devotional use parallel.
- **Medieval grimoire tradition** — *Clavicula Salomonis* (Greek and Latin manuscripts c. 14th–16th c.) uses pentagram and hexagram interchangeably as "seals of Solomon"; Davies 2009 documents.
- **Renaissance Hermeticism** — Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa, *De Occulta Philosophia* (1531/1533): pentagram-with-human-inscribed becomes the iconic microcosm-macrocosm image, inherited by subsequent occult literature.
- **19th-c. occult systematization** — Eliphas Levi, *Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie* (1854–1856): explicit point-up / point-down distinction; the "Baphomet-style" inverted pentagram is a Levi innovation.
- **20th-c. Wicca and modern paganism** — Gerald Gardner, *Witchcraft Today* (1954); subsequent Wiccan literature adopts the pentacle (pentagram-on-disc) as ritual tool and altar emblem.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Pythagorean** — the five elements (earth, water, air, fire, *aether*) at the five points; golden-ratio recursion as cosmic harmony emblem. (Thorndike vol. 1)
- **Medieval Christian** — Five Wounds of Christ, Five Joys of Mary, five virtues. (*Sir Gawain* 619-665.)
- **Hermetic / Agrippan** — humanity inscribed in the cosmos; the pentagram as figure of human-cosmic correspondence; classical-element synthesis. (Agrippa 1533.)
- **19th-c. occult (Levi)** — point-up: spirit over matter; point-down: matter over spirit, "infernal." (Levi 1854.) **The "satanic" reading post-dates 1854 and is NOT a medieval inheritance.**
- **20th-c. Wiccan** — five elements (frequently re-read with Spirit at the top point); ritual focus tool. (Hutton 1999.)
- **Anti-Wiccan Christian polemic (20th-c.)** — the pentagram (especially point-down) is read as "satanic." This is a 20th-c. reception of the Levi distinction, not a continuous Christian tradition; medieval Christian use was unambiguously positive (Five Wounds).

## Variants

The five attested variants (Pythagorean, Christian Five-Wounds, Agrippan microcosm, Levi point-up/point-down, Wiccan pentacle) are documented in the YAML. The point-up / point-down distinction is post-1854.

## Cross-family appearance

The pentagram is one of the most cross-tradition Mediterranean-Western symbols — but its trans-cultural use is mostly *continuous within one broad Mediterranean-Hermetic lineage* (Pythagorean → medieval Christian → Renaissance Hermetic → 19th-c. occult → 20th-c. Wicca), not a multi-continent parallel like the swastika. Outside that lineage, decorative five-point star use is common but not iconographically loaded.

## Contested interpretations

- Whether the point-up / point-down distinction has any pre-Levi (1854) basis. The scholarly consensus (Hutton 1999, Davies 2009) is **no**: it is a 19th-c. occult innovation now retrojected by both occultists and Christian polemicists.
- Whether the 20th-c. Wiccan reception is "ancient" (Gardner's own claim) or "constructed" (the scholarly consensus per Hutton 1999). The latter is the academic standard.
- The relationship of the medieval *Clavicula Salomonis* magical pentagram to both the Renaissance Hermetic and the modern Wiccan pentagram. Davies 2009 documents the continuity at the grimoire-textual level.
