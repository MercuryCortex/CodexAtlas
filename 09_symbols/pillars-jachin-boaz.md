# Pillars of Jachin and Boaz

## Identity

The **Pillars of Jachin and Boaz** are the two **free-standing bronze columns** that stood at the entrance porch (*ulam*) of **Solomon's Temple** in Jerusalem, described in *1 Kings 7:13–22* and *2 Chronicles 3:15–17*. Cast by **Hiram of Tyre** — the Phoenician bronze-smith who is the prototype of the Masonic legendary master builder *Hiram Abiff* — the pillars were named **Jachin** (יָכִין, "He will establish") on the right and **Boaz** (בֹּעַז, "In Him is strength") on the left. Approximately 8–9 metres tall, they bore elaborate capitals decorated with lily-work and pomegranates. They were **not structural** — they stood freely at the entrance, their function entirely symbolic: marking the threshold between the profane world and the sacred precinct, establishing divine protection and covenant-strength as the framework within which the Temple and its people stand.

The pillars are the origin emblem of speculative **[[tradition-freemasonry|Freemasonry]]** — the Fellowcraft (2°) degree ritual involves symbolic passage between them as an initiatory threshold, and Masonic lodge rooms replicate them at the entrance or display them on tracing boards. They became the central architectural metaphor of the Masonic tradition's Solomonic origin myth.

**THE MASSIVE WIN**: The Masonic lodge's two pillars are simultaneously the Kabbalistic Tree of Life's two outer sefirotic columns — the **Pillar of Severity** (Boaz/left) and the **Pillar of Mercy** (Jachin/right). The lodge room IS the Kabbalistic cosmological diagram.

## Earliest attestation

- **c. -950 BCE** — Solomon's Temple construction (c. 957–950 BCE): the two pillars cast by Hiram of Tyre, described in *1 Kings 7:13–22*. This is the primary scriptural attestation.
- **c. -750 to -500 BCE** — Herodotus (*Histories* II.44) describes two free-standing **Phoenician pillars at the temple of Melqart at Tyre**: one of gold, one of emerald. These pre-date Herodotus's text by several centuries and document the **Phoenician free-standing pillar tradition** from which Hiram of Tyre's work descended.
- **c. -1550 to -1100 BCE** — **Egyptian New Kingdom** obelisk-pairs flanking temple entrances (Luxor, Karnak) — two free-standing vertical columns as cosmological gateposts, documented centuries before the Solomonic Temple.
- **13th century CE** — **Zohar** explicitly maps the sefirotic Tree of Life's three columns onto Jachin (Pillar of Mercy), Boaz (Pillar of Severity), and the Middle Pillar — the definitive Kabbalistic synthesis.
- **1723** — **[[phase-6-026-anderson-constitutions-free-masons|Anderson's *Constitutions*]]** enshrines the Solomonic Temple as the origin-myth of Freemasonry.
- **1871** — **Pike's *Morals and Dogma*** explicitly identifies the Masonic pillars with Egyptian obelisks and with the Kabbalistic sefirotic columns.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Biblical (1 Kings 7)**: Threshold markers at the entrance to the sacred precinct. The names encode divine covenant language: Jachin (YHWH "establishes" the Davidic dynasty) and Boaz (YHWH's "strength" upholds it). Together the names form a liturgical formula: "In the strength of YHWH shall the king rejoice" — a royal enthronement statement.
- **Masonic Operative**: The pillars are hollow and serve as storage for the lodges of the Fellow Craft — the plans and maps of the building works. In Masonic ritual allegory, they contain all knowledge of geometry and architecture.
- **Masonic Speculative**: Passage between the pillars = initiation into the mysteries of the Fellowcraft degree. Jachin = knowledge; Boaz = strength. The Masonic Fellow Craft passes from darkness (West, Boaz) to light (East, Jachin) through the pillars' gateway.
- **Kabbalistic**: Left pillar (Boaz) = Pillar of Severity (Binah, Gevurah, Hod — divine judgment, constraint, splendor); Right pillar (Jachin) = Pillar of Mercy (Chokmah, Chesed, Netzach — divine wisdom, loving-kindness, victory). This is the core Kabbalistic architecture of the Tree of Life. The Shekinah ([[shekhinah]]) dwells in the space between the pillars (Zohar).
- **Masonic-Enochic reading**: Some Masonic monitors identify the two pillars with the pre-Flood **Pillars of Enoch** — two great columns on which Enoch inscribed all human knowledge before the Flood, so that it would survive. This connects to the *Book of Enoch* tradition and Masonic claims of antediluvian origin.

## Cross-family appearance

**Phoenician parallels (Tier 1 documented)**:
- Herodotus (*Histories* II.44) describes two free-standing pillars — one of gold, one of emerald — at the **temple of Melqart at Tyre** (the Phoenician Hercules, [[melqart]]). Given that *1 Kings 7:13–14* states Hiram of Tyre was a Tyrian craftsman, this is almost certainly the direct precedent tradition: **Hiram built the Solomonic pillars in the Phoenician free-standing-pillar tradition** of his own religious culture.

**Egyptian parallel (visual cognate, reception documented)**:
- Two **obelisks** flanking Egyptian temple entrances (Luxor, Karnak, Abu Simbel) represent the same architectural grammar: two vertical columns as gateway to the sacred. The **[[obelisk]]** node documents the Egyptian form. Masonic literature from Pike (1871) onward explicitly maps the Egyptian obelisk-pair as the Egyptian Jachin and Boaz.

**Hindu parallel (no transmission documented)**:
- **Dvārapāla** (guardian-deity) column pairs at the entrance to Hindu temples function as threshold guardians with cosmological significance. This is a visual cognate with no documented historical connection.

**Norse parallel (no transmission documented)**:
- The **Irminsul** (the great world-pillar of the Saxons, destroyed by Charlemagne in 772 CE) is a single cosmic pillar rather than a pair — a partial parallel for the axis-mundi function but not the twin-pillar structure.

## Contested interpretations

- **Were the pillars Phoenician in religious meaning, not Israelite?** *1 Kings 7:13–14* explicitly states that Hiram of Tyre was the craftsman. The Phoenician free-standing temple-pillar tradition (documented at the temples of Melqart at Tyre and Byblos, and later at Carthage) predates the Solomonic Temple. Some scholars (e.g., Carol Meyers, *Temple in Ancient Israel*) argue the pillars were Phoenician sacred-column typology imported into the Israelite cult — which would make them direct imports from Canaanite-Phoenician religion rather than Israelite invention.
- **Did a historical Solomon's Temple exist?** The historicity of Solomon's Temple is disputed in current archaeology. The Temple Scroll and later Rabbinic descriptions are consistent with a real structure, but no archaeological remains of the First Temple have been directly confirmed (the Temple Mount is archaeologically inaccessible). The pillars' biblical description is taken as historically reliable by most scholars.
- **The Masonic Hiram Abiff legend vs. the biblical Hiram of Tyre**: The Masonic master builder *Hiram Abiff* (murdered by three Fellow Crafts for refusing to reveal the Master Mason's word) is a Masonic legendary figure with no biblical prototype — the biblical Hiram of Tyre is a craftsman, not a narrative protagonist. The murder-and-resurrection allegory in the Masonic legend is a speculative accretion, not a biblical text.
