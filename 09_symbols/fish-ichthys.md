# Fish / Ichthys

## Identity

The fish is a multi-tradition iconographic-religious figure attested across the ancient Near East, Mediterranean, and South Asia in several distinct registers: civilization-bringer (Oannes / apkallu in Mesopotamia, Matsya in India); fertility / mother-deity attribute (Atargatis in Syria); polemicized cult-image (Dagon among the Philistines, condemned in Hebrew Bible); and the early Christian *ichthys*-glyph as covert identification-emblem of Christ. The early Christian fish-glyph has TWO competing origin-hypotheses in modern scholarship: (a) acrostic of Ἰησοῦς Χριστός Θεοῦ Υἱὸς Σωτήρ — "Jesus Christ, God's Son, Savior" (mainstream Anglo-American reading: Snyder 1985, Jensen 2017); (b) derivation from the lower arc of the [[vesica-piscis]] geometric form (Bagatti 1971, held by some Italian Catholic archaeologists). The two views are not strictly incompatible — the early Christians may have noted multiple resonances — but are usually presented as alternative origin-stories.

## Earliest attestation

- **Mesopotamian (c. -2000 onward)** — Oannes / *U-Anna*, first of the seven *apkallu* fish-sages who taught humanity. Berossus (3rd c. BCE) preserves the tradition; iconographic attestation: fish-cloaked priestly figures on Mesopotamian reliefs (Neo-Assyrian).
- **Vedic Matsya (c. -800)** — *Shatapatha Brahmana* I.8.1: Manu and the fish who rescues him from the cosmic flood. Subsequently elaborated as Vishnu's first avatar. ([[phase-2-006-brahmanas-aranyakas]], [[phase-2-017-mahabharata-ramayana-oral-layers]])
- **Philistine Dagon (c. -1200 onward textual)** — Judges 16:23-31 (Samson at Dagon's temple); 1 Samuel 5 (Ark at Ashdod). The Hebrew Bible's polemic against Dagon. ([[phase-2-019-deuteronomistic-history]])
- **Syrian Atargatis (Hellenistic period, 4th c. BCE onward)** — Hierapolis-Bambyce temple-precinct with sacred fish-pool; cult spreads across Hellenistic Mediterranean. Lucian's *De Dea Syria* (2nd c. CE) principal source.
- **Early Christian ichthys (2nd c. CE onward)** — catacomb graffiti at Rome (Callixtus, Priscilla, Domitilla); funerary inscriptions. Tertullian *De Baptismo* 1 (c. 200) explicitly notes "we, little fishes, are born from the great Fish" (*pisciculi nostri secundum Ἰχθύν nostrum*). Acrostic-origin reading attested from Sibylline Oracles VIII (3rd–4th c. CE).
- **Continuous Christian use** — the ichthys recedes in late antique and medieval iconography (replaced by the cross) and is revived in 20th-c. American Evangelical context as "Jesus fish" car-emblem.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Mesopotamian Oannes / apkallu** — civilization-bringer; the fish-sages who emerged from the sea to teach humanity writing, agriculture, law. Berossus preserves the tradition. (López-Ruiz 2010.)
- **Vedic Matsya** — flood-rescuer; Vishnu's first avatar; preserves Manu, the Vedas, and the seven *rishis* through the cosmic deluge.
- **Philistine Dagon** — disputed (fish vs. grain), but the iconographic and rabbinic tradition reads as fish-deity polemicized in the Hebrew Bible.
- **Syrian Atargatis** — fertility / mother-goddess with fish-aspect; sacred-fish-pool cult at Hierapolis.
- **Christian ichthys** — covert / catechetical identification-emblem of Christ; the acrostic reading (Ἰησοῦς Χριστός Θεοῦ Υἱὸς Σωτήρ) is the mainstream scholarly origin-account (Snyder 1985, Jensen 2017); the vesica-piscis-derivation hypothesis (Bagatti 1971) is the alternative.

## Variants

The six attested variants (ichthys-acrostic, ichthys-vesica-derivation, Dagon, Oannes / apkallu, Matsya, Atargatis) are documented in YAML.

## Cross-family appearance

- **Mesopotamian Oannes ↔ Vedic Matsya** — `parallel-form`. Both fish-civilization-bringer / flood-rescuer figures, with the Mesopotamian and Vedic flood-narrative families having known cross-influence (cf. Atrahasis ↔ Genesis flood — see [[phase-1-006-atrahasis]] and [[phase-1-016-eridu-genesis-flood]]). The fish-figure specifically is not documented as transmitted; both attestations may draw on a Bronze Age substrate.
- **Philistine Dagon → Hebrew polemic** — `polemic-inversion`. The Hebrew Bible explicitly opposes the Philistine Dagon-cult.
- **Hellenistic Syrian Atargatis → Greek Aphrodite-fish (the fish-aspect of Aphrodite in Hellenistic syncretism)** — `syncretic-fusion`.
- **Christian ichthys** — derives either from the acrostic-letter-construction (Snyder 1985, Jensen 2017) or from the [[vesica-piscis]] geometric form (Bagatti 1971). The two origin-hypotheses are both cited but the acrostic is the mainstream Anglo-American reading.

## Contested interpretations

- The ichthys-origin question (acrostic vs. vesica-piscis). The mainstream Anglo-American reading is acrostic-origin (Snyder 1985, Jensen 2017); the Italian Catholic archaeological tradition (Bagatti 1971) prefers vesica-piscis-derivation. Both views cited.
- The Dagon-as-fish vs. Dagon-as-grain question. The fish-reading is rabbinic medieval and iconographically supported in some attestations; some modern scholars (notably John Day) prefer the grain-reading from the Hebrew *dāgān*. The matter is not settled.
- Whether the Hellenistic Atargatis-fish cult has any genealogical connection to either the Mesopotamian Oannes-tradition or the Philistine Dagon-tradition. Some Hellenistic-era syncretic theology made such connections; modern scholarship is more cautious.
