# Baphomet

## Identity

Baphomet is a composite anthropomorphic symbol: a goat-headed androgynous winged figure, seated in a pentagrammatic Hermetic posture, inscribed *SOLVE ET COAGULA* (dissolve and bind) on its arms, with a torch of intelligence between the horns, a pentagram on the forehead, and a caduceus at the groin. The canonical image is the engraving in Éliphas Lévi's *Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie* (1854) — a 19th-century Hermetic synthesis symbol with NO iconographic continuity to the Templar trial accusations that supplied its name.

The vault records Baphomet simultaneously as a **deity node** ([[baphomet]]) tracing its reception history and a **symbol node** (this file) tracing its iconographic structure and cross-tradition parallels.

## Earliest attestation

- **1307 CE** — the name "Baphomet" (as "Baphometh") first appears in Templar trial confessions coerced under torture (see Fabrications section below). This is the textual first appearance of the word; no image, no iconographic tradition.
- **1854 CE** — Éliphas Lévi's engraving in *Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie* is the **earliest attestation of the symbol as it exists iconographically**. Every element of the visual Baphomet — the goat head, the androgyny, the torch, the caduceus, the wings, the pentagram, SOLVE/COAGULA — is Lévi's original synthesis.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Lévi's explicit reading (Tier-1 primary source):** "This is not the devil as imagined by the Middle Ages. It is Pan, it is the Universal Agent ... It synthesizes all opposing forces in equilibrium: male/female, fire/water, light/shadow, dissolve/bind." Lévi's Baphomet is a **cosmological diagram of the Hermetic coincidentia oppositorum** — not a deity of evil, not a Templar idol, not Satan.
- **SOLVE ET COAGULA reading:** The two-part alchemical maxim inscribed on the arms is the Hermetic-alchemical formula for the Great Work: *solve* (dissolve the compound into its elements) and *coagula* (re-bind the purified elements into a higher unity). Baphomet is the embodied alchemical maxim.
- **Torch of Intelligence:** Lévi drew explicitly on the symbolism of Prometheus and the Luciferian "bringer of light" tradition — the torch between the horns is the light of Gnosis/Intelligence.
- **Pan identification:** Lévi linked Baphomet explicitly to the Greek god [[pan-greek]] — the goat-legged fertility deity of nature, wildness, and the transgressive "other." Pan's Latin equivalents are Faunus and Silvanus.

## Fabrications and contested interpretations

**THIS SECTION IS MANDATORY under vault methodology for all Templar-zone content.**

### The Templar idol accusation (1307–1314): a documented fabrication

The "Baphomet" accusations in the Templar trials are **documented inquisitorial fabrications**. This is the consensus of the two leading Templar historians:

- **Malcolm Barber** (*The Trial of the Templars*, 1978; 2nd ed. 2006, Cambridge UP — **Tier 1**): concludes that the trial confessions were extracted under torture by Philip IV's agents and cannot be accepted as evidence of actual Templar practice. The descriptions of "Baphomet" across different confessions are so wildly inconsistent (a bearded head; a cat; a figure with two faces; a carved skull; a man on a horse; a woman) as to indicate confabulation under extreme duress.
- **Barbara Frale** (*Processus Contra Templarios*, 2007 — **Tier 1 critical edition**): the Chinon Parchment (1308; rediscovered Vatican Secret Archive 2001) records Pope Clement V's private absolution of the Templar grand master and dignitaries of heresy — while Clement publicly condemned them under political pressure from Philip IV.

**Verdict:** Philip IV's motive was the seizure of Templar assets and political removal of a powerful order that had refused a forced merger with the Hospitallers. The "Baphomet" accusation was the instrument of that suppression.

### The name "Baphomet": etymology debated

Four competing etymologies, none definitively proven:
1. **Mahomet corruption (Tier-2 hypothesis):** "Baphomet" as Old French/Provençal corruption of "Mahomet" (Muhammad) — reflecting the broader medieval slander that the Templars had become corrupted by contact with the Islamic world during the Crusades.
2. **Atbash cipher of Sophia (Tier-3 speculation):** Applying the Hebrew Atbash cipher (reverse-alphabet substitution) to "Baphomet" produces "Sophia" — proposed by Hugh Schonfield, popularized in *Holy Blood, Holy Grail* (1982, **Tier-4**). This reading has attracted considerable conspiracy-theory attention but lacks Tier-1 scholarly support.
3. **Inquisitorial invention (Tier-2 hypothesis):** The name was invented or distorted by the inquisitors themselves, with no documentary basis in actual Templar usage.
4. **Unknown origin:** The honest scholarly conclusion (Barber 2006) is that the etymology remains unresolved.

### Lévi → Templar continuity: zero documented

Lévi's 1854 image has **no historical continuity** to the Templar accusations. Lévi borrowed only the name; he was aware of the Templar trials but constructed his image entirely from Hermetic-alchemical-Kabbalistic iconographic vocabulary. There is no documented transmission from medieval Templars to 19th-c. French occultism.

### Masonic/Satanic conspiracy: Tier-4, no Tier-1 support

The claim that Baphomet represents a continuous Masonic or Satanic underground from the Templars through to the present is **Tier-4** speculation (e.g., Léo Taxil's hoax, 1885–1897, which fabricated "Palladian Masonry" and Masonic devil-worship and was publicly confessed as a hoax in 1897; *Holy Blood, Holy Grail*, 1982). No Tier-1 source supports any such institutional continuity.

## Cross-family appearance

Baphomet is **specifically modern Western occult** (Lévi, 1854) in origin. Its cross-family appearance is in the sense of the goat-deity substrate:

- **Egyptian:** The "Goat of Mendes" — *Banebdjedet*, the Ram-of-Mendes, an Egyptian deity of the Delta city Mendes associated with the *ba*-soul and fertility — was identified with Baphomet by 18th–19th c. occultists (notably Crowley). The identification is itself an occultist invention, but *Banebdjedet* is a real Egyptian deity, attested from the Old Kingdom (Herodotus, *Histories* II.46 describes the Mendes cult).
- **Greek:** Lévi's explicit identification of Baphomet with [[pan-greek]] (see Identity above) grounds the symbol in the Greek goat-deity tradition of nature, wildness, and the chthonic.
- **Masonic:** Crowley adopted "Baphomet" as his magical name in the [[tradition-thelema|OTO]]. The 19th-degree context of the Scottish Rite does not use Baphomet explicitly, but the Hermetic synthesis vocabulary is shared.
