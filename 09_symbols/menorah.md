# Menorah (Seven-branched Lampstand)

## Identity
The **Menorah** (Hebrew מְנוֹרָה — "lampstand") is the **seven-branched golden lampstand** described in **Exodus 25:31-40** as one of the principal furnishings of the wilderness Tabernacle and subsequently of the **Jerusalem Temple** (both First Temple, c. -957 to -586 BCE, and Second Temple, -515 to +70 CE). The form consists of **a central shaft with three branches on each side**, ornamented with **almond-shaped cups, knops, and flowers** (Ex. 25:33–36), holding **seven lamps burning pure olive oil** (Ex. 25:37). The Menorah is **the single most continuously-attested specifically-Jewish religious symbol**, with continuous use from the late-Second-Temple period (~ -200 BCE iconographic evidence) through medieval synagogue mosaics through to the **modern emblem of the State of Israel** (adopted 1949).

The Menorah's modern political-symbol use is particularly striking: the **State of Israel chose the Menorah as its central state-emblem** (1949), explicitly based on the **Arch of Titus relief in Rome** (~+82 CE) depicting the Roman triumphal procession with the looted Second Temple Menorah. This is one of the **rare modern political-state symbols that directly reproduces an ancient religious-iconographic form** — and is doubly significant because the Arch-of-Titus relief commemorates *the destruction* of the Temple, which the State of Israel iconographically *reclaims* as state-symbol.

## Earliest attestation
- **c. -1300 to -1000 BCE (traditional)** — the wilderness Tabernacle Menorah (Mosaic-period; the Exodus narrative). The historical actuality is debated; the textual attestation is in the **P source** of the Pentateuch ([[phase-2-018-hebrew-bible-p-source]]) redacted c. -500 BCE.
- **c. -957 to -586 BCE** — First Temple Menorah; described in 1 Kings 7:49 and 2 Chronicles 4:7 (10 menorot in Solomon's Temple per these accounts). Destroyed in 586 BCE Babylonian destruction.
- **c. -515 to +70 CE** — Second Temple Menorah; the actual physical Menorah looted by Titus in 70 CE.
- **~+82 CE** — Arch of Titus relief in Rome (the principal extant ancient iconographic representation).
- **3rd-7th c. CE** — synagogue mosaics across the diaspora (Hammat Tiberias, Beth Alpha, Sepphoris, Dura-Europos).
- **medieval-modern** — continuous Jewish religious and identifying use.
- **1949** — adopted as central emblem of the State of Israel.

## Meaning(s) attested
- **Tabernacle / Temple cultic** — the cultic lampstand burning continuously in the Holy Place of the Tabernacle and Temple; physical-religious furniture serving the daily cult.
- **Symbolic (Philonic)** — Philo of Alexandria reads the seven branches as the **seven classical planets** (sun-moon-Mercury-Venus-Mars-Jupiter-Saturn), making the Menorah a cosmic-cosmological emblem.
- **Symbolic (rabbinic)** — the seven branches as the **seven days of creation**, or as the **seven Noahide commandments**, or as the **wisdom-tree** (Proverbs 3:18, the *etz chaim*).
- **Mystical (Kabbalistic)** — the seven branches as the **lower seven sefirot** (Chesed through Malkhut), with the central shaft = *tiferet*.
- **Modern political (State of Israel)** — the Menorah as **continuity of Jewish national-religious identity** from the ancient Temple to the modern state. The reclamation of the Arch-of-Titus iconography.

## Cross-family appearance
The Menorah is **specifically Jewish in origin** and **does not significantly migrate to other religious traditions**. Early Christian appropriations of Temple iconography exist (the seven-branched lampstand appears in Revelation 1:12 and 1:20 as **the seven churches of Asia**) but the Christian seven-lampstand-of-Revelation is distinct in function from the Jewish Temple Menorah and the iconographic traditions diverge.

## Contested interpretations
- **Historical actuality of the Tabernacle Menorah.** The P source's description is post-exilic (~ -500 BCE) and may project Second-Temple-period cultic-furniture back onto the legendary Mosaic period. The First-Temple Menorah's historicity is also debated.
- **The fate of the Second Temple Menorah after 70 CE.** Variously: melted by Vespasian for the *templum Pacis*; looted by Alaric 410; looted by Geiseric 455; recovered by Belisarius 533 and returned to Constantinople or Jerusalem; ultimately disappeared. The physical Menorah's location is one of the great archaeological mysteries.
- **The Arch of Titus relief accuracy.** The relief is a Roman propaganda monument and may distort the actual Menorah-design; scholars debate how reliable it is as iconographic-historical evidence.
