# Yin-Yang (Taijitu)

> **Methodology discipline note:** The textual yin-yang concept is ancient (Warring States, c. -475 onward); the canonical visual *taijitu* diagram is Song-dynasty Neo-Confucian (c. 1060, Zhou Dunyi). Conflating these is a common popular error. The Roman / Sasanian "yin-yang shield-boss" attestations are `visual-cognate` only — NOT evidence of Chinese-to-Mediterranean transmission.

## Identity

The yin-yang is the canonical visual diagram of complementary opposing principles in Chinese cosmology: a circle bisected by an S-curve into two equal swirling-comma sections (one black, one white), with a smaller dot of the opposite colour in each section. The form encodes the Daoist and Neo-Confucian doctrine that all phenomena arise from the dynamic complementarity of *yin* (陰 — dark, cool, passive, feminine, water, earth, moon) and *yang* (陽 — bright, warm, active, masculine, fire, sky, sun), with each containing the seed of its opposite (the dot). The canonical diagram is from Zhou Dunyi's *Taijitu Shuo* (c. 1060 CE, Northern Song), but the textual concept of yin-yang complementarity is much older, attested from the Warring States period (c. -475 onward).

## Earliest attestation

- **Warring States period (c. -475 to -221 BCE)** — yin-yang concept attested in textual record. The *Yijing* (*Book of Changes*) develops the eight trigrams (*bagua*) and 64 hexagrams from yin-yang line combinations. The *Daodejing* (c. -350 textual layer) uses yin-yang implicitly (the *Daodejing* itself uses the binary in chapter 42's "myriad things carry yin and embrace yang").
- **Han dynasty (-206 to 220 CE)** — yin-yang becomes the foundational vocabulary of Chinese natural philosophy, medicine (*Huangdi Neijing*), divination, and cosmology. Yin-yang and the Five Phases (*wuxing*) become the core conceptual apparatus.
- **Song dynasty (960–1279)** — Zhou Dunyi (周敦頤, 1017–1073) writes *Taijitu Shuo* (*Diagram of the Supreme Ultimate Explained*) around 1060 CE, presenting the canonical visual diagram. **The famous swirling-comma yin-yang form dates from here.** Subsequent Neo-Confucian philosophy (Zhu Xi) systematizes yin-yang within the metaphysics of *li* and *qi*.
- **Yuan, Ming, Qing dynasties** — yin-yang and *taijitu* iconography becomes universal in Chinese cultural-religious-philosophical contexts.
- **Modern reception** — yin-yang diagram universalized as "Chinese / Daoist symbol" in Western reception from the late 19th c. onward; adopted into Western New Age, martial arts, and popular-cosmology contexts.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Textual yin-yang (Warring States onward)** — complementary opposing cosmic principles; the dynamic of all phenomena. (Kohn 2000; Needham 1956.)
- **Daoist** — the original undifferentiated *Dao* (道) gives rise to the One, which gives rise to yin and yang, which give rise to the myriad things (*Daodejing* 42). Yin-yang is one of the principal mediating principles between *Dao* and phenomena.
- **Neo-Confucian (Zhou Dunyi, Zhu Xi)** — *taiji* (the Supreme Ultimate) generates yin and yang, which generate the Five Phases, which generate the myriad things. The *taijitu* diagram visualizes this generative cosmology.
- **Chinese medicine** — yin-yang balance as physiological-pathological framework.
- **Modern Western reception** — generic "duality / balance / complementarity" emblem; in popular use the original cosmological specificity is often lost.

## Variants

The four attested variants (textual yin-yang, Song *taijitu*, bagua-trigram arrangement, Roman shield-boss cognate) are documented in YAML. The Roman cognate is `visual-cognate` only.

## Cross-family appearance

The yin-yang is essentially a **Chinese** symbol with subsequent global modern reception. The cross-tradition reach is one-way (Chinese → modern global) rather than multi-source.

- **Roman / Sasanian shield-boss attestations** — `visual-cognate`. The *Notitia Dignitatum* (5th c. CE Roman administrative document) records military shield patterns including a yin-yang-like swirling-comma design for the *Armigeri Defensores Seniores* unit. Similar Sasanian-period parallels exist. **NO documented Chinese-to-Mediterranean transmission at this date** — the cognate is iconographically striking but methodologically not evidence of cultural transmission. The Silk Road existed but iconographic transmission of this specific form is not documented.
- **Modern Western reception** — well-documented reception history from late 19th c. (China-to-West academic transmission, Pound's reception, Jung's reception) onward. The diagram is now globally legible.

## Contested interpretations

- The exact date of the canonical visual *taijitu* form. Zhou Dunyi's *Taijitu Shuo* (c. 1060) is the standard origin; some scholars (Wang Mingming) note possible Daoist alchemical antecedents in Tang-dynasty contexts. The mainstream consensus is Song-dynasty Neo-Confucian crystallization. (Robinet 1997; Kohn 2000.)
- Whether the Roman / Sasanian shield-boss attestations represent any contact-transmission or are pure independent invention. The conservative scholarly position is independent invention. The transmission hypothesis (Monier 1996, popular) lacks evidentiary basis.
- The conflation of *taijitu* (Song visual diagram) with ancient Daoism in popular reception. Daoism's textual yin-yang vocabulary is ancient; the diagram is medieval Neo-Confucian.
