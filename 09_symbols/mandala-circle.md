# Mandala / Cosmological Circle

> **Methodology discipline note:** This is a 4-ref entry because it has appropriation / contested-attribution branches. Jung's universal-archetypal-mandala thesis is **Jungian-interpretive**, NOT historical-transmission evidence. Eliade *Images and Symbols* on mandala-as-imago-mundi is Tier 2 (his phenomenological-categories work, not his Iron Guard period). Treat individually.

## Identity

A mandala (Sanskrit *maṇḍala* मण्डल, "disc / circle") is a circular or square cosmological diagram used as ritual focus, meditation aid, and cosmographic representation. The form is canonical in Hindu Tantric (*yantra*) and Buddhist Vajrayana iconographic-ritual tradition, with documented attestation from at least the mid-1st-millennium CE in textual record. Cognate forms appear in medieval Christian rose-window iconography (independent development with structural and devotional parallels) and in Navajo sand-painting (independent New World attestation). C. G. Jung's 20th-c. depth-psychology reception reads the mandala as universal-archetypal symbol of psychic wholeness; this Jungian interpretation, while internally coherent, is NOT historical-transmission evidence and should be cited as Jungian-tradition rather than as objective comparative-religion finding.

## Earliest attestation

- **Hindu yantra (textual c. -500 onward)** — Tantric textual tradition; the Sri Yantra and its variants are attested in Shakta and Shaiva textual sources from at least the mid-1st-millennium BCE textual layers onward, with the canonical form crystallizing in the medieval *Tantraloka* (Abhinavagupta c. 1000 CE, see [[phase-5-014-abhinavagupta-tantraloka]]) and similar texts. Iconographic earliest is harder to fix; the Sri Yantra in its standard form is medieval, with possible earlier antecedents.
- **Buddhist Vajrayana (c. 700 onward CE)** — Tantric Buddhist mandalas attested in the Tibetan Bardo Thödol tradition and its Indian Vajrayana antecedents. The Garbhadhātu and Vajradhātu mandalas standardize in the Shingon and Tibetan Vajrayana traditions from the 8th c. onward.
- **Christian rose-window (12th–13th c. CE)** — Chartres Cathedral north rose (c. 1230), south rose (c. 1230), Notre Dame de Paris (c. 1230), Lincoln Cathedral Dean's Eye (c. 1240), Reims, Strasbourg. Independent development, not transmitted from South Asian mandala traditions.
- **Navajo sand-painting (documented from 19th c., tradition older)** — Diné *iikááh* ritual sand-paintings in Blessingway and other ceremonies. The tradition is older than its documentation; 19th-c. and 20th-c. ethnographic record (Washington Matthews, Gladys Reichard) preserves the iconographic conventions.
- **Jungian reception (1944 onward)** — Jung *Psychology and Alchemy* (CW 12, 1944) and *Mandala Symbolism* (CW 9.i, 1950) systematize the depth-psychology reception. Jung's own private collection of mandala-drawings (now at the Jung Institute, Zürich) supplements the published record.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Hindu Tantric yantra** — *yantra* as ritual focus + cosmological diagram + meditation aid; specific yantras encode specific deities (Sri Yantra = Lalita Tripura Sundari, the Goddess as supreme reality). The central *bindu* is the cosmogonic point. (Kramrisch 1981 cited in [[hexagram]]; *Tantraloka*.)
- **Buddhist Vajrayana** — mandala as the abode of a particular Buddha and his entourage; ritual construction-and-destruction (sand-mandala) teaches impermanence; visualization-practice during meditation. (Snellgrove 1987; Tucci 1961; Beer 1999.)
- **Christian rose-window** — cosmological diagram with Christ-in-Glory or Marian centre, biblical / virtue / zodiacal-cosmological figures arranged in radiating petals. The window as theological diagram. (Cowen 2005.)
- **Navajo** — Holy People (*Diyin Diné'é*) arranged around a central axis; ritual function in healing ceremonies. The painting is destroyed at ceremony's end.
- **Jungian** — universal-archetypal symbol of the Self / psychic wholeness; individuation. Jung explicitly identifies Hindu, Buddhist, Christian rose-window, and Navajo attestations as supporting his thesis.

## Variants

The five attested variants (Hindu yantra, Buddhist Vajrayana, Christian rose-window, Navajo sand-painting, Jungian individuation) are documented in YAML. The Buddhist Vajrayana mandala has the most elaborate sub-variant tradition (Garbhadhātu, Vajradhātu, Kalachakra, deity-specific mandalas).

## Cross-family appearance

The mandala is a particularly important **multi-tradition** symbol where the discipline-call between `parallel-form` and `ancestor-of` is critical:

- **Hindu yantra → Buddhist Vajrayana mandala** — `ancestor-of` candidate. Documented historical transmission: Buddhist Vajrayana developed in continuity with Hindu Tantric tradition in 7th–11th c. India, with explicit iconographic and ritual inheritance. (Snellgrove 1987.)
- **Buddhist Vajrayana → Tibetan / East Asian Vajrayana** — `ancestor-of`. Documented manuscript and lineage-transmission from India through Tibet to East Asia (Shingon).
- **South Asian mandala ↔ Christian rose-window** — `parallel-form`, NOT `ancestor-of`. **NO documented historical transmission** between South Asian mandala tradition and medieval Christian Gothic iconography. The 12th–13th c. Gothic rose-window crystallizes independently in the Western Christian tradition; the iconographic-structural cognacy is striking but Cowen 2005 and standard medieval-art scholarship do not propose transmission.
- **South Asian mandala ↔ Navajo sand-painting** — `parallel-form`. Independent New World attestation. Jung 1944 noted the cognacy as supporting his archetypal thesis, but no historical transmission is documented.
- **Mandala → Jungian individuation** — `appropriated-by`. Jung explicitly adopts the mandala as central symbol of his depth-psychology system. Well-documented as 20th-c. Western intellectual reception.

## Contested interpretations

- The strength of the Hindu yantra → Buddhist Vajrayana transmission. Snellgrove 1987 documents the continuity. Some scholars (e.g., Davidson 2002) emphasize the more rupture-and-innovation aspects of Tantric Buddhism's emergence.
- Whether Christian rose-window iconography has any contact with South Asian mandala tradition. Standard medieval-art scholarship says NO; popular sacred-geometry literature claims transmission via Crusader-Islamic-Indian contacts, but this is Tier 4 and undocumented.
- Whether the Jungian universal-archetypal-mandala thesis is sound or is selection-bias projection. The thesis is internally coherent within Jungian psychology; as a comparative-religion claim it is methodologically problematic per the dilettante's-trap warning in our methodology.

## Mystery / unknown variants

None significant.
