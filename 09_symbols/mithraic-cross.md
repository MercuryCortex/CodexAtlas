# Mithraic cross

## Identity

The "Mithraic cross" is not a single freestanding emblem like the Latin or Greek cross — it is the **X-form geometric substructure** of the canonical Mithraic *tauroctony* (bull-slaying) composition: Mithras astride the slain bull, with the torchbearers Cautes and Cautopates flanking, Sol and Luna in the upper corners, and the cosmic animals (snake, dog, scorpion, raven) arranged in a cruciform / X-pattern across the relief. The X is structural, not decorative; it is read by Ulansey 1989 and others as a deliberate cosmographic diagram — the intersection of celestial equator and ecliptic, the cosmic crossroads — encoded in the central act of the Mithraic mystery.

The point of including the Mithraic cross in this symbol family is that it occupies the **same Roman urban environments** as early Christianity in the 2nd–4th c. CE, and its iconographic relationship to the Christian cross is a documented question of the history of Roman religion.

## Earliest attestation

- **2nd–4th c. CE Mithraic reliefs** — survive in Roman *mithraea* (cult sanctuaries) across the empire, with high densities at frontier garrison towns (Ostia, the Rhineland, the Danube provinces, Dura-Europos). The Walbrook Mithraeum (London, 3rd c.) is one well-preserved Northern example; the Capua, Heddernheim, and Mithraeum of the Seven Spheres (Ostia) are major centres.
- **Tauroctony composition** — the bull-slaying composition is iconographically standardized across the empire by the 2nd c., suggesting central transmission of the form (Beck 2006).
- **No textual exegesis from inside the cult** — Mithraic doctrine is reconstructed from iconography, ritual archaeology, and (later) Christian polemic; the cult was mystery-bound and produced no surviving theological text. This is why iconographic interpretation has been so contested.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Cosmographic** — Ulansey 1989's central thesis: the tauroctony depicts the cosmological act of Mithras moving the cosmic axis (precession of the equinoxes), with the X-form encoding the celestial-equator / ecliptic intersection. Beck 2006 broadly accepts the cosmographic framework while differing on specifics.
- **Initiatory / soteriological** — the bull-slaying is the founding mythic act enacted in the cult; initiates (in seven grades) participated ritually in its meaning. The X-form is then a soteriological diagram, not just an astronomical one.
- **Sacrificial** — older (Cumontian) accounts read the tauroctony as a sacrificial act analogous to Iranian Mithra; this Iranian-derivative reading is now largely rejected. The Roman Mithraic cult is a Greco-Roman late-antique mystery, not a direct Iranian transplant (Beck 2006 ch. 2–3).

## Variants

The *tauroctony X*, the *seven-grade iconography*, and the *celestial axis X* (Ulansey reading) are body sub-sections here.

## Cross-family appearance

- **Christianity** — Mithraism and Christianity coexisted in the same urban environments through the 2nd–4th c. and competed for similar adherents. Older accounts (Cumont's classic *Mystères de Mithra* 1900, popular through mid-20th c.) asserted Christian borrowing from Mithraic ritual and iconography. **Current scholarship (Beck 2006; Ulansey 1989; Hinnells 1994) explicitly rejects this thesis.** The two cults shared a Roman cultural matrix; iconographic resemblances (the cross-form, the bull-slaying, the cave-temple) are best read as `parallel-form`, NOT transmission. The Christian cross does NOT descend from the Mithraic X.
- **Sol Invictus** — the syncretism of Mithras with [[sol-invictus]] in the 3rd c. (under Aurelian and subsequent emperors) is well-documented; this is internal to Roman religion, not cross-cultural transmission.

## Contested interpretations

- **Iranian vs. Greco-Roman origin**: the older Cumontian view (Mithraism as Iranian Mithra-cult transplanted) is largely rejected. Current scholarship treats Roman Mithraism as a Greco-Roman late-antique mystery cult that *adopted Iranian names and imagery* but is doctrinally and ritually a Roman novelty (Beck 2006 ch. 2).
- **Ulansey's precession thesis**: influential but contested in detail (Beck 2006 differs on some interpretations; the broader cosmographic framework is mainstream).
- **Christian-Mithraic relationship**: as above — direct transmission is no longer the scholarly default; `parallel-form` is.

## Mystery / unknown variants

The cult was mystery-bound; specific doctrinal content remains partly recovered. `mystery-status: partially-deciphered` is defensible, but the iconography itself is well-described — so I'll keep `documented` here, with a body note flagging the doctrinal opacity.
