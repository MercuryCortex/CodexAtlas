# Dharmachakra (Wheel of Dharma)

## Identity
The **Dharmachakra** (Sanskrit *dharmacakra* = "wheel of dharma/law"; Pali *dhammacakka*) is the **principal symbol of Buddhism** — a wheel iconographically representing **the Buddha's teaching** (the *Dharma*) and his foundational act of **"turning the wheel of dharma"** in the First Sermon at Sarnath (the *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta*, traditionally dated to ~ -528 BCE, immediately after the Buddha's enlightenment). The canonical form is an **eight-spoked wheel** corresponding to the **Noble Eightfold Path**, though earlier and variant forms have different spoke-counts (the **24-spoked Ashoka Chakra** on the Flag of India is one such variant). The Dharmachakra is the **universal Buddhist identifying-emblem across all major Buddhist traditions** (Theravada, Mahayana, Vajrayana) and is **iconographically antecedent to Buddhist mandala-traditions, the Tibetan prayer-wheel, and the broader Buddhist iconographic vocabulary**.

## Earliest attestation
- **~ -528 BCE (traditional)** — the Buddha's First Sermon at Sarnath; *"turning the wheel of dharma"* is the foundational dharmacakra-narrative. The earliest *iconographic* attestation is later.
- **c. -250 BCE** — the **Lion Capital of Ashoka** at Sarnath, the earliest securely-dated Dharmachakra iconographic attestation. Four lions stand back-to-back on an abacus bearing four Dharmachakras.
- **c. -200 to +200 CE** — universal Buddhist iconographic use across South Asia; Bharhut, Sanchi, Amaravati, Gandhara Buddhist art.
- **c. +200 onward** — diffusion across Southeast Asia, Central Asia, China, Korea, Japan, Tibet with the Buddhist mission.
- **1947–1950** — modern Indian national emblem (Lion Capital) and Flag of India (Ashoka Chakra) adopt the Mauryan iconography as state-symbol of independent India.

## Meaning(s) attested
- **Original (canonical Buddhist)** — the Buddha's teaching as a *wheel that turns* (continues, propagates) through history. The First Sermon at Sarnath is *the* Buddhist beginning-of-history-event.
- **Eight-spoked canonical form** — the Noble Eightfold Path.
- **Three-part iconographic glossing**: the **rim** = *samadhi* (concentration); the **spokes** = *prajna* (wisdom); the **hub** = *sila* (moral discipline). Together: the three trainings (*tisikkha*) of Buddhist practice.
- **Universal Buddhist identifying-emblem** — analogous to the Christian cross or the Islamic star-and-crescent in its identifying-function for the tradition.
- **Modern Indian national symbol** — the Ashoka Chakra signals continuity with the Mauryan-Buddhist state heritage.

## Variants
See YAML — Ashokan eight-spoked, Buddhist canonical eight-spoked, Tibetan Vajrayana, modern Indian Ashoka Chakra (24-spoked), Jain Dharmachakra.

## Cross-family appearance
The Dharmachakra is **specifically Indic-Buddhist in origin** (and parallel Jain origin) but has substantial cross-tradition reception:
- **Universal across all Buddhist traditions** — Theravada, Mahayana, Vajrayana.
- **Jain Dharmachakra** — parallel-form, independent within the broader Indic religious milieu.
- **Hindu adoption** — the Indian state-emblem (1947–1950) adopts the Buddhist Dharmachakra for the modern Hindu-majority state, signaling cultural-heritage continuity rather than religious-doctrinal alignment.
- **East Asian Buddhist iconography** (Chinese, Korean, Japanese) — universal.
- **Modern Western Buddhist reception** — Tibetan-Buddhist diaspora communities, modern Buddhist organizations.

## Contested interpretations
- **The exact dating of the canonical eight-spoked form.** Eight-spoked Dharmachakras are securely attested from the Ashokan period (-250 BCE); earlier oral-tradition references are not iconographically traceable.
- **The relation of Buddhist and Jain Dharmachakras.** Both are wheel-symbols in adjacent Indic religious traditions; the parallel development is best understood as **common Indic religious heritage** rather than direct cross-tradition transmission.
