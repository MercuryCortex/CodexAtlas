# Flower of Life

## Identity
The **Flower of Life** is a geometric figure composed of **overlapping circles arranged in a hexagonal pattern**, each circle centered on the circumference of its six neighbors. The foundational generative operation is minimal: draw a circle; draw another circle of the same radius centered on the circumference of the first; repeat through six-fold symmetry. The resulting figure — 19 circles in the standard "Flower" form — generates, as sub-sets and extensions, the **Seed of Life** (7 circles), the **Fruit of Life** (13 circles, substrate of Metatron's Cube), and potentially the [[vesica-piscis]] (the first intersection of two overlapping circles) at every node. The pattern is notable for appearing independently across multiple civilizations and millennia — either because of documented transmission (debated for the Abydos case), independent discovery from the same simple geometric logic, or both.

## Earliest attestation
- **Abydos, Egypt, Temple of Seti I** (primary temple construction c. 1290 BCE) — circular patterns on granite column bases have been identified as Flower of Life configurations. **Attribution is contested**: the precise dating of the carvings is uncertain (they may post-date the primary construction by centuries); some Egyptologists argue the circles result from overlapping compass work rather than deliberate sacred-geometric encoding. The attribution was popularized by Drunvalo Melchizedek (*The Ancient Secret of the Flower of Life*, 1990) and is widely repeated in New Age literature — document as Tier-3 claim pending specialist Egyptological confirmation.
- **Assyria, Dur-Sharrukin** — hexagonal circle patterns appear in Assyrian palace decoration (c. 700 BCE), documented in architectural relief.
- **Pythagoras and the Greek geometric tradition**: the hexagonal circle-packing geometry is implicit in Pythagorean sacred number theory and is encoded in Euclid's *Elements* (c. -300), which constructs the hexagon within the circle as a fundamental proposition (Book IV, Prop. 15).
- **Islamic geometric art** (from c. 900–1200 CE onward): the girih tile system and arabesque hexagonal patterns deploy the same circle-hexagon geometry independently as an expression of Islamic geometric art principles (tawhid — unity expressed through mathematical pattern). Documented in Critchlow 1976.
- **Modern revival**: Melchizedek's 1990 books created the current New Age reception and established the name "Flower of Life."

## Meaning(s) attested
- **In Egyptian contexts (if Abydos attribution holds)**: The circles may encode cosmological proportion — the same sacred-geometric thinking documented in the Great Pyramid's proportions and in Egyptian temple geometry generally. No Egyptian text explicitly names or interprets the Flower of Life figure as such.
- **In Pythagorean tradition**: Hexagonal circle-packing encodes the Pythagorean cosmological principle that geometric ratios are the underlying structure of physical reality. The hexagon's generation from the circle (six circles of equal radius pack perfectly around one central circle) was seen as proof that geometric harmony is fundamental.
- **In Islamic geometric art**: The hexagonal circle pattern expresses *tawhid* (divine unity) — an infinite, non-representational pattern that reflects the infinite nature of Allah through mathematical regularity. Not mystically named but cosmologically significant in Islamic aesthetic theology.
- **In modern sacred-geometry reception**: The Flower of Life is held to contain all the fundamental geometric forms (the Platonic solids via Metatron's Cube), the structure of DNA, the proportions of the human body, and the cosmological blueprint of reality. These claims are Tier-3/4 — document as reception history of the New Age movement, not as academically established facts.

## Contested interpretations
- **The Abydos attribution**: The claim that Flower of Life patterns appear at the Temple of Seti I (c. 1290 BCE) is the cornerstone of the "ancient sacred knowledge" narrative in New Age reception. The Egyptological evidence is ambiguous: the circles are real; their status as deliberate Flower of Life configurations (vs. incidental compass marks or later additions) is not established by specialist Egyptological scholarship. This must be documented as a Tier-3 claim.
- **Universal transmission vs. independent generation**: Even if the Abydos attribution is accepted, the subsequent global appearances of the hexagonal circle pattern almost certainly reflect independent generation from the same minimal geometric logic (draw circle, center another circle on its circumference, repeat), rather than global diffusion from an Egyptian origin. The geometry is so simple that convergence is the default expectation.
- **Kabbalistic mapping**: The mapping of the Flower of Life's circles to the Kabbalistic Tree of Life sefirot (central 7 circles = 7 lower sefirot) is a modern esoteric construction (Tier-3/4), without classical Kabbalistic textual basis. It is real as reception history.
