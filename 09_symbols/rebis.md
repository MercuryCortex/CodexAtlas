# Rebis

## Identity

The **Rebis** (from Latin *res bina* — "double thing," "twofold matter") is the hermaphroditic culminating figure of alchemical iconography: the product of the **coniunctio oppositorum**, the conjunction of opposites that completes the Great Work. Typically depicted as a **two-headed figure** — one head male (crowned, solar), one female (crowned, lunar) — on a single body, standing on a winged dragon or globe, holding a **compass and square** (the Masonic measurement instruments that recur here centuries before Freemasonry as alchemical precision-symbols), with a **sun-disk** on one side and a **crescent moon** on the other. The Rebis is the alchemical image of the *Filius Philosophorum* ("Son of the Philosophers") — the glorified being born from the union of Sol and Luna, Sulphur and Mercury, King and Queen after the successive operations of the Great Work reach their completion.

The Rebis is one of the most visually arresting images in the Western esoteric tradition, deployed most memorably in the woodcuts of the **[[phase-6-032-rosarium-philosophorum|Rosarium Philosophorum]]** (c. 1470 manuscript, printed 1550) and extensively analyzed by **[[carl-gustav-jung]]** as the archetypal image of the psychic Self.

## Earliest attestation

- **c. 300–100 BCE** — The Sleeping Hermaphrodite sculpture type (Hellenistic; multiple Roman copies; the Louvre example is the most famous) represents the mythological Hermaphroditos — the figure in whom masculine and feminine have been permanently fused. Greek mythology traces the figure to Hermaphroditos, son of Hermes (Mercury) and Aphrodite, merged with the nymph Salmacis (Ovid, *Metamorphoses* IV.285–388).
- **c. 300 CE** — **Zosimos of Panopolis**, *Book of Sophe* and the *Visions of Zosimos* — the earliest Greek-alchemical texts to deploy anthropomorphic figures undergoing hermaphroditic transformation in mystical-alchemical visions.
- **c. 13th–14th c. CE** — Medieval Latin alchemical manuscripts begin depicting the crowned androgyne/Rebis figure explicitly.
- **c. 1470** — [[phase-6-032-rosarium-philosophorum|Rosarium Philosophorum]] — the most influential image-sequence of the *coniunctio* and Rebis; the 20-woodcut sequence is the canonical visual program of the alchemical marriage.
- **1617** — [[phase-6-035-maier-atalanta-fugiens|Michael Maier, *Atalanta Fugiens*]] — the Rebis and coniunctio themes throughout the 50 emblematic fugues.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Operative-alchemical** — The Rebis represents the **conjunction of gold (Sol) and silver (Luna)** in the alchemical vessel after the successive operations have been completed. The "two-headed" substance is the intermediate state before the final fixation into the Philosopher's Stone.
- **Spiritual-alchemical** — The Rebis is the perfected human being who has integrated the masculine and feminine principles of the soul — intellect and intuition, active and receptive, solar-conscious and lunar-unconscious. The Paracelsian tradition calls this the *Filius Philosophorum*.
- **Jungian-psychological** — The Rebis is [[carl-gustav-jung|Jung]]'s archetypal **Self** — the whole psyche achieved through individuation, in which the conscious persona (Solar King) unites with the previously unconscious shadow and anima/animus (Lunar Queen). *Psychology and Alchemy* and *Mysterium Coniunctionis* are the two major works developing this reading.
- **Cosmological** — The Rebis encodes the alchemical doctrine that the *prima materia* was originally undivided (pre-sexual) and that the Great Work returns matter to this original pre-differentiated completeness — a return to the primordial androgyne.

## Cross-family appearance

The Rebis as a *named concept* is Western alchemical. But the underlying structure — **the divine union of masculine and feminine principles as the completion of reality** — has extraordinarily close parallels in multiple traditions:

### Massive Win 1: Ardhanāriśvara (Hinduism)
**Ardhanāriśvara** (Sanskrit: "the Lord who is half woman") is the Hindu deity who is simultaneously **[[shiva|Shiva]]** (the right half, masculine, white, with the ascetic attributes of the Shaiva tradition) and **[[parvati|Pārvati/Śakti]]** (the left half, feminine, golden, with the abundant attributes of the Śākta tradition). Both the Rebis and Ardhanāriśvara represent the **divine totality as a permanent union of the masculine and feminine** in a single form that transcends the duality of sex. Both have two-sided iconography (the Rebis: two heads on one body, left-female/right-male; Ardhanāriśvara: left-half female/right-half male body). Both represent the completion of the cosmos — the state beyond opposition.

The parallel is **cosmological**, not merely iconographic. In Śaiva cosmology, Shiva without Śakti is *śava* (a corpse — Sanskrit: Shiva without the 'i' of Śakti becomes *śava*). The divine union is the precondition for the cosmos's existence and the goal of Tantric practice. [[tradition-kashmir-shaivism]] is the primary tradition carrying Ardhanāriśvara theology; [[tradition-vajrayana-buddhism]] (with its Tibetan *yab-yum* iconography of deity in union with consort) is a Buddhist parallel.

No documented historical transmission — genuinely independent convergence. Wendy Doniger O'Flaherty's *Ardhanāriśvara: The Androgyne* (1980) is the Tier-1 scholarly treatment.

### Massive Win 2: Plato's Original Androgyne (*Symposium*)
**Aristophanes' speech in Plato's *Symposium*** (c. 385 BCE, 189c–193e) describes the original human beings as **round, double-bodied spherical creatures** — some male-male, some female-female, some male-female (androgynous). Zeus split them in half to reduce their power, and the halves spend their lives seeking reunification. The Platonic androgyne is the **direct Greek philosophical ancestor** of the alchemical Rebis: both are images of a primordial completeness that was divided and must be restored. The transmission is historically documented: Renaissance alchemical authors (Ficino, Pico, Agrippa) read Plato's *Symposium* and explicitly drew on the androgyne speech in elaborating alchemical coniunctio theory. [[plato]] and [[tradition-neoplatonism]] are the nodes.

### Massive Win 3: Valentinian Gnostic Syzygy (Pneumatic Androgyne)
The Valentinian Gnostic tradition (2nd c. CE; Irenaeus, *Adversus Haereses* I; the Nag Hammadi tractates [[phase-4-006-hypostasis-of-the-archons]], [[phase-4-056-zostrianos]]) describes the perfected Gnostic being — the **Pneumatic** — as one who has reintegrated the masculine (divine spark of light) with the feminine (the Sophia/Ennoia complement). The Valentinian cosmological drama is the story of a divine syzygy (paired opposites) being broken apart (the Fall of Sophia) and ultimately restored (the Pleroma's completion). The alchemical Rebis enacts exactly this Valentinian-Gnostic theological structure at the level of the laboratory vessel. [[tradition-gnosticism]] carries this framework; the connection between Valentinian syzygy theology and alchemical coniunctio is documented in Gilles Quispel's scholarship.

### Massive Win 4: Tibetan Yab-Yum Iconography (Vajrayana Buddhism)
**Yab-yum** (Tibetan: "father-mother") — the Vajrayana Buddhist iconographic type depicting a male deity (compassion/method = masculine principle) in sexual union with a female consort (wisdom/emptiness = feminine principle) — represents the **inseparability of compassion and wisdom** as the enlightened state. The Rebis and yab-yum are independently produced anthropomorphic images of the same underlying insight: that the supreme state transcends or unifies masculine/feminine polarity. [[tradition-vajrayana-buddhism]] is the tradition; no documented historical transmission to Western alchemy — parallel-form.

### Massive Win 5: Dragon and Tiger (Chinese Neidan Internal Alchemy)
In Daoist **neidan** (internal alchemy), the **dragon** (yang, masculine, fire, mercury) and the **tiger** (yin, feminine, water, lead) are the two polar principles whose conjunction (*hé*) produces the elixir. The image of the dragon and tiger locked in embrace — depicted in Daoist alchemical manuscripts from the Tang dynasty onward — is the Chinese *Rebis*: the androgynous conjunction of yang and yin that transcends the polar duality. [[tradition-daoism]] carries this framework. No documented historical transmission — parallel-form across two independent alchemical traditions that developed simultaneously.

## Contested interpretations

- **Is the alchemical hermaphrodite figure "spiritual" or "operative"?** Principe 2013 argues for holding both readings simultaneously — the Rebis is neither purely symbolic-psychological nor purely laboratory-technical; it is a concrete image of what happens when gold and silver are *genuinely united* in the vessel, which alchemists believed was both a material and a spiritual event.
- **Does Ardhanāriśvara parallel the Rebis, or is the comparison anachronistic?** The comparison is structural, not historical. O'Flaherty 1980 is the Tier-1 Indological resource; no Western alchemist documented knowledge of Ardhanāriśvara before the modern period. The convergence is cross-tradition parallel-form, not transmission.
- **The Rebis and gender theory** — Modern feminist and queer readings of the Rebis (Stanton Linden, *Darke Hierogliphicks*; Lyndy Abraham, *A Dictionary of Alchemical Imagery*) have debated whether the alchemical androgyne represents a genuine non-binary vision or a patriarchal absorption of the feminine into a male-coded synthetic figure.
