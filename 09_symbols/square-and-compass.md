# Square and Compasses

## Identity

The **Square and Compasses** is the primary iconographic emblem of speculative **[[tradition-freemasonry|Freemasonry]]** — an interlocked carpenter's square (the right-angle tool of builders, forming an L-shape) and drafting compasses (the arc-drawing instrument for inscribing circles), typically with the letter **G** at the centre. As a composite symbol it is the most universally recognized Masonic glyph: it appears on lodge buildings, aprons, rings, certificates, and the covers of books of Masonic law worldwide. First documented in Grand Lodge usage after the formation of the Premier Grand Lodge of England in **1717**, the composite emblem becomes visually standardized on *tracing boards* by the **1730s–1740s**.

The symbol operates simultaneously on three levels: (1) **operative** — the actual tools of the stone-mason's trade; (2) **speculative-moral** — the square as righteousness (keeping one's actions within the rule of virtue), the compasses as circumscription of desire; (3) **cosmological** — the square measures the Earth (Chinese cosmological convention: the square = Earth), the compass inscribes the Heavens (the compass = Heaven), and together they represent the **Grand Architect of the Universe** who "laid the foundations of the earth" with measure and proportion.

## Earliest attestation

- **c. -2500** — Egyptian scribal-architect's square and plumb-bob appear in iconography of **[[thoth]]** (the divine scribe and measurer) and in tomb paintings depicting the weighing and measuring of the dead. The right angle is the instrument of divine justice.
- **c. -200 to +200 CE** — Han-dynasty tomb tiles (Shandong, China) depict the primordial pair **Nüwa** (holding compasses) and **Fuxi** (holding the square) flanking the cosmos — the earliest documented paired square + compass in explicit cosmological context.
- **c. -500** — **[[confucius]]** uses the *ju* (矩, carpenter's square) in *Analects* XV.24 as the emblem of moral rectitude: "Do not impose on others what you yourself do not want" — the square as the Golden Rule.
- **c. -500** — The **Pythagorean** sacred right triangle (3:4:5 = 90°) makes the right angle a sacred mathematical object. Pythagoras attributed divine perfection to the right angle; the Masonic tradition inherits this directly (see **[[tradition-pythagorean]]**).
- **1564** — **John Dee's *Monas Hieroglyphica*** uses the compass to inscribe the primordial geometric circle from which all creation proceeds — the geometer-God thesis in its Renaissance-Hermetic form ([[monas-hieroglyphica]]).
- **1723** — **[[phase-6-026-anderson-constitutions-free-masons|Anderson's *Constitutions* (1723)]]** enshrines Geometry (the first operative skill) as the root of all Masonic liberal arts.
- **1730s** — Earliest surviving Masonic tracing boards show square, compasses, and G as the standard lodge-floor-cloth emblem.
- **1794** — **[[william-blake]]**'s *The Ancient of Days* depicts the demiurgic god Urizen bending from a solar disk to inscribe a circle with golden compasses — Blake's ambivalent critique of the Masonic/Deist geometer-God.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Operative Masonic (pre-1717 guild tradition)**: The square tests right angles (soundness of construction); the compasses mark proportion (regularity of form). Without these, a building falls. The Junior Warden calls the lodge to labour; the square and compasses mark proper work.
- **Speculative Masonic (post-1717)**: The square = moral rectitude; the compasses = keeping passions "within due bounds." Masonic monitors universally interpret the two tools as governing the twin virtues of action (square) and restraint (compasses).
- **G = Geometry**: Mackey's *Encyclopedia* (1874) gives the primary reading as **Geometry** — "the first and noblest of sciences, the basis upon which the superstructure of Freemasonry is erected." The *Ars Geometriae* (the art of measurement) was the seventh of the seven liberal arts, the capstone of the medieval *quadrivium*.
- **G = God / Grand Architect**: The Deist reading, dominant in English Masonry since the 18th century. The Grand Architect of the Universe holds a square and compass as the builder of Creation.
- **G = Hebrew Gimel**: In Kabbalistic Masonry (especially *Régime Écossais Rectifié* and Scottish Rite contexts), G = the third letter of the Hebrew alphabet, numerical value 3, associated with divine grace (*gimel* = generosity of spirit). The square = Gevurah (Severity), the compass = Chesed (Mercy), G = the balance-point Keter (Crown) in the sefirot tree ([[sefirot-tree-of-life]]).
- **Chinese cosmological reading**: Square = Earth (*di*, 地); Compass = Heaven (*tian*, 天). This reading is **not** historically documented in Masonic monitors but is a formal parallel independently attested in Han iconography (Nüwa/Fuxi tomb tiles) that predates Freemasonry by 1,900 years.

## Cross-family appearance

The **square alone** as moral emblem appears in:
- **Chinese Confucian** — 矩 (carpenter's square) as metaphor for moral rectitude in the *Analects* XV.24 (see **[[confucius]]**). This is a documented independent parallel, not a transmission.
- **Egyptian** — The right-angle square (*menkhet*) is the instrument of divine measurement in temple construction; **[[thoth]]** as divine scribe holds or uses measurement tools.
- **Pythagorean** — The right angle is the sacred mathematical object in the 3-4-5 triangle; the *Pythagorean theorem* is invoked in Masonic degree ritual explicitly (**[[tradition-pythagorean]]**).

The **compass/circle** as divine-creative emblem appears in:
- **Dante** — *Paradiso* XXXIII: God as cosmic geometer inscribing the universe in circular form.
- **Blake** — *The Ancient of Days* (1794): demiurgic compass-wielder, ambivalently positioned between divine creator and repressive law.
- **Hebrew/Kabbalistic** — The compass inscribes the *Ein Sof* (the infinite circle) from which the sefirot emanate.

No documented transmission pathway connects the Chinese or Egyptian carpenter's-square-as-virtue emblem to the Masonic tradition. The convergence is a **parallel-form** instance of the same tool acquiring the same semiotic load (moral measurement) in independent cultural contexts.

## Contested interpretations

- **Did operative mason guilds actually use these symbols ritually before 1717?** Stevenson (1988) documents that the earliest verifiable Masonic rituals come from **Scottish** operative lodges of the late 16th century, where the square and compasses were indeed working tools with moral-allegorical overlay — but the composite *emblem* as a single iconographic unit may be a speculative-Masonic invention of the 18th century.
- **The G = Geometry vs. G = God priority question**: Mackey argues Geometry is primary; Pike argues God is primary. Neither can settle the question historically because the earliest monitors are ambiguous.
- **The Nüwa/Fuxi parallel as evidence of origin**: A persistent alternative-history claim holds that Freemasonry descended from ancient Chinese secret brotherhoods that transmitted the square-and-compass symbolism westward. No Tier-1 historical evidence supports this transmission; the parallel is documented but should be classified as a visual cognate / parallel form, not ancestral.
- **Was [[william-blake]]'s Ancient of Days pro-Masonic or anti-Masonic?** Blake scholars (Frye 1947, Raine 1968) generally read the image as a critique of the Masonic/Newtonian reduction of God to a measuring machine — the Urizen figure is not sympathetically portrayed. The image is simultaneously the most famous artistic deployment of the Masonic geometer-God and the most powerful artistic critique of it.
