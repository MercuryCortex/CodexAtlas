# Eye of Horus (Wedjat)

## Identity
The Wedjat (Egyptian *wḏꜣ.t*, "the Whole / Sound One") is the **stylized human eye** with characteristic markings — the elongated cosmetic line beneath, the curling "drop" or "tear-line" descending from the inner corner, the elaborated brow — that represents in Egyptian religion **the divine eye of [[horus]]** (in lunar contexts, the left eye, torn out by [[set]] and restored by [[thoth]]) or **the Eye of [[ra]]** (in solar contexts, the right eye, the divine power of the sun-god). It is **the single most-attested Egyptian protective-amulet symbol** — millions of wedjat-amulets survive from every period of Egyptian history. For the Codex Atlas mission the Wedjat is doubly load-bearing: (1) it is **the iconographic source** for the **Christian "Eye of Providence" / All-Seeing Eye** that runs from Coptic-Egyptian Christian art through medieval Christian iconography into Masonic symbolism into the US Great Seal (printed on every US dollar bill since 1935) — **the most-recognizable transmuted Egyptian symbol in modern Western iconography**; (2) it is **the principal Egyptian apotropaic amulet** of the entire 3,000-year pharaonic tradition, attested in tens of thousands of surviving copies.

## Earliest attestation
- First documentary appearance: First-Dynasty royal stelae (c. -3000); systematically attested in the [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] (Old Kingdom).
- The **Wedjat-restoration myth** is told in the **Contendings of Horus and Set** (preserved in Papyrus Chester Beatty I, ~-1160) — Horus's eye torn out by Set in their cosmic duel, recovered and made-whole by Thoth, returned to Horus. The restored eye is the *wḏꜣ.t*, "the Whole One."
- Iconographic earliest: First-Dynasty palette-fragments and seal-impressions; the canonical stylized form is fully developed by the Fourth Dynasty (c. -2600).
- Mass-attested in the **millions of surviving wedjat-amulets** from Old Kingdom through Roman period — the most-produced amulet-type in human history.

## Meaning(s) attested
- **Divine seeing-and-protection.** The Wedjat is the divine eye that watches over the wearer; the principal Egyptian apotropaic symbol.
- **Cosmic wholeness / restoration.** The restoration of the torn eye by Thoth represents cosmic order (*ma'at*) re-established after disorder; the Wedjat is the symbol of *being made whole again*.
- **Mathematical-fractional reading.** In Egyptian arithmetic the Wedjat's six parts represented six fractional values (1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 1/64) — the **"Horus-eye fractions"** used in grain measurement. The fractions sum to 63/64, with the missing 1/64 sometimes mystically explained as the portion Thoth provided to make the eye whole.
- **Lunar phases.** The left-Wedjat (Horus's eye) is associated with the moon; its waxing-and-waning is the eye's torn-and-restored cycle.
- **Solar power** (right-Wedjat / Eye of Ra). The right eye of Ra is the destructive-protective solar power dispatched against humanity in the *Destruction of Mankind* myth.

## Cross-family appearance

### MASSIVE-win transmission chain: Wedjat → Christian Eye of Providence → Masonic All-Seeing Eye → US Dollar Bill

1. **Greco-Egyptian magical-amulet continuity (1st–4th c. CE).** The Wedjat survives as a generic eye-amulet in Greco-Egyptian magical-papyri amulet tradition (Bonner 1950 documents extensively); the eye-amulet becomes apotropaic-protective vocabulary across the eastern Mediterranean.

2. **Coptic Christian continuity (200–700 CE).** Coptic Christianity continues to use eye-protective imagery on textiles, manuscripts, and grave-stelae. The Wedjat-form is gradually Christianized. **Frankfurter 1998** documents the continuity. The eye-of-God in early Christian art (e.g., 4th-c. catacomb frescoes) has continuity with the Coptic eye-imagery.

3. **Medieval Christian "Eye of God" / divine Providence (10th–17th c.).** The Christian "eye of God" iconography — single eye looking down from heaven, often within a triangle representing the Trinity — develops in medieval European Christian art. The triangular frame is theologically explained as the Trinity; the eye is divine omniscience-and-providence.

4. **Masonic All-Seeing Eye (18th c. onward).** The Masonic "All-Seeing Eye" emerges in 18th-c. European Freemasonry, drawing on (a) the Christian Eye of Providence, (b) Renaissance Hermetic Egyptophilia (Kircher's *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* 1652–1654 included extensive Wedjat-imagery — see [[tradition-freemasonry]]).

5. **US Great Seal and dollar bill (1782 / 1935).** The All-Seeing Eye atop the unfinished pyramid on the reverse of the US Great Seal was designed in 1782; placed on the dollar bill in 1935. Whether the designers consciously drew on Egyptian-Masonic-Christian iconographic vocabulary is debated — but the iconographic genealogy through the Christian-Masonic chain back to Egypt is real.

The chain represents one of the **single most-documented surviving pieces of Egyptian-religious iconography in modern Western culture**, though almost nobody walking past a dollar bill knows it.

### Other cross-tradition diffusions
- **Hand of Fatima / Hamsa** (Levantine-Islamic) — apotropaic palm-with-eye amulet. **Visual cognate** with Wedjat; some scholars argue genealogical descent via Phoenician transmission, others see independent invention from the broader Near Eastern eye-amulet tradition.
- **Greek/Mediterranean *mati* / evil-eye-eye-amulet** — the blue-glass eye-amulet of modern Greek/Turkish/Levantine apotropaic practice. **Parallel-form** with Wedjat — same Near Eastern eye-amulet tradition.

## Contested interpretations
- **Wedjat-fractions reality.** The Horus-eye-fractions reading was the standard 20th-c. Egyptological interpretation; some recent scholarship (Ritter 2003) argues the fractional system is **post hoc Egyptological reconstruction** and not how Egyptians actually used the eye-marks. The system is real as a Late-period-Egyptian-priestly construction but not as a deep-historical practice.
- **Wedjat → Eye of Providence directness.** Whether the Christian Eye of Providence is *directly* descended from Wedjat (through Coptic-continuity) or whether it emerged independently in Christian art from biblical "eye of the Lord" passages (Ps 33:18, Prov 15:3) is debated. Most scholarship treats it as **multiply-determined** — biblical text providing scriptural warrant + Egyptian iconographic vocabulary providing the visual form, mediated through Coptic-Christian Egyptian Christianity.

## Mystery / unknown variants
None — the Wedjat is a well-documented and decipherable hieroglyph. `mystery-status: documented`.
