# Rongorongo

## Identity

Rongorongo (*kohau rongorongo* = "recitation lines" or "lines to chant") is an **undeciphered hieroglyphic/logographic script** found on 26 surviving wooden tablets from Rapa Nui (Easter Island). It consists of rows of stylized zoomorphic, anthropomorphic, and abstract figures incised into wood, arranged in a distinctive **reverse boustrophedon** pattern: the reader proceeds left-to-right for one line, then physically rotates the tablet 180° to read the next line right-to-left — each line is read in the opposite direction and upside-down from the previous. Rongorongo is one of only three or four writing systems believed to have been **independently invented** in human history, making it the world's most isolated and most recently documented case of spontaneous script invention — and the most completely lost.

## Earliest attestation

- **Settlement of Rapa Nui (c. 800–1200 CE)**: Polynesian settlers, probably from the Marquesas Islands or Society Islands, arrived on Rapa Nui. Polynesian cultures generally did not use writing; no ancestor of rongorongo is known in the Polynesian homeland.
- **First European contact (1722, 1770)**: Dutch explorer Jacob Roggeveen (1722) and Spanish expedition (1770) make contact. The 1770 Spanish expedition recorded "signatures" of Rapa Nui chiefs on a treaty — the marks are consistent with rongorongo glyph forms, suggesting the script existed before Spanish contact.
- **Bishop Jaussen of Tahiti (1869)**: Tepano Jaussen, Catholic Bishop of Tahiti, received a gift of a rongorongo tablet and subsequently interviewed a Rapa Nui informant (Metoro Tau'a Ure) who could chant the tablets — but whose chant apparently did not correspond closely to the glyphs. This is the only recorded instance of an eyewitness encounter with someone claiming to read rongorongo.
- **Last documented reader (c. 1868–1870s)**: The Peruvian slave raids of 1862 (which killed or enslaved ~1,500 Rapa Nui people, including virtually all the *ariki* [chiefs] and *tangata rongorongo* [learned men] who could read the tablets) and the subsequent smallpox epidemic that killed most returnees destroyed the oral tradition of reading. By the 1870s, no one on the island could read the tablets.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Creation chants and cosmological texts**: Rapa Nui oral tradition (recorded by Jaussen, Métraux 1940, and Fischer 1997) associates the tablets with cosmological creation chants — specifically *ko te tanga* (the creation chant) and liturgical genealogies of deities and sacred lineages. The tablets were recited by the *tangata rongorongo* during religious ceremonies, not read silently.
- **Ritual performance medium**: The tablets functioned as **mnemonic devices for performance** rather than direct transcription of speech — similar to the Aztec *amoxtli* or Polynesian *tapa* genealogy objects. The glyphs may have cued specific chants without encoding them phonetically.
- **Fischer's 1997 proposed decipherment**: Fischer claimed to have partially deciphered the script as cosmological procreation chants of the form "[sun] impregnated [moon], so that there came forth: the birds, the fishes, the plants." His reading of Tablet C (Mamari) has gained some acceptance; his broader system is **widely rejected by specialists** as requiring too many assumptions. Fischer's corpus work (sign catalogue, tablet descriptions) is indispensable Tier 1; his decipherment is Tier 3/contested.
- **The astronomical tablet claim**: Tablet C (Mamari) contains a section Barthel identified as a lunar calendar — 29-30 glyphs corresponding to the lunar cycle. This is the most widely accepted partial reading (Tier 2), though exact interpretation varies.

## Variants

### The 26 Authentic Tablets
The corpus is small and finite: 26 tablets held in museums worldwide. The most important:
- **Tablet B (Aruku-Kurenga)**: Vatican; the "recitation lines" tablet, possibly liturgical.
- **Tablet C (Mamari)**: Chilean museums; contains the possible lunar calendar section.
- **Tablet I (Santiago Staff)**: Museo Nacional de Historia Natural, Santiago; the longest text (~2300 glyphs), possibly a staff of authority. Its relationship to other tablets is debated.
- **Tablet H (Great St. Petersburg Tablet)**: Kunstkamera, St. Petersburg; very long text.

### The Boustrophedon Reading System
The reverse boustrophedon is itself a symbol: the reader's physical rotation of the tablet at each line-break — turning the world upside-down and right-side-around alternately — is a formal enactment of the cosmic alternation encoded in the chants. The act of reading mirrors the cosmological oscillation of the content. This physical-cognitive loop (the form of reading reflects the content being read) is a distinctive feature with no known parallel in other writing systems.

### The Glyph Vocabulary
Barthel (1958) catalogued approximately **600 distinct signs**, reducible to about 120 base glyphs with systematic combination variations. The signs are predominantly **zoomorphic** (bird-men, fish, lizards, turtles) and **anthropomorphic** (figures with heads, limbs, phalluses), combined with geometric elements. The bird-man (Tangata Manu) — the central religious figure of Rapa Nui's annual Birdman competition ceremony — appears prominently, as does the deity [[make-make]] (supreme creator deity in Rapa Nui tradition, though a deity node does not yet exist in this vault).

## Cross-family appearance

**Independent Invention: The Rarest Category**
The scholarly consensus (Fischer 1997, Barthel 1958, Horley 2010) is that rongorongo represents one of only three to four cases of independent writing-system invention in human history:
1. Mesopotamian cuneiform (c. 3400 BCE)
2. Egyptian hieroglyphs (c. 3200 BCE — possibly inspired by Mesopotamia, though debated)
3. Chinese oracle-bone script (c. 1250 BCE)
4. Rongorongo (post-contact, if the Spanish-inspiration hypothesis is correct, or pre-contact, if independent)

The question of independence is the central scholarly debate. If rongorongo was invented after 1770 Spanish contact — inspired by seeing Europeans write on documents during the treaty-signing — it is a remarkable case of **stimulus diffusion** (seeing that writing is possible → inventing your own system from scratch). If it predates contact, it is the most recent genuine independent invention.

**The Isolation Factor: The Most Extreme Case**
Easter Island is the most isolated inhabited place on Earth: 3,512 km from the nearest continental coast (Chile), 1,900 km from the nearest inhabited island (Pitcairn). The Polynesian settlers who developed rongorongo (if independently) did so in total intellectual isolation from all other writing traditions. The tablets themselves are carved on scarce wood — driftwood and the nearly-extinct toromiro tree — in a treeless landscape. Every aspect of rongorongo's material culture reflects the island's extreme resource scarcity.

**The Destruction of the Tradition: A Unique Historical Event**
In 1862–1865, Peruvian slave ships kidnapped approximately 1,500 Rapa Nui people — roughly one-third of the entire population — specifically targeting the chiefly class (*ariki*) and the learned men (*tangata rongorongo*) who could read the tablets. Disease killed most on the slave ships; a few survivors returned, carrying smallpox that killed most of the remaining population. By 1877, the Rapa Nui population was reduced from ~3,000–15,000 (at contact) to **111 people**. The oral tradition of reading rongorongo — whatever it contained — was destroyed in a decade by the Peruvian slave trade. This is the fastest and most complete destruction of a writing tradition on record.

## Contested interpretations

- **Independent invention vs. stimulus diffusion**: The major divide. If Spanish contact (1770) preceded rongorongo's invention, it may be stimulus diffusion — the idea of writing adopted; the system invented locally. The 1770 Spanish treaty "signatures" are ambiguous; they could be existing glyph forms or ad hoc marks. Métraux (1940) and Barthel (1958) lean toward pre-contact origin; Fischer (1997) is agnostic.
- **Fischer's decipherment (1997)**: His reading of the procreation chant formula has been criticized by Sproat (2003) using information-theoretic methods: the formula is too repetitive to match the statistical properties of known language, suggesting it may be an artifact of Fischer's interpretive method rather than a real decipherment.
- **Logographic vs. phonetic**: Whether rongorongo encodes words directly (logographic), sounds (phonetic), or functions as a mnemonic device without strict encoding is unknown. The small corpus prevents statistical analysis.
- **Carbon dating paradox**: The tablets date to the 19th century CE (the wood; the inscriptions are presumably contemporary with or slightly later than the wood). This makes rongorongo the most recent writing system in the vault — yet potentially the most isolated independent invention.

## Mystery / unknown variants

Rongorongo is classified **mystery**: no confirmed reading of any inscription exists. The sign values are unknown; the language being encoded (if any) is unknown; the direction of decipherment — whether to start from phonetic, logographic, or mnemonic assumptions — is unknown. The corpus is too small (26 tablets, ~15,000 total glyphs) for statistical decipherment methods to be decisive. Rongorongo stands as the world's most completely and most recently lost writing system.

**What IS known**: The approximate sign inventory (Barthel 1958 sign catalogue, 600 signs / 120 base glyphs); the reading direction (reverse boustrophedon); the probable association with ritual chanting and cosmological content; the date of destruction of the reading tradition (1862–1870s). Everything else is silence.
