# Star of Ishtar (Eight-pointed Star)

## Identity
The **Star of Ishtar** is an **eight-pointed star** that serves as the principal iconographic emblem of **[[inanna-sumerian|Inanna]] (Sumerian)** / **[[ishtar-akkadian|Ishtar]] (Akkadian/Babylonian/Assyrian)** — the Mesopotamian goddess of love, war, and the **planet Venus**. The eight points correspond to **Venus's eight-year synodic cycle** (5 inferior-superior conjunctions = ~8 Earth-years — the closest near-integer ratio in inner-planet astronomy). The star is one of the most continuously-attested Mesopotamian iconographic forms — appearing on **cylinder seals, boundary-stones (*kudurru*), royal-inscription steles, and temple-reliefs** from the **Akkadian period (~-2350)** through the **Neo-Babylonian period (~-539)** — approximately 1,800 years of continuous attestation.

The Star of Ishtar is **the iconographic anchor of the Mesopotamian substrate of the Mediterranean mystery-cult tradition** — Inanna's descent-and-return to the underworld (the foundational [[phase-1-017-descent-of-inanna|*Descent of Inanna*]] narrative, c. -2000 BCE) is the documented prototype of the Mediterranean mystery-cult katabasis-and-anabasis motif (see [[katabasis-and-anabasis]]).

## Earliest attestation
- **c. -2350 BCE** — Akkadian-period cylinder seals show the goddess Ishtar with the eight-pointed star.
- **c. -1750 BCE** — **[[phase-1-007-code-of-hammurabi|Code of Hammurabi stele]]** — the eight-pointed star appears on the stele alongside the sun-disk of Shamash and the moon-crescent of Sin.
- **c. -1650 BCE** — **Venus Tablet of Ammisaduqa** (the foundational Babylonian astronomical document for Venus observations).
- **Kassite, Middle Assyrian, Neo-Assyrian, Neo-Babylonian periods** — continuous attestation on *kudurru*, royal steles, temple-reliefs, and cylinder seals.
- **c. -539 BCE onward** — declining attestation after the Persian conquest; the iconographic form passes into broader Achaemenid Near-Eastern visual vocabulary.

## Meaning(s) attested
- **Inanna/Ishtar's astral identity** — the goddess as the planet Venus.
- **Venus's eight-year synodic cycle** — the eight-pointedness encodes the astronomical-Venus-Earth-year ratio.
- **Celestial trinity** — with Sin (moon) and Shamash (sun), Ishtar (Venus-star) forms the celestial-witness trinity invoked on *kudurru* and royal inscriptions.
- **Divine sovereignty + love + war** — Inanna/Ishtar's three-aspect identity is the conceptual content the star-emblem points to.

## Cross-family appearance
The Mesopotamian eight-pointed star is **iconographically antecedent** to some later Western and Near-Eastern eight-pointed-star forms, but **direct documented transmission is rare**:
- **Maltese cross** (~12th c. CE) — eight-pointed but distinct genealogy (Christian heraldic).
- **Christian octagram** — early Christian baptisterial-architecture eight-sided forms (the 8 = "eighth day" = resurrection) are theologically motivated, not Mesopotamian-descended.
- **Islamic *Rub al-Hizb*** (the eight-pointed star marking Quranic divisions) — visually parallel but no documented Mesopotamian descent.
- **Star of Lakshmi** (Hindu) — parallel-form only.

The Mesopotamian eight-pointed-star is best understood as **a regional Mesopotamian iconographic form that exerted broad cultural influence on Near-Eastern and Mediterranean visual vocabulary without securely-documentable specific-symbol-transmissions** to later traditions.

## Contested interpretations
- **Astronomical content.** The Venus-cycle interpretation is well-defended (Brown 2000) but some Mesopotamian-religion scholars (Frankfort) read the eight-pointedness primarily as *celestial-radiance* rather than specifically Venus-cycle. The Venus-Tablet of Ammisaduqa anchors the Venus identification securely.
- **Transmission to later iconographic forms.** Most cross-tradition eight-pointed-star claims are **parallel-form** rather than **ancestor-of** — direct iconographic descent is rarely documentable.
