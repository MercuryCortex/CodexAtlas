# Mount Ararat (the Ark's resting-place)

## Identity
**Mount Ararat** is the cosmological-geographical symbol of the Ark's post-Flood resting-place in the Hebrew-Christian-Islamic-Armenian flood-tradition. Genesis 8:4 names the *plural* "mountains of Ararat" (Hebrew *harey Ararat*) as the location where Noah's Ark grounds after the flood-waters recede; the *Ararat* of Genesis is the Hebrew form of the Akkadian *Urartu* — the kingdom of eastern Anatolia / the Armenian Highlands (c. 9th–6th c. BCE, the principal northern neighbor and rival of Neo-Assyria).

The specific identification with the *single* peak now called Mount Ararat (Turkish *Ağrı Dağı*, "Mountain of Pain"; Armenian *Masis*; 39°42′N 44°17′E; 5137 m elevation; volcanic; on the modern Turkey-Armenia-Iran-Azerbaijan border-region) is a **medieval and post-medieval Christian-Armenian identification**, not the original Hebrew Bible specification (which names a *region*, the mountains-of-Ararat / Urartu, not a *specific* peak). The Armenian Christian tradition has identified Masis-Ararat as the Ark's resting-place from at least the 5th century CE (Movses Khorenatsi's *History of Armenia*).

## Earliest attestation

- **Genesis 8:4 (Hebrew Bible, P-source ~-550 BCE):** *"And the ark rested in the seventh month, on the seventeenth day of the month, upon the mountains of Ararat"* — plural *harey Ararat*, the *region* of Urartu.
- **Gilgamesh XI 140–145 (Standard Babylonian, ~-1300 BCE):** [[utnapishtim]]'s Ark grounds on **Mount Nimush** (older reading *Mount Nisir*) — a different mountain. Mount Nimush has been variously identified with peaks in the Zagros range (Kurdistan / Iran).
- **Quran 11:44 (~7th c. CE):** *"It was said: O earth! Swallow your water; O sky! Withhold. And the water abated and the matter was completed and the Ark came to rest upon [the mountain] al-Jūdī."* — Mount **Cudi** (in southeastern Anatolia, near Cizre, modern Turkey/Iraq border) is the Islamic identification, distinct from the Genesis Ararat.
- **Movses Khorenatsi, *History of Armenia* (5th c. CE):** the Armenian Christian identification of Masis-Ararat with the Ark's resting-place.

The three principal flood-tradition mountain-identifications (Mount Ararat / Genesis; Mount Cudi / Quran; Mount Nimush / Gilgamesh XI) are **distinct mountains** in three different traditions, all roughly in the same geographic-cultural region of eastern Anatolia / northern Mesopotamia. The cross-tradition geographic convergence-but-divergence is itself an interesting comparative-religion data point.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Hebrew:** the geographic terminus of the Flood-narrative; the place where God's restorative-covenant work begins.
- **Christian:** continuous-with-Hebrew; further mythologized in medieval-Christian-Armenian tradition as a place of pilgrimage and theological-historical contemplation.
- **Islamic (Cudi):** Quranic explicit-naming of the Ark's resting-place at Jūdī, with Islamic pilgrimage and Sufi-mystical reception centered there.
- **Armenian Christian:** Masis-Ararat as Armenia's *holy mountain*, integrated with Armenian national-religious identity; the mountain appears on the modern Armenian coat-of-arms despite its post-1923 location entirely within Turkey.

## Cross-family appearance

The Mount Ararat / Mount Cudi / Mount Nimush triple-identification across Hebrew / Islamic / Mesopotamian flood-traditions is structurally cognate: each tradition specifies a mountain as the Ark's resting-place, but the specific peak differs. This is **parallel-form** across traditions rather than direct transmission — each tradition independently localizes the Ark-resting in its own geographic-religious frame.

## Contested interpretations

- **The "Ark on Mount Ararat" search-claims.** Multiple modern claims (1840s onward; intensifying since the 1970s with the Hagopian, Wyatt, and Cornuke claims) of Ark-discoveries on or near Mount Ararat are NOT accepted by mainstream archaeology. Feder 2017 *Frauds, Myths, and Mysteries* gives the critical treatment. The Ararat-Ark search-tradition is part of the broader Tier-4 alternative-archaeology field that occasionally surfaces in popular media; mainstream archaeological-and-geological scholarship treats the search as misguided.
- **Geological volcano-vs-flood-witness question.** Mount Ararat is geologically a *volcano* (Quaternary), not a sedimentary feature; the search for water-borne Ark debris on a volcanic peak is geologically inappropriate.
