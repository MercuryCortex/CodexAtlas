# The Sacred Mirror

## Identity

The sacred mirror is a distinct symbolic type: unlike most sacred objects that mediate *upward* (human → divine), the mirror mediates *reflexively* — it shows the divine TO the divine, or reflects the divine world back to the human observer. The mirror's defining quality is its reflexivity: it does not transform what it receives but returns it. This reflexivity becomes theologically generative across traditions: the divine-as-mirror means that creation is God's self-reflection; the purified human heart-as-mirror means that the mystic reflects the divine attributes back to their source; the obsidian-mirror-as-oracle means that the future is already present and the mirror merely reveals what is hidden. Four distinct traditions develop these implications independently.

## Earliest attestation

- Bronze mirrors as sacred objects in China: from c. 3000 BCE (Majiayao culture), though the fully developed sacred-mirror theology is Shinto Japan (7th–8th c. CE in extant written sources, though much older in practice).
- **Yata no Kagami** (Japan): Created in the myth cycle recorded in the *Nihon Shoki* (720 CE) and the *Kojiki* (712 CE) — Aston (1896), Tier 1 for primary source — during the episode of Amaterasu's retreat into the cave. The gods hung the mirror outside the cave to lure Amaterasu out; she saw her own radiance in it and emerged.
- **Ibn ʿArabī's heart-as-mirror**: Fully developed in the *Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam* (1229 CE) — [[phase-5-021-ibn-arabi-fusus-al-hikam]] — though the heart-mirror image appears in earlier Sufi literature (al-Ghazālī, *Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn*, 11th c.).
- **Tezcatlipoca**: Attested in post-conquest codices (16th c. CE) recording pre-Columbian traditions; the smoking-mirror is one of the earliest and most fundamental Aztec divine attributes.
- **John Dee's obsidian mirror**: The specific object is now in the British Museum (EA 1966 Am12.1). Provenance established by Read (1925), Tier 1: Aztec ritual object acquired by European collectors in the 16th c. and attributed to Dee by the mid-17th c.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Shinto**: The Yata no Kagami is not a representation of Amaterasu — it is her *shintai* (divine body-object, the physical substrate of her presence). The mirror shows the deity to herself = self-knowledge as divinity. This is theologically distinct from a cult-image: the mirror does not depict the deity, it IS the deity's presence. Aston (1896) provides the foundational primary-source documentation (Tier 1).
- **Sufi / Ibn ʿArabī**: "The heart of the believer is the mirror of God" (*qalb al-muʾmin mirʾāt Allāh*). The mirror must be polished (= purified by spiritual practice, especially dhikr) to reflect truly. A tarnished mirror reflects a distorted image = spiritual ignorance. The fully polished mirror reflects ALL of the divine attributes = the Perfect Human (*al-Insān al-Kāmil*). This is the central epistemological image of Akbarian Sufism. Chittick (1989) is the Tier 1 scholarly treatment.
- **Aztec Tezcatlipoca**: The smoking obsidian mirror set in place of Tezcatlipoca's severed foot (lost in the cosmic battle with Cipactli) sees all that is done and concealed. Those who gaze into it see their sins and their futures. Tezcatlipoca uses the mirror to test and destroy, or reward. The mirror = omniscience = the night-sky reflection of the stars. Clendinnen (1991) is Tier 1.
- **Greek (Perseus/Medusa)**: Athena provides Perseus a shield polished to mirror-brightness to see Medusa's reflection without being turned to stone. The mythological logic: the direct gaze of the sacred/divine kills (cf. the vision of God, Ex. 33:20); the mediated gaze (reflection) is survivable. The mirror is the technology of safe divine encounter.

## Variants

### The Tabernacle Laver as Mirror-to-Water

Exodus 38:8: The *kiyor* (כִּיּוֹר, bronze laver) of the Tabernacle was made from the **bronze mirrors of the serving women** (*ṣōbəʾōt*) who served at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting. The mirrors were melted and recast as the purification vessel. The same reflective surface quality (bronze mirror = water surface = reflection) serves the same function (purification before sacred encounter). This is a documented intra-Biblical transformation.

### Cultural Reception: Mirror of Erised

J.K. Rowling's Mirror of Erised (*Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone*, 1997) — a mirror that shows the viewer their deepest desire — is a modern literary recapitulation of the scrying mirror and the Sufi heart-mirror: the mirror reveals what is most essentially true about the viewer. The name is "desire" spelled backwards (mirrors reverse). This is cultural reception, not independent tradition; noted as evidence of the symbol's ongoing generative power.

## Cross-family appearance

The sacred mirror appears across traditions with **no documented transmission** between Shinto Japan, Aztec Mesoamerica, and Sufi Islam:

- **Shinto Japan**: Yata no Kagami as divine embodiment — 8th c. CE written sources, oral tradition much older.
- **Aztec Mesoamerica**: Tezcatlipoca's smoking mirror — independent development in a completely isolated tradition.
- **Sufi Islam**: Ibn ʿArabī's heart-as-mirror — emerging from Neoplatonic and Quranic sources, no Japanese or Mesoamerican influence possible.
- **Greek tradition**: Perseus's shield-mirror — earlier than all of the above, with no influence on them.

The **physical transmission** of the Dee obsidian mirror is the one documented exception: an Aztec ritual mirror, possibly a Tezcatlipoca cult-object, in the hands of the foremost Elizabethan magician. The Aztec sacred-mirror technology reached Europe through colonial contact and was repurposed in a Hermetic context — a genuine cross-cultural transmission mediated by material culture (Read 1925, Tier 1).

## Contested interpretations

- Whether the Yata no Kagami is currently housed at the Ise Inner Shrine (Naikū) or at the Imperial Palace (Kashikodokoro) is a matter of ongoing respectful ambiguity in Shinto scholarship; the actual objects are not publicly viewable and their physical state is not confirmed.
- Whether Ibn ʿArabī's heart-as-mirror is a fully original contribution or a systematization of earlier Sufi mirror imagery (al-Ghazālī, Rūmī) is debated in Islamic mysticism scholarship; Chittick (1989) traces the development.
- The provenance attribution of the British Museum obsidian disc specifically to John Dee is documented but not beyond doubt; the object is certainly Aztec and certainly circulated in English hermetic circles by the 17th c.

## Mystery / unknown variants

No undeciphered elements. The symbolism is thoroughly documented in each tradition. The Yata no Kagami itself is the closest thing to a genuine mystery: a sacred object that is physically inaccessible and whose current state is, by design, unknown.

## Cross-symbol edges (provisional)

- **Parallel-form** with [[eye-of-the-heart]]: Ibn ʿArabī's mirror-heart and the Sufi eye of the heart are co-extensive concepts — the purified organ of divine perception, described now as seeing, now as reflecting.
- **Parallel-form** with [[tetragrammaton]]: In Dee's practice, the Tetragrammaton and the obsidian mirror are co-instruments of angelic communication — the divine name and the reflective surface as paired access-points.
- **Parallel-form** with [[gorgoneion]]: Perseus's mirror-shield and the Gorgoneion: the polished surface that makes the encounter survivable; the apotropaic face that the mirror indirectly confronts.
- **Parallel-form** with [[ouroboros]]: The mirror's reflexivity (divine self-showing) mirrors the ouroboros's self-consuming/self-generating loop — both figure divine self-containment.
