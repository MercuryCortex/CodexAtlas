# Vesica piscis

## Identity

The vesica piscis (Latin "fish-bladder") is the lens-shaped figure formed by the overlap of two equal circles whose centres lie on each other's circumferences. Mathematically the figure is foundational: Euclid's *Elements* I.1 (c. -300) uses precisely this two-circle construction to construct the equilateral triangle. Iconographically the form becomes central to medieval Christian art as the *mandorla* (Italian "almond"), the vesica-shaped frame enclosing Christ in Glory or other full-body theophanies. A separate hypothesis (Bagatti 1971) reads the early Christian *ichthys* fish-symbol as derived from the lower arc of the vesica.

## Earliest attestation

- **c. -300 BCE** — Euclid's *Elements* I.1 uses the two-equal-circle (vesica-piscis) construction. Foundational Greek-geometric attestation.
- **Hellenistic and Roman-period geometric and Pythagorean contexts** — the figure recurs as a foundational geometric construction with possible mystical readings in late Pythagorean and Neoplatonic literature.
- **Early Christian iconography (3rd–4th c. CE)** — possible vesica-piscis form underlying the *ichthys* fish-glyph (Bagatti 1971); disputed reading.
- **Byzantine icon tradition (6th c. CE onward)** — vesica-piscis-shaped *mandorla* enclosing Christ Pantokrator, Christ in Glory, the Virgin in Assumption, and the Transfiguration scene. Standard iconographic convention from this period onward.
- **Romanesque and Gothic Western sculpture (10th–13th c. CE)** — tympana of Vézelay, Moissac, Autun, Conques use mandorla as Christ-in-Glory frame on church portals.
- **Renaissance Hermeticism** — the vesica-piscis becomes a "sacred geometry" figure alongside hexagram and pentagram in the Hermetic-Pythagorean revival (Ficino, Pico, Agrippa, Dee, Fludd).
- **Modern New Age / sacred-geometry literature (Tier 4)** — extensive 20th-c. reception in popular sacred-geometry literature (Drunvalo Melchizedek, etc.); these are NOT scholarly sources but are noted for reception-history completeness.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Euclidean** — geometric primitive used to construct the equilateral triangle. (*Elements* I.1.)
- **Byzantine / Christian mandorla** — the lens-shaped frame as the visible boundary of the divine presence; encloses Christ in Glory, the Virgin, the Transfiguration. Iconographically signals theophanic moment. (Demus 1948; Cormack 2000.)
- **Romanesque-Gothic Western** — same mandorla function, often with Tetramorph (four evangelists in symbolic form) at the corners.
- **Pythagorean / Renaissance Hermetic** — figure of harmony between unity (each circle) and multiplicity (their intersection); golden-ratio-derivable properties similar to the pentagram.
- **Bagatti's ichthys hypothesis** — the vesica-piscis lower arc as origin of the early Christian fish-glyph. Disputed; see [[fish-ichthys]] for full discussion.

## Variants

The three principal attestations (Euclidean construction, Christian mandorla, ichthys-derivation hypothesis) are documented in YAML. The mandorla is iconographically by far the most-studied variant.

## Cross-family appearance

The vesica-piscis is essentially a **Mediterranean Greek-Christian-Hermetic** form, not multi-continental like the swastika or lotus. Its cross-tradition reach is internal to the Greek → Christian → Renaissance Hermetic line.

- **Greek geometric → Christian mandorla** — `parallel-form`. The Christian mandorla likely draws on the Greek-geometric vocabulary but the iconographic transmission is not document-specific; the form was available in late-antique geometric and decorative contexts when Byzantine icon-tradition crystallized.
- **Possibly underlying the ichthys** — `ancestor-of` candidate per Bagatti 1971, disputed by Snyder 1985 and most Anglo-American patristics; both views cited at [[fish-ichthys]].

## Contested interpretations

- The vesica-piscis origin of the ichthys (Bagatti 1971) vs. the acrostic-letter origin (Snyder 1985, Jensen 2017). The acrostic-origin reading is the Anglo-American mainstream; the vesica-derivation reading is held by some Italian Catholic archaeologists. The two are not strictly incompatible (the early Christians may have noted multiple resonances) but are usually presented as alternative origin-stories.
- Whether the Byzantine mandorla was a deliberate Greek-geometric inheritance or rediscovered iconographically. The scholarly consensus (Cormack 2000) is that the mandorla is best read as developed within the late-antique iconographic vocabulary rather than as a self-conscious "sacred geometry" act.
