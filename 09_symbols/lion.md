# Lion

## Identity

The lion is one of the densest royal-power-and-divine-guardian iconographic vocabularies in the human record. The animal carries multiple distinct registers: royal hunt-as-mastery (Assyrian palace reliefs); guardian-of-throne (Mesopotamian lamassu, Throne of Solomon); cult-mount (Durga's lion); cult-deity attribute (Sekhmet, Cybele); enthroned-saviour (Buddha's *siṃhāsana*); evangelist-symbol (Mark in the Tetramorph); messianic-emblem (Lion of Judah → Jesus → Haile Selassie). The form is attested from Sumerian and Egyptian Bronze Age contexts (c. -3000 onward) through the present Rastafari practice.

## Earliest attestation

- **c. -3000 BCE Egyptian and Mesopotamian** — Egyptian lion-deities (Sekhmet, Mehit), royal-lion-hunt iconography. Mesopotamian temple-guardian lions, kings-as-lions.
- **c. -2500 BCE — Great Sphinx of Giza** — the iconic lion-bodied royal-guardian.
- **Mesopotamian palace lion-hunt reliefs (c. -883 to -627 BCE)** — Assyrian palace reliefs (Nimrud Ashurnasirpal II, Nineveh Ashurbanipal) depict the royal-lion-hunt as imperial ideology. (Bleibtreu 1991.)
- **Lamassu-with-lion-variant (c. -900 onward)** — Assyrian palace gate guardians include winged-lion-headed forms.
- **Hebrew Bible — Throne of Solomon (1 Kings 10:18-20)** — twelve lions flanking. Lion of Judah (Genesis 49:9, J-source). ([[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]])
- **Anatolian-Phrygian Cybele (c. -1500 onward; Roman period 2nd c. BCE adoption)** — lion-throne / lion-drawn-chariot Magna Mater iconography.
- **Daniel-in-the-lion's-den (Book of Daniel, c. -165 redaction)** — Hellenistic-era Jewish narrative inverting the lion-hunt: God protects the righteous Daniel from the lions. ([[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]])
- **Buddha's *siṃhāsana* (c. -100 onward Indian art)** — Mathura, Gandhara, Amaravati schools; standard Buddha-throne iconography. (Snellgrove 1987.)
- **Christian Tetramorph (c. 4th c. CE)** — Santa Pudenziana mosaic (Rome, c. 410 CE); subsequent universal Christian iconographic convention. Mark = lion.
- **Rastafarian (1930 onward)** — Haile Selassie I crowned 1930 takes title "Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah, King of Kings of Ethiopia." The Lion of Judah emblem is central to Rastafarian iconography.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Egyptian Sekhmet / Mehit** — destructive feminine solar power; Sekhmet as the wrathful aspect of Hathor / Ra-as-Eye-of-Ra. (Pinch 2002.)
- **Mesopotamian** — royal-power emblem; the king's lion-hunt as imperial ideology; lamassu as gate-guardian. (Bleibtreu 1991.)
- **Hebrew Lion of Judah** — Judah-tribe messianic emblem; cosmic-royal authority. (Neusner 2013.)
- **Anatolian-Greek-Roman Cybele** — the Great Mother enthroned among lions; the lion as fierce-feminine power.
- **Buddhist *siṃhāsana*** — the Buddha's seat of authority; Buddha's voice as *siṃhanāda* ("lion's roar") proclaiming the dharma. (Snellgrove 1987.)
- **Christian Mark / Tetramorph** — the lion as evangelist Mark's symbol, associated with the kingly aspect of Christ.
- **Rastafarian Lion of Judah** — Haile Selassie as messianic king; African royal-spiritual lineage.

## Variants

The seven attested variants (Throne of Solomon / Lion of Judah, Egyptian sphinx, Cybele lion-throne, Buddha *siṃhāsana*, evangelist Mark Tetramorph, lamassu winged-lion, St Jerome's companion lion) are documented in YAML.

## Cross-family appearance

- **Egyptian sphinx → Greek sphinx** — `ancestor-of`. The Egyptian form is the older; the Greek Theban sphinx is the derived (winged, female-headed) form.
- **Mesopotamian / Egyptian → Hebrew Lion of Judah** — `parallel-form`. The Hebrew lion-iconography is one element in the broader ancient-Near-Eastern lion-as-royal-power vocabulary; the Lion of Judah specifically is an internal Hebrew development on Genesis 49.
- **Hebrew Lion of Judah → Christian Lion of Judah (Christ)** → Rastafarian Lion of Judah (Haile Selassie) — `ancestor-of` chain. Continuous textual inheritance.
- **Indic lion → Buddha's *siṃhāsana*** — `ancestor-of`. Documented inheritance within Indic art.
- **Hellenistic-Roman Cybele → Christian iconography** — `parallel-form`. The Cybele-with-lions iconography is older but no documented borrowing into Christian Mary-iconography occurred; the two developed in parallel mystery-cult and Christian frames.

## Contested interpretations

- The relationship between the Egyptian and Greek sphinx-iconographies. Standard scholarship (Walker & Higgs 2001) reads the Greek as derived from the Egyptian via Hellenistic-period contacts.
- Whether the Christian Mark-as-lion convention is an early Christian or medieval crystallization. Standard Christian iconography scholarship dates the firm Mark-lion association to c. 4th c. CE.
- Whether the Rastafarian Haile Selassie = Conquering Lion identification is theological-original or politically-recovered. Rastafari sources read it as continuous from Solomon-Sheba lineage claims.
