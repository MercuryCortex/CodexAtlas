# Nāga (Multi-headed Sacred Serpent)

## Identity

The **nāga** (Sanskrit नाग, "serpent"; feminine *nāginī*) is the sacred multi-headed cobra of Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain iconography — typically depicted with three, five, seven, or thousand heads radiating from a single body, often rearing in a *hood-canopy* over a deity or sage. Distinct from the generic Indic word for snake (*sarpa*), the nāga is **specifically a sacred-divine class of being**: water-guarding, treasure-keeping, dharma-protecting, knowledge-bearing. Nāgas inhabit *Patala* (the seven-tiered underworld of waters), guard the rivers and lakes of the world, attend deities, and serve as cosmic-architectural elements (Shesha as the bed of Vishnu; Vasuki as the churning-rope of the milk-ocean; Mucalinda as the shelter of the Buddha). The form is one of the most-attested cross-tradition iconographic units in South and Southeast Asia, with continuous use from Indus Valley seals (c. -2000) through present-day worship.

## Earliest attestation

- **Indus Valley Civilization (c. -2600 to -1900)** — multi-headed cobra seals; the continuity from IVC nāga to historical Hindu nāga is contested but the iconographic substrate is suggestive.
- **Rig Veda (c. -1500 to -1200)** — Vritra is the serpent-foe of Indra in RV 1.32; the *nāga-as-sacred-positive* register develops more fully in the post-Vedic period.
- **Mahābhārata (c. -400 to +400)** — Adi Parva contains the full *samudra manthana* episode (Vasuki as churning-rope, ch. 18) and the snake-sacrifice of Janamejaya.
- **Buddhist canonical (c. -300 onward)** — Mucalinda episode in Vinaya I.3 and Nidanakatha (the Buddha's biography); nāgas as protective beings in Mahayana sutras.
- **Iconographic earliest** — Bharhut and Sanchi stupas (c. -100 to +25): nāga-king worship scenes, Mucalinda-Buddha, multi-headed cobras on railing-medallions. **Bharhut Stupa railing-pillars are the earliest dated nāga iconography in the historical-Indian art-record.**
- **Southeast Asian transmission** — Angkor Wat (Cambodia, 12th c.) is the densest single-monument nāga iconographic corpus globally — the entire causeway-balustrade system is nāga-bodied. Phaya Naga cult in Thailand and Laos continues actively into the 21st c.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Sacred water-guardian / treasure-keeper** — nāgas inhabit rivers, lakes, springs; protect the wealth-of-the-earth.
- **Dharma-protector** — Mucalinda's sheltering of the Buddha during the storm establishes the nāga as devotionally-loyal-to-the-dharma; Mahayana sutras (especially Saddharma-Pundarika / Lotus Sutra ch. 11) explicitly include nāgas in the buddha-assembly.
- **Cosmic-architectural** — Shesha as Vishnu's bed; Vasuki as the cosmic churning mechanism; Ananta as the world-supporting serpent.
- **Royal-lineage emblem** — Cambodia's Khmer royal dynasty traced descent from Soma the moon-god + a nāginī princess.
- **Esoteric / Tantric** — the *kuṇḍalinī* serpent at the base of the spine in tantric subtle-anatomy is iconographically and theologically continuous with the nāga register.
- **Folk apotropaic** — *Nag Panchami* festival (5th day of bright fortnight of Shravana, July-August) — the principal pan-Hindu nāga-worship day.

## Variants

The Shesha-Ananta variant (Vaishnava cosmic-bed), the Mucalinda variant (Buddhist storm-shelter), Vasuki (churning-rope), Kaliya (Krishna-subdued), Manasā (folk-goddess), Parshvanatha's hood (Jain), and Southeast Asian nāga-causeway iconography are the principal sub-tradition specializations.

## Cross-family appearance

- **Pre-Vedic Indus Valley substrate (contested)** — IVC seal-iconography includes multi-headed cobra worship scenes.
- **Buddhist transmission to East and Southeast Asia** — Mucalinda iconography spreads with Buddhism into Sri Lanka, Burma, Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, Indonesia, China, Korea, Japan.
- **Jain Parshvanatha hood** — independent from Buddhist Mucalinda but iconographically near-identical.
- **Chinese-Buddhist *lóng* (dragon)** — Buddhist texts entering China translate Sanskrit *nāga* as *lóng* (dragon), fusing two distinct iconographic traditions. The Chinese long has a different physical form but shares water-guarding, weather-controlling, dharma-protecting functions.

## Contested interpretations

- **IVC continuity** — whether the Mohenjo-daro multi-headed cobra seals are the iconographic ancestor of the historical Hindu nāga is debated.
- **Caduceus relationship** — the visual cognacy between Greek caduceus and Indic kuṇḍalinī iconography is striking but no documented historical transmission.
