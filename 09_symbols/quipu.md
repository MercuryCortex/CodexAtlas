# Quipu (Khipu)

## Overview

The quipu (also written *khipu*; Quechua: *khipu* = "knot") is the Andean knotted-cord recording system used by the Inca Empire (*Tawantinsuyu*) and its predecessors for administrative, narrative, calendrical, and ritual purposes. Quipus are among the most distinctive recording technologies in world history: instead of marks inscribed on a flat surface, they consist of a main cord from which pendant cords hang at intervals, each bearing specific knots in specific positions and colors that encode information.

The quipu presents a unique situation in the archaeology of recording systems: its **numerical function is well-understood** and thoroughly documented (Tier-1 consensus since Leland Locke's decipherment in 1923), while its **possible narrative or textual function remains actively debated** (Gary Urton's binary-encoding hypothesis, 2003, is a credible Tier-1 scholarly proposal but not yet consensus). The quipu is simultaneously a solved puzzle and an unsolved one.

As of the most recent archaeological inventories, approximately 900 quipus survive in museum and private collections worldwide — a tiny fraction of what once existed, given that the Spanish colonial administration systematically destroyed quipus it deemed idolatrous or incomprehensible (or both).

## Physical Description

### Structure
A typical quipu consists of:

- **Cuerda madre** (main cord): a thick horizontal cord, often 30–60 cm long, to which everything else attaches. This is the spine of the record.
- **Pendant cords** (*cuerdas colgantes*): strings that hang from the main cord at regular intervals. A quipu may have anywhere from 3 to several hundred pendant cords.
- **Subsidiary cords** (*cuerdas subsidiarias*): additional cords attached to the pendant cords, extending the hierarchical structure. These may themselves have subsidiaries, creating a tree-like branching structure that mirrors the hierarchical organization of Inca administration.
- **Top cords** (*cuerdas principales*): some quipus have cords attached above the main cord rather than below; these often serve as summary or totaling cords.

### Knot Types and Numerical Encoding

The Inca numerical system was decimal (base-10). Knot position on a cord encodes decimal place; knot type encodes the specific digit:

| Digit Value | Knot Type | Position |
|-------------|-----------|----------|
| 0 | No knot | (empty position) |
| 1–9 (units position) | Single long-slip knot (figure-eight) | Lowest position on cord |
| 1–9 (tens) | Single overhand knot per unit | Middle positions |
| 1–9 (hundreds) | Single overhand knot per unit | Higher positions |
| 1 (units) | Figure-eight knot (Flemish knot) | Units position |

Reading proceeds from top to bottom: the highest knot cluster on a cord represents the highest decimal place; the lowest cluster represents units. A cord with knot clusters at the hundreds, tens, and units positions is read as a three-digit number. Zero is represented by the absence of a knot at that position.

### Color Encoding

Cord color encodes categorical information — the type of item being counted or recorded (people, llamas, maize, labor-days, etc.). Color codes were not universal; they were conventionally agreed upon within each administrative context and presumably known to the *quipucamayoc* (knot-keeper/record-keeper) who maintained the quipu. Without contextual knowledge of a specific quipu's color-coding system, the category represented by a colored cord cannot be recovered.

Colors were produced by dyeing the cotton or wool cords with plant and mineral pigments. Both the natural color of the fiber and intentional dyeing contributed to the palette.

## Documented Function: Imperial Administration

The Inca used quipus extensively across the administrative apparatus of *Tawantinsuyu* (the Inca empire, "The Four Regions Together"):

- **Census records**: population counts by sex, age, occupation, and tributary status for every community in the empire
- **Tribute accounting**: quantities of textiles, food, precious metals, and labor owed and received by the state
- **Military logistics**: troop numbers, supply inventories, garrison records
- **Astronomical and calendrical records**: the Inca calendar system (solar and lunar cycles) may have been encoded in quipus
- **Historical records**: Inca oral tradition includes accounts of events that were "held in" quipus and recited by trained quipucamayoc specialists

Leland Locke's 1923 decipherment of the numerical system was confirmed by subsequent analysis of hundreds of quipus and is now established fact at the Tier-1 archaeological level. The quipu as a decimal numerical record is not in dispute.

### The Ceque System and the Quipu Cosmos

The Inca imperial capital Cusco was the organizational center of the empire in both political and sacred terms. From the Coricancha (Temple of the Sun), 41 *ceque* lines (imaginary lines like spokes of a wheel) radiated outward to sacred sites (*huacas*) across the surrounding landscape. The entire ceque system — 41 lines, 328 huacas — was encoded in a quipu maintained at Cusco. This quipu was the empire's sacred-geographic database: the knotted cord and the radiating lines together constituted the Inca information-cosmos, mapping both the physical world and the ritual calendar onto the same cord-and-knot medium. The connection between quipu and ceque demonstrates that the technology was not merely practical but was the medium through which the empire understood and organized its sacred geography.

## Contested Function: Narrative Encoding

### Urton's Binary Hypothesis

Gary Urton (2003) proposed that quipus encode a binary information system beyond numerics. His analysis suggests that each pendant cord on a quipu carries a 7-element binary string determined by:

1. Construction direction of the main cord (S or Z twist)
2. Attachment of the pendant cord (recto or verso)
3. Ply direction of the pendant cord (S or Z)
4. Cord color (two-color distinction)
5. Number of plies
6. Knot direction (S or Z)
7. Number of knots in a cluster (odd or even)

7 binary choices yield 2^7 = 128 possible "words." Urton proposes this constitutes a syllabic or logographic encoding system capable of carrying narrative text — not just numbers. If correct, this would make the quipu one of the largest undeciphered writing systems in the world.

This hypothesis is scientifically serious and published in a peer-reviewed academic monograph (University of Texas Press). It is not yet consensus and has been debated by other specialists who argue the binary patterning may be epiphenomenal to the cord-construction process rather than intentional encoding. The question remains open.

### The Tupicocha Living Tradition

Frank Salomon's landmark ethnography *The Cord Keepers* (2004) documents the community of San Andrés de Tupicocha in the highlands of Peru, which maintains a living quipu tradition to the present day. The Tupicocha community uses quipus — which they call *pachaka* — to encode community governance records: the structure of community labor obligations (*faenas*), the obligations of community authorities, and the records of communal decision-making. These are not numerical accounts in the Inca administrative sense; they are governance documents. The Tupicocha quipus demonstrate that the technology was adaptable to multiple recording purposes and that at least one form of non-numerical quipu encoding remained intelligible to its community of practice into the 21st century.

## Cross-Tradition Cord Wisdom — The Universal Knot

The quipu belongs to a broader cross-tradition pattern: across human cultures, **the knotted or beaded cord functions as a technology for encoding order** — whether administrative, sacred, or mnemonic. This convergence occurs independently in multiple world regions:

### Wampum (Haudenosaunee/Algonquian)
Wampum belts — cylindrical shell beads woven into patterns on leather cords — encode treaty terms, historical events, and diplomatic agreements among the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy) and neighboring Algonquian peoples. The Great Law of Peace (*Gayanashagowa*), the constitution of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy, exists in wampum form alongside oral tradition. Wampum is not merely mnemonic decoration — it is the material form of legal and political record, read by trained interpreters. See **[[tradition-haudenosaunee]]** and **[[event-great-law-of-peace-haudenosaunee-c1450]]**.

### Buddhist Mala (Prayer Beads)
The Buddhist *mala* (Sanskrit: "garland") is a string of 108 beads used to count mantra repetitions during meditation practice. 108 is a sacred number in Hindu and Buddhist cosmology. The beads encode sacred numeracy in cord form: each bead = one mantra, and the cord tracks the practitioner's progress through the counting. The Tibetan *mala* often incorporates counters on subsidiary cords — a structural parallel to the quipu's pendant-cord hierarchy. See **[[sacred-number-108]]**.

### Islamic Tasbih (Prayer Beads)
The *tasbih* (or *misbaha*) is a string of 99 or 33 beads used by Muslims to count the 99 names of Allah (*Asma ul-Husna*) during *dhikr* (remembrance of God). The cord is the medium through which divine names are enumerated. As with the mala, sacred enumeration is encoded in the bead-and-string format.

### The Convergence Pattern

These technologies — quipu, wampum, mala, tasbih — represent independent convergent solutions to the same cognitive problem: **how to encode sequence, quantity, or category in a portable, durable, tactile medium**. String and cord are among the oldest human technologies (twisted fiber precedes pottery and agriculture in the archaeological record). The knotting, beading, and braiding of cord may be cognitively linked to the neural substrates of pattern recognition and memory — the cord as an externalized memory device exploiting the hand's evolved capacity for fine tactile discrimination.

The quipu takes this to its most elaborate administrative expression: a hierarchical, multi-variable, color-coded, positional encoding system capable of representing the entire economic life of a 10-million-person empire.

## Mystery Status: Partially Deciphered

- **DOCUMENTED**: Numerical encoding system (decimal positional notation via knot type and position). Confirmed Tier-1 consensus since 1923.
- **CONTESTED**: Whether quipus encode narrative, syllabic, or logographic information beyond numerics. Urton's binary hypothesis is the leading scholarly proposal; not yet confirmed.
- **LOST**: The color-coding conventions of most surviving quipus are irrecoverable without contextual records. The quipucamayoc specialist class was effectively eliminated by Spanish colonial destruction of indigenous administrative infrastructure.
