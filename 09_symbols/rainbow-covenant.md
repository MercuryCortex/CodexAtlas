# Rainbow (Covenant-sign)

## Identity
The **Rainbow** as a religious-iconographic symbol is most consequentially the **post-Flood divine covenant-sign** of Genesis 9:13–17: after the flood subsides and Noah leaves the Ark, God places his "bow in the cloud" (Hebrew *qashti b'anan*) as the *sign of the covenant* that humanity will never again be destroyed by flood. The Hebrew *qeshet* is the same word as the warrior's bow — Yahweh's hung-up-in-the-sky bow signifies the *cessation* of his flood-judgment-warfare (Turner 1993 is the standard study). The rainbow is thus iconographically integrated with [[noahs-ark]] and [[mount-ararat]] as the three constitutive post-Flood narrative-symbols.

The rainbow appears in distinctive religious-iconographic functions across multiple traditions, not always as covenant-sign:

- **Hebrew:** post-Flood covenant-sign (Genesis 9:13–17); divine-warrior's hung-up bow.
- **Christian (patristic):** typological reading — the rainbow signifies God's *mercy* / *peace* / *reconciliation* with humanity; integrated into apocalyptic iconography (Revelation 4:3 — the rainbow around God's throne; Revelation 10:1 — the angel "with a rainbow over his head").
- **Islamic (Quran 11:36–48):** no direct rainbow-covenant material; the post-Flood divine assurance is in different terms.
- **Norse:** *Bifröst* — the rainbow-bridge connecting humanity's realm (Midgard) and the gods' realm (Asgard); a *cosmic-bridge* function rather than covenant-sign. The bridge is destroyed at *Ragnarök* — eschatological inverse.
- **Greek:** *Iris* — the personified rainbow-messenger goddess; her function is *divine-communication* (carrying messages between gods and humans) rather than covenant-sign.

## Earliest attestation
- **Hebrew Bible Genesis 9:13** (P-source, ~-550 BCE) — the covenant-rainbow attestation.
- **Greek Homer Iliad 11.27** — the rainbow as the bow of Zeus's messenger Iris.
- **Norse Snorri Gylfaginning 13–14** (~1220 CE redaction of older oral tradition) — Bifröst the rainbow-bridge.

The Hebrew covenant-rainbow is the most theologically-elaborated of the three traditions; the Greek Iris is the most personified; the Norse Bifröst is the most cosmologically-architectural.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Hebrew (Genesis):** divine covenant-sign; Yahweh's hung-up warrior-bow signifying the end of flood-judgment.
- **Christian (Revelation 4:3; 10:1):** mercy / peace / divine-presence; integrated into apocalyptic-throne iconography.
- **Norse:** cosmic-bridge connecting humanity and the gods; destroyed at Ragnarök.
- **Greek:** Iris's personification — divine-communication, messages between Olympus and earth.

## Cross-family appearance

The rainbow's appearance as a religious symbol across Hebrew + Christian + Norse + Greek traditions is *parallel-form* rather than *transmissive*: each tradition independently develops a religious-iconographic engagement with the natural phenomenon of the rainbow, with distinctive theological functions across the four. The Hebrew covenant-rainbow's specific function (post-Flood divine-warrior's hung-up bow) is not transmitted to the other three; it is a Hebrew-Israelite theological development.

## Contested interpretations

- **Genesis 9:13 — bow-of-the-warrior or just rainbow?** Turner 1993 demonstrates the warrior-bow reading is original; some older Christian-traditional readings flatten the iconography to just "rainbow as divine sign of mercy."
- **Was the rainbow believed to exist *before* the Flood?** Genesis 9:13 is ambiguous between (a) the rainbow now appears for the first time, marking the post-Flood covenant, and (b) the rainbow has always existed but now acquires *new meaning* as covenant-sign. Both readings are present in patristic and rabbinic reception.
