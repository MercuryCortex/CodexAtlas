# Black Sun

> **Methodology discipline note (per `00_meta/methodology.md` "Symbol-research discipline"):** This entry MUST surface BOTH the pre-Nazi solar-wheel tradition AND the post-1945 neo-Nazi 'Black Sun' appropriation without conflating them. The pre-Nazi tradition (Bronze Age solar wheels) is legitimate archaeological material; the specific 'Black Sun' as a named emblem is a post-WWII neo-Nazi construction. The Wewelsburg mosaic itself predates the name 'Schwarze Sonne' by approximately three decades. **The name 'Black Sun' / 'Schwarze Sonne' is NOT documented in any WWII-era SS primary source.** This distinction is critical for methodological integrity.

## Identity

The "Black Sun" as a named symbol designates a specific 12-spoke sun wheel in a specific configuration, primarily known from the green-gold marble floor mosaic of the "Obergruppenführer Hall" (Hall of SS Group Leaders, North Tower) of Wewelsburg Castle in Büren, Westphalia, Germany — installed 1933–1943 under Heinrich Himmler's direction as the ceremonial headquarters of the SS.

**Critical terminological fact**: The name *Schwarze Sonne* ("Black Sun") is NOT documented in any SS primary source or WWII-era document. The identification of the Wewelsburg mosaic as "the Black Sun" originates in post-war neo-Nazi esotericist literature, primarily Wilhelm Landig's novel *Götzen gegen Thule* (1971). The mosaic itself had no documented name during the SS period; the mosaic design is described in SS records only as an ornamental floor inlay. The "Black Sun" identity was retroactively constructed by post-war occult Nazism.

The symbol exists in two entirely different contexts that must not be conflated:
1. **Bronze Age solar wheels**: Spoked circular designs representing the sun's motion across genuine archaeological contexts in Northern European Bronze Age material culture (petroglyphs, votive objects) — legitimate pre-religious heritage predating Nazi Germany by 2,000+ years.
2. **The post-1945 'Black Sun'**: A named neo-Nazi emblem constructed retroactively from the Wewelsburg mosaic design, used by post-war Nazi occultists and now by contemporary neo-Nazi and accelerationist movements worldwide.

## Earliest attestation

- **Bronze Age solar wheels** (generic): Spoked circular solar designs appear in Scandinavian Bronze Age petroglyphs (Bohuslän, Sweden; Tanumshede; Østfold, Norway) from approximately -1500 to -500 BCE. The Trundholm sun-chariot (Denmark, c. -1400 BCE) is the most famous Bronze Age solar object in Northern Europe. These are GENERIC solar wheels — not "Black Suns."
- **The Wewelsburg mosaic**: Installed between 1933 and 1943 under Himmler's direction; the castle was purchased by the SS in 1934 and redesigned as a ceremonial center. Karl Maria Wiligut ("Weisthor"), Himmler's personal occultist, advised on the castle's ceremonial symbolism.
- **The name "Schwarze Sonne"**: First documented in Wilhelm Landig, *Götzen gegen Thule: Ein Roman voller Wirklichkeiten* (Grabert-Verlag, 1971). Landig was a former SS officer and post-war neo-Nazi ideologue; the novel elaborated an esoteric-Nazi mythology in which the "Black Sun" was a hidden energy source and symbol of Aryan occult power.

## Meaning(s) attested

**In Bronze Age context (solar wheel / Sonnenrad)**:
- The rotating-sun or solar-wheel motif in Bronze Age Northern European iconography encodes the sun's cyclical passage — the spokes represent either the cardinal/solstice directions (4-spoke = cross-quarters), the months of the year (12-spoke = possible but not universally attested), or simply rotational motion.
- In Germanic neo-paganism (Ásatrú, heathenry) the solar wheel is used as a symbol of the solar year and agricultural cycle without Nazi association, provided the specific Wewelsburg 12-spoke form is not used.

**In SS/post-war neo-Nazi context**:
- In Landig's mythology and subsequent neo-Nazi esotericist literature (Miguel Serrano, *Adolf Hitler: The Last Avatar*, 1984; Russell McCloud), the "Black Sun" represents: an invisible or occult sun behind the visible sun; a source of Aryan spiritual power; the "inner sun" of the "Aryan race's" esoteric tradition.
- In contemporary neo-Nazi usage (accelerationist movements, Nordic-themed white nationalists, some "esoteric Hitlerism" practitioners) the specific Wewelsburg 12-spoke form is used as an identifier — chosen partly because it is not as immediately recognizable as the swastika but is understood within neo-Nazi networks.

## Appropriation and Political Weaponization

> **MANDATORY SECTION** — required methodology discipline equivalent to the swastika node.

### The Wewelsburg SS Program

Wewelsburg Castle was purchased by the SS in 1934 and redesigned by Himmler as the ceremonial and ideological center of the SS order — modeled on a medieval warrior-brotherhood (the Round Table myth was deliberately invoked: the North Tower's round room was named the "Obergruppenführer Hall" with places for twelve SS group leaders around a circular table, like Arthurian knights). The floor mosaic was installed in the castle's ground floor as part of this ceremonial redesign.

The SS Ahnenerbe (Ancestral Heritage Bureau), established 1935, systematically appropriated pre-historic and medieval European symbols to construct a false "Aryan" cultural heritage for Nazi ideology (Kater 1974). The Wewelsburg mosaic represents this program: a composite solar-wheel emblem designed to evoke (without directly citing) Bronze Age Northern European solar iconography, reframed as a specifically SS/Aryan sacred symbol.

### Post-1945 Construction of the "Black Sun" Identity

The SPECIFIC identification of the Wewelsburg mosaic as "the Black Sun" (*Schwarze Sonne*) does not appear in any SS primary source. The name was constructed retroactively in post-war neo-Nazi fiction and esotericist literature:

- **Wilhelm Landig**, *Götzen gegen Thule* (1971): First documented use of "Schwarze Sonne" as an esoteric emblem in a neo-Nazi fictional context. Landig was a former SS member; the novel constructs a post-war occult-Nazi mythology.
- **Savitri Devi** (*The Lightning and the Sun*, 1958) and **Miguel Serrano** (*Adolf Hitler: The Last Avatar*, 1984) developed "esoteric Hitlerism" — an occult-Nazi worldview in which Hitler was a divine avatar. The "Black Sun" entered this tradition as a cosmological symbol.
- **Goodrick-Clarke** (*Black Sun*, 2002, Tier-1) is the definitive academic account of this post-war development.

### Current Legal Status

The specific Wewelsburg 12-spoke form is **banned in Germany** under §86a StGB (Strafgesetzbuch — German Criminal Code), which prohibits symbols of unconstitutional organizations (specifically the Nazi state and its subordinate organizations including the SS). Displaying this specific form in public in Germany is a criminal offense.

In other jurisdictions (USA, UK, most of Europe) the symbol is not legally banned but is classified as a hate symbol by organizations including:
- The Southern Poverty Law Center (SPLC)
- The Anti-Defamation League (ADL)
- German Verfassungsschutz (domestic intelligence)

### Important Nuance: Generic Sun Wheels vs. the Wewelsburg Form

**Not all sun wheels are "Black Suns."** Generic spoked circular sun-wheel designs have a continuous legitimate use in:
- Archaeological and heritage contexts (Bronze Age studies, museum presentations)
- Germanic neo-paganism (Ásatrú, heathenry) practitioners who use solar-wheel forms without Nazi association

The **specific** Wewelsburg 12-spoke form — identifiable by its proportions, the specific arrangement of its spokes, and its historical provenance — is the problematic form. Generic 4-spoke, 6-spoke, or 8-spoke sun wheels used in non-Nazi contexts are not automatically Nazi symbols. Scholars and educators must distinguish between generic Bronze Age solar iconography and the specific post-war neo-Nazi "Black Sun" emblem.
