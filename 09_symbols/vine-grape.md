# Vine / Grape

## Identity

The vine and grape constitute the iconographic-ritual vocabulary of Greco-Roman Dionysiac / Bacchic religion and (in derivative reception) Christian eucharistic theology. The Dionysian vine-iconography is attested from at least the 6th c. BCE in Greek pottery and votive reliefs and persists through the Roman Imperial period as the central iconographic vocabulary of one of the major mystery-cult complexes. Christian eucharistic theology (1st c. CE onward) appropriates the wine-as-blood iconography in continuity with Jewish Passover *kiddush* practice but also in iconographic dialogue with the surrounding Greco-Roman Dionysiac context. The Vedic Soma is `parallel-form` at the ritual-structural level (sacrificial drink poured out / consumed for divine communion) but `visual-cognate` only at the substance-level — Soma is not a vine or grape but a different (debated) plant.

## Earliest attestation

- **Greek (c. -1500 onward iconographic; -700 onward textual)** — Bronze Age Mediterranean wine-cultivation attestation. Homer *Iliad* and *Odyssey* (c. -700) reference wine extensively. ([[phase-2-008-homeric-epics]])
- **Dionysiac cult (c. -600 onward iconographic)** — Greek archaic and classical pottery uses Dionysian iconography (vine, thyrsus, kantharos, satyrs, maenads). Dionysian mystery cult attested from 6th c. BCE.
- **Vedic Soma (Rig Veda book 9, c. -1500 textual)** — entire 9th book of the Rig Veda is the Soma-mandala. ([[phase-2-001-rig-veda-family-books]])
- **Christian eucharist (1st c. CE onward)** — Pauline *Epistles* (1 Corinthians 11:23-26, c. 53-57 CE); Synoptic Gospels (Mark, Matthew, Luke, c. 65-90 CE); Gospel of John 6:53-56, 15:1-8 (c. 90-110 CE). McGowan 2014 documents the 1st-c.-onward continuous theology.
- **Roman Imperial Bacchic spread (1st c. BCE onward)** — Roman *Senatus Consultum de Bacchanalibus* 186 BCE attests both the spread and the political controversy. Bacchic mystery cult continues across Imperial period.
- **Norse Vinland (c. 1000 CE)** — *Vinland Sagas* record Leif Eriksson's voyage and the grapes / vines found in North America.

## Meaning(s) attested

- **Greek Dionysian** — ecstatic possession; theater; the *bios* released from cultivated form; the dismembered-and-restored god (Zagreus dimension). (Burkert 1985.)
- **Roman Bacchic** — Hellenistic-mystery initiatory; controversial in 2nd c. BCE Rome, normalized later.
- **Christian eucharistic** — wine = blood of Christ; vine = Christ-as-true-vine (John 15:1-8); the wine-of-the-new-covenant theology. (McGowan 2014.)
- **Vedic Soma** — sacrificial drink poured out for the gods (Indra particularly); psycho-active / ecstatic-ritual context; *amrta* (immortal-drink) parallels.

## Variants

The five attested variants (Dionysus-Bacchus, Christian eucharist, Vedic Soma parallel-form, Norse Vinland, Hellenistic-mystery iconographic vocabulary) are documented in YAML.

## Cross-family appearance

- **Greek Dionysian → Roman Bacchic** — `ancestor-of`. Direct continuity.
- **Greco-Roman Dionysian iconography → Christian eucharistic iconography** — `parallel-form` with some `appropriated-by` features at the *iconographic vocabulary* level (the grape-cluster on early Christian sarcophagi is iconographically ambiguous Dionysiac-Christian). The Christian theological content is from Jewish-Passover origins (Matthew 26 / Mark 14 / Luke 22), but the iconographic-Mediterranean vocabulary is partly inherited from the Greco-Roman environment. (McGowan 2014.)
- **Vedic Soma ↔ Greek wine** — `parallel-form` at ritual-structural level; `visual-cognate` only at iconographic-substance level (since Soma is not a grape). Indo-Iranian Soma / Iranian Haoma are the closer parallel-pair.

## Contested interpretations

- The Dionysian-to-Christian-eucharist transmission question. The mainstream scholarly view (McGowan 2014, Burkert 1985) is that the *theological content* of the Eucharist is Jewish-Passover (Last Supper as Passover-seder), not Dionysian, but the *iconographic vocabulary* of the surrounding Greco-Roman environment did color early Christian visual culture. Older claims of straightforward Dionysian-origin of the Eucharist (Reitzenstein, Cumont) are now widely rejected.
- The identity of Soma. Genuinely contested: Ephedra (Wasson 1968 challenged), Cannabis, Psilocybe / Amanita muscaria (Wasson 1968), and various other identifications. The matter is open.
- Whether the Bacchic mystery cult's spread in 2nd c. BCE Italy was as politically threatening as the *Senatus Consultum* presents or whether the Roman authorities' alarm was disproportionate. Modern scholarship is divided.

## Mystery / unknown variants

The botanical identity of Soma is genuinely contested. *Iconographically* the Soma references in the Rig Veda are not grape / vine, but a plant whose identity remains open.
