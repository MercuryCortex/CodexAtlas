# Tetragrammaton (YHWH / יהוה)

## Identity
The **Tetragrammaton** (Greek *tetragrammaton* = "four-letter word") is the four-Hebrew-consonant Divine Name **יהוה** — *yod-he-waw-he* — read in Hebrew right-to-left as **YHWH** (English transliteration). It is the personal name of the God of Israel as attested in the Hebrew Bible, where it appears approximately 6,800 times. **The pronunciation was lost** or deliberately suppressed in the Second Temple / post-Second-Temple Jewish tradition; subsequent liturgical practice reads ***Adonai*** (Lord) or ***ha-Shem*** (the Name) in place of the consonants. The form *Jehovah* is a medieval Christian misreading combining the consonants of YHWH with the Masoretic vowel-pointing of *Adonai*. The form *Yahweh* is the most-defensible modern scholarly vocalization-reconstruction.

The Tetragrammaton is **the single most cross-tradition Divine-Name symbol in Western religion** — operative in (1) ancient and modern Judaism, (2) Christian biblical translation and theology, (3) Renaissance and modern Christian Kabbalah, (4) Hermetic-magical literature from Agrippa through Lévi to the Golden Dawn, and (5) modern academic Hebrew-Bible scholarship.

## Earliest attestation
- **c. -850 BCE** — the **Mesha Stele** (Moabite, line 18, the earliest extra-biblical attestation of YHWH). The Hebrew biblical attestations (J source, E source) are roughly contemporary but only securely datable as redacted in the post-exilic period.
- **c. -600 BCE** — the **Ketef Hinnom silver amulets** (excavated outside Jerusalem 1979); contain the Priestly Blessing including the Tetragrammaton. **The oldest surviving Hebrew biblical text by ~400 years.**
- **c. -1000 onward** — ostraca and inscriptions from Israelite-and-Judahite contexts: Kuntillet 'Ajrud (c. -800; the famous *"YHWH of Samaria and his Asherah"* graffiti, attesting popular-religious YHWH-and-consort iconography pre-monotheist), Lachish letters (c. -590), Arad ostraca.
- **Dead Sea Scrolls (~ -200 to +70)** — frequent paleo-Hebrew rendering of the Tetragrammaton within otherwise-Aramaic-square-script texts; the typographic distinction is itself a theological-cultural statement.
- **Greek transliterations** — early Christian Greek Bible manuscripts variously render the Tetragrammaton as ΚΥΡΙΟΣ (*Kyrios*, "Lord"), paleo-Hebrew יהוה, or transliterated ΙΑΩ.
- **Renaissance Christian Kabbalah (1480s onward)** — [[giovanni-pico-della-mirandola|Pico]], [[johannes-reuchlin|Reuchlin]], [[cornelius-agrippa|Agrippa]] develop the Tetragrammaton as a Christian-magical Name; the **pentagrammaton** YHSVH (Yeshua / Jesus as the *pronounceable* Tetragrammaton).
- **Modern occult tradition (19th c. onward)** — Eliphas Lévi, the Golden Dawn, Crowley — the Tetragrammaton as elemental-magical Name.

## Meaning(s) attested
- **Biblical Hebrew** — the personal name of the God of Israel; the *Shem ha-Meforash* ("the Name Explicit"). The Exodus 3:14 derivation links the Name to the verb *hayah* (to be) — "I am that I am" / "I will be what I will be." Modern scholarship debates whether this is genuine etymology or a folk-etymological theological gloss.
- **Pronunciation prohibition (Second Temple period onward)** — Mishnah *Yoma* 3.8, *Sotah* 7.6: the Name is pronounced only by the High Priest on Yom Kippur in the Holy of Holies. After the Temple's destruction (70 CE), the pronunciation is functionally lost.
- **Christian theological reception** — early Christian theology (Justin Martyr, Tertullian, Origen) variously identifies the Tetragrammaton with Christ (the *pre-incarnate Logos* who appears as YHWH in Old Testament theophanies) or with the Father (YHWH as the First Person of the Trinity). The harmonization-problem of Tetragrammaton-Christology is one of the foundational early-Christian theological debates.
- **Renaissance Christian Kabbalah** — Reuchlin and Pico read the Tetragrammaton as a *magical* Name whose insertion of *shin* (the letter of *ruach* / Spirit) into YHWH spells YHSVH = Yeshua / Jesus — the *pronounceable* Tetragrammaton.
- **Modern Hermetic-occult** — Lévi and the Golden Dawn read the four letters as elemental correspondences (yod=fire, first he=water, waw=air, second he=earth) and as ceremonial-magical Word-of-Power.

## Variants
- **Biblical Hebrew YHWH** — the consonantal-text form across the Hebrew Bible.
- **Paleo-Hebrew YHWH** — Qumran-period typographic distinction.
- **Greek transliterations** — ΚΥΡΙΟΣ, ΙΑΩ, paleo-Hebrew within Greek texts.
- **Pentagrammaton YHSVH** — Renaissance Christian Kabbalah.
- **Modern Hermetic-elemental YHWH** — Lévi 1854 onward.

## Cross-family appearance
The Tetragrammaton is **specifically Jewish in origin** but acquired substantial **Christian theological** and **Christian-Kabbalist + Hermetic-magical** receptions:
- **Christian biblical translation** — universal: every Christian-language Bible translation has to make a decision about how to render the Tetragrammaton.
- **Renaissance Christian Kabbalah** — Pico, Reuchlin, Agrippa.
- **Hermetic-magical literature** — Agrippa *De Occulta Philosophia* III (1533) treats the Tetragrammaton as central magical Name.
- **Modern occult tradition** — Lévi, Mathers, Crowley.
- **Modern Christian fundamentalist movements** — some (Jehovah's Witnesses, some Sacred-Name groups) restore the Tetragrammaton in liturgical practice.

## Contested interpretations
- **Original meaning and etymology of YHWH.** Smith 2001/2002 surveys the major hypotheses: causative form of *hayah* ("he causes to be"); Midianite-Kenite origin (the *Kenite-Midianite hypothesis* — YHWH as Midianite deity adopted by Moses); Egyptian-derived form (*Shasu-Yahu* place-name in Egyptian topographic lists, c. -1400). The Midianite-Kenite hypothesis (Yahweh's origin outside Israel proper) remains the leading scholarly position.
- **Pronunciation.** *Yahweh* is the leading reconstruction; *Yehowah / Jehovah* is medieval-Christian misreading. Both are scholarly-reconstructed; the actual ancient pronunciation is unrecoverable.
- **Pre-monotheist YHWH cult.** The *Kuntillet 'Ajrud "YHWH of Samaria and his Asherah"* inscriptions document a pre-exilic popular YHWH-with-consort religion. Smith 2002 reconstructs the gradual evolution from polytheistic Yahwism (YHWH within a Canaanite-El pantheon) toward exilic-era exclusive monotheism.
