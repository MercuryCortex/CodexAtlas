# Tauroctony (Mithras Killing the Bull)

## Identity
The **tauroctony** (Greek *ταυροκτονία*, "bull-killing") is the **cult-image of every Mithraeum in the Roman empire** — over **300 surviving instances** documented in Vermaseren's *CIMRM* catalog. The scene: the god [[mithras-roman]] in Phrygian cap astride a struggling bull, pulling the bull's head back with one hand and plunging a dagger into its shoulder with the other; the bull's tail tipped with wheat-ears (or transforming into wheat at the moment of sacrifice); a **dog** leaping up to drink the blood from the wound; a **serpent** drinking from the blood on the ground; a **scorpion** attacking the bull's testicles; **Cautes** (torch-up) and **Cautopates** (torch-down) flanking; [[helios]]/Sol in the upper-left, [[selene]]/Luna in the upper-right. **The tauroctony is the single most-attested religious iconographic-scene of the Roman imperial period**, and the **principal pagan-mystery-cult cult-image that competed institutionally with Christianity** for 300 years (mid-1st to late-4th c. CE). For the Codex Atlas mission it is doubly load-bearing: (1) it is the **cult-iconographic site** of the Mithraic-Christian competition repeatedly addressed in patristic polemic (Justin *1 Apol* 66; Tertullian *De Praescriptione* 40); (2) it is the **principal piece of pagan iconography** the Christian apologetic tradition had to engage as direct ritual-iconographic competitor.

## Earliest attestation
- **Earliest surviving tauroctony**: late 1st c. CE — the foundational deposit at the Mithraeum of Sidon (Beirut Museum); Sant'Angelo in Capua Mithraeum (Italy, ~+100); Caracalla-era Vatican Mithraeum.
- **Massive proliferation 2nd–3rd c. CE**: the cult spreads rapidly along Roman military and trade routes. Highest density: Italy (Rome, Ostia), Germany (Carnuntum, Saalburg, Trier), the Danubian provinces, Britain (London, Carrawburgh on Hadrian's Wall, Caernarvon).
- **Latest examples**: late 4th c. CE — destruction of Mithraea begins with Theodosian anti-pagan legislation (-391); the Aventine Mithraeum in Rome shows late-4th-c. Christian-iconoclast destruction.

## Meaning(s) attested

### What the iconographic scene shows
The scene canonical to all 300+ Mithraea is the **bull-sacrifice as cosmogonic act** — the killing of the cosmic bull whose blood (drunk by dog and serpent), seed (attacked by scorpion), and tail (transforming into wheat) generate the elements of the cosmos. Mithras is the agent of cosmogony; the bull is the sacrificed cosmic primal-victim.

### Multiple interpretive layers (Mithraic mysteries' content is partially-recoverable)
- **Iranian-Zoroastrian background.** The Mithras-name has a Persian-Indo-Iranian theonymic root ([[mitra-vedic]]; [[mithra-zoroastrian]]); the cosmogonic-bull-sacrifice motif has a Zoroastrian precedent (Gayōmard / *Geush Urvan*, the primal bull). Whether Roman Mithraism is genuinely descended from Iranian Mithraism or is a 1st-c. CE Roman construction *named* after the Iranian god is debated (Beck 2006 favors substantial Iranian content; Ulansey 1989 favors more-Roman astrological origin).
- **Astrological-cosmic reading (Ulansey 1989).** All scene-elements correspond to constellations: Taurus (bull), Canis Minor (dog), Hydra (serpent), Scorpio (scorpion), Corvus (raven, sometimes present), Crater (cup), Leo (lion, sometimes present). Ulansey argues the tauroctony depicts the **precession of the equinoxes** — the realization, discovered by Hipparchus c. -130, that the spring equinox had shifted from Taurus to Aries; Mithras is the cosmic figure performing this shift, "killing the Age of Taurus." This thesis is influential but contested.
- **Soteriological-initiatic reading.** Initiates progressed through **seven grades** (Corax/Raven, Nymphus/Bride, Miles/Soldier, Leo/Lion, Perses/Persian, Heliodromus/Sun-Runner, Pater/Father); the tauroctony was the central icon of the cult's salvation-promise — through the cosmic sacrifice, the initiate participates in cosmic renewal.

## Cross-family appearance

### MASSIVE-win edge: Mithraic tauroctony as Christianity's principal pagan-cult-iconographic competitor

The Mithraic tauroctony stood as the **principal pagan-mystery-cult cult-image competing with Christianity for the religious-experiential allegiance of the Roman empire** for nearly three centuries (mid-1st CE through late-4th CE). The competition was institutional, geographic, and theological:

1. **Geographic competition.** Mithraic and Christian house-temples (the *titular* churches) emerge in many of the same Roman cities at the same time. The Basilica of San Clemente in Rome is the iconic case: built directly over a Mithraeum, with the Mithraeum (still partially visitable) physically beneath the church.

2. **Theological-iconographic competition.** [[justin-martyr]] *1 Apology* 66 explicitly addresses the Mithraic-Christian iconographic parallel: "*the wicked devils have imitated [the Eucharist] in the mysteries of Mithras, commanding the same thing to be done. For, that bread and a cup of water are placed with certain incantations in the mystic rites of one who is being initiated, you either know or can learn.*" The polemical anxiety presupposes the priority of the pagan ritual. **Tertullian** *De Praescriptione Haereticorum* 40 extends the polemic.

3. **Ritual-meal parallels.** Mithraic banquet-scenes (reliefs depicting initiates dining in the Mithraeum, with Mithras and Sol enthroned) iconographically parallel Christian Eucharistic-banquet imagery. **Whether this is genuinely-parallel-mystery-cult-Eucharistic-meal practice or coincidental visual cognate is debated**; most current scholarship treats it as substantial structural parallel without direct genealogical influence.

4. **Sol-Invictus iconographic overlap.** Mithras = Sol Invictus from the late-3rd c. onward (the cult-title "Deus Sol Invictus Mithras" is widely attested epigraphically); when Constantine institutionalizes Sol-Invictus solar imperial cult (early 4th c.) and then transitions to Christianity, **Mithraic and Sol Invictus iconography directly feeds into Christ-Pantokrator imagery**. See [[halo-nimbus]] for the principal solar-iconographic transmission.

### Decline and destruction
- **391 CE**: Theodosian anti-pagan legislation outlaws pagan worship; Mithraea begin to be destroyed.
- **Late 4th c.**: systematic Christian destruction of Mithraea across the empire. Many are converted into churches (San Clemente, Rome). Cult-images are smashed in situ — the broken pieces of tauroctonies preserved in many Mithraea are the archaeological signature of forced-Christian-iconoclasm rather than gradual abandonment.
- **The cult's institutional end** is conventionally dated to the early 5th c.; some scholars argue for marginal continuation into the early 5th c. in remote areas.

## Contested interpretations
- **Iranian-origin vs. Roman-construction.** Cumont 1903 (the founder of modern Mithraic studies) argued Roman Mithraism was substantially-Iranian Mithraism transplanted. Ulansey 1989 and the "Beck-Gordon-Bjornebye" school have argued for greater Roman-imperial-period innovation, with Iranian content thinner than Cumont claimed. Beck 2006 attempts a balanced synthesis; the debate continues.
- **Astrological-cosmic reading vs. soteriological reading.** Whether the tauroctony is primarily (a) an astronomical-cosmological diagram (Ulansey 1989), (b) a soteriological-initiatic icon (Beck 2006), or (c) both simultaneously (the dominant current view) is debated.
- **Eucharistic-Mithraic-parallel question.** See above. Most current scholarship treats the structural parallels as real but the direct-influence reading as overdrawn — the parallels reflect shared Hellenistic-Roman mystery-religion vocabulary, not Christian-borrowing or pagan-borrowing in either direction.
- **20th-c. fascist-occult appropriation.** Some 20th-c. occult-fascist movements ([[stephen-mcnallen]] and modern "Mithraic-Aryan" revival fringe; Nazi-period Aryanism's Mithraic-iconographic adoption) revived tauroctony imagery in conjunction with swastika and Aryan-Iranian symbolism. **Catalogued here as a Tier-4 fact** per symbol-discipline (the swastika rule mandates surfacing the polemic-inversion when appropriation history exists). Goodrick-Clarke 1985/2004 is the principal scholarly treatment.

## Mystery / unknown variants
The Mithraic mysteries' **textual content is almost wholly lost** — no Mithraic scripture survives. Reconstruction depends almost entirely on (a) the 300+ surviving tauroctonies and other Mithraic iconography catalogued in *CIMRM*, (b) Christian polemical references (Justin, Tertullian, Firmicus Maternus, Jerome), (c) some Mithraic inscriptions. `mystery-status: partially-deciphered` — much known iconographically, much unknown ritually.
