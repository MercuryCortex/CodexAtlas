# Cross-Pattée (Templar Cross)

## Identity
The **Cross-Pattée** (French *croix pattée* — "footed cross"; Latin *crux patens* — "open cross") is a **Christian cross form with arms widening toward the ends** like splayed feet (hence the name). The form is iconographically associated with the [[tradition-knights-templar|Knights Templar]] from **1147 onward** — when Pope Eugenius III granted the Order the **red cross-pattée on white mantle** at Vézelay as the Order prepared to depart for the [[event-second-crusade-1147-1149|Second Crusade]] — and **with the [[tradition-teutonic-knights|Teutonic Knights]] from 1198** in the color-inverted **black cross-pattée on white** form. The Teutonic form survived the Order's secularization and became the **Iron Cross** of Prussia (1813 onward), making the cross-pattée one of the **most continuously-attested Western European military-religious iconographic forms** — Templar 1147 → Teutonic 1198 → Prussian Iron Cross 1813 → German military decoration to the present.

## Earliest attestation
- **1147** — Pope Eugenius III grants the red cross-pattée on white mantle to the Templars at Vézelay. From this date the form is the canonical Templar visual identity.
- **1198** — Teutonic Order formally militarized; adopts the black cross-pattée on white as its identifying mark.
- **1307–1312** — [[event-trial-of-templars-1307-1314|Templar trial]] and suppression; the cross-pattée disappears from active Templar use but survives in:
  - **Iberian Templar-successor orders** — the [[tradition-order-of-christ|Portuguese Order of Christ]] (1319; see [[event-order-of-christ-foundation-1319]]) and the Aragonese Order of Montesa (1317) preserve modified-Templar visual identity. The [[cross-order-of-christ|Cross of the Order of Christ]] is a red cross-pattée with internal white cross-pattée — the form Vasco da Gama and other Portuguese-Age-of-Discovery navigators used on their sails.
  - **Continuous Teutonic Order use** — the Order survives in modified-Catholic form to the present (headquartered in Vienna).
- **1813** — Prussian **Iron Cross** instituted by Friedrich Wilhelm III; explicit visual descent from Teutonic Cross.
- **Modern military use** — continuous from 1813 in Prussian-German military decoration tradition.

## Meaning(s) attested
- **Templar 1147 form** — the red cross-pattée is the *crusader's blood-offering*; the white mantle is *purity-of-monastic-vow*. Together: the integrated Templar identity as armed monk consecrated to the defense of the Holy Land.
- **Teutonic 1198 form** — the color-inverted black-on-white signals the parallel-but-distinct German military-religious identity.
- **Iron Cross 1813 form** — *military valor in defense of the German state*; explicit appropriation of the medieval-Christian-military-religious-order visual heritage for the modern nation-state military.
- **Modern reception** — the cross-pattée has substantial **20th-21st-c. esoteric-Templar reception** in connection with the [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis]] — modern Templarist groups (the Order of the Solar Temple, various neo-Templar groups) frequently use the cross-pattée.

## Variants
See YAML — Templar 1147, Teutonic 1198, Prussian Iron Cross 1813, modern military.

## Cross-family appearance
The cross-pattée is **specifically Christian** and does not migrate to other religious traditions. Within Christianity, it is one of the **principal variant cross-forms** alongside the [[latin-cross]], [[greek-cross]], [[maltese-cross]], [[celtic-cross]], [[tau-cross]], and [[coptic-cross]].

## Contested interpretations
- **The exact geometric origin of the splay-form.** Some heraldic historians trace it to early-medieval Frankish-Carolingian gold-leaf-cross designs; others to Byzantine military iconography. Templar adoption in 1147 is securely documented.
- **Modern Iron Cross associations.** The cross-pattée's continuous use through the Third Reich complicates modern reception. German law (StGB §86, §86a) restricts public display of specifically-Nazi versions of the Iron Cross while preserving the modern Bundeswehr form. Modern public-domain Templar-cross use is unrestricted but contested in some contexts due to neo-fascist Templar-myth appropriations.
