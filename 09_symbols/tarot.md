# Tarot

## Identity

The **tarot** is a set of 78 playing cards — 22 **Major Arcana** (or "Trump" cards) + 56 **Minor Arcana** (four suits of 14 cards each) — that originated as a **card game** in northern Italy around 1440 CE and were subsequently elaborated, in 18th–19th century France, into the world's most comprehensive **visual-symbolic system of Western esotericism**. The two identities coexist: the tarot is still played as a card game in France, Germany, and Italy; it is also, in its esoteric reading, a portable diagram of the entire Hermetic-Kabbalistic-astrological cosmological framework — the Western mandala.

## Critical chronology (many popular accounts are wrong)

The history of the tarot is one of the most frequently distorted in Western esotericism. The following is the documented scholarly account (Dummett 1980, Tier 1):

- **c. 1440 CE** — **Visconti-Sforza deck** (Milan): earliest surviving tarot deck. Hand-painted on vellum for the Visconti and Sforza ducal families. A card game (*gioco dei tarocchi*) with allegorical trump cards added to a standard 4-suit deck. **No evidence of esoteric intent.**
- **c. 1450–1500** — The card game spreads across northern Italy; numerous early decks documented (Tarot of Marseilles pattern crystallizes c. 1500).
- **1781** — **Antoine Court de Gébelin** (*Le Monde Primitif*, vol. 8): FIRST claim that the tarot encodes ancient Egyptian wisdom. **Historically false.** No Egyptian tarot cards have ever been found. Playing cards entered Europe from the Islamic world in the 14th century (attested in Florence 1377, Catalonia 1371). Court de Gébelin invented a compelling myth — not a historical fact.
- **1788** — **Etteilla** (Jean-Baptiste Alliette): first deck explicitly designed for divination. The divinatory tarot is an **18th-century French invention**.
- **1854–1856** — **Eliphas Lévi** (*Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie*): Kabbalistic-tarot synthesis. Maps 22 Major Arcana to 22 Hebrew letters and 22 paths of the Tree of Life. Enormously influential; becomes the organizing logic of Western esoteric tarot.
- **1888** — **Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn** (founded by MacGregor Mathers, William Wynn Westcott): elaborates the Lévi system; assigns each Major Arcanum to a Hebrew letter + planet/sign; assigns each pip card to a zodiacal decan.
- **1909** — **Rider-Waite-Smith deck** (A.E. Waite + Pamela Colman Smith, Rider Company): the most influential tarot deck ever published. First to fully illustrate all 78 cards. Establishes the iconic imagery that defines "tarot" globally.
- **1944** — **Aleister Crowley + Lady Frieda Harris** (*The Book of Thoth*): the Thoth Tarot. Projective geometry in every card; complex astrological-alchemical-Kabbalistic correspondences; *Thelema* system. Second most-used deck globally.

## The 78-card structure as cosmological diagram

In the Golden Dawn system, the 78-card tarot is a **complete diagram of the Hermetic-Kabbalistic universe**:

**Major Arcana (22 cards):**
- = 22 Hebrew letters (Aleph–Tav)
- = 22 paths on the Kabbalistic Tree of Life (connecting the 10 sefirot)
- = The soul's journey: **The Fool** (0, Aleph, pure potential, pure *ayin* — nothingness before creation) → **The World** (XXI, Tav, completion, the perfected universe)
- The sequence is a narrative of spiritual initiation through every level of existence

**Minor Arcana (56 cards):**
- 4 suits = 4 elements = 4 Kabbalistic Worlds (Atziluth/Beriah/Yetzirah/Assiah) = 4 letters of YHWH
- 10 pip cards per suit = 10 sefirot as experienced in each element
- 36 pip cards (2–10 of each suit) = 36 astrological decans (each card assigned 10 degrees of the zodiac)
- 4 court cards per suit = 4 levels of personality/soul within each elemental world

The result: the 78-card deck is a **portable Kabbalistic-astrological-elemental cosmological map** — the entire universe from the unmanifest Ain Soph through all creation to the material world, laid out as 78 positions through which the soul can navigate.

## MASSIVE WIN: The tarot as the Western mandala

**The tarot is the closest analog to the Buddhist mandala in Western esotericism.** Both are:
1. **Complete cosmological diagrams** — every level of existence mapped
2. **Simultaneously a diagram and a practice** — you don't just look at them; you work with them to navigate the cosmological structure
3. **Portable** — a mandala can be carried; so can a deck of cards
4. **Initiatory** — the sequence (Arcana / mandala quadrants) encodes a path of spiritual progression from periphery to center, from ignorance to enlightenment

The **Fool's Journey** (tarot's inner narrative: The Fool = 0, the unmanifest soul, travels through all 21 Major Arcana stages to reach The World = completion) is structurally identical to the mandala's journey from the palace gates inward to the central deity. Both are **complete maps of the soul's trajectory through existence**.

The mandala has Vajradhara or Vairocana at its center; the Major Arcana has **The World** — a dancing androgyne surrounded by a wreath, holding two wands (the axis mundi of beginning and ending), set against the four tetramorph figures (bull/lion/eagle/angel = the four evangelists in Christian iconography; the four fixed signs of the zodiac in astrological terms; the four elements; the four Kabbalistic worlds). **The World is the perfected cosmos.** The Fool's journey to The World = the soul's journey through all levels of existence to its completed, integrated, cosmic form.

No documented transmission between Buddhist mandala tradition and the esoteric tarot. This is **parallel spiritual technology**: independent invention of the "complete cosmological map as portable visual-spiritual practice tool."

## Contested interpretations

- **The Egyptian-origin myth**: Court de Gébelin (1781) invented this; Etteilla elaborated it; it has no evidentiary basis. The playing card entered Europe from the Islamic world, not Egypt. The claim persists in popular occultism because it is a more glamorous origin story than "14th-century Islamic card game."
- **Did the tarot encode occult wisdom from the beginning?** Dummett (1980) definitively argues: no. The trump sequence encodes a Renaissance hierarchy of allegorical virtues and temporal powers (Emperor, Pope, etc.) — a cultural statement about social and moral order, not an occult initiation system. The esoteric meaning was projected backward onto the cards by Court de Gébelin and Lévi centuries later.
- **The Lévi correspondence (22 Arcana = 22 letters)**: Lévi claimed this correspondence was ancient; most scholars (Dummett, Decker) believe Lévi either invented it or adapted it from a very recent oral tradition. The correspondence is elegant and generative, but it is almost certainly an 18th–19th century synthesis, not an ancient secret.
- **Divination validity**: The academic consensus (Dummett) is that tarot divination has no scientifically established validity. The tarot's value as a divinatory tool is psychological and reflective (Carl Jung's concept of synchronicity; the tarot as a projective system that surfaces unconscious content) — not predictive in a literal sense.
