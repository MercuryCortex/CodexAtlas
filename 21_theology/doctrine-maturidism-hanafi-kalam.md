# Māturīdism — Ḥanafī-Samarqandī Sunni Kalām

**Māturīdism** (*al-Māturīdiyya*) is the systematic-theological school of mainstream Ḥanafī Sunni Islam, founded by Abū Manṣūr al-Māturīdī (c. 853–944 CE) in Samarqand and developed by the Samarqandī + Bukhārān commentary tradition through the 14th c. It is the "other Sunni school" alongside Ashʿarism — the two together constituting the doctrinal range of *Ahl al-Sunna wa-l-Jamāʿa* (the orthodox Sunni mainstream) in post-classical doxography. Geographically Māturīdism is the dominant Sunni theology of Central Asia, the Indian subcontinent (both Deobandī + Barelvi), the Ottoman Empire, and modern Turkey — a population reach larger than Ashʿarism in adherent count.

## The doctrinal core

Māturīdism shares Ashʿarī systematic theology on most major positions but diverges in characteristic ways:

1. **Divine attributes are real, eternal, and inhere in the divine essence** — the same *lā huwa wa-lā ghayruhu* formula as Ashʿarism. Distinctively, the Māturīdī school affirms *takwīn* (divine bringing-into-being) as itself an eternal attribute — the Ashʿarī school treats acts-of-creation as temporal effects of the eternal attribute of power. The disagreement is technical but doctrinally consequential for the relation between the eternal divine essence and the temporal created order.
2. **The Qurʾān is uncreated** — same post-miḥna traditionalist position as Ashʿarism.
3. **The difficult attributes** (face, hand, throne-sitting) are affirmed *bilā kayfa* — same synthetic middle as Ashʿarism between allegorisation and anthropomorphism.
4. **Unaided rational moral knowability** — distinctively, Māturīdism affirms that reason alone (without revelation) can know that God exists, that God is one, and that certain major virtues are good (truthfulness, gratitude, justice) and certain major vices are evil (lying, ingratitude, injustice). This is closer to the Muʿtazilī ethical objectivism than to Ashʿarī divine-command ethics — though Māturīdism stops short of the Muʿtazilī position that the moral status of *all* acts is rationally knowable without revelation (the specific Sharīʿa obligations require the *samʿ* / revelation).
5. **Human action is *iḫtiyār + qudra ḥādiṯa* (choice + created power)** — the human has a real created power of action that genuinely produces the act, while the act is also referred to divine creation. This is more libertarian than Ashʿarī *kasb* (acquisition) and approaches a real compatibilism. Pessagno 1984 is the standard technical study.
6. **Faith (*īmān*) is verbal-cognitive assent (*taṣdīq*) only** — works are required by the Sharīʿa but are not constituent of faith itself. The Ḥanafī inheritance from Abū Ḥanīfa is preserved. This distinguishes Māturīdism from some Ashʿarī formulations that include works in the constitution of faith and from the Khārijī + Muʿtazilī positions that grave sin removes faith.

## The school's historical trajectory

The Samarqandī origin gives way over the 11th-14th c. to a Ḥanafī-Māturīdī complex that spreads with the Saljūq + Ghaznavid + Khwārazmian + Mongol-Ilkhanate + Mughal + Ottoman expansions:

- **Central Asia + Khwarezm + Khorasan** — the Māturīdī heartland; the standard madrasa theology from the 11th c. to the Russian / Soviet conquest.
- **The Indian subcontinent** — the dominant Ḥanafī-Māturīdī theology of the Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526) and the Mughal Empire (1526–1857), and of the major 19th-20th-c. Indo-Muslim reform movements (Deobandī, Barelvi, Tablīghī Jamāʿat — all formally Māturīdī).
- **The Ottoman Empire** — the official madrasa-curriculum theology of the Ottoman ʿulamāʾ; al-Taftāzānī's commentary on al-Nasafī's *ʿAqāʾid* is the standard text.
- **Modern Turkey** — Diyanet's official theology + the academic faculty-of-theology curriculum remain Māturīdī.

## The Ashʿarī-Māturīdī systematic-difference catalogues

From the late classical period onward, Sunni theologians produced catalogues of Ashʿarī-Māturīdī doctrinal differences — typically running 40-50 specific systematic points. Al-Bayāḍī's 17th-c. *Ishārāt al-marām* is the standard. The catalogues serve simultaneously to mark the genuine local differences and to reinforce the shared mainstream identity (both schools are *Ahl al-Sunna* against Muʿtazila, Shīʿa, Khārijīs, and the Salafī-traditionalist anti-kalām position).

## Cross-tradition resonances

- **Catholic *via media* between Thomism and Scotism.** The Māturīdī compromise on human action (real created power + divine sovereignty) is structurally parallel to the Catholic post-Tridentine *via media* between strong Thomism (God moves the will internally — Báñez) and Molinism (God's middle knowledge accommodates real human freedom). Both traditions developed sophisticated compatibilist positions that mediate between strong determinism and libertarianism.
- **Ḥanafī procedural reasoning + Anglican Hookerian middle-way ecclesiology.** The Ḥanafī-Māturīdī self-presentation as the rational + reasoned middle-way Sunni school (Muʿtazilī rationalism in method, traditionalist in substance) is structurally parallel to the Hookerian-Anglican self-presentation as the reasoned via media between Catholic and Calvinist extremes. The structural parallel has been noted in modern comparative theology literature.
