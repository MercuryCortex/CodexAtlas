# Trimūrti (Hindu Doctrine of the Triple-Form Divinity)

The systematic Hindu doctrine of the **triple-formed God** — one divine reality manifesting in three principal *mūrtis* (forms, images, embodiments): **Brahmā** as Creator (*sṛṣṭi*), **Viṣṇu** as Preserver (*sthiti*), and **Śiva** as Destroyer / Transformer (*saṁhāra*). The Trimūrti is the canonical Hindu articulation of triadic-divinity and the principal cross-tradition structural parallel to the Christian Trinity.

## The functional triad

The doctrinal core distinguishes three cosmic functions performed by the one divine reality:

1. **Brahmā** = *sṛṣṭi* — the cosmic creation function; cosmos brought into manifest existence.
2. **Viṣṇu** = *sthiti* — the cosmic preservation / sustenance function; cosmos maintained through its life-cycle.
3. **Śiva** = *saṁhāra* — the cosmic destruction / transformation function; cosmos dissolved at end of cycle, returning to potential.

The three functions are coordinated into one cosmic cycle (*kalpa*) that repeats endlessly. The Trimūrti is thus the divinity-side of the *yuga-kalpa* cyclic-time doctrine — the same divine reality presiding over each phase of cosmic process.

## Sectarian inflections

The Trimūrti is read differently across the three major theistic Hindu schools:

- **Vaiṣṇavas** (devotees of Viṣṇu) read Viṣṇu as the *supreme* divine reality, with Brahmā emerging from Viṣṇu's navel-lotus (Bhāgavata Purāṇa) and Śiva as a manifestation of Viṣṇu's destructive aspect. The Trimūrti is preserved but ranked.
- **Śaivas** (devotees of Śiva) read Śiva as the *supreme* divine reality, with Brahmā and Viṣṇu as aspects of Śiva's cosmic functions. The Linga Purāṇa narrates the cosmic-pillar-of-fire episode in which Brahmā and Viṣṇu fail to find Śiva's beginning and end, establishing Śiva's supremacy.
- **Smārtas** (followers of the Pañcāyatana / Five-Form worship system codified by Śaṅkara) hold a *true triadic equality* — the three forms are equally valid concretizations of the one *nirguṇa* Brahman behind them. Advaita Vedānta reads the Trimūrti at the *saguṇa* (with-attributes) conventional level.

## The Devī substrate

A significant Śākta (Goddess-devotional) reading — the Devī-Māhātmya in the Markaṇḍeya Purāṇa, the Devī-Bhāgavata Purāṇa — treats the *Mahādevī* (the Great Goddess) as the *substrate* of the Trimūrti: the three male gods (Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Śiva) are her cosmic agents, and she is the underlying divine power (*śakti*) through which they act. This Goddess-as-substrate reading inflects the doctrine without negating the male-triad surface structure.

## Cross-tradition positioning — the comparison-pair surface

The Trimūrti is the **central comparison-pair node** with the Christian Trinity for the Atlas's triadic-divinity cluster. Three principal cross-tradition surfaces:

**Cross-tradition parallel — Christian Trinity (Nicene)**: the structural similarity of two triadic-divinity schemes has been continuously remarked since Müller 1878. The *closest single cross-tradition comparison* in the world's theological articulations of triadic ultimate. The asymmetry: the Trimūrti is a *functional-role* triad (three activities within one divine reality), while the Trinity is a *hypostatic* triad (three distinct subjects sharing one essence). The Trimūrti has the form **one God, three forms / mūrtis**; the Trinity has the form **one essence, three persons / hypostases**. Perennial-philosophy collapse (Schuon, Smith) is rejected by mainstream comparative theology — but the structural illumination is rich, and Panikkar 1973 develops it as a sustained theological project.

**Parallel — Mahāyāna Trikāya (Buddha-Three-Bodies)**: the Mahāyāna *Three Bodies* doctrine (Dharmakāya / Sambhogakāya / Nirmāṇakāya) is an Indic-Buddhist counterpart triadic scheme emerging in the same cultural matrix as the Trimūrti's Puranic articulation (-100 to +500). Whether the parallel reflects shared substrate, mutual influence, or independent convergence is debated; the structural family resemblance — one ultimate, three distinct aspects — is the cleanest within the Indic religious family.

**Parallel — other triadic schemes globally**: the Egyptian Theban triad Amun-Mut-Khonsu (father-mother-son family triad); the Heliopolitan Atum-Shu-Tefnut origination triad; the Sumerian An-Enlil-Enki cosmic-realm sovereigns; the Slavic Triglav three-headed god; the Indo-Iranian Mitra-Varuna-Indra functional triad (Dumézil's trifunctional reconstruction). The world's triadic-divinity schemes form a comparative family in which Trimūrti + Trinity + Trikāya are the three most-systematized articulations.

## The Indian-vs-Western "one God / three forms" formula

The standard pan-Hindu summary — "*ekam sad viprā bahudhā vadanti*" ("the One Real is by sages called by many names"; Ṛg-Veda 1.164.46) — frames the Trimūrti as the **most-systematic** instance of the broader Indic principle that the one divine reality admits many names and forms. The Christian Trinity has no analogous principle — the three hypostases are *exhaustively* the divine personhood, and other names/forms are not multiple-divinity-aspects but creatures.

This is the key doctrinal distinction at the heart of the structural parallel: the Trimūrti is one instance of an Indic *one-many* divine ontology; the Trinity is the *unique* triadic structure of the Christian God. The mainstream comparative-theology literature (Klostermaier, Panikkar, Coward) treats this as the most important asymmetry to preserve when reading the two doctrines side-by-side.
