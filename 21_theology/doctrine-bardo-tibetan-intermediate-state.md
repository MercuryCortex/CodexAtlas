# Bardo (Tibetan Vajrayāna Doctrine of the Intermediate State)

The Tibetan Vajrayāna doctrine of the **post-mortem intermediate state** between death and the next rebirth (or liberation). Tibetan *bar do* literally means "**between two**" — the interval between two existential conditions. The doctrine descends from the Sanskrit-Sarvāstivāda *antarābhava* (intermediate-state) articulated in Vasubandhu's *Abhidharmakośa* (~4th c.) but is most-elaborately developed in the Tibetan Vajrayāna six-bardos doctrine + the canonical *Bardo Thödol* ("Liberation Through Hearing in the Intermediate State"), popularly known as the *Tibetan Book of the Dead*.

## The six bardos

The developed Vajrayāna doctrine articulates **six bardos** — six intermediate-states covering both this-life and the post-mortem interval:

1. **Kye-né bardo** (*skye gnas bar do*) — the bardo of birth + ordinary waking-life (this entire embodied life IS a bardo).
2. **Milam bardo** (*rmi lam bar do*) — the dream-state bardo.
3. **Samten bardo** (*bsam gtan bar do*) — the meditation-bardo.
4. **Chikai bardo** (*ʼchi khaʼi bar do*) — the bardo of the moment of death.
5. **Chönyi bardo** (*chos nyid bar do*) — the bardo of dharmatā (ultimate-nature; the post-mortem clear-light + peaceful-and-wrathful deity visions).
6. **Sipa bardo** (*srid pa bar do*) — the bardo of becoming (the rebirth-search-and-conception).

The six-fold schema treats *all* of conditioned-existence as bardo-states; this-life and the after-life are continuous intermediate-states.

## The clear-light at the moment of death

The doctrinal centerpiece is the **chikai bardo** — at the moment of death, the dissolution of the gross + subtle elements reveals the **clear light** (*ʼod gsal*) of the dharmakāya — the unmediated luminosity of ultimate reality. If the dying person recognizes this clear-light as their own innate buddha-nature, **instantaneous liberation** (nirvāṇa / buddha-attainment) is possible. The bardo-doctrine treats the death-moment as the **most-favorable single opportunity** for liberation in the entire human existence.

If recognition fails, the soul passes into the **chönyi bardo** — the dharmatā-bardo of post-mortem visions. The Karma Lingpa cycle (*kar gling zhi khro*) describes the sequential appearance of the **42 peaceful deities** (days 1-7) and the **58 wrathful deities** (days 8-14), each offering an opportunity for recognition-and-liberation. The deities are projections of one's own mind; recognition as such = liberation.

If recognition continues to fail across the 14-day cycle, the soul enters the **sipa bardo** — the bardo of becoming — and is driven by karmic-conditioning toward the next rebirth.

## The Bardo Thödol (Tibetan Book of the Dead)

The canonical bardo-text is the *Bardo Thödol* — literally "**Liberation Through Hearing in the Intermediate State**." It is **read aloud by a lama to the dying or recently-dead person** to assist recognition of the post-mortem visions. The text is structured as direct address to the deceased: "O nobly-born, listen carefully ..."

Textual-historical status (Cuevas 2003): the text is **attributed to Padmasambhava** (8th c.) and **revealed as terma** by Karma Lingpa (14th c.). Cuevas demonstrates that the actual textual-formation is largely a 14th-c. Tibetan synthesis drawing on earlier Indic + Tibetan materials; the "Padmasambhava attribution" reflects Nyingma terma-tradition theology rather than direct authorship.

Western reception (Lopez 2011): the 1927 Evans-Wentz English edition popularized the text but laundered it through Theosophical + Jungian interpretive frames foreign to the Tibetan tradition. Subsequent 1960s reception (Leary, Metzner, Alpert *The Psychedelic Experience* 1964) read it as a manual of consciousness-states. The modern critical translation (Gyurme Dorje 2005) restores the doctrinal Tibetan context.

## Cross-tradition positioning

**MASSIVE-WIN parallel — Catholic purgatory**: Tibetan bardo and `doctrine-purgatory-catholic` are the **two most-elaborately-developed religious doctrines of the post-mortem intermediate state** in world religion. Structural parallels:

| Parallel | Catholic purgatory | Tibetan bardo |
|---|---|---|
| Post-mortem interval | Yes — between death and beatific vision | Yes — between death and next rebirth |
| Literary tradition | Dante's *Purgatorio* | *Bardo Thödol* |
| Living's ritual duty | Masses, indulgences, prayers | Lama recitation, 49-day rituals, *phowa* |
| Religious-economic system | Indulgence economy | Lama-officiated death ritual economy |

The eschatological framings differ radically (linear-bodily-resurrection vs cyclic-rebirth-and-liberation), but the **structural religious function of intermediate-state-doctrine** — the living's ongoing ritual duty to the recently-dead, the post-mortem-state requiring doctrinal-systematic articulation — is strikingly parallel. Lopez 2011 + Cuevas 2003 + Le Goff 1984 treat the comparison.

**Parallel — Christian death-bed visions**: medieval Christian *ars moriendi* (art-of-dying) literature shares structural features with the Vajrayāna death-instruction tradition — both presuppose the death-moment as soteric-critical, requiring guided-attention.

**vs. Christian bodily-resurrection**: bardo presupposes the Indic cyclic-rebirth eschatology; it has no place within the linear Christian eschatology of one-life-one-death-one-resurrection. See `doctrine-reincarnation-samsara` for the underlying eschatological divide.

## Disputes within the tradition + scholarship

- **Sarvāstivāda vs Theravāda** — the Theravāda Abhidhamma denies the antarābhava (intermediate-state); the Sarvāstivāda + Mahāyāna + Vajrayāna affirm it. The doctrinal-difference is one of the principal Abhidharma controversies.
- **Padmasambhava attribution** — devotional-traditional vs critical-historical readings (Cuevas 2003).
- **Western popularization** — Lopez 2011 documents the misreadings + appropriations.
- **49-day duration** — the canonical bardo-period; classical Indic source (*Abhidharmakośa* III.14).
