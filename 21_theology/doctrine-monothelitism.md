# Monothelitism

The 7th-century Christological compromise position: Christ has two natures (per Chalcedon 451) but one will (*thelēma*) and one operation. Articulated by Patriarch Sergius of Constantinople and promulgated under [[heraclius-byzantine-emperor|Emperor Heraclius]] (the *Ekthesis* of 638). Designed to reconcile the Chalcedonian and Miaphysite (Coptic / Syriac / Armenian) churches at a time of catastrophic eastern losses to the Sasanian Persians and then the Arab-Muslim conquests. The compromise was rejected by Maximus the Confessor and Pope Martin I (both imperially persecuted) and condemned at the [[event-third-council-of-constantinople-680|Third Council of Constantinople, 680–681]], which posthumously anathematized Pope Honorius for his initial endorsement.
