# Karma (Indic Doctrine of Action and Its Fruit)

The foundational ethical-metaphysical doctrine of all classical Indic religious traditions: **every intentional action produces karmic-fruit (*phala*) that conditions future experience** — both within this life and across the cycle of rebirths. Sanskrit *karman* literally means "**action, deed**" — in the technical Indic-religious sense, the law of moral causation. Pre-Upaniṣadic Vedic usage applied *karma* to **ritual action** (sacrifice); the **transposition into moral action with future-life consequence** is the great Upaniṣadic theological development that emerges (~-800 to -500) and propagates through the four classical Indic traditions.

## The Upaniṣadic moral-karma articulation

The earliest moral-karma passage is **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.5-6**:

> "A person becomes good by good action, bad by bad action."

And Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.2.13 — the dialogue between Yājñavalkya and Ārtabhāga — where Yājñavalkya reveals the karma-doctrine privately ("we shall talk of this in secret"; the doctrine is initially esoteric). The Upaniṣadic transposition from ritual-karma to ethical-karma is one of the great religious-cognitive innovations of Indian history.

## The four-fold Indic variation

The classical Indic traditions agree on the **principle** of moral causation through karma but differ on the **mechanism**:

1. **Hindu (Brahmanical)** — karma produces *saṃskāra* (latent-impression) in the soul; the saṃskāras determine future-rebirth conditions. Patañjali *Yoga Sūtras* II.12-14 articulates the saṃskāra-theory canonically.
2. **Buddhist** — karma = *cetanā* (volition); the volitional moment of action produces karmic-effect operating through pratītya-samutpāda. The Buddhist innovation is **karma-without-persisting-self** (anātman-karma).
3. **Jain** — karma = literal-subtle-matter (karmic-particles, *karma-paramāṇu*) that adheres to the *jīva* (soul) like dust to wet cloth. Liberation = stripping off the karmic-matter through ascetic-tapas.
4. **Sikh** — karma is real but the goal is union-with-the-formless-One (Akāl Purakh) through divine-grace + gurmukh-living; rebirth is the consequence of *haumai* (ego).

## The Bhagavad Gītā's karma-yoga

The Gītā (~-200) provides the canonical Hindu integration of karma-doctrine with soteric practice through **karma-yoga**: act without attachment to the fruits of action. The doctrinal solution to the problem of action-binding-the-agent: if the agent renounces the *fruit* (not the *act*), the action produces no binding karma. Kṛṣṇa's teaching to Arjuna at Kurukṣetra integrates the metaphysics of karma with the practical-ethical question of right action.

## The taxonomy of karma

Classical Indic theology distinguishes types of karma:

- **Sañcita karma** — the accumulated stock of karma across all previous lives.
- **Prārabdha karma** — the portion of accumulated karma now in operation in this lifetime (one's current circumstances).
- **Āgāmi / kriyamāṇa karma** — the karma being created now, that will bear fruit in future lives.
- **Tribal classification** (Mānava Dharmaśāstra 12): *sattvic* (luminous/good), *rajasic* (passionate/mixed), *tamasic* (dark/bad) acts producing corresponding rebirths.

## Cross-tradition positioning

**Cross-tradition comparison — Augustinian original-sin**: Indic karma and `doctrine-original-sin-augustinian` both articulate **the inheritance of pre-personal-moral-conditioning** as the underlying problem of human existence — but their mechanisms are radically asymmetric:

| Dimension | Indic Karma | Augustinian Original Sin |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Beginningless accumulation across rebirths | Single historical event (Adam's Fall) |
| Scope | Individual — each soul carries its own karma | Collective — all humanity shares Adam's guilt |
| Mechanism | Continuation through saṃskāra / cetanā / karma-matter | Transmission by carnal descent |
| Inheritability | NO — strictly per-individual across rebirths | YES — across generations within one life-frame |
| Solution | Path + ethical purification | External grace + atonement |

The two articulations represent the **two principal world-religious solutions** to the problem of pre-volitional moral inheritance — one **collective-genealogical-Christian**, one **individual-trans-life-Indic**. The cross-tradition asymmetry on inheritability-of-moral-status is one of the deepest religious-anthropological divides between Abrahamic + Indic traditions. O'Flaherty 1980 + Halbfass 1991 + Pagels 1988 develop the comparison.

**Central mechanism of saṃsāra**: see [[doctrine-reincarnation-samsara]]. Karma and rebirth are structurally inseparable.

**Solved by mokṣa / nirvāṇa**: see [[doctrine-moksha-hindu-liberation]] and [[doctrine-nirvana-buddhist-cessation]]. Liberation = cessation of the karmic-accumulation-and-fruition mechanism.

**Operates through pratītya-samutpāda (Buddhist)**: see `doctrine-pratitya-samutpada-dependent-origination`. In Buddhist articulation, karma is *cetanā* operating through the 12-link conditioned-origination chain.

**vs. Western juridical-atonement**: the Indic problematic (karmic-conditioning of self) and the Western problematic (inherited debt + guilt requiring external satisfaction) are structurally different — the Indic solution is self-purification through path-practice; the Western solution is external-grace through vicarious-substitution. No Indic equivalent to satisfaction-atonement; no Western equivalent to karma-as-trans-life-self-accumulation.

## Disputes within Indic traditions

- **Hindu-Buddhist anātman-karma debate** — how does karma operate without a persisting self? The principal classical Indian philosophical debate.
- **Jain karma-as-matter vs Hindu/Buddhist karma-as-process** — the metaphysical status of karmic-residue.
- **Karma + grace** — Rāmānuja's Viśiṣṭādvaita + Sikh + bhakti traditions integrate divine-grace into the karma-mokṣa schema. Pure-karmic-self-sufficiency vs grace-supplemented-karma.
- **Free will + karma** — does karma determine the present (fatalism) or merely condition it (compatibilism)? The classical position is compatibilist — past karma sets conditions; present free-will creates new karma.
