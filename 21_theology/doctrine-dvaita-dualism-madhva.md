# Dvaita Vedānta

**Dvaita Vedānta** is the dualist (*dvaita* = "two") systematic theology of Brahmanical Vaiṣṇavism — the doctrinal position that Brahman (identified with Viṣṇu) and the souls / world are eternally distinct realities, related by the absolute dependence of the latter on the former. It is the most thoroughgoing rejection of Advaitin monism within Vedānta, systematised by [[madhva|Madhva]] (Ānanda-tīrtha / Pūrṇaprajña; 1238–1317 CE) on the Karnataka coast and developed by a 700-year scholastic tradition centred on the Udupi monastery and the medieval Vijayanagara court.

## The doctrinal core

Six linked propositions constitute the canonical Dvaitin position:

1. **Brahman is *saguṇa*** (with qualities) and is identified specifically with Viṣṇu-Nārāyaṇa — *para-brahman* is not a generic absolute but a specific personal God with eternal auspicious attributes (*kalyāṇa-guṇas*).
2. **The *pañca-bheda* — five eternal distinctions** are the structural doctrinal centre:
   - God ↔ individual soul (*Īśvara-jīva-bheda*)
   - God ↔ matter (*Īśvara-jaḍa-bheda*)
   - individual soul ↔ individual soul (*jīva-jīva-bheda*)
   - individual soul ↔ matter (*jīva-jaḍa-bheda*)
   - matter ↔ matter (*jaḍa-jaḍa-bheda*)
   These are not appearances or conventional truths — they are *real*, *eternal*, *intrinsic* features of reality.
3. **Souls and matter depend absolutely on Brahman** (*svatantra-asvatantra* — independent vs. dependent realities). Brahman alone is *svatantra* (independent); everything else is *paratantra* (dependent). The dependence is metaphysical, not merely causal — the contingent reality of souls and matter is sustained moment-by-moment by the divine will.
4. **The *tāratamya* — eternal gradation** of souls. Madhva is unique in classical Hindu thought in holding a doctrine of *eternal threefold predestination*: souls are eternally divided into the *mukti-yogya* (those fit for liberation), the *nitya-saṁsārin* (those eternally bound to transmigration), and the *tamo-yogya* (those eternally destined for the lowest hells). This is the closest pre-modern non-Abrahamic structural parallel to Calvinist double predestination + the closest classical Hindu approach to a doctrine of eternal damnation. The doctrine is theologically singular within Hinduism.
5. **Liberation is *bhakti* + grace** — eternal loving service to Viṣṇu in Vaikuṇṭha. The *jīva* retains its individuality, *and* the *tāratamya* persists in the liberated state: even among the *muktas* there are eternal hierarchies of bliss, with the goddess Lakṣmī highest among created beings (and never herself liberated, because never bound).
6. **Realist epistemology** — the world is real as we perceive it; *pratyakṣa* (perception), *anumāna* (inference), and *āgama* (scripture) are the three valid means of knowledge (*pramāṇa*), and they yield real knowledge of a real world of real distinctions.

## The anti-Advaita polemic

Madhva's entire doctrinal structure is anti-Advaitin: *pañca-bheda* directly negates *advaita*; the realist epistemology negates *māyā*; the eternal individuation of souls negates the universal *Ātman*; the grace-dependent soteriology negates *jñāna*-liberation; and the doctrine of eternal damnation negates the universalist horizon of Advaitin *mokṣa*. The 16th-c. *Nyāyāmṛta* of Vyāsatīrtha and the responding *Advaita-siddhi* of Madhusūdana Sarasvatī are the locus classicus of the Advaita-Dvaita scholastic exchange — one of the most sustained philosophical disputes in any tradition.

## The Vijayanagara context

Stoker 2016 traces how the 14th-16th-c. Vijayanagara court patronised the Advaita-Dvaita debate as part of its sectarian-political positioning: the Mādhva polemic flourished institutionally because it served the court's strategy of religious legitimation, with Vyāsatīrtha's polemics receiving direct royal patronage. The institutional reading complicates the older view of pure philosophical-theological motivation.

## Cross-tradition resonances

- **Calvinist double predestination.** The *tāratamya* doctrine is the closest pre-modern non-Abrahamic structural parallel to Calvinist *praedestinatio gemina*. Both hold that some souls are eternally elected and others eternally damned by divine sovereignty, not by their own merit. Sharma 1962 notes the comparative-theological singularity; Buchta 2014 explores the parallel with care. The doctrines arose in different centuries (Madhva 13th, Calvin 16th) and in unrelated traditions but converge on a structurally identical move: the absolute sovereignty of God + the heterogeneity of eternal destinies.
- **Christian Trinitarian + incarnational realism.** Madhva's *saguṇa* personal-God realism + the eternal distinction-in-relation between God and souls has structural parallels with the Trinitarian relations of subsistence and the Chalcedonian distinction-without-confusion. Sarma 2003 notes that modern comparative theology has begun to take Madhva seriously as a non-Western theistic realist interlocutor.
- **The Hindu-Christian comparative window.** Because of the body-and-soul realism + the personal God + the eternal individuation of souls + the predestination doctrine, Madhva has been the Hindu theologian most engaged by Western Christian comparative theology — first by Jesuit missionaries in 16th-c. Goa, then by 19th-c. Indologists, and most systematically by 20th-c. comparative theologians.
