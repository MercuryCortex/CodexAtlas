# Pratītya-samutpāda (Buddhist Doctrine of Dependent Origination)

The **central metaphysical doctrine** of Buddhism: all phenomena arise in **mutual conditioned dependence** — nothing exists with self-sufficient independent existence (*svabhāva*); everything is co-arising-with-and-conditioned-by other phenomena. Sanskrit *pratītya-samutpāda* (Pāli *paṭicca-samuppāda*) = "**dependent co-arising**." The doctrine is articulated in seed-form in the Buddha's first sermons (~-500) and developed across all subsequent Buddhist schools. The Buddha himself characterized the centrality: "**Whoever sees dependent origination sees the Dharma; whoever sees the Dharma sees dependent origination**" (MN 28).

## The foundational formula

The abstract formula appears throughout the Sutta Piṭaka:

> "When this is, that is.
> From the arising of this, that arises.
> When this is not, that is not.
> From the cessation of this, that ceases."

This abstract formula articulates the **principle** of dependent origination as such — every phenomenon depends on conditions; nothing arises self-sufficiently.

## The twelve nidānas (links)

The most-developed articulation is the **twelve-link chain** (the *nidānas*), explaining how rebirth occurs without a persisting self:

1. **Avijjā** (ignorance) →
2. **Saṅkhāra** (volitional formations / karma) →
3. **Viññāṇa** (consciousness) →
4. **Nāma-rūpa** (name-and-form / mind-and-body) →
5. **Saḷāyatana** (the six sense-bases) →
6. **Phassa** (contact) →
7. **Vedanā** (feeling) →
8. **Taṇhā** (craving) →
9. **Upādāna** (clinging) →
10. **Bhava** (becoming) →
11. **Jāti** (birth) →
12. **Jarā-maraṇa** (aging-and-death) → loops back to **avijjā**.

The chain is the canonical Buddhist explanation of how saṃsāra perpetuates itself — and, in reverse-cessation reading (the *anuloma-paṭiloma*), how nirvāṇa is attained: cease ignorance, and the entire chain unravels.

## Three-life vs single-moment readings

Classical Theravāda Abhidhamma (Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga* XVII) reads the 12 links as covering **three lives**:

- Past life: links 1-2 (ignorance + formations)
- Present life: links 3-10 (consciousness through becoming)
- Future life: links 11-12 (birth + aging-and-death)

Mahāyāna + later Theravāda commentators emphasize **single-moment** readings where all 12 links operate in every moment of conditioned-experience.

## Nāgārjuna's emptiness-equivalence

Nāgārjuna's *Mūlamadhyamaka-kārikā* ch. 24 provides the most-influential Mahāyāna reading: **dependent-origination IS emptiness** (*śūnyatā*). Because nothing exists with self-sufficient *svabhāva*, everything is "empty" of independent essence. The doctrines of pratītya-samutpāda + śūnyatā are equivalent — two descriptions of the same metaphysical structure.

This reading is the principal Madhyamaka contribution to Buddhist metaphysics + soteriology. The 14th-c. Tibetan Gelug systematization (Tsongkhapa's *Lam Rim Chen Mo*) takes this as the doctrinal-summit of the Mahāyāna teaching.

## Yogācāra extension

Asaṅga + Vasubandhu's Yogācāra school extends pratītya-samutpāda through the **ālaya-vijñāna** (storehouse-consciousness) — the karmic-seeds (*bīja*) generated by saṅkhāra are stored in the ālaya-vijñāna and ripen as future-experience. The Yogācāra storehouse-consciousness preserves the karmic-continuity that pratītya-samutpāda requires, without requiring a persisting self-substance.

## Cross-tradition positioning

**Central mechanism of saṃsāra**: see [[doctrine-reincarnation-samsara]]. Pratītya-samutpāda IS the Buddhist mechanism for cyclic rebirth without persisting-self.

**Central mechanism of karma (Buddhist)**: see [[doctrine-karma-action-fruit]]. The 2nd link (*saṅkhāra* / formations) is the karmic-acting moment.

**Presupposed by nirvāṇa**: see [[doctrine-nirvana-buddhist-cessation]]. Nirvāṇa = cessation of the chain.

**Cross-tradition parallel — Augustinian original-sin**: both articulate the conditioning of the present by prior causes; both reject autonomous-rational-agent anthropology. The asymmetry: pratītya-samutpāda = beginningless-chain operative every moment; original-sin = single-historical-Fall + collective-inheritance. See [[doctrine-original-sin-augustinian]].

**vs. monotheistic creation doctrines**: pratītya-samutpāda explicitly rejects an external-creator or first-cause — the chain is beginningless. The Buddhist tradition is structurally non-theistic in the Abrahamic-creator sense.

## Disputes within Buddhism

- **Three-life vs single-moment reading** — Theravāda Abhidhamma vs Mahāyāna commentarial tradition.
- **Madhyamaka emptiness-equivalence** — Nāgārjuna's pratītya-samutpāda = śūnyatā reading vs Sarvāstivāda-Vaibhāṣika realism about the dharmas.
- **Yogācāra ālaya-vijñāna** — is the storehouse-consciousness an ontological-deviation from anātman, or a phenomenological description?
- **Critical Buddhism (hihan bukkyō)** — late-20th-c. Japanese movement (Matsumoto, Hakamaya) argues tathāgatagarbha-thought is non-Buddhist; only pratītya-samutpāda is authentically Buddhist. Controversial.
