# Shema Yisrael

The central credal-liturgical declaration of Judaism: *Shema Yisrael, Adonai Eloheinu, Adonai Eḥad* — "Hear, O Israel: the LORD is our God, the LORD is one" (Deuteronomy 6:4). The Shema is *the* foundational Jewish theological declaration — recited twice daily in the rabbinic-Orthodox liturgy, traditionally on entering + leaving any space, traditionally as a Jew's last words at death.

## The text + its liturgical expansion

The core Shema is Deuteronomy 6:4-9: the declaration + the *vehavta* commandment to love God *b'khol levavkha, u'v'khol nafshekha, u'v'khol meodekha* ("with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your might"), and the practical instructions (teach to your children, recite when sitting + walking + lying down + rising, bind as a sign on hand + as frontlets between the eyes, write on doorposts of house + gates — the textual basis for tefillin + mezuzah).

The rabbinic *liturgical Shema* expands the unit to three paragraphs (Deuteronomy 6:4-9 + Deuteronomy 11:13-21 + Numbers 15:37-41), bracketed by blessings (Yotzer Or + Ahavah Rabbah before; Emet ve-Yatziv after) — a compositional form fixed by the early rabbinic period (Mishnah Berakhot 1-2; 1st-2nd c. CE).

## The "Eḥad" question — what "one" means

The pivotal interpretive question is what the Hebrew *Eḥad* ("one") affirms. The literal numerical reading ("the LORD is one rather than many") is the obvious surface meaning. But the Hebrew word *eḥad* can also signal *unity* (as in Genesis 2:24, "they shall become *one* flesh") rather than *unicity* — and the rabbinic + medieval Jewish-philosophical tradition has elaborated the Shema as affirming a deeper *unity* or *uniqueness* of God (Maimonides, *Mishneh Torah*, *Hilkhot Yesodei ha-Torah* 1:7 — the divine unity is *absolutely simple*, not merely numerical).

## The Abrahamic-monotheism triangle

The Shema sits at the center of a three-way cross-tradition theological-credal cluster:

| Tradition | Pithy monotheistic credal declaration |
|---|---|
| Judaism | **Shema** — "Hear, O Israel: the LORD is our God, the LORD is one" (Deut. 6:4) |
| Islam | **Shahāda + [[doctrine-tawhid|Tawḥīd]]** — *lā ilāha illā Llāh* ("there is no god but God") |
| Christianity | **Nicene Creed + [[the-trinity|Trinitarian doctrine]]** — affirms one God in three Persons (a more elaborate articulation, in part to maintain compatibility with the Shema while accommodating Christological theology) |

The three traditions affirm broadly compatible strict monotheism but differ on what theological articulations are compatible with that monotheism. Christian Trinitarian theology has maintained from the 4th-c. conciliar definitions that the Three Persons constitute *one God* in a way consistent with the Shema; Jewish + Islamic theology has held that the Trinitarian articulation crosses the line into multiplicity.
