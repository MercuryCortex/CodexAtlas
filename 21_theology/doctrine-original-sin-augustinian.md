# Original Sin (Augustinian Doctrine of Inherited Guilt)

The Western Christian doctrine that **all humanity inherits, from Adam, a fallen condition** consisting of (a) **guilt** for the original transgression and (b) a corrupted-disordered nature (concupiscence) inclining toward further sin. Formulated systematically by Augustine of Hippo (354-430) in the anti-Pelagian writings 412-430, canonized at Carthage 418, Orange II 529, and Trent 1546, intensified at the Reformation, and rejected by Eastern Orthodoxy. The doctrine is one of the principal architectural-pillars of Western Christianity and the principal Catholic-Orthodox + Catholic-Protestant doctrinal hinge.

## Augustine's articulation (412-430)

Augustine develops the doctrine in sustained polemic against **Pelagius** (~360-420) and **Julian of Eclanum** (~386-454). Pelagius taught that human beings are born morally neutral (capable of either sin or righteousness through their own free will) and that Adam's sin was an individual-moral-example with no metaphysical-ontological inheritance. Against this Augustine articulates:

1. **Adam's sin is the catastrophe of the human race** — not merely a personal failing but a cosmic-ontological event.
2. **The Fall is transmitted to all Adam's descendants** by carnal-procreative descent — Augustine identifies the **transmission mechanism with concupiscence**, the disordered sexual desire by which children are conceived. The transmission is by **descent**, not by imitation (against Pelagius).
3. **Inherited guilt** — newborns are *reatu obnoxii* (guilt-bound) from Adam, requiring baptism for remission.
4. **Inherited corruption** — the human will is bent toward sin; the *posse non peccare* of pre-Fall Adam is replaced by *non posse non peccare* (unable not to sin) in fallen humanity. Only saving grace restores the capacity for righteousness.
5. **The infant who dies unbaptized** — Augustine reluctantly concludes such an infant is condemned (the "Augustinian unease" that medieval theology resolves via *Limbo*).

The interpretive crux: **Romans 5:12 *eph' hō pantes hēmarton*** — "in whom (Adam) all sinned" (Augustine, via Vulgate's *in quo*) vs "because of which (death) all sinned" (Greek-patristic reading). The Augustinian Latin reading entails collective-juridical-imputation; the Greek reading affirms inherited mortality without inherited guilt. The exegetical-divide is the principal source of the East-West theological asymmetry.

## Conciliar canonization + Reformation intensification

- **Carthage (418)** — affirms the doctrine against Pelagianism: infants are baptized for remission of original sin (not merely as a future-precaution).
- **Orange II (529)** — anti-Semi-Pelagian; affirms that even the *desire* for faith requires preceding grace.
- **Trent (1546)** *Decretum de peccato originali* — the canonical Catholic articulation against Reformation modifications.
- **Luther** *De servo arbitrio* (1525) — intensifies into total-depravity: the bound will is unable to choose anything but sin without grace.
- **Calvin** *Institutes* II.1-3 (1559) — the Reformed canonization.
- **Westminster Confession** ch. 6 (1647).

The Reformation **intensification**: where Augustine + Aquinas treat original-sin as primarily-privative (the loss of original justice + the disordering of nature), Luther + Calvin treat it as **active-corruption**, with total-depravity entailing that humanity is unable to do any spiritual good. The intensification underwrites the Reformation soteriology of imputed-righteousness *sola fide*.

## The Eastern Orthodox rejection

Eastern Orthodoxy never adopts the Augustinian formulation. The Greek-patristic tradition (Athanasius, Cappadocians, Maximus, John Damascene) articulates a doctrine of **propatorikon hamartēma** ("ancestral sin"): humanity inherits from Adam **mortality + corruption + the inclination-to-sin**, but **not guilt**. Each person is responsible for their own sins, not for Adam's transgression.

The Eastern critique (Romanides 2002; Meyendorff 1974; Lossky 1957): the Augustinian collective-juridical-Adamic-imputation is a Latin distortion that:
- Misreads Romans 5:12 (the Greek *eph' hō* = "because of which" not the Latin *in quo* = "in whom").
- Misframes the soteric problem as legal-debt rather than ontological-corruption.
- Underwrites the Western juridical-satisfaction-atonement framework (Anselm) that the Eastern tradition rejects.

The Augustinian doctrine is structurally inseparable from the Anselmian-Reformation Western soteriological framework; the Eastern rejection of both is consistent.

## Cross-tradition positioning

**MASSIVE-WIN comparison — Indic karma**: Augustinian original-sin and Hindu/Buddhist `doctrine-karma-action-fruit` both articulate **the inheritance of pre-personal-moral-conditioning** as the underlying problem of human existence — but their mechanisms are radically asymmetric:

| Dimension | Augustinian Original Sin | Indic Karma |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Single historical event (Adam's Fall) | Beginningless accumulation across rebirths |
| Scope | Collective — all humanity shares Adam's guilt | Individual — each soul carries its own karma |
| Mechanism | Transmission by carnal descent | Continuation through stream-of-consciousness |
| Inheritability | Yes — across generations | No — strictly per-individual |
| Solution | External grace + atonement | Path + ethical purification |

The two are the **two principal world-religious solutions** to the problem of pre-volitional moral inheritance. The cross-tradition asymmetry on inheritability-of-moral-status is one of the deepest religious-anthropological divides between Abrahamic + Indic traditions. O'Flaherty 1980 + Halbfass 1991 develop the comparison.

**Parallel — Buddhist pratītya-samutpāda**: both reject the autonomous-rational-agent self-understanding; both articulate the **conditioning-of-the-present-by-prior-causes** as central. The asymmetry: original-sin = single-historical-Fall + collective-inheritance; pratītya-samutpāda = beginningless-chain operative in every moment. See `doctrine-pratitya-samutpada-dependent-origination`.

**Presupposed by — Anselmian satisfaction-atonement**: see [[doctrine-atonement-substitutionary-anselmian]]. The Anselmian infinite-debt-of-honor presupposes the Augustinian inherited-guilt. The Western Original-Sin → Satisfaction-Atonement linkage is one of the principal architectural-axes of medieval Catholic + Reformation soteriology.

**Polemic-against — Eastern Orthodox propatorikon hamartēma**: see [[doctrine-theosis-divinization-orthodox]]. The Eastern tradition's rejection of the Augustinian inheritance-of-guilt structure is integral to its rejection of the entire Western juridical-soteriological framework.

## Disputes

- **Pelagian controversy** — the foundational early-medieval debate.
- **Romans 5:12** — the Augustinian *in quo* vs Greek-patristic *eph' hō* exegetical-divide.
- **Total depravity** — Augustinian + Reformed vs Catholic-Aquinas-modified-form vs Eastern-rejection.
- **Immaculate conception** — Catholic 1854 dogma that Mary was preserved from original sin; Eastern Orthodox rejects as nonsensical within their framework where guilt isn't inherited anyway.
- **Modern Catholic ressourcement** — Rahner, Schoonenberg, Dulles reformulate original sin in terms of "sin of the world" (collective socio-historical sin) rather than Adamic-imputation.
- **Evolutionary critique** — given the absence of a historical-individual Adam in modern paleoanthropology, the doctrine's literal-Adamic basis is contested even in conservative theology.
