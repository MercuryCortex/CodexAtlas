# Chinvat Bridge (Zoroastrian Doctrine of Post-Mortem Judgment-by-Crossing)

The Zoroastrian doctrine of the **Chinvat Bridge** (Avestan *Činvatō Pərətu*, "Bridge of the Separator"): the **cosmic-bridge** that every soul must cross at the third day after death. The bridge **widens for the righteous** (becoming a broad, easy passage to the House of Song / Zoroastrian paradise) + **narrows for the wicked** (becoming as narrow as a razor's edge, from which the wicked soul falls into the House of Lies / hell-zone below). The bridge is presided over by **three judges** — Mithra + Sraosha + Rashnu — and the soul's first encounter at the bridge is the dramatic **[[doctrine-daena-zoroastrian|daēnā]] vision**.

## The Gathic core

The Chinvat Bridge doctrine appears already in the Gathas (-1200 to -1000):

- **Yasna 46.10-11**: "The soul of the righteous and the believer in the truth shall cross the Chinvat Bridge... but the soul of the wicked shall be barred from the bridge."
- **Yasna 51.13**: the Chinvat-Bridge as the **cosmic-axis at which the righteous + wicked are sorted**.

The Gathic stratum thus already contains the essential structure: bridge + judgment + sorting-by-righteousness.

## The developed cosmography (Younger Avesta + Pahlavi)

The Younger Avesta (Vendidad 19; Hādōxt Nask) + the Pahlavi tradition (Bundahishn 30; Ardā Vīrāf Nāmag) elaborate the full topographical-narrative cosmography:

1. **The 3-day post-mortem period**: the soul remains at the body for the first three nights, recapitulating its life-content.
2. **The dawn of the fourth day**: the soul approaches the Chinvat Bridge.
3. **The daēnā encounter**: the soul meets the personified-vision of its own lived life — beautiful maiden for the righteous, hideous hag for the wicked.
4. **The three judges**: [[mithra-zoroastrian|Mithra]] (cosmic-contract overseer), [[sraosha|Sraosha]] (divine-word hearkener), [[rashnu-zoroastrian|Rashnu]] (weigher of good vs. evil deeds on a golden balance).
5. **The crossing**: the righteous soul finds the bridge broad + crosses to the House of Song (paradise); the wicked soul finds the bridge narrowed + falls into the House of Lies (hell-zone).
6. **Permanent post-mortem state** until the cosmic renovation, when bodily resurrection + the cosmic-Frashokereti restore the cosmos.

## MASSIVE-WIN: Chinvat Bridge → Islamic Sirat

The Islamic ***Ṣirāṭ*** bridge — the cosmic-bridge across hell, **sharp as a sword + thin as a hair**, that all the dead must cross at the Day of Judgment — is **structurally + functionally parallel** to the Zoroastrian Chinvat Bridge:

- **Both are post-mortem cosmic-bridges** across the abyss between this world + the next.
- **Both narrow for the wicked + widen for the righteous**.
- **Both involve judgment-at-the-bridge** (the Islamic *Ṣirāṭ* is preceded by the *Mizan*, the cosmic balance).
- **Both are central to the eschatological-judgment cosmography**.

The Persian-Islamic cultural-contact window (~7th-10th c. CE — the centuries of majority-Zoroastrian Iran under Arab + Abbasid rule) makes **substantial Iranian-influence on Islamic Sirat doctrine** the standard scholarly position (Asín Palacios 1919; Boyce 1991 vol. 3; Russell 1987 *Zoroastrianism in Armenia*).

The Chinvat → Sirat inheritance is **one of the cleanest documented Iranian-to-Islamic eschatological structural-inheritance lines**.

## MASSIVE-WIN: Chinvat → medieval Christian otherworld-vision → Dante

Through the **Ardā Vīrāf Nāmag** (Pahlavi otherworld-vision, ~6th-9th c.) + the Islamic **Mi'rāj** literature, the Chinvat-Bridge-template propagates into medieval Christian otherworld-vision literature (Bede's Drythelm vision; Saint Patrick's Purgatory; Visio Tnugdali) and ultimately into **Dante's *Divina Commedia*** (1320 CE). The cross-civilization bridge-of-judgment template thus has a documented inheritance chain spanning **2500 years + four major religious traditions** (Vahman 1986; Asín Palacios 1919).
