# Rada vs Petwo — Vodou's Two Great Lwa Families

## What this is
The two great branches of the Haitian Vodou lwa pantheon — **Rada** (the "cool" lwa, Dahomey-Fon derived, ancestral and stabilizing) and **Petwo** (the "hot" lwa, Kongo-Bantu derived plus Saint-Domingue-creole emergents, fierce and revolutionary). The distinction is structural: every Vodou ceremony begins with Rada protocols and may transition to Petwo, with distinct drumming patterns, songs, dance-styles, and offerings for each family. Métraux (1959) is the foundational mid-20th-c reference; Desmangles (1992) develops the structural analysis; Brown (1991) provides contemporary diaspora ethnography.

## The two families

### Rada (cool, ancestral)
| Lwa | Domain |
|---|---|
| [[damballa]] | Primordial creator, sky-serpent |
| [[aida-wedo]] | Rainbow-serpent consort |
| Loko | Tree-of-vodou, plant-knowledge |
| Ayizan | Marketplace, initiation |
| [[papa-legba]] | Crossroads opener |
| Agwé | Sea (some classifications) |
| Erzulie Freda | Love, beauty, longing |
| Ogou Badagri (some) | Cool ogou variants |

**Characteristics:** Dahomey-Fon origin; "cool" temperament; white-and-pastel iconography; offerings of egg, white flour, water, perfume; sweet drinks; opens every Vodou ceremony.

### Petwo (hot, revolutionary)
| Lwa | Domain |
|---|---|
| Petwo lwa | Kongo-derived water-spirits |
| [[simbi-kongo]] / Simbi d'l'eau | Water-mediator spirits |
| Erzulie Dantor | Fierce protective mother; revolutionary |
| Baron Lakwa / Bawon Petwo | Death + violent transition |
| Ti-Jean Petwo | Petwo wild-man |
| Kalfou Petwo | Crossroads of the Petwo "hot" mode |
| Marinette | Fierce female lwa of the revolution |
| Bossou | Three-horned bull-lwa |

**Characteristics:** Kongo-Bantu plus Saint-Domingue-creole emergent; "hot" temperament; red-black iconography; offerings of rum, gunpowder, pepper, blood; aggressive percussive drumming; associated with the Haitian Revolution and resistance.

## MASSIVE WIN — The Petwo as Atlantic resistance theology
A defining argument in Vodou scholarship (Métraux 1959; Desmangles 1992; Hurbon 1995; Brown 1991): the Petwo branch is not merely a "wild lwa family" but specifically a Saint-Domingue-creole theological emergence — the lwa-family that emerged from enslaved African resistance against French colonial slavery. The 1791 Bois Caïman ceremony that launched the Haitian Revolution was a Petwo ceremony (Boukman the houngan presided); the revolutionary lwa Erzulie Dantor's scarred face is iconographically linked to Maroon women warriors of Saint-Domingue. Desmangles (1992) and Hurbon (*Voodoo: Search for the Spirit* 1995) make the argument explicit: Petwo is the theological-ritual form that the resistance took, the cosmological infrastructure of revolution.

This makes Vodou one of the few documented cases of a religious tradition with a structurally-anti-imperial sub-pantheon — a specific family of deities developed historically AS revolutionary theology, not retrofitted as such. The continuing political-religious significance is documented through the 20th-c Duvalier dictatorship's manipulation of Vodou and through contemporary Haitian-political-religious-praxis discussions.

## Ritual structure
A Vodou ceremony typically proceeds:
1. Opening with [[papa-legba]] (always first, regardless of family)
2. The Rada section — invocation of the cool lwa, often Damballa-Aida-Wedo, Loko, Ayizan, the Marasa (sacred twins, who bridge Rada and Petwo)
3. Transition — sometimes through the Marasa, sometimes a percussive break
4. The Petwo section — invocation of the hot lwa
5. Closing protocols

The drumming patterns differ between Rada and Petwo (Rada drums are slower, three-beat; Petwo drums are faster, two-beat with specific syncopation); the songs differ in language (Rada songs preserve more Fon language; Petwo songs include more Kongo language and Saint-Domingue Creole); the dance-styles differ in tempo and intensity.

## The Marasa-bridge
The Marasa (sacred twins; see [[marassa-twins]]) bridge Rada and Petwo. They are invoked in both sections and stand as the structural-mediator between the cool and hot families. The Marasa thus play a function analogous to Èṣù-as-mediator in Yoruba religion but at the Vodou-family-level rather than at the orisha-human-level.

## Cross-tradition parallels
The cool-vs-hot lwa-family structure is distinctive to Vodou and not directly paralleled in other major traditions. Loose structural parallels include:
- The Hindu *sāttvik* / *rājasik* / *tāmasik* (light / passionate / dark) guna classification of deities and foods.
- The Japanese Shinto distinction between *kami* of natural ancestral peace and the *aragami* (wild/violent kami).
- The Greek *Olympian* / *chthonic* divisions (sky-gods vs underworld-gods).

None of these is a direct match — Vodou's Rada/Petwo is HISTORICALLY produced from the Dahomey-Kongo substrate intersection plus Saint-Domingue creole emergence, not a structural division of pre-existing pantheon-elements.
