# Cataphatic Theology / Via Positiva

The **affirmative method** of systematic theology: predicating qualities of God on the basis of scripture, tradition, and the analogy of creature-to-Creator. Greek *kataphasis* = "affirming, asserting"; Latin *via positiva* / *via affirmativa* = "way of affirmation." Cataphatic theology is the necessary **affirmative phase** of all confessional theology — to say "God is good," "God is wise," "God is just" is to operate cataphatically.

The dyad **cataphatic-apophatic** is the principal methodological structure of Christian-Mediterranean systematic theology. The mature theological tradition holds both in tension: cataphatic affirms what can be said (with appropriate analogical-qualification); apophatic denies that any affirmation captures the divine essence (which is beyond all predicate). Pseudo-Dionysius's companion-treatises *Divine Names* (cataphatic) + *Mystical Theology* (apophatic) provide the canonical articulation.

## The Pseudo-Dionysian framework

Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite (~500) provides the foundational Christian framework. The three-phase ascent:

1. **Cataphatic phase** — predicate the divine names drawn from scripture + the analogy of creation. "God is good," "God is light," "God is love." Each predicate communicates something true *analogically* but inadequate *modally*.
2. **Apophatic phase** — negate each affirmation, recognizing that no finite predicate captures the divine essence. "God is not good *in the way creatures are good*" — the negation purifies the affirmation.
3. **Hyper-apophatic phase** — transcend both affirmation and negation. God as *hyper-good*, *hyper-being*, *hyper-divinity* — beyond the very dyad.

The *Divine Names* explores the cataphatic phase; the *Mystical Theology* the apophatic-and-hyper-apophatic.

## The Thomistic analogia entis

Aquinas's *Summa Theologiae* I q. 13 provides the Scholastic systematization. The problem: how can finite-human concepts predicate truly of an infinite-simple God?

**Aquinas's solution**: predication is **analogical**, not univocal (the same meaning of "good" applied to God and creature) and not equivocal (entirely different meanings). The *analogia entis* (analogy of being) holds that creatures bear a participatory-similarity to their Creator-source; therefore words derived from creatures can be predicated analogically of God. "God is good" affirms a real but analogical similarity — not the same kind of goodness as creatures', but a goodness of which creature-goodness is the participatory-effect.

The Thomistic analogia-entis is the canonical Catholic articulation of cataphatic-theology. Karl Barth's 20th-c. neo-orthodox theology famously rejects the *analogia entis* in favor of an *analogia fidei* (analogy of faith) — God-known-only-through-revelation, not through ontological-similarity — while remaining cataphatic in massive-content (*Church Dogmatics*).

## Cross-tradition positioning

**Companion-dyad with apophatic**: see `doctrine-apophatic-via-negativa`. The cataphatic-apophatic dyad operates across all three Abrahamic systematic theologies. The integration is most-canonical in Pseudo-Dionysius (Christian), Maimonides (Jewish), and al-Ghazālī + Ibn ʿArabī (Islamic).

**Method of Trinitarian doctrine**: see [[doctrine-trinity-nicene]]. Nicene Trinitarianism uses cataphatic resources for the positive articulation of three-hypostases-one-essence while integrating apophatic resources for the unknowability of the divine essence. The Cappadocian achievement is the integration.

**Method of tawḥīd**: see [[doctrine-tawhid]]. The 99 Beautiful Names of God (*al-Asmāʾ al-Ḥusnā*) — al-Raḥmān, al-Raḥīm, al-Mālik, al-Quddūs, etc. — are the canonical Islamic cataphatic predications. The Ashʿarī-Mutazilī kalām debate on whether divine attributes are real (Ashʿarī) or merely conceptual (Mutazila) is internally cataphatic-apophatic.

**Method of theōsis**: see `doctrine-theosis-divinization-orthodox`. Gregory Palamas's essence/energies distinction is the mature integration — essence is apophatic (unknowable), energies are cataphatic (participable). The dyad enables theōsis.

**Contrast with strict apophatic radicals**: Madhyamaka Buddhist śūnyavāda + radical Christian apophatic (Eckhart, Marguerite Porete) approach a *collapse* of cataphatic into apophatic — the affirmative-phase is provisional, the negative is fundamental. Mainstream Christian + Islamic + Jewish theology preserves the dyad.
