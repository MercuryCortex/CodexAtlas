# Moral Influence / Exemplarist Atonement (Abelardian)

The **subjective** (Aulén's third type) theory of how Christ's death accomplishes salvation: the cross does not pay an objective debt or defeat cosmic powers — it **reveals divine love so intensely that it kindles answering love in the human heart**, and that transformative love-response *is* the redemption. The model is formulated by **[peter-abelard]** in his *Commentary on Romans* (~1140) against both the prevailing patristic ransom-to-the-devil model and the new Anselmian satisfaction-theory.

## Abelard's formulation
The principal text is Abelard's *quaestio* on Romans 3:19-26 in the *Commentary on Romans*:

> "Now it seems to us that we have been justified by the blood of Christ and reconciled to God in this way: through this unique act of grace manifested to us — in that his Son has taken upon himself our nature and persevered therein in teaching us by word and example, even unto death — he has more fully bound us to himself by love; with the result that our hearts should be enkindled by such a gift of divine grace, and true charity should not now shrink from enduring anything for him."

The mechanism is **pedagogical-revelatory**: Christ's incarnation, life, and death **display** the depth of divine love; the *display* itself is salvific by kindling the answering love that transforms the human soul. There is no juridical-transaction, no defeated-cosmic-adversary, no debt-paid. The transaction is entirely between the divine self-revelation and the human heart's response.

## What Abelard rejects

The two-front polemic:

1. **Against the ransom-to-the-devil** (patristic + early medieval): the devil has no *rights* over fallen humanity. To imagine God paying the devil is to imagine God-vassal-to-Satan — incoherent.
2. **Against Anselmian satisfaction**: "How cruel and wicked it seems, that anyone should demand the blood of an innocent person as the price for anything, or that it should in any way please him that an innocent person should be slain — still less that God should consider the death of his Son so agreeable that by it he should be reconciled to the whole world!" The satisfaction-logic, Abelard argues, slanders the divine character.

## Condemnation at Sens (1141) and suppression

Bernard of Clairvaux, the great Cistercian polemicist, brings 19 charges against Abelard at the Council of Sens (1141), several touching the atonement-doctrine. Pope Innocent II condemns Abelard's books; Abelard retires to Cluny under Peter the Venerable and dies the next year. The moral-influence reading is suppressed in 12th-13th c. Scholastic theology — Aquinas treats it briefly and dismissively in *ST* III.46.

## Recovery: Socinians → Schleiermacher → Rashdall

The lineage of moral-influence atonement runs through the recurring **anti-substitutionary minority traditions** of Western Christianity:

- **Faustus Socinus** *De Jesu Christo Servatore* (1594) — the Polish-Italian Socinian Brethren reject satisfaction-theory on biblical-philological + rational grounds; Christ saves by *teaching and example*. The Socinian polemic is the principal early-modern recovery.
- **Schleiermacher** *Glaubenslehre* (1830) §100-105 — Christ's *perfect God-consciousness* communicates itself to believers and gradually transforms their consciousness. The 19th-c. Romantic-liberal-Protestant transposition.
- **Horace Bushnell** *The Vicarious Sacrifice* (1866) — Christ's "sympathetic suffering" reveals divine love and inspires moral transformation. American liberal-Protestant systematization.
- **Hastings Rashdall** *The Idea of Atonement in Christian Theology* (1919) — the canonical 20th-c. defense. The Bampton Lectures argue that moral-influence is *the* authentic NT teaching against the Anselmian distortion.

20th-c. **liberal Protestantism** generally inherits the moral-influence reading; mid-20th-c. neo-orthodoxy (Barth, Brunner) restores objective atonement-categories but generally without returning to Anselmian satisfaction.

## Cross-tradition positioning

**Structural parallel — Hindu bhakti**: the Abelardian salvation-by-love-response has its closest non-Christian analogue in Hindu *bhakti* — devotional love-response to the divine's self-disclosure as the path of liberation. [bhagavad-gita] ch. 12 (Bhakti Yoga), Rāmānuja's [[doctrine-vishishtadvaita-qualified-non-dualism]], and the medieval bhakti-poets (Mīrābāī, Tukārām, Caitanya) all articulate a salvation-by-love-kindled-by-divine-self-revelation that is structurally homologous to Abelard. The mechanism — transformation-by-love-response-to-divine-self-disclosure — is one of the deepest cross-tradition soteriological structures.

**Asymmetry with Sufi [doctrine-fana-sufi-annihilation]**: Sufi *fanā* also involves love-driven self-transformation but proceeds through self-annihilation rather than love-kindled-response. The Sufi structure is closer to apophatic-mystical-union; Abelardian moral-influence is closer to bhakti-affective-transformation.

**vs. Buddhist [doctrine-nirvana-buddhist-cessation]**: no analogue. Buddhism's path is ethical-meditative-cognitive transformation toward cessation, not love-response to a self-revealing-deity.

## Critiques

- **Insufficient for sin's gravity** (Anselmian + Reformed critique): moral-influence reduces redemption to pedagogy and underestimates the *objective* alienation of sin from God.
- **Hides the cross's necessity**: if Christ saves by revealing love, why does He need to *die*? Why not just teach? The death becomes accidental rather than essential.
- **Pelagian tendency**: if salvation operates through human love-response, the locus of saving action drifts toward human capacity, edging toward Pelagianism (a charge as old as Bernard's at Sens).
- **Loses cosmic dimension**: the Christus-Victor framing includes a cosmic-historical drama; the moral-influence model becomes inwardly-individualistic.
