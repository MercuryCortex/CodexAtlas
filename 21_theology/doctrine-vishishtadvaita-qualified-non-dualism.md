# Viśiṣṭādvaita (Qualified Non-Dualism)

**Viśiṣṭādvaita** is the qualified non-dualist (*viśiṣṭa-advaita* = "non-dual with internal distinctions") systematic theology of Brahmanical Vaiṣṇavism — the doctrinal position that Brahman is non-dual (one) but internally articulated, with souls (*cit*) and matter (*acit*) constituting Brahman's *body* (*śarīra*) and Brahman the *embodied one* (*śarīrin*) and *inner controller* (*antaryāmin*) of both. It is the foundational theology of the Tamil Śrī-Vaiṣṇava sampradāya, systematised by [[ramanuja|Rāmānuja]] (c. 1017–1137 CE) on a base of Upaniṣadic + Brahma-Sūtra + Bhagavad-Gītā exegesis read through the devotional substrate of the 6th-9th-c. Tamil Āḻvār poet-saints.

## The doctrinal core

Six linked propositions constitute the canonical Viśiṣṭādvaitin position:

1. **Brahman is *saguṇa*** (with qualities) — Rāmānuja rejects Śaṅkara's *nirguṇa* Brahman as theologically incoherent (a thing without any predicates cannot be the object of revelation, the source of grace, or the goal of devotion). The qualities of Brahman are real, eternal, infinite (the *kalyāṇa-guṇas* — auspicious attributes: knowledge, power, lordship, strength, valour, splendour, etc.). Brahman is identified with Viṣṇu-Nārāyaṇa.
2. **The world and souls are real** — neither *māyā* nor *avidyā* in Śaṅkara's sense; they are real internal articulations of the one Brahman, sustained as Brahman's body.
3. **The body-soul analogy** (*śarīra-śarīrin bhāva*) is the structuring doctrinal image. As the human body is inseparable from the soul yet distinct from it, so the world (matter + souls) is inseparable from Brahman (*aprthak-siddhi* — inseparably established) yet not identical with Brahman. This is the *qualified* part of qualified non-dualism: the one Brahman is internally articulated by qualifications (*viśeṣaṇa*) that are real but non-separable.
4. **Brahman is *antaryāmin*** — the inner controller of all souls and all matter (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 3.7's *antaryāmin-brāhmaṇa* is foundational scripture). This is the panentheistic structure: the world is in Brahman as body in soul.
5. **Liberation is loving devotion + grace** — *mokṣa* is not non-difference-recognition (Advaita) but eternal loving union with Brahman in *Vaikuṇṭha*, achieved through *bhakti* (devotional love) and ultimately *prasāda* (divine grace). The *jīva* in liberation retains its individuality.
6. **The Tamil Āḻvār corpus is *drāviḍa-veda*** (Tamil Veda) — equal scriptural authority with the Sanskrit Upaniṣads. This is a structural elevation of vernacular devotional poetry to canonical status, unique to Śrī-Vaiṣṇavism in medieval Hinduism.

## The Śrī-Bhāṣya's anti-Advaita polemic

The *Śrī-Bhāṣya* opens with the *Mahā-pūrva-pakṣa* — the "Great Prior Objection" — seven systematic objections to Śaṅkara's *avidyā* doctrine, the most famous of which is the locus-of-ignorance dilemma: avidyā cannot inhere in Brahman (Brahman is by Śaṅkara's own admission knowledge itself, so cannot be the locus of ignorance) and cannot inhere in the *jīva* (the jīva itself is constituted by avidyā in Śaṅkara's system, so a regress threatens). The *anupapatti-saptaka* has been the touchstone of post-12th-c. Advaita-Viśiṣṭādvaita polemics; Madhusūdana Sarasvatī's *Advaita-siddhi* is the classical Advaitin response.

## The sub-schools

Post-13th-c. Śrī-Vaiṣṇavism split into the **Vaḍakalai** ("northern", Sanskrit-leaning, Vedānta Deśika) and the **Teṅkalai** ("southern", Tamil-leaning, Piḷḷai Lokācārya + Maṇavāḷa Māmuni) sub-schools, divided most sharply on the *bhakti* vs *prapatti* question (effortful devotion vs. total self-surrender to grace), the "monkey vs cat" analogy (the baby monkey clings actively; the kitten is carried passively). The dispute is the structural Hindu parallel to the medieval Christian grace-and-works dispute and the medieval Buddhist self-power vs other-power (*jiriki* vs *tariki*) dispute.

## Cross-tradition resonances

- **Process / panentheistic theology.** Hartshorne 1953 explicitly identifies Viśiṣṭādvaita as the cleanest pre-modern systematic panentheism — the body-of-God + soul-as-body analogy + the qualified one-and-many structure parallel his dipolar theism. Process theologians Cobb, Griffin, and McFague have engaged Rāmānuja for the same reason.
- **Christian incarnational theology.** The body-soul Christology of Cyril of Alexandria and the hypostatic-union doctrine of Chalcedon (one *prosōpon* with two natures) share structural moves with Rāmānuja's *aprthak-siddhi* of jīva-in-Brahman, though Carman 1974 carefully notes the differences (Christian incarnation is a particular historical event; Brahman's embodiment is the universal cosmological structure).
- **Sufi waḥdat al-shuhūd.** Sirhindī's 17th-c. Naqshbandī articulation of "oneness of witness" (vs. Ibn ʿArabī's *waḥdat al-wujūd* = "oneness of being") is the closest Islamic structural parallel — a one-with-internal-distinctions position against a more thoroughgoing identity-thesis.
