# Maya Religion

## Overview
The religious traditions of the Maya peoples — pan-Maya in some structural respects (calendar, key deities, cosmological framework) and regionally-specific in others (Yucatec, K'iche', Tzotzil, Lacandon, and many other Maya nation-traditions). Maya religion is one of the longest-continuous religious traditions in the Americas, with continuity from Preclassic Maya (~1000 BCE) through Classic (~250–900 CE), Postclassic (~900–1500), colonial (~1500–1810), and contemporary (Maya religious revitalization in Guatemala, Mexico). Distinctive features: extremely elaborate dual calendar (tzolk'in 260-day + haab 365-day + Long Count); hieroglyphic writing (deciphered substantially in 20th c., Knorozov breakthrough 1952); deep cosmogony preserved in the Popol Vuh; itzam / cosmic-caiman framing of the cosmos.

## Historical phases
1. **Preclassic (~1000 BCE–250 CE)** — emergence of Maya religious-iconographic complex; San Bartolo murals (~100 BCE) show Hero Twins and Maize God iconography.
2. **Classic (250–900 CE)** — height of Maya civilization; Tikal, Palenque, Copán, Calakmul; extensive monumental religious-political inscriptions.
3. **Postclassic (900–1500 CE)** — Chichén Itzá, Mayapán; Toltec-influenced Yucatec religious-cultural synthesis; Quetzalcoatl-Kukulkán imported.
4. **Spanish conquest (~1527–1546 in Yucatán; longer in highlands)** — destruction of codices (Maní auto-da-fé 1562); imposition of Catholicism.
5. **Post-conquest preservation (~1554 onward)** — Popol Vuh transcribed (~1554); Books of Chilam Balam (continuous tradition into 19th c.).
6. **Contemporary (20th–21st c.)** — Guatemalan Maya religious-cultural revitalization; reincorporation of pre-conquest religious elements.

## Core texts
- [[phase-8-001-popol-vuh]] — K'iche' Maya cosmogonic-dynastic narrative (~1554, drawing on pre-Columbian tradition).
- [[phase-8-003-dresden-codex-maya]] — pre-Columbian astronomical-divinatory codex.
- [[phase-8-005-chilam-balam]] — Yucatec Maya post-conquest religious-prophetic compilation.
- Madrid Codex; Paris Codex; possibly Grolier/Mexico Maya Codex.
- Classic-period hieroglyphic inscriptions (Palenque Group of the Cross, etc.).

## Pantheon
- [[itzamna]] — supreme creator-god (especially Yucatec)
- [[kukulkan]] — Feathered Serpent (Yucatec) / Q'uq'umatz (K'iche')
- [[ixchel]] — moon, childbirth, weaving
- [[chac]] — rain (with the four directional Chaacs)
- Hunahpu and Xbalanque — the Hero Twins
- Maize God; God L (merchant-underworld); God D (Itzamna); many others

## Defining doctrines
- Cyclical cosmogony — multiple creations
- Tzolk'in / haab dual calendar; Long Count
- Hero Twins narrative as cosmogonic-paradigmatic
- Cosmic-tree axis mundi
- Xibalba (the underworld) and the trials therein
- Bloodletting as religious-political technology (Classic period; the *k'uhul ajaw* "holy lord" performed self-bloodletting publicly)

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-aztec-mexica]]: pan-Mesoamerican substrate (calendar, Feathered Serpent, rain-god); separate ethnic-political traditions with intense ~9th–10th c. contact at Chichén Itzá.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: post-1546 forced Christianization; significant syncretic forms persist (Maximón / San Simón cult in Guatemalan highlands).
