# Polynesian Religious Traditions

## Overview
The religious traditions of Polynesian peoples across the Polynesian Triangle (Hawai'i in the north, Aotearoa / New Zealand in the southwest, Rapa Nui / Easter Island in the southeast). Polynesian religion descends from an ancestral Polynesian religious system (linguistically reconstructable: Ancestral Polynesian Society, ~1000 BCE in Tonga / Samoa) elaborated through ~2,000 years of voyaging-settlement-divergence across the Pacific. Each Polynesian society has its own religious tradition (Hawaiian, Maori, Tahitian, Samoan, Tongan, etc.); shared cognate features include: the four principal *atua* (Tāne/Kāne, Tū/Kū, Rongo/Lono, Tangaroa/Kanaloa, with variable cosmic ranking); *tapu* / *kapu* (sacred-prohibition) as the regulative religious-social category; *mana* as religious-political power; genealogical (*whakapapa* / *moʻokūʻauhau*) cosmogony.

## Historical phases
1. **Ancestral Polynesian Society (~1000 BCE+)** — formation in the Lapita-cultural-complex zone (Tonga / Samoa).
2. **Voyaging-settlement period (~1000 BCE–1300 CE)** — Polynesian voyaging expands to Tahiti / Marquesas (~200 BCE), Hawai'i (~400 CE? — re-dated continuously), Aotearoa (~1280 CE).
3. **Pre-contact florescence (~1300–1800)** — divergent traditional elaboration; complex chiefly societies; Tahitian, Hawaiian, Maori, Samoan, Tongan traditions develop distinctive features.
4. **European contact (~1769–)** — Cook voyages; missionary period from ~1797 (LMS in Tahiti); rapid Christianization across Polynesia in the 19th c.
5. **20th- and 21st-c. revitalization** — Hawaiian cultural-political revitalization (Hawaiian sovereignty movements); Māori Renaissance; pan-Polynesian voyaging tradition revitalization (Hōkūleʻa from 1976).

## Pantheon
- Tāne (Maori) / Kāne (Hawaiian) — forests / creation / life
- Tū (Maori) / Kū (Hawaiian) — war / fierce action
- Rongo (Maori) / Lono (Hawaiian) — peace / agriculture
- Tangaroa (Maori) / Kanaloa (Hawaiian) / Ta'aroa (Tahitian) — sea / supreme-creator (variably)
- Hina / Sina — pan-Polynesian female figure
- Region-specific: [[pele]] (Hawaiian volcano); Maui (trickster culture-hero across Polynesia)

## Defining doctrines
- *Tapu* / *kapu* (sacred-prohibition) regulates ritual life
- *Mana* — religious-political power
- *Whakapapa* / *moʻokūʻauhau* (genealogy) as cosmological organization
- Voyaging cosmography — navigational-religious knowledge

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-australian-aboriginal]]: separate continental traditions; parallel "Indigenous Pacific" status in pan-Indigenous Pacific solidarity, but religious-cultural distinct.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: 19th-c. Christianization extensive; modern Polynesian Christianity is genuine but variably syncretic with pre-Christian elements.
