# Gnosticism

## Overview
A family of related religious movements in the second through fourth centuries CE, mostly Christian but with non-Christian variants, sharing several broad features: cosmic dualism (a transcendent unknowable God vs. an ignorant or malevolent demiurge who created the material world); a soteriology of *gnosis* (salvific knowledge) rather than faith or works; a mythology featuring divine emanations from the unknowable Father (the Pleroma of aeons), the tragic fall of an aeon (Sophia), and the descent of a redeemer to awaken divine sparks trapped in matter; ascetic ethics rejecting the demiurgic cosmos. Williams 1996 and King 2003 have argued the very category "Gnosticism" is a heresiological construction that obscures the diversity of the movements; current scholarship uses sub-categories (Sethian, Valentinian, Thomasine, Basilidean) more carefully.

## Historical phases
1. **Early second century** — Basilides in Alexandria, Saturninus in Antioch; earliest named teachers.
2. **Mid second century** — Valentinus' Roman teaching (~136–155); the Valentinian school proliferates.
3. **Late second / early third century** — Sethian texts (Apocryphon of John, Hypostasis of the Archons) take their developed form; Irenaeus, Tertullian, Hippolytus, and Clement compose major heresiologies.
4. **Third–fourth century** — late synthetic texts (On the Origin of the World, Pistis Sophia); progressive suppression under Christian imperial authority.
5. **Disappearance** — by the late fourth century the Gnostic communities had been largely suppressed; the Nag Hammadi codices were buried ~370.
6. **Modern recovery** — Nag Hammadi 1945; Pistis Sophia (Askew Codex) 1785; Berlin Codex 1896.

## Core texts
See key-documents above; supplemented by external testimony in [[phase-4-023-irenaeus-against-heresies]], Hippolytus *Refutatio*, Epiphanius *Panarion*, and Clement of Alexandria.

## Defining doctrines
- The unknowable Father / Monad / Bythos behind all hypostatic emanations.
- The Pleroma of aeons.
- Sophia's fall and the consequent existence of the material cosmos.
- The demiurge (Yaldabaoth in Sethian; identified with the OT creator in Sethian polemic; reframed as psychic / unwitting in Valentinian).
- Gnosis as the awakening of the divine spark in humans.
- A redeemer descending from the Pleroma to teach gnosis.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: shared scriptures (variously); contested over the identity of the creator-God, the nature of Jesus' body, the role of the Hebrew Bible.
- vs. [[tradition-hermeticism]]: shared apophatic theology, emanationism, gnosis-soteriology, divine-spark anthropology; non-Christian register.
- vs. [[tradition-manichaeism]]: shared cosmic dualism but different metaphysical structure (Mani's two co-eternal principles vs. Sophia's fall from one Father).
- vs. [[tradition-neoplatonism]]: Plotinus' Ennead II.9 explicitly polemicizes against Gnostics he names whose texts we now have at Nag Hammadi.

## Medieval and modern continuation / reception
- **Medieval dualist continuation (10th–14th c.):** [[tradition-bogomilism|Bogomilism]] (Bulgaria/Byzantium, ~940+) and [[tradition-catharism|Catharism]] (Northern Italy and Languedoc, ~1100–1330) preserve structurally Gnostic features (demiurge, fallen-spark anthropology, ascetic perfecti-credentes distinction). Whether this is a direct historical-transmission line (Stoyanov 2000, Obolensky 1948) or independent re-emergence under shared theological pressures (Moore 2012, Pegg 2008) is the central debate. The Cathar suppression at the [[event-albigensian-crusade-1209-1229|Albigensian Crusade]] ends the medieval dualist visible institutional presence in the Latin West.
- **[[tradition-mandaeism|Mandaeism]] (continuous, ~100 CE to present):** the only continuously-surviving Gnostic religion, in southern Iraq / Iran / diaspora.
- **18th–21st-c. esoteric reception:** the [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis]] constructs (post-1750) a Templar-mediated transmission of Gnostic doctrine into Western European esotericism. **Historically unrecoverable** but extremely productive as reception. Reinforced by the 1773 recovery of Ge'ez 1 Enoch, the 1785 publication of the Askew Codex (Pistis Sophia), the 1896 Berlin Codex, and the 1945 Nag Hammadi discovery, which together restore the actual textual evidence of Gnostic Christianity to scholarly and esoteric attention.

## MASSIVE WIN: Gnosticism as the mirror of canonical religion

The primary intellectual achievement of Gnosticism, from the perspective of Codex Atlas's investigation goals, is that it **makes the canonical tradition legible by inversion**. Every major Christian-canonical claim has a Gnostic counter-claim that reveals what is being asserted:

| Canonical claim | Gnostic counter | What the polemic reveals |
|---|---|---|
| God created the world and it was "very good" (Gen 1:31) | The creator is ignorant; the world is a prison | At issue: is the material cosmos good or fallen? |
| "I am God and there is no other" (Isa 45:5) | Yaldabaoth's claim; proves his ignorance of the true God | At issue: is biblical monotheism ultimate, or is the biblical God a subordinate being? |
| Salvation by faith in Christ's resurrection | Salvation by gnosis — knowledge of one's divine origin | At issue: is human nature fallen (needing rescue) or divine (needing awakening)? |
| Jesus "came in the flesh" (1 John 4:2) | Christ appeared to take a body but was purely spiritual (docetism) | At issue: is matter redeemable or simply escaped? |
| The Hebrew Bible is God's revelation | The Hebrew Bible is the demiurge's propaganda | At issue: is Israel's covenant with the true God or a regional archon? |

**This mirroring is the MASSIVE WIN:** Gnosticism is the best evidence we have of what early Christianity looked like from the *outside* of orthodoxy-in-formation. The heresiological polemics of Irenaeus, Tertullian, and Hippolytus — written to combat Gnosticism — preserve more detail about Gnostic theology than most Gnostic primary texts that survived. The enemy's portrait is evidence.

## The Isaiah 45:5 polemic — the sharpest edge

The Sethian re-reading of Isaiah 45:5 ("I am God and there is no other" → placed in [[yaldabaoth]]'s mouth as proof of ignorance) is the single most consequential textual operation in ancient religion. It transforms the foundation of Jewish-Christian monotheism — the Deutero-Isaianic declaration of divine uniqueness — into evidence for the creator's metaphysical blindness. Every tradition that came after had to respond to this reading:

- **Orthodox Christianity** (Irenaeus, *Adversus Haereses*): the demiurge of the Gnostics is the same as the Father of Christ; the creator and redeemer are not different gods
- **Neoplatonism** (Plotinus, *Ennead* II.9): criticized the Gnostics for their arrogance in despising the material cosmos and for making the creator malevolent
- **Manichaeism**: accepted a version of the demiurge-theology (the material world is created by the Prince of Darkness) but with two eternal principles, not one good God plus a derivative blunder
- **Kabbalah**: never accepted the anti-Yahweh reading, but the Lurianic shevirat ha-kelim imports the structural logic (a cosmic rupture producing a broken world) without the polemic against the creator
