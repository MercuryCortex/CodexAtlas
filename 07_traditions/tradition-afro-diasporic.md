# Afro-Diasporic Religious Traditions

Umbrella term for the religious traditions that emerged from West and Central African religious-cultural traditions transported into the Americas via the Atlantic slave trade (~1500-1888) and reconstituted as living traditions in syncretic engagement with Roman Catholicism, indigenous American religions, and (later) Protestant Christianity and Spiritism.

The umbrella covers regionally-distinct but structurally-related traditions:

- **[[tradition-vodou-haitian]]** — Haitian Vodou: Fon-Yoruba substrate + Catholic syncretism + Taíno influence
- **[[tradition-santeria-lucumi]]** — Cuban Santería / Lucumí: Yoruba (Òrìṣà) substrate + Catholic syncretism
- **[[tradition-candomble]]** — Brazilian Candomblé: Yoruba + Fon + Bantu substrates + Catholic syncretism
- **[[tradition-yoruba]]** — the West African source tradition
- **[[tradition-yoruba-ifa]]** — the Ifá oracle system that grounds many diasporic ritual systems
- **[[tradition-rastafari]]** — Jamaican Rastafari: Protestant + Ethiopianism + Pan-Africanist synthesis (chronologically late but structurally part of the cluster)

## Cross-tradition pattern

The Afro-diasporic cluster is one of the most-documented examples of *deliberate syncretism under conditions of religious persecution*: enslaved African religious practitioners hid their Òrìṣà worship behind Catholic saint-iconography (Òṣun ↔ Our Lady of Charity; Changó ↔ Saint Barbara) in order to preserve the underlying tradition. The result is a structurally-distinct religious form that operates on Catholic surface + Yoruba/Fon/Bantu substrate, with the substrate increasingly emerging from the surface in the 20th-21st centuries as state and ecclesial persecution receded.

See [[afro-diasporic-syncretism]] for the cross-tradition syncretism analysis; [[syncretism-deliberate]] for the deliberate-synthesis pattern this cluster instantiates; [[event-haitian-revolution-1791-1804]] for the political-religious context of Haitian Vodou's institutional consolidation.
