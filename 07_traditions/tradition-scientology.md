# Scientology

## Overview
Religion founded by L. Ron Hubbard out of his 1950 Dianetics work; formally incorporated as the Church of Scientology beginning 1953–1954. Distinctive doctrine: humans are *thetans* — immortal, originally godlike spiritual beings — who through traumatic experiences across many lifetimes have become trapped in identification with bodies and the material universe ("MEST" — Matter, Energy, Space, Time). The practice of *auditing* (Scientology's central sacrament, performed with the E-meter) progressively releases these traumas (engrams) and eventually returns the thetan to its native state of *Operating Thetan* (OT). The advanced "OT levels" contain confidential cosmological material including the Xenu narrative (revealed in court 1995; unauthorized publication contested by the church).

**The tradition is in active legal and reputational controversy.** Substantial external scholarship (Urban 2011, Wright 2013, Atack 1990, Miller 1987, Reitman 2011) documents disputes over Hubbard's biography, the religious-vs-business status of Scientology institutions, the treatment of members of the Sea Org and of "Suppressive Persons" (SPs) declared by the church, fair-game and disconnection policies, and patterns of harassment of critics. Sect-internal sources and church-released materials present substantially different framings. **All claims about Scientology — sect-internal or external — should be treated with the standard source-integrity care; the Tier 4 framing applies.**

## Historical phases
1. **Dianetics period** (1950–1953) — *Dianetics* publishing sensation; scientific reception failure; Foundation for Dianetic Research.
2. **Religious incorporation** (1953–1967) — Church of Scientology in DC (1955), London, Saint Hill Manor; the Founding Church.
3. **Sea Org period** (1967–1986) — Hubbard at sea aboard the *Apollo* and other vessels; the Sea Org as senior organization; first OT levels developed; Operation Snow White (FBI infiltration scandal 1977).
4. **Miscavige period** (1986 onward) — David Miscavige succeeds Hubbard; institutional and legal consolidation; 1993 IRS tax-exemption settlement (US); continuing legal and journalistic controversy globally.

## Core texts
Hubbard's published works (Bridge Publications). The *tech* — Hubbard's policy letters, technical bulletins, lectures — is held to be infallible Scripture and may not be altered.

## Defining doctrines
- Humans are thetans trapped in MEST identification.
- The auditing technology releases engrams and progressively restores the thetan.
- Cosmic history (post-Dianetics): the OT cosmology including the Xenu narrative is the most distinctive Scientology doctrine.
- Suppressive Persons (SPs) and disconnection are doctrines/policies regarding individuals (often family members) who criticize the church.

## Relations to neighbors
Scientology positions itself as a religion among religions; mainstream religious studies (Urban 2011, Lewis 2009 *Scientology*) treats it as a new religious movement; many national governments (Germany, France, others) have refused it religious-status recognition.
