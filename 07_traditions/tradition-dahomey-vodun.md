# Dahomey Vodun — Fon-Ewe West African Religion

The traditional religion of the **Fon** and **Ewe** peoples of present-day Benin, Togo, and southeastern Ghana — historically codified within the Kingdom of Dahomey (c. 1600–1894). The word *vodun* (Fon: *vodún*) means "spirit" or "deity" and refers to the numerous divinities ([[mawu-lisa]], Sakpata, [[heviosso]], [[dan-aida-hwedo]], [[gu-vodun]], Legba, etc.) that mediate between humans and the high creator. Dahomey Vodun is the **substrate-religion of Haitian Vodou** and a principal contributor to Brazilian Candomblé Jeje and Cuban Arará — making it one of the most consequential West African religious transmissions of the transatlantic period.

## Cosmological structure

- **Primordial grandmother**: [[nana-buluku]] — the great primordial source from whom the creator pair emerges
- **High creator pair**: [[mawu-lisa]] — Mawu (feminine, moon, night, mystery) + Lisa (masculine, sun, day, strength). Sometimes treated as one dual-gendered being.
- ***Vodun*** **clans** organized by element / domain:
  - **Sky / thunder**: [[heviosso]] and his celestial siblings
  - **Earth / smallpox / agriculture**: Sakpata (cognate with Yoruba [[babalu-aye]])
  - **Serpent / rainbow / cool waters**: [[dan-aida-hwedo]]
  - **Iron / war / smithing**: [[gu-vodun]] (cognate with Yoruba [[ogun]])
  - **Sea**: Agbe + Naete
  - **Wilderness / hunters**: Age + Loko
- **Ancestral vodun**: the deified kings of Dahomey (the Tohousoun dynastic cult was the state religion) and the ancestors of every lineage

## Distinguishing features

Dahomey Vodun is **cognate with but distinct from** the Yoruba Òrìṣà religion to the east and the Akan religion to the west:

- **Cognate-pair structure with Yoruba**: many figures pair across the two systems (Heviosso ↔ [[shango]], Gu ↔ [[ogun]], Sakpata ↔ [[babalu-aye]]). The Atlantic diaspora typically preserves both rather than merging them.
- **State integration**: Dahomey Vodun was tightly bound to the royal cult. The king was supreme religious authority; the dynastic cult of Tohousoun anchored political legitimacy.
- **Convent system**: extended (months to years) initiation seclusion of devotees becoming vodun-priests. The convent (*kpame*) is a recognised institution still active in Benin today.
- **Possession choreography**: drumming + dance + the *vodun* "mounting" the devotee is the kinetic centre of ritual, and the specific Fon rhythms travel into Haitian Vodou's Rada rite with high fidelity.

## MASSIVE-WIN: the Atlantic transmission via Ouidah

The principal slave-trading port of the Kingdom of Dahomey was **Ouidah** (also Whydah), through which an estimated 1–2 million enslaved people were transported in the 17th–19th centuries, the great majority to **Saint-Domingue** (modern Haiti) and **Bahia** (Brazil). Dahomey Vodun was correspondingly the **principal Fon-Ewe substrate** of:

- **[[tradition-vodou-haitian]]** — the very word *Vodou* is Fon. The Rada rite preserves the Fon-Yoruba layer in figures like [[damballa]] (= Dan), [[erzulie]] Freda, Loko, Ogou.
- **[[tradition-candomble]] Jeje nation** — the *Jeje* nation is the Fon-Ewe ritual lineage in Bahia, distinct from the Yoruba *Nagô* nation, preserving Dahomey vodun under cognate-Portuguese names (Sogbo, Bade, Xevioso).
- **Cuban Arará tradition** — the smaller-but-attested Fon-derived layer in Cuban Afro-Cuban religion, parallel to the dominant Yoruba-derived Lukumí ([[tradition-santeria-lucumi]]).

The transmission is documented two-pole in Herskovits 1938 (working both Dahomey + Haiti), Verger 1957 (working both Ouidah + Bahia), and Bay 1998 (Dahomean state structure).

## Relations to neighbors

- vs. [[tradition-yoruba]]: cognate-neighboring system; cognate-pair figures (Heviosso/[[shango]], Gu/[[ogun]], Sakpata/[[babalu-aye]]); diaspora typically preserves both
- vs. [[tradition-akan]]: more distant cognate to the west; shares West African Niger-Congo substrate but distinct pantheon
- vs. [[tradition-bantu-kongo]]: structurally distinct (Central African Bantu vs. West African Niger-Congo); layers cleanly in Haitian Vodou (Rada = Fon-Yoruba; Petwo = Kongo-influenced)
- vs. [[tradition-vodou-haitian]]: parent-substrate of the daughter diaspora tradition; the daughter is more layered (Fon + Yoruba + Kongo + Saint-Domingue Catholic + colonial slave-history)
