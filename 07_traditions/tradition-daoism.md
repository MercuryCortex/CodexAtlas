# Daoism

## Overview
Daoism (Taoism) is the Chinese tradition centered on the *dào* (道, "way") — the ineffable cosmic principle from which all things arise — and on the cultivation of *wu-wei* (non-coercive action) and *ziran* (spontaneity). It is one of the three classical Chinese religious-philosophical traditions (with Confucianism and Buddhism) and has two broad streams: (a) *philosophical Daoism* (*daojia*), centered on the Daodejing and Zhuangzi; and (b) *religious Daoism* (*daojiao*), an organized religious tradition with deities, liturgies, monasteries, and alchemical practices, emerging from the Han period (~+2nd c.).

## Historical phases
1. **Classical / Warring States (~-400 to -200)** — Composition of the Daodejing and Zhuangzi; philosophical Daoism.
2. **Han syncretism (-200 to +200)** — Huang-Lao Daoism; Daoist elements absorbed into imperial ideology; correlative cosmology systems.
3. **Early Religious Daoism (~+100 to +600)** — Way of the Celestial Masters (Tianshi Dao, founded by Zhang Daoling ~+142); Shangqing (Highest Clarity) and Lingbao (Numinous Treasure) traditions; Daoist Canon (Daozang) compiled.
4. **Medieval (~+600 to +1300)** — State patronage under Tang (Li imperial family claimed descent from Laozi); Quanzhen ("Complete Perfection") monasticism founded by Wang Zhe ~+1170.
5. **Late Imperial and Modern (~+1300 onward)** — Sustained tradition; suppression and revival in modern China.

## Defining doctrines
- **Dao** as ineffable cosmic principle.
- **Wu-wei** — non-coercive action; effortless effectiveness.
- **Ziran** — spontaneity, self-so-ness.
- **Yin-yang complementarity** (developed in classical and Han Daoism in dialogue with broader Chinese cosmology).
- **The sage** (*shengren*) as one aligned with the dao.
- (Religious Daoism: pantheon, immortality cultivation, alchemy, talismanic ritual.)

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-confucianism]]: the canonical Chinese pairing. Daoist critique of Confucian ritualism and active virtue; Confucian critique of Daoist quietism. Both traditions historically interpenetrate and complement.
- vs. [[tradition-buddhism]]: Buddhism enters China ~+1st c. CE and is initially translated via Daoist vocabulary (*geyi*, "matching concepts"). Chinese Buddhism (especially Chan/Zen) is deeply shaped by Daoist sensibility. Polemical relations also occur (Buddho-Daoist *Huahujing* debate).
