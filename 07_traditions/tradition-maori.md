# Maori Religion

## Overview
The religious tradition of the Maori people of Aotearoa / New Zealand. Maori arrived in Aotearoa ~1280 CE from East Polynesia in waves of voyaging-settlement; their pre-contact religious tradition is a developed variant of broader Polynesian religion adapted to the temperate Aotearoa environment over ~500 years before European contact (Cook 1769). The cosmogonic narrative centers on Ranginui (Sky Father) and Papatūānuku (Earth Mother), their long embrace in primordial darkness, and their forcible separation by their son Tāne — creating the lit-world. The system of *atua* (gods) descended from Rangi-Papa, *tapu* / *noa* (sacred-restricted / free) ritual regulation, *mana* (religious-political authority), and *mauri* (life-principle) is the operational structure.

## Historical phases
1. **Voyaging-settlement (~1280 CE)** — Polynesian arrival; rapid iwi (tribal) differentiation across the islands.
2. **Pre-contact development (~1280–1769)** — distinct iwi religious-cosmogonic traditions develop within shared frame; whare wānanga (esoteric houses of learning) transmit specialized knowledge.
3. **Early contact (1769–1840)** — Cook 1769; sealing, whaling, missionary period from 1814 (Samuel Marsden); rapid syncretic-Christian Maori movements.
4. **Treaty of Waitangi (1840) and aftermath** — colonization, New Zealand Wars 1845–1872; Pai Mārire (1862), Ringatū (Te Kooti, 1868), Rātana (1918) syncretic-prophetic movements.
5. **20th c. revitalization** — Māori Renaissance (~1970s+); Te Reo (language) revitalization; *kohanga reo* and *kura kaupapa* (Maori-medium education); resurgent *tikanga* (proper practice) recognition in New Zealand law.

## MASSIVE WIN — The Sky-Father / Earth-Mother Separation Myth: One of the Most Widely Distributed Mythological Structures in World Religion

**The claim (investigative):** The mythological structure in which a primordial Sky Father and Earth Mother are locked in embrace in primordial darkness, separated by their children to let light into the world, appears in at least five major traditions across three continents and is almost certainly among the oldest recoverable narratives in human religion.

| Tradition | Sky Father | Earth Mother | Agent of Separation | Result |
|-----------|-----------|--------------|---------------------|--------|
| Māori | Ranginui | Papatūānuku | Tāne (pushes them apart with his shoulders / legs) | Light; the world as we know it; Rangi's tears as rain, Papa's sigh as mist |
| Greek | Ouranos (Heavens) | Gaia (Earth) | Kronos cuts them apart (with the sickle Gaia gives him) — more violent | The Titans freed; Aphrodite born from Ouranos's severed genitals in the sea |
| Vedic / Hindu | Dyaus Pitā (Sky Father — cognate with Greek Zeus Pater, Latin Jupiter) | Pṛthivī Mātā (Earth Mother) | Varuna / Mitra separate them; or Indra holds them apart | Creation; light; agriculture |
| Mesopotamian (Babylonian) | Anu (Heaven) | Ki (Earth) | Enlil (Air) separates them | The world space created; Enlil fills it with air |
| Norse | The vault of Ymir's skull (sky) | Jörð (Earth, sometimes) | Odin lifts the sky above the earth after slaying Ymir | The world created from Ymir's body; sky from skull |
| Yoruba | Obatala (sky / creator figure) | Odudua (earth, in some traditions) | Contested; Obatala molds earth below the sky | The earth formed |

**Investigative finding (best scholarly position):**

This mythological structure is one of the candidates for *reconstructed Proto-Indo-European mythology*. The Greek (Ouranos), Vedic (Dyaus), and Latin (Jupiter) sky-father names are linguistically cognate — all derive from the PIE root *\*dyew-* (to shine, the sky). This means the sky-father concept and likely the sky-earth separation myth are at minimum 5,000+ years old and spread with Indo-European language dispersal.

The Māori case is significant because Polynesian languages are entirely unrelated to Indo-European. The structural isomorphism of Ranginui/Papatūānuku with Ouranos/Gaia is either:
1. **Deep pre-human-dispersal substrate** (if the myth predates the migration out of Africa ~70,000 BCE — speculative but not impossible)
2. **Independent convergence** (both traditions solving the same cosmogonic problem — how did the current differentiated world emerge from primordial undifferentiated darkness? — in the same way)
3. **The universality of the sky-above / earth-below perceptual structure** translating into narrative (all humans live under sky on earth; the myth is the narrative form of that universal experience)

Most scholars favor option 2 or 3. The key finding remains: the myth is so widely distributed that it functions as a cross-cultural archetype regardless of the explanation.

**What Māori adds:** The emotional register of the Māori version is exceptional. Ranginui's tears (rain) and Papatūānuku's sighs (mist rising toward Rangi) are expressions of grief for the separation — the world is suffused with the longing of the primordial parents for each other. This gives the myth an emotional depth and ecological specificity (the mist of Aotearoa's landscape is literally the breath of the Earth Mother reaching for the Sky Father) absent from the more violent Greek version.

## MASSIVE WIN — Maui: Trickster-Demigod, Fisherman of Islands, Mortality's Discoverer

**Maui** is the most widely known figure in Polynesian mythology, present across Māori (Aotearoa), Hawaiian, Samoan, Tongan, and other Pacific traditions. In Māori tradition he is a trickster-demigod born premature and abandoned in the ocean by his mother, who thought he was dead; he was raised by his ancestor the sea and returned to his family with semi-divine powers. His great deeds:

- **Fished up the North Island** (Te Ika-a-Māui — "the fish of Māui") from the ocean floor using his grandmother's jawbone as a fishhook and the blood of his own nose as bait. The South Island is his canoe (*Te Waka o Māui*)
- **Lassoed the sun** (*Te Rā*) with ropes to slow its passage across the sky, giving people enough daylight to complete their work
- **Brought fire** to humanity from the underworld (a Promethean parallel — fire-theft from a divine/underworld source)
- **Attempted immortality** by crawling into the sleeping body of Hine-nui-te-pō (the goddess of death) while his bird companions watched. A fantail (pīwakawaka) laughed at the sight; Hine-nui-te-pō woke and crushed him — the origin of human mortality

**Cross-tradition parallel:** The Maui complex overlaps with:
- **Prometheus** (Greek): fire theft from divine authority, giving technology to humanity
- **Heracles / Hercules**: superhuman feats as cultural gifts (clearing obstacles for human flourishing)
- **Loki** (Norse): trickster with genuinely creative and destructive capacities; his actions shape the world's features
- **Coyote** (Navajo/Southwest): trickster who introduces both gifts and mortality; structurally necessary disruptor

The lasso-of-the-sun myth specifically (slowing the sun to give humans more daylight) appears independently in several Pacific and some African traditions, suggesting it may derive from the universal human experience of wishing for more daylight during short-day seasons.
