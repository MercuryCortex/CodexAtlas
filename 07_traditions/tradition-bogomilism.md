# Bogomilism

## Overview
Bogomilism was a medieval Christian dualist movement that emerged in Bulgaria in the mid-10th century under the priest Bogomil during the reign of Tsar Peter I, spread through the Byzantine Empire and the Balkans during the 11th–14th centuries, and propagated westward to give rise to Western European Catharism. Doctrinally Bogomilism held a mitigated dualism (a Good God with a fallen son Satanael who creates the material world); rejected the institutional Church (Eastern Orthodox in Bulgaria and Byzantium); rejected the Old Testament; held a docetic Christology; practiced strict asceticism (vegetarianism, sexual abstinence among the *perfecti*); and was institutionally organized with bishops, perfecti, and credentes. The movement survived suppression in Bulgaria and Byzantium and persisted longest in Bosnia, where Bosnian Bogomilism is sometimes argued to have continued until the Ottoman conquest and substantial conversion of the Bosnian aristocracy to Islam (the historicity of this Bosnian-Bogomil-to-Islam thesis is contested).

## Historical phases
1. **Bulgarian emergence (~940–970)** — the priest Bogomil; Cosmas's *Sermon against the Bogomils* (~970) is the foundational anti-Bogomil source.
2. **Byzantine spread (~1050–1110)** — Bogomil cells in Constantinople; the 1099 trial and burning of Basil the Physician under Alexios I Komnenos; Euthymius Zigabenus's anti-Bogomil *Panoplia Dogmatica*.
3. **Balkan and Anatolian spread (~1100–1300)** — Bogomil missionary activity through Bulgaria, Serbia, Bosnia, Anatolia; some local successes despite repeated official persecution.
4. **Western export (~1140–1200)** — Bogomil missionary activity propagates the doctrine to Northern Italian and Languedocian Cathar churches; Nazarius's ~1190 transmission of the *Interrogatio Iohannis* to Concorezzo is a documented direct link.
5. **Bosnian survival (~1200–1463)** — Bosnian Church (alleged Bogomil affiliation, disputed); Ottoman conquest 1463 and substantial Islamic conversion.
6. **Modern reception** — Bogomilism is a foundational subject of Bulgarian and Slavic religious history; the medieval transmission to Cathars is the major comparative-religion question.

## Core texts
- [[phase-5-020-interrogatio-iohannis]] — the principal surviving Bogomil-Cathar text.
- No original Bogomil writings survive in Slavonic or Greek; doctrine is reconstructed from Byzantine (Cosmas, Euthymius Zigabenus, Anna Komnene) and Bulgarian polemical sources.

## Pantheon (or rather, divine principles)
- [[the-good-god-cathar]] — the transcendent Good Father, equivalent in role.
- [[satanael]] — the fallen-angelic-son demiurge of the material world.

## Defining doctrines
- **Mitigated dualism** — single Good God with fallen Satanael as material creator.
- **Rejection of Old Testament and institutional Church**.
- **Docetic Christology**.
- **Ascetic perfecti / lay believer (credentes) institutional structure**.
- **Rejection of sacraments of the institutional Church**, but acceptance of a Bogomil *consolation* (parallel to the Cathar *consolamentum*).
- **Rejection of marriage and procreation** among the perfecti (consistent with cosmic anti-materialism).

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-catharism]]: direct doctrinal and probably textual ancestor of Western European Catharism.
- vs. Paulician dualism (Armenian / Anatolian, ~7th–9th c.): widely regarded as Bogomilism's proximate dualist ancestor. Byzantine emperors had resettled large Paulician populations in Thrace (~872 onward), bringing dualist communities into the Bulgarian frontier region where Bogomilism would emerge.
- vs. [[tradition-manichaeism]]: the deeper-ancestor debate. Stoyanov, Obolensky, Hamilton: continuous transmission Manichaean → Paulician → Bogomil → Cathar. Moore: largely independent.
- vs. Eastern Orthodox Christianity: the principal target of anti-Bogomil polemic; the Byzantine-Bulgarian official response.
- vs. [[event-fourth-crusade-1204|Fourth Crusade]] and Crusader-Byzantine contact: Latin Crusader presence in Constantinople (1204–1261) and the broader Crusader-era Eastern Mediterranean expanded contact between Bogomil-derived dualism and Latin Christendom — though the principal transmission channel into Western European [[tradition-catharism|Catharism]] (Bishop Nazarius's ~1190 visit to Concorezzo) predates the Fourth Crusade.

## MASSIVE-WIN: Bogomilism as the transmission vector for Zoroastrian dualism into Western Europe

Bogomilism is the **documented transmission link** in the chain Zoroastrian cosmic dualism → Mani → Paulicians → Bogomils → Cathars. Without Bogomilism, this chain breaks: the Cathars of Languedoc would lack their most direct dualist ancestor and the direct organizational contacts that shaped their theology. The key documented transmission event is the **Council of Saint-Félix (1167 CE)**: a Bogomil bishop named Nicetas traveled from Drugunthia (probably Bulgaria/Macedonia) to Languedoc and **ordained the bishops of the major Cathar churches** — Toulouse, Carcassonne, Val d'Aran, and possibly the Italian Cathar church of Concorezzo. This is not a hypothesis but a documented organizational event, attested in the *Acta Concilii Sancti Felicis* (though the document's authenticity is debated by Wakefield; Barber 2000 defends it as substantially genuine).

The Bogomil-to-Cathar connection makes Bogomilism the most consequential node in the transmission chain:
- **Upstream** from [[tradition-manichaeism]] via Paulician intermediaries (Byzantine emperors resettled large Paulician populations from Armenia into Thrace ~872 CE, bringing dualist communities into the Bulgarian frontier zone where Bogomilism would emerge).
- **Downstream** to [[tradition-catharism]]: Bogomil missionaries and the documented ordination contacts are the direct channel through which Cathar dualist theology reached its Languedocian form.

The Interrogatio Iohannis ([[phase-5-020-interrogatio-iohannis]]) — the principal surviving Bogomil-Cathar text — was carried by Nazarius, Cathar bishop of Concorezzo, from a Bogomil source ~1190: a documented text-transmission event within living memory of multiple Cathar sources.
