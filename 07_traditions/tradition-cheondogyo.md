# Cheondogyo — Religion of the Heavenly Way

Cheondogyo (천도교, "Religion of the Heavenly Way") is the institutional successor to [[tradition-donghak]], formally reorganized in 1905 by Son Byong-hui as the movement's third patriarch. The renaming — from "Donghak" (Eastern Learning) to "Cheondogyo" (Heavenly Way) — was a strategic rebranding after the catastrophic failure of the 1894 Peasant Revolution; it retained all of Donghak's core theology while distancing the movement from the association with armed rebellion.

The central doctrine remains *innaecheon* (人乃天, "Humans are Heaven") — the radical claim that every person contains the divine principle, without class or gender hierarchy. Cheondogyo played a significant role in the March First Independence Movement of 1919 against Japanese colonial rule; Son Byong-hui was among the signatories of the Korean Declaration of Independence. Today Cheondogyo has roughly one million adherents in North Korea and a smaller community in South Korea, making it the only recognized indigenous Korean religion with a substantial presence in the DPRK.
