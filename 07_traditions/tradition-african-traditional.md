# African Traditional Religions

## Overview
The vast and plural family of indigenous religious traditions of sub-Saharan Africa — Yoruba, Igbo, Akan, Asante, Dogon, Zulu, Shona, Kongo, Ewe, Fon, Bantu (manifold subtraditions), Nilotic (Dinka, Nuer, Maasai, etc.), and very many more. "African Traditional Religions" (plural) is a *family-resemblance* category — there is no single African religion any more than there is a single European religion. Common typological features (with appropriate caution about generalization across hundreds of distinct nations and several major language-families): a high god or supreme being often *deus otiosus*; intermediate divinities, spirits, or ancestors mediating between high god and humans; profound integration of ritual with land, agriculture, life-cycle, and kinship; divination as principal religious technology (Yoruba Ifá the best-documented but versions across many traditions); ancestor veneration as core religious practice; sacred-king institutions in many traditions; spirit-possession ritual; *power* (in various names — *àṣẹ* Yoruba, *nyama* Mande, *manzu* Kongo) as ontological category.

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-colonial (~3000 BCE+)** — millennia of indigenous development; major political-religious centers (Egypt, Nubia, Aksum, Ghana, Mali, Songhay, Kongo, Benin, Oyo, Great Zimbabwe, Asante, Buganda); migration and contact patterns (Bantu expansion ~1000 BCE onward).
2. **Islamic encounter (~700 CE+)** — Arab-Islamic expansion into North and East Africa; Sahel and Swahili coast Islamization; ongoing complex relations between Islam and traditional religion.
3. **Christian missionary encounter (~1500 CE+)** — Portuguese, Spanish, Dutch, French, British, German colonial missions; substantial Christianization especially during 19th–20th c. colonial period.
4. **Atlantic slave trade and diaspora (~1500–1866)** — forced transmission of African religion to the Americas; emergence of Vodou, Santería, Candomblé, Umbanda, Obeah, Hoodoo, etc.
5. **Colonial period (~1880–1960)** — formal European colonial administration; suppression and incorporation of traditional religious institutions; African Independent Church movements (Aladura, Kimbanguism, Zion Christian Church, etc.) as syncretic responses.
6. **Post-colonial (1960+)** — independence; substantial continued practice; complex coexistence with Christianity and Islam; revitalization movements.
7. **Trans-Atlantic re-integration (~1980+)** — Yoruba *babaláwo* and Cuban-Brazilian-Haitian diasporic priests exchanging visits; reciprocal recognition.

## Core texts and traditions
- [[phase-8-007-ifa-divination-corpus]] — Yoruba Ifá, the best-documented African textual-religious tradition.
- Substantially oral and ritual-performative across the continent; ethnographic-textual recovery from 19th–20th c.

## Sub-traditions (selected; full coverage would require many additional nodes)
- Yoruba (see [[tradition-yoruba-ifa]])
- Igbo (Chukwu, *chi* personal-destiny; *ofo* / *ogu* ritual implements)
- Akan / Asante (Nyame the supreme being; *abosom* lesser deities; ancestor stools)
- Dogon (Amma; Nommo twins; cosmogonic-cosmological system documented controversially by Marcel Griaule)
- Zulu / Xhosa / Nguni (uNkulunkulu / uMvelinqangi; ancestor *amadlozi*)
- Kongo (Nzambi a Mpungu; *minkisi* sacred-objects)
- Maasai / Kalenjin (Enkai / Ngai pastoral monotheism)
- Dinka / Nuer (Nhialic / Kwoth; Evans-Pritchard's classic Nuer ethnography)
- Many others

## Diasporic continuities
- [[tradition-vodou-haitian]] — Saint-Domingue/Haiti, drawing on Fon-Yoruba-Kongo substrate
- [[tradition-santeria-lucumi]] — Cuba, predominantly Yoruba
- Brazilian Candomblé and Umbanda — Yoruba, with regional and Bantu admixture
- Trinidadian Shango cult; Jamaican Kumina; New Orleans Voodoo; Hoodoo

## Defining features (with appropriate plurality)
- Layered cosmology: supreme being → spirits/ancestors → humans
- Ancestor veneration as central practice
- Divination as religious-diagnostic technology
- Spirit-possession ritual
- Sacred-king institutions in many traditions
- Profound place-and-land bondedness

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-islam]]: significant Sahel and East African Islamization; complex syncretic forms.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: extensive 19th-20th-c. Christianization; African Independent Churches as syncretic responses.
- Diasporic continuations (see above) constitute their own traditions with distinctive features but African substrate.
