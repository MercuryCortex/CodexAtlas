# Syriac Orthodox Church

## Overview
The Syriac Orthodox Church is the Antiochene branch of the Oriental Orthodox communion that rejected the [[event-council-of-chalcedon-451|Council of Chalcedon (451)]]'s two-natures Christology and held to the Miaphysite formula "one nature of the Word Incarnate" (*mia physis tou theou Logou sesarkōmenē*) inherited from Cyril of Alexandria. Originally the major Christian church of Greater Syria, Mesopotamia, and Edessa, its theological self-understanding is continuous with pre-Chalcedonian Antiochene Christianity (Ignatius, Ephrem, the Peshitta tradition). It survived Justinian's 6th-c. persecution underground via the consecration program of [[jacob-baradaeus|Jacob Baradaeus]] (from whom the pejorative "Jacobite" comes), then endured Arab conquest, Crusader rule, Mongol invasion, Mamluk-Ottoman tributary status, the 1915 Sayfo genocide alongside the Armenian Genocide, and the 21st-c. ISIS displacement.

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-Chalcedonian Antiochene Christianity (~33-451)** — apostolic foundation traditions (Peter, Paul, Thaddeus/Addai); Edessene Christianity (*Doctrina Addai*, the Abgar legend); Ephrem the Syrian's hymnody at Nisibis and Edessa (~363); the Peshitta translation tradition.
2. **Post-Chalcedonian formation (~451-543)** — Miaphysite resistance under [[dioscorus-of-alexandria|Dioscorus]]'s heirs; [[severus-of-antioch|Severus of Antioch]] (patriarch 512-518, exiled to Egypt) systematizes Miaphysite theology; Justinian's anti-Miaphysite persecution.
3. **Jacob Baradaeus underground revival (~543-578)** — [[jacob-baradaeus|Jacob Baradaeus]] consecrated bishops in disguise across Syria-Mesopotamia, preserving an independent Miaphysite hierarchy that becomes the institutional Syriac Orthodox Church.
4. **Arab conquest + dhimmi period (~640-1258)** — Syriac Christians become *ahl al-kitāb* under early caliphates; the great Greek-to-Syriac-to-Arabic translation movement (Ḥunayn ibn Isḥāq +-his son and disciples were East Syriac / Church-of-the-East, but the Syriac Orthodox also participated) channels Greek philosophy into Islamic *falsafa*; Bar Hebraeus + Michael the Syrian are the great medieval polymaths.
5. **Mongol-Mamluk decline (~1258-1517)** — Mongol invasion and Mamluk reconquest devastate the Mesopotamian heartland.
6. **Ottoman millet system (~1517-1915)** — recognized as the *Süryani* millet; survives by retreat into the Tur Abdin mountain fastness.
7. **Sayfo / Year of the Sword (1915-1923)** — co-occurring with the Armenian Genocide, the Syriac-Aramean-Assyrian Christians lose ~300,000+ people; many flee to Lebanon, Syria, Iraq, and the Americas.
8. **Modern + ISIS displacement (~1990-present)** — Iraq War + ISIS occupation of Nineveh/Mosul 2014-2017 displaces the Iraqi Syriac population; Syria war 2011+ devastates the Syriac heartland of Aleppo + Homs + Hasakah; major diaspora communities in Sweden, Germany, the Netherlands, the US, Australia.

## Core texts
- **The Peshitta** — the Syriac Bible; Old Testament from Hebrew, New Testament from Greek; the New Testament Peshitta excludes 2 Peter, 2-3 John, Jude, Revelation (the canonical Syriac NT is a 22-book canon).
- [[phase-4-055-ephrem-hymns-on-paradise]] — Ephrem the Syrian's hymns; foundational Syriac theological poetry.
- **Anaphora of St James** + the ~80 surviving Syriac Orthodox eucharistic anaphoras (the largest extant eucharistic-liturgy collection in any Christian tradition).
- ***Doctrina Addai*** (4th-c.) — the Edessene apostolic-foundation narrative.
- **Bar Hebraeus's *Chronicon*** — 13th-c. universal Christian history.
- **Michael the Syrian's *Chronicle*** — 12th-c. Crusader-period universal history (unique Syriac source for Crusader-era Levant events).

## Key persons
- [[dioscorus-of-alexandria]] — the deposed Alexandrian patriarch of Chalcedon 451; Syriac Orthodox traditional founder-figure (along with Coptic and Ethiopian).
- [[severus-of-antioch]] — 6th-c. patriarch; the foundational Miaphysite systematic theologian.
- [[jacob-baradaeus]] — the underground consecrator who saved the Miaphysite hierarchy; eponym of "Jacobite."
- **Ephrem the Syrian** (~306-373) — the great pre-Chalcedonian Syriac theological poet.
- **Jacob of Edessa** (~640-708) — Syriac Orthodox scholar-bishop; biblical scholar + grammarian + canonist.
- **Bar Hebraeus** (1226-1286) — Maphrian (eastern primate) of the church; encyclopedist, philosopher, historian, physician — the Syriac equivalent of Ibn Sina or Aquinas.
- **Michael the Syrian** (1126-1199) — Patriarch of Antioch; *Chronicle* is THE Syriac primary source on the Crusader period.

## Defining doctrines
- **Miaphysite Christology** — see [[miaphysite-christology]]. One nature of the Word Incarnate (Cyril of Alexandria's formula); rejects Chalcedon's "two natures" as Nestorian-leaning.
- **Liturgy in classical Syriac (Aramaic)** — direct linguistic continuity with the language of Jesus and the apostolic preaching.
- **The Peshitta canon** — distinctive 22-book NT (excludes 2 Peter, 2-3 John, Jude, Revelation).
- **Strong monastic ethos** — the Syriac monastic tradition is one of the oldest and most influential globally (Egyptian + Syriac are the two foundational monastic streams).

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-oriental-orthodox]] sister churches ([[tradition-coptic-orthodox]], [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]], tradition-armenian-apostolic): full doctrinal communion; the four (plus Indian Malankara) constitute Oriental Orthodoxy.
- vs. Chalcedonian (Eastern Orthodox + Catholic) Christianity: schismatic since 451 on Christology; modern dialogue (1980s+) acknowledges that the historical Christological dispute was substantially terminological rather than substantial (the 1990 Anba Bishoy + 1971 Vienna Christological agreed-statements). Not yet in formal communion.
- vs. **Church of the East / East Syriac (Nestorian)**: same Syriac linguistic-liturgical family but opposite Christological pole; split 431 Ephesus. Modern dialogue has substantially closed this gap too.
- vs. **Maronite Church**: originally Syriac-Antiochene; entered union with Rome ~12th c.; now Eastern Catholic.
- vs. **Islam**: dhimmi millet status from 7th c. forward; the Syriac translation movement channeled Greek philosophy into Arabic *falsafa* (al-Kindi onward — see opus-islam-1's batch).
