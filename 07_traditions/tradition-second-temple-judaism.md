# Second Temple Judaism

## Overview

Second Temple Judaism covers the period from the reconstruction of the Jerusalem Temple after the Babylonian exile (515 BCE) through its destruction by the Roman armies of Titus (70 CE) — a 585-year span that is the **single most generative transmission period in the vault**. From this matrix, two world religions emerged simultaneously: Rabbinic Judaism (which outlasted the Temple by adapting Torah study as the new religious center) and early Christianity (which reinterpreted the Temple's sacrificial system through the crucifixion). Both are Second Temple Judaism's children.

## Historical phases

1. **Persian period (515–333 BCE)** — Ezra and Nehemiah lead the restoration; Torah becomes the constitutional document of the Jewish community; the canon of the Hebrew Bible begins closing; Persian Zoroastrian cosmology (angels, demons, eschatology) enters Jewish thought through sustained contact — the [[persian-period-injection]] begins here.

2. **Hellenistic period (333–63 BCE)** — Alexander's conquest forces a creative collision between Hebrew tradition and Greek philosophy. The **Septuagint** (LXX, ~250 BCE) is the first great translation event of the ancient world; it imports Greek conceptual vocabulary into Jewish scripture. The **Maccabean crisis** (167–164 BCE) — when Antiochus IV Epiphanes desecrated the Temple and banned Torah practice — triggered the explosion of apocalyptic literature (Daniel, 1 Enoch, Jubilees) and the Hasmonean dynasty. **Qumran** separates from Temple Judaism at this point; the Dead Sea Scrolls are their library.

3. **Roman period (63 BCE – 70 CE)** — Pompey's conquest; Herod the Great's Temple expansion; fragmentation into Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, Zealots, and the Jesus movement. Philo of Alexandria (~20 BCE – 50 CE) writes the definitive synthesis of Jewish revelation and Platonic philosophy. Paul of Tarsus (~5–67 CE) carries one strand of the Jesus movement into the Gentile world.

## Sectarian diversity (Sanders's "common Judaism")

E.P. Sanders (1992) demonstrated that the Pharisees, Sadducees, and Essenes shared a "common Judaism" of Temple piety, Torah observance, and covenant identity — they were intra-Jewish variants, not separate religions. Key distinctions:

- **Pharisees** — oral Torah as equally authoritative to written Torah; resurrection of the dead; synagogue as parallel institution to Temple. The direct ancestors of Rabbinic Judaism.
- **Sadducees** — only written Torah authoritative; no resurrection; priestly-aristocratic leadership aligned with the Temple and Roman administration. Vanished after 70 CE.
- **Essenes (Qumran community)** — rigorous halakhic purity; predestinarian theology; solar calendar vs. Temple lunar calendar; "Teacher of Righteousness" vs. "Wicked Priest"; apocalyptic *War Scroll*; Dead Sea Scrolls their library. Often connected to the John the Baptist tradition.
- **Zealots** — political-military resistance; "no king but God"; immediate precursor to the First Jewish-Roman War (66–73 CE).
- **Jesus movement** — apocalyptic prophet tradition within Galilean Judaism; Paul's mission transforms it from Jewish sect to Gentile religion.

## MASSIVE WINS — transmission edges

- **Second Temple Judaism → both Rabbinic Judaism AND Early Christianity**: This is the vault's central transmission node. Both traditions emerge from the same matrix in the same generation (70 CE). Both are responses to the Temple's destruction: Rabbinic Judaism replaces sacrifice with Torah study and prayer; Christianity interprets the destruction as divine judgment validating the crucifixion narrative. The split between the two traditions happens *within* Second Temple Judaism, not from outside it.

- **Zoroastrian eschatology → Second Temple apocalypticism**: The distinctive eschatological features of Second Temple literature — cosmic dualism (God vs. Satan), bodily resurrection, last judgment, new creation — all appear in the 600-year Persian contact period. The Book of Daniel (165 BCE) is the clearest case: written under Greek persecution, it deploys Persian cosmic-historical schema. [[tradition-zoroastrianism]] is the upstream; [[persian-period-injection]] is the channel. This is the most important cross-tradition transmission edge in the Phase 3 vault.

- **Hellenistic philosophy → Philo's Logos → NT Prologue**: Philo of Alexandria deploys the Platonic-Stoic concept of *Logos* (divine reason) as the intermediary between the transcendent God and the material world — identical in structure to what John 1:1 does with Christ. Philo was writing in the same generation as the Paul epistles. The Logos doctrine in Christianity is not purely Christian; it is Philo's Hellenistic Jewish synthesis appropriated and personalized in the Gospel of John. Wire to [[logos-cosmic-reason]], [[philo-of-alexandria]], [[tradition-neoplatonism]].

- **Qumran/Dead Sea Scrolls → New Testament vocabulary**: The Scrolls (discovered 1947) revealed that key NT concepts formerly attributed to Hellenistic influence (light/darkness dualism, Holy Spirit, "sons of light," community as Temple) were native to pre-Christian Palestinian Judaism. The "Teacher of Righteousness" at Qumran prefigures the anointed-leader-rejected-by-authorities pattern. Wire to [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]].

- **Second Temple canon wars → Christian canon formation**: The process of Hebrew Bible canonization was still in progress during the Second Temple period (Sirach, 1 Enoch, and Jubilees were used as scripture by some communities). When Christians later inherited the Hebrew Bible, they inherited a still-contested canon — which explains why Protestant, Catholic, and Orthodox Bibles still differ in their Old Testament contents. The vault's Phase 3 document layer IS the literature of this contested canon.

## Relations to neighbors

- vs. [[tradition-judaism]]: Second Temple Judaism is a phase *within* the longer Jewish tradition — the period when Judaism's most distinctive later features (resurrection, Satan, the synagogue, rabbinic authority) were developing.
- vs. [[tradition-early-christianity]]: Christianity is a *product* of Second Temple Judaism; early Christians used the LXX as their scriptures and debated within the framework of Second Temple sectarian categories.
- vs. [[tradition-zoroastrianism]]: The Iranian tradition is the single largest external influence on Second Temple cosmology — angels, demons, the cosmic battle, the eschatological drama.
