# Yoruba Religion

## Overview

Yoruba religion is the religious system of the Yoruba peoples of West Africa — one of the largest ethno-religious populations in sub-Saharan Africa, numbering over 40 million people in Nigeria, Benin, and Togo, with a vast diaspora across the Atlantic world. It is structured around four interlocking concepts:

1. **[[olodumare]]** — the supreme being (*deus otiosus*): transcendent, ultimate, not approached directly in cult
2. The **Orishas** (*Òrìṣà*) — approximately 401 divine intermediaries, each governing specific natural forces and human domains (see [[orisha-pantheon]])
3. **Egungun** — the ancestors, who require veneration and remain active in community life
4. **Orí** (*individual divine soul-destiny*) — each person's personal divine head, chosen before birth in dialogue with Olódùmarè

The primary operational technology is **Ifá divination** — a 256-*odù* binary combinatorial oracle consulted through the *babaláwo* (divination priesthood) — documented in the [[phase-8-007-ifa-divination-corpus]], recognized by UNESCO in 2005 as an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. For the full religious-system analysis, see [[tradition-yoruba-ifa]].

## Historical phases

1. **Pre-modern (~1000 BCE – 1500 CE)** — formation of Yoruba religious system across Yorùbáland; Ile-Ife as the sacred cosmogonic center (place where Obatala descended to shape the earth from primordial waters); Oyo Empire (~1300–1835) as principal political-religious center; elaborate palace-centered orisha cult.
2. **Slave-trade transmission (~1500–1866)** — forced transmission of Yoruba peoples and religion across the Middle Passage to the Americas; sustained religious-community formation under conditions of enslavement; emergence of Lukumí (Cuba), Candomblé (Brazil), Vodou (Haiti, Fon-Yoruba substrate), Shango cult (Trinidad).
3. **Colonial-era textualization (~1840–1920)** — first systematic external documentation; Samuel Ajayi Crowther's Yoruba Bible translation (1862) and his contested rendering of *Èṣù* as Satan; European ethnographic work.
4. **20th-c. academic establishment** — Bascom (1937 onward); Abimbola (1968 onward); Idowu (1962); systematic Ifá textualization; professional *babaláwo* training standardization; Nigerian-government recognition.
5. **Trans-Atlantic re-integration (1980s onward)** — Yoruba *babaláwo* and Cuban Lukumí/Brazilian Candomblé priests exchanging visits and recognizing each other; diasporic traditions feeding back into Yorùbáland.

## Core texts

- [[phase-8-007-ifa-divination-corpus]] — the Ifá literary corpus; 256 *odù* chapters, each with hundreds of *ese Ifá* (verse-texts); the primary theological and cosmological canon.
- Yoruba *oríkì* (praise-poetry), *ìjálá* (hunter-chants for Ògún), *iyere* (Ifá chant-style) — oral genres constituting a vast literary tradition outside the formal Ifá corpus.

## Pantheon

The orisha system (see [[orisha-pantheon]]) includes:

- [[olodumare]] — supreme being, transcendent, aniconic, deus otiosus
- [[oduduwa]] — first king of Ile-Ife; in some traditions the creator-deity sent by Olodumare to make the earth; in others, first Yoruba ancestor
- [[obatala]] — creator of human bodies; purity; elder authority; patron of the differently abled; white cloth
- [[oshun]] — river, love, fertility, wealth, honey, gold, freshwater
- [[shango]] — thunder, lightning, royal justice, masculine power; historically a deified Oyo king
- [[yemoja]] — motherhood, river (Africa) / ocean (diaspora), vast embrace
- [[ogun]] — iron, war, roads, labor, technology; patron of surgeons and soldiers
- [[eshu]] — crossroads, communication, trickster; first orisha approached in every ritual; opener of all paths
- [[oya]] — wind, storms, death-threshold, the Niger River, transformation
- [[orunmila]] — wisdom, Ifá divination, cosmic knowledge; only orisha who witnessed all destinies at creation

## The Orí Doctrine (MASSIVE WIN: personal divine soul)

One of Yoruba religion's most theologically distinctive features is the *Orí* — the personal divine head chosen before incarnation. Before birth, each person kneels before [[olodumare]] and chooses their destiny (*orí*). This choice is irrevocable but can be re-oriented through Ifá divination and the correct *ebó* (ritual offerings). The *Orí* is literally a portion of the divine — a shard of Olodumare's creative power lodged in each person as their innermost self.

**Cross-tradition parallel — `parallel-form`:** This doctrine structurally parallels:
- The Gnostic **divine spark** (*pneuma* imprisoned in matter) — [[soul-as-spark]], [[tradition-gnosticism]]
- The Kabbalistic **nitzotz** (*divine spark* from the *shevirat ha-kelim* — shattering of the vessels) — [[shevirat-hakelim]], [[tradition-kabbalah]]
- The Sufi **sirr** (*the divine secret/spark in the heart*) — [[tradition-sufism]]
- The Akan **Okra** (see [[tradition-akan]]) — a portion of Nyame placed in each person

All four traditions arrived independently at the same theological insight: there is a portion of the divine lodged in each human being, which constitutes the deepest self and is the ground of spiritual practice.

## MASSIVE WIN: Ifá's 256 Odù — binary divination parallel to the I Ching

The Ifá divination system uses a binary combinatorial structure to generate its 256 *odù*: the *babaláwo* casts 16 palm nuts (*ikin*), recording whether 1 or 2 nuts remain in each throw to generate a binary figure of 8 positions. 16 primary *odù* × 16 = 256 total combinations. The resulting corpus of verse is organized around these 256 binary codes.

The [[phase-1-026-yijing]] (*I Ching*) uses a structurally identical binary combinatorial method: broken (yin) or unbroken (yang) line positions generate 64 hexagrams (2⁶). Both systems:
- Are binary in their fundamental combinatorial logic
- Generate a fixed-size corpus of oracular texts organized by the binary codes
- Require specialist interpreters (babalawo / divination master)
- Address the full range of human situations through poetic-mythological verse
- Are among the most elaborately documented divination systems in world religion

The mathematical parallel is striking — both arrived at binary combinatorial divination independently, one in West Africa (~1000 BCE or earlier), one in Bronze-Age China. Whether this is convergent human cognition, diffusion (proposed but not documented), or coincidence is an open research question. **Edge type: `parallel-form`** (no documented transmission pathway). Scholars including Bascom (1969) and William Bascom note the structural parallel explicitly without claiming transmission.

## Diaspora forms

- **[[tradition-candomble]]** (Brazil, especially Bahia) — Yoruba-origin Nagô nation is the most theologically prestigious; preserves Yoruba liturgical language and orisha theology.
- **[[tradition-santeria-lucumi]]** (Cuba) — Lukumí tradition; the closest Caribbean preservation of Yoruba orisha liturgy; ongoing trans-Atlantic recognition exchange.
- **[[tradition-vodou-haitian]]** (Haiti) — the *Rada* rite of Vodou derives from Yoruba-Fon substrate transmitted via Saint-Domingue; *lwa* such as Ogou, Erzulie, Ayizan are Yoruba-Dahomean figures.
- Trinidad Shango cult, Jamaican Revival, New Orleans Voodoo, Candomblé Angola — further diasporic forms.

The diaspora's mapping of orishas onto Catholic saints (*sincretismo*) is documented in [[afro-diasporic-syncretism]] — one of the most theologically sophisticated instances of syncretic theology in the modern period.

- vs. [[tradition-yoruba-ifa]]: the *Yoruba Religion* node (this file) covers the broad tradition and its cultural context; the [[tradition-yoruba-ifa]] node focuses on the Ifá-orisha religious system in depth, including the cross-tradition theological analysis.
- vs. [[tradition-vodou-haitian]]: Vodou's *Rada* rite is a Middle-Passage transmission of Yoruba-Fon religion; same theological substrate, regional differentiation.
- vs. [[tradition-santeria-lucumi]]: Cuban Lukumí is the closest diaspora preservation; ongoing reciprocal recognition.
- vs. [[tradition-islam]]: northern and some southwestern Yorùbáland is substantially Muslim; the triple coexistence of Yoruba traditional religion, Christianity, and Islam is one of the defining facts of modern Nigerian religious life.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: extensive 19th-c. Christianization; Crowther's contested Èṣù→Satan translation became a generator of subsequent colonial-era demonization of traditional practice.
