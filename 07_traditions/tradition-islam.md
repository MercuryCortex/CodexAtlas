# Islam

## Overview
A monotheistic Abrahamic tradition founded in the early seventh century by the prophet [[muhammad-ibn-abdullah]] (~570–632) in the Hijaz region of western Arabia. Islam claims to fulfill and correct the prior Abrahamic revelations to Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, and Jesus; the Quran is held to be God's uncreated final word, transmitted to Muhammad via the angel Jibrīl (Gabriel) over 22 years (610–632), codified under Caliph Uthman ~650. Islam's foundational practices are the Five Pillars (shahada, salat, zakat, sawm, hajj); its foundational doctrine is *tawhid* (radical divine unity).

## Historical phases
1. **Meccan period** (610–622) — Muhammad's preaching of monotheism amidst Meccan polytheism; persecution.
2. **Medinan period** (622–632) — the Hegira; founding of the Muslim *umma*; military and political consolidation.
3. **Rashidun caliphate** (632–661) — the four "Rightly Guided" caliphs; conquest of Sasanian Iran and most of Byzantine Syria/Egypt/North Africa.
4. **Umayyad caliphate** (661–750) — Damascus-centered Arab empire; expansion to Spain (711) and Sind (711).
5. **Abbasid caliphate** (750–1258) — Baghdad-centered cosmopolitan empire; classical Islamic civilization (Quranic exegesis, hadith collection, jurisprudence, theology, philosophy, science).
6. **Subsequent caliphates and dynasties** — Fatimid, Mamluk, Ottoman, Mughal, Safavid; modern nation-states.

## Defining doctrines
- Tawhid (radical divine unity).
- Muhammad as Seal of the Prophets.
- Quran as uncreated revelation.
- Five Pillars: shahada (witness), salat (prayer), zakat (alms), sawm (fasting in Ramadan), hajj (pilgrimage).
- Final Judgment.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-pre-islamic-arabian]]: the parent religious milieu — Quran 53:19-22 + 71:23 polemic against [[allat]] / [[al-uzza]] / [[manat]] / [[wadd]] / [[nasr-pre-islamic]]; [[hubal]] smashed at the Kaaba in 8 AH ([[event-conquest-of-mecca-630]]); the [[hanif-monotheism|*ḥanīf*-tradition]] as transitional bridge.
- vs. [[tradition-judaism]]: shared Abrahamic monotheism; Quran reframes biblical figures; relationship with Medinan Jewish clans formative; the *ḥanīf*-tradition (Quran 2:135 / 3:67) self-identifies Islam as restoration of Abrahamic religion preceding both Judaism and Christianity.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: shared Jesus traditions (Quranic Jesus is prophet and miracle-worker, not divine son); Quran rejects Trinity; Syriac Christian late-antique milieu provides substantial Quranic background (Reynolds 2010, Griffith 2013).
- vs. [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]]: Aksumite Christian Arabia in the immediate run-up to Islam — Quran 105 (Year of the Elephant — Abreha's Aksumite Yemeni expedition c. 570); first Muslim hijra to Aksum c. 615; [[event-kaleb-aksum-himyar-invasion-525]] context.
- vs. [[tradition-mandaeism]]: Quranic *Sabians* often identified with Mandaeans — protected ahl al-kitāb status.
- vs. [[tradition-zoroastrianism]]: post-conquest interaction in Iran transformed both.
- vs. [[tradition-sufism]]: an internal mystical tradition within Islam, treated as a separate node here for graph clarity.
- vs. [[tradition-shia-islam]]: the foundational intra-Muslim sectarian fault-line — [[event-death-of-muhammad-632|Saqīfa succession 632]], [[event-first-fitna-656-661|First Fitna 656-661]], [[event-karbala-680|Karbala 680]] are the foundational events.
- vs. Western philosophy: the [[falsafa]] tradition is the principal Greek-philosophical bridge from late antiquity into medieval Latin scholasticism (Avicenna → Aquinas; Averroes = "the Commentator"); the [[tahafut-controversy]] is its dialectical bookend.
