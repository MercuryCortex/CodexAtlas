# Sethian Gnosticism

## What it is
Sethian Gnosticism (Sethianism) is a major current of late-antique [[tradition-gnosticism|Gnosticism]] organized around the figure of [[seth|Seth]], the third son of Adam, reinterpreted as the heavenly progenitor and redeemer of a spiritual race ("the seed of Seth"). Its mythology features a transcendent unknowable [[the-monad-gnostic|Monad]], the first emanation [[barbelo|Barbelo]], the fall of [[sophia-gnostic|Sophia]], and the ignorant creator-god [[yaldabaoth|Yaldabaoth]] who fashions the material world and arrogantly claims to be the only god.

## Earliest attestation
The defining Sethian text is the Apocryphon (Secret Book) of John, extant in multiple Nag Hammadi copies and summarized already by Irenaeus (~180 CE). The Nag Hammadi library (1945) and the later Codex Tchacos preserve the Sethian corpus: the Trimorphic Protennoia, Zostrianos, Allogenes, the Three Steles of Seth, and [[gospel-of-judas-sethian|the Gospel of Judas]].

## Context
Sethianism is one of the systems modern scholarship long grouped under "Gnosticism" (a category Williams 1996 and King have problematized). It is marked by a **radical cosmological dualism**: the true God is utterly transcendent, while the visible cosmos is the botched work of an inferior demiurge. Salvation comes through *gnosis* — saving knowledge of one's divine origin. In the 3rd century some Sethian "Platonizing" treatises (Zostrianos, Allogenes) circulated in Plotinus's Roman circle and were attacked by him and his pupil Porphyry, evidence of the tradition's engagement with Neoplatonism (Turner 2001).

## Cross-tradition findings
- **Polemic inversion of Genesis.** Sethian myth systematically inverts the Hebrew Bible: the creator [[yaldabaoth|Yaldabaoth]] is a deluded tyrant, the serpent and Eve can be revalued as bringers of knowledge, and Seth becomes a savior. This is a textbook `polemic-inversion` of an earlier tradition's god, and one of the clearest cases of cross-tradition theological reversal in the Atlas.
- **Sethian Platonism.** The documented interaction with Plotinus's school (Porphyry, *Life of Plotinus* 16) makes Sethianism a real meeting point of Gnostic myth and Greek metaphysics.
