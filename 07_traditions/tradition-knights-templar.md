# Knights Templar

## Overview
The **Order of the Poor Knights of Christ and of the Temple of Solomon** — Latin *Pauperes commilitones Christi Templique Salomonici* — was a medieval Latin Christian military-religious order founded ~1119 in Jerusalem to protect Christian pilgrims after the [[event-first-crusade-1095-1099|First Crusade]], formally constituted at the Council of Troyes in 1129 under a Rule largely drafted by [[bernard-of-clairvaux]], and dissolved by Pope [[pope-clement-v]] in 1312 (bull *Vox in excelso*) following a five-year mass suppression initiated in 1307 by King [[philip-iv-of-france]]. At its peak the Order operated a continent-spanning network of preceptories, a sophisticated proto-banking system (transferring funds for pilgrims and crusader princes via *letters of payment* drawn on local Temple preceptories), and a standing field-army of professional military monks in Outremer — making the Templars an early example of a transnational religious *corporation* with its own treasury, fleet, and military command structure independent of any single crown.

The Templars matter to the Codex Atlas wedge in three distinct registers, each requiring its own evidentiary discipline:

1. **As a documented medieval Latin Christian institution** (1119–1312) — well-attested, fully inside critical-historical scholarship.
2. **As a survivor of the Crusader-era Levantine contact zone** — the Templars are one of the few medieval Latin Christian institutions with sustained, documented physical presence among Eastern Christians, Mandaeans, Druze, Sufi orders, and the Assassin (Nizari Ismaili) network. What, if anything, was *theologically* exchanged across that contact zone is the [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis|Templar-Gnostic transmission hypothesis]] — a high-prize but evidentially fragile claim that has generated centuries of legendry. (See **Disputes**.)
3. **As a reception phenomenon** — from the trial-extracted [[baphomet-controversy|Baphomet]] confessions through the 18th-c. [[tradition-freemasonry|Masonic]] Strict Templar Observance to the modern Holy-Blood-Holy-Grail / Priory of Sion literature, the *idea* of the Templars as carriers of secret Gnostic, Johannite, or Hermetic knowledge is itself a major early-modern and modern esoteric phenomenon, regardless of whether the historical Order ever held such doctrine.

Keeping (1), (2), and (3) tiered separately is the core methodological discipline of any Templar entry in this vault — see [[../00_meta/methodology.md|methodology]] on source-tiering.

## Historical phases

1. **Foundation in the Holy Land (1119–1129).** Hugues de Payens, Godfrey de Saint-Omer, and seven companions present themselves to King Baldwin II of Jerusalem (~1119) and are quartered in the *Templum Salomonis* — the al-Aqsa Mosque on the Temple Mount, which the Latin crusaders identified as Solomon's Temple. The Order's name derives from this lodging.
2. **Council of Troyes and Rule (1129).** Bernard of Clairvaux drafts (or substantially shapes) the *Regula pauperum commilitonum Christi Templi Salomonici* — the Latin Rule. Bernard's separately-published *De Laude Novae Militiae* ([[phase-5-037-bernard-de-laude-novae-militiae]]) is the ideological manifesto justifying the unprecedented combination of monastic vow + sanctioned violence: a "new chivalry" in which the killing of *malefactores* is reconceived as *malecide* rather than homicide.
3. **Crusader-state military expansion (1129–1187).** Templar castles (Safed, Atlit / Château Pèlerin, Tortosa, Beaufort, Belvoir, etc.) anchor the Latin defensive line. Templars fight at Montgisard (1177) and disastrously at Hattin (1187), where Grand Master Gérard de Ridefort survives but Saladin executes ~230 captured Templar and Hospitaller knights.
4. **Latin Kingdom contraction (1187–1291).** The Order maintains its Levantine presence through the [[event-third-crusade-1189-1192|Third Crusade]] and the disastrous [[event-fourth-crusade-1204|Fourth Crusade]] (which the Templars and Hospitallers notably *did not* support). The 1291 [[event-fall-of-acre-1291|Fall of Acre]] ends the Latin Kingdom; the Templar HQ relocates to Cyprus, then to the Paris Temple.
5. **Banking and political weight (~1150–1307).** Throughout this period the Order develops the first medieval transnational banking system (deposit-letter / draft-payment instruments allowing pilgrims and crown treasuries to move value without moving coin), accumulates vast lands across Western Europe, and becomes the principal lender to the French crown — including to Philip IV, who, by 1307, is heavily in debt to the Order.
6. **Suppression (1307–1314).** On the morning of Friday 13 October 1307 — the legendary "Friday the 13th" — Philip IV simultaneously arrests every Templar in France (~5,000+ men). The charges, extracted under torture, include heresy, idolatry of an image called "Baphomet," sodomy, denial and spitting on the cross, and obscene initiation rites. Clement V (avignon papacy, French royal pressure) eventually grants conditional absolution at Chinon (1308 — the [[phase-5-038-chinon-parchment-1308|Chinon Parchment]], rediscovered in 2001 by Barbara Frale in the Vatican Secret Archives) but ultimately suppresses the Order at the [[event-council-of-vienne-1311-1312|Council of Vienne]] (1311–1312). Jacques de Molay and Geoffroi de Charney are burned in Paris on 18 March 1314.
7. **Reception (1312–present).**
   - Iberian survival: the order is partially reconstituted in Portugal as the [[tradition-order-of-christ|Order of Christ]] (1319; see [[event-order-of-christ-foundation-1319]]), in Aragón as the **Order of Montesa** (1317). The Order of Christ is **the single best-documented institutional Templar-survival in Latin Christendom** — papal-bull-authorized (*Ad ea ex quibus*, 14 March 1319, John XXII at Avignon), Portuguese-crown-supported, continuous in personnel from cleared Iberian Templars, continuously seated at Templar sites (Castro Marim then Tomar from 1356/1357), and the institutional vehicle through which the Templar visual identity ([[cross-order-of-christ|Cross of the Order of Christ]]) became globally visible on the sails of Portuguese exploration caravels during the Age of Discoveries.
   - 18th-c. Masonic revival: the **Strict Templar Observance** (Karl Gotthelf von Hund, ~1751) claims Templar lineage for high-degree Freemasonry — a claim that is unrecoverable as history but documentable as reception.
   - Modern legendary continuation: the Templar-as-Gnostic-or-Grail-keeper genre (von Hammer-Purgstall 1818 *Mysterium Baphometis revelatum* → Eliphas Lévi → 20th-c. Holy-Blood-Holy-Grail literature → mass-market modern). See [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis]] for the historiographical state-of-debate and [[baphomet-controversy]] for the iconographic strand.

## Core texts
- [[phase-5-036-rule-of-templars-latin]] — the Latin Rule of 1129 (Council of Troyes). Later expanded with French additions (the *Retraits* / *Statutes* and the *Penances*).
- [[phase-5-037-bernard-de-laude-novae-militiae]] — *In Praise of the New Knighthood* (Bernard of Clairvaux, ~1129–1136). The theological manifesto.
- [[phase-5-038-chinon-parchment-1308]] — papal absolution document from Chinon, 1308; lost in the Vatican Secret Archive misfiling for nearly seven centuries; rediscovered 2001 by Barbara Frale.
- **Trial transcripts** — Paris, Poitiers, Chinon, London, Aragonese, German tribunal records, 1307–1311. The *Processus Contra Templarios* (Frale 2007 critical edition).
- **The Templar New Testament/Liturgical books** — Templar churches followed standard Latin liturgy; no separate "Templar Bible" exists, contrary to popular legend.

## Defining doctrines and practices
- **Combined monastic and military vow.** Poverty, chastity, obedience — *plus* commitment to armed combat in defense of pilgrims and the Holy Land. This *novum* required Bernard's *De Laude Novae Militiae* as theological justification.
- **Direct papal allegiance.** The Order answered to the Pope, not to local bishops — granted by Innocent II's *Omne datum optimum* (1139). This jurisdictional independence both empowered the Order and made enemies of every local episcopate.
- **Marian devotion.** Strong Cistercian-Bernardine Marian piety, threaded through the Rule.
- **The Temple Church round-form.** Templar churches were frequently built in a circular plan ("Templum Domini" — the Dome of the Rock — was identified by the crusaders as the actual Solomonic Temple, and many Templar foundation-churches imitated this round form: Temple Church London, Tomar in Portugal, Laon in France).
- **The white mantle with red cross.** Granted by Eugenius III at Vézelay in 1147 in preparation for the Second Crusade. The visual identity that becomes globally iconic.

## Relations to neighbors
- **vs. [[tradition-hospitallers]] (Knights of St John):** parallel military-religious order, often rivals, but theologically and institutionally similar. The Hospitallers absorbed most Templar assets in 1312 (*Ad providam*) and survived as the Order of Malta into the present.
- **vs. [[tradition-teutonic-knights]]:** parallel German-Latin military order founded 1190; relocated focus to the Baltic Crusades; survived as a sovereign monastic state to 1525.
- **vs. [[tradition-catharism]]:** the Templars had substantial holdings in Languedoc-Occitan territory before, during, and after the [[event-albigensian-crusade-1209-1229|Albigensian Crusade]]. **They are notably absent from the Crusader armies.** Whether this absence reflects active sympathy, prudent neutrality, mere preoccupation with Outremer, or local landlord-tenant complexity is debated. Both Barber 1994 and Nicholson 2001 caution against the Cathar-Templar-sympathy thesis (no documentary basis) while acknowledging the suggestive geographic overlap.
- **vs. [[tradition-mandaeism]]:** physical contact between Templar garrisons in Outremer and the Aramaic-speaking *nasoraean* communities of the Mesopotamian marshes is geographically plausible but textually undocumented. The Johannite-Templar legend (Templars as inheritors of a John-the-Baptist-not-Jesus Christianity preserved by the Mandaeans) is an early-19th-c. construction (Lévi, Hammer-Purgstall) and has no medieval evidentiary basis. **Label as `[claim: pseudoscholarship]` when stating it without that disclaimer.**
- **vs. [[tradition-islam]] (Sufi orders, Nizari Ismaili "Assassins"):** Templars negotiated, fought, and occasionally allied with Muslim powers (e.g., truce networks with Damascus). Idries Shah's claim of direct Sufi-Templar doctrinal transmission (*The Sufis*, 1964) is Tier 3 / Tier 4 and not supported by the consensus.
- **vs. [[tradition-freemasonry]]:** the Strict Templar Observance (von Hund ~1751) and other 18th-c. Templar-revival rites assert Templar succession but cannot demonstrate it. As a *reception* fact, however, the Masonic appropriation of Templar iconography is a major 18th-21st c. religious-cultural phenomenon.

## Connections — the load-bearing edges

- **→ [[bernard-of-clairvaux]] / [[phase-5-037-bernard-de-laude-novae-militiae]]** *direct-quote*: Bernard's *De Laude Novae Militiae* is the founding theological text. The Order's self-understanding is unintelligible without Bernardine Cistercian theology.
- **→ [[event-council-of-troyes-1129]]** *founding*: the Order is canonically constituted here.
- **→ [[event-first-crusade-1095-1099]]** *direct-causation*: the Order exists because the First Crusade created the pilgrim-protection problem.
- **→ [[event-fall-of-acre-1291]]** *direct-causation*: the loss of the Holy Land is the *de facto* end of the Order's founding purpose.
- **→ [[event-trial-of-templars-1307-1314]]** + **→ [[event-council-of-vienne-1311-1312]]** *direct-causation*: the Order is dissolved through these two linked events.
- **→ [[tradition-freemasonry]]** *reception-claim*: 18th-c. Masonic Templar revivalism (Strict Observance, Scottish Rite Templar grades). Reception, not historical succession.
- **→ [[baphomet-controversy]]** *controversy-edge*: the "Baphomet" charge is the single most influential reception-element of the Templar suppression.
- **→ [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis]]** *contested-edge*: the central modern speculative-historical claim about the Order.

## Disputes

- **Did the Templars hold heretical / Gnostic doctrine?** The consensus position of post-1980 critical scholarship (Barber, Nicholson, Forey, Demurger) is: **the trial confessions are not credible historical evidence of Templar doctrine.** Torture-extracted, jurisdiction-shopped (Philip IV's inquisitors had pre-prepared the charges), and contradictory across geographies (English and German tribunals largely *did not* obtain confessions matching the French formulary). Barbara Frale's reading of the Chinon Parchment (Frale 2004, 2007) interprets the "denial of Christ" element as a documented *internal initiation drill* meant to harden knights against the trauma of capture and renunciation-under-duress — a defensible reading of the strongest contemporary witness. **Position to label clearly:** the *historical* Templars were Latin Catholic Christians with conventional medieval Latin theology; the *legendary* Templars are a Gnostic-Hermetic-Johannite construct of the 18th-21st c.
- **Was the Order destroyed for its money?** A widely-held but partially-revisionist position. Philip IV's debt to the Order, his prior expulsion of the Jews (1306) and Lombards (also for fiscal reasons), and his subsequent appropriation of Templar treasure all support a fiscal-motive reading. But the *speed* and *coordination* of the 1307 arrests also indicate a politically pre-prepared move; and the support of his Dominican confessor Guillaume de Paris (the chief Inquisitor of France) suggests an ideological-political alliance with the mendicant inquisitorial apparatus. Best reading: layered motives. Barber 2006 ch. 1–3.
- **Was anything *unusual* in the Templar internal rite?** The probable elements of the trial-confessions that have some non-zero defensible-evidence basis (Frale 2007): an admission-rite that included *denial of Christ* + *spitting on the cross* + a kiss of obedience. Frale interprets these as *prophylactic against capture-trauma*. Others (Demurger, Read) read them as nothing at all — a complete fabrication of inquisitors. The consensus is closer to "fabrication or extremely marginal ritual practice misread by hostile inquisitors."
- **Could the Templars have been preserved any Levantine doctrine?** This is the load-bearing edge for John's "MASSIVE win" framing. *Geographically*, yes — sustained physical presence in Outremer ~1119–1291 included contact with Coptic, Syriac, Maronite, Mandaean, Druze, Sunni, and Nizari Ismaili communities. *Textually*, no — no Templar-authored document survives that engages any Eastern theology, and the trial confessions do not match any Eastern doctrine. The honest position: the *opportunity* was real, the *evidence of transmission* is absent.
