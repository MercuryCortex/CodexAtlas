# Sumerian / Mesopotamian Religion

## Overview
The religious culture of the city-states and empires of the Tigris-Euphrates valley, from the late fourth millennium BCE (with proto-cuneiform writing) through the late first millennium BCE (when Akkadian gave way to Aramaic and Greek as spoken languages, and the cuneiform tradition died out by ~100 BCE). The tradition is a bilingual continuum: **Sumerian** (a language isolate) is the older liturgical and literary prestige language; **Akkadian** (a Semitic language with Old Akkadian, Old/Middle/Standard Babylonian, and Old/Middle/Neo-Assyrian dialect strata) is the spoken-and-written language of most of the historical period. The same gods and myths are transmitted in both languages, with Akkadian-language theology absorbing and re-articulating Sumerian originals. This is the world's longest-continuous documented religious tradition before its end — over three thousand years of cuneiform religious literature.

## Historical phases
1. **Late Uruk / Jemdet Nasr** (~3500–2900 BCE) — proto-cuneiform; earliest temple economies and divine names.
2. **Early Dynastic** (~2900–2334 BCE) — first religious literature ([[phase-1-001-kesh-temple-hymn]], [[phase-1-005-instructions-of-shuruppak]]); the classical Sumerian city-state pantheon.
3. **Sargonic / Akkadian Empire** (~2334–2154 BCE) — first multi-ethnic empire; rise of Akkadian; [[enheduanna]]'s hymns.
4. **Ur III** (~2112–2004 BCE) — Sumerian renaissance; royal hymns to Shulgi and his successors.
5. **Old Babylonian** (~2000–1595 BCE) — Hammurabi; scribal canonization of Sumerian literature; integrated Akkadian Gilgamesh; [[phase-1-006-atrahasis]]; [[phase-1-007-code-of-hammurabi]].
6. **Middle / Late Bronze** (~1595–1155 BCE) — Kassite Babylon; [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] in its final form; international Akkadian as Late Bronze diplomatic lingua franca.
7. **Neo-Assyrian** (~911–609 BCE) — imperial religion centered on Aššur; Library of [[ashurbanipal]] at Nineveh preserves much of the literature.
8. **Neo-Babylonian** (~626–539 BCE) — Nebuchadnezzar II; Nabonidus's controversial Sin-of-Harran promotion.
9. **Persian, Hellenistic, Parthian periods** (~539 BCE – CE 100) — gradual decline; the latest known cuneiform astronomical text is from ~75 CE.

## Core texts
- [[phase-1-001-kesh-temple-hymn]] — Early Dynastic temple hymn; among the oldest religious texts.
- [[phase-1-003-enheduanna-hymns]] — first named author's hymns to Inanna and the Sumerian temples.
- [[phase-1-005-instructions-of-shuruppak]] — Sumerian wisdom literature.
- [[phase-1-004-gilgamesh-old-babylonian]] — Akkadian epic; the OB integrated version of the Sumerian Bilgames poems.
- [[phase-1-006-atrahasis]] — Akkadian creation-flood epic.
- [[phase-1-007-code-of-hammurabi]] — Old Babylonian royal law.
- [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] — Babylonian creation epic.
- (later periods) the Mesopotamian wisdom corpus (*Ludlul bel nemeqi*, *Babylonian Theodicy*), the great divinatory series, royal annals — to be cataloged in later phases.

## Pantheon
- [[an-sumerian]] — sky-father, nominal head.
- [[enlil]] — chief executive god; Nippur.
- [[enki-ea]] — wisdom-and-water god; Eridu.
- [[ninhursag-nintud]] — mother-goddess; birth.
- [[inanna-sumerian]] / [[ishtar-akkadian]] — love and war; Uruk / Akkad / Nineveh.
- [[nanna-sin]] — moon; Ur, Harran.
- [[utu-shamash]] — sun and justice; Larsa, Sippar.
- [[marduk]] — Babylonian high god from Old Babylonian period.
- [[dumuzi-tammuz]] — dying-rising vegetation god; consort of Inanna/Ishtar.
- [[tiamat]], [[apsu]], [[kingu]] — chaos and rebellion figures of the Enuma Elish cosmogony.

## Defining doctrines
- **Polytheism with civic-patron deities.** Each city has a patron god; theology is organized around city-temple economies.
- **Cosmos as ordered through divine combat or differentiation.** Chaos must be defeated or transformed (chaoskampf in [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]]; primordial waters partitioned).
- **Anthropology of service.** Humans are created to serve the gods — to provide labor (irrigation, agriculture) and offerings (food, beer). The anthropology is duty-based, not soteriological.
- **Divine kingship.** The king is the gods' earthly representative; his legitimacy comes from divine election; his function is to maintain the cosmic-political order on behalf of the gods.
- **Wisdom and lament.** Mesopotamian wisdom literature (*Ludlul*, *Theodicy*, *Dialogue of Pessimism*) probes the problem of innocent suffering long before Job.
- **Afterlife: minimal.** The dead descend to a shadowy, dusty underworld; no judgment, no reward, no rebirth (contrast Egypt). Death is the great limit.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-canaanite-ugaritic]]: distinct pantheons, but shared regional patterns — divine council, chaoskampf, sacred marriage, divine kingship. The Late Bronze international system was bilingually Akkadian and West Semitic-aware.
- vs. [[tradition-egyptian]]: parallel ancient cultures, modest direct cross-borrowing, but striking shared structural patterns and significant Late Bronze contact (e.g., [[phase-1-012-amarna-letters]]).
- vs. early Israelite religion: the strongest documented influence on the Hebrew Bible's primeval history (Gen 1–11) — creation, flood, antediluvians, the Tower of Babel. Mediation chain debated.
