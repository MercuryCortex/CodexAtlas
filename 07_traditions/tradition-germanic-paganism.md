Germanic paganism is the pre-Christian religious tradition of the Germanic-speaking peoples (including Goths, Franks, Saxons, Angles, Frisians, and the Norse). It shares core mythological features with Norse religion (Odin/Woden, Thor/Donar, Tiwaz/Tyr) while showing regional variation across the Germanic world.

The tradition is most fully preserved in Old Norse sources (Eddas, sagas) but also attested in Continental Germanic contexts (Merseburg Charms, place-name evidence, Tacitus's *Germania* c. 98 CE).

## Sacred Script

The Elder Futhark runic alphabet was the shared sacred writing system across Germanic-speaking peoples. See [[alphabet-elder-futhark]] for the operative theory of writing — runes as magical instruments, Odin's sacrifice for the alphabet.
