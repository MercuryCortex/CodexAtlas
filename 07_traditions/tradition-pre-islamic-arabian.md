# Pre-Islamic Arabian religion

## Overview
The diverse religious traditions of the Arabian peninsula in the millennium before Islam (c. -1000 to 630 CE), spanning **South Arabian** state religions (Sabaic / Minaean / Qatabanian / Himyarite Yemen, with their astral high gods *Almaqah*, *Wadd*, *ʿAmm*, *Sīn*); the **Nabataean** caravan-kingdom religion (Petra, Hegra — Dushara / Allat / al-ʿUzzā); **Palmyrene** religion at the Syrian-Arabian frontier (Bel / Baalshamin / Allat-Athena); **Safaitic / Hismaic / Thamudic** desert religion (~30,000+ rock inscriptions, c. -100 to 400 CE — chiefly invoking the high god *Lt* "the Goddess" = Allat, *Rḍw* "Ruda," *Bʿls¹mn* Baalshamin); and the **Hijazi** religious system in immediate pre-Islamic Mecca and Medina (the high god *Allāh* with the three goddess-daughters al-Lāt / al-ʿUzzā / Manāt, [[hubal]] inside the Kaaba, multiple lesser deities including [[wadd]] / *Suwāʿ* / *Yaghūth* / *Yaʿūq* / [[nasr-pre-islamic]] preserved as a fossilized antediluvian pantheon at Quran 71:23). Islam emerges from within this religious matrix in the early seventh century CE; the [[phase-4-034-quran|Quran]] is the principal Islamic-period textual witness to pre-Islamic Arabian religion, attested through its polemical engagement with it.

## Historical phases
1. **Bronze Age — early Iron Age South Arabian state religions** (c. -1000 onward) — Sabaean astral religion (Almaqah moon-god at Marib), Minaean (Wadd moon-god at Maʿīn), Qatabanian (ʿAmm), Hadramaut (Sīn). Monumental temple architecture (Marib dam-temple, ʿAlmaqah temple at Sirwah).
2. **Hellenistic-Roman period, north and central Arabia** (c. -300 to 400 CE) — Nabataean kingdom (Dushara / Allat / al-ʿUzzā / Manawat at Petra and Hegra), Palmyrene cosmopolitan religion (Bel-Yarhibol-Aglibol triad; Baalshamin; Allat-Athena), and the Safaitic / Hismaic / Thamudic graffito-religious culture of the nomadic / semi-nomadic desert population.
3. **Late antiquity in Yemen** (c. 300-630 CE) — Himyarite kingdom converts to a Jewish-style monotheism c. 380 ("Rahmanan" inscriptions, Lecker; Robin); the Christian Aksumite invasion under Kaleb defeats the Jewish-Himyarite king Dhū Nuwās at the Najran-massacre context in 525 ([[event-kaleb-aksum-himyar-invasion-525]] — already wired by `opus-ethiopian-1`); Abreha governs Yemen as Aksumite proxy and (per Islamic tradition) attempts the *Year of the Elephant* expedition against Mecca c. 570 (Quran 105 — *al-Fīl*).
4. **Immediately pre-Islamic Hijaz** (c. 500-622 CE) — the Quraysh-controlled Meccan sanctuary system (Kaaba with [[hubal]] as principal cult-image and the three goddesses al-Lāt / al-ʿUzzā / Manāt as "daughters of Allāh"); Medinan tribal religion mixed with substantial Jewish presence (Banu Qaynuqa, Banu Nadir, Banu Qurayza); Christian and Jewish presence in Najran (south Hijaz) and the Syrian-frontier oases; the *[[hanif-monotheism|ḥunafāʾ]]* tradition (Islamic-tradition memory of pre-Islamic Arabian Abrahamic-style monotheists: Waraqa ibn Nawfal, Zayd ibn ʿAmr ibn Nufayl, Quss ibn Saʿida, Khalid ibn Sinan).
5. **Quranic polemic and the conquest of Mecca, 630 CE** ([[event-conquest-of-mecca-630]]) — Muhammad's destruction of the Kaaba idols including Hubal; the Quranic polemic against pre-Islamic Meccan religion is concentrated in the Meccan suras (especially Sura 53 *al-Najm* against the three goddesses, Sura 71 *Nūḥ* on the antediluvian pantheon, Sura 105 *al-Fīl* on the Year of the Elephant).

## Core texts (epigraphic and external — there is no pre-Islamic Arabian textual canon)
- [[phase-4-034-quran]] — the principal Islamic-period textual witness, by polemical engagement. Names al-Lāt / al-ʿUzzā / Manāt (53:19-22), Wadd / Suwāʿ / Yaghūth / Yaʿūq / Nasr (71:23), the Year of the Elephant (sura 105), the *ḥunafāʾ* (2:135, 3:67, 3:95, 6:79, 6:161, 16:120-123) and Abrahamic origins of the Kaaba (2:127, 3:96-97), the Sabians as *ahl al-kitāb* (2:62, 5:69, 22:17).
- **Nabataean inscriptions** (c. -200 to 100 CE) — Petra, Hegra (Madāʾin Sāliḥ), Sinai, Bostra. Aramaic-Nabataean dedications to Dushara, Allat, al-ʿUzzā, Manawat. Healey 2001.
- **Palmyrene inscriptions** (1st-3rd c. CE) — bilingual Aramaic-Greek; Bel-Yarhibol-Aglibol, Baalshamin, Allat-Athena temples. Drijvers 1976; Kaizer 2002.
- **Safaitic / Hismaic / Thamudic rock graffiti** (~30,000+ inscriptions, c. -100 to 400 CE) — the densest epigraphic record of any ancient nomadic society. Macdonald 2000; the Online Corpus of the Inscriptions of Ancient North Arabia (OCIANA, Oxford).
- **South Arabian (Sabaic, Minaean, Qatabanian, Himyaritic) inscriptions** — corpus of monumental and dedicatory texts. Robin 2015; Beeston-Ghul-Müller-Ryckmans dictionary.
- **Ibn al-Kalbi *Kitāb al-Aṣnām*** (~9th c. CE) — later Islamic-tradition reconstructive catalog of pre-Islamic Arabian idols. Tier-4 source-critically per Hawting 1999; valuable as Islamic-tradition reception-history of pre-Islamic cult.
- **Greek-period external attestations** — Herodotus *Histories* 1.131 + 3.8 (5th c. BCE, Alilat = Aphrodite Urania, Orotalt = Dionysus); Strabo *Geography* book 16; Diodorus Siculus on the Nabataeans; Pliny *Natural History* book 6.

## Pantheon (selected — see individual deity nodes for evidence)
- **High gods.** [[allah]] (Hijazi high god); Dushara (Nabataean); Bel / Baalshamin (Palmyrene, Syrian-Arabian); Almaqah (Sabaean Yemen, moon-god); ʿAmm (Qatabanian); Sīn (Hadramaut, moon-god); the Himyarite *Rahmanan* (late-antique Yemen monotheist high god).
- **The three "daughters of Allāh" in Hijaz** (Quran 53:19-22). [[allat|al-Lāt]] (Taif cult-stone), [[al-uzza|al-ʿUzzā]] (Nakhla sanctuary), [[manat|Manāt]] (Qudayd between Mecca and Medina).
- **The Kaaba pantheon.** [[hubal]] (principal cult-image inside the Kaaba); the Islamic-tradition memory of 360 idols at the Kaaba (likely stylized).
- **The Quran 71:23 antediluvian pantheon.** [[wadd]] (Sabaean / Minaean moon-god, preserved in Sura 71 as one of the gods worshipped in Noah's time); *Suwāʿ*; *Yaghūth*; *Yaʿūq*; [[nasr-pre-islamic|Nasr]] (south Arabian vulture-god). The Quranic preservation of this list is the principal Islamic-tradition memory of pre-Islamic Arabian polytheism.
- **Safaitic / Hismaic graffito gods.** Allat is dominant; *Rḍw* (Ruda — twilight star?); *Bʿls¹mn* (Baalshamin); *Gd* (luck/fortune); *Ds²r* (Dushara). Macdonald 2000.

## Defining features
- **No single pan-Arabian religion** — instead a network of regional cults with shared Semitic theological vocabulary, varying sanctuary-and-pilgrimage practices, and overlapping high-god / consort / astral / fortune / oracular structure.
- **Aniconic *baetyls*** — the dominant Arabian cult-image is a sacred standing stone, not anthropomorphic statuary. Allat at Taif, Dushara, the Black Stone of the Kaaba — all baetyl-form. (Hubal is the exception: an anthropomorphic image, possibly Nabataean / Levantine import.)
- **Astral theology** — moon-god high deities in Yemen (Almaqah, Wadd, Sīn); solar / Venus / triple-goddess patterns elsewhere.
- **Sanctuary + pilgrimage system** — the Hijazi *ḥajj* and *ʿumrah* pilgrimages have pre-Islamic origins; the Quranic *manāsik al-ḥajj* (rites of pilgrimage) preserves pre-Islamic ritual structures within an Abrahamic-monotheist reframing (Mecca = Abrahamic sanctuary at Quran 2:127, 3:96-97).
- **Oath / divination culture** — arrow-divination (*qidāḥ*) at Hubal's image (per Islamic-tradition memory); seer-poet (*kāhin*) oracular speech; tribal honor-oath rituals.
- **Tribal genealogy as theological framework** — the *qabīla* (tribe) is the religious-as-much-as-political unit; sanctuary protection (*ḥaram*), blood-feud and blood-money, oath-binding.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-judaism]] — Jewish populations were present in Hijazi oases (Khaybar, Yathrib / Medina with the Banu Qaynuqa / Nadir / Qurayza), in Najran-Yemen, and the Himyarite kingdom converted to a Jewish-style monotheism c. 380 (*Rahmanan* inscriptions). The Talmudic-and-Targumic narrative repertoire is deeply present in the Quranic engagement with biblical figures.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]] — Christian populations were present at Najran (Yemen, martyred 523 under Dhū Nuwās), along the Syrian frontier (Ghassanid Monophysite Arabs), and in caravan-trade contact across the peninsula. Syriac Christological controversies provide the religious background for the Quranic engagement with Jesus and Mary (Griffith 2013; Reynolds 2010).
- vs. [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]] — Christian Aksumite Ethiopia invades Himyar in 525 ([[event-kaleb-aksum-himyar-invasion-525]]); first Muslim hijra to Aksum c. 615 (per Islamic-tradition narrative); Quran 105 (the *Sūrat al-Fīl*) remembers the Aksumite-Yemeni Abreha's elephant-army expedition against Mecca c. 570 — the most direct Aksumite-Ethiopia → emerging-Islam edge.
- vs. [[tradition-zoroastrianism]] — Sasanian-Persian political and religious influence in eastern Arabia (Bahrayn) and Yemen (Sasanian conquest of Yemen 570 expelling the Aksumites); the Sasanian-Byzantine wars exhausting both empires before the Islamic conquests is the standard late-antique geopolitical frame.
- vs. [[tradition-canaanite-ugaritic]] — deep Northwest-Semitic linguistic and theological continuity (the Semitic ʾl / ʾlh / Allāh; ʾlt / Allāt; ʾttr / ʿAṯtar; Baal; Hadad-storm-god patterns).
- vs. [[tradition-mesopotamian]] — Akkadian / Babylonian astral theology and divination practices substantially influence south Arabian and broader Semitic religious culture.
- vs. [[tradition-mandaeism]] — the *Sabians* of the Quran (2:62, 5:69, 22:17) — protected *ahl al-kitāb* — are most often identified with the Mandaeans (continuous community in southern Mesopotamia / Khuzestan) or with the late-antique Sabians of Harran; both identifications have late-antique evidence.

## Source-critical note
Pre-Islamic Arabian religion is one of the most epistemically fraught areas in late-antique religious studies because **the principal narrative sources are Islamic-period reconstructions** with strong polemical interests in constructing pre-Islamic Arabian *jāhiliyya* ("ignorance / pre-Islamic barbarism") as the foil for Islamic monotheism. Hawting 1999 is the load-bearing methodological intervention: read Ibn al-Kalbi etc. as Islamic-tradition reception-history, not as transparent attestation. The contemporary epigraphic and archaeological record (Nabataean, Palmyrene, Safaitic, South Arabian, Himyarite) provides the firm primary attestation; the Islamic-tradition narrative material provides the polemical-interpretive layer that the Quran itself substantially shaped.
