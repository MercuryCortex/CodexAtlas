# Mesoamerican Religion

## Overview
The pan-regional religious-cultural complex shared across Mesoamerica from the Olmec horizon (~1500 BCE) through the Spanish conquest (1521 in central Mexico; 1697 for the last Maya stronghold at Tayasal). Mesoamerica encompasses multiple distinct ethnic-political traditions — Olmec, Teotihuacan, Zapotec, Maya, Toltec, Mixtec, Aztec/Mexica — sharing fundamental religious-cultural features that have warranted the "Mesoamerica" cultural-religious areal designation since Paul Kirchhoff's 1943 article. Defining shared features: dual calendar (260-day ritual *tonalpohualli* / *tzolkin* + 365-day solar *xiuhpohualli* / *haab* meshing in a 52-year cycle); pantheon including pan-Mesoamerican Feathered Serpent (Quetzalcoatl / Kukulkán / Q'uq'umatz), Rain God (Tlaloc / Chaac / Cocijo), Maize deity, life-and-death duality; cyclical cosmogony of successive cosmic ages; ritual bloodletting and (especially in late-Postclassic central Mexico) large-scale human sacrifice; ballgame as ritual-cosmological practice; pyramid-temple architecture organized around astronomical alignments; the painted-codex book tradition.

## Historical phases
1. **Olmec (~1500–400 BCE)** — "mother culture"; San Lorenzo, La Venta; jaguar imagery and proto-Feathered-Serpent iconography; the iconographic substrate for much subsequent Mesoamerican religion.
2. **Late Preclassic (~400 BCE–250 CE)** — San Bartolo Maya murals (~100 BCE) attest the Maize God and proto-Hero-Twins iconography; Teotihuacan rises.
3. **Classic (~250–900 CE)** — Teotihuacan's height (Temple of the Feathered Serpent ~150 CE); Classic Maya florescence (Tikal, Palenque, Copán, Calakmul); extensive hieroglyphic religious-political inscriptions; ballcourt complexes.
4. **Postclassic (~900–1521)** — Toltec at Tula (~900–1150); Chichén Itzá Maya-Toltec synthesis; Mixtec codex traditions; Aztec Triple Alliance (1428+); the surviving pre-Columbian codices are mostly Postclassic.
5. **Conquest (1519–1521 + extended)** — Cortés conquers Tenochtitlan 1521; Yucatán conquered 1527–1546; last independent Maya kingdom (Itzá at Tayasal) falls 1697.
6. **Colonial ethnographic recovery (~1524–1700)** — Sahagún, Durán, Landa, Ávila ethnographies; Indigenous-authored texts (Popol Vuh, Chilam Balam, Huarochirí, Guaman Poma); foundational for modern Mesoamerican religious-historical knowledge.
7. **Modern (19th–21st c.)** — archaeological recovery (Stephens-Catherwood 1840s; subsequent vast expansion); hieroglyphic decipherment (Knorozov 1952 → Schele, Stuart, Houston); contemporary Indigenous Maya religious revitalization in Guatemala and Mexico.

## Pantheon (pan-Mesoamerican core)
- Feathered Serpent — [[quetzalcoatl]] / [[kukulkan]] (creator-civilizer / wind)
- Rain God — [[tlaloc]] (central Mexican) / [[chac]] (Maya)
- Sun — [[tonatiuh]] (Aztec) / Kinich Ahau (Maya)
- Underworld — [[mictlantecuhtli]] (Aztec) / God D-A complex (Maya)
- Mexica-specific — [[huitzilopochtli]], [[tezcatlipoca]], [[coatlicue]]
- Maya-specific — [[itzamna]], [[ixchel]]

## Defining doctrines
- Dual calendar with 52-year meshing cycle
- Cyclical cosmogony — successive cosmic ages
- *Tonalli* / *k'inan* — personal-destiny / soul-energy concept
- Sacred geography organized around four cardinal directions + center
- Sacrifice as cosmic-political technology
- Ballcourt as cosmic-ritual space
- Codex-painting and hieroglyphic writing

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-inca-andean]]: separate continental religious-cultural development; comparative parallels (calendar elaboration, monumental architecture, ancestor cult) are typological.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: post-1521 catastrophic forced Christianization; subsequent extensive syncretic forms (Quetzalcoatl-Christ; Coatlicue-Tonantzin-Guadalupe; Maximón / San Simón).
- vs. [[tradition-mormonism]] (Phase 7 cross-current): Book of Mormon's "Lamanite" claims about Indigenous American descent from Israelites is a Phase-7 appropriation of Mesoamerican peoples; substantially DNA-disconfirmed.
