# Buddhism

## Overview
Buddhism is the religious tradition stemming from the teaching of Siddhārtha Gautama, the Buddha, in the Gangetic plain ~-450 BCE. Its central doctrines are: (a) all phenomena are *anitya* (impermanent), *duḥkha* (suffering / unsatisfactoriness), and *anātman* (without enduring self); (b) the cause of suffering is craving / clinging; (c) liberation from suffering is possible via the cessation of craving; (d) the path to that cessation is the Noble Eightfold Path. The tradition is non-theistic in its early forms (no supreme creator god), holds karma and rebirth as cosmic givens, and offers liberation (*nirvāṇa*) as exit from the rebirth-cycle.

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-sectarian Early Buddhism (~-450 to -250)** — Buddha's teaching career, formation of the Saṅgha, oral transmission, first councils.
2. **Sectarian period (~-250 to +100)** — Schisms producing the "Eighteen Schools" (Theravāda, Sarvāstivāda, Mahāsāṅghika, etc.); Aśoka's patronage (~-250); spread to Sri Lanka and Central Asia.
3. **Mahāyāna emergence (~+100 to +500)** — Mahāyāna sūtras; new bodhisattva ideal; Madhyamaka (Nāgārjuna) and Yogācāra (Asaṅga, Vasubandhu) philosophies. (Phase 4 work.)
4. **Tantric / Vajrayāna (~+500 to +1200)** — Tantric Buddhism in India; spread to Tibet (~7th c. onward).
5. **East Asian and Tibetan elaborations** — Chan / Zen, Pure Land, Tendai, Shingon, Tibetan schools.
6. **Decline in India (~+1200)** — Muslim invasions and internal absorption into Hindu traditions.
7. **Modern (~+1850 onward)** — Western encounter, modernist reform movements, global spread.

- [[phase-4-075-mulamadhyamakakarika]] — Nāgārjuna's MMK.

## Defining doctrines
- **The [[four-noble-truths|Four Noble Truths]]** — suffering, its cause (craving), its cessation, the path.
- **[[noble-eightfold-path|Noble Eightfold Path]]** — right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration.
- **Three Marks of Existence** — *anicca* (impermanence), *dukkha* (suffering), *anattā* ([[anatman-no-self|anātman]]).
- **Dependent origination** ([[pratitya-samutpada]]) — phenomena arise interdependently, no independent self-existence.
- **Karma and rebirth** — shared with surrounding śramaṇa and Brahmanical traditions, but with the *anātman* twist.
- **Nirvāṇa** — extinction of craving, liberation.
- **In Mahāyāna**: [[bodhisattva-vow]] — universal liberation as soteriological goal; [[emptiness-sunyata|śūnyatā]] as metaphysical extension.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-vedic-hinduism]]: shared rebirth / karma framework; opposed on *ātman*, Vedic authority, sacrificial ritual. Long historical entanglement: Buddha addresses brahmins; later Hinduism absorbs Buddha as an avatāra of Viṣṇu (a polemical incorporation).
- vs. [[tradition-jainism]]: closest sister tradition. Same milieu (Greater Magadha), same period (~-500), shared rejection of Vedic authority and brahmanic ritual, shared ascetic ideal. Differ on metaphysics (Jain *jīva* vs. Buddhist *anātman*) and ethics (Jain *ahiṃsā* even stricter).
- vs. [[tradition-greek-religion]] / Hellenistic philosophy: Indo-Greek contact in Bactria from ~-300; some scholars argue Pyrrhonist skepticism shows Buddhist influence (Beckwith 2015, contested).
