# Santería / Lucumí (Regla de Ocha)

## Overview
The Cuban Yoruba-Catholic syncretic religion, also called *Regla de Ocha* (the Rule of the Orishas), *La Regla Lucumí*, or popularly Santería ("way of the saints"). Emerged in 19th-c. Cuba among enslaved Yoruba people (especially after the collapse of the Oyo Empire ~1836 sent large Yoruba populations to Cuba) and free Afro-Cubans, syncretizing the Yoruba orisha system with Cuban Catholic saint-cult. The associated *Regla de Ifá* (Ifá-practice) is reserved for male *babaláwo*; everyday Santería practice uses the *Dilogún* (sixteen-cowries) oracle. Each orisha has Catholic-saint pairings (Obatalá ↔ Las Mercedes; Oshún ↔ Caridad del Cobre; Shangó ↔ Santa Bárbara; Yemayá ↔ Virgen de Regla; etc.).

## Historical phases
1. **Emergence (~1800–1880)** — Yoruba enslaved populations and free Afro-Cubans synthesize Yoruba religion with Cuban Catholicism within Spanish-Catholic *cabildos de nación* (ethnic-religious confraternities).
2. **Post-emancipation (1886+) and Republic period (1898–1959)** — Santería persists as primarily Afro-Cuban religion, periodically suppressed; Fernando Ortiz's pioneering (initially racist, later more sympathetic) work; Lydia Cabrera's *El Monte* (1954).
3. **Cuban Revolution and after (1959–present)** — initial revolutionary religious suppression; gradual accommodation; post-Soviet period explosion of public Afro-Cuban religious visibility.
4. **Diaspora and US legal-religious establishment (1959–1993)** — Cuban diaspora carries Santería to the US; *Church of the Lukumi Babalu Aye v. Hialeah* (1993) US Supreme Court landmark religious-freedom decision.
5. **Trans-Atlantic re-integration (1980s+)** — Cuban *babaláwo* and Yoruba ritual specialists exchanging visits.

## Pantheon
- *Orishas* (Yoruba origin, Cuban modification):
  - [[obatala]] (white-cloth) — Las Mercedes
  - [[oshun]] (yellow, river, love) — Caridad del Cobre
  - [[shango]] (red-white, thunder) — Santa Bárbara
  - [[yemoja]] (blue, sea) — Virgen de Regla
  - [[ogun]] (green-black, iron) — San Pedro / Santiago
  - [[eshu]]/Elegguá (red-black, crossroads) — Niño de Atocha / San Antonio
  - [[oya]] (multicolored, wind/cemetery) — Candelaria
  - Babalú-Ayé (healing, smallpox) — San Lázaro
  - Olófin (epithet of Olódùmarè in Lucumí)

## Defining doctrines
- Layered cosmology (Olófin → orishas → humans)
- Catholic-saint syncretic pairings (theologically internalized)
- *Asiento* initiation — "being seated" with one's principal orisha; week-long, expensive
- *Padrino/madrina* ritual-kinship lineages
- *Dilogún* and *Ifá* divinatory technologies
- Animal sacrifice as legitimate ritual practice (vindicated *Lukumi Babalu Aye v. Hialeah* 1993)

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-yoruba-ifa]]: Lukumí is the closest Caribbean preservation of Yoruba liturgy; ongoing exchange.
- vs. [[tradition-vodou-haitian]]: sister Caribbean African-diasporic tradition; different substrate (Vodou is more Fon-Kongo; Santería more purely Yoruba).
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: theologically genuine Catholic syncretism; many practitioners simultaneously practice Catholic devotion.
