# Église Johannite des Chrétiens Primitifs (Johannite Church)

## Overview
The **Johannite Church of the Primitive Christians** (French: *Église Johannite des Chrétiens Primitifs*) is a neo-Templarist Christian denomination founded in **Paris in 1804** by **[[bernard-raymond-fabre-palaprat|Bernard-Raymond Fabré-Palaprat]]** as an integrated **Order of the Temple + Christian denomination** — Fabré-Palaprat simultaneously asserted himself as *Patriarch of the Order of the Temple* (with a forged "Larmenius Charter" claiming Templar-Grand-Master succession from [[jacques-de-molay|Molay]]) and as *Sovereign Pontiff of the Johannite Church* (a primitive-Christian denomination claiming to preserve the *true* Christianity of **John the Baptist** against the institutional Pauline-Petrine Church). **The Johannite-Templar synthesis explicitly bridges three contested sectors**:

1. **The Templar-revival tradition** (claiming continuous succession from the medieval Order through forged documents).
2. **The Mandaean / Baptist-centered Eastern-Christian tradition** (claiming the Templars had carried the *Mandaean John-the-Baptist tradition* from Outremer back to Europe — the historically-unrecoverable [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis|Templar-Gnostic transmission hypothesis]] in one of its principal early-19th-c. articulations).
3. **The Western esoteric / Masonic / Hermetic milieu** (Fabré-Palaprat's circle overlapped institutionally with continental Masonic-Templar-revival networks descending from [[tradition-strict-templar-observance|von Hund's Strict Templar Observance]]).

The Johannite Church is **one of the principal 19th-century institutional bridges** between (a) the 18th-c. Masonic-Templar-revival (von Hund 1751, repudiated at [[event-wilhelmsbad-convent-1782|Wilhelmsbad 1782]]), (b) the contemporary 19th-c. occult Templar literature ([[joseph-von-hammer-purgstall|Hammer-Purgstall]] 1818, [[eliphas-levi|Lévi]] 1854), and (c) modern self-identified neo-Templar organizations. Modern Johannite-tradition continuations exist (small revived churches in France, North America, and Brazil) — none historically continuous with Fabré-Palaprat's 19th-c. organization in any rigorous sense, but real as **reception-history phenomena**.

## Historical phases

1. **Foundation (1804)** — Fabré-Palaprat (a physician and former Catholic seminarian) constitutes the Order of the Temple in Paris with the support of approximately 200 French Masons and ex-Catholic clergy. The **Larmenius Charter** is produced — a forged document dated 1324 claiming continuous Templar-Grand-Master succession from Molay's named successor *Johannes Marcus Larmenius* through 22 Grand Masters to Fabré-Palaprat.
2. **Napoleonic patronage (~1804–1812)** — claimed but unverified attendance by Napoleon Bonaparte. Documentation: Fabré-Palaprat's own *Manuel*; not corroborated by independent sources.
3. **Johannite Church formalization (1812 onward)** — Fabré-Palaprat formally constitutes the Église Johannite as a Christian denomination alongside the Order of the Temple. The two institutional structures share personnel and theology.
4. **Levitikon (1831)** — Fabré-Palaprat publishes his revised Johannite gospel-and-liturgy compilation, with explicit *Mandaean-style* Baptist-centered Christology.
5. **Post-Fabré-Palaprat fragmentation (1838 onward)** — after Fabré-Palaprat's death (18 February 1838), the Order and the Church fragment into multiple claimant-successor lineages.
6. **19th-20th c. continuations** — various small institutional descendants persist; substantial 20th-c. reception in the broader Western esoteric / new-age scene.
7. **Modern revivals (1990s onward)** — small revived Johannite churches in France, North America, Brazil; none historically continuous with the 19th-c. Fabré-Palaprat tradition.

## Core texts
- **Larmenius Charter** (forged, c. 1804) — the principal foundational document.
- **Levitikon** (1831) — Fabré-Palaprat's revised Johannite gospel-and-liturgy.
- **Manuel des Chevaliers de l'Ordre du Temple** (1817 / 1825) — Fabré-Palaprat's Order-of-the-Temple manual.

## Defining doctrines
- **Templar-succession** through the Larmenius Charter (historically forged).
- **John the Baptist as the true Christ-figure** — the Mandaean-resonant Baptist-centered Christianity, claiming the medieval Templars carried this tradition from Outremer back to Europe.
- **Anti-Petrine-Pauline polemic** — the institutional Roman Catholic Church is read as a *deviation* from primitive Johannite Christianity.
- **Templar-Hermetic-Masonic integration** — Fabré-Palaprat's milieu was substantially Masonic-Hermetic-overlapping.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. **[[tradition-knights-templar]]**: the *claimed* parent tradition. No historical succession.
- vs. **[[tradition-mandaeism]]**: the *claimed* Eastern precedent. **No documented Templar-Mandaean contact-and-transmission**; the Mandaean-Johannite-Templar legendary chain is a 19th-c. construction.
- vs. **[[tradition-christianity-canonical]] (Roman Catholic)**: the Johannite Church explicitly positions itself as the *true* Christianity against the institutional Church.
- vs. **[[tradition-strict-templar-observance]]**: institutional-historical predecessor; Fabré-Palaprat's Templar-claim builds on the 18th-c. Masonic-Templar-revival.
- vs. **[[tradition-freemasonry]]**: institutional-network adjacency; many Johannite Church members were also Masons.

## Deviant bridges this tradition opens
- **Templar → Mandaean (Johannite) legendary transmission** — see [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis]]. The Fabré-Palaprat 1804–1838 construction is one of the principal institutional vehicles of this hypothesis.
- **Masonic Templar-revival → 19th-c. occult Templar literature** — Fabré-Palaprat's network overlaps with [[joseph-von-hammer-purgstall|Hammer-Purgstall]]'s and (slightly later) [[eliphas-levi|Lévi]]'s circles.
- **Forged-document tradition** — the Larmenius Charter is one of the canonical 19th-c. esoteric-tradition forgeries (alongside the much-later [[event-priory-of-sion-fabrication|*Dossiers Secrets* of the Priory of Sion]] in the 1960s).

## Disputes
- **The 1804 Larmenius Charter is universally regarded as forgery** by modern critical scholarship. The 1324 date is impossible; the paleography is 19th-c.; no medieval witness exists.
- **Whether Fabré-Palaprat was personally sincere.** Modern scholarship is split: Hanegraaff 2012 leans skeptical (treats Fabré-Palaprat as opportunistic); Collin 1985 is more generous about his religious sincerity.
- **Napoleon's actual involvement.** Unverified by independent sources.
- **Modern Johannite Church continuities.** Various modern claimants; none historically continuous in any rigorous sense, but as **reception-history phenomena** they are real and documented.
