# Australian Aboriginal Religious Traditions

## Overview
The religious traditions of the Aboriginal peoples of Australia — at least 65,000 years of continuous religious-cultural practice on the Australian continent, the longest documented continuous religious tradition of any human population on any landmass. Aboriginal religion is not a single tradition but a family of distinct nation-traditions (200+ pre-contact languages; corresponding plurality of religious traditions). Shared features: the Dreaming / Tjukurpa / Altyerre cosmological category (see [[phase-8-009-dreaming-narratives]]); profound bondedness to specific country (*Ngurra*); ancestral beings whose creative-foundational journeys established country, law, kinship; songline-geographies connecting countries across vast distances; graded initiatory transmission of religious knowledge with restricted-sacred material at higher levels.

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-contact (~65,000 BP – 1788)** — continuous religious-cultural development; archaeological evidence (rock-art, especially Arnhem Land and Kimberley); inter-nation contact and ritual exchange.
2. **Colonial period (1788–1901)** — catastrophic disruption: frontier killings, smallpox epidemics, dispossession of country; Aboriginal religion explicitly suppressed in some colonies.
3. **Protection / Stolen Generations period (~1905–1969)** — [[event-aboriginal-stolen-generations-1905-1969]] — forced child removal directly targeted religious-cultural transmission.
4. **Land-rights and Native-Title era (1976+)** — Aboriginal Land Rights (NT) Act 1976; Mabo (1992); Native Title Act 1993; legal recognition of continuing religious-territorial relationship.
5. **Contemporary (1970s+)** — revitalization movements; the Aboriginal art movement (Papunya Tula 1971+; Yirrkala bark painting); Indigenous-Australian scholars (Marcia Langton, Aileen Moreton-Robinson, Larissa Behrendt, Ani Mikaere) reclaiming interpretive authority.

## Core texts
- [[phase-8-009-dreaming-narratives]] — the Dreaming / Tjukurpa / Altyerre oral tradition.
- Specific iwi/nation textualizations (Strehlow on Arrernte; Munn on Warlpiri; Berndt-Berndt on multiple traditions).

## Pantheon / key figures
- [[rainbow-serpent]] — widely attested across many nations
- [[baiame]] — southeastern Australian sky-father (specific nations only)
- Ancestral beings — nation-specific (e.g., Yiwarra Kuju ancestors in Western Desert; Wandjina in Kimberley; Bunjil among Wurundjeri)

## Defining doctrines
- The Dreaming as "everywhen" (Stanner) — past-and-present simultaneously
- Country (*Ngurra*) as fundamental religious-ontological category
- Songlines / *Yiri* — long-distance ritual-song routes
- Graded initiatory transmission with restricted-sacred levels
- Kinship as religious-cosmological structure

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-polynesian]] / [[tradition-maori]]: separate continental religious traditions; structural parallels (Indigenous song-tradition cosmography) but no historical contact in any direct sense.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: post-1788 mission activity; significant Aboriginal Christian communities (especially Lutheran Hermannsburg / Ntaria; Catholic Tiwi; Anglican Yolŋu); complex syncretic relations.
- Phase-7 misappropriations: Aboriginal religion is regularly misappropriated by New Age / Hancock-style pseudoarchaeology; flagged as Phase-7 misappropriation edge.
