# Tartessos

## Identity
Tartessos is the earliest identifiable complex civilization on the Iberian Peninsula — a wealthy maritime-commercial culture of the southwestern corner of Iberia (lower Guadalquivir valley, Huelva, southern Portugal) that reached its peak in the late Bronze Age and early Iron Age (~900–500 BCE). It is known primarily from: (a) Greek and Roman literary references (*Herodotus* I.163, IV.152; *Strabo* III.2; *Avienus*, *Ora Maritima*); (b) rich archaeological finds of gold and silver metalwork (the *tesoros tartésicos*, including the Carambolo Treasure, the Aliseda Treasure); (c) the **Tartessian script** — an undeciphered writing system, the earliest known indigenous Iberian script, found on ~100 inscribed stelae primarily from southwestern Portugal (Algarve, Alentejo) and Spanish Extremadura. It vanishes from the historical record around 500 BCE, possibly absorbed by Carthaginian expansion, possibly disrupted by the cessation of the Atlantic tin trade, possibly devastated by environmental change.

## The Biblical connection: Tarshish
Tartessos is almost certainly the **Tarshish** of the Hebrew Bible — the distant western destination for ships of Tarshish (1 Kings 10:22; Jonah 1:3; Psalm 72:10; Isaiah 66:19). "Ships of Tarshish" in the Hebrew Bible connotes the furthest, most exotic maritime trade, consistent with Tartessos's position at the western terminus of the Phoenician trade network. Ezekiel 27:12 lists Tarshish as a source of silver, iron, tin, and lead — matching Tartessos's documented mineral wealth (the Huelva-Río Tinto mining region is one of the richest copper and silver deposits in the ancient world). The identification is not certain but is the scholarly majority position.

## The Phoenician connection
The primary external contact for Tartessos was **Phoenician**. The Phoenician colony of **Gades** (Cádiz — *Gadir* in Phoenician, meaning "enclosed/fortified place") was established ~1100 BCE on an island near the Atlantic coast, making it one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in Western Europe. Gades served as the Phoenician gateway to Tartessian silver and Atlantic tin routes. The relationship between Tartessos and Gades is sometimes described as indigenous-Iberian wealth + Phoenician commercial infrastructure + Atlantic-Mediterranean trade network = the wealth that funded the Phoenician western expansion. Phoenician religious practices (worship of Melqart/Hercules at the Temple of Melqart at Gades — the famous pillars of Heracles) penetrated Tartessian culture.

## The Tartessian language
The **Tartessian script** (~80–100 inscriptions) has been partially read as a semi-syllabic writing system adapted from Phoenician script — but the language it encodes remains undeciphered. Koch (2009, 2013) made a controversial argument that Tartessian was **Celtic** (or an archaic Celtic branch) — which would push Celtic languages into southwestern Iberia by ~700 BCE, earlier and further west than previously assumed. Most Celtic linguists are skeptical; the debate remains open. If Koch is correct, Tartessos represents an archaic Celtic civilization in what is now Portugal and Andalusia, with implications for the entire Celtic cultural and linguistic map of Atlantic Europe.

## The Atlantic Bronze Age system
Tartessos's wealth rested partly on its position within the **Atlantic Bronze Age** trade network (~1300–700 BCE): a maritime system connecting the Atlantic façade from Portugal/Morocco to Scotland and Scandinavia via coastal and river-based trade. Tin from Cornwall and Ireland + copper from Iberia → bronze metallurgy → traded up and down the Atlantic coast. The artifacts (swords, cauldrons, horse gear) found from Portugal to Denmark in this period are strikingly similar in type — suggesting not a unified culture but a unified exchange network. Tartessos controlled the southern anchor of this system. Barry Cunliffe's *Facing the Ocean* (2001) is the defining account.

## The disappearance (~500 BCE)
Tartessos vanishes from the archaeological and literary record around 500 BCE. The most likely causes:
1. **Carthaginian expansion** — after the Battle of Alalia (~535 BCE), Carthage asserted control over western Mediterranean trade, cutting off the Phocaean Greek-Tartessian connection. Carthaginian colonization of the Atlantic seaboard (including modern Portugal) followed.
2. **Collapse of the Atlantic tin trade** — possibly disrupted by the end of the Late Bronze Age system.
3. **Environmental change** — silting of the lower Guadalquivir, destruction of agricultural base.

Whatever the cause, Tartessos disappears *as an entity* but its population and culture continues in the subsequent Iberian and Turdetanian culture of southern Spain — the Turdetani, whom Strabo (~1st c. BCE) identified as the Tartessians' heirs.

## Cross-tradition significance

**Tartessos and the "Pillars of Heracles" mythology**
The Phoenician temple of Melqart (Hercules) at Gades stood at the Strait of Gibraltar — the mythological boundary of the *oikoumene* (inhabited world). The "Pillars of Heracles" as the western limit of the known world is a Phoenician-Tartessian construct absorbed into Greek mythology. Heracles's tenth labor (stealing the cattle of Geryon, who lived on an island near Gades) is set explicitly in Tartessian territory. The mythological zone around Tartessos — the far west, the land of the setting sun, the edge of the world — connects to the same eschatological geography that generated the Celtic Otherworld (Tír na nÓg = the Western Islands beyond the ocean), the Isles of the Blessed, and the Hesperides. See [[world-axis]].

**Tartessos and Portugal's deep history**
Portugal's national territory covers the northern part of the Tartessian cultural zone (Alentejo, Algarve). The mining wealth of the Huelva-Alentejo zone (Rio Tinto copper mines, Aljustrel silver mines) was already the basis of Tartessian prosperity 2,500 years before the Order of Christ's ships sailed to India. The deep historical continuity — pre-Roman metal wealth → Roman Lusitania → Moorish al-Andalus → Reconquista → Age of Discoveries — is one long thread of resource-exploitation, trade, and cross-cultural contact in the same geographic zone. See [[tradition-lusitanian-religion]], [[event-order-of-christ-foundation-1319]].
