Norse-Germanic religion is the polytheistic tradition of the Scandinavian and broader Germanic peoples, attested from c. 500 BCE through the Christianization of Scandinavia (c. 1000–1200 CE). Its primary literary preservation is in the Old Norse Eddas (Poetic Edda and Prose Edda, compiled 12th–13th c. CE) and the Icelandic sagas.

## Cosmological Structure

**Yggdrasil:** The World Tree — a cosmic ash or yew connecting nine worlds. Its roots reach Hel (the land of the dead), Jotunheimr (the giants' world), and the underworld spring of wisdom. [[Odin]] hung on this tree for nine nights to obtain the runes.

**The Nine Worlds:** Asgard (gods), Midgard (humans), Jotunheimr (giants), Helheim (dead), Niflheim (primordial cold), Muspelheim (primordial fire), Vanaheim (Vanir gods), Alfheim (light elves), Svartalfheim (dark elves/dwarves).

**Two divine families:** The Aesir (warrior gods: Odin, Thor, Tyr, Frigg) and the Vanir (fertility gods: Freyr, Freyja, Njord). Their original war ended in union and hostage exchange.

## The Runes

The runic alphabet — Elder Futhark, 24 signs — was the sacred writing system. See [[alphabet-elder-futhark]] for detailed account of the operative theory of writing in the Norse-Germanic tradition, including Odin's sacrifice for the runes.
