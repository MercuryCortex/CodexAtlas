# Strict Templar Observance (Strikte Observanz)

## Overview
The **Rite of Strict Templar Observance** (German *Strikte Observanz*; French *Stricte Observance Templière*) was an 18th-century continental Masonic ritual-system founded by **[[karl-gotthelf-von-hund|Karl Gotthelf von Hund]]** in Saxony around 1751, claiming that the **modern Masonic tradition is the secret continuation of the suppressed [[tradition-knights-templar|Knights Templar]]** through a Scottish refuge after the 1314 burning of [[jacques-de-molay|Molay]]. The system established seven (later eight) hierarchical degrees built on the standard three Craft Masonic degrees, with elaborated ritual-content claiming Templar-succession authority and lineage from Aumont — von Hund's invented Templar Grand Master who supposedly escaped to Scotland with the Order's secret tradition.

The Strict Templar Observance is **the principal 18th-century institutional vehicle for the Templar-revival current in Western Masonic tradition** and is one of the load-bearing 18th-century bridges between (a) the suppressed medieval Templar order, (b) post-Enlightenment continental Masonic ritualism, and (c) the subsequent 19th-c. occult and esoteric Templar-revival literature (Lévi, Hammer-Purgstall, Fabré-Palaprat).

**The Scottish-Templar-succession claim is historically unrecoverable** — no medieval Scottish documentation supports it; the consensus scholarly view (Stevenson 1988; Barber 2006 ch. 11) is that the Strict Observance's Templar-lineage is **an 18th-c. construction**. The historical-claim was already substantially relativized at the **[[event-wilhelmsbad-convent-1782|Convent of Wilhelmsbad]]** (1782), the major Masonic convention where the Templar-succession was formally repudiated by the broader European Masonic body.

## Historical phases
1. **Foundation (~1751)** — Karl Gotthelf von Hund organizes the system at his Saxon estate at Unwürde, claiming initiation by "unknown superiors" (Aumont's Templar successors) who had granted him authority to revive the Order. The claim is unverifiable and was already controversial in von Hund's lifetime.
2. **Expansion (1751–1772)** — the system spreads through German-speaking Masonic territories. Johann August von Starck develops a parallel "Clerical Templar" variant.
3. **Ferdinand of Brunswick's patronage (1772–1782)** — Ferdinand of Brunswick becomes *Heermeister* (military-master) of the system, lending it royal-aristocratic prestige.
4. **[[event-wilhelmsbad-convent-1782|Wilhelmsbad Convent (1782)]]** — major European Masonic convention. Jean-Baptiste Willermoz, Joseph de Maistre, and other senior continental Masons formally repudiate the von Hund Scottish-Templar-succession claim. The Strict Templar Observance effectively dissolves; its institutional remains fragment.
5. **Successor traditions** — Willermoz's **Rectified Scottish Rite** (*Régime Écossais Rectifié*, 1778+) is the principal post-Wilhelmsbad continuation, with **explicit Templar-symbolic content but historical-succession-claim removed**. The Swedish Rite and various 19th-c. continental Templar-revival systems also descend from the Strict Observance.

## Core texts and witnesses
- **No medieval Templar-survival documentation** is produced by the Strict Observance; the system was anchored on von Hund's claim of personal initiation by "unknown superiors," not on textual evidence.
- **Willermoz's reform documents (1778)** establish the *Régime Écossais Rectifié*.
- **Wilhelmsbad Convent minutes (1782)** — partial publication in 19th-c. Masonic scholarly literature.
- **Albert Pike's *Morals and Dogma* (1871)** — gives the principal late-19th-c. Anglo-American Masonic interpretation of the Strict Observance Templar-tradition.

## Defining doctrines and practices
- **Scottish-Templar succession claim** — von Hund's Aumont-narrative.
- **Seven (later eight) degree structure** built on standard Craft Masonry: Novice, Knight, *Eques*, etc.
- **Knight-Templar ritual content** — chivalric ceremony with Templar-period iconography, costume (white mantle, red cross), and oaths.
- **Initiatic-historical claim** — the candidate is told that the Strict Templar Observance is the secret continuation of the medieval Order.

## Relations to neighbors

- vs. **[[tradition-knights-templar]]**: the *claimed* parent tradition. No historical succession can be documented.
- vs. **[[tradition-freemasonry]]**: institutional context. The Strict Observance is a *high-degree elaboration* of standard Craft Masonry, not a separate institution.
- vs. **[[tradition-rosicrucianism]]**: substantial overlap with the contemporary **Gold und Rosenkreutz** order (founded c. 1750–1780 in adjacent German-speaking Masonic territories). Both 18th-c. continental Masonic high-degree systems integrating esoteric-historical content.
- vs. **[[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis|Templar-Gnostic transmission hypothesis]]**: the Strict Observance is **one of the principal institutional carriers** of post-1750 Templar-revival mythology that feeds the 19th-c. Hammer-Purgstall + Lévi + Fabré-Palaprat occult Templar-tradition.

## Deviant bridges this tradition opens

- → **[[tradition-freemasonry]] high-degree systems** — Scottish Rite, Rectified Scottish Rite, York Rite Templar degrees all carry forward Strict-Observance-derived Templar iconographic content.
- → **19th-c. occult Templar revival** — Hammer-Purgstall 1818 *Mysterium Baphometis revelatum*, Lévi 1854, [[bernard-raymond-fabre-palaprat|Fabré-Palaprat]]'s 1804 *Église Johannite*.
- → **20th-c. neo-Templar groups** — including documented modern phenomena: the Order of the Solar Temple ([[event-solar-temple-mass-suicides-1994-1997]]), various OSMTH-style modern Templar-NGOs.

## Disputes
- **Whether von Hund was sincere.** Defenders (some 19th-c. Masonic historians) argue von Hund genuinely believed his initiation-claim. Critical scholarship (Stevenson 1988, Bogdan & Snoek 2014) is skeptical but typically agnostic on von Hund's personal motives.
- **The exact Templar-content of the Strict Observance ritual.** Substantial Masonic-internal documentation survives but the ritual-text editing-history is complex; modern scholarship distinguishes early-von-Hund-period rituals from later-Brunswick-period elaborations.
