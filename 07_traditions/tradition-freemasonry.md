# Freemasonry

## Overview
A fraternal-philosophical institution emerging in its modern speculative form with the 1717 founding of the Premier Grand Lodge of London and Westminster and the 1723 publication of *The Constitutions of the Free-Masons* by James Anderson. Freemasonry combines: (1) the legacy of medieval operative stonemason guilds (with substantial Scottish operative-to-speculative continuity per Stevenson 1988); (2) Renaissance esoteric currents including Rosicrucian and Hermetic-Kabbalist materials (per the contested Yates thesis); (3) early-Enlightenment Newtonian-Latitudinarian deism and fraternal sociability. Members are organized in *lodges* under provincial and grand-lodge jurisdictions; initiated through the three Craft degrees (Entered Apprentice, Fellowcraft, Master Mason) and, in many systems, through higher-degree initiations (Scottish Rite to 33°, York Rite, etc.) that draw on substantial esoteric symbolic material. Membership requires belief in a Supreme Being (the Great Architect of the Universe) and the moral law; specific confessional commitments are explicitly set aside for lodge purposes. Freemasonry was for two centuries the principal organizational vehicle of male Enlightenment sociability across confessional and political lines.

## Historical phases
1. **Operative origins** (medieval through 16th c.) — stonemason guilds in England and especially Scotland.
2. **Operative-speculative transition** (17th c., especially Scotland) — Elias Ashmole's 1646 initiation at Warrington is among the earliest documented purely speculative receptions in England; the Scottish lodges (Edinburgh, Aberdeen, etc.) accepted speculative members from the early 17th c.
3. **Grand Lodge founding** (1717) — the Premier Grand Lodge of London and Westminster, established by four pre-existing London lodges meeting at the Goose and Gridiron alehouse on St. John the Baptist's Day 1717.
4. **Anderson Constitutions** (1723) — the founding charter of modern speculative Masonry.
5. **Continental and American expansion** (1730s+) — French Grand Lodge 1728/1738, Irish Grand Lodge 1725, Scottish 1736, American colonial lodges from the 1730s.
6. **Higher-degree systems** (1740s+) — Scottish Rite, Rectified Scottish Rite, Royal Arch, York Rite, Strict Observance, Gold und Rosenkreutz, etc., drawing substantially on Rosicrucian and Templar symbolic materials.
7. **Conflict with Catholicism** (1738+) — Pope Clement XII's *In Eminenti* (1738) condemns Freemasonry; the prohibition continues to the present.
8. **Enlightenment and revolutionary period** (1750–1815) — Masonic networks play substantial roles in American (Washington, Franklin, etc.) and French (less directly) revolutions.
9. **19th-c. American expansion and African American Prince Hall Masonry** (founded 1784).
10. **20th-21st centuries** — global continuation with substantial regional variation; declining membership in the West; continuation in many countries; the United Grand Lodge of England (founded 1813 by merger of the Antients and Moderns) as principal anglophone authority.

## Defining doctrines
- Non-confessional monotheism: belief in a Supreme Being (Great Architect of the Universe) under any religious tradition's name.
- Moral law as the operating ethical framework.
- Initiatory transformation through ritual degrees.
- Brotherhood and sociability across confessional and (in principle) class lines.
- Symbolic operative-tool imagery (square, compasses, level, plumb, trowel) as figures of moral and spiritual operations.
- Secrecy regarding ritual specifics and signs of recognition; openness regarding existence and ethical principles.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-rosicrucianism]]: substantial 18th-c. cross-influence; the higher-degree Masonic systems incorporate Rosicrucian symbolic material; Christopher McIntosh treats Rosicrucianism and Freemasonry as parallel-and-intermingling currents through the 18th–19th centuries.
- vs. [[tradition-renaissance-hermeticism]]: indirect inheritance, contested in directness (Yates' thesis: substantial; Stevenson's reconstruction: more limited).
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: doctrinally non-confessional but historically conflictual with Catholicism (Clement XII 1738) and varyingly with Protestant denominations.
- vs. 19th-c. occult orders (Golden Dawn, OTO, AMORC): substantial overlap of membership and symbolism; many esoteric orders have adopted Masonic-style initiatory structures.
- vs. 18th-c. political-philosophical movements (American founding, Encyclopédistes, etc.): Masonic networks substantially overlap with Enlightenment political-philosophical networks.
- vs. [[tradition-knights-templar]] (Templar-revivalist current): the **Strict Templar Observance** of Karl Gotthelf von Hund (~1751) and the **Rectified Scottish Rite** (Jean-Baptiste Willermoz, 1778) and the **Scottish Rite Templar grades** (29° Knight of St. Andrew, 30° Knight Kadosh, the 32° system, etc.) construct a positive Templar-succession narrative claiming continuous transmission from the suppressed 1312 Order via Scottish refuge into 18th-c. Masonry. **The claim is historically unrecoverable** (Stevenson 1988) but is a major substantive component of 18th-21st-c. Masonic higher-degree symbolism. The [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis]] is the principal node tracking this construction.
