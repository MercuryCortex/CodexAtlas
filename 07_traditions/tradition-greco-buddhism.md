# Greco-Buddhism

## Overview
Greco-Buddhism is the cultural-religious synthesis that emerged in the contact zone between Hellenistic-Greek and Indian civilizations from Alexander's invasion of the Persian-Mauryan frontier (~-326) through the end of the Kushan empire (~+375). It is the load-bearing transmission corridor through which (a) the **first anthropomorphic Buddha image** was created in Apollo-styled form in Gandhāran workshops, (b) the **Aśokan dhamma-missions** of ~-250 reached named Hellenistic kings, (c) the **Pali *Milindapañha*** preserves a Greek-Buddhist philosophical dialogue, and (d) **Pyrrho of Elis** plausibly carried Buddhist-style view-skepticism back to Greece. The Greco-Buddhist axis is the most under-recognized cross-tradition contact zone in axial-age religion, and the one through which the visual iconography of the Buddha — and therefore of all subsequent East Asian Buddhist art — passed through Hellenistic Apollonine and Athena-bodhisattva prototypes.

## Historical phases
1. **Alexander's eastern campaign (-334 to -323)** — Alexander reaches the Punjab (-326); Pyrrho of Elis accompanies the army as a philosopher; encounters with "gymnosophists" (naked-philosophers — likely Jain and Ājīvika, possibly Buddhist) are recorded in Diogenes Laertius and Strabo.
2. **Seleucid-Mauryan settlement (-305 / -303)** — Seleucus I cedes the eastern satrapies to Chandragupta Maurya in exchange for 500 war-elephants; Megasthenes serves as Seleucid ambassador at Pāṭaliputra and writes *Indica*; foundational ethnographic-religious encounter.
3. **Aśokan dhamma-missions (~-250)** — Aśoka in [[phase-3-030-asokan-edicts|Rock Edict XIII]] (Kandahar Aramaic and Greek bilingual versions extant) names Antiochus II Theos, Ptolemy II Philadelphus, Antigonus Gonatas, Magas of Cyrene, and Alexander II of Epirus as recipients of dhamma-missions. **The first attested Indian religious outreach to the Hellenistic West.**
4. **Diodotid Bactria + Indo-Greek kingdoms (-250 to -10)** — Diodotus I declares Bactrian independence ~-250; Euthydemus and Demetrius expand south; [[menander-i-soter|Menander I Sōter]] (~-150) extends Indo-Greek rule across the Punjab; the *Milindapañha* is the literary product of this milieu.
5. **Indo-Scythian / Indo-Parthian transition (-10 to +30)** — Sakas and Parthians inherit Indo-Greek territory; Gandhāran cosmopolitanism continues; Kharosthi inscriptions name Greek officers serving Saka kings.
6. **Kushan empire (~+30 to +375)** — Kushans (Yuezhi origin) unify Bactria + Gandhāra + northern India under [[kanishka|Kaniṣka]] (r. ~127-150); Greek alphabet used on coinage (the Kushan "Boddo" = Buddha coin of Kaniṣka is the earliest dated explicit image of the Buddha); patronage of Mahāyāna emergence; Gandhāran sculpture workshops at full output.
7. **Decline (~+375 to +500)** — Hephthalite (White Hun) invasions; Gandhāran Buddhist monasteries decline; Greco-Buddhist style absorbed into Indian Gupta and Central Asian art; transmitted forward into Chinese Buddhist iconography via the Silk Road.

## Core documents
- [[phase-3-029-milindapanha]] — *Questions of King Milinda*; the Pali version is in the Theravāda canon's Khuddaka Nikāya (Burmese/Thai recensions); Chinese parallels exist. **The single highest-fidelity Greco-Buddhist primary document.**
- [[phase-3-030-asokan-edicts]] — Rock Edict XIII names the five Hellenistic kings; the Kandahar bilingual edict is Aśoka's text translated into Greek and Aramaic for the Greek-Aramaic-speaking population of his northwest frontier.
- **Coinage of Menander I, Kaniṣka, and successor kings** — bilingual Greek + Kharosthi legends; deity-iconography mixing Greek (Apollo, Athena, Tyche, Zeus, Heracles) with Indian (Boddo / Buddha, Śiva, Skanda).
- **Gandhāran Buddhist sculpture** (Hadda, Begram, Taxila, Butkara, Bamiyan) — the visual corpus.
- **Megasthenes, *Indica*** (~-300, fragments preserved in Diodorus, Strabo, Arrian) — the earliest detailed Greek ethnography of Mauryan India.
- **Strabo 15.1.59-65** + **Diogenes Laertius 9.61-69** — Pyrrho's encounter with the *gymnosophists*.
- **Plutarch *Moralia* 821D-E** (*Reg. Imp. Apophth.* "Menander") — Greek source for Menander's Buddhist devotion: "When Menander, who had reigned in Bactria with mildness, died in camp, the cities celebrated his obsequies as usual in other respects, but in regard to his remains they put forth rival claims and only with difficulty came to terms, agreeing that they should divide the ashes equally and go away and should erect monuments to him in all their cities." — direct parallel to the parinirvāṇa-relics distribution.

## Key persons
- [[asoka-maurya]] — Mauryan emperor; commissioned the Greek-Aramaic Kandahar edict; sent dhamma-missions to the Hellenistic West.
- [[menander-i-soter]] — Indo-Greek king of Bactria/Punjab; the "Milinda" of the Milindapañha.
- [[kanishka]] — Kushan emperor; Mahāyāna patron; first dated Buddha coinage.
- [[nagasena]] — Buddhist monk-interlocutor of Menander.
- **Pyrrho of Elis** (~-360 to -270) — accompanied Alexander to India; Beckwith 2015 argues his philosophical formation was Buddhist-derived (contested but defensible).
- **Megasthenes** (~-350 to -290) — Seleucid ambassador at Pāṭaliputra; *Indica*.
- **Heliodorus of Taxila** (~-110) — Greek envoy to the Śuṅga court at Vidiśā; erected the **Heliodorus pillar** declaring himself a devotee (*bhāgavata*) of Vāsudeva. **Hard archaeological evidence of a Greek officer converting to a major Indian religious cult.**

## Defining features
- **Cross-tradition contact zone** — not a "school" but a sustained period and geography of Greek-Indian-Iranian religious-philosophical exchange.
- **Iconographic synthesis** — the anthropomorphic Buddha image is a Greco-Buddhist invention. Earlier Indian Buddhist art (Bharhut, Sanchi, Amaravati ~-150 to +100) is *aniconic* — the Buddha is represented by symbols (bodhi-tree, dharma-wheel, footprints, empty seat) rather than by figure. The Gandhāran workshops in the 1st-3rd c. CE create the first standing/seated Buddha figures, drawing on Hellenistic Apollo (head, drapery, *himation*-style robe), and on Hellenistic ruler-portrait conventions.
- **Bilingual textuality** — the Kandahar edict (Greek + Aramaic), Indo-Greek coinage (Greek + Kharosthi), and the Milindapañha-genre dialogue between Greek king and Buddhist monk are all products of this bilingual culture.
- **Philosophical contact** — Pyrrhonist view-skepticism may have Buddhist roots (Beckwith 2015 contested); some scholars argue parallel directions of influence; consensus accepts at minimum the *opportunity* for contact.

## Relations to neighbors
- → [[tradition-buddhism]] / [[tradition-mahayana-buddhism]]: Greco-Buddhism is one of the contact-zone matrices in which Mahāyāna emerges. The Kuṣāṇa-Gandhāran milieu hosts the earliest extant Mahāyāna manuscripts (Gāndhārī Mahāyāna texts, Schøyen Collection, ~1st-3rd c. CE).
- → [[tradition-greek-religion]] / Hellenistic philosophy: Apollo, Heracles, Tyche, Athena appear on Indo-Greek and Kushan coinage; Pyrrhonist skepticism has the Indian-contact hypothesis.
- → [[tradition-zoroastrianism]]: Iranian-Bactrian substrate; Kushans worship Mithra (Kushan "Miiro") alongside the Buddha; Iranian light-cosmology may feed into Pure Land's *Buddha of Measureless Light* iconography.
- → [[tradition-vedic-hinduism]] / Brahmanical religion: the Heliodorus pillar (~-110) is the hard-archaeological case of a Greek officer becoming a Vaiṣṇava devotee.
- → Chinese Buddhism: the Gandhāran iconographic vocabulary is transmitted forward into Chinese Buddhist art via the Silk Road; the standing Maitreya at Bezeklik, the Bamiyan colossi (destroyed 2001 — see [[event-destruction-of-nalanda-1193]] context), and the early Chinese Buddha figures at Yungang and Longmen are direct descendants of the Gandhāran types.
