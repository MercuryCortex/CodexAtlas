# Catharism

## Overview
Catharism — from Greek *katharos*, "pure" — was the principal medieval Western European Christian dualist movement, active especially in Northern Italian communes and in Languedoc / Occitan southern France from roughly 1100 to 1330. Doctrinally, Catharism held a two-principles cosmology — either mitigated dualism (a single Good God with a fallen Satanael creating the material world, the Bogomil-Concorezzan position) or absolute dualism (two coeternal principles of good and evil, the Albanense position) — and a sharp spirit / matter dualism in which the material world is the work of the evil principle, bodies are prisons for fallen spirits, and salvation requires escape from materiality. The movement was institutionally organized with bishops (overseeing dioceses), *perfecti* / *boni homines* / *bonae feminae* (the initiated who had received the *consolamentum* and lived in strict abstinence), and *credentes* (believers who had not yet received the sacrament; expected to receive it before death). Exterminated by the Albigensian Crusade (1209–1229) and the institutional Inquisition; the last named perfectus, Guillaume Bélibaste, was burned in 1321.

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-Cathar Bogomil precursor (~940–1100)** — Bogomil missionary activity reaches northern Italy and southern France.
2. **Crystallization (~1100–1180)** — Cathar churches established in Lombardy and Languedoc; Council of Saint-Félix (~1167) — disputed-historicity Cathar council allegedly organizing the regional churches.
3. **Bishop Nazarius's import (~1190)** — explicit Bulgarian-Cathar textual transmission documented (the colophon of the *Interrogatio Iohannis*).
4. **Albigensian Crusade (1209–1229)** — military extermination phase.
5. **Inquisitorial extinction (~1230–1330)** — institutional Inquisition; fall of Montségur (1244); Cathar shepherd Bélibaste's burning (1321).
6. **Modern reception** — 19th-c. Romantic recovery (especially in Occitan regional identity); 20th-c. academic critical work.

## Core texts
- [[phase-5-020-interrogatio-iohannis]] — Bogomil-Cathar cosmological dialogue.
- [[phase-5-023-liber-de-duobus-principiis]] — systematic Albanense absolute-dualist defense.
- Cathar Latin / Provençal New Testament (recensions preserved in inquisitorial archives).
- Cathar ritual texts (esp. for the *consolamentum*).

## Pantheon (or rather, two divine principles)
- [[the-good-god-cathar]] — the transcendent Good Father; creator of spiritual reality only.
- [[satanael]] (mitigated dualist) — the fallen creator of the material cosmos.
- [[the-evil-god-cathar]] (absolute dualist) — the coeternal evil principle.

## Defining doctrines
- **Two-principles cosmology** — material world is the work of the evil principle.
- **Docetic Christology** — Christ's body was not material; he was an angelic / spiritual being sent by the Good Father.
- **Rejection of Old Testament** — identified the OT creator with the evil principle; OT is, with qualifications, the testament of the wrong god.
- ***Consolamentum*** — the operative sacrament; a laying-on of hands by which a believer became a *perfectus*, free from rebirth and bound to strict abstinence and renunciation (sexual celibacy, vegetarianism, no killing).
- **Endura** — voluntary fasting unto death practiced by some perfecti facing capture.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-bogomilism]]: direct doctrinal and textual ancestor; the Concorezzan Cathar position retained Bogomil mitigated dualism; the Albanense moved to absolute dualism.
- vs. [[tradition-manichaeism]]: the debated transmission edge. Stoyanov, Hamilton: continuous transmission via Paulicians and Bogomils. Moore, Pegg: largely independent emergence with inquisitorial labeling.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]] (Catholic Roman): the principal target of medieval anti-heretical institutional response (Dominican order founded against Cathars; Albigensian Crusade; institutional Inquisition).
- vs. [[tradition-gnosticism]]: structural parallels (demiurge, fallen-spirit-in-matter, knowledge-as-salvation) widely noted; whether genealogical or independent re-emergence is the same debate as for Manichaean genealogy.
- vs. [[tradition-knights-templar]]: substantial geographic overlap. The [[tradition-knights-templar|Templars]] held extensive properties across Languedoc-Occitan territory before, during, and after the [[event-albigensian-crusade-1209-1229|Albigensian Crusade]] and are **notably absent from the Crusader armies**. Whether this reflects doctrinal sympathy is contested (Barber 1994, Nicholson 2001 caution against the sympathy thesis — the absence is explainable by Outremer commitments). The Templar-Cathar contact hypothesis is *itself* a key element of the modern [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis]].
