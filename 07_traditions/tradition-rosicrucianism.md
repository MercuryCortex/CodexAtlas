# Rosicrucianism

## Overview
A Christian-esoteric tradition emerging publicly in 1614–1616 with three foundational German texts — the *Fama Fraternitatis*, *Confessio Fraternitatis*, and *Chymical Wedding of Christian Rosenkreutz* — produced by the Tübingen circle of Johann Valentin Andreae, Tobias Hess, and Christoph Besold. The manifestos announce a secret Christian brotherhood founded by the legendary Christian Rosenkreutz (1378–1484), call for a "universal reformation" of arts, sciences, and religion, and combine Lutheran Christianity, Paracelsian medicine, Hermetic-Kabbalist natural philosophy, and apocalyptic-providential historical expectation. Whether the original Brotherhood existed historically is now consensus-rejected (it is a literary construction, possibly an Andreae *ludibrium*); the *Rosicrucian movement*, however, became real — the manifestos sparked one of the great pamphlet wars of the 17th century, attracted defenders such as Robert Fludd and Michael Maier, and seeded an esoteric current that continued through 18th-century Masonic-Rosicrucian higher degrees and 19th–20th-century Rosicrucian orders.

## Historical phases
1. **Manifesto period** (1610–1620) — Tübingen circle composition and publication; the European pamphlet response (~400 texts in Carlos Gilly's count).
2. **17th-c. continuation** — Fludd's defense in England, Maier's Frankfurt-Magdeburg alchemical-Rosicrucian publishing program, Andreae's own later distancing.
3. **18th-c. Masonic-Rosicrucian degrees** — the Gold und Rosenkreutz (1750s German), the Strict Observance, the Rectified Scottish Rite, and various higher-degree Masonic systems incorporate Rosicrucian symbolic material.
4. **19th-c. occult revival** — Eliphas Lévi, the Société Rosicrucienne en France, the English Societas Rosicruciana in Anglia (1865+), the Golden Dawn.
5. **20th-c. modern orders** — AMORC (Rosicrucian Order, 1915+), Rosicrucian Fellowship (Max Heindel 1909+), Lectorium Rosicrucianum (1924+), various others. All claim symbolic descent from the 1614 manifestos.

## Core texts
- *Fama Fraternitatis* (1614).
- *Confessio Fraternitatis* (1615).
- *Chymical Wedding of Christian Rosenkreutz* (1616).
- Fludd's *Apologia Compendiaria* (1616) and *Tractatus Apologeticus* (1617).
- Michael Maier's *Atalanta Fugiens* (1617).

## Defining doctrines
- Universal reformation: a third stage of reformation beyond Luther's and Calvin's, integrating natural-philosophical and mystical-magical dimensions.
- Christ-centered Christianity with Paracelsian-Hermetic-Kabbalist scientific-philosophical content.
- Secret brotherhood / invisible college trope.
- Apocalyptic providential historiography.
- Microcosm-macrocosm correspondence as the operating principle of natural and spiritual knowledge.
- Initiatory transformation modeled on alchemical operations.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-renaissance-hermeticism]]: direct descendant.
- vs. [[tradition-christian-kabbalah]]: substantial overlap; Rosicrucian texts use Kabbalistic letter and Sefirot material.
- vs. [[tradition-freemasonry]]: 18th-c. Masonic higher degrees incorporate Rosicrucian symbolism; the historical relationship is contested (Yates' thesis vs. Stevenson's Scottish-operative reconstruction).
- vs. [[tradition-protestantism]]: founded by Lutheran authors; explicitly anti-Catholic in the *Confessio*.
- vs. modern esoteric orders (Theosophy, Anthroposophy, Thelema): all draw on Rosicrucian materials.
