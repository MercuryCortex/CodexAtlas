# Hindutva (Hindu Nationalism)

## Overview
A 20th- and 21st-century Indian political-cultural ideology defining "Hindu-ness" (*hindutva*) as the cultural-national-civilizational identity of those whose fatherland and holy land are within the territory of historical Bharatavarsha (the Indian subcontinent). Distinct from Hinduism the religion (Hindutva includes Sikhs, Jains, Buddhists; excludes Indian Muslims and Christians on the criterion that their holy lands lie elsewhere). Foundational text: Savarkar's *Hindutva* (1923). Principal institutional vehicle: the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS, founded 1925). Principal electoral vehicle: the Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP, founded 1980), in national-government power 1998–2004 and 2014 onward.

## Historical phases
1. **Founding period** (1923–1947) — Savarkar's *Hindutva*; RSS founding by Hedgewar (1925); Hindu Mahasabha politics.
2. **Post-Independence marginal period** (1947–1980) — Gandhi assassination (1948) by Nathuram Godse (RSS-associated background) led to RSS being banned briefly; subsequent rebuilding through the Bharatiya Jana Sangh (1951) and Janata Party experiment (1977–1979).
3. **BJP rise period** (1980–2014) — BJP founding; Ayodhya / Babri Masjid mobilization (1990s); BJP-led national government (Vajpayee, 1998–2004).
4. **Modi-BJP dominance** (2014 onward) — sustained electoral dominance; Hindutva as actively-governing ideology; the 2019 Citizenship Amendment Act, the 2019 revocation of Article 370 (Jammu and Kashmir), the 2020 farm-laws protests, and the broader transformation of Indian secular-constitutional order toward a more explicit Hindu-civilizational frame.

## Defining doctrines
- Hindutva as ethno-civilizational rather than narrowly religious category.
- The *pitribhumi/punyabhumi* (fatherland/holy-land) double criterion.
- *Akhand Bharat* (unbroken India, including currently Pakistani and Bangladeshi territory) as the aspirational geographic frame.
- Cultural nationalism with selective religious-symbolic content.
- "Integral Humanism" (Upadhyaya, 1965) as the principal post-Savarkar ideological synthesis.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-hindu-modernism]]: shared vocabulary, divergent political project. Hindutva claims Vivekananda and other Hindu-modernist figures; Hindu-modernist universalists contest this claim.
- vs. classical [[tradition-vedic-hinduism]]: Hindutva is selective-civilizational rather than narrowly religious; it appropriates classical Hindu symbolism for nationalist ends.
