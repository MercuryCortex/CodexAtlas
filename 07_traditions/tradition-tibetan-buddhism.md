Tibetan Buddhism is the comprehensive form of Buddhist practice that developed in Tibet from the 7th century CE onward, synthesizing Indian Mahayana and Vajrayana Buddhism with indigenous Tibetan religious practices (Bon). It is preserved in four major school-lineages (Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, Gelug) and remains the primary living form of Vajrayana Buddhism.

## Script and Transmission

The creation of the Tibetan alphabet by Thonmi Sambhota (c. 630 CE) was the foundational act that enabled Tibetan Buddhist literary culture — see [[alphabet-tibetan-tantric]] for detailed account of the script, seed syllables, and the embodied letter practices that characterize Tibetan tantric practice.

## Key Figures

- Padmasambhava (Guru Rinpoche, 8th c. CE): brought Vajrayana to Tibet; founder of the Nyingma school
- Milarepa (1052–1135 CE): poet-saint, disciple of Marpa; archetypal figure of the yogi achieving enlightenment in one lifetime
- Tsongkhapa (1357–1419 CE): founder of the Gelug school; synthesizer of Tibetan Buddhist philosophy
