# Etruscan Religion

## Overview
The pre-Roman religious tradition of Etruria (modern Tuscany, Lazio, Umbria), c. 800–100 BCE — distinct from but in continuous contact with Greek and later Roman religion. Etruscan divinatory practice (haruspicy — inspection of sheep livers; observation of lightning; observation of birds) was systematically detailed and was substantially adopted into Roman state-religion. The Etruscan *disciplina* (ritual-divinatory technical literature) survived only in fragmentary Roman quotation and in archaeological objects like the Piacenza bronze liver (a divinatory teaching-aid with the celestial regions and their deities). The longest extant Etruscan text — the *Liber Linteus*, a linen ritual calendar reused as Egyptian mummy-wrapping, c. 200 BCE — survives some 1200 words.

## Historical phases
1. **Orientalizing** (-750 to -600) — formation; influence from Phoenician/Greek east-Mediterranean.
2. **Archaic** (-600 to -480) — height of Etruscan dominance; period of Etruscan kings of Rome (Tarquins).
3. **Classical** (-480 to -300) — gradual eclipse by Roman expansion.
4. **Hellenistic** (-300 to -100) — Roman absorption; Etruscan language and religion gradually lost (Etruscan was extinct as a spoken language by 1st c. CE).
5. **Disciplina Etrusca preserved within Roman religion** — haruspicy continued as Roman state practice into the imperial period.

## Note
The Etruscan religious tradition's principal long-term influence is its absorption into Roman religion; the gods Tinia, Uni, Menrva map onto the Capitoline triad (Jupiter, Juno, Minerva). The script and language are partly readable; the religious content is mostly inferred from inscriptions, tomb-paintings, and bronze objects.
