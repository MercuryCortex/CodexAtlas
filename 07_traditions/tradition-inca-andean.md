# Inca / Andean Religion

## Overview
"Andean religion" covers a vast and deep tradition: pre-Inca religious traditions (Chavín ~1200–500 BCE, Moche, Tiwanaku, Wari, Chimu) extending over ~3,000 years before Inca consolidation; Inca state religion as an imperial superstructure ~1200–1533 imposed on pre-existing local *huaca* (sacred-being) cults; the colonial-period extirpation campaigns and parallel preservation (notably the Huarochirí Manuscript); and continuous contemporary Quechua and Aymara religious practice (Pachamama veneration, despachos, mountain-spirit / *apu* cult). The *huaca* concept is the most distinctive Andean religious category — every distinctive landscape feature is or contains a sacred being; religion is fundamentally place-bound.

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-Inca (~1200 BCE–~1200 CE)** — Chavín cult center; Moche state religion; Tiwanaku and the Gateway-of-the-Sun staff-god (likely a Viracocha-type creator); Wari; coastal traditions (Chimu).
2. **Inca period (~1200–1533)** — Inca state expansion imposes the imperial Sun cult of Inti on local *huaca* religions; Cusco's Coricancha (Temple of the Sun) as imperial-religious center.
3. **Spanish conquest (1532–1533)** — [[event-pizarro-conquest-of-inca-1533]] destroys Inca state-cultic apparatus.
4. **Extirpation campaigns (~1582–1660)** — Third Council of Lima (1582–83); Francisco de Ávila's campaigns (~1608+); destruction of *huaca* idols, mummified ancestors, ceremonial paraphernalia; trials of Indigenous religious specialists.
5. **Colonial syncretism (~1600–1810)** — Andean Catholic forms (Virgen del Cerro / Pachamama-Mary; Virgen of Copacabana; Christ-Pachacamac).
6. **Contemporary (19th–21st c.)** — continuous Quechua-Aymara religious practice especially in rural areas; Pachamama veneration; resurgent Indigenous religious-political identity (Bolivian 2009 plurinational constitution).

## Core texts
- [[phase-8-006-huarochiri-manuscript]] — only extensive surviving Indigenous-language narrative of pre-Hispanic Andean religion.
- Guaman Poma de Ayala, *Nueva corónica y buen gobierno* (~1615).
- Garcilaso de la Vega, *Comentarios reales* (1609).
- Bernabé Cobo, *Historia del Nuevo Mundo* (1653).
- Polo de Ondegardo, *Errores y supersticiones de los indios* (1571).
- Cristóbal de Molina, *Relación de las fábulas y ritos de los Incas* (~1574).

## Pantheon
- [[inti]] — Sun; Inca imperial patron
- [[viracocha]] — creator-god; pre-Inca Tiwanaku origin; Inca-period absorbed
- [[pachamama]] — Earth Mother; pan-Andean, deeply continuous to present
- Mama Killa — Moon
- Pachacamac — coastal deity / cosmic principle
- *Apus* — mountain-spirits; each major mountain has its own apu
- *Huacas* — local sacred-beings; thousands across the Andes

## Defining doctrines
- *Huaca* religion: place-bound, locally-specific, ancestor-related sacred beings
- Pachamama as continuous earth-substance
- *Ayni* — reciprocity as cosmic-ethical principle (humans owe Pachamama and the apus; she/they give in return)
- Three-tier cosmos: *Hanan Pacha* (upper world), *Kay Pacha* (this world), *Ukhu Pacha* (inner world)
- Ancestor mummification (the Inca *mallquis*); royal ancestors as continuing political-religious agents
- *Quipu* knotted-cord recording (partly religious-ritual)

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: post-1533 forced Christianization with extensive extirpation; persistent Andean Catholic syncretism (Pachamama-Mary; Christ-Pachacamac).
- vs. [[tradition-mesoamerican]]: separate continental religious-cultural development; no historical contact; comparative parallels (calendar elaboration, monumental architecture, ancestor cult) are typological.
