# Rastafari

## Overview
Religious and political movement that emerged in Jamaica ~1930 around the identification of the new Ethiopian emperor Haile Selassie I (crowned 1930) as messianic Black King, fulfilling Marcus Garvey's prophecy ("Look to Africa for the crowning of a Black King") and serving as the returning Christ for the African diaspora. Rastafari draws on the Hebrew Bible / King James Bible read from an Ethiopian-Black-diasporic perspective; on the *Kebra Nagast* (Ethiopian Solomonic-genealogical foundation text); on Garveyite pan-African Black nationalism; and on Jamaican Afro-Protestant religious tradition. Distinctive practices: dreadlocks (the Nazirite vow, Numbers 6); ital food (vegetarian / vegan / no salt); ganja sacramental use; Ethiopian colors (red, gold, green); rejection of "Babylon" (the oppressive white/Western colonial-capitalist system); identification with the lost-tribes-of-Israel reading of the African diaspora.

## Historical phases
1. **Proto-Rastafarian (~1916–1930)** — Marcus Garvey's UNIA (Universal Negro Improvement Association); his prophecy of a Black king to be crowned in Africa.
2. **Foundation (1930–1934)** — Haile Selassie's 1930 coronation interpreted by Leonard Howell, Joseph Hibbert, Archibald Dunkley, Robert Hinds as the prophesied event. Leonard Howell preached the new religion in Jamaica from 1932.
3. **Suppression and establishment (1934–1959)** — Howell's Pinnacle commune (founded 1940, destroyed by Jamaican police 1954); Rastafari spreads despite official suppression.
4. **University Report and recognition (1960)** — the University College of the West Indies' *Report on the Rastafari Movement in Kingston, Jamaica* (M.G. Smith, Roy Augier, Rex Nettleford) is the first official-scholarly engagement, recommending recognition.
5. **Selassie's visit to Jamaica (April 21, 1966)** — "Grounation Day," central religious event.
6. **Global reach via reggae (1970s+)** — Bob Marley, Peter Tosh, Burning Spear, and other reggae artists carry Rastafarian theology globally.
7. **Post-Selassie's-death (1975+)** — the emperor's death/disappearance is variously interpreted: some Rastafari hold he did not die; some hold his physical death is irrelevant to his spiritual reality; movement diversifies into multiple sects (Nyahbinghi, Bobo Ashanti, Twelve Tribes of Israel).

## Core texts
- [[phase-8-008-kebra-nagast]] — foundational; provides the Solomonic genealogy.
- Holy Bible (King James Version) — read from Ethiopian-African-diasporic perspective; Revelation 5:5 (Lion of the Tribe of Judah) and 19:16 (King of Kings and Lord of Lords) particularly cited.
- *The Promised Key* (Leonard Howell, 1935) — first Rastafarian manifesto.
- Marcus Garvey's writings and speeches.
- *The Holy Piby* (Robert Athlyi Rogers, 1924) — proto-Rastafarian Pan-African religious text.

## Pantheon / key figures
- [[wolega-tafari-makonnen-haile-selassie]] — Jah / King / Christ-returned (variously theologized across sects).
- Jah (Yahweh) — the Ethiopian-African name of God.
- Empress Menen Asfaw (Haile Selassie's consort; in some Rastafarian theology, the Empress).
- Marcus Garvey — prophet (some sects accord him explicit prophetic status).

## Defining doctrines
- Identification of Haile Selassie I as Jah / Christ-returned (with variations across sects)
- *Babylon* as the system of white-Western-capitalist-colonial oppression
- *Zion* as Africa, specifically Ethiopia; ultimate repatriation
- Nazirite-derived practices (dreadlocks; ganja as sacramental herb)
- *Livity* — the proper Rastafarian way of life
- Generally non-violent; many Rastafari are pacifist

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[phase-8-008-kebra-nagast]] / Ethiopian Orthodoxy: the Solomonic-genealogy is shared, but Ethiopian Orthodoxy does not accept the Rastafarian deification of Haile Selassie.
- vs. [[tradition-vodou-haitian]]: sibling Afro-Caribbean tradition; shared diasporic religious-political consciousness.
- vs. [[tradition-mormonism]] (Phase 7 cross-current): both NRMs of the 19th–20th c. with restorationist-genealogical religious claims about marginalized peoples.
