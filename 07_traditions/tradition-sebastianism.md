# Sebastianism (Sebastianismo)

## Overview

**Sebastianism** is the Portuguese messianic-prophetic tradition holding that King [[sebastian-i-portugal|Sebastian I]] — who disappeared at the [[event-battle-of-alcacer-quibir-1578|Battle of Alcácer Quibir]] on 4 August 1578 and whose body was never positively identified — will **return at Portugal's darkest hour** to restore the kingdom and inaugurate the [[fifth-empire-quinto-imperio|Fifth Empire]] (*Quinto Império*) prophesied by Daniel 2 and the [[phase-6-040-bandarra-trovas|*Trovas* of Bandarra]]. The returning king is **O Encoberto** ("the Shrouded / Hidden One") and **O Desejado** ("the Desired One"). The tradition emerges immediately after the 1578 succession crisis, deepens during the [[event-battle-of-alcacer-quibir-1578|Iberian Union]] (1580–1640) as a vehicle of national-identity resistance to Habsburg rule, and persists into the modern period as a literary-mythological motif most influentially recovered by [[fernando-pessoa|Fernando Pessoa]] in [[phase-7-037-pessoa-mensagem|*Mensagem*]] (1934).

Sebastianism is best read as a **convergence of three pre-existing currents** that the 1578 trauma crystallized into a single tradition:

1. **Iberian Jewish-converso messianism** (Maimonidean + medieval Iberian Jewish eschatology + Sabbatean-precursor currents in the Portuguese-Jewish *Cristão-Novo* communities) — providing the substrate of "the king will return / the messianic age will come."
2. **Joachimist Latin-Christian apocalyptic** — the Calabrian abbot **Joachim of Fiore**'s (1135–1202) tripartite-age schema (*Status* of Father / Son / Spirit) and his prophetic-political theology — which had been read in Iberian monastic communities since the 13th century.
3. **The *Trovas* of [[goncalo-annes-bandarra|Bandarra]]** (c. 1530s) — vernacular prophetic verse that *predated* Sebastian's birth but was *retrospectively* read after 1578 as predicting his return. Bandarra's "Hidden King" is the structural seat into which Sebastian's image was poured.

## Historical phases

1. **Pre-1578 prophetic substrate (1530–1578).** Bandarra writes the *Trovas* in Trancoso in the 1530s; the Inquisition tries him in 1541 for *judaizante* tendencies; he is acquitted (or escapes serious sentence) and continues to circulate the *Trovas* in manuscript until his death c. 1556. The *Trovas* are added to the Index of Prohibited Books in 1581 but continue circulating clandestinely.
2. **The 1578 catastrophe.** [[sebastian-i-portugal|Sebastian I]] disappears at [[event-battle-of-alcacer-quibir-1578|Alcácer Quibir]]; the body is never definitively identified. The Cardinal-King Henry rules briefly (1578–1580); Philip II of Spain inherits the Portuguese crown; the Iberian Union begins.
3. **First-wave Sebastianism (1578–1640).** Bandarra's *Trovas* are reinterpreted as predicting Sebastian's return. **Four false Sebastians** appear claiming to be the returned king: the Hermit of Penamacor (1584), the King of Ericeira (1585), the Pastry-Maker of Madrigal (1594; Spanish, executed), and the Marco Túlio Catizone "Calabrian Sebastian" (1598–1603; also executed). Pamphlets, prophecies, and *folhetos* of expected return circulate widely.
4. **Restoration and Vieira's Fifth-Empire systematization (1640–1697).** The 1640 Portuguese Restoration ends the Iberian Union; João IV ascends; some Sebastianists transfer the *Encoberto* expectation onto him. Jesuit António Vieira (1608–1697) — the towering Portuguese-Catholic-Brazilian-mission preacher — produces *História do Futuro* (composed 1640s–1660s, partial editions; full posthumous publication 1718–1720) and *Clavis Prophetarum* — synthesizing Bandarra + Joachim of Fiore + Daniel 2 into the systematic *Quinto Império* doctrine. Vieira is tried by the Portuguese Inquisition (1665–1667) partly for these views and partly for his defence of the *Cristãos-Novos*.
5. **18th-c. Sebastianism becomes literary-cultural rather than political (1700–1850).** As Bourbon dynasties stabilize, the political-prophetic urgency fades; Sebastianism survives as a cultural-mythological motif, particularly in folk poetry, popular pamphlets, and the persistent rural belief that Sebastian sleeps in a hidden cave or island and will emerge.
6. **Romantic-nationalist recovery (1850–1910).** The 19th-c. Portuguese Romantic generation (Garrett, Herculano, Antero de Quental, Oliveira Martins) treats Sebastianism as a foundational national myth; Oliveira Martins's *História de Portugal* (1879) explicitly frames Alcácer Quibir as the great national trauma.
7. **Brazilian transplantation: Canudos and northeast messianism (1893–1897).** Antônio Conselheiro's Canudos movement in Bahia preaches the return of *D. Sebastião* as messianic figure; the Brazilian republic's military response (1896–1897 campaign destroying Canudos) is the subject of Euclides da Cunha's *Os Sertões* (1902). The transplantation of Sebastianism into Lusophone-Atlantic peasant millenarianism is a key data-point for the tradition's plasticity.
8. **Pessoan modernist consolidation (1910s–1935).** [[fernando-pessoa|Fernando Pessoa]] grew up immersed in this material. [[phase-7-037-pessoa-mensagem|*Mensagem*]] (1934) — the only Portuguese-language book of his poetry published in his lifetime — closes with *O Encoberto*: the third section of three (after *Brasão* and *Mar Português*), explicitly fusing Sebastianism with Rosicrucian-Hermetic-Thelema-influenced occultism into a modernist national epic.
9. **20th–21st-c. scholarly + esoteric continuation.** Mainstream historiography (Hermano Saraiva, José Mattoso, José Hermano Saraiva *História de Portugal*; international: Disney 2009 *A History of Portugal*) treats Sebastianism as cultural-mythological-political phenomenon. Within the Portuguese hermetic-symbology circle ([[manuel-j-gandra|Gandra]], Yvette Centeno, Dalila Pereira da Costa, António Quadros), Sebastianism is treated as a living spiritual-political tradition with continuous contemporary purchase; Gandra's *Joaquim de Fiore: Joaquimismo e Esperança Sebástica* (1999) is the principal recent monograph of this continuator-school.

## Defining doctrines

- ***O Encoberto* ("The Hidden / Shrouded One").** Sebastian (or, post-1640, João IV; in some readings, a future un-named restorer) is hidden but will return. The figure is consciously ambiguous — the "shrouding" preserves political-prophetic plausibility across changing dynasties.
- ***Quinto Império* (Fifth Empire).** Following Daniel 2's four-kingdoms schema (Babylon → Persia → Greece → Rome), Portugal will be the fifth-and-final world-empire — but a *spiritual* empire of universal Christianization rather than territorial conquest. Vieira develops this systematically; Pessoa renews it in *Mensagem*.
- **Joachimist tripartite age.** The world moves through three *Status*: the age of the Father (Old Testament law), the age of the Son (institutional Church, c. 33–1260 CE per Joachim's chronology), and the age of the Spirit (the coming age of monastic-spiritual freedom). Sebastianist-Joachimist synthesis places the Fifth Empire as the inauguration of the Age of the Spirit.
- **The cave / island / mist topology.** Folk Sebastianism places the Hidden King in a specific liminal location — a cave on the Atlantic coast, an island in the mist, the cape of São Vicente — from which he will emerge at the appointed hour. The geographical specificity is essential to the tradition's local-cultural rootedness.

## Relations to neighbors

- **vs. [[tradition-portuguese-hermeticism]]:** sister-overlapping; Sebastianism is the messianic-political branch of the broader Portuguese hermetic complex.
- **vs. [[tradition-order-of-christ]]:** the Templar-survival institution and the Sebastianist expectation share the same Portuguese-imperial-eschatological matrix; the Order's cross on caravel sails is a visual continuation of the Templar-Crusader expectation now reframed as Sebastianist-Fifth-Empire missionary expansion.
- **vs. [[tradition-judaism]] / [[tradition-kabbalah]] / Sabbateanism:** the Iberian Jewish-converso messianic substrate is a foundational input. Bandarra's *Trovas* are repeatedly accused of *judaizante* tendencies; the 1666 Sabbatean apocalyptic synchronizes with Portuguese-Sebastianist expectation in the *Cristão-Novo* communities. The [[event-sabbatean-apostasy-1666|Sabbatean apostasy of 1666]] reshapes both currents.
- **vs. Joachimism (Latin Christian apocalyptic):** the doctrinal-prophetic substrate. The Calabrian Joachim of Fiore's tripartite-age schema enters Iberian-monastic circulation in the 13th c. and becomes one of Bandarra's structural sources.
- **vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]:** complex. Sebastianism was tolerated when politically convenient (post-1640 Restoration); suppressed when inconvenient (Inquisition trials of Vieira, Index of *Trovas*). The relationship to institutional Portuguese Catholicism is best read as periodic-tense rather than fixed-doctrinal.

## Disputes

- **Is Sebastianism primarily Jewish-converso or Christian in origin?** The Lipiner / Saraiva / Valdez line emphasizes the converso-Jewish-messianic substrate (Bandarra's *judaizante* trial, the Cristão-Novo audiences for Sebastianist literature). The Cohen 1998 / Marques 2017 line emphasizes the Catholic-Joachimist-Iberian-Christian apocalyptic substrate. **Best position:** both, in convergence — Sebastianism is the syncretic product of two pre-existing currents that the 1578 trauma fused.
- **Is *Mensagem* (1934) Sebastianist in earnest or ironic?** Some Pessoa-scholarship (Lopes, Bréchon) reads *Mensagem* as Pessoa's most ideologically-committed work, sincerely Sebastianist-imperialist. Others (Zenith 2021, Mendia 2023) read it as a *modernist-occult re-presentation* of Sebastianism that maintains ironic-aesthetic distance even as it deploys the mythology. The book's publication context — Pessoa submitted it to the Salazarist Secretariado de Propaganda Nacional prize, won Category B in 1934 — complicates simple readings either way.
