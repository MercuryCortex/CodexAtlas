# Canonical Christianity (Earliest stratum)

## Overview
**Canonical Christianity** as treated in this node is the *earliest stratum* (~+30/+150) of the movement that emerged from Jesus's life and death — bracketed at the front by Jesus's crucifixion (~+30/+33), and at the back roughly by the closing of the apostolic period and the consolidation of the proto-orthodox canon (~+150). This node seeds the tradition that fuller Phase-4 and Phase-5 nodes will continue.

The earliest stratum is characterized by (a) **emergence within Second Temple Judaism** — Jesus, the disciples, Paul, and the Jerusalem community are all Jews operating within Jewish religious-political frameworks; (b) **a defining christological proclamation** of Jesus as crucified-and-resurrected Messiah and Lord; (c) **rapid Gentile-mission expansion** via the Pauline and Antiochene networks; (d) **internal diversity** — Pauline Gentile-Christianity, Jewish-Christian (James-led) Jerusalem-and-Diaspora Christianity, Johannine Christianity, Q-prophetic Galilean Christianity, proto-Gnostic Christianity all coexist; (e) **textual production** of a body of literature (epistles, gospels, apocalypses) that will eventually be canonized.

## Historical phases
1. **Jesus-movement** (~+30/+50): immediate post-resurrection community in Jerusalem; Paul's conversion (~+34); Antiochene Gentile mission (~+40); Jerusalem Council (~+49).
2. **Pauline expansion and earliest writings** (~+50/+65): Pauline missionary journeys; the seven undisputed letters; Paul's death (~+64/+65); Peter's death (~+64/+67).
3. **Post-apostolic / Gospel-writing era** (~+65/+100): destruction of Jerusalem and the Temple (+70); composition of Mark, Matthew, Luke-Acts, John; James the brother of Jesus killed (+62); first deutero-Pauline writings.
4. **Sub-apostolic / Apostolic Fathers period** (~+100/+150): 1 Clement, Ignatius, Polycarp, Didache, Shepherd of Hermas; Marcion's canon (~+140) and the proto-orthodox response; Valentinian Gnosticism rising; consolidation of the four-fold Gospel collection.

## Defining doctrines (forming, contested in the period)
- **Jesus as crucified-and-resurrected Christ / Messiah / Lord** — the foundational confession (1 Cor 15:3–5).
- **Faith-in-Christ as the basis of salvation** (Pauline formulation, with substantial intra-tradition variation about works, Torah, and Gentile inclusion).
- **Imminent eschatology** — return of Christ within the generation (1 Thess 4–5; Mk 13; gradually adjusted).
- **Spirit-empowered communal life** — gifts, prophecy, ecstatic experience (1 Cor 12–14; Acts 2; Didache).
- **Baptism and Eucharist** — ritual identification with Christ's death and ongoing communal meal.
- **Doctrinal consolidations** (Trinitarian theology, two-natures Christology) are *later* (Phase 4); the earliest stratum is theologically diverse.

## Relations to neighbors
- **vs. [[tradition-judaism]]**: born within Judaism; gradually separating through the late 1st and 2nd centuries (the "Parting of the Ways" — Boyarin 2004 problematizes the once-clean narrative).
- **vs. [[tradition-gnosticism]]**: the earliest stratum is in tension with proto-Gnostic currents; the proto-orthodox identity solidifies partly through anti-Gnostic polemic (Phase 4 — Irenaeus, Tertullian).
- **vs. [[tradition-mystery-cults]]**: contemporaneous; parallel-motif (initiation, savior, resurrection) but not direct dependence; the Christian-pagan polemic is strong.
- **vs. [[tradition-roman-religion]]**: hostile through three centuries of intermittent persecution.
- **vs. [[tradition-hellenistic-philosophy]]**: simultaneously embedded in (Pauline natural-law arguments, Johannine logos) and critical of (Pauline foolishness-of-the-cross).
