# Spiritualism (Modern)

## Overview
Religious-philosophical movement based on the premise that the dead can communicate with the living through *mediums* — typically by means of *séances* (rapping, table-tilting, spirit-voices, materialization, automatic writing, planchette). Modern Spiritualism dates conventionally from the Fox sisters' rappings in Hydesville, New York, 31 March 1848. Rapid 19th-century growth in the US and UK (estimated millions of adherents at peak ~1870s–1890s); enthusiastically embraced by some major Victorian figures (Arthur Conan Doyle, William Crookes, Alfred Russel Wallace).

## Historical phases
1. **Founding period** (1848–1888) — Fox sisters; rapid growth; major mediums (Home, Cora Hatch, Florence Cook); foundational works (Davis, Kardec in France).
2. **Investigative period** (~1880s onward) — Society for Psychical Research (London, 1882), American Society for Psychical Research (1885); systematic investigation; Margaret Fox's 1888 public confession that the rappings had been faked (she partially retracted in 1889).
3. **Post-WWI revival** — mass death of WWI produced a surge in Spiritualist interest (Conan Doyle's advocacy in this period).
4. **Decline and continuation** (mid-20th c. onward) — institutional Spiritualism declined while diffuse "channeling" and mediumship continued and re-emerged in New Age contexts.
5. **Brazilian Spiritism** (Kardecism) — distinct national tradition; substantial 20th- and 21st-century growth in Brazil and Latin America.

## Core texts
- Kardec, *The Spirits' Book* (1857), *The Book of Mediums* (1861), *The Gospel According to Spiritism* (1864).
- Davis, *The Principles of Nature, Her Divine Revelations* (1847).
- Conan Doyle, *The History of Spiritualism* (1926, 2 vols).

## Defining doctrines
- Continuity of consciousness after death.
- Communication between the living and the dead through trained or naturally gifted mediums.
- (In Kardec) reincarnation; cosmic moral evolution through successive lives.
- (In Anglophone Spiritualism) typically Christian-vocabulary but with the Trinity de-emphasized.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-theosophy]]: Theosophy emerged from and against Spiritualism (Blavatsky and Olcott met at Spiritualist gatherings); Theosophy's *Mahatmas* replaced Spiritualism's *spirits of the dead* as the source of revealed wisdom.
- vs. [[tradition-new-age]]: New Age channeling is a direct functional continuation of Spiritualist mediumship in a different theological register.
