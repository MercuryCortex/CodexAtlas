# Theravāda Buddhism

## Overview
Theravāda ("Doctrine of the Elders") is the southern transmission of Buddhism, the only surviving school of the early "Eighteen Schools" stratum. It preserves the Pali Tipiṭaka — the only complete early-Buddhist canon extant in an Indian language — and treats it as a closed, fully-authoritative scriptural collection. Theravāda is the dominant form of Buddhism in Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Cambodia, and Laos, and the doctrinal substrate of the modern global *vipassanā* and mindfulness movements. Its self-understanding is conservative: it claims to preserve what the Buddha actually taught, against the Mahāyāna polemic that characterizes it as *Hīnayāna* (Lesser Vehicle). Modern Theravāda scholarship (Gombrich, Bronkhorst, Schopen) supports the school's broad claim to early-Buddhist textual authenticity while contesting some of its later sectarian narratives.

## Historical phases
1. **Aśokan transmission (~-247)** — Mahinda (Aśoka's son or junior contemporary) brings Buddhism to Sri Lanka under King Devānaṃpiyatissa; Mahāvihāra monastery at Anurādhapura founded.
2. **Sri Lankan canonical period (~-247 to +400)** — oral transmission of the Pali canon by *bhāṇaka* reciter-lineages; first writing-down at Aluvihāra (~-29 BCE) under King Vaṭṭagāminī Abhaya; consolidation of the Mahāvihāra orthodoxy against the Abhayagiri / Jetavana fraternities.
3. **Buddhaghosa's classical synthesis (~5th c.)** — [[buddhaghosa]] arrives at the Mahāvihāra and composes the *Visuddhimagga* (*Path of Purification*) plus commentaries on most of the Pali canon, establishing the systematic doctrinal framework still definitive for the school.
4. **Southeast Asian transmission (~11th–13th c.)** — King Anawrahta of Pagan (Burma) adopts Theravāda ~1057; Sukhothai (Thailand) under Ramkhamhaeng ~13th c.; Khmer Cambodia and Laos follow. Sri Lankan ordination lineages re-seed the region multiple times after persecutions.
5. **Colonial-modernist period (~1850–1950)** — encounter with Western Christianity (Sri Lankan "Buddhist modernism" via Anagārika Dharmapāla); Olcott's *Buddhist Catechism* 1881; reformist re-engagement with the canon as anti-colonial cultural assertion.
6. **Modern global *vipassanā* movement (~1950 onward)** — Mahāsi Sayadaw and Sayagyi U Ba Khin in Burma; S.N. Goenka's lay teaching; Western reception via Joseph Goldstein, Sharon Salzberg, Jack Kornfield (the Insight Meditation Society, 1975); MBSR (Jon Kabat-Zinn, 1979) extracts secularized vipassanā into clinical psychology.

## Core texts
- [[phase-2-016-early-buddhist-suttas]] — Sutta Nipāta (Aṭṭhakavagga, Pārāyanavagga) — earliest stratum.
- [[phase-2-029-dhammapada]] — the verse anthology; most-translated Buddhist text.
- The full **Pali Tipiṭaka** in three baskets:
  - *Suttapiṭaka* — five Nikāyas (Dīgha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta, Aṅguttara, Khuddaka)
  - *Vinayapiṭaka* — monastic discipline
  - *Abhidhammapiṭaka* — systematic philosophy (seven books)
- [[phase-4-076-visuddhimagga]] — Buddhaghosa's *Path of Purification*; THE Theravāda systematic synthesis.
- Aṭṭhakathā commentaries (Buddhaghosa, Dhammapāla) on most canonical books.
- *Milindapañha* (the Theravāda Pali version — see [[phase-3-029-milindapanha]]).

## Key figures (founder + chief disciples + canonical transmitters)
- [[siddhartha-gautama-buddha]] — founder; the Theravāda canon's authority claim is direct from him.
- [[ananda-disciple]] — reciter of the Sutta-piṭaka at the First Council.
- [[mahakasyapa]] — convened the First Council; presiding figure of canonical formation.
- [[shariputra]] — Abhidhamma-tradition associated him with this systematic stratum.
- [[asoka-maurya]] — patron-emperor; without his missions Theravāda would not exist outside India.
- [[buddhaghosa]] — 5th-c. systematizer.

## Defining doctrines
- **The Four Noble Truths** ([[four-noble-truths]]) and **Noble Eightfold Path** ([[noble-eightfold-path]]) as the diagnostic frame.
- **Three Marks of Existence** — *anicca* (impermanence), *dukkha* (suffering), *anatta* ([[anatman-no-self]]).
- **Dependent origination** ([[pratitya-samutpada]]) — twelvefold formula.
- **Theravāda Abhidhamma** — analysis of experience into momentary *dhamma*s (mind-and-matter events), causal conditioning (*paṭṭhāna*), and the seven Abhidhamma-piṭaka books.
- **Path-result map** — sotāpanna (stream-enterer) → sakadāgāmi → anāgāmi → arahant (the four "noble persons").
- **Arahant as soteriological goal** (vs. Mahāyāna's bodhisattva-ideal — though Theravāda also recognizes the bodhisattva path for those aspiring to full Buddhahood).
- ***Suñña* (emptiness) — recognized but interpreted as emptiness-of-self in phenomena, not the Mādhyamika emptiness-of-inherent-existence.**

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-mahayana-buddhism]]: Mahāyāna polemic dubs Theravāda "*Hīnayāna*" (Lesser Vehicle); Theravāda historically rejects the Mahāyāna sūtras as non-Buddhavacana. Modern relations are ecumenical (World Fellowship of Buddhists; shared global Buddhist identity).
- vs. early Sectarian Buddhism: Theravāda is the only continuously-extant school of the ancient "Eighteen Schools"; it claims direct continuity with the Sthavira / Vibhajjavāda lineage. The Sarvāstivāda, Mahāsāṅghika, Pudgalavāda, etc., are known mainly through Chinese and Tibetan translations and through hostile mentions in Theravāda commentaries.
- vs. [[tradition-vedic-hinduism]]: shared karma/rebirth framework; opposition on *anātman*, Vedic authority, brahmin caste-priority.
