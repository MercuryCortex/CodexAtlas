# Hopi Traditional Religion

## Overview

The Hopi (*Hopituh Shi-nu-mu*, "Peaceful People") are a federally recognized nation occupying the Hopi Mesas in northeastern Arizona — a continuous occupation site for approximately 1,000+ years, making the Hopi villages among the oldest continuously inhabited settlements in North America. Hopi traditional religion is a richly layered cosmological-ceremonial system organized around:

1. **Four-worlds cosmology**: The current world is the Fourth World; humanity has emerged through three previous worlds destroyed by human failure to maintain right relationship (the emergence through the *sipapu* marks each transition)
2. **Kachina system**: Spirit beings (*Kachinas*, *Katsinas*) who live in the San Francisco Peaks and visit the Hopi mesas during half the year, embodied in ceremonial masked dances
3. **Ceremonial calendar**: A year-round cycle of ceremonies maintaining balance between the human and spirit worlds; the Soyal (winter solstice) and Niman (Home Dance, midsummer) frame the kachina season
4. **Spider Grandmother's cosmogony**: [[spider-grandmother-hopi]] (Kokyangwuti) as the supreme creative intelligence; creation by thought and song as the cosmogonic model

## The Four Worlds

Hopi cosmology describes four successive worlds, each ended by human failure to maintain the proper spiritual orientation (*the Hopi Way*):
- **First World** (Tokpela — endless space): Destroyed by fire
- **Second World** (Tokpa — dark midnight): Destroyed by ice
- **Third World** (Kuskurza): Destroyed by flood
- **Fourth World** (Túwaqachi — completed world): The present world; its maintenance requires ongoing ceremonial attention

Spider Grandmother guides the people through the sipapu (emergence hole) from each destroyed world to the next. The sipapu — the small hole in the floor of every kiva (ceremonial chamber) — represents this cosmic emergence point and is the architectural axis-mundi of Hopi ceremonial life.

## Relations to Neighboring Traditions

- vs. [[tradition-navajo]]: Historically antagonistic neighbors; distinct cosmological systems. Spider Woman appears in both but in different roles. The Navajo Nation surrounds Hopi lands; land disputes have been a modern source of conflict.
- vs. [[tradition-haudenosaunee]]: Both have divine-twin creation figures (Hopi Twin War Gods / Haudenosaunee Good Mind - Evil Mind twins) — parallel-form, not transmission.
