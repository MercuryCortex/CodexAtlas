# Jungian Depth Psychology

## Overview
The school of analytical psychology founded by C.G. Jung after his 1913 break from Freud. Treated here as a **quasi-religious tradition** as well as a therapeutic-psychological school because (a) its central concepts (archetype, Self, individuation, anima/animus, shadow) function for many practitioners as a comprehensive worldview; (b) the therapeutic practice (Jungian analysis) functions as a kind of initiation; (c) the Eranos Conferences and the Bollingen Foundation provided institutional structures parallel to religious-philosophical schools; (d) Jung's late writings (especially *Answer to Job*) are confessional-theological as much as scientific-psychological. Modern Jungianism has both *clinical* (training analysts, IAAP) and *cultural/spiritual* wings.

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-Jungian Freudian period** (1907–1913) — Jung as Freud's "Crown Prince"; the break.
2. **Confrontation-with-the-unconscious period** (1913–1930) — *Red Book*; the foundational visionary period.
3. **Mature analytical psychology** (1930–1944) — *Psychological Types*; first archetypal essays; the move toward alchemy.
4. **Alchemical period** (1944–1955) — *Psychology and Alchemy* through *Mysterium Coniunctionis*.
5. **Post-Jung development** (1961 onward) — Marie-Louise von Franz; the Zürich Jung Institute; archetypal psychology (Hillman); developmental schools (London Jungian post-1980s); academic Jung-studies (Sonu Shamdasani, Roderick Main, Robert Segal).

## Defining concepts
- The unconscious (personal and collective).
- Archetypes as inherited psychic structures.
- The Self as totality (= conscious + unconscious + their integration).
- Individuation as the lifelong psychological task.
- The shadow, anima/animus, persona as principal archetypal structures.
- Synchronicity as an acausal connecting principle.
- The alchemical *opus* as projected individuation.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. Freudian psychoanalysis: the foundational schism; Jung treats the unconscious as not exclusively biographical-repressed but also collective-archetypal.
- vs. [[tradition-comparative-religion-academic]]: Jung is on the threshold; some of his work is fully comparative-religion scholarship, some is therapeutic-clinical, some is confessional-theological.
- vs. [[tradition-theosophy]] and [[tradition-new-age]]: extensive vocabulary and conceptual sharing; some popular New Age reception treats Jungian categories as quasi-mystical structures.
