# Renaissance Hermeticism

## Overview
A current of Renaissance philosophical-religious-magical thought, originating in Florence in 1463 with Marsilio Ficino's Latin translation of the *Corpus Hermeticum* under Cosimo de' Medici's commission, and crystallizing into a self-conscious intellectual movement from then through the early-17th-century philological-redating crisis. The tradition treats Hermes Trismegistus as the most ancient sage in a chain of *prisci theologi* (Hermes → Orpheus → Pythagoras → Plato → and ultimately Christ) and reads the Hermetic corpus as primordial revelation compatible with — and even foundational for — Christianity. It combines Platonic metaphysics, Hermetic theology, natural magic, astrology, alchemy, and (from Pico onward) Kabbalah into a synthetic project of universal wisdom.

## Historical phases
1. **Florentine founding** (1463–1499) — Ficino's translations and *Theologia Platonica*; the Platonic Academy at Careggi.
2. **Pichian expansion** (1486–1494) — Pico della Mirandola adds Kabbalah and pushes the synthesis toward maximalist syncretism; papal condemnation 1487 marks the boundary with orthodoxy.
3. **Northern diffusion** (1500–1560) — Reuchlin in Germany (Christian Kabbalah), Agrippa across Northern Europe (systematic Renaissance magic), Paracelsus (alchemical-medical-mystical synthesis).
4. **High Renaissance radicalization** (1560–1600) — Bruno's pantheistic infinite-cosmos Hermeticism; Dee's English mathematical-magical synthesis; the end-point is Bruno's 1600 execution.
5. **Rosicrucian moment and last great syntheses** (1600–1660) — the German Rosicrucian manifestos (1614–1616); Fludd's English Paracelsian-Hermetic universal philosophy; Kircher's Jesuit universal-history project.
6. **Casaubon redating and gradual decline as scholarly project** (1614+) — Casaubon's philological demonstration that the Hermetica are Hellenistic late-antique compositions, not pre-Mosaic revelations, demolishes the prisca-theologia framework; subsequent Hermeticism survives as esoteric current rather than mainstream scholarly project.
7. **Continuation into Western esotericism** (1700+) — Freemasonry, 18th-c. Rosicrucianism, 19th-c. occult revival (Lévi, Golden Dawn), 20th-c. Western esotericism (Theosophy, modern Hermetic orders).

## Defining doctrines
- *Prisca theologia*: a single ancient revealed wisdom, recoverable through the chain of ancient sages.
- The cosmos as living, animate, intelligible through symbolic correspondences (microcosm-macrocosm).
- The human as *deus terrenus* — earthly god, microcosm of the cosmos, magical operator.
- Hermes Trismegistus as the pre-Mosaic or contemporary-with-Moses prophet of natural revelation (until Casaubon).
- Christian compatibility — the project is Christianizing, not pagan-rejectionist.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-hermeticism]] (Phase 4): direct manuscript-transmission descendant. The Phase-4 Hermetic corpus is the Phase-6 source.
- vs. [[tradition-christian-kabbalah]]: tightly coupled — Pico, Reuchlin, Agrippa, Fludd, and Kircher are all both Hermetic and Kabbalist.
- vs. [[tradition-rosicrucianism]]: 17th-c. descendant / parallel current.
- vs. [[tradition-neoplatonism]]: direct revival — Plotinus, Iamblichus, Proclus are foundational sources.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: substantial Catholic intellectual current early (Ficino was a priest under Medici-papal protection), increasingly suspect later (Pico condemned, Bruno executed).
