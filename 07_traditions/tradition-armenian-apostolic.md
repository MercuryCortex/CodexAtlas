# Armenian Apostolic Church

## Overview
The Armenian Apostolic Church is the national church of Armenia and one of the four foundational members of the Oriental Orthodox communion (along with [[tradition-coptic-orthodox|Coptic]], [[tradition-syriac-orthodox|Syriac]], and [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo|Ethiopian Tewahedo]]). Its traditional founding date — **King Tiridates III's conversion under Gregory the Illuminator in ~301 CE** — gives Armenia the claim of being **the first state to adopt Christianity as state religion**, ~12 years before the Edict of Milan (313). The church's apostolic-foundation tradition (Thaddeus and Bartholomew evangelizing Armenia in the 1st c.) supplies the "Apostolic" of its name. Doctrinally Miaphysite (rejecting [[event-council-of-chalcedon-451|Chalcedon 451]]), the Armenian Apostolic Church is in full communion with the other Oriental Orthodox churches.

## Historical phases
1. **Apostolic foundation traditions (1st c.)** — Thaddeus (Addai), Bartholomew (traditional martyrdoms in Armenia).
2. **State conversion (~301)** — King Tiridates III, baptized by Gregory the Illuminator. Possibly the **first Christian state in history**, though the precise date is debated (some scholars place the conversion later, ~314 CE, post-Milan).
3. **Mashtots's alphabet + Armenian Bible (~405)** — Mesrop Mashtots invents the 36-letter Armenian alphabet; in collaboration with Catholicos Sahak Partev, translates the Bible into Armenian over the next 30 years. The 5th c. is the "Golden Age" of Armenian Christian literature.
4. **Council of Dvin / rejection of Chalcedon (506, 555)** — the Armenian Church formally rejects the [[event-council-of-chalcedon-451|Chalcedonian Definition]] at successive councils of Dvin; Armenia and Georgia (which accepts Chalcedon) part ways theologically.
5. **Arab + Byzantine periods (~640-1045)** — Armenia as a buffer zone; the Bagratid kingdom of Ani.
6. **Cilician Armenian Kingdom (1198-1375)** — Armenian state in exile after Seljuk conquest; Crusader contact; period of Latin-Armenian ecumenical attempts; the Catholicos Nerses IV Shnorhali corresponds with Byzantine Constantinople on Christological reunion.
7. **Ottoman + Safavid periods (~1500-1894)** — Armenian millet under Ottoman + dhimmi status under Safavid Iran; the Armenian Patriarchate of Constantinople and the Armenian Patriarchate of Jerusalem are established as ecclesiastical centers.
8. **Hamidian massacres (1894-1896) + Armenian Genocide (1915-1923)** — Ottoman state-orchestrated extermination of Anatolian Armenians; ~1.5 million killed; the entire Anatolian Armenian Christian world is destroyed. The Genocide is the central event of modern Armenian identity.
9. **Soviet period (1920-1991)** — the Armenian SSR is officially atheist but tolerated the church as a national-cultural institution.
10. **Independent Republic of Armenia (1991+)** — the church regains state-church status; Etchmiadzin remains the global Catholicosate.

## Core texts
- **Armenian Bible** (Mashtots-Sahak translation, ~5th c.) — the foundational Armenian canonical text; some Armenian biblical mss preserve readings closer to the Old Greek than the Greek-canonical text.
- **Movses Khorenatsi**, *Patmut'iwn Hayots'* (*History of the Armenians*) — the foundational Armenian historical narrative (dating disputed: 5th c. per traditional Armenian view; 8th c. per modern critical scholarship — Garsoian).
- **Eznik of Kolb**, *Refutation of the Sects* (5th c.) — Armenian patristic polemic against Zoroastrians, Marcionites, Stoics, and others.
- **Catholicos Nerses IV Shnorhali**'s extensive corpus of hymns + theological treatises + correspondence (12th c.).
- **Liturgy** — the Armenian Holy Liturgy / *Patarag* (closely related to the Syrian and Cappadocian liturgical families, sung in Classical Armenian *grabar*).

## Key persons
- **Gregory the Illuminator** (~257-331) — foundational evangelist; converts King Tiridates III; first Catholicos.
- **King Tiridates III** (~250-330) — first Christian king of Armenia; co-founder of the state-church.
- **Mesrop Mashtots** (362-440) — inventor of the Armenian alphabet; co-translator of the Armenian Bible.
- **Catholicos Sahak Partev** (~338-439) — co-translator with Mashtots.
- **Eznik of Kolb** (5th c.) — foundational patristic theologian.
- **Movses Khorenatsi** — the foundational Armenian historian.
- **Catholicos Nerses IV Shnorhali** (~1102-1173) — the "Grace-Filled" Catholicos; bridge to Byzantine theology.

## Defining doctrines
- **Miaphysite Christology** — see [[miaphysite-christology]]; rejects [[event-council-of-chalcedon-451|Chalcedon]] as Nestorian-leaning.
- **Distinctive Christian-Armenian national identity** — the church-state-language fusion of Armenian identity dates from the Mashtots alphabet onward.
- **The Armenian biblical canon** — broadly similar to the Eastern Orthodox / Septuagintal canon plus some unique features (e.g., 3 Corinthians is canonical in some Armenian mss).
- **Distinctive liturgical practices** — unleavened bread in the Eucharist (like the Latin West but unlike Greek East); communion in both kinds intinctively.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. **Georgian Orthodox Church**: the closest Christian neighbor (the only other Caucasian state-Christian church). Both originated in 4th-c. evangelism; Georgian accepted Chalcedon (~607); Armenian rejected it (~506, 555). They have been formally not in communion since.
- vs. [[tradition-oriental-orthodox]] sister churches: in full communion.
- vs. **Russian Orthodox Church**: Russian Empire intermittently protected Armenian Christians from Ottoman/Safavid persecution; not in formal communion.
- vs. **Roman Catholic Church**: Armenian Catholic Church (Eastern Catholic, est. ~1742 from Cilician Armenian families) is in full communion with Rome but is a separate jurisdiction; Armenian Apostolic Church is not in communion with Rome.
- vs. **Islam (Ottoman + Persian)**: dhimmi millet status; complex relationship punctuated by mass violence (Hamidian massacres 1894-96; Armenian Genocide 1915-1923).
