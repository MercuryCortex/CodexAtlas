# Vajrayāna Buddhism

## Overview
Vajrayāna ("Adamantine Vehicle" / "Diamond Vehicle") is the Tantric form of Mahāyāna Buddhism that emerged in India ~500–700 CE and became the dominant Buddhist form in Tibet, Bhutan, Mongolia, and the Himalayan regions, with substantial East Asian presence (Japanese Shingon, Tendai esoteric). It is doctrinally Mahāyāna — same emptiness doctrine, same bodhisattva ideal — but ritually-methodologically distinct: practice through *deity yoga* (visualization of oneself as an awakened Buddha-figure), *mantra* recitation, elaborate *maṇḍala* ritual, somatic disciplines (breath, subtle-body, sexual yogas in some lineages), and master-disciple transmission lineages (*paramparā*) that are themselves a central feature of the path.

## Historical phases
1. **Late Indian Tantric Buddhism (~500–1200)** — emergence of the Kriya, Carya, Yoga, and Anuttarayoga tantra-classes; great Indian universities (Nālandā, Vikramaśīla, Odantapurī).
2. **Early diffusion to Tibet (Snga-dar, ~7th–9th c.)** — Padmasambhava and Trisong Detsen; foundation of Samye (~779); Bön-Buddhist negotiation; collapse of imperial sponsorship under Langdarma (~840).
3. **Later diffusion (Phyi-dar, ~10th–11th c.)** — Atiśa's mission (~1040) and the Kadampa school; Marpa's translations and the Kagyu lineage; Sakya formation. The Sarma (New Translation) schools emerge.
4. **Tibetan classical period (~1200–1400)** — formation of the Nyingma, Sakya, Kagyu sub-schools and (later) Gelug; massive composition of indigenous Tibetan commentary, terma literature, and ritual.
5. **Gelug consolidation (~1400 onward)** — Tsongkhapa (1357–1419) founds the Gelug school; Dalai Lama lineage from Gendün Drub (~1450); Gelug becomes politically dominant in central Tibet.
6. **East Asian transmissions** — Shingon (Kūkai, ~806 in Japan) and Tendai (Saichō, ~805); independently developed East Asian esoteric Buddhism.
7. **Modern (~1850 onward)** — Tibetan diaspora (post-1959); global transmission.

## Core texts
- Indian Tantras: Guhyasamāja, Cakrasaṃvara, Hevajra (Anuttarayoga), Kālacakra (~11th c. — the last great Indian Tantric system).
- Tibetan tantric corpus: the *Kangyur* (~translated Buddhavacana, 100+ vols) and *Tengyur* (~translated commentaries, 200+ vols).
- Terma literature: [[phase-5-029-bardo-thodol]] (Karma Lingpa, ~14th c.); Longchenpa's Dzogchen corpus; many other revealed-treasure texts.
- Dzogchen and Mahāmudrā instruction-corpus.

## Pantheon
- Primordial Buddhas: [[vajradhara]] (Sarma schools); [[samantabhadra-buddha]] (Nyingma).
- Tantric Buddha-families and consorts: the Five Dhyāni Buddhas and their consorts.
- Bodhisattvas, especially [[tara]] and her 21 forms.
- Yidam (meditation deities): Cakrasaṃvara, Hevajra, Vajrayoginī, Yamāntaka, Kālacakra, Guhyasamāja.
- Wrathful protectors (*dharmapālas*): [[mahakala]], Yama, the *Mahākāli*-Palden Lhamo cycle.
- [[padmasambhava]] — Guru Rinpoche; the deified-historical founder.

## Defining doctrines
- **Mantra and deity yoga** — operative ritual identification with awakened Buddha-figures.
- **Two stages** — *generation stage* (visualization-construction) and *completion stage* (subtle-body and emptiness-realization).
- **Master-disciple transmission** (*paramparā*) — initiation (*abhiṣeka* / *wang*) as enabling condition.
- **Three roots** — guru (root of blessing), yidam (root of accomplishment), dharma-protector (root of activity).
- **Mahāmudrā** (Kagyu, Gelug) and **Dzogchen** (Nyingma) — apex teachings on the direct recognition of mind's nature.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-mahayana-buddhism]]: doctrinal continuity, ritual-methodological distinction.
- vs. Kashmir Shaivism (Trika) — *parallel-tantric*: structural parallels (deity yoga, maṇḍala, mantra theory) are extensive; Sanderson 2009 argues Buddhist appropriation of Śaiva originals; Davidson 2002 contests.
- vs. Bön (indigenous Tibetan religion): substantial mutual influence in formative Tibetan period; modern Bön is largely indistinguishable from Buddhist Nyingma in many respects.
