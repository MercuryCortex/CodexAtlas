# Cao Dai — Third Divine Alliance

## Overview
Cao Dai (高臺, "High Tower / Supreme Being") is a Vietnamese syncretic religion founded in 1926 in Tây Ninh Province by a circle of Vietnamese civil servants led by Ngô Văn Chiêu (1878–1932) and institutionalized by Phạm Công Tắc (1890–1959). The religion presents itself as the **Third Divine Alliance** between God and humanity: the First Alliance was through Moses and the Hebrew prophets, the Second through Buddha, Confucius, Laozi, and Jesus, and the Third — Cao Dai — synthesizes all previous revelations into a complete and final religion.

Its scriptures were received through *cơ bút* (spirit-writing planchette sessions) from divine beings communicating through the planchette — a technique derived from French Spiritism (Allan Kardec, *Le Livre des Esprits*, 1857), encountered by Vietnamese civil servants through their French colonial employers. The transmission chain — French 19th-century Spiritism → Vietnamese colonial context → new syncretic world religion — is one of the most unexpected syncretism-as-transmission chains in recorded religious history.

With 4–6 million adherents in Vietnam and diaspora communities in France, the United States, and Australia, Cao Dai is the world's largest explicitly syncretic religion and the most-cited example in modern religious studies of deliberate theological syncretism as founding program.

## The saint-pantheon
Cao Dai's divine hierarchy includes figures from world religion and history venerated equally on the main altar of the Tây Ninh Cathedral (the Holy See):
- **The Supreme Being** (*Cao Đài*) — the monotheistic God above all
- **Quan Âm (Guanyin)** — Bodhisattva of Compassion
- **Three patron saints of the Third Alliance**: Sun Yat-sen (Chinese revolutionary), **Victor Hugo** (French novelist), and Nguyễn Bỉnh Khiêm (16th-century Vietnamese poet-prophet)
- **Buddha, Confucius, Laozi, Jesus** — venerated as prior divine teachers

## Cross-tradition significance
- **The vault's thesis made conscious**: Cao Dai is the only religion that explicitly enacts the vault's fundamental thesis — that all major religious traditions share structural roots — as its founding theological program. It puts Buddha, Confucius, Laozi, Jesus, and Victor Hugo on the same altar and venerates them equally.
- **Progressive-revelation structure**: Cao Dai's three-alliance theology is structurally identical to Islamic progressive revelation (prophets → Muhammad), Bahai progressive revelation (prophets → Bahaullah), and Mormon progressive revelation (Old + New Testament → Book of Mormon). All four: "I complete the previous traditions" theologies.
