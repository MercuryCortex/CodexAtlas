# Manichaeism

## Overview
A world religion founded in mid-third-century Sasanian Mesopotamia by the prophet [[mani]] (216–277), claiming to fulfill and supersede the partial revelations of all prior prophets — Adam, Seth, Enoch, the Buddha, Zoroaster, and Jesus. The system rests on rigorous *cosmic dualism*: two co-eternal principles (the Father of Greatness in the Realm of Light; the Prince of Darkness in the Realm of Darkness) whose contact produces the present cosmos of mixed Light and Darkness. The Manichaean cosmic drama is the *separation* of trapped Light from Matter, accomplished through the cosmic wheels, the celestial bodies, and ultimately the ascetic discipline of the Manichaean Elect.

## Historical phases
1. **Mani's mission** (240–277) — composition of the canonical scriptures; Sasanian royal favor under Shapur I; execution under Bahram I.
2. **Diaspora** (3rd–5th c.) — Manichaeism spreads across the Roman Empire (Augustine's nine Manichaean years), Iran (under persecution), Central Asia.
3. **Asian flourishing** (6th–11th c.) — Sogdian, Old Turkic, Chinese Manichaean texts; Uighur state religion 762/763; Chinese Mingjiao tradition.
4. **Decline** (11th–14th c.) — progressive suppression; final Chinese Manichaean communities documented into Ming dynasty.

## Core texts
Mani's canon of seven: Living Gospel, Treasure of Life, Pragmateia, Book of Mysteries, Book of Giants, Šābuhragān, Epistles. Most survive only in fragments.

## Defining doctrines
- Two co-eternal principles (Light and Darkness).
- Three Times: initial separation, present mixture, final separation.
- Five emanations from the Father; five sons of the Living Spirit; five Manichaean elements.
- Prophetic succession ending in Mani as seal of the prophets.
- Two-tier community: Elect (strict ascetics) and Hearers (lay supporters).

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-zoroastrianism]]: structurally adjacent (cosmic dualism); Mani's Šābuhragān uses Zoroastrian vocabulary; Zoroastrian priesthood (Kartir) persecuted Manichaeism.
- vs. [[tradition-gnosticism]]: shared gnosis-soteriology and Light-spark anthropology; more rigorous dualism than Sethian.
- vs. [[tradition-mandaeism]]: shared southern-Mesopotamian Jewish-Christian baptist milieu (Mani's Elchasaite origin).
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: jurisdictional rival; Augustine's anti-Manichaean polemic is a major source.
