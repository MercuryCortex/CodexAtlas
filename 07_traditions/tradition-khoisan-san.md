# Khoisan / San Traditional Religion

## Overview
The religion of the San (Bushman) peoples of southern Africa is one of the oldest continuously-documented religious systems on Earth. The Khoisan language family — Khoekhoegowab, !Kung, /Xam (extinct), Naro, Hai//om, others — covers a population concentrated today in Botswana, Namibia, Angola, and South Africa, descended from the original hunter-gatherer populations of southern Africa. Religious continuity is evidenced by rock-art (some over 27,000 years old at Apollo 11 Cave, Namibia) plus extensive 19th–21st-c ethnographic record.

## Pantheon and cosmology

- **[[kaang-san]] (Kaggen, the Mantis)** — creator-trickster, especially in /Xam tradition. Took the form of the praying mantis; created the eland; made the moon; etc.
- **Hishe / Heiseb / Gaona / various Kalahari high-god names** — high-god equivalents in other Khoisan branches.
- **The trickster-creator fusion** — distinctively, the San creator-figure IS the trickster (Kaang). Most world religions split these roles; San preserves them as a single figure.
- **Shamanic spirit-world** — accessed via the trance-dance and the *!gi:xa* (Ju/'hoansi term) or equivalent specialist roles in other branches.

## The trance-dance and *!gi:xa* shamanism
The San curing-dance (*n/om-tchai* among Ju/'hoansi) is one of the most-documented contemporary hunter-gatherer shamanic systems. Specialists called *!gi:xa* (or other terms in other branches) enter altered states through dancing around a central fire, accumulating *n/om* (a heating spiritual energy) until they pass into trance. In trance they extract sickness from community members, contact the spirits of the dead, and engage Kaang and other cosmic figures. Lewis-Williams (1981) and Katz (1982 *Boiling Energy*) are the standard treatments.

The trance-dance is one anchor for Lewis-Williams's argument (controversial but influential, 1988 *Signs of All Times*; 2002 *The Mind in the Cave*) that European Upper Paleolithic cave art is shamanic — based on structural parallels with San rock-art that is independently documented to be shamanic-trance imagery. The argument is contested in archaeology; the SAN-side documentation is solid.

## Rock-art tradition
San rock-paintings depict: trance-dance scenes; eland (Kaang's animal); shamans transforming into half-human-half-animal forms (therianthropic figures); rain-animals (cosmic beings appearing in trance); geometric entoptic patterns (perceived in early trance states, per Lewis-Williams's neuropsychological model). The tradition is continuous from Late Pleistocene to ~1880 (last documented San painters in southern Drakensberg).

The Apollo 11 Cave in southern Namibia preserves painted stones securely dated to ~27,000 BP — among the oldest dated representational art globally. This makes the San rock-art tradition one of the longest-continuous religious-artistic traditions on Earth.

## The Bleek-Lloyd /Xam archive
In the 1870s–1880s, philologist Wilhelm Bleek and his sister-in-law Lucy Lloyd worked with /Xam-language informants (mostly prison-laborers) at the Cape Colony to record the Kaggen-narratives, /Xam cosmology, and the /Xam language. The /Xam went extinct as a community in the early 20th c. — the Bleek-Lloyd archive is the only substantive surviving record of their religious tradition. The published *Specimens of Bushman Folklore* (1911) is the foundational text; the manuscript archive is at the University of Cape Town.

## Diaspora and contemporary status
San communities today face severe pressure: dispossession from ancestral land (Botswana CKGR expulsions; Namibian land conflicts), mass poverty, alcohol dependence, language loss, and integration into adjacent Bantu economies. The trance-dance survives — well-documented among contemporary Ju/'hoansi in Botswana and Namibia (Katz 1982; Marshall Thomas 1959). Kaang/Kaggen narratives circulate in modified form. The /Xam-tradition is gone except for the Bleek-Lloyd archive.

## Cross-tradition position
San religion is significant for comparative religion because:
1. **Antiquity** — among the oldest continuously-documented traditions (rock-art ~27,000 BP).
2. **Hunter-gatherer baseline** — the San are among the few remaining hunter-gatherer societies; their religion offers data on what hunter-gatherer religion looks like, against which the pastoralist + agricultural religious systems can be contextualized.
3. **Creator-trickster fusion** — Kaang's role as BOTH supreme-creator AND trickster-figure suggests a possibly-older theological pattern that other traditions later split.
4. **Shamanism** — the *!gi:xa* practice is one of the best-documented contemporary shamanic systems and informs broader comparative shamanism studies (Lewis-Williams 2002).

The cross-tradition wiring is mostly `scholarly-parallel` — no transmission claims connect San religion to other regions; the comparative value is parallel-form structural comparison.
