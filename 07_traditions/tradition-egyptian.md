# Ancient Egyptian Religion

## Overview
The religion of pharaonic Egypt, attested for roughly three and a half millennia (from late Predynastic, ~3500 BCE, through the closure of the last Egyptian temple at Philae in 535 CE under Justinian). Egyptian religion is *polytheistic* with a strong tendency to *composite* deities (Atum-Ra, Amun-Ra, Ptah-Sokar-Osiris, Ra-Horakhty) and *henotheistic* tendencies (different periods elevate different gods to nominal supremacy without eliminating others). Its theological structures: (a) cosmogony emphasizing self-emergence out of primordial waters; (b) the daily and nightly solar cycle as cosmic rhythm; (c) the Osirian afterlife with judgment of the dead; (d) divine kingship — the pharaoh as Horus in life and Osiris in death; (e) Ma'at, cosmic order/truth/justice, as the principle the pharaoh must uphold.

## Historical phases
1. **Predynastic** (~5000–3100 BCE) — emergence of regional cults; earliest divine iconography.
2. **Early Dynastic** (~3100–2686 BCE) — unification of Upper and Lower Egypt; royal Horus name; first temple precincts.
3. **Old Kingdom** (~2686–2181 BCE) — pyramid age; [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] inscribed in royal pyramids beginning with [[unas]].
4. **First Intermediate** (~2181–2055 BCE) — political fragmentation; "democratization of the afterlife" begins.
5. **Middle Kingdom** (~2055–1650 BCE) — [[phase-1-009-coffin-texts]]; mature Osirian afterlife; literary classical Egyptian.
6. **Second Intermediate / Hyksos** (~1650–1550 BCE) — Levantine "Hyksos" rule of the Delta; introduction of West Semitic deities into Egyptian religious vocabulary.
7. **New Kingdom** (~1550–1069 BCE) — Egyptian empire; [[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]]; theological developments around Amun-Ra at Thebes; the Amarna reform under [[akhenaten]] (~1353–1336 BCE) and its reversal; underworld books (Amduat, Book of Gates, Book of the Earth).
8. **Third Intermediate / Late Period** (~1069–525 BCE) — Libyan, Kushite, Saite dynasties; theological "personal piety" movement.
9. **Persian and Greco-Roman** (~525 BCE – CE 395) — Ptolemaic-Roman Egypt; Hellenistic Isis-mysteries spread Mediterranean-wide; Hermetic Greek-Egyptian syncretism; closure of pagan temples by Christian Roman emperors.
10. **Final closure** — Temple of Isis at Philae closed by Justinian, 535 CE — conventional end of pharaonic religion.

## Core texts
- [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] — earliest funerary corpus, Old Kingdom.
- [[phase-1-009-coffin-texts]] — Middle Kingdom successor and elaboration.
- [[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]] — New Kingdom and after, the most widely-known Egyptian religious corpus.
- [[phase-1-011-great-hymn-to-aten]] — the Amarna interlude.

- (later phases) the underworld books (Amduat, Book of Gates, Book of Caverns, Book of the Earth, Litany of Ra), the Memphite Theology / Shabaka Stone, hymns to Amun-Ra, Demotic ritual texts, Hermetic Greek-Egyptian corpus.

## Pantheon
- [[ra]] — sun; Heliopolis; daily cycle.
- [[atum]] — primordial self-emergent creator; Heliopolis.
- [[ptah]] — creator by thought-and-word; Memphis.
- [[osiris]] — lord of the dead; dying-rising god; Abydos.
- [[isis]] — great mother and magician; Philae.
- [[horus]] — falcon sky-god; the living pharaoh; Edfu.
- [[set]] — desert, storm, disorder; Ombos.
- [[thoth]] — writing, knowledge, recorder of judgment; Hermopolis.
- [[anubis]] — embalming, psychopomp; Cynopolis.
- [[maat]] — cosmic order personified; pervasive.
- [[aten]] — the visible solar disc; sole god of Amarna.
- [[nun]] — primordial waters.

## Defining doctrines
- **Cosmogony of emergence.** Cosmos arises by self-actualization out of [[nun]] (primordial waters); creation by [[atum]] (Heliopolitan), by [[ptah]]'s thought-and-word (Memphite), or by the Ogdoad (Hermopolitan).
- **Ma'at as cosmic order.** Truth, justice, and right order are a single principle; the pharaoh's primary duty is to maintain ma'at.
- **Divine kingship.** Pharaoh as Horus in life, Osiris in death; the king is the cosmos's pivot.
- **Afterlife judgment.** Earliest fully developed afterlife judgment scene in human religion: weighing of the heart against the feather of ma'at in the Hall of Two Truths.
- **Solar theology.** Daily sun-cycle as cosmic master-rhythm; the king is "son of Ra"; the dead king ascends to join Ra.
- **Composite and henotheistic flexibility.** Egyptian theology routinely compounds deities (Atum-Ra, Amun-Ra) and elevates one without eliminating others. The Amarna reform's radical exclusion was an anomaly the system rejected.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-sumerian-mesopotamian]]: parallel cultural development, modest direct exchange in the third and second millennia, significant Late Bronze contact (e.g., [[phase-1-012-amarna-letters]]).
- vs. [[tradition-canaanite-ugaritic]]: progressive contact across the Late Bronze and Iron Age; the Hyksos period and Egyptian imperial holdings in the Levant produced substantial cultural exchange (Set-Baal syncretism, Astarte and Anat in Egyptian cult).
- vs. early Israel and the Hebrew Bible: extensive cultural and religious contact (Egyptian wisdom literature on Hebrew Proverbs and Ecclesiastes; the Aten hymn and Psalm 104; Israelite negative memory of Egypt as the Exodus paradigm); the Egyptian-Hebrew nexus is one of the longest-studied comparative topics.
- vs. Hellenistic Greek and Roman religion: massive influence in the Hellenistic period via the Isis-mysteries, Hermetic synthesis (Thoth → Hermes Trismegistus), and Alexandrian theology that bridges into early Christianity.
