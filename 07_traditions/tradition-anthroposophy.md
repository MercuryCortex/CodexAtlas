# Anthroposophy

## Overview
Christian-centered Western esoteric tradition founded by Rudolf Steiner in 1913 after his break from the Theosophical Society (over the TS leadership's promotion of Krishnamurti as the future World Teacher). Anthroposophy retains substantial Theosophical structure (sevenfold cosmology, reincarnation, karma) while recentering the cosmological narrative on the **Christ-event** of the Crucifixion as the cosmic-evolutionary turning point. Steiner produced a vast lecture-and-book corpus and founded a number of applied programs that continue to grow: Waldorf education (first school 1919), biodynamic agriculture (1924), Anthroposophical medicine, eurythmy (a movement art).

## Historical phases
1. **Theosophist period** (1902–1913) — Steiner as General Secretary of the German Section of the TS.
2. **Founding of Anthroposophical Society** (1913–1925) — Goetheanum buildings 1 (1920, burned 1922) and 2 (begun 1924, completed 1928) at Dornach, Switzerland.
3. **Post-Steiner Anthroposophy** (1925 onward) — General Anthroposophical Society at Dornach; multiple national branches; substantial application programs; Anthroposophy banned by the Nazi regime 1935 (though some Anthroposophist Nazis existed); postwar revival.

## Defining doctrines
- The Mystery of Golgotha as the cosmic-evolutionary turning point.
- The cosmic Christ-being incarnates in Jesus at the Baptism (the "Jordan event").
- Sevenfold cosmic-evolutionary stages (Saturn → Sun → Moon → Earth → Jupiter → Venus → Vulcan).
- Reincarnation and karma in a Christian-cosmological frame.
- Supersensible cognition as a developed faculty.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-theosophy]]: direct parent-tradition with major reorientation.
- vs. mainstream Christianity: the Christology is unusual (cosmic Christ-being who incarnates at the Baptism); the broader Anthroposophical Christology has had limited mainstream Christian uptake.
