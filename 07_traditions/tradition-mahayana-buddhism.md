# Mahāyāna Buddhism

## Overview
Mahāyāna ("Great Vehicle") is the major branch of Buddhism that emerged in India ~100 BCE–100 CE, characterized by: (1) the bodhisattva ideal — aspiration to full Buddhahood for the benefit of all beings rather than personal *arhat*-style liberation; (2) the doctrine of universal Buddha-nature — all beings possess the capacity for awakening; (3) the philosophical doctrine of *śūnyatā* (emptiness) — phenomena lack inherent self-existence; (4) elaborate cosmologies populated by celestial Buddhas (Amitābha, Akṣobhya, Vairocana) and bodhisattvas (Avalokiteśvara, Mañjuśrī); (5) substantial new sūtra corpus claiming Buddhavacana status. Mahāyāna became the dominant form of Buddhism across East Asia (China, Korea, Japan, Vietnam) and contributed the doctrinal substrate to Vajrayāna in Tibet and the Himalayas.

## Historical phases
1. **Emergence (~-100 to +100)** — earliest Mahāyāna sūtras (Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā); transition from earlier *Mahāsāṃghika* trajectories.
2. **Indian classical period (~100–500)** — Madhyamaka (Nāgārjuna, ~200; Candrakīrti ~600), Yogācāra (Asaṅga and Vasubandhu, ~350); the great sūtras (Lotus, Diamond, Heart, Avatamsaka, Lankavatara) reach standard form.
3. **Transmission to East Asia (~100–700)** — Silk Road transmission; Kumārajīva's Chinese translation school in Chang'an (~400); the formation of distinctively East Asian schools (Tiantai, Huayan, Pure Land, Chan).
4. **Chinese classical Mahāyāna (~600–1100)** — Tang dynasty flourishing; Xuanzang's pilgrimage and translation work; Chan / Zen consolidation.
5. **Spread to Korea (~400), Japan (~600), Vietnam (~1st c.)** — distinctive East Asian developments.
6. **Tibetan reception (~700 onward)** — Mahāyāna substrate of Tibetan Buddhism, integrated with Vajrayāna.
7. **Decline in India (~+1200)** — Muslim invasions and Hindu absorption.
8. **Modern (~+1850 onward)** — Western reception; modernist reform; global diaspora.

## Core texts
- [[phase-5-002-heart-sutra]] (~7th c. standard form) — the compressed Prajñāpāramitā essence.
- [[phase-5-004-platform-sutra-huineng]] (~780) — foundational text of Chan / Zen.
- Lotus Sutra (~100 BCE–200 CE) — universal salvation and the One Vehicle.
- Diamond Sutra (Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā) — compact wisdom-of-emptiness.
- Avatamsaka (Flower Ornament) Sutra — cosmic interpenetration; basis of Huayan philosophy.
- Pure Land sutras (Larger and Smaller Sukhāvatī-vyūha) — devotional access to Amitābha's pure land.
- Lankavatara Sutra — Yogācāra-influenced; early Chan canonical text.
- Madhyamaka (Nāgārjuna, *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*) and Yogācāra (Asaṅga, *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*) commentarial corpus.

## Pantheon (celestial Buddhas and bodhisattvas)
- Celestial Buddhas: Amitābha, Akṣobhya, Vairocana, Ratnasaṃbhava, Amoghasiddhi (the Five Dhyāni Buddhas).
- Bodhisattvas: Avalokiteśvara (compassion; East Asian Guanyin/Kannon), Mañjuśrī (wisdom), Maitreya (future Buddha), Kṣitigarbha (Jizō, hell-realm savior), Samantabhadra (universal goodness).
- [[prajnaparamita-goddess]] — the personified Perfection of Wisdom, "mother of all Buddhas."
- [[tara]] — feminine bodhisattva / Buddha (especially Vajrayāna).

## Defining doctrines
- **Bodhisattva ideal** — postpone personal nirvāṇa for the salvation of all beings.
- **Universal Buddha-nature** (*tathāgatagarbha*) — all beings have the capacity for full awakening.
- **Two Truths** — conventional truth (*saṃvṛti*) and ultimate truth (*paramārtha*), most fully developed in Madhyamaka.
- **Emptiness** (*śūnyatā*) — no phenomena have inherent self-existence; all are dependently originated.
- **Three Bodies of the Buddha** (*trikāya*) — *dharmakāya* (truth-body), *saṃbhogakāya* (enjoyment-body), *nirmāṇakāya* (manifestation-body).
- **Skillful means** (*upāya*) — the Buddha teaches differently to different audiences for their good.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-buddhism]] (Theravāda / early): Mahāyāna polemics characterize Theravāda as "*Hīnayāna*" (Lesser Vehicle); modern relations are more ecumenical.
- vs. [[tradition-vajrayana-buddhism]]: continuous in doctrinal substrate (Mahāyāna emptiness, bodhisattva ideal); diverges in ritual-tantric method.
- vs. [[tradition-zen]]: Zen is a Mahāyāna sub-tradition (Chan in China, Seon in Korea, Thiền in Vietnam, Zen in Japan).
