# Vedic Religion and early Hinduism

## Overview
The Vedic tradition and its development into what we now call Hinduism is the religion stemming from the Sanskrit corpus of the four Vedas (Rig, Sāma, Yajur, Atharva), elaborated through Brāhmaṇa exegesis, internalized in the Upaniṣads, and expanded through epic, Purāṇic, and tantric literature. It is the world's longest continuously transmitted religious tradition with surviving primary texts — ~3,500 years of unbroken oral and textual lineage, though its forms have transformed substantially across that span.

## Historical phases
1. **Early Vedic (~-1500 to -1000)** — Rig Veda family books composition in the Sapta Sindhu region; pastoral / semi-nomadic Indo-Aryan society; deva-centered hymnology.
2. **Mantra / late Vedic (~-1200 to -800)** — Composition of remaining Vedic Saṃhitās (Sāma, Yajur, Atharva); settlement of the Gangetic plain.
3. **Brāhmaṇa / Iron Age (~-900 to -600)** — Elaborate ritual systematization; Kuru-Pañcāla cultural center; emergence of Prajāpati cosmogony; the Aśvamedha and other great rites codified.
4. **Upaniṣadic / Axial (~-800 to -400)** — Philosophical introspection; Bṛhadāraṇyaka and Chāndogya; emergence of *ātman*-*brahman* identification and the karma-rebirth-liberation complex.
5. **Classical Hindu / epic-Purāṇic (~-400 to +600)** — Composition and elaboration of Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa, early Purāṇas, Dharma-śāstra; rise of theistic devotional traditions (Vaiṣṇavism, Śaivism).
6. **Medieval Hindu (~600 to 1750)** — Bhakti movements, Tantra, Vedanta commentaries (Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Madhva), regional theistic traditions.
7. **Modern Hindu (~1750 to present)** — Reform movements, colonial encounter, diaspora.

## Pantheon
- [[indra]] — Vedic king of devas; storm-warrior; demoted in classical era.
- [[agni]] — fire-god, sacrificial mediator.
- [[varuna]] — cosmic-order Asura; demoted to god of waters.
- [[soma]] — deified ritual substance; later moon-god.
- [[mitra-vedic]] — covenant-god, paired with Varuṇa.
- [[ushas]] — Indo-European dawn-goddess.
- [[rudra-shiva-early]] — outsider storm-god, becomes Śiva.
- [[prajapati]] — Brāhmaṇa-period creator; partly displaced by Brahmā.
- (Phase 3+: classical Hindu pantheon centered on Viṣṇu, Śiva, Devī)

## Defining doctrines
- **Veda as śruti** ("what was heard"): the texts are revealed, beginningless.
- **Yajña** (sacrifice) as central religious act, eventually internalized.
- **Ṛta** (cosmic order) → **Dharma** (in classical period).
- **Brahman = ātman** (Upaniṣadic identification).
- **Karma, rebirth, mokṣa** (Upaniṣadic, shared with Buddhism and Jainism).
- **Varṇāśrama-dharma** (the four-class system + four life-stages — Dharma-śāstra elaboration).

## MASSIVE wins — cross-tradition edges
- **Vedic Ṛta ↔ Zoroastrian Asha ↔ Egyptian Ma'at ↔ Confucian Lǐ (cosmic-order convergence)**: Vedic *ṛta* (cosmic order, truth, the principle underlying both natural regularity and ethical right-action) is the structural and etymological root of the Zoroastrian *Asha Vahishta* ("Best Truth," the supreme divine principle). The parallel extends independently to Egyptian *Ma'at* (truth/justice/cosmic-order as the goddess holding the scales) and Confucian *Lǐ* (cosmic-social ritual order). Four traditions on four continents arrived independently at the concept of a single cosmic-moral order governing both natural and social reality. The Vedic/Zoroastrian parallel is genetic (shared Proto-Indo-Iranian *\*Hṛta-*); the Egyptian and Confucian parallels are independent convergences. See [[tradition-zoroastrianism]].
- **Deva/Asura inversion ↔ Zoroastrian Daeva/Ahura inversion**: In the Rig Veda, *asura* is an honorific for Varuṇa and other great deities ("Lord"); *deva* is the standard divine title. In the classical period, *asura* shifts to mean "demon." Meanwhile in Zoroastrian religion, *ahura* (= Vedic *asura*) is the divine title (*Ahura Mazdā*) and *daēva* (= Vedic *deva*) is the demonic title — the precise inversion. Both shifts are documented in the texts. This [[asura-deva-inversion]] is the vault's clearest single case of two traditions inheriting identical vocabulary and applying it in systematically inverted theological evaluation — probably reflecting the polemical differentiation of proto-Zoroastrian from proto-Vedic religion in the Late Bronze Age Indo-Iranian sphere.
- **Vedic → Buddhist / Jain karma-rebirth-liberation framework (shared substrate with polemical modification)**: The karma-rebirth-*mokṣa*/nirvāṇa complex appears first in the Upaniṣadic texts (~800–400 BCE) and is taken over — with modification — by both Buddhism and Jainism (~500 BCE). Buddhism denies the *ātman* (permanent self) that Vedic tradition posits but retains karma and rebirth. Jainism retains *ātman* and karma but makes karma material (fine matter adhering to the soul). The shared framework and the precise points of divergence make this the most analytically useful single-tradition-generation event in the South Asian cluster. See [[tradition-buddhism]], [[tradition-jainism]].
- **Ātman = Brahman ↔ [[tradition-advaita-vedanta]] full elaboration ↔ Sufi *waḥdat al-wujūd***: The Upaniṣadic formula *tat tvam asi* ("that thou art" — Chāndogya 6.8–16) and *ahaṃ brahmāsmi* ("I am Brahman" — Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.10) identify the individual self (*ātman*) with the cosmic absolute (*Brahman*). Shankara's Advaita Vedanta (~700 CE) makes this the systematic foundation of all philosophy. The structural identity with Ibn Arabi's *waḥdat al-wujūd* ("unity of being," ~1200 CE) is complete: both posit one Being only, apparent multiplicity as the self-manifestation of the One, and awakening as recognition of this identity. The verbal parallel is exact: Vedic *ahaṃ brahmāsmi* / Sufi *Ana'l-Haqq* / Eckhartian "I and the Father are one." See [[tradition-advaita-vedanta]], [[tradition-sufism]].
- **Vedic → [[tradition-greek-religion]] Indo-European pantheon cognates**: The reconstructed Proto-Indo-European sky-father *\*Dyēus ph₂tḗr* generates Vedic *Dyauṣ Pitṛ* and Greek *Zeus* (both with "father" epithets; the Vedic divine name survives more archaically). The dawn-goddess *\*H₂éwsōs* generates Vedic *Uṣas* and Greek *Eos*, both described as "rosy-fingered," both daughters of the sky-father. These cognates are the direct evidence base for the Proto-Indo-European religious reconstruction — the Vedic texts are chronologically the closest to the ancestor tradition and thus the most important primary source for comparing Greek and other IE religious traditions.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-zoroastrianism]]: shared Indo-Iranian inheritance (Mitra/Mithra, Soma/Haoma, deva/daēva inverted). The single closest parallel tradition.
- vs. [[tradition-buddhism]] / [[tradition-jainism]]: rivals within the South Asian religious field from -500 onward. Shared karma-rebirth framework; opposed on *ātman*, Veda-authority, ritual.
- vs. [[tradition-greek-religion]]: distant Indo-European cognates (Dyauṣ-Zeus, Uṣas-Eos); no direct contact in Phase 2 (some scholars argue Phase 3 contact via Hellenistic Bactria).
- vs. [[tradition-advaita-vedanta]]: Advaita is the philosophical summit of the Upaniṣadic trajectory — Shankara's systematic non-dualism is the most influential single elaboration of the *ātman-Brahman* identification developed in the Chāndogya and Bṛhadāraṇyaka.
