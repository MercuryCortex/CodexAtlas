# Vedic Religion and early Hinduism

## Overview
The Vedic tradition and its development into what we now call Hinduism is the religion stemming from the Sanskrit corpus of the four Vedas (Rig, Sāma, Yajur, Atharva), elaborated through Brāhmaṇa exegesis, internalized in the Upaniṣads, and expanded through epic, Purāṇic, and tantric literature. It is the world's longest continuously transmitted religious tradition with surviving primary texts — ~3,500 years of unbroken oral and textual lineage, though its forms have transformed substantially across that span.

## Historical phases
1. **Early Vedic (~-1500 to -1000)** — Rig Veda family books composition in the Sapta Sindhu region; pastoral / semi-nomadic Indo-Aryan society; deva-centered hymnology.
2. **Mantra / late Vedic (~-1200 to -800)** — Composition of remaining Vedic Saṃhitās (Sāma, Yajur, Atharva); settlement of the Gangetic plain.
3. **Brāhmaṇa / Iron Age (~-900 to -600)** — Elaborate ritual systematization; Kuru-Pañcāla cultural center; emergence of Prajāpati cosmogony; the Aśvamedha and other great rites codified.
4. **Upaniṣadic / Axial (~-800 to -400)** — Philosophical introspection; Bṛhadāraṇyaka and Chāndogya; emergence of *ātman*-*brahman* identification and the karma-rebirth-liberation complex.
5. **Classical Hindu / epic-Purāṇic (~-400 to +600)** — Composition and elaboration of Mahābhārata, Rāmāyaṇa, early Purāṇas, Dharma-śāstra; rise of theistic devotional traditions (Vaiṣṇavism, Śaivism).
6. **Medieval Hindu (~600 to 1750)** — Bhakti movements, Tantra, Vedanta commentaries (Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Madhva), regional theistic traditions.
7. **Modern Hindu (~1750 to present)** — Reform movements, colonial encounter, diaspora.

## Pantheon
- [[indra]] — Vedic king of devas; storm-warrior; demoted in classical era.
- [[agni]] — fire-god, sacrificial mediator.
- [[varuna]] — cosmic-order Asura; demoted to god of waters.
- [[soma]] — deified ritual substance; later moon-god.
- [[mitra-vedic]] — covenant-god, paired with Varuṇa.
- [[usha]] — Indo-European dawn-goddess.
- [[rudra-shiva-early]] — outsider storm-god, becomes Śiva.
- [[prajapati]] — Brāhmaṇa-period creator; partly displaced by Brahmā.
- (Phase 3+: classical Hindu pantheon centered on Viṣṇu, Śiva, Devī)

## Defining doctrines
- **Veda as śruti** ("what was heard"): the texts are revealed, beginningless.
- **Yajña** (sacrifice) as central religious act, eventually internalized.
- **Ṛta** (cosmic order) → **Dharma** (in classical period).
- **Brahman = ātman** (Upaniṣadic identification).
- **Karma, rebirth, mokṣa** (Upaniṣadic, shared with Buddhism and Jainism).
- **Varṇāśrama-dharma** (the four-class system + four life-stages — Dharma-śāstra elaboration).

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-zoroastrianism]]: shared Indo-Iranian inheritance (Mitra/Mithra, Soma/Haoma, deva/daēva inverted). The single closest parallel tradition.
- vs. [[tradition-buddhism]] / [[tradition-jainism]]: rivals within the South Asian religious field from -500 onward. Shared karma-rebirth framework; opposed on *ātman*, Veda-authority, ritual.
- vs. [[tradition-greek-religion]]: distant Indo-European cognates (Dyauṣ-Zeus, Uṣas-Eos); no direct contact in Phase 2 (some scholars argue Phase 3 contact via Hellenistic Bactria).
