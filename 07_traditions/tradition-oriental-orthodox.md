# Oriental Orthodox Communion

## Overview
The **Oriental Orthodox** (also "Non-Chalcedonian" or "Miaphysite") communion is the family of ancient Christian churches that rejected the dyophysite (two-natures) Christological definition of the [[event-council-of-chalcedon-451]] in favor of the Cyrillian *mia physis tou theou logou sesarkōmenē* — "one incarnate nature of God the Word." The communion is constituted by six churches sharing eucharistic communion with each other but not with the Chalcedonian (Eastern Orthodox / Roman Catholic) world: the **Coptic Orthodox** (Egypt), the **Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo**, the **Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo** (autocephalous 1993), the **Syriac Orthodox** (West-Syriac), the **Armenian Apostolic**, and the **Malankara Orthodox Syrian** (Indian Saint Thomas Christians; affiliated with Syriac since c. 17th c.). Modern scholarship (Frend 1972; Davis 2004) treats this body as *Miaphysite* — one *united* nature with both divine and human elements unmixed — distinct from Eutyches's *Monophysite* position (which absorbed the human nature into the divine and was rejected by both Chalcedon and the Miaphysites).

## Historical phases

1. **Cyrillian-Alexandrian formation (~412–451)** — [[cyril-of-alexandria]] articulates the *mia physis* formula in opposition to Nestorius at [[event-council-of-ephesus-431]] (where Cyril's position prevails); Cyril dies 444; his successor [[dioscorus-of-alexandria]] inherits the formula.
2. **Schism at Chalcedon (451)** — [[event-council-of-chalcedon-451]] adopts the Tome of Leo and the "two natures in one person" formula. Dioscorus is deposed. The Egyptian, much of the Syrian, and the Armenian churches reject the council. Within decades the divisions harden.
3. **Severus and the Syriac consolidation (~518–578)** — [[severus-of-antioch]] (Patriarch 512–518, then exiled) becomes the most influential post-Chalcedonian Miaphysite theologian. [[jacob-baradaeus]] (d. 578) reconstitutes the Syriac Miaphysite hierarchy under Roman persecution, traveling in disguise to ordain priests (whence "Jacobite" as a Syriac-Orthodox label, formerly pejorative).
4. **Justinianic persecution (~519–565)** — Roman state pressure on Miaphysites; the displacement of Syriac monks into the Aksumite-Ethiopian context as the [[the-nine-saints]] occurs in this window (~480–530), seeding Ethiopian Miaphysite monasticism.
5. **Islamic conquest (~634–700)** — Arab conquest of Egypt, Syria, Mesopotamia, Armenia removes Roman state pressure on the Miaphysite churches but installs Muslim political authority over them; the communion survives but loses imperial protection.
6. **Medieval continuity (~700–1500)** — Coptic monastic culture persists (Wadi al-Natrun); Syriac Orthodox preserve extensive theological-literary tradition (Bar Hebraeus, 13th c.); Armenian church survives Seljuk, Mongol, and Ottoman pressure; Ethiopian church flowers under Solomonic dynasty.
7. **Modern persecution and renewal (~1900–present)** — Armenian Genocide (1915); Syriac Sayfo (1914–1920) — comparable Ottoman-era mass killings of Syriac Christians; Coptic discrimination under modern Egyptian states; Ethiopian church survives the Derg; massive global diaspora.
8. **Ecumenical Christological convergence (1964–present)** — beginning with the Aarhus consultations (1964) and culminating in the 1990 Geneva Joint Statement of the Joint Commission of the Theological Dialogue between the Orthodox Church and the Oriental Orthodox Churches, theologians on both sides affirmed that the historical schism was largely terminological rather than substantive — both sides reject Nestorius and Eutyches and affirm the union of divine and human in Christ. Formal communion has not been restored, but the modern theological assessment is convergence.

## Core texts
- **The Cyrillian corpus** — Cyril's *On the Unity of Christ*, his Twelve Anathemas, his correspondence with Nestorius. Cyril is the load-bearing theological author for all Oriental Orthodox doctrine.
- **The Severan corpus** — Severus of Antioch's polemical and pastoral writings; the *Cathedral Homilies*.
- **The Anaphora of St. Basil** (Coptic recension), the *Anaphora of St. Gregory*, the *Anaphora of St. Mark* — liturgical texts.
- **The Acts of the Council of Ephesus** (431) — Cyrillian victory over Nestorius.
- **[[phase-4-050-leo-tome-to-flavian]]** — the Roman dyophysite tome rejected by Dioscorus at Chalcedon (the founding negative text of the communion).
- **The [[the-nine-saints]] hagiographic tradition** — transmits Syriac-Miaphysite monasticism into Ethiopia.
- **[[phase-4-080-garima-gospels]]** — the Aksumite gospel illuminations (~330–650 CE).

## Pantheon / key figures
- [[cyril-of-alexandria]] (patriarch 412–444) — theological founder of the position.
- [[dioscorus-of-alexandria]] (patriarch 444–451) — deposed at Chalcedon; honored as saint in the Coptic and Ethiopian calendars.
- [[severus-of-antioch]] (patriarch 512–518) — definitive Miaphysite systematic theologian.
- [[jacob-baradaeus]] (d. 578) — reconstitutes Syriac hierarchy.
- [[shenoute]] (d. 465) — Coptic monastic father, near-contemporary of Chalcedon.
- [[frumentius]] (4th c.) — apostle of Ethiopia, brings the Cyrillian-Alexandrian Christology to Aksum *before* the Chalcedonian split — Ethiopia's Miaphysite alignment is therefore the *continuation* of the Alexandrian position it received pre-451, not a post-schism choice.
- [[the-nine-saints]] (5th–6th c.) — transmit Syriac monastic culture into Ethiopia.

## Defining doctrines
- **Miaphysite Christology** — *mia physis tou theou logou sesarkōmenē*; one united nature, both divine and human, without confusion, change, division, or separation.
- **Rejection of the Tome of Leo and the Chalcedonian Definition**, while continuing to honor the first three Ecumenical Councils (Nicaea 325, Constantinople 381, Ephesus 431).
- **Rich liturgical and monastic life** — among the longest continuous monastic traditions in Christianity (Egyptian desert fathers → Coptic monasteries; Syriac stylites and dayrayē; Ethiopian monastic networks).
- **Strong veneration of theotokos and patristic-era saints**, mirroring but pre-dating much of Eastern Orthodox practice.

## Relations to neighbors
- **vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]] (Chalcedonian)** — schismatic at 451; major theological-political conflict for ~1500 years; modern convergence since 1964 ecumenical dialogues.
- **vs. [[tradition-islam]]** — political conquerors of most Oriental Orthodox heartlands after the 7th century; complex dhimmi relationship; long survival under Islamic political authority despite gradual demographic erosion.
- **Internal communion** — Coptic, Ethiopian, Eritrean, Syriac, Armenian, Malankara — full eucharistic communion among these six.
