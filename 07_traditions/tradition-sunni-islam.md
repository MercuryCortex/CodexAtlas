# Sunni Islam

**Sunni Islam** (Arabic: *Ahl al-Sunnah wa'l-Jamāʿah*, "People of the Prophetic Tradition and the Community") is the largest branch of Islam, encompassing approximately 85–90% of the world's 1.8 billion Muslims. The Sunni-Shia split crystallized following the death of the Prophet Muhammad in 632 CE over the question of rightful succession to the caliphate — Sunnis accepted the first four caliphs (Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, Ali) as legitimate; Shia Muslims insisted Ali should have been first.

Sunni Islam encompasses four major legal schools (*madhāhib*): Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali. The Hanbali school, through Ibn Taymiyya and later Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab, produced the Wahhabi/Salafi reform tradition that undergirds much of contemporary political Islam. The Sunni intellectual tradition includes the Ashari and Maturidi schools of theology, the vast hadith sciences, and classical Sunni Sufism.
