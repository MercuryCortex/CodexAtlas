# Knights Hospitaller

## Overview
The **Order of St John of Jerusalem** — founded ~1099 by a Latin Christian pilgrim hospital in Jerusalem dedicated to John the Baptist, militarized through the 12th c. into a full military-religious order alongside the [[tradition-knights-templar|Templars]]. Unlike the Templars, the Hospitallers survived suppression and persist to the present as the **Sovereign Military Order of Malta** — a continuous corporate religious institution older than any modern nation-state.

The Order's John-the-Baptist patronage is notable for the Codex Atlas wedge: across the medieval-to-modern transmission landscape, *Johannite Christianity* — the strand that elevates the Baptist over (or alongside) Christ — recurs in [[tradition-mandaeism|Mandaean]] cosmology, in some Templar trial-confession material (the supposed "Baphomet head" = bearded skull of the Baptist, in one decoding tradition), and in the later 18th-19th-c. esoteric "Johannite" Christianity (Bernard-Raymond Fabré-Palaprat's *Johannite Church*, claiming Templar succession). The Hospitaller Baptist-patronage is *not itself* a Johannite-heretical claim — it is mainstream Latin Catholic — but it is the institutional anchor of a Baptist-centered Christian iconography in the medieval Latin West.

## Historical phases
1. **Hospital phase (~1080–1113)** — pilgrim infirmary in Jerusalem.
2. **Recognition** — Pope Paschal II's bull *Pie postulatio voluntatis* (1113) makes the Order a recognized religious congregation.
3. **Militarization** (~1120–1160) — under Raymond du Puy, the Order takes on the same combined monastic-military form as the Templars.
4. **Outremer phase** (~1160–1291) — castles include Krak des Chevaliers, Margat, Belvoir.
5. **Cyprus** (1291–1310) — refuge after the [[event-fall-of-acre-1291|Fall of Acre]].
6. **Rhodes** (1310–1522) — the Order conquers Rhodes from Byzantine and Turkish powers; becomes a sovereign island-state and Mediterranean naval power; the **Knights Hospitaller** thereafter often called **Knights of Rhodes**.
7. **Wandering** (1522–1530) — Suleiman the Magnificent expels the Order from Rhodes (1522).
8. **Malta** (1530–1798) — Charles V grants the Order Malta. Survives the **Great Siege of Malta** (1565) — a major event of the early-modern Christian–Ottoman frontier. Expelled by Napoleon 1798.
9. **Sovereign Military Order of Malta** (1834–present) — Rome-based, retains UN observer status; runs international humanitarian operations.

## Relations to neighbors
- **vs. [[tradition-knights-templar]]:** parallel order, often institutional rival, but theologically and ideologically similar; received the bulk of Templar assets after the 1312 suppression (papal bull *Ad providam*).
- **vs. [[tradition-teutonic-knights]]:** parallel order; in northern campaigns the Teutons take the lead role.
- **vs. [[tradition-islam]] (Ottoman naval power):** sustained early-modern military-religious frontier; the Great Siege of Malta (1565) is the keystone event.
