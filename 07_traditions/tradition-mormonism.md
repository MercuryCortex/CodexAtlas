# Mormonism / Latter-day Saint Movement

## Overview
Restorationist religious tradition founded by Joseph Smith Jr. in 1830 in western New York; doctrinally distinct from mainstream Trinitarian Christianity in significant ways (the Godhead as three distinct beings rather than one; pre-mortal existence of human spirits; theosis as deification; eternal progression; restoration of the Aaronic and Melchizedek priesthoods; modern continuing revelation; the Book of Mormon as additional scripture). Largest branches: The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (Utah-based, ~17 million members worldwide as of mid-2020s); Community of Christ (formerly Reorganized LDS, Independence, Missouri, ~250,000 members).

## Historical phases
1. **Founding period** (1820s–1844) — Smith's First Vision (~1820); Book of Mormon (1830); successive migrations Ohio → Missouri → Illinois; Smith's death at Carthage Jail 1844.
2. **Migration / Utah settlement** (1846–1890) — Brigham Young's leadership; the great trek; settlement of the Great Basin; plural marriage practiced openly; conflict with US federal authorities.
3. **Accommodation period** (1890–1978) — 1890 Manifesto ending official polygamy; Utah statehood (1896); gradual mainstream-American accommodation; racial priesthood ban (Black men) maintained until 1978.
4. **Global growth period** (1978 onward) — international expansion; the 1978 lifting of the racial priesthood ban; women's-ordination and LGBT+ controversies of the 21st century.

## Core texts
- [[phase-7-001-book-of-mormon]] (1830)
- *Doctrine and Covenants* (compilation of Joseph Smith and successor-prophet revelations; current LDS edition: 138 sections + 2 declarations)
- *Pearl of Great Price*
- King James Bible (LDS canonical English Bible)

## Defining doctrines
- The First Vision (Smith's 1820 vision of the Father and the Son as distinct beings).
- Restoration of the apostolic church through Smith.
- Continuing revelation through the office of Prophet (President of the Church).
- Eternal progression / theosis ("As man is, God once was; as God is, man may become" — Lorenzo Snow couplet).
- Pre-mortal existence and post-mortal three-degree-of-glory salvation.
- Temple ordinances (endowment, sealing, baptism for the dead).

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. mainstream Trinitarian Christianity: Mormonism's distinctive Godhead theology, additional scripture, and theosis-doctrine produce a substantial confessional distance. Mainstream Christian denominations (Catholic, Orthodox, mainline Protestant, evangelical) typically classify Mormonism as outside Christianity narrowly construed; LDS self-identification as Christian is sincere but historically distinct.
- vs. Community of Christ: the Reorganized LDS / Community of Christ branch has moved toward a more mainstream Protestant theological register since the late 20th century.
