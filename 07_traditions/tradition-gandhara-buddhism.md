# Gandhāran Buddhism

## What it is
Gandhāran Buddhism is the regional Buddhist tradition that flourished in **Gandhāra** — the Peshawar basin and Swat valley of present-day northwest Pakistan and eastern Afghanistan — from roughly the 2nd century BCE to the 6th century CE. It is historically pivotal for two reasons: it produced the **first anthropomorphic images of the Buddha** (in a Hellenistic sculptural idiom), and it preserved the **oldest surviving Buddhist manuscripts** (the Gāndhārī Kharoṣṭhī birch-bark scrolls).

## Earliest attestation
The Gāndhārī Buddhist texts (Kharoṣṭhī script on birch bark, ~1st c. BCE onward) are the earliest physical Buddhist manuscripts known. Gandhāran stone sculpture, dated from around the 1st century CE under Kushan patronage, gives us the earliest datable Buddha figures.

## Context
Gandhāra sat at the crossroads of Indian, Iranian, and Greek worlds, having absorbed a Hellenistic settler culture after the campaigns of [[alexander-the-great|Alexander]] and the Indo-Greek kingdoms. Under the Kushan empire (1st–3rd c. CE) it became a Buddhist heartland and a launch-point for the transmission of Buddhism along the Silk Road to Central Asia and China. The region was a major nursery of [[tradition-mahayana-buddhism|Mahāyāna]] devotionalism, including the cult of the future Buddha [[maitreya]].

## Cross-tradition findings
- **The Buddha image as Greco-Buddhist synthesis.** The standard iconography of the Buddha — robed in classical drapery, with wavy hair and serene Apollonian features — was created in Gandhāra by sculptors working in a Hellenistic visual vocabulary. This is one of the Atlas's clearest cases of `iconographic-borrowing`: Greek sculptural form transferred onto an Indian religious subject, without a transfer of Greek cult-identity (Boardman 1994; Behrendt 2007). It is wired to [[tradition-greco-buddhism]], the broader cultural syncretism of which Gandhāran art is the signature product.
- **Manuscript transmission.** As the source of the oldest Buddhist manuscripts, Gandhāra anchors the textual history of Buddhism and the spread of Buddhist canon into Central and East Asia.
