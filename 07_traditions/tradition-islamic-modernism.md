# Islamic Modernism

**Islamic Modernism** is the 19th–20th century reform current within Islam that seeks to demonstrate the compatibility of Islamic theology and law with modern science, democracy, and rational philosophy — while maintaining Islamic authenticity against both Western secularism and traditionalist Salafism. Its intellectual program centers on the reopening of **ijtihad** (independent reasoning in jurisprudence), which mainstream Sunni tradition had declared "closed" since the 10th century.

Key figures: Jamal al-Din al-Afghani (1838–1897, pan-Islamic anti-colonialism); Muhammad Abduh (1849–1905, *Risālat al-Tawḥīd*); Rashid Rida; **[[muhammad-iqbal]]** (*Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam*, 1930 — the tradition's most philosophically systematic text); Fazlur Rahman (*Islam and Modernity*, 1982).

Islamic Modernism stands in explicit opposition to Wahhabi/Salafi traditionalism (which demands return to 7th-century norms) and to Western-imposed secularism (which demands the privatization of religion). It occupies the contested middle ground: Islam can and must engage modernity on its own intellectual terms, using ijtihad to derive contemporary rulings from Quranic principles rather than from medieval precedents.
