# Wicca / Modern Pagan Witchcraft

## Overview
A new religious movement founded by Gerald Gardner in mid-20th-century England (publicly emerged 1954), inspired by Margaret Murray's now-discredited witch-cult-survival thesis, by Crowley's ceremonial magick, by Charles Leland's *Aradia*, by Robert Graves's *White Goddess*, and by Gardner's own creative synthesis. Core duotheism: the Goddess and the Horned God; eight Sabbats marking the seasonal "wheel of the year"; coven-based initiatory practice (with various branches); the Threefold Law (ethical principle of return).

## Historical phases
1. **Gardnerian founding** (1939 claimed coven initiation; 1954 *Witchcraft Today*) — emergence in postwar England.
2. **Branch proliferation** (1960s–1970s) — Alexandrian (Alex Sanders), Dianic (Z. Budapest), various eclectic branches; expansion to the United States via Raymond Buckland (1964).
3. **Eclectic / feminist Wicca** (1970s–1980s) — Starhawk's *Spiral Dance* (1979); feminist Goddess-spirituality; "Pagan" as broader umbrella covering Wicca, Druidry, Heathenry, and reconstructionist polytheisms.
4. **Mainstream visibility** (1990s–present) — Wicca's growth into one of the larger New Religious Movements globally; recognized by US military chaplaincy (Wiccan symbol on military headstones authorized 2007).

## Defining doctrines / practices
- Goddess and Horned God.
- Eight Sabbats (Samhain, Yule, Imbolc, Ostara, Beltane, Litha, Lughnasadh, Mabon).
- Coven initiatory structure (in Gardnerian-Alexandrian lineages; many eclectic Wiccans are solitary).
- Threefold Law.
- The Wiccan Rede: "An it harm none, do what ye will."
- Ritual magic, often skyclad (nude) in Gardnerian-lineage practice.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-thelema]]: Gardner met Crowley 1947; documented liturgical borrowings (Aidan Kelly 1991).
- vs. mainstream Christianity: Wicca self-identifies as a non-Christian religion; not Satanic (which is a recurring misconception); concerned with nature-cycles and immanent deity rather than transcendent monotheism.
- vs. [[tradition-asatru]]: parallel modern-Pagan-revival tradition with different ethnocultural-symbolic basis.
