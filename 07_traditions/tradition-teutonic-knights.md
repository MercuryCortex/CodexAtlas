# Teutonic Knights

## Overview
The **Order of the German House of Saint Mary in Jerusalem** — Latin *Ordo domus Sanctae Mariae Theutonicorum Hierosolymitanorum*, founded 1190 at the siege of Acre as a German national counterpart to the Templars and Hospitallers, formally militarized 1198. Under the strategic direction of Grand Master Hermann von Salza (1209–1239), the Order pivoted from the Outremer to the **Baltic Crusades** — the northern campaigns against pagan Prussian, Lithuanian, and Estonian peoples — and became the central institution of the Northern Crusades, eventually a sovereign monastic state across Prussia and Livonia (1230–1525). Secularized in Prussia by Grand Master Albrecht von Hohenzollern in 1525 (he converted to Lutheranism and turned the Order's Prussian holdings into the secular Duchy of Prussia, the seed of the later Hohenzollern dynasty); the Catholic remainder of the Order survives in modified form in Vienna to the present.

## Historical phases
1. **Acre foundation (1190–1198)** — initially a hospital for German pilgrims; militarized.
2. **Outremer / Mediterranean phase (1198–1211).**
3. **Hungarian / Burzenland phase (1211–1225)** — King Andrew II of Hungary grants land in Transylvania, then expels the Order when they begin to operate as an autonomous power.
4. **Prussian / Baltic Crusades (1226–1525)** — under the *Golden Bull of Rimini* (1226, Emperor Frederick II) and the *Golden Bull of Rieti* (1234, Gregory IX), the Order receives sovereign rights over its Baltic conquests; founds the **Ordensstaat** (the monastic state) with administrative capital at Marienburg / Malbork.
5. **Decline after Tannenberg / Grunwald (1410)** — defeat by Polish-Lithuanian forces at the Battle of Grunwald.
6. **Secularization of Prussia (1525)** — Albrecht von Hohenzollern's conversion.
7. **Catholic remainder (1525–present)** — survives as a small Catholic religious order, headquartered in Vienna.

## The Northern Crusades wedge
The Teutonic Knights are the institutional vector by which Latin Christianity is imposed on the **last pagan polities of Europe** — the Old Prussians (~1230–1283), the Estonians and Latvians (Livonian Crusade, 1198–1290), and eventually Lithuania (continuous warfare from 1230 until the 1386 baptism of Jogaila / Władysław II as part of the Lithuanian-Polish dynastic union). The Lithuanian case is especially important for the Codex Atlas wedge: **Lithuania was the last officially-pagan European state**, baptized only in 1386 — long after the rest of Europe — and pagan-Baltic religion (Dievas, Perkūnas, Žemyna, etc.) persisted in folk practice well into the 19th c., making the Baltic the longest-surviving European source for non-Christianized Indo-European religion.

## Relations to neighbors
- **vs. [[tradition-knights-templar]] / [[tradition-hospitallers]]:** parallel military-religious orders; the Teutons' German national identity differentiates the Order institutionally.
- **vs. Old Prussian / Baltic paganism:** the Order is the institutional destroyer-and-replacer of this religion (continuous warfare ~1230–1283, sustained suppression thereafter).
- **vs. Orthodox Russia:** the Order fights the Battle of the Ice (1242, against Alexander Nevsky's Novgorod army) and remains a perpetual Latin-Orthodox frontier through the medieval period.
