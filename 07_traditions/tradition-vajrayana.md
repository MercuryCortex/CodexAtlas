Vajrayana ("Diamond Vehicle" or "Thunderbolt Vehicle") is the esoteric form of Buddhism that developed in India c. 600–900 CE and was transmitted to Tibet, Nepal, Mongolia, and East Asia. It builds on the Mahayana bodhisattva ideal and adds a framework of tantric practices — mantra, mandala, mudra, and deity yoga — as accelerated paths to enlightenment.

## Core Practices

**Mantra and Bija:** Seed syllables (bija) are the compressed sonic essence of deities and cosmic principles. The practice of OM, MANI, PADME, HUM, and HRIH involves the letters themselves as objects of visualization and sound production. See [[alphabet-tibetan-tantric]] for the detailed account of how Tibetan script serves as a vehicle for these practices.

**Mandala:** The mandala is a cosmological diagram — typically a palace with four gates and a central deity — used as a visualization space and ritual field. The practitioner enters the mandala through visualization and identifies with the central deity.

**Deity Yoga:** The practitioner visualizes a specific deity, identifies with that deity completely, and recites the deity's mantra. The goal is not worship of an external being but transformation into the deity's nature.

**The Vajra:** The thunderbolt-scepter (*vajra* in Sanskrit, *dorje* in Tibetan) is the primary ritual implement — representing indestructible clarity and the union of compassion and wisdom.

## Tibetan Transmission

Vajrayana was transmitted to Tibet primarily through two major transmission periods:
- Early transmission (7th–9th c. CE): Padmasambhava, Vimalamitra, and others
- Later transmission (10th–12th c. CE): Marpa, Milarepa, and the founding of the major Tibetan schools (Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, Gelug)

The Tibetan script itself was created specifically to enable this transmission — see [[alphabet-tibetan-tantric]] and [[alphabet-brahmi-origin]] for the script-creation story.
