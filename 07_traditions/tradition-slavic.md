# Pre-Christian Slavic Religion

## Overview
The polytheistic religious system of pre-Christian Slavic peoples (Eastern, Western, and Southern Slavic branches), c. -500 to ~1100 CE. Centered on a sky-god / thunder-god ([[perun]], cognate with Baltic Perkūnas, Vedic Parjanya, Norse Þórr in storm-god function), an opposing chthonic-underworld god ([[veles]], lord of cattle, the dead, and oaths), the earth-mother goddess [[mokosh]] (the only female deity in the canonical Vladimirian pantheon of 980 CE), the smith-fire god [[svarog]], and his solar son [[dazhbog]] ("giving god"). Christianized definitively in stages — Bulgaria 864, Bohemia 9th–10th c., Rus' 988 (Vladimir's baptism), Poland 966, Polabian-Pomeranian Slavs into the 12th c. (Rügen-Arkona temple destroyed 1168). The tradition survives in modern reception as folk practice (*dvoeverie* — "double belief": Christian saints overlaid on pagan deities — St Elijah ↔ Perun, St Blaise ↔ Veles, St Paraskeva-Friday ↔ Mokosh) and as 19th–21st-c. Slavic neopagan revival (Rodnovery, Native Polish Church, etc.).

## Sources & evidence problem
Slavic religion is significantly underdocumented compared to Norse, Greek, or Vedic — there is **no surviving native primary text from the pagan period**. The evidence base is:

1. **Christian-polemic accounts** — *Primary Chronicle* / *Tale of Bygone Years* (12th c., on the Vladimirian Kievan pantheon of 980); Helmold of Bosau *Chronica Slavorum* (12th c., on Western Slavs); Saxo Grammaticus *Gesta Danorum* (early 13th c., on Rügen-Arkona); Thietmar of Merseburg (early 11th c.).
2. **Archaeological** — the Zbruch Idol (9th–10th c., Ukraine; the most-discussed Slavic cult-object), Arkona-Svetovid temple findings, kurgan / cremation burial evidence.
3. **Folk-survival reconstruction** — extensive Russian, Ukrainian, Polish, Serbian, Bulgarian folk traditions retain pagan layers; Boris Rybakov's reconstructive program (*Yazychestvo drevnih slavyan*, 1981) is the most ambitious 20th-c. attempt.
4. **Comparative Indo-European** — Dumézilian trifunctional analysis (Perun = warrior-thunder, Veles = priest/wealth, Mokosh = third-function fertility) has been applied with mixed reception.
