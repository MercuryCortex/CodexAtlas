# Merkavah / Hekhalot Mysticism

## Overview
The earliest stratum of Jewish mystical literature, focused on heavenly ascent to the *Merkavah* (the chariot-throne of God in Ezekiel 1) and the *Hekhalot* (heavenly palaces / halls). The corpus comprises a loose grouping of Hebrew (with some Aramaic) texts — *Hekhalot Rabbati*, *Hekhalot Zutarti*, *Maʿaseh Merkavah*, *Shiʿur Qomah*, *3 Enoch / Sefer Hekhalot* — featuring tannaitic protagonists (R. Akiva, R. Ishmael) ascending through seven palaces of seven heavens, encountering the chariot-throne, and engaging with angelic hierarchies dominated by the figure of Metatron, the "Lesser YHWH."

## Historical phases
1. **Late-antique formation (~200–500)** — composition of core Hekhalot strata in Palestinian and Babylonian Jewish circles.
2. **Geonic systematization (~500–900)** — final redaction; cross-pollination with magical / theurgic literature.
3. **Ashkenazi reception (~1150–1250)** — Hasidei Ashkenaz (R. Yehudah he-Hasid, R. Eleazar of Worms) develop distinctive theology around the corpus in the Rhineland.
4. **Modern recovery** — Scholem 1941 reframes the corpus academically; Schäfer's *Synopse* 1981 establishes the modern critical edition; debate continues.

## Core texts
- [[phase-5-013-hekhalot-literature]] — Hekhalot Rabbati, Hekhalot Zutarti, Maʿaseh Merkavah, 3 Enoch / Sefer Hekhalot, Shiʿur Qomah.
- [[phase-5-012-sefer-yetzirah]] — possibly Merkavah-adjacent in some early strata.
- Talmudic discussions of *maʿaseh merkavah* (b. Ḥagigah 11b–16a) and the Pardes narrative.

## Pantheon
- [[yahweh]] — the divine throne-occupant; God's body in the *Shiʿur Qomah* tradition.
- [[metatron]] — the chief angel, transformed Enoch, "Lesser YHWH."
- Multitudes of angelic figures with operative names: Sandalphon, Anaphi'el, Suriel, etc.

## Defining doctrines
- The possibility of heavenly ascent for prepared adepts.
- Esoteric transmission through gates / passwords / angelic-name liturgies.
- Theology of *Shiʿur Qomah*: the cosmic-anthropic measurements of God's body.
- Restricted teaching: per the Talmudic *m. Ḥagigah*, *maʿaseh merkavah* is not to be taught publicly.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-judaism]]: stands in tension with rabbinic discomfort; the Aher-Pardes narratives are operative warnings.
- vs. [[tradition-gnosticism]]: structurally parallel ascent literature with shared late-antique milieu; Scholem's "Jewish Gnostic" thesis (1965) is the foundational comparative claim. Schäfer modifies this substantially.
- vs. [[tradition-kabbalah]] (medieval): Kabbalah inherits Hekhalot palaces, angelic hierarchies, and esoteric ascent vocabulary while transforming the theosophic framework.
