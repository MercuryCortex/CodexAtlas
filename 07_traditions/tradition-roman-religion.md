# Roman Religion

## Overview
Roman religion is the **state-cult, household-cult, and imperial-cult complex** of the Roman state and Roman society from the regal period (~-700) through the Theodosian suppression of public pagan religion (+391/+392). It is characterized by (a) ritual exactness — religion is fundamentally *orthopraxy* rather than orthodoxy; (b) civic embedding — religious office-holding is a function of civic-political authority (the *pontifex maximus* was a state office, held by Julius Caesar and inherited by every emperor through Gratian in +382); (c) syncretism — Roman religion absorbed and integrated cults from every region it encountered (Greek, Egyptian, Anatolian, Persian, Celtic, Germanic), under the general principle of *interpretatio Romana*; (d) household and ancestor cult — the *Lares*, *Penates*, *genius* of the paterfamilias, and ancestor-veneration formed the deep substrate.

## Historical phases
1. **Regal-Etruscan period** (~-700/-509): the foundational triad of Jupiter-Mars-Quirinus is structured; Etruscan influence shapes augury and haruspicy.
2. **Early Republic** (~-509/-300): the Capitoline triad (Jupiter-Juno-Minerva) replaces the older triad; the *Lectisternium* (banquet for the gods) ritual introduces Greek-style honoring of deities.
3. **Middle and Late Republic** (~-300/-30): Greek interpretation expands; eastern cults are first imported (Cybele, -204; Bacchic Senate-suppression, -186). Cicero's theological-philosophical works frame religion in philosophical terms.
4. **Imperial period — Augustan reform** (~-30/+14): Augustus orchestrates a comprehensive religious revival, restoring/rebuilding temples, reviving priestly colleges, instituting the imperial cult, and aligning state religion with the new monarchical order.
5. **High Empire** (~+14/+250): imperial cult universalizes; eastern mystery cults (Isis, Mithras, Sol Invictus) flourish; philosophical theology (Stoic, Middle Platonist) interprets traditional cult.
6. **Crisis and Christianization** (~+250/+400): Aurelian's Sol Invictus (+274) signals monotheizing tendency; Diocletian's Great Persecution (+303/+311); Constantine's conversion (+312); Theodosius's anti-pagan legislation (+391/+392) ends public state cult.

## Core texts
- **XII Tables** (~-450) — earliest Roman legal-religious code.
- **Cicero, *De Natura Deorum*, *De Divinatione*, *De Legibus*** — philosophical theology of late Republican religion.
- **Vergil, *Aeneid*** — the great Augustan religious-mythological epic.
- **Ovid, *Fasti*** — verse calendar of Roman festivals.
- **Varro, *Antiquitates rerum humanarum et divinarum*** (~-50, lost; preserved in fragments via Augustine *De Civitate Dei* 6–7) — the lost masterwork of Roman religious antiquarianism.
- **Pliny the Elder, *Natural History*** — religious-magical materia.

## Pantheon (key deities)
- [[jupiter]] — sky-king; Iuppiter Optimus Maximus on the Capitoline.
- [[juno]] — queen of the gods; protectress of women.
- [[minerva-roman|Minerva]] — wisdom, crafts, war strategy.
- [[mars-roman]] — war and agriculture; father of Romulus.
- [[venus-roman]] — love; ancestress of the Julian gens.
- [[mercury-roman]] — commerce, travelers, messages.
- [[vesta]] — hearth fire; the Vestal Virgins.
- [[janus]] — beginnings and gates; uniquely Roman.
- [[saturn-roman]] — agriculture; Golden Age; Saturnalia.
- [[apollo]] — adopted from Greek; Augustus's patron.
- [[diana]] — Greek Artemis.

## Defining doctrines
- **Pax deorum** — peace with the gods, maintained through correct ritual; failure of ritual brings disaster.
- **Interpretatio Romana** — foreign deities can be understood as the same gods under different names; this licenses both syncretism and conquest-incorporation.
- **No theological orthodoxy** — Romans had no creed, no scripture, no doctrinal teaching authority. Religion was *practice*.
- **Civic-religious integration** — political and religious authority are fused; no separation of church and state.

## Relations to neighbors
- **vs. [[tradition-greek-religion]]**: extensive overlap via *interpretatio*; Roman literary mythology IS Greek mythology under Latin name.
- **vs. [[tradition-mystery-cults]]**: tense incorporation; the Bacchanalia were suppressed (-186), Isis cult periodically banned then accepted, Mithras flourished informally.
- **vs. [[tradition-judaism]]**: Roman administration generally tolerated Judaism as an ancient religion (*religio licita*), with periodic crises (Caligula's statue plan, Temple destruction +70, Bar Kokhba +132/+135).
- **vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]**: hostile through three centuries of intermittent persecution; converted by Constantine; the relationship is the central pivot of Late Antique religious history.
