# Judaism

## Overview
Judaism is the oldest continuously practiced Abrahamic religion and the parent tradition of both Christianity and Islam, making it the single most structurally influential node in the vault's Abrahamic cluster. It emerges in the Iron Age Levantine highlands (~1200 BCE) within a broadly Canaanite religious matrix, develops a distinctive and increasingly exclusive devotion to YHWH through the prophetic revolution (8th–7th c. BCE), undergoes a catastrophic-and-productive transformation in the Babylonian exile (586–539 BCE), and re-emerges in the Persian period as Second Temple Judaism — the form of Judaism that generated both early Christianity and Rabbinic Judaism as its heirs.

The tradition spans ~3,200 years of continuous practice across vast geographic and cultural terrain. The vault's primary focus is the **formative phase** (pre-exilic through early Second Temple) and its **cross-tradition transmission events** — particularly the five documented channels of Zoroastrian influence in the Persian period ([[persian-period-injection]]) and the generation of Christianity and Islam as daughter traditions.

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-monarchic Israel (~1200–1000 BCE)** — Iron Age I village confederations in the Levantine highlands; YHWH probably introduced from the southern desert (Shasu / Midianite tradition, cf. Exod 3–4); coexistence with broader Canaanite religion; Baal, Asherah, El all present in the cult.
2. **Monarchic (~1000–586 BCE)** — United Monarchy (David, Solomon — historical cores debated); Divided Kingdoms (Israel / Judah split ~930 BCE); J source ~950 BCE, E source ~850 BCE; the classical 8th-century prophets ([[amos-prophet]], [[hosea-prophet]], [[isaiah-first]]) redefine YHWH as the one god of universal moral justice; Deuteronomic reform under [[josiah-king]] (~622 BCE, D source); conquest of Israel by Assyria 722 BCE.
3. **Babylonian Exile (586–539 BCE)** — Destruction of the First Temple by Nebuchadnezzar II; deportation of Judean elites to Babylon; [[ezekiel]]'s chariot-vision theology (Merkabah seed); Deuteronomistic History compiled; theological transformation of YHWH from national deity to cosmic God of history; P source priestly redaction.
4. **Persian Period (539–332 BCE)** — [[cyrus-the-great]]'s Edict allows return; Second Temple rebuilt (~520–515 BCE); Second Isaiah develops explicit monotheism and the Servant Songs (Isa 40–55); Ezra promulgates Torah; the five-channel Zoroastrian injection (cosmic dualism, bodily resurrection, angelology, demonology, final judgment) enters Jewish theology. See [[persian-period-injection]].
5. **Hellenistic and Second Temple (332–70 CE)** — Alexander's conquest; Septuagint translation (~250 BCE); apocalyptic literature (Daniel, Enoch, 4 Ezra); Maccabean revolt (167–160 BCE); Pharisee / Sadducee / Essene / Zealot sectarianism; Qumran Dead Sea Scrolls community; Jesus movement emerges within and then breaks from this context.
6. **Rabbinic Judaism (70 CE onward)** — Destruction of Second Temple; Rabbinic tradition (Tannaim, Amoraim) redirects practice to Torah study and synagogue; Mishnah (~200 CE); Jerusalem Talmud (~400 CE); Babylonian Talmud (~500 CE); the normative form of Judaism to the present.
7. **Medieval and Modern** — [[maimonides]] (rationalist-Aristotelian synthesis, *Guide for the Perplexed*); Kabbalistic mysticism (see [[tradition-kabbalah]], medieval Spain / Provence, Zohar ~1280 CE); Hasidism (18th c. Poland, Baal Shem Tov); Reform / Conservative / Orthodox / Reconstructionist movements (19th–20th c.).

## Core concepts
- **Henotheism → monolatry → monotheism** — the trajectory from "YHWH, your God" through "no other gods before me" to "there is no other god" runs across the Hebrew Bible and reaches explicit monotheism in Deutero-Isaiah (Isa 43:10 — "before me no god was formed, nor shall there be any after me").
- **Covenant** — the YHWH-Israel covenantal bond (Sinai, Deuteronomy) grounds both obligation and election. The Deuteronomistic History reads all of Israelite history through this lens: fidelity = prosperity, apostasy = catastrophe.
- **Torah** — instruction, law, and narrative tradition; the first five books (Pentateuch) as the constitutive document. The Rabbinic tradition extends Torah to include the "Oral Torah" (Mishnah / Talmud) as equally binding.
- **Prophecy** — direct divine speech mediated by named prophets, sharply distinguished from divination. The classical prophets' critique of cultic religion in favor of ethical action is one of the most influential moves in Western religious thought.
- **Eschatology** — the "Day of YHWH" (Amos 5) develops through exile theology into full apocalyptic eschatology (Daniel, 4 Ezra); resurrection of the dead appears in post-exilic texts (Dan 12, Isa 26) with likely Zoroastrian input.

## MASSIVE wins — cross-tradition edges
- **Judaism → [[tradition-christianity-canonical]] (parent → child)**: Christianity emerges from within Second Temple Judaism — Jesus as a Jewish eschatological prophet, Paul as a Pharisee trained under Gamaliel, the New Testament's constant hermeneutical engagement with the Hebrew Bible (fulfillment, typology, allegory). The parting of the ways (70–135 CE) is gradual; both traditions were transformed by the Temple's destruction. The Hebrew Bible (as "Old Testament") constitutes more than 75% of the Christian canon. This is the vault's single most productive generative edge.
- **Judaism → [[tradition-islam]] (grandparent → grandchild via Abrahamic stream)**: Muhammad's monotheism explicitly references Abraham, Moses, and the Hebrew prophets as precursors. The Quran's biblical re-narrations (Joseph, Moses, Abraham) constitute a sustained midrashic engagement with Jewish scripture. Halal/kashrut dietary law, prayer postures, and the concept of revealed scripture (*kitāb*) all show structural parallels. Islam identifies itself as the restoration of Abraham's original submission (*hanif*).
- **[[tradition-zoroastrianism]] → Judaism via [[persian-period-injection]]**: The five-channel documented theological injection: (1) cosmic dualism — Satan as active adversarial principle vs. earlier divine council model; (2) bodily resurrection — absent in pre-exilic texts, fully present in Daniel 12; (3) messianic savior-figure — Saoshyant template; (4) named angelology — Gabriel, Michael, Raphael absent before Persian period; (5) final cosmic judgment — fully developed in 2nd-c. BCE texts. All five appear in the 600-year window of Persian/Hellenistic contact (539–1 BCE). See [[persian-period-injection]].
- **Judaism → [[tradition-kabbalah]] (mystical dimension)**: The Kabbalistic tradition emerges from within medieval Judaism as an esoteric reading of Torah, deploying the ten Sefirot emanation scheme to map divine structure. Its roots reach into Merkabah mysticism (chariot-vision, [[ezekiel]]'s throne-room) and the Second Temple apocalyptic literature. The Zohar (~1280 CE) achieves the synthesis. Kabbalah is Judaism's most syncretically productive mystical form — it feeds into Christian Kabbalah (Pico della Mirandola, Agrippa), Hermeticism, and Rosicrucianism.
- **Judaism ↔ [[tradition-canaanite-ugaritic]] (matrix and polemic)**: YHWH absorbs the iconography and epithets of both El (the divine patriarch, *El Elyon*, "Most High") and Baal (storm-warrior, cloud-rider) while the prophetic tradition condemns Baal worship. The archaeology shows YHWH and Asherah worshipped together at Kuntillet Ajrud (~800 BCE). Judaism's emergence is inseparable from its transformation of its Canaanite matrix — the polemic against Baal in Elijah narratives (1 Kgs 18) is simultaneously a marker of differentiation and evidence of competition with something real.
- **Second Temple Judaism → [[tradition-gnosticism]] (sibling offspring)**: Gnosticism is in part a Jewish heterodoxy before it becomes a Christian heterodoxy — the *Demiurge* concept of an ignorant creator-god is a radical reinterpretation of the God of Genesis, the Gnostic archons re-code the Elohim of the P source, and Wisdom/Sophia is a hypostasis of the Hebrew *Ḥokmah* (Proverbs 8). The Dead Sea Scrolls' dualist theology (sons of light / sons of darkness) provides the closest pre-Gnostic Jewish template.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-canaanite-ugaritic]]: continuity (El → Yahweh; Baal imagery absorbed) plus polemic (prophets against Baal). Both relations are simultaneously operative.
- vs. [[tradition-zoroastrianism]]: transformative Persian-period contact; five-channel theological injection documented. See [[persian-period-injection]].
- vs. [[tradition-egyptian]]: traditional bondage-exodus narrative; some Hellenistic philosophical contact (Philo of Alexandria); Elephantine Jewish colony shows mixed worship.
- vs. [[tradition-mesopotamian]]: shared primeval-history motifs (flood, creation, garden) — whether by direct borrowing or shared ancient Near Eastern religious grammar is debated; the structural parallels (Atrahasis/Noah, Enuma Elish/Genesis 1) are undeniable.
