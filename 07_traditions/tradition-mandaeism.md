# Mandaeism

## Overview
The only surviving Gnostic religion — a living community of perhaps 60,000–100,000 adherents in modern Iraq, Iran, and diaspora. Mandaeism crystallized in southern Mesopotamia in the late-antique period, almost certainly with roots in Jewish-Christian baptist sects of the Jordan Valley (the Elchasaite environment from which Mani also emerged). The religion centers on John the Baptist (Yahya) as the prophet of true baptism (*masbuta*) and polemicizes against both Jesus (rejected as a false messiah) and Muhammad. Mandaean theology is gnostic in shape: a supreme Life (Hayyi Rabbi) and the World of Light, a problematic demiurge (Ptahil), exiled souls (sparks of Light), and a redeemer (Manda d-Hayyi) who descends with gnosis.

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-Mandaean Jewish-Christian baptist milieu** (1st–2nd c.) — southern Mesopotamian Elchasaite and adjacent sects.
2. **Mandaean crystallization** (~200–700) — composition strata of the Ginza Rba; migration to lower Mesopotamia.
3. **Islamic period** — Mandaeans recognized as Sabians, given dhimmī status; final compositions (Book of John) ~600–800.
4. **Modern community** — continuous priestly tradition; ethnographic study by Drower 1937 and successors; community displacement post-2003.

## Core texts
- [[phase-4-017-ginza-rba]] — the Great Treasure.
- [[phase-4-018-mandaean-book-of-john]] — Drasha d-Yahya.
- Qolasta — canonical Mandaean prayer book.

## Defining doctrines
- Two worlds: World of Light vs. World of Darkness.
- John the Baptist as supreme prophet; rejection of Jesus and Muhammad.
- The masbuta (frequent ritual baptism in flowing "living water").
- The masiqta (ritual ascent of the soul at death — the principal Mandaean funerary service).
- The priestly hierarchy (tarmidia, ganzibre) maintained in unbroken succession.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-gnosticism]]: structurally adjacent; the Mandaean *manda* IS gnosis.
- vs. [[tradition-judaism]]: emerges from Jewish baptist milieu but polemicizes against rabbinic Judaism.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: shares John the Baptist; rejects Jesus.
- vs. [[tradition-manichaeism]]: shared southern Mesopotamian Jewish-Christian baptist origin.
- vs. [[tradition-islam]]: identified by Muslims as Sabians (Quran 2:62, 5:69, 22:17) — gives Mandaeans protected status.

## Reception and the Johannite-Templar legend
The Mandaean elevation of John the Baptist over Jesus is the load-bearing motif behind the 19th-c. Johannite-Templar legend — the post-1750 esoteric construction that the [[tradition-knights-templar|Templars]] acquired and transmitted a Baptist-centered "true Christianity" through Outremer contact (1099–1291) with Mandaean and similar Eastern-Christian communities. **No medieval evidentiary basis exists for this transmission claim** (no Templar document engages Mandaean theology, no Mandaean source records Templar contact, geographic contact between Templar garrisons and the Mesopotamian-marshes Mandaean communities is plausible but undocumented). The Johannite-Templar construction is a real *reception phenomenon* of post-Enlightenment Western esotericism, surveyed in [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis]]. The legend has been an important vector for 19th-21st-c. Western interest in Mandaeism — including some early scholarly attention to Mandaean texts (Pognon 1898, then Lidzbarski 1905–1925, then Drower 1937+).
