# Sāṃkhya-Yoga

## Overview
Sāṃkhya and Yoga are two of the six classical *āstika* (Veda-accepting) philosophical schools (*darśanas*) of Hinduism, and are traditionally paired because they share a metaphysical foundation and differ primarily in method. **Sāṃkhya** is India's oldest systematic philosophical tradition, offering a dualist cosmology of *puruṣa* (pure consciousness) and *prakṛti* (primordial matter-energy) from which all 25 categories (*tattvas*) of the universe evolve. **Yoga** accepts this metaphysics and adds a practical eight-limbed (*ashtānga*) disciplinary path for achieving liberation through the stilling of mental fluctuations (*citta-vṛtti-nirodha*), codified by [[patanjali-yoga-sutras-author|Patañjali]] in the *Yoga Sūtras* (~2nd c. BCE – 4th c. CE).

## Historical phases
1. **Proto-Sāṃkhya** (~-600 to -200) — traces in the Upanishads, especially the Katha (5-element evolutes, senses, mind, intellect). The legendary founder Kapila is attested in the Mahābhārata and Bhāgavata Purāṇa but cannot be dated historically.
2. **Classical Sāṃkhya** (~+200 to +500) — the *Sāṃkhyakārikā* of Īśvarakṛṣṇa (~4th–5th c. CE) is the first surviving systematic text; its 72 verses established the canonical 25-tattva evolution scheme. The *Yuktidīpikā* is the fullest classical commentary.
3. **Classical Yoga** (~-150 to +400) — the *Yoga Sūtras* of [[patanjali-yoga-sutras-author|Patañjali]] codify the eight-limbed system (*ashtānga yoga*): yama, niyama, āsana, prāṇāyāma, pratyāhāra, dhāraṇā, dhyāna, samādhi. Bhāshya commentary by Vyāsa provides the standard interpretation.
4. **Medieval elaboration** (~500–1700) — Yoga texts proliferate: Haṭha Yoga (body-centered practices; *Haṭhapradīpikā* ~1450 CE), *Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā*, *Shiva Saṃhitā*. Sāṃkhya persists as a philosophical school but is increasingly overshadowed by Advaita in elite discourse.
5. **Modern / Global yoga** (19th c. onward) — Swami Vivekananda's *Rāja Yoga* (1896) reframes Patañjali's system for a Western audience; the 20th-century global yoga industry exports āsana practice largely stripped of Sāṃkhya-Yoga's soteriological context.

## Core doctrines

### Sāṃkhya
- **Puruṣa** — pure witnessing consciousness; plural (each individual soul is a distinct puruṣa), eternal, inactive. Cf. *ātman* in Vedanta but: Sāṃkhya is *dualist*, not non-dualist — puruṣa and prakṛti are genuinely distinct.
- **Prakṛti** — primordial, undifferentiated matter-energy; the sole material cause of all physical and mental phenomena. Constituted by three *guṇas*: *sattva* (luminosity, clarity), *rajas* (activity, passion), *tamas* (inertia, heaviness).
- **The 25 tattvas** — from prakṛti evolve in sequence: mahat (cosmic intellect), ahaṃkāra (ego-principle), five sense organs, five action organs, five subtle elements (*tanmātras*), five gross elements (*mahābhūtas*), and mind (*manas*). Plus the 25th: puruṣa.
- **Liberation (*mokṣa / kaivalya*)** — when puruṣa recognizes that it is distinct from and uninvolved with prakṛti (*vivekakhyāti*, discriminative wisdom), prakṛti ceases to evolve for that puruṣa; the soul abides in its own pure consciousness.

### Yoga (Patañjali)
- **Citta-vṛtti-nirodha** — "Yoga is the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind-field" (YS 1.2). The entire system aims at this.
- **The eight limbs (*ashtānga*)**: (1) *yama* — ethical restraints (non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing, celibacy, non-possessiveness); (2) *niyama* — observances (cleanliness, contentment, austerity, self-study, surrender to Īśvara); (3) *āsana* — posture (originally: a stable seat for meditation); (4) *prāṇāyāma* — breath regulation; (5) *pratyāhāra* — sense withdrawal; (6) *dhāraṇā* — concentration; (7) *dhyāna* — meditation; (8) *samādhi* — absorption.
- **Samādhi** — differentiated into *samprajñāta* (with cognitive support) and *asamprajñāta* (without support, ultimate); culminates in *kaivalya* (aloneness-isolation of puruṣa from prakṛti).
- **Kleśas** — the five afflictions that perpetuate [[reincarnation|saṃsāra]]: *avidyā* (ignorance), *asmitā* (I-am-ness), *rāga* (attachment), *dveṣa* (aversion), *abhiniveśa* (clinging to life).

## MASSIVE wins — cross-tradition edges
- **Sāṃkhya guṇas ↔ Aristotle's hylomorphism / matter-form duality**: both systems derive multiplicity from a single material substrate constituted by dynamic forces; both distinguish passive matter from an active principle of form/consciousness. The Indo-Greek contact period (~-300 to -100) makes indirect influence theoretically possible, though no direct textual link is established.
- **Yoga / samādhi ↔ Buddhist jhāna / dhyāna**: the *jhāna* stages in Buddhist Pali texts and the *samādhi* levels in Patañjali use virtually identical terminology and describe structurally identical meditative absorptions. Whether Patañjali borrowed from Buddhism, Buddhism borrowed from an earlier yoga tradition, or both drew on a common śramaṇic meditation culture is a central debate in Indology. See [[tradition-buddhism]].
- **Puruṣa/Prakṛti [[dualism-spirit-matter|dualism]] ↔ Gnostic Spirit/Matter dualism**: Sāṃkhya's cosmic dualism of unentangled spirit and entangled matter is the closest Indian parallel to Gnostic cosmologies in which a luminous *pneuma* is trapped in *hylē* (matter). The liberation structure (recognition → disentanglement → return) is structurally homologous. See [[tradition-gnosticism]].
- **Prāṇāyāma / subtle body ↔ Daoist neidan**: Sāṃkhya-Yoga's *prāṇa* / *nāḍī* / *chakra* subtle-body theory and the Daoist *qi* / *jing* / *shen* circulation system (internal alchemy) both describe an invisible energetic anatomy that mediates between gross body and pure consciousness. Both deploy a systematic transformative practice through this anatomy. No historical contact; the parallel is structural and potentially cross-tradition mapping gold.
- **Yoga Sūtras ↔ Stoic *hēgemonikon* and self-governance**: the Stoic concept of the ruling faculty (*hēgemonikon*) directing assent and impression-management parallels the Yoga project of directing the citta (mind-field); both are *praxis*-oriented philosophical systems aimed at freedom from passion through disciplined attention.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-advaita-vedanta]]: most significant Indian philosophical rivalry. Both accept the Vedas; both aim at moksha. But Advaita is *non-dualist* (one real ground: Brahman); Sāṃkhya is *dualist* (two ultimate reals: puruṣa + prakṛti). Shankara's Brahmasūtra commentary attacks Sāṃkhya at length. The two schools share vocabulary (ātman, moksha) but mean radically different things.
- vs. [[tradition-buddhism]]: the parallel samādhi/jhāna terminologies and the śramaṇic milieu link them; Buddhism denies both the permanent puruṣa and the creator-God (*Īśvara*) of Yoga.
- vs. [[tradition-jainism]]: same śramaṇic competitive milieu; both posit a plurality of individual souls; both have elaborate cosmologies. Jainism's *jīva/ajīva* duality echoes Sāṃkhya's puruṣa/prakṛti, but with different mechanics.
- vs. [[tradition-daoism]]: see the cross-tradition findings above — the prāṇa/qi subtle-body parallel.
