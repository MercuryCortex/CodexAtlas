# Samaritanism

The **Samaritan** religion — centered on Mount Gerizim near Nablus and tracing continuous existence from Second Temple antiquity — is the surviving tradition of Israelites who did not accept the rabbinic post-exilic redefinition of Judaism and who maintain that **Mount Gerizim** (not Jerusalem) is the divinely-designated sacred mountain of Israel. The Samaritan canon consists of only the Pentateuch (*Torah Qedosha*, "Holy Torah") in a distinct textual tradition — the [[phase-3-007-samaritan-pentateuch|Samaritan Pentateuch]] — which diverges from the rabbinic Masoretic Text in approximately 6,000 places.

The community numbers approximately 800 people today — split between Kiryat Luza village on Mount Gerizim and Holon, Israel — making it one of the world's smallest and oldest continuously-practicing religious communities.

**Cross-tradition significance**: the Samaritan Pentateuch, together with the [[phase-3-006-septuagint|Septuagint]], proves that the Masoretic Text (the basis of modern Jewish and Protestant Bibles) is one member of a family of ancient Hebrew textual traditions, not the uniquely original text. The Samaritans preserved a different branch of that family — validating the broader picture of a fluid Second Temple Jewish scriptural tradition that was not standardized until rabbinic Judaism crystallized it after 70 CE. See [[tradition-second-temple-judaism]].
