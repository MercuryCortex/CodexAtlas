# Candomblé

Afro-Brazilian religious tradition developed primarily in Bahia (Salvador), Brazil, by enslaved Africans of Yoruba, Fon, and Bantu origin from the early 19th century onward. The Nagô (Yoruba-derived) nation is the most theologically influential, preserving Yoruba orisha theology, liturgical language (Yoruba as ceremonial language), and Ifá-derived cosmology in a Brazilian context. Candomblé is distinguished from Cuban Lucumí/Santería by its more explicit preservation of African identity and its resistance to Catholic syncretism (though Oxalá/Obatalá-as-Jesus identification remains prominent in some houses). Iemanjá celebrations on New Year's Eve at Rio de Janeiro beaches draw millions of participants. UNESCO recognized the Candomblé tradition-complex as cultural heritage.
