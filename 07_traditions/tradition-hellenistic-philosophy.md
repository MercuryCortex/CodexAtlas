# Hellenistic Philosophy

## Overview
**Hellenistic philosophy** is the cluster of philosophical schools that flourished from the death of Alexander (-323) through the closing of the Athenian Academy by Justinian (+529) — though its core productive period is ~-300 to ~+200. Four major schools were institutional rivals at Athens: **Stoicism** (Zeno's Stoa Poikilē, ~-300), **Epicureanism** (Epicurus's Garden, ~-307), **Academic Skepticism** (the Platonic Academy under Arcesilaus, ~-265, and Carneades, ~-160), and the continuing **Peripatetic** school (Aristotle's Lyceum). Two further developments matter centrally: **Middle Platonism** (~-100/+250) and **Neoplatonism** (~+250 onward, Phase 4).

Hellenistic philosophy is characterized by (a) a **eudaimonist** ethical orientation — the goal of philosophy is the good human life (*eudaimonia*), variously specified as virtue (Stoics), pleasure-as-tranquility (Epicureans), suspension of judgment (Skeptics), philosophical contemplation (Platonists, Aristotelians); (b) **systematicity** — philosophy is divided into logic-physics-ethics with worked-out doctrines in each; (c) **practical orientation** — philosophy is a *way of life* (Hadot), not merely intellectual analysis; (d) **theological commitment** — most schools developed serious theology (the major exception, Epicureanism, deserves its anomaly status).

## Historical phases
1. **Early Hellenistic** (~-300/-200): the great schools establish their doctrines under Zeno, Chrysippus, Epicurus, Arcesilaus, and Theophrastus.
2. **Middle Hellenistic** (~-200/-50): the schools mature; Stoic and Academic debates structure philosophical life; Middle Stoa (Panaetius, Posidonius) modernizes; Roman aristocrats absorb Greek philosophy.
3. **Early Imperial / Middle Platonist** (~-50/+250): Roman Stoa (Seneca, Musonius, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius); Middle Platonism re-elevates Plato; Philo synthesizes Platonism with Judaism; Plutarch writes religious-philosophical synthesis; Hermetism emerges.
4. **Neoplatonist** (~+250/+529): Plotinus founds Neoplatonism; Porphyry, Iamblichus, Proclus develop it as the dominant late antique philosophy; theurgic Neoplatonism integrates philosophy and ritual; closure of the Athenian Academy by Justinian in +529.

## Defining doctrines (varies by school)
- **Stoicism**: cosmos as single divine living being; virtue as the only good; *apatheia* (extinction of passions); providence + fate.
- **Epicureanism**: atomism; gods exist but in the intermundia; pleasure (mental tranquility) is the goal; death is not to be feared.
- **Skepticism**: suspension of judgment (*epochē*) on metaphysical claims; tranquility (*ataraxia*) follows.
- **Platonism (Middle)**: hierarchy First God → Logos → World Soul → cosmos; Forms exist; soul ascends through philosophical purification.
- **Aristotelianism / Peripatetic**: hylomorphism; the unmoved mover; teleology; moderation in ethics.

## Relations to neighbors
- **vs. [[tradition-judaism]] / Hellenistic Judaism**: massive cross-fertilization in Alexandria; Philo is the case study; Wisdom of Solomon and 4 Maccabees show further Hellenistic-Jewish philosophical work.
- **vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]**: Christianity born within Hellenistic philosophical culture; logos prologue presupposes it; Justin Martyr explicitly identifies Christianity with the true philosophy; Origen, Augustine, the Cappadocians are all philosophically trained.
- **vs. [[tradition-gnosticism]]**: Gnostic systems are philosophical-mythic; Sethian and Valentinian theology is Platonic in structure.
- **vs. [[tradition-mystery-cults]]**: many philosophers (Middle Platonists, Neoplatonists) treated mystery initiation as symbolic of philosophical truth; Iamblichus integrates them.
- **vs. [[tradition-hermeticism]]**: shared Middle Platonist substrate.
