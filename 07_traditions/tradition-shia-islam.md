# Shi'a Islam

## Overview
Shi'a Islam — *Shīʿat ʿAlī*, "the Party of ʿAlī" — is the second-largest branch of Islam (~10–15% of Muslims worldwide), emerging from the 7th-c. dispute over the proper succession to the Prophet Muhammad. Shi'a Muslims hold that the Prophet designated his cousin and son-in-law ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (601–661) as his successor (*walī*), and that legitimate Islamic leadership passes through ʿAlī's descendants (the *Imams*). The major Shi'a branches divide over how many Imams there are and where the line ends: the Imāmī / Twelver Shi'a (the largest, by far) recognize twelve Imams, the last of whom (Muḥammad al-Mahdī, b. ~869) went into occultation (*ghayba*) and will return as the eschatological Mahdī; the Ismā'īlī / Sevener Shi'a recognize a different lineage diverging at the seventh Imam; the Zaydī Shi'a (largely Yemeni) recognize a different post-fifth-Imam lineage.

## Historical phases
1. **Early Shi'a (~656–680)** — the First Fitna; ʿAlī's caliphate; [[event-karbala-680]] (10 October 680) — the martyrdom of [[husayn-ibn-ali]], the founding traumatic event of Shi'a identity.
2. **Imami formation (~680–940)** — the line of Imams under Umayyad and Abbasid persecution; [[jafar-al-sadiq]] (Sixth Imam, d. 765) as the supreme jurist-figure and cross-confessional teacher; the 874 succession crisis at [[hasan-al-askari]]'s death and the Minor / Major Occultation of [[muhammad-al-mahdi]] (the Twelfth Imam).
3. **Buyid Shi'a flourishing (~932–1055)** — Imami Shi'a hadith compilation (Kulayni, ibn Babawayh, al-Tusi); the great Twelver scholars; Ismaili Fatimid Caliphate in Egypt 909–1171.
4. **Mongol and post-Mongol period (~1258–1500)** — Mongol conversion to Shi'a (Ilkhanid Olçaytu briefly); Hilli's *uṣūl al-fiqh*; Imamism crystallizes.
5. **Safavid establishment in Iran (1501)** — Shah Ismā'īl I declares Twelver Shi'ism the state religion of Iran; substantial post-1501 Twelver flourishing (Phase 6).
6. **Modern (~1850 onward)** — Iranian Constitutional Revolution; 1979 Islamic Revolution; modern Shi'a global presence.

## Core texts
- The Qur'an (shared with Sunni Islam).
- *Nahj al-Balagha* — sayings and sermons of ʿAlī, compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi (~1009).
- The Four Books of Twelver hadith: al-Kulayni's *al-Kāfī* (~941), Ibn Bābawayh's *Man lā yaḥḍuruhu al-faqīh*, and two works of al-Ṭūsī (*Tahdhīb*, *Istibṣār*).
- Imami theological / philosophical literature (Mulla Sadra in Phase 6 is the high point).

## Pantheon (key figures)
- [[allah]] — the same God of Sunni Islam.
- [[ali-ibn-abi-talib]] — the first Imam; cousin and son-in-law of Muhammad; central Shi'a figure.
- [[husayn-ibn-ali]] — third Imam; martyred at [[event-karbala-680]]; the foundational Shi'a martyr.
- The Twelve Imams (Twelver Shi'a) — proceeding through [[ali-ibn-abi-talib]] → [[hasan-ibn-ali]] → [[husayn-ibn-ali]] → [[ali-zayn-al-abidin]] → [[muhammad-al-baqir]] → [[jafar-al-sadiq]] → [[musa-al-kazim]] → [[ali-al-rida]] → [[muhammad-al-jawad]] → [[ali-al-hadi]] → [[hasan-al-askari]] → [[muhammad-al-mahdi]] (in occultation since 874 / 941).
- Fāṭima al-Zahrāʾ — daughter of Muhammad, wife of ʿAlī, mother of Hasan and Husayn; central female figure.

## Defining doctrines
- **Imāma** — the doctrine of the divinely-appointed Imam as the proper successor / interpreter / guide. Distinguishes Shi'a from Sunni at the most fundamental institutional level.
- **ʿIṣma** — the sinlessness / infallibility of the Imams (extending also to the Prophet and Fāṭima).
- **Taqiyya** — pious dissimulation in times of danger to protect oneself and the community; a Shi'a-distinctive practice.
- **Walāya / wilāya** — loyalty to / friendship with the Imams as a fundamental religious obligation.
- **Mahdism** — eschatological return of the Hidden Imam to restore justice.
- **Ashura** — annual commemoration of Husayn's martyrdom; central Shi'a ritual.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-islam]] (Sunni): the foundational schism; doctrinal, ritual, and political differences across 14 centuries. Modern relations vary by region: pacific coexistence in some places, intense conflict in others (esp. modern Iraq, Syria, Yemen, Saudi-Iranian regional rivalry).
- vs. [[tradition-ismailism]]: sister Shi'a branch diverging at the seventh Imam.
- vs. [[tradition-sufism]]: substantial overlap. Many Sufi orders incorporate ʿAlī-centered devotion (the Naqshbandi being a notable Sunni exception). The Iranian Safavid revolution was both Shi'a and Sufi-mediated.
