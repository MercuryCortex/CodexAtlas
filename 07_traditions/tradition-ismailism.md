# Ismā'īlī Shi'ism

## Overview
Ismā'īlī Shi'ism — the "Sevener" branch of Shi'a Islam — emerged from the dispute over the succession after the Sixth Imam [[jafar-al-sadiq]] (d. 765). One group accepted his eldest son Ismāʿīl (d. ~760, before his father) and Ismāʿīl's son Muḥammad as the legitimate seventh Imam; another (the proto-Twelvers) followed Ismāʿīl's brother [[musa-al-kazim]]. The Ismā'īlī tradition produced the great medieval esoteric-philosophical synthesis: a graded cosmology of emanation (drawing on Plotinian Neoplatonism via the Arabic *Theology of Aristotle*), an esoteric (*bāṭin*) hermeneutic of the Qur'an alongside the exoteric (*ẓāhir*) surface, and an institutional *daʿwa* (missionary network) of graded initiation. The Fatimid Caliphate (909–1171) was the great Ismā'īlī state; the Nizārī "Assassins" of Alamut (1090–1257, destroyed by the Mongols) the famous fortress-based esoteric militancy. Modern Nizārī Ismā'īlīs follow the Aga Khan (Nizārī Imam); modern Ṭayyibī (Bohra) Ismā'īlīs follow the Dāʿī al-Muṭlaq (representing the hidden Imam).

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-Fatimid (~765–909)** — clandestine Ismā'īlī daʿwa across the Islamic world; Qarmatian uprisings; gradual cosmological-philosophical development.
2. **Fatimid Caliphate (909–1171)** — Ismā'īlī state in North Africa (909) and Egypt (969); foundation of al-Azhar university in Cairo (970); Fatimid daʿwa networks across the Islamic world; great Ismā'īlī theologians (al-Sijistānī, al-Kirmānī, al-Muʾayyad fī al-Dīn al-Shīrāzī, Nāṣir-i Khusraw).
3. **Nizārī-Mustaʿlī split (1094)** — succession dispute on the death of Caliph al-Mustanṣir; the Nizārī branch (proceeding through Nizār, ~1095) and the Mustaʿlī branch (proceeding through al-Mustaʿlī).
4. **Nizārī Alamut state (1090–1257)** — Ḥasan-i Ṣabbāḥ's network of mountain fortresses in Iran and Syria; the "Assassins" of medieval European legend; the Great Resurrection (*qiyāma*) of Alamut (1164); destruction by the Mongols (1256–1273).
5. **Mustaʿlī / Ṭayyibī Yemen and India (1130 onward)** — the Ṭayyibī daʿwa continues through the Dāʿīs al-Muṭlaq; in India, the Bohra community.
6. **Modern period** — Aga Khan I (1804–1881) and the modern Nizārī Imamate; substantial reform and global institutional development; modern Ismā'īlī community led by Aga Khan IV (1957–2025).

## Core texts
- [[phase-5-011-rasail-ikhwan-al-safa]] — the Epistles of the Brethren of Purity; likely Ismā'īlī-affiliated propaedeutic encyclopedia.
- Fatimid daʿwa corpus: al-Sijistānī's *Kitāb al-Yanābīʿ*, *Kashf al-Maḥjūb*; al-Kirmānī's *Rāḥat al-ʿAql*; al-Muʾayyad fī al-Dīn's *Majālis*; Nāṣir-i Khusraw's *Wajh-i Dīn*, *Jāmiʿ al-Ḥikmatayn*.
- Nizārī esoteric corpus (substantially destroyed in the Mongol conquest; some survives in Ṭayyibī libraries and via later Nizārī revivals).

## Pantheon (key figures)
- [[allah]] (shared Islamic).
- The Ismā'īlī Imams in the lineage from Ismāʿīl ibn Jaʿfar.
- The cosmological hypostases: the First Intellect (*al-ʿAql al-Awwal*), the Universal Soul, the cosmic spheres — a Plotinian-Neoplatonic emanationist structure adapted to Islamic theology.

## Defining doctrines
- **Esoteric / exoteric hermeneutic** (*bāṭin* / *ẓāhir*) — Qur'an has surface meaning available to all and esoteric meaning available only through Imami guidance.
- **Imāma** — the Imam is the operative living guide, possessing infallible knowledge.
- **Cosmological emanationism** — Neoplatonic emanation adapted to Islam.
- **Graded initiation** (*daʿwa*) — the missionary network is hierarchical; initiates ascend through grades.
- **Cyclical history** — seven prophet-cycles (each opened by a *nāṭiq* / speaking prophet and continued by a *ṣāmit* / silent imam); the final cycle is that of the Qāʾim / Resurrector.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-shia-islam]] (Twelver): sister Shi'a tradition diverging at the seventh Imam.
- vs. [[tradition-sufism]]: substantial structural and personnel overlap; Suhrawardī's Illuminationism is Shi'a-leaning; Nāṣir-i Khusraw is both Ismā'īlī and Sufi-poetic.
- vs. [[tradition-neoplatonism]]: deep methodological borrowing of the Plotinian-Procline emanationist apparatus.
- vs. [[tradition-islam]] (Sunni): historically substantial conflict; the Fatimid-Abbasid rivalry was civilizational.
