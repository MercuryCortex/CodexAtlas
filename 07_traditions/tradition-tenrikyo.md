# Tenrikyo — Religion of Divine Wisdom

## Overview
Tenrikyo (天理教, "Religion of Divine Wisdom" or "Way of Heavenly Reason") is a Japanese new religion (*shin shūkyō*) founded by Nakayama Miki (1798–1887) following a series of divine possessions beginning in 1838 in Yamato Province (modern Nara Prefecture). Nakayama Miki, a peasant woman in her late thirties, reported that *Oyagami* ("God the Parent") descended into her as a living shrine and commanded her to devote her life to the salvation of humanity. Over the following decades she dispensed faith healings, composed scripture in waka verse ([[phase-7-043-ofudesaki|Ofudesaki]]), and attracted thousands of followers — founding what became, by 2 million adherents, one of Japan's largest indigenous religious movements.

Tenrikyo teaches that God the Parent (*Oyagami*) created humanity to live the *Joyous Life* (*yokigurashi*), but that "mental dust" (*hokori*) — arrogance, greed, hatred, covetousness, and related mental states — obscures the original pure mind and produces suffering. The path is to continuously sweep the dust away through right living, service, and the *Teodori* (Service Dance) ritual, gradually restoring the mind to its original joyous clarity.

Tenrikyo is the founding model for the *shin shūkyō* wave — the dozens of Japanese new religions that emerged from the 1830s through the 20th century (Konkokyo, Kurozumikyo, Oomoto, PL Kyodan, Soka Gakkai, and others). Its organizational structure — charismatic founder → canonical scripture → institutional church → headquarters city — became the template. Tenri, Nara Prefecture, is today effectively an entire city owned and run by the Tenrikyo church: its hospitals, university, library, and stadium all belong to the tradition.

## Cross-tradition significance
- **Female founder pattern**: Nakayama Miki is one of the clearest cases of a woman founding a major religion through direct divine revelation, producing its canonical scripture, and building the institutional structure that survives her death. The parallel with Ann Lee (Shakers, 1770s), Mary Baker Eddy (Christian Science, 1879), and the early Bahai prophetess Tahirih (1848) is remarkable: all arose within ~60 years (1770–1838) across different continents, all via the same pattern of female divine authorization overcoming patriarchal resistance.
- **Healing theology**: The *Joyous Life* / spiritual-dust-clearing model has structural parallels with Christian Science's metaphysical healing, Daoist *yangsheng* (nourishing life), and Buddhist notions of the pure mind obscured by defilements — independent convergence on the same paradigm.
