# Navayana — New Vehicle Buddhism

## Overview
**Navayana** (Sanskrit: "New Vehicle") is the form of Buddhism founded by [[ambedkar|B.R. Ambedkar]] through his mass conversion at Nagpur on October 14, 1956 ([[event-ambedkar-mass-conversion-1956]]). It constitutes a deliberate reconstruction of Buddhist doctrine that strips elements Ambedkar identified as brahminical interpolations — particularly karma and rebirth — and refocuses Buddhism on collective social liberation in this world. With approximately 7–8 million adherents in India, Navayana is the largest Buddhist tradition on the subcontinent and the one with the most politically explicit theological program.

## Core doctrinal departures

Navayana departs from all three traditional Buddhist schools (Theravada, Mahayana, Vajrayana) on key points:

| Doctrine | Traditional Buddhism | Navayana |
|---|---|---|
| Karma | Moral causation across rebirths | Rejected as brahminical interpolation justifying caste |
| Rebirth | Central cosmological/soteriological doctrine | Rejected; liberation is in this lifetime, in this world |
| Nirvana | Individual transcendence of samsara | Social liberation: end of caste discrimination, poverty |
| Sangha | Monastic community | Lay community of Dalit activists |
| Bodhisattva ideal | Individual who delays enlightenment to liberate all | Template for the Dalit social activist |

## Cross-tradition significance
- **Liberation Theology structural parallel**: The same theological operation as Latin American Liberation Theology — identify which doctrines have been co-opted to legitimate oppression, excise or reinterpret them, and re-read the tradition's core as a mandate for the liberation of the marginalized. Both arise from colonial contexts; both are explicitly political theologies.
- **The most internally radical Buddhist school**: More doctrinally innovative than Zen (which retains karma), more politically explicit than Engaged Buddhism (Thich Nhat Hanh), more confrontational than any previous Buddhist reform. The only Buddhist tradition that *names karma as a casteist ideology*.
- **Modern mass conversion**: The 1956 Nagpur conversion reversed 700+ years of Buddhist near-absence from India and created a new social-religious bloc that reshaped Maharashtra politics. Ambedkar's face appears on banners at every major Dalit political rally in India today.
