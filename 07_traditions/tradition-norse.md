# Pre-Christian Norse Religion

## Overview
The polytheistic religious system of pre-Christian Scandinavia and Iceland, broader Germanic in deep substrate, focused on the worship of two families of gods (the Æsir and Vanir) within a cosmogonic frame including: the cosmic tree Yggdrasill rooted in the well of fate; the nine worlds (Ásgarðr, Miðgarðr, Jǫtunheimr, Niflheimr, Múspellsheimr, Vanaheimr, Álfheimr, Svartálfaheimr, Hel); the three Norns (Urðr, Verðandi, Skuld) who weave fate; Ragnarǫk, the apocalyptic-cyclical final battle in which the gods die and the world is renewed. The tradition is preserved principally through 13th-century Icelandic Christian-era redactions — the *Poetic Edda* (Codex Regius ~1270) and Snorri Sturluson's *Prose Edda* (~1220) — supplemented by saga literature, skaldic poetry, runic inscriptions, archaeology, and continental references (Tacitus *Germania* 98 CE; Adam of Bremen 11th c.; Ibn Fadlan 10th c. on the Volga Rus).

## Historical phases
1. **Indo-European / Germanic substratum (pre-500 BCE)** — Tīwaz (Týr) cognate with PIE *Dyēus suggests the older sky-god structure later displaced by the Óðinn-Þórr complex.
2. **Iron Age Germanic (~500 BCE–~500 CE)** — Tacitus's *Germania* (98 CE) attests Mercury (= Wōden / Óðinn), Hercules (= Þunor / Þórr), Mars (= Tīw / Týr), and Isis-equivalent (= Nerthus, the Earth-Mother in the Suebian rite).
3. **Migration period (~500–800)** — Anglo-Saxon settlement of Britain; continental Germanic patterns; Old English Wōden in royal genealogies.
4. **Viking Age (~800–1100)** — the classical period of Norse religion; cult-sites at Uppsala (Old Uppsala temple complex), Lejre (Denmark); Viking expansion (the Danelaw, Iceland 870+, Greenland 985, Vinland ~1000); first systematic continental encounters.
5. **Conversion (~960–1100)** — Denmark 960s (Harald Bluetooth); Norway under Olaf Tryggvason and Olaf Haraldsson (10th–11th c.); Iceland's parliamentary conversion 999/1000 CE; Sweden last to convert (~1100). Pagan and Christian practice coexist transitionally.
6. **Manuscript preservation period (~1200–1300, Iceland)** — Snorri Sturluson's *Prose Edda* (~1220); *Heimskringla* (~1230); the saga corpus; the Codex Regius of the *Poetic Edda* (~1270). Christian Icelandic literary culture preserves pre-Christian religious material in writing in a way no continental Germanic culture did.
7. **Modern reception (17th c. onward)** — Codex Regius rediscovered 1643; 19th-c. Romantic-nationalist German and Scandinavian appropriations (Wagner's Ring 1869–76); 20th-21st-c. modern revivalism ([[tradition-asatru]] from 1972).

- [[phase-8-014-poetic-edda]] — Codex Regius mythological poems; principal pre-Christian-content witness.
- [[phase-8-015-prose-edda-snorri]] — Snorri's mythographic-poetic handbook.
- *Heimskringla* — sagas of the Norwegian kings.
- The saga corpus (Egils saga, Njáls saga, Laxdæla saga, Eyrbyggja saga, etc.).
- Skaldic poetry (Eyvindr skáldaspillir, Egill Skallagrímsson, Snorri's own skaldic).
- Runic inscriptions (especially the Rök stone, Eggjum stone).
- Tacitus *Germania*; Adam of Bremen.

## Pantheon
**Æsir:**
- [[odin]] — all-father, wisdom, war, death, sorcery
- [[thor]] — thunder, protection
- [[tyr]] — old sky-god / oath / war
- [[frigg]] — queen, marriage, foreknowledge
- [[baldr]] — light, innocence, dying-god
- [[hel]] — underworld
- [[loki]] — trickster
- Heimdallr — guardian of Bifröst; Idunn — goldenapples; Bragi — poetry; Forseti — justice; Sif — golden hair; Ullr — bow; others

**Vanir:**
- [[freyr]] — fertility, kingship
- [[freyja]] — love, sorcery, slain
- Njörðr — sea, prosperity (Freyr and Freyja's father)

## Defining doctrines
- Cosmogony from primordial cold-and-fire meeting in Ginnungagap; Ymir's dismembered body shapes the cosmos
- Yggdrasill as cosmic axis
- Æsir-Vanir war and reconciliation (hostage exchange producing Njörðr, Freyr, Freyja with the Æsir)
- *Wyrd* / *Urðr* — fate, even gods subject
- Ragnarǫk — cyclical apocalypse
- Honor-shame complex; *drengskapr* (manliness) and reputation
- Specific cultic practices: *blót* (sacrifice), feasting, drinking-rituals

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-celtic]]: separate Indo-European traditions in Northwest Europe; some structural parallels (tree-cosmology; warrior-poetry); contact zone in British Isles especially Viking-Age.
- vs. [[tradition-finno-karelian]]: Norse-Finnish contact in the Baltic; some Sami substrate elements (especially in Norse magic / *seiðr* traditions).
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: ~960–1100 conversion; in Iceland, peaceful parliamentary conversion 1000 preserved pagan literary tradition; Christian-period reshaping of some materials (Baldr-Christ; Óðinn's self-sacrifice and Christ-crucifixion comparison).
- vs. [[tradition-asatru]]: modern revivalism explicitly drawing on Norse sources, formally established 1972 in Iceland and the US.
