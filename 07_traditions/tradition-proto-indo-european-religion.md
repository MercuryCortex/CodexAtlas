# Proto-Indo-European Religion (Reconstructed)

## What Is PIE Religion?
Proto-Indo-European (PIE) religion is a **scholarly reconstruction** — a set of religious beliefs, mythological narratives, and deity-types that linguists and comparative mythologists have inferred must have existed in the community that spoke Proto-Indo-European, the ancestor language of the Indo-European language family. This includes almost every European language (Greek, Latin, Celtic, Germanic, Slavic, Baltic, Armenian, Albanian), plus the Indo-Iranian languages (Sanskrit, Avestan, Persian, and their descendants including Hindi, Bengali, Urdu, Kurdish, etc.), plus the extinct Anatolian languages (Hittite, Luwian) and Tocharian (Central Asian).

The reconstruction methodology is **comparative**: when Greek, Vedic Sanskrit, Baltic, and Celtic traditions all independently preserve structurally identical deity-types or mythological narratives, the probability is high that they inherited these from a common ancestor. The asterisked forms (e.g., *Dyeus Pḥtḗr*) are linguistic reconstructions — not attestations but probabilistic ancestors.

The **PIE homeland** (the community that spoke PIE) is now most convincingly located on the **Pontic-Caspian steppe** (modern Ukraine/Russia), with a timeframe of approximately 4500–2500 BCE. The Yamnaya and related cultures' archaeological record — including characteristic burial practices (kurgans/burial mounds), cattle and horse domestication, wheeled vehicles, and steppe-derived genetics — matches the dispersal pattern of IE languages across Eurasia. Modern archaeogenetics (ancient DNA analysis) has confirmed massive steppe-origin migration into Europe beginning ~3000 BCE, replacing significant portions of the earlier Neolithic farmer population.

## The Reconstructed Deities

### *Dyeus Pḥtḗr — The Sky Father
The most securely reconstructed PIE deity: a sky/heaven god whose name combines *\*dyew-* (sky, to shine — the same root as Latin *dies*, day; Greek *Zeus*; Sanskrit *deva*, divine; English "deity") + *\*pHtḗr* (father). Attested as:
- **Greek**: Zeus (Ζεύς — the phonological reflex of *\*Dyews*)
- **Roman**: Jupiter (*Iovis Pater* — "Sky Father")
- **Vedic**: Dyaus Pitā (a faded sky-father figure, largely replaced by Indra in the Rig Veda)
- **Norse**: Týr (Germanic *\*Tīwaz* — etymologically = Zeus/Jupiter; functionally displaced by Odin and Thor)
- **Baltic**: Dievas (Lithuanian — "god" = the sky-father root)

**The theological function**: sovereignty over the cosmic order; the oath-deity (oaths are sworn by the sky); father of gods and heroes; the principle of the orderly, bright sky as opposed to the storm (which belongs to a separate deity).

### *Perkwunos — The Thunder-God
The **second** major PIE deity reconstructed with high confidence: the storm/thunder deity, whose name derives from *\*perkw-* (oak tree — the tree most struck by lightning in European forests). Attested as:
- **Norse**: Þórr (Thor — *\*Þunraz*, "Thunder"; functionally the storm-protector god)
- **Baltic**: Perkūnas (Lithuanian) / Pērkons (Latvian) — name directly from *\*perkwunos*
- **Slavic**: Perun — from the same *\*per-* root (to strike)
- **Celtic/Gaulish**: Taranis (*\*taran-* = thunder)
- **Vedic**: Parjanya (rain/thunder god); also Indra (cosmic storm-warrior who defeats the dragon Vṛtra)
- **Lusitanian**: **[[reue-lusitanian|Reue]]** — proposed by Prósper from PIE *\*reu-* (to roar/thunder)

**The theological function**: the god who battles the cosmic dragon/serpent (a cross-IE mythological narrative: Thor vs. Jörmungandr; Indra vs. Vṛtra; Zeus vs. Typhon; the dragon-slaying hero); the god of rain and agricultural abundance; the protector of the sky-father's order against chaos; associated with the oak (his tree), the mountain (his home), and cattle (his domain).*

### The Divine Twins (*Diwó Sunū — "Sons of Dyeus")
The **Divine Twins** are one of the most beautifully attested cross-IE mythological structures:
- **Greek**: Castor and Pollux (*Dioskouroi* — "Sons of Zeus")
- **Vedic**: Aśvins — twin divine horsemen who rescue and heal
- **Baltic**: Dieva dēli (Latvian — "Sons of Dievas/the Sky")
- **Norse**: The Alcis (mentioned by Tacitus; Germanic twin gods)
- **Irish**: The Macha twins / twin aspects of the Tuatha Dé Danann

The Divine Twins function: rescuers at sea (particularly of drowning sailors — Greek Castor and Pollux were the patron saints of sailors even into the Christian era, appearing as St. Elmo's fire); horse-tamers; healers; appearing at dawn (the morning star). The dawn-twin connection: they may be associated with the twin dawn stars (Venus as morning and evening star) or with the twin horses of the sun-chariot.

### *Dʰéǵʰōm Méh₂tēr — The Earth Mother
The **Earth Mother** is the complement to the Sky Father in PIE cosmology:
- **Greek**: Demeter (*Dā-māter* = "Earth Mother")
- **Vedic**: Pṛthivī Mātā ("Wide Mother Earth")
- **Slavic**: Mat' Syra Zemlya ("Moist Mother Earth")
- **Lithuanian**: Žemyna (earth goddess; see [[zemyna]])
- **Norse**: Jörð (earth personified; mother of Thor by Odin)

**The sacred marriage**: The union of sky-father (*Dyeus Pḥtḗr*) and earth-mother (*Dʰéǵʰōm*) is the PIE cosmological foundation — the hieros gamos that produces the world's life. Rain from the sky + receptive earth = agricultural productivity. This cosmic marriage is attested in explicit form in Greek (Zeus-Gaia) and Vedic (*Dyaur asmé pitā* — "Sky is our father, Earth our mother" — Atharva Veda).

### The Horse and the Cattle-Raid
The **cattle-raid narrative** is one of the most distinctive PIE mythological structures: the hero steals the cattle of a monster (dragon, giant, or enemy king) and recovers them. Attested as:
- **Vedic**: Indra frees the cattle of the gods from the demon Vala
- **Greek**: Heracles steals the cattle of Geryon (set in the far west — Tartessian territory)
- **Irish**: The *Táin Bó Cúailnge* (Cattle Raid of Cooley)
- **Roman**: Hercules and Cacus (cattle-theft near Rome)

This narrative is so consistent across traditions that it must go back to PIE. The original theological meaning: cattle = wealth; cattle-raid = the hero's acquisition of the sun (cattle = metaphor for the shining sun); the monster holding the cattle = the dark that holds the sun. See [[heracles]] for the Iberian cattle-raid (Geryon).

## MASSIVE WIN — PIE Religion as the Deep Grammar of the Vault

The reconstructed PIE religious system is the **root node** of half the vault's cross-tradition connections. Once you can see that Zeus, Jupiter, Thor, Perkūnas, Taranis, and Reue are all reflexes of the same *\*Dyeus/Perkwunos* complex, every comparison between these traditions becomes more precise: we are not comparing random parallels but **sister traditions that diverged from a common documented source**.

The key distinctions for the vault:
1. **PIE heritage** (shared because of common ancestry) vs. **independent parallel** (shared because of universal human psychology/environment) vs. **documented transmission** (shared because one tradition borrowed from another)
2. The **Lusitanian node** in the vault is the **westernmost attestation** of the PIE religious system — Reue (PIE thunderer), Nabia (PIE river goddess), Bandua (PIE protective-sovereignty), Trebaruna (PIE sovereignty mystery) are all at the Atlantic edge of the PIE diaspora, 6,000 km from the Pontic steppe homeland
3. The **Finisterre/Atlantic sacred edge** mythology is WHERE THE PIE TRADITION MET ITS WESTERN LIMIT — and the edge-of-the-world mythology that built up there (Hesperides, Melqart's *ne plus ultra*, Celtic Otherworld, Camino de Santiago) is what happens when PIE religious geography reaches the Atlantic coast and has nowhere further west to go

## Dumézil's Trifunctional Theory
Georges Dumézil (1898–1986) proposed that PIE society was organized into three ideological "functions":
1. **Sovereignty** (magic and law — the priestly/royal domain): Vedic Varuṇa + Mitra; Roman Flamen Dialis; Norse Odin; Celtic Druids
2. **Martial power** (the warrior function): Vedic Indra; Roman Mars; Norse Thor; Celtic warrior aristocracy
3. **Fertility and abundance** (producers, farmers, third estate): Vedic Aśvins; Roman Quirinus; Norse Freyr/Njörðr; Celtic farming deities

Applied to the Lusitanian vault cluster: Reue (sky-sovereignty, function 1) + Bandua (martial-territorial, function 2) + Nabia (water-fertility, function 3) maps onto Dumézil's schema with reasonable precision. See [[dumezil-trifunctional]] for the full analysis.
