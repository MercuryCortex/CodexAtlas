# Vaishnavism

## What it is
Vaishnavism is one of the three principal devotional traditions ("sampradāyas") of Hinduism, alongside [[tradition-shaivism]] and [[tradition-shaktism]]. It is defined by the worship of [[vishnu]] — and his manifestations [[krishna]], [[rama]], and [[narasimha]] — as the supreme deity, the preserver and the very ground of being. Vaishnavas are typically theistic and frequently monotheistic in practice: Vishnu (often as Krishna or Rama) is the personal God to whom the soul (*jīva*) relates through loving devotion (*bhakti*).

## Earliest attestation
The cult of Vishnu and his early forms (Vāsudeva-Krishna, the Bhāgavata cult) is attested from around the 2nd century BCE — the Heliodorus Pillar at Besnagar (~110 BCE), erected by a Greek ambassador to the "god of gods Vāsudeva," is an early epigraphic witness. The [[phase-2-027-bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gita]] (core ~2nd–1st c. BCE) is the foundational scripture, and the [[phase-4-109-vishnu-purana|Vishnu Purana]] and Bhagavata Purana systematise the theology in the first millennium CE.

## Context
Vaishnavism crystallised the **avatar doctrine** ([[avatar-doctrine]]): the belief that Vishnu descends into the world in successive forms (the *daśāvatāra* — Matsya, Kurma, Varaha, Narasimha, Vamana, Parashurama, Rama, Krishna, Buddha/Balarama, Kalki) to restore cosmic order (*dharma*). From the 7th century the Tamil Alvar poet-saints inaugurated the emotional *bhakti* movement, later systematised philosophically by [[ramanuja]] (Vishishtadvaita / qualified non-dualism) and [[madhva]] (Dvaita / dualism), and carried north by figures including [[chaitanya-mahaprabhu]] (Gaudiya Vaishnavism). The intense Krishna- and Rama-bhakti strand of this tradition is treated in depth at [[tradition-bhakti-vaishnavism]].

## Cross-tradition findings
- **The avatar typology and Christology.** Vaishnava *avatāra* doctrine — God personally descending into history in bodily form to save the world — has been compared since the 19th century with the Christian doctrine of [[doctrine-incarnation|incarnation]]. The mainstream scholarly position treats this as a **structural parallel** (independent theistic convergence), not a documented transmission; the surface resemblance is real but the conceptual mechanics (repeated *avatāras* vs. unique hypostatic union) differ. See [[avatar-doctrine]].
