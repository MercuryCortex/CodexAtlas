# Jainism

## Overview
Jainism is the religious tradition founded — or, in tradition's own account, *re-established as the 24th Tīrthaṅkara's teaching* — by Mahāvīra (~-540 to -468) in the same eastern Gangetic śramaṇa milieu as Buddhism. Its central doctrines are radical non-violence (*ahiṃsā*), pluralism / non-absolutism of viewpoints (*anekāntavāda*), non-attachment (*aparigraha*), and a karmic cosmology in which every soul (*jīva*) accumulates subtle karmic matter through action and can be liberated only by complete ascetic austerity. Jainism is the oldest continuously practiced ascetic tradition.

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-Mahāvīra (legendary, with possible Pārśvanātha kernel ~-800)** — Tīrthaṅkara lineage; Pārśvanātha (23rd) may have a historical core.
2. **Mahāvīra and early Saṅgha (~-540 to -300)** — Foundation / reform; oral teaching.
3. **Council and division (~-300 to +500)** — Council of Pātaliputra (~-300); the Digambara-Śvetāmbara split (~+1st c., over monastic nudity and other issues); Vallabhī council (~+5th c., final Śvetāmbara canon redaction).
4. **Medieval Jainism (~500 to 1700)** — Royal patronage in Gujarat and Karnataka; great temple complexes; philosophical elaboration.
5. **Modern Jainism (~1700 onward)** — Reform movements, modern diaspora.

## Core texts
- The Jain Āgamas — Ācārāṅga Sūtra, Sūtrakṛtāṅga, Sthānāṅga, Samavāyāṅga, Bhagavatī, etc. (Śvetāmbara canon; Digambaras reject the canon as corrupted).
- The Kalpa Sūtra (Mahāvīra's biography).
- (Phase 3+ work: Umāsvāti's *Tattvārtha Sūtra* (~+200 CE), foundational philosophical text accepted by both branches.)

## Key persons
- [[mahavira]] — 24th Tīrthaṅkara.
- Pārśvanātha (23rd, legendary-historical).
- (Phase 3+: Umāsvāti, Kundakunda, Hemacandra.)

## Defining doctrines
- **Ahiṃsā** — non-violence toward all life-forms, including microscopic; strictest in the ancient world.
- **Anekāntavāda** — non-absolutism of viewpoints; *syādvāda* (perspectival predication).
- **Aparigraha** — non-possession; non-attachment to objects, ideas, and even one's own body.
- **Karma as subtle matter** — karma is a literal subtle substance accreting to the soul; liberation requires preventing new karma and burning off existing karma through austerity.
- **Liberation (*mokṣa*)** — the soul, freed from all karmic matter, ascends to the top of the universe to dwell forever as a perfected being (*siddha*).
- **Non-theism (regarding a creator)** — no supreme creator god; the universe is eternal and uncreated.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-buddhism]]: closest sister tradition, same milieu and period. Buddhist Pali canon names Mahāvīra as Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta (independent attestation). Differ on metaphysics (Jain *jīva* permanence vs. Buddhist *anātman*) and ascetic radicalism (Jainism stricter).
- vs. [[tradition-vedic-hinduism]]: rejects Vedic authority; rejects ritual sacrifice as violent; karma framework shared with critical reinterpretation.
