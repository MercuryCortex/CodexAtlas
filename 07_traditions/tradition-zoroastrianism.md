# Zoroastrianism

## Overview
Zoroastrianism is the religion founded by — or radically reformed by — the prophet Zarathustra (Greek: Zoroaster) in eastern Iran, plausibly ~1200 BCE. Its core doctrines are: a supreme god (Ahura Mazda); cosmic dualism with a hostile spirit (Angra Mainyu / Ahriman); ethical dualism requiring individual moral choice; eschatological final judgment, bodily resurrection, and renewal of the world (*frashokereti*). It was the state religion of the Achaemenid (550–330 BCE), Parthian (~250 BCE–224 CE), and Sasanian (224–651 CE) empires, and through these — especially the Achaemenid encounter with the Jewish exiles in the Persian period — exercised disproportionate influence on the development of Jewish, Christian, and Islamic theology relative to its modern community size.

## Historical phases
1. **Gathic / Early (~-1200 to -1000)** — Zarathustra and the composition of the Gathas.
2. **Younger Avestan (~-1000 to -400)** — Restoration of Mithra and Anāhitā; full pantheon of yazatas; the Yashts, Yasna, Vendidad.
3. **Achaemenid (-550 to -330)** — Imperial cult of Ahura Mazda; Darius and successors. Persian-period contact with Judaism.
4. **Hellenistic & Parthian (-330 to +224)** — Greek-Iranian syncretisms; Mithra cults; magus tradition.
5. **Sasanian (224–651 CE)** — Codification of the Great Avesta (21 nasks); Pahlavi commentaries (Bundahishn, Denkard, Zādspram); state-church consolidation.
6. **Post-Islamic (~651 onward)** — Massive decline in Iran; Parsi migration to India (Gujarat) from ~9th c. CE; modern diaspora.

- [[mithra-zoroastrian]] — yazata of covenant and light.
- [[anahita]] — yazata of waters.

## Defining doctrines
- **Cosmic and ethical dualism** — two primordial principles, Truth (*aša*) and the Lie (*druj*).
- **Individual moral choice** — every soul chooses sides.
- **Eschatology** — Chinvat Bridge judgment of the soul; final Saoshyant; resurrection; *frashokereti* (renewal).
- **Ritual purity** — codified in the Vendidad.
- **Reverence for fire** — fire as symbol/presence of Ahura Mazda; fire temples (*ātaškadag*).

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-vedic-hinduism]]: shared Indo-Iranian inheritance, inverted theological valuation. Closest parallel.
- vs. [[tradition-judaism]] (Persian-period contact): widely argued mechanism for transmission of dualism, organized angelology, demonology, and developed eschatology to Second Temple Judaism. Strongly contested in details, broadly accepted in outline. Refs: Boyce & Grenet 1991; Barr 1985; Shaked 1984.
- vs. Manichaeism (Phase 4): direct Iranian background; Mani drew on Zoroastrian categories.
