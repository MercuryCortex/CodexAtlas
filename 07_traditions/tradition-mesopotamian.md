# Mesopotamian Religion

## Overview
The continuous religious tradition of the southern and northern Mesopotamian alluvium from the late 4th millennium BCE through the Hellenistic period — Sumerian (3rd millennium), Akkadian (Sargonic and post-Sargonic), Old Babylonian, Middle Babylonian/Kassite, Neo-Assyrian, Neo-Babylonian, late period. Despite political successions and linguistic shifts (Sumerian replaced as spoken language by Akkadian; Aramaic later supplants Akkadian), there is substantial *religious* continuity: the gods of the city-states (each with a tutelary god — Enlil at Nippur, Inanna at Uruk, Marduk at Babylon, Ashur at Assur), the ritual calendar, the prayer-and-incantation forms, the divinatory practices, the cosmological narrative.

## Historical phases
1. **Sumerian** (c. -3500 to -2000) — proto-cuneiform → cuneiform; city-state cult; the Sumerian King List; Enheduanna's hymns.
2. **Akkadian / Old Babylonian** (-2000 to -1600) — Marduk rises with Babylon under Hammurabi; cuneiform Akkadian as international language; Atrahasis flood narrative.
3. **Middle and Neo-Assyrian / Babylonian** (-1600 to -539) — *Enuma Elish*; the Neo-Assyrian state cult; the Neo-Babylonian revival.
4. **Persian and Hellenistic** (-539 to -100) — gradual marginalization; Hellenistic-Babylonian astronomy.
