# Pythagoreanism

## Overview
**Pythagoreanism** is the religious-philosophical-scientific tradition descending from **Pythagoras of Samos** (~-570/-495), who emigrated to Croton in southern Italy ~-530 and founded a school combining: (a) **mathematical-cosmological speculation** — number is the principle (*archē*) of reality; the cosmos is structured by numerical-musical ratios; (b) **religious community life** — disciplined ascetic communities (vegetarian, with property in common, organized by initiate-ranks); (c) **soul-transmigration (*metempsychosis*)** doctrine — soul is divine and immortal, transmigrates through human, animal, and plant lives; (d) **ritual prohibitions and *akousmata*** — short oral precepts (e.g., "do not eat beans", "do not stir the fire with a knife") interpreted symbolically or literally by different streams.

Pythagoras himself was a legendary figure in his own lifetime — claimed to have been seen simultaneously in two cities, to remember his past lives, to have a golden thigh, to be the son of Apollo or Apollo himself in human form. The historical core is recoverable but constantly veiled by hagiographical accretion.

## Historical phases
1. **Old Pythagoreanism** (~-530/-400): the original Crotonian community, expanded into other Magna Graecian cities; eventually suppressed by political violence in Croton (~-450); diaspora.
2. **Middle Pythagoreanism** (~-400/-100): Philolaus, Archytas; significant mathematical-philosophical work; Plato visits Pythagorean centers (388, 367, 361); Pythagorean ideas absorbed into Platonism.
3. **Decline period** (~-100/+50): institutional Pythagoreanism nearly extinct; doctrines preserved in Platonist tradition.
4. **Neo-Pythagoreanism** (~+50/+300): revival in early imperial period; Apollonius of Tyana, Nicomachus of Gerasa, Numenius of Apamea; significant influence on Middle Platonism and ultimately on Neoplatonism (Iamblichus's *De Vita Pythagorica* is the great Neoplatonic hagiography).

## Core texts (most lost; reconstructed from later quoters)
- **Philolaus** fragments — earliest Pythagorean texts we have.
- **Archytas** mathematical-political fragments.
- **Pythagorean *Akousmata*** (sayings) — preserved by Aristotle, Diogenes Laertius, Iamblichus.
- **Golden Verses** — late ethical poem transmitted as "Pythagoras's."
- **Iamblichus, *De Vita Pythagorica*** (~+300) — the definitive late-antique synthesis.
- **Porphyry, *Life of Pythagoras***.
- **Diogenes Laertius, *Lives* VIII** — sympathetic biographical compilation.

## Defining doctrines
- **Mathematics-as-cosmology**: number ratios structure reality; the musical scale, planetary orbits, the basic geometric shapes (tetraktys: 1+2+3+4 = 10) all express divine harmony.
- **Soul transmigration**: continuous with Orphism on this point.
- **Vegetarianism and ascetic regimen**: respect for animal life (because they may be reincarnated humans); ritual prohibitions.
- **Sacred mathematics**: the tetraktys, the right triangle (Pythagorean theorem), the golden ratio — all carry theological weight.
- **Hierarchy of initiates**: *akousmatikoi* (those who followed the sayings) and *mathematikoi* (those who understood the doctrines) — an early version of the exoteric/esoteric distinction.
- **Politics**: oligarchic-aristocratic; Pythagoreans were political actors in Magna Graecia and suffered violent backlash.

## Relations to neighbors
- **vs. [[tradition-orphic]]**: extensive overlap on reincarnation, vegetarianism, ascesis; mutual influence in Magna Graecia.
- **vs. [[tradition-hellenistic-philosophy]] (esp. Platonism)**: Plato is in important ways a Platonized-Pythagorean; the Academy preserved much Pythagorean doctrine; Old Academy figures (Speusippus, Xenocrates) explicitly Pythagoreanizing.
- **vs. [[tradition-mystery-cults]]**: shares the initiate/secrecy structure.
- **vs. [[tradition-neoplatonism]]**: Iamblichus and Proclus integrate Pythagorean mathematical mysticism into late Neoplatonism.
- **vs. early Christianity / Gnosticism**: Pythagorean asceticism, secret-teaching structure, and number-symbolism feed into Gnostic (esp. Valentinian) cosmology via shared Middle Platonist substrate.
