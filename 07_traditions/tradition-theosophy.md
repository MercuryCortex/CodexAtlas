# Theosophy

## Overview
Modern esoteric religious movement founded in New York City on 17 November 1875 by Helena Petrovna Blavatsky, Henry Steel Olcott, and William Quan Judge. Theosophy proposed that all major religions are corrupted fragments of a single primordial wisdom-tradition (the *prisca theologia* / perennial philosophy) accessible through esoteric study and through contact with adepts (the Mahatmas / Masters). Theosophy substantially originated the modern Western synthesis of Indian, Egyptian, Hermetic, Kabbalistic, and Gnostic materials, providing the foundational vocabulary for what became the broader New Age movement.

## Historical phases
1. **Founding period** (1875–1891) — Blavatsky and Olcott; *Isis Unveiled* (1877); *Secret Doctrine* (1888). HQ moves: New York → Bombay (1879) → Adyar near Madras (1882).
2. **Besant-Leadbeater leadership** (1891–1933) — global expansion; Krishnamurti promoted as World Teacher (1909) and the founding of the Order of the Star (1911); Steiner's break (1913) producing Anthroposophy; Krishnamurti's 1929 dissolution of the Order of the Star.
3. **Post-Krishnamurti period** (1929 onward) — Theosophy continues in multiple branches (Adyar TS, Pasadena TS / Theosophical Society Point Loma, United Lodge of Theosophists). Membership decline globally through the 20th century.
4. **Alice Bailey corpus and downstream New Age** (1919–1949 Bailey period; 1950s onward New Age) — Bailey's "ascended master" teachings (in the Theosophical lineage but autonomous from the formal Society) became foundational for New Age channeling and ascended-master traditions (Saint Germain Foundation / "I AM" Activity 1930s; Church Universal and Triumphant 1958+; etc.).

## Pantheon (or key figures)
Theosophy does not have a pantheon in the traditional sense; the Hierarchy of Masters / Mahatmas (Morya, Koot Hoomi, Djwal Khul, the Maha Chohan, Saint Germain, Serapis, others) plays an analogous role.

## Defining doctrines
- Single primordial wisdom-tradition behind all religions.
- The Mahatmas / Masters as living transmitters of esoteric knowledge.
- Seven-fold structure of cosmos and human (seven principles, seven planes, seven rounds, seven root-races).
- Reincarnation and karma adapted to a Western frame.
- Cosmic-evolutionary cycles (manvantaras and pralayas).

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-anthroposophy]]: Steiner's 1913 break installed a Christ-centered Western cosmology in place of Theosophy's Eastern-leaning frame.
- vs. [[tradition-spiritualism]]: Theosophy emerged from and against Spiritualism; the séance-medium phenomena of Spiritualism gave way in Theosophy to the master-letter and inner-plane phenomena.
- vs. [[tradition-new-age]]: New Age channeling, ascended-master traditions, and perennialist vocabulary are continuous downstream developments.

## Cross-phase notes
**Theosophy is the first major modern synthesis of Phase 1–4 esoterica through a Phase 7 lens.** Phase 4 Gnostic and Hermetic materials, Phase 5 Kabbalah, Phase 1 Egyptian and Mesopotamian materials, and Phase 2 Vedic and Buddhist materials all enter into Theosophy's framework — typically before scholarly academic recovery (Jonas 1958, Yates 1964, Scholem 1941) had made these traditions academically respectable. Theosophy's synthesis is *premature* by current scholarly standards (Jonas's Gnosticism, Yates's Hermetica, Scholem's Kabbalah were not yet philologically established when Blavatsky wrote); but Theosophy was nonetheless the cultural channel through which Phases 1–4 esoterica became part of late-19th- and 20th-century Western intellectual circulation, well in advance of academic recovery.
