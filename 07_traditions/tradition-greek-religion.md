# Greek Religion (Archaic and Classical)

## Overview
Greek religion is the polytheistic tradition centered on the Olympian pantheon, with deep Mycenaean Bronze Age roots (~-1600), elaborated in the Archaic period (~-800 to -480) into the world of Homer and Hesiod, flourishing in the Classical period (~-480 to -323) in tandem with the city-state (*polis*) system, surviving — transformed and partly displaced by Christianity — into Late Antiquity until the suppression of pagan cults in the 4th–6th centuries CE. It encompasses civic cult, family / household religion, oracular and divinatory practice, hero cults, and the mystery cults that promised post-mortem benefits to initiates.

## Historical phases
1. **Mycenaean / Bronze Age (~-1600 to -1100)** — Linear B tablets attest Zeus, Poseidon, Hermes, Athena, Hera, Dionysus, Artemis, Ares; palace-based cult.
2. **Dark Age (~-1100 to -800)** — Loss of writing; oral tradition preserves religious knowledge; basis of the Homeric epics.
3. **Archaic (~-800 to -480)** — Reintroduction of writing (Greek alphabet); composition of Homer and Hesiod; emergence of major sanctuaries (Delphi, Olympia, Eleusis); city-state cults; orientalizing influences.
4. **Classical (~-480 to -323)** — Athenian dominance; tragedy as religious ritual; mystery cults flourishing; philosophical critique (Pre-Socratics, Plato, Aristotle).
5. **Hellenistic (~-323 to -31)** — Phase 3 work. Syncretism with Eastern traditions; Greek deities exported across Alexander's empire.
6. **Roman / Late Antique (-31 to +400)** — Phase 3-4 work. Roman *interpretatio* and absorption; mystery cults flourish; final suppression of public pagan cults under Theodosius (~+391).

## Pantheon (the Olympians and the older generation)
- The Twelve Olympians: Zeus, Hera, Poseidon, Demeter, Athena, Apollo, Artemis, Ares, Aphrodite, Hephaestus, Hermes, Dionysus (or Hestia, in some lists).
- Hades — Olympian sibling, but rules the underworld separately.
- Older generation: Kronos, Rhea, Uranus, Gaia (the Titans and primordials).
- Innumerable minor deities, daimones, nymphs, river-gods, hero-cults.

## Defining doctrines
- **Polytheistic and anthropomorphic** — many gods with distinct personalities and domains.
- **Reciprocity** (*charis*) — sacrifice in exchange for divine favor.
- **No fixed scripture or creed** — Homer and Hesiod were canonical poets but not "scripture" in the Abrahamic sense.
- **Civic and household integration** — religion embedded in city, family, agricultural calendar.
- **Mystery cults** as supplementary initiatory paths offering post-mortem benefits.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-canaanite-ugaritic]] / Phoenician: Bronze Age and Iron Age contact via Cyprus, the Levant, and Greek colonies. Aphrodite from the Inanna-Ishtar-Astarte chain via Phoenician Cyprus is the cleanest single example.
- vs. Hittite / Hurrian / Mesopotamian: the Hesiodic succession myth's structural parallel to the Kumarbi cycle is the gold-standard transmission case (West 1997).
- vs. [[tradition-egyptian]]: extensive Hellenistic-period syncretism (Herodotus 2.50 already identifying Greek and Egyptian gods systematically); Greek mystery cults absorb Egyptian Isis-Osiris material.
- vs. [[tradition-vedic-hinduism]]: distant Indo-European cognates (Zeus / Dyauṣ Pitṛ, Eos / Uṣas, structural parallel of dragon-slaying formulae). No direct Phase 2 contact.
