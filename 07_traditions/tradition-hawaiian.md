# Hawaiian Religion (pre-Christian Kanaka Maoli)

## Overview
The religious tradition of the Kanaka Maoli (Native Hawaiian) people. Settled by Polynesian voyagers (likely from the Marquesas and Society Islands ~400–1200 CE — the dating has been substantially refined and shortened by recent archaeology), Hawaiian religion developed a distinctive variant of broader Polynesian religion over the centuries of relative isolation before re-contact (the second-wave Tahitian arrivals ~1200–1300, and ultimately European contact 1778 — Cook). Distinctive features: the four principal *akua* (Kāne, Kū, Lono, Kanaloa) organized in cosmological-cultic functions; an extensive *kapu* (sacred-prohibition) system regulating all of social life; the Kumulipo cosmogonic-genealogical chant; the distinct Pele volcanic-divine complex; the *Makahiki* annual four-month peace-festival of Lono; *luakini heiau* (war temples) of Kū with associated human sacrifice.

## Historical phases
1. **Settlement (~400–1200 CE)** — Polynesian voyaging; first wave Marquesas-origin; second wave Society Islands.
2. **Pre-contact florescence (~1200–1778)** — development of distinctive Hawaiian religion; ahupuaʻa land-management; major heiau construction; chiefly competition.
3. **Cook contact and Kamehameha unification (1778–1819)** — Cook arrives at Kealakekua Bay 1778, killed 1779 (the famous Lono-identification debated); Kamehameha I unifies the islands ~1795–1810.
4. **Abolition of the *kapu* system (1819)** — under Kamehameha II, influenced by Queen Kaʻahumanu and Keōpūolani, the formal *kapu* system is overturned in a single dramatic act (the *ʻAi Noa* — free-eating). Kū's *luakini heiau* are burned.
5. **Christianization (~1820+)** — American Calvinist missionaries arrive 1820; rapid (though uneven) Christianization through 19th c.
6. **Overthrow and annexation (1893, 1898)** — Queen Liliʻuokalani overthrown by US-backed coup 1893; US annexation 1898. Liliʻuokalani's 1895–98 Kumulipo translation in prison is a sovereignty act.
7. **20th- and 21st-c. revitalization** — Hawaiian Renaissance from 1970s; Hawaiian sovereignty movements; Kumulipo and pre-Christian religion as living tradition for many Native Hawaiians.

## Pantheon
- [[kane-hawaiian]] — creation, fresh water, life
- [[ku]] — war, kingship
- [[lono]] — peace, agriculture, Makahiki
- Kanaloa — sea (lesser-developed than Maori Tangaroa or Tahitian Ta'aroa)
- [[pele]] — volcano (separate complex with Hi'iaka, others)
- Many *aumakua* (ancestral family deities); the major *akua* listed above are the most-public

## Defining doctrines
- *Kapu* / *noa* regulation of ritual life
- *Mana* as sacred-political power
- Four principal *akua* + Pele complex + *aumakua* family deities
- Royal-divine genealogical continuity (Kumulipo)
- Hawaiian heiau (temple) architecture and ritual practice

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-maori]] / [[tradition-polynesian]]: shared Polynesian substrate; cognate akua and structures.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: 19th-c. rapid Christianization; modern Hawaiian Christianity is genuine but variably syncretic; revitalization since 1970s.
- Phase-7 Lono-Cook reception: the Sahlins-Obeyesekere debate is itself a Phase-7 methodological event.
