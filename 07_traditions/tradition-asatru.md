# Ásatrú / Heathenry

## Overview
Modern revival/reconstruction of pre-Christian Norse/Germanic polytheism, emerging in the early 1970s in parallel in Iceland (Sveinbjörn Beinteinsson's Ásatrúarfélagið, recognized by the Icelandic government as a religion in 1973) and in the United States (Else Christensen's Odinist Fellowship 1969; Stephen McNallen's Asatru Free Assembly 1972). "Ásatrú" (Old Norse: "faith in the Æsir") is the Icelandic-derived term; "Heathenry" is the broader umbrella favored especially in the United Kingdom and increasingly in the United States.

## Historical phases
1. **Founding period** (1969–1976) — Icelandic Ásatrúarfélagið; US Asatru Free Assembly; Odinist Fellowship.
2. **Folkish-universalist division** (1980s onward) — McNallen's AFA and similar folkish-Heathen organizations (emphasizing ethnic-ancestral identity); The Troth (1987 onward) and similar universalist-Heathen organizations.
3. **Mainstream growth** (2000s onward) — Heathens currently estimated in the tens of thousands globally; the largest US Heathen organizations are non-folkish; the folkish-Heathen wing remains active and is the focus of ongoing internal disputes.

## Core texts
The medieval Icelandic Eddas and sagas — the *Poetic Edda* (Codex Regius c. 1270), Snorri's *Prose Edda* (c. 1220), and the saga corpus — are the principal Phase-5 textual sources from which modern Heathenry reconstructs.

## Defining doctrines / practices
- Polytheistic worship of the Æsir (Odin, Thor, Tyr, Frigg, Heimdall, others) and Vanir (Freyr, Freyja, Njord).
- *Blót* (sacrifice/offering ritual) and *sumbel* (ritual drinking and oath-taking).
- The Nine Noble Virtues (a 20th-century synthesis; not in the medieval sources).
- Wyrd (the Anglo-Saxon "fate" concept, related to ON *urðr*) as a central cosmological category.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-norse]]: Heathenry self-identifies as the modern continuation/restoration of pre-Christian Norse religion; the historical-continuity claim is theological rather than historical (the pre-Christian Norse religious tradition was broken at the conversion ~1000 CE in Iceland, earlier elsewhere).
- vs. [[tradition-wicca-modern-pagan]]: parallel modern-Pagan-revival with different ethnocultural-symbolic basis.
- **Politics**: the folkish-universalist division is sharp. Some Heathen organizations have been categorized as racist or white-nationalist (Southern Poverty Law Center has so categorized McNallen's Asatru Folk Assembly); other Heathen organizations (The Troth, Heathens Against Hate) are explicitly anti-racist.
