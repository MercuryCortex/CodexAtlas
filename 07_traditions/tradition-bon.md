# Bön

## Overview
The non-Buddhist (or rather: alongside-Buddhist) Tibetan religious tradition tracing itself to the legendary founder Tonpa Shenrab Miwoche of the kingdom of Zhang-Zhung (western Tibet, pre-Tibetan empire). Modern Bön (sometimes called *Yungdrung Bön* or "Eternal Bön") is structurally parallel to Tibetan Vajrayana Buddhism — it has its own canon (Bön Kanjur and Tenjur), its own monastic-academic curriculum, its own Dzogchen tradition (the *Zhang Zhung Nyengyud*), its own pantheon (the Bön Samantabhadra, Kuntu Zangpo). Whether modern Yungdrung Bön preserves substantive elements of pre-Buddhist Tibetan religion or is essentially a Buddhist sect with distinct labels is debated; recent Bön and academic scholarship (Karmay, Norbu) argues for genuine continuity with pre-Buddhist Zhang-Zhung tradition.

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-Buddhist Tibetan religion** (?-7th c. CE) — the religious substrate of pre-imperial and early-imperial Tibet; royal funerary cult, divination, mountain-spirit veneration.
2. **First-period Bön / suppression** (8th c.) — Buddhism becomes Tibetan state religion under King Tri Songdetsen; Bön is partially suppressed.
3. **Renaissance / systematization** (11th–14th c.) — Yungdrung Bön emerges in its modern monastic-textual form; mutual influence with the new Buddhist schools.
4. **Modern** — recognized as the fifth Tibetan religious school (alongside Nyingma, Kagyu, Sakya, Gelug) by the Dalai Lama's exile administration; substantial Western interest in Bön Dzogchen.
