# Madhyamaka

## Identity
The Madhyamaka ("Middle Way") school, founded by **[[nagarjuna]]** (~150–250 CE), is the most influential philosophical school in Mahāyāna Buddhism. Its central thesis: all phenomena, without exception, lack *svabhāva* (inherent, independent, self-subsistent existence). They arise only through *pratītyasamutpāda* (dependent origination) and therefore are *śūnya* (empty). This includes consciousness, the Tathāgata, and śūnyatā itself.

## Sub-schools

**Prāsaṅgika** (consequence / reductio school):
- **Buddhapālita** (~5th c.) and **Candrakīrti** (~7th c.) — the rigorous position: Madhyamaka can only use reductio ad absurdum arguments (*prasaṅga*) against opponents; it cannot assert any positive thesis of its own, including assertions about śūnyatā
- This is the *strictest* anti-foundationalist position: no positive ground, not even "emptiness is the ground"
- **Tsongkhapa** (1357–1419, Gelug) made Prāsaṅgika the official Tibetan Gelugpa standard

**Svātantrika** (autonomy / inference school):
- **Bhāvaviveka** (~6th c.) — argues that Madhyamaka *can* use positive syllogistic arguments (*svātantra-anumāna*)
- A middle position: the ultimate is empty, but we can reason about it positively at the conventional level
- Represents an attempt to integrate Madhyamaka with other schools of logic

## The rangtong / shentong split (Tibetan reception)

In Tibet, the Madhyamaka inheritance split along the lines of the Indian schools:

**Rangtong** ("self-empty," Gelugpa / Prāsaṅgika mainstream):
- All phenomena are empty of their own nature — including Buddha-nature, including consciousness
- No positive "luminous ground" — even buddha-nature is empty in the rangtong sense
- This is Nāgārjuna + Candrakīrti taken to their logical conclusion

**Shentong** ("other-empty," Kagyu / Jonang alternative):
- The ultimate (Buddha-nature, *tathāgatagarbha*) is empty *of everything other than itself* — it is not empty of its own positive qualities (wisdom, compassion, luminosity)
- This makes Buddha-nature a positive luminous ground — structurally identical to Trika's Śiva
- Dolpopa Sherab Gyaltsen (1292–1361) systematized shentong; the Jonangpa school carried it; later Jamgon Kongtrul and the Rimé movement synthesized rangtong and shentong

The rangtong / shentong debate is the single most important unsettled philosophical dispute in Tibetan Buddhism and directly maps onto the Trika vs. Madhyamaka fault line in Indian philosophy. See [[emptiness-sunyata]].

## Cross-tradition position

**← Nāgārjuna → Candrakīrti → Tsongkhapa**: The internal lineage; documented transmission chain over 1,200 years.

**↔ Trika Kashmir Shaivism (the fundamental rival)**: Madhyamaka is the most precise philosophical counter to Trika. Where Trika says "Śiva = luminous consciousness ground," Madhyamaka says "even 'consciousness' cannot be asserted as a ground without falling into *svabhāva*-thinking." These are not different formulations of the same insight; they are mutually exclusive metaphysical positions. The debate runs through Dzogchen's *rigpa*, the *tathāgatagarbha* literature, and the rangtong/shentong split. See [[tradition-kashmir-shaivism]], [[emptiness-sunyata]].

**↔ Advaita Vedanta**: Nāgārjuna's critique of *svabhāva* applies equally to Advaita's *Brahman* — any entity asserted as the unchanging ground has the *svabhāva* Madhyamaka rejects. Adi Shankaracharya was aware of this critique and responded; the debate was live.

**↔ Pseudo-Dionysius / apophatic theology**: The Western apophatic tradition's "God beyond being" resonates with Madhyamaka's refusal to predicate anything of the ultimate. But Ps-Dionysius's God still *is* (hyper-being), where Nāgārjuna's śūnyatā denies even that. See [[pseudo-dionysius-the-areopagite]].
