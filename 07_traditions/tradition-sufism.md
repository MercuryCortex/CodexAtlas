# Sufism (Tasawwuf)

## Overview
The mystical-ascetic dimension of Islam — *tasawwuf* derives from the Arabic *suf* (wool), referring to the ascetic woolen garments of early Sufis. Sufism crystallized in the second Islamic century (8th c. CE) under early figures like Hasan al-Basri and Rabi'a al-Adawiyya, and developed through the great classical synthesizers (al-Ghazali, Ibn Arabi, Rumi) into a multifaceted tradition combining ascetic practice, ecstatic poetry, theological metaphysics, and organized brotherhoods (*turuq*) with chains of master-disciple transmission.

This is a *seed* entry — Sufism is largely post-Phase-4 and will be expanded in Phase 5 alongside the medieval consolidation of Islamic civilization. The Phase 4 root is documented here because the late-antique mystical and ascetic milieu (Christian monasticism, Neoplatonist contemplation, late-antique gnosis traditions) constitutes part of the intellectual environment within which early Sufism emerged.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-islam]]: an internal dimension of Islam (though periodically contested by jurists).
- vs. [[tradition-neoplatonism]]: substantial intellectual debt — Plotinian and Proclean material reached Sufi metaphysics via Arabic translations of *Theology of Aristotle* (an Arabic paraphrase of Enneads IV–VI mistakenly attributed to Aristotle) and the *Liber de Causis* / *Pure Good* Proclean tradition.
