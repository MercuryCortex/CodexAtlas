# Hindu Modernism (Neo-Vedanta)

## Overview
A 19th–20th-century re-formulation of Hindu philosophy and practice in dialogue with Western modernity (colonialism, science, comparative religion, evolutionism, mass print, Protestantism). Hindu modernism systematized — sometimes innovated — a universalist Advaita-Vedantic register positioned as compatible with science, ethically humanist, anti-caste-reformist (variably), and globally exportable. Distinct from but historically interwoven with Hindu nationalism / Hindutva (which uses some of the same vocabulary in a substantially different politico-cultural project).

## Historical phases
1. **Bengal Renaissance period** (1828–1870s) — Ram Mohan Roy's Brahmo Samaj (1828); Dayananda Saraswati's Arya Samaj (1875); Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay's literary religious-cultural revival.
2. **Ramakrishna-Vivekananda period** (1860s–1902) — Ramakrishna's experiential-mystical foundation; Vivekananda's 1893 Chicago Parliament address; Vedanta Society (1894); Ramakrishna Mission (1897).
3. **Mature philosophical period** (1900–1950) — Aurobindo's *Life Divine* (1939–1940); Yogananda's American mission (1920–1952); Radhakrishnan's academic Vedanta (Oxford chairs; eventually President of India).
4. **Post-Independence period** (1947 onward) — TM (Maharishi Mahesh Yogi from 1957), Hare Krishna (Bhaktivedanta from 1965), Iyengar yoga (1966+), Osho/Rajneesh (1970+), Sai Baba, BAPS Swaminarayan; global expansion of the modern-Hindu spiritual export.

## Core texts
See key-documents; additionally the various commentaries on the Upanishads and Bhagavad-Gita that re-presented these classical texts for modernist audiences.

## Defining doctrines
- Advaita Vedanta (non-dualism) — the metaphysical core.
- Religious universalism — all religions are valid paths to the same goal.
- Compatibility with modern science.
- Anti-caste reform (in many but not all currents).
- Social engagement (Vivekananda's "Karma Yoga" emphasis).

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. classical [[tradition-vedic-hinduism]]: continuous in self-presentation but selectively-reformist in substance; some classical Hindu schools have criticized neo-Vedanta as oversimplification.
- vs. [[tradition-hindutva]]: shared vocabulary, divergent political project. Vivekananda is cited by both modernist-universalists and Hindu-nationalists; the dispute over his legacy is itself an Indian-cultural issue.
- vs. [[tradition-new-age]]: substantial flow of Hindu-modernist concepts (chakras, yoga, karma, reincarnation, third eye) into Western New Age vocabulary.
