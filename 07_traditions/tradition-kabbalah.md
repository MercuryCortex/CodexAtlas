# Kabbalah (medieval formation)

## Overview
Kabbalah — "received tradition" — is the medieval Jewish mystical-theosophic system that emerged in 12th-c. Provençal Jewry, crystallized in 13th-c. Castilian Jewry, and developed into the dominant strand of Jewish mystical thought thereafter. Its defining move: taking the ten *sefirot* of *Sefer Yetzirah* (originally primordial numbers / dimensions) and reinterpreting them as *divine emanations* / hypostases within the Godhead — generating a complex theosophy of inner-divine dynamics, divine sacred marriage (Tiferet ↔ Shekhinah), exile and return, and the human role in cosmic *tikkun* (repair).

## Historical phases
1. **Pre-Kabbalistic strata (~200–1100)** — Hekhalot-Merkavah literature; *Sefer Yetzirah*; Hasidei Ashkenaz currents.
2. **Provençal proto-Kabbalah (~1150–1200)** — *Sefer ha-Bahir*; Isaac the Blind and circle; the foundational reinterpretation of *sefirot* as emanations.
3. **Castilian classical Kabbalah (~1240–1290)** — Naḥmanides, the Cohen brothers (Isaac and Jacob ha-Cohen), Moses of Burgos, Abulafia (prophetic Kabbalah), Moses de León (Zohar).
4. **Sephardic dispersion (~1300–1492)** — Kabbalah becomes mainstream Jewish learning across the Mediterranean.
5. **Safed Lurianic Kabbalah (Phase 6, ~1570)** — Isaac Luria's systematic re-interpretation of the Zohar; the doctrines of *tzimtzum*, *shevirat ha-kelim*, *tikkun*.
6. **Hasidic and modern (Phase 6/7)** — Hasidism (Baal Shem Tov, ~1740 onward) is the principal vector of Kabbalistic mysticism into modern Eastern European Judaism.

## Core texts
- [[phase-5-012-sefer-yetzirah]] — proto-Kabbalistic foundation (composition uncertain, 200–900).
- [[phase-5-026-sefer-ha-bahir]] — first explicit Kabbalistic text (~1180).
- [[phase-5-027-sefer-ha-zohar]] — classical Kabbalah's monumental text (~1280, Moses de León).
- (Phase 6+ canonical: Cordovero's *Pardes Rimmonim*; Vital's *Etz Ḥayyim*; Hasidic literature.)

## Pantheon
- [[ein-sof]] — the Infinite, beyond all manifestation.
- [[the-sefirot]] — the ten divine emanations (Keter, Chokhmah, Binah, Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, Malkhut/Shekhinah).
- [[shekhinah]] — the lowest sefirah and the feminine divine partner in inner-divine sacred marriage.
- [[metatron]] — chief angel; in mature Kabbalah associated with Malkhut.
- [[lilith]] — demonic counterpart to Shekhinah; *sitra achra* (Other Side).

## Defining doctrines
- The ten *sefirot* as divine emanations.
- *Ein Sof* as apophatic Godhead.
- Inner-divine sacred marriage and the human's role in effecting cosmic *tikkun* (repair).
- *Gilgul* (reincarnation) as developed in *Bahir* onward.
- Esoteric reading of Torah; every verse encodes sefirotic dynamics.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-merkavah-mysticism]]: inherits Hekhalot apparatus while introducing the emanationist theosophy.
- vs. [[tradition-gnosticism]]: Scholem 1962's celebrated thesis of Gnostic-influenced origins; the foundational debate of medieval Jewish mysticism scholarship. Idel 1988 pushes back substantially.
- vs. [[tradition-sufism]] / Ibn ʿArabian *waḥdat al-wujūd*: structurally parallel apophatic-emanationist metaphysics (Sells 1994); minimal direct historical transmission but shared Mediterranean intellectual environment.
- vs. mainstream rabbinic Judaism: tension never absolute; classical Kabbalah was widely accepted by Sephardic and (later) Ashkenazi rabbinic authorities. Modern Haskalah and German Reform Judaism rejected Kabbalah; Hasidism and Sephardic / Mizrahi traditions retained it; modern Orthodox Judaism is split.
