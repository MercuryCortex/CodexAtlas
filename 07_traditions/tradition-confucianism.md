# Confucianism

## Overview
Confucianism is the Chinese tradition stemming from the teaching of Confucius (~551–479 BCE) and elaborated by his disciples and successors (Mencius, Xunzi) into a comprehensive ethical, political, ritual, and educational tradition. It centers on cultivating moral character (*rén* — humaneness; *yì* — righteousness; *lǐ* — ritual propriety; *zhì* — wisdom; *xìn* — faithfulness), exemplary persons (*jūnzǐ*), proper relationships (the Five Relationships — ruler-subject, parent-child, husband-wife, elder-younger, friend-friend), and a moral cosmic order grounded in *Tiān* (Heaven). It served as the official state ideology of imperial China from the Han dynasty (~+136 BCE) until 1911 and remained the foundation of East Asian moral-political culture in Korea, Japan, and Vietnam.

## Historical phases
1. **Classical / Spring-Autumn-Warring States (~-500 to -200)** — Confucius; Mencius (~-372 to -289) elaborating innate moral goodness; Xunzi (~-310 to -235) elaborating moral cultivation against innate human badness.
2. **Han Confucianism (-200 to +220)** — Establishment as imperial orthodoxy; Five Classics canonized; correlative cosmology synthesis with yin-yang and Five Phases (Dong Zhongshu).
3. **Six Dynasties decline (+220 to +600)** — Buddhism rises; Confucianism in tension.
4. **Tang revival (~+600 to +900)** — Han Yu's anti-Buddhist polemic; Classical revival.
5. **Neo-Confucianism (~+1000 to +1900)** — Synthesis with Buddhist and Daoist metaphysics; Zhu Xi (~+1130–1200) compiles the Four Books (Analects, Mencius, Great Learning, Doctrine of the Mean) as the canonical core; Wang Yangming (~+1472–1529) inward-turn.
6. **Modern (~+1900 onward)** — Attacked by reformers and revolutionaries; persecuted under Maoism; partial revival in contemporary China and global "New Confucianism".

## Core texts
- [[phase-2-015-analects-of-confucius]] — Analects (*Lunyu*).
- The Four Books (*Sìshū*, canonized by Zhu Xi ~1190): Analects, Mencius, *Great Learning* (*Dàxué*), *Doctrine of the Mean* (*Zhōngyōng*).
- The Five Classics (*Wǔjīng*): Book of Songs (*Shījīng*), Book of Documents (*Shūjīng*), Book of Changes (*Yìjīng* / *I Ching*), Book of Rites (*Lǐjì*), Spring and Autumn Annals (*Chūnqiū*). These became the basis of the imperial examination system.

## Key persons
- [[confucius]] — founder.
- (Phase 3+: Mencius, Xunzi, Dong Zhongshu, Han Yu, Zhu Xi, Wang Yangming.)

## Defining doctrines
- **Ren** (仁, humaneness / co-humanity).
- **Li** (禮, ritual propriety as the channel of moral cultivation).
- **Junzi** (君子, the exemplary person — paradigm of moral cultivation).
- **Xiao** (孝, filial piety as root of all virtue).
- **Tian Ming** (天命, the Mandate of Heaven).
- **Self-cultivation through learning, ritual, and self-examination.**

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-daoism]]: foundational Chinese pairing. Daoists ridicule Confucian ritualism; Confucians criticize Daoist quietism. Yet both traditions historically share scholarly milieu and personnel; the educated Chinese gentleman characteristically held Confucian outward, Daoist inward.
- vs. [[tradition-buddhism]]: tense; Confucians often attacked Buddhism as foreign, anti-familial (monastic celibacy), and economically parasitic. Neo-Confucianism quietly absorbed Buddhist metaphysics while polemicizing against it.
- vs. Legalism: classical rival; Confucians and Legalists fought for influence at Warring States courts. Legalism briefly triumphed under the Qin (~-221) and was then displaced by Confucianism under the Han.
