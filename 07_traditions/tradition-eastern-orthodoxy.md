# Eastern Orthodox Christianity

## Overview
Eastern Orthodoxy is the second-largest Christian communion (~260 million adherents), claiming unbroken continuity from the apostolic church through the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) imperial tradition. While it formally split from Rome in the **Great Schism of 1054 CE**, it identifies its theological formation with the seven Ecumenical Councils (325–787 CE) and the **Cappadocian Fathers** (Basil, Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa). Its most distinctive doctrine is **theosis** (θέωσις) — the gradual transformation of the human person into divine likeness through participation in God's uncreated energies, not merely forensic justification.

## Core doctrines
- **Theosis** — "God became human so that humans might become God" (Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* §54). The goal of Christian life is participatory union with the divine, not merely forgiveness of sin. This aligns Eastern Orthodoxy with mystical traditions globally more closely than Western scholastic theology does.
- **Palamism (divine essence vs. energies)** — Gregory Palamas (†1359) distinguished the unknowable divine *essence* from the uncreated divine *energies* that permeate creation. Hesychast monks access the uncreated *energies* directly; the Tabor light (seen at Christ's Transfiguration) is one such energy. Codified by the Council of Constantinople 1351.
- **Apophatic theology** — the Eastern tradition (Pseudo-Dionysius, Gregory of Nyssa) holds that God is fundamentally beyond all positive description; the most truthful theology is silence and negation. Positive statements about God are accommodations to human language, not accurate predications.
- **Iconography as theology** — the Seventh Ecumenical Council (Nicaea II, 787 CE) established icon veneration as doctrinally required. Icons are *windows into the divine*, not idols; the image participates in what it represents. The theology of the icon is a sophisticated semiotic position.

## MASSIVE wins — cross-tradition edges
- **Hesychasm ↔ Sufi fanāʾ/baqāʾ ↔ Hindu samādhi**: The hesychast practice (stillness, inner prayer, vision of uncreated light) is the most precise Christian parallel to contemplative absorption traditions worldwide. Gregory Palamas' Council of 1351 established the possibility of direct experiential encounter with the divine as Orthodox doctrine — a claim structurally identical to Sufi *fanāʾ* (annihilation of self in God) and Yoga's *samādhi*. All three systems posit a stage of contemplative practice that results in transformative contact with ultimate reality, and all three generated institutional controversy (Palamas was condemned before being vindicated; al-Hallaj was executed; samādhi traditions were absorbed into Brahmanical orthodoxy with difficulty). See [[tradition-sufism]], [[tradition-samkhya-yoga]].
- **Pseudo-Dionysius ↔ Neoplatonism**: [[pseudo-dionysius-the-areopagite]]'s *Celestial Hierarchy* and *Mystical Theology* are the primary conduit through which Neoplatonic apophatic theology entered Christian orthodoxy. The ninefold angelic hierarchy maps directly onto Proclus' emanation scheme. Orthodox theology is incomprehensible without this Neoplatonic substrate. See [[tradition-neoplatonism]].
- **Iconoclasm controversy (726–843 CE) ↔ Islamic aniconism ↔ Jewish second commandment**: The Byzantine iconoclast crisis (emperors destroying icons) was explicitly influenced by Islamic and Jewish critiques of image veneration. All three traditions trace back to the same Sinai second commandment; all three resolved it differently (Islam: total prohibition; Judaism: no images of God; Orthodoxy: icons required). The theological debate intersects [[tradition-islam]] and [[tradition-judaism]] at a precise historical moment.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. tradition-roman-catholicism: Filioque dispute (does the Spirit proceed from the Father alone or from Father *and* Son?), papal supremacy, purgatory doctrine, scholastic theology — the 1054 schism crystallized these into formal separation.
- vs. [[tradition-coptic-orthodox]] / [[tradition-syriac-orthodox]]: Oriental Orthodox churches separated at Chalcedon (451 CE) over Christ's nature (miaphysite vs. dyophysite). Eastern Orthodoxy is Chalcedonian.
- vs. [[tradition-sufism]]: Closest mystical structural parallel outside Christianity; mutual influence possible via shared Neoplatonic background and the geography of the Byzantine-Islamic frontier.
