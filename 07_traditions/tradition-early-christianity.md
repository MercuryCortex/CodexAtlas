# Early Christianity

## Overview
Early Christianity covers the period from the Jesus movement (~30 CE) through the Council of Nicaea (325 CE), when Christianity transformed from a Jewish sectarian movement within the Roman Empire into the empire's state religion. This is the vault's most contentious and well-documented **zone of cross-tradition fusion**: early Christianity simultaneously draws on [[tradition-judaism]] (scripture, apocalypticism, messianism), [[tradition-zoroastrianism]] (cosmic dualism, eschatology via the [[persian-period-injection]]), Greek philosophy ([[logos-cosmic-reason]], Neoplatonic emanation), and [[tradition-mystery-cults]] (dying-rising-deity pattern, initiatory structure).

## Historical phases
1. **Jesus movement (30–70 CE)** — Galilean prophet movement; Crucifixion (~30 CE); resurrection appearances; Pentecost community in Jerusalem; Paul's missionary journeys (37–67 CE); Gospels not yet written; Jewish-Christian debate over circumcision and Torah observance (Acts 15, Galatians 2).
2. **Post-Temple formation (70–135 CE)** — Destruction of Jerusalem Temple (70 CE) radically transforms both Judaism and Jewish Christianity; Gospels written (Mark ~70, Matthew/Luke ~85–90, John ~90–100 CE); deutero-Pauline letters; competition with Gnosticism; Ignatius of Antioch letters define episcopal structure.
3. **Second-century consolidation (135–200 CE)** — Justin Martyr's Logos apologetics; Irenaeus' canon formation and anti-Gnostic polemic; Marcion's challenge (proposed rejecting the Hebrew Bible); persecutions under Marcus Aurelius; Tertullian's Latin theology.
4. **Pre-Nicene synthesis (200–325 CE)** — Origen's systematic theology at Alexandria; Manichaeism's challenge (Mani deploys Christian vocabulary against Christianity); Constantine's Edict of Milan (313 CE); Council of Nicaea (325 CE) on the Trinity.

## Core formation processes
- **Canon formation**: The NT canon took ~300 years to settle. Marcion (~140 CE) forced the question; Irenaeus (~180 CE) argued for a fourfold Gospel; Athanasius' Easter letter (367 CE) lists the 27 canonical books. The process was contested, political, and geographically uneven.
- **Creedal formation**: Logos theology (Justin Martyr: Christ is the pre-existent rational principle through which the world was made) absorbed Greek philosophical universalism into Jewish messianism. The Nicene Creed (325 CE) formalized the Trinity after the Arian controversy.
- **Martyrdom theology**: Persecution shaped doctrine. Ignatius of Antioch wrote his letters en route to execution; his letters institutionalize the bishop and valorize martyrdom as *imitatio Christi*. Tertullian: *the blood of the martyrs is the seed of the Church*. The theological embrace of suffering as salvific is structurally continuous with the [[executed-divine-claimant]] pattern.

## MASSIVE wins — cross-tradition edges
- **Early Christianity ← [[persian-period-injection]]**: The three most distinctive Christian doctrines not derived from Hebrew Bible — cosmic dualism (Satan as real adversarial force), bodily resurrection, and cosmic eschatology (final judgment, new creation) — all appear in the 600-year window (550–1 BCE) when Jewish communities were under Persian then Hellenistic rule. Zoroastrian angelology, demonology, and eschatology (Frashokereti) provide the structural template.
- **Early Christianity ↔ [[tradition-gnosticism]]**: Gnosticism and proto-orthodox Christianity are not parent and child — they are **siblings** competing for the same Jesus tradition. Irenaeus' *Adversus Haereses* is the primary evidence that Gnostic Christianity was widespread and internally coherent before being defined as heretical. The vault's Gnostic nodes (Valentinus, Marcion, Basilides) map the alternative Christianity that lost.
- **Logos theology ↔ [[logos-cosmic-reason]]**: Justin Martyr's claim that Christ is the *Logos* (John 1:1) that Plato and Heraclitus dimly perceived was the foundational move that made Greek philosophy available to Christian theology. It also made Christianity universally applicable: wherever there is rational thought, the pre-incarnate Christ is present. The [[philo-of-alexandria]] Logos as intermediary being is the direct precursor.
- **Dying-rising pattern ↔ [[dying-rising-god]]**: Paul's language of Christ's death and resurrection (*1 Cor 15*) uses vocabulary structurally identical to mystery religion dying-rising deity patterns (Osiris, Tammuz, Adonis). Whether this is direct influence (Frazer → Cumont hypothesis), parallel independent development, or deliberate appropriation is debated; the structural parallel is undeniable and was noticed by early Christian apologists (*Justin Martyr*) who called it diabolical mimicry.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-judaism]]: Early Christianity emerges from within Second Temple Judaism (Pharisaism, Essenes, apocalyptic movements). The split is gradual (~70–135 CE); both traditions were transformed by the Temple's destruction.
- vs. [[tradition-gnosticism]]: Gnosticism competed on the same terrain for the same texts (Paul's letters, the Gospel of John). Proto-orthodoxy won institutionally; Gnosticism survived in Egypt (Nag Hammadi) and in Manichaean reception.
- vs. [[tradition-neoplatonism]]: Origen studied under Ammonius Saccas alongside Plotinus (the [[third-century-collision-zone]]). The mutual absorption of Platonic and Christian concepts shaped both traditions.
