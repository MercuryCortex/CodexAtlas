# Pure Land Buddhism

## Overview
Pure Land Buddhism is the devotional Mahāyāna tradition centered on rebirth in Sukhāvatī ("the Pure Land of Bliss") — the Buddha-field of [[amitabha]] (Amita / Amida) — as the operative soteriological goal. Practice is dominated by *nembutsu* / *nianfo* (恭念佛 / 念仏 / "calling the name of the Buddha"), the recitation of *Namo Amituofo* (Chinese) / *Namu Amida Butsu* (Japanese), as the karmically-efficacious act that secures rebirth in Sukhāvatī, where awakening becomes effectively guaranteed. Pure Land is the single most widely practiced form of Buddhism in East Asia by lay-numerical reach. In Japan, the Hōnen-Shinran reform sequence (12th–13th c.) developed it into the most theologically radical Mahāyāna position: *tariki* ("other-power") soteriology — that all human self-effort is futile, and that liberation depends entirely on Amida's already-accomplished compassion-vow ("the eighteenth vow" of the *Sukhāvatī-vyūha*).

## Historical phases
1. **Indian origins (~-100 to +200)** — earliest Sukhāvatī-vyūha sūtras emerge in northwest India / Gandhāra; Iranian-influenced light-Buddha cosmology possibly contributory (Mironov / Mochizuki / Schopen contested debate).
2. **Chinese reception (~150 to 400)** — Lokakṣema translates the Pratyutpanna-Buddha-Saṃmukhāvasthita-Samādhi-Sūtra ~179; Sukhāvatī-vyūha translated multiple times across the 3rd-5th c.; Huiyuan founds the White Lotus Society on Mount Lu in 402 with 123 monks and laymen vowing rebirth in Amitābha's pure land.
3. **Chinese classical formulation (~5th-7th c.)** — Tanluan, Daochuo, Shandao articulate the "easy path" (*yi xing dao*) vs. the "hard path" (the standard meditation-monastic discipline); the *Pure Land patriarch lineage* canonized.
4. **Tendai-Shingon integration in Japan (~9th-11th c.)** — Pure Land practice integrated into Tendai esoteric practice as a meditative *nembutsu*; Genshin's *Ōjōyōshū* (985) systematizes Pure Land meditation as a Tendai discipline.
5. **Kamakura reform (1175-1263)** — Hōnen breaks away from Tendai and founds Jōdo-shū ("Pure Land school") in 1175 with the radical thesis that *nembutsu* alone suffices and meditation/discipline are not soteriologically necessary. Shinran (Hōnen's disciple, married, declared himself "neither monk nor layman") radicalizes further into Jōdo-shinshū ("True Pure Land school"): no number of nembutsu recitations earns rebirth — Amida's vow is already accomplished, recitation is gratitude not effort.
6. **Mass institutionalization (~14th-19th c.)** — Jōdo-shinshū under Rennyo (1415-1499) becomes the largest organized Buddhist body in Japan; survives Tokugawa registration system as the *de facto* parish-religion of much of rural Japan.
7. **Modern (~1850 onward)** — global diaspora; first Buddhist institution in North America = Buddhist Churches of America (BCA), Jōdo-shinshū, 1899; modern intellectual reception via D.T. Suzuki and Buddhist-Christian dialogue (Pure Land's *tariki* / "other-power" → Lutheran *sola gratia* comparative-theology, especially Karl Barth's "Yodo School" footnote in *Church Dogmatics* I/2).

## Core texts
- [[phase-4-064-sukhavativyuha-larger]] — Larger Sukhāvatī-vyūha Sūtra (Aparimitāyus-sūtra; the 48 vows of Dharmākara → Amitābha).
- *Smaller Sukhāvatī-vyūha Sūtra* (Amitābha Sūtra) — the most-chanted Pure Land liturgical text in East Asia.
- *Amitāyurdhyāna Sūtra* (Contemplation Sūtra) — the 16 visualization meditations on Amitābha and Sukhāvatī; possibly a Chinese composition (Pas 1995).
- *Wangsheng lun* (Tanluan's commentary).
- *Anrakushū* (Daochuo).
- *Guannian famen* (Shandao).
- *Senchakushū* (Hōnen, 1198) — the founding manifesto of Jōdo-shū.
- *Kyōgyōshinshō* (Shinran, 1224) — Jōdo-shinshū's central doctrinal text.
- *Tannishō* (Shinran-school disciple Yuien, c.1290) — short post-Shinran reflection; the most-read text in modern Jōdo-shinshū.

## Pantheon
- [[amitabha]] — Buddha of Measureless Light; Amita in Sanskrit, Amituofo 阿彌陀佛 in Chinese, Amida 阿弥陀 in Japanese.
- [[avalokitesvara]] — chief bodhisattva attendant of Amitābha (Guanyin / Kannon in East Asian iconography).
- Mahāsthāmaprāpta (Daseizhi 大勢至 / Seishi 勢至) — second bodhisattva attendant.
- Dharmākara — the bodhisattva-name of Amitābha before he became a Buddha; the figure who makes the 48 vows.

## Defining doctrines
- ***Nembutsu* / *nianfo*** — recitation of "Namo Amituofo" / "Namu Amida Butsu" as central practice.
- ***Tariki*** ("other-power") — liberation depends on Amida's vow, not on self-effort. The most theologically radical Mahāyāna soteriology.
- **The Eighteen Vow** — the eighteenth of Dharmākara's 48 vows in the Larger Sukhāvatī-vyūha: "If, when I attain Buddhahood, sentient beings in the lands of the ten quarters who sincerely and joyfully entrust themselves to me, desire to be born in my land, and call my Name even ten times, should not be born there, may I not attain perfect enlightenment."
- ***Akunin shōki* — "the evil person is the rightful object [of Amida's vow]"** — Shinran's signature paradox: the more aware of one's incapacity, the more decisively one is the *intended* object of Amida's other-power.
- **The Pure Land as expedient** vs. **as ontological reality** — internal Pure Land debate. Shandao + Hōnen + Shinran: the Pure Land is real, accessible by rebirth. Chan/Zen-leaning syntheses (Yongming Yanshou ~960): the Pure Land is mind-only, accessed by direct realization.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-mahayana-buddhism]]: Pure Land *is* Mahāyāna; the question is whether it operates as an "easy path" alternative to the standard bodhisattva-discipline, or as the *only* viable path in the *mappō* "Degenerate-Age" frame (Hōnen-Shinran position).
- vs. [[tradition-zen]]: Yongming Yanshou (~960) and the Chinese "Chan-Pure Land combined practice" tradition reconciles them; the Japanese Kamakura reform (Hōnen, Shinran, Dōgen) splits them into self-conscious opposition (*tariki* vs. *jiriki*, "other-power" vs. "self-power"). Modern Chinese practice continues the combined model; Japanese practice retains the Kamakura distinction.
- vs. Christianity (esp. Lutheran *sola gratia*) — substantial comparative-theology discussion. Shin-Lutheran dialogue (e.g., the Buddhist-Christian Studies journal) takes the *tariki* / *sola gratia* parallel as the closest cross-tradition soteriological resonance. Whether this constitutes "influence" in either direction is disputed; the resonance is structural. *Parallel-form* not *ancestor-of*.
- vs. Iranian / Manichaean light-cosmology — Amitābha as "Buddha of Measureless Light" + Pure Land in the *western* quarter has plausibly Iranian / Zoroastrian / Manichaean substrate (Schopen 2005 contested; the earlier Mironov 1928 / Mochizuki 1942 hypothesis is now seen as overstated but not eliminated).
