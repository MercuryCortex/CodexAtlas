# Hermeticism

## Overview
A philosophical-religious tradition emerging in Hellenistic-Roman Egypt (1st–3rd centuries CE), centered on revelations attributed to Hermes Trismegistus ("thrice-greatest Hermes"), a Hellenized form of the Egyptian god Thoth. The tradition divides into "philosophical Hermetica" (the seventeen treatises of the *Corpus Hermeticum*, the Latin *Asclepius*, the Stobaean Fragments, and the Coptic Hermetica from Nag Hammadi) and "technical Hermetica" (astrology, alchemy, magic) — but the division is porous.

## Historical phases
1. **Greek-Egyptian crystallization** (100–300 CE) — composition of the surviving treatises in Alexandrian / Hermopolitan Egyptian milieu.
2. **Late-antique reception** (300–600) — Iamblichus, Proclus, and other Neoplatonists treat Hermetic literature as authoritative; Christian authors (Augustine, Lactantius) cite Hermes Trismegistus as a pagan-prophet predecessor.
3. **Medieval transmission** — Latin *Asclepius* preserves Hermetic theurgy in the West; Arabic Hermetica develop alchemy and magic.
4. **Renaissance revival** (1460+) — Ficino's Latin translation of the Greek Corpus (1471) launches Renaissance Hermeticism (Pico, Bruno).
5. **Casaubon's redating** (1614) ends Hermetic *authority* but begins Hermetic *historical study*.
6. **Modern Western esotericism** — Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn (1888+) and broader esoteric currents.

## Defining doctrines
- An unknowable Father / Mind / Nous as the supreme principle.
- Cosmos as living and divine (against the Gnostic and Christian world-rejection).
- The human as *deus terrenus* — earthly god, microcosm of the divine.
- Gnosis as the goal — direct vision of the divine attainable in this life.
- Ascent of the soul through the seven planetary spheres.

## Relations to neighbors
- vs. [[tradition-gnosticism]]: shared apophatic theology, gnosis-soteriology, soul-spark anthropology; but Hermetism does *not* devalue the cosmos as Gnosticism does.
- vs. [[tradition-neoplatonism]]: cousin currents, especially via Iamblichus and Proclus who took Hermetism seriously.
- vs. [[tradition-christianity-canonical]]: cited by Christian authors as pagan-prophet predecessor of Christian truth (Lactantius); attacked by Augustine on theurgy.
