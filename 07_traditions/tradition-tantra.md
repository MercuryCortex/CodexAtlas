Tantra is a family of esoteric Hindu and Buddhist traditions that developed c. 500–1200 CE, characterized by the use of ritual techniques — mantra (sacred sound), yantra (sacred diagram), mudra (sacred gesture), and specific meditative practices — to directly access and transform cosmic energies within and through the practitioner's body.

## Core Principles

**The Body as Cosmos:** Tantric practice is grounded in the identity of the human body with the cosmic body. The chakra system maps the cosmos onto the practitioner's anatomy; the 50 letters of the Sanskrit alphabet correspond to the 50 petals of the chakra system (see [[alphabet-devanagari-sacred]]). To work with the body's subtle anatomy is to work with cosmic structure directly.

**Mantra:** Sanskrit seed syllables (bija) and longer mantras are the primary operative tools. The phoneme, not the meaning, is the active element — precise pronunciation of specific syllables produces specific effects in the subtle body and in external reality.

**Yantra and Mandala:** Geometric diagrams (yantras) incorporating sacred syllables are ritual objects that create fields of sacred presence. The Sri Yantra (nine interlocking triangles) is the most complex example.

**Kundalini:** The serpent-power (*kundalini shakti*) coiled at the base of the spine; Tantric practice aims to raise this energy through the central channel (sushumna) to the crown chakra. See [[kundalini]] and [[caduceus]] for the cross-tradition parallel.

## Hindu and Buddhist Tantra

Hindu Tantra (Shaiva and Shakta schools) developed around the Shiva-Shakti polarity; the universe arises from the creative tension between Shiva (pure consciousness) and Shakti (dynamic energy).

Buddhist Tantra (Vajrayana) adapted many of the same techniques into a Buddhist framework. The Tibetan tradition represents the most elaborate preserved form — see [[tradition-vajrayana]] and [[alphabet-tibetan-tantric]] for the specifically Tibetan development.
