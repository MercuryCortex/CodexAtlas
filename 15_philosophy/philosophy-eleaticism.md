# Eleaticism (the Eleatic School)

## Identity

Eleaticism is the school of Presocratic philosophy founded at **Elea** (Velia, on the Tyrrhenian coast of southern Italy) by **[[parmenides|Parmenides of Elea]]** (b. c. -515) and carried by his associate `Zeno of Elea` (c. -490 to c. -430) and by `Melissus of Samos` (floruit c. -440). Its defining thesis is among the most radical in early Greek thought: **what-is** (*to eon*, "Being") *is*, and **what-is-not** cannot be — from which Parmenides derives that Being is **ungenerated and imperishable** (it cannot come from not-being), **one and indivisible**, **changeless and motionless**, and complete. Generation, destruction, change, and plurality — the entire manifest world — are relegated to the "Way of Opinion" (*doxa*), the deceptive seeming of mortals, as against the "Way of Truth" (*alētheia*). The school is named for the town, and the label "Eleatic" is already used by Plato (who calls Parmenides and his heirs "the Eleatic tribe," *Sophist* 242d).

The school's importance is out of all proportion to its size. By making not-being unthinkable, Parmenides rendered *change itself* philosophically illegitimate, and the whole subsequent course of Presocratic natural philosophy is an attempt either to vindicate that verdict or to build a cosmology that could survive it.

## The doctrine and its defenders

**Parmenides** states the core ontology in the hexameter poem *On Nature* ([[document-parmenides-poem]], ~160 lines surviving as DK 28): the goddess who receives the philosopher lays down that the only coherent path of inquiry is "that it is and cannot not-be." Being is then shown to lack birth, death, internal division, and motion.

**Zeno of Elea** defends this monism *indirectly*, by reductio: if you assume the opposite of Parmenides — that plurality and motion are real — you are driven into contradiction and absurdity. Hence the famous paradoxes preserved by Aristotle (*Physics* VI, 239b) and Simplicius:

- **Achilles and the tortoise** — the swift runner can never overtake the slow, because he must first reach where the tortoise was, by which time it has moved on, *ad infinitum*.
- **The dichotomy** — to cross a stadium one must first cross half, and half of that, and so on: motion cannot even begin.
- **The arrow** — at every instant of its flight the arrow occupies a space equal to itself and so is at rest; if it is at rest at every instant, it never moves.
- The **paradoxes of plurality** — if things are many, they must be both infinitely large and without size, both limited and unlimited in number.

Plato (*Parmenides* 128c) reports Zeno's own account of his aim: to defend Parmenides against those who mocked monism, by showing that the pluralist alternative is *more* ridiculous. Aristotle called Zeno the inventor of **dialectic**.

**Melissus of Samos** restates and generalizes the doctrine in prose (DK 30). His most consequential departure from Parmenides is to argue that Being is **spatially infinite** ("unlimited," *apeiron*) rather than a finite sphere, and explicitly to **deny the void** (*kenon*): there is no empty space, because empty space would be a "what-is-not," and so nothing could move into it — motion is impossible for want of anywhere unoccupied to move. This flat denial of the void is the exact claim the atomists were built to overturn.

## Cross-tradition significance

**Eleaticism ↔ Greek atomism (the reply that posited the void).** The single most important reception of Eleaticism is negative and historically documented: **[[philosophy-atomism-greek|Greek atomism]]** (Leucippus, Democritus) is constructed *as the pluralist answer to Eleatic monism*. The Eleatics had argued that motion and plurality require a "what-is-not" — empty space — and that since not-being cannot be, there is no void and hence no real change. The atomists accept the terms of the argument and reverse the conclusion: they assert that **not-being (the void) genuinely is**, real empty space in which a plurality of full, indivisible, Parmenidean-style "beings" (the atoms — each ungenerated, indivisible, changeless, exactly as Parmenides described Being) move and recombine. Atomism is thus best read as *Parmenidean Being pluralized and set in the very void the Eleatics denied* — Kirk-Raven-Schofield and Sedley present it in precisely these terms. This is transmission by *reaction*, not by borrowing, and the channel is secure.

**Eleatic monism ↔ the doctrine of the One (divine-unity).** Eleatic Being is the earliest rigorous metaphysical monism in the West. Within the Greek line the wire is genealogical: through Plato's engagement in the *Parmenides* and *Sophist* (where "not-being as difference" is the classic ancient solution to the Eleatic puzzle) and through Neoplatonism's *to Hen* — the One of Plotinus ([[the-one-plotinus]]) beyond being and multiplicity — Parmenidean Being is the direct ancestor of the philosophical treatment of the One under the [[divine-unity]] cluster. Toward the non-Greek members of that cluster (Kabbalistic Ein Sof, Islamic *tawhid*), by contrast, the resemblance is **typological**, a shared destination reached independently, not a demonstrable line of descent.

**Eleatic Being ↔ Advaita brahman; Zeno ↔ Madhyamaka (convergence, not contact).** Two comparative parallels recur in the literature and are held here honestly as convergence. First, the Eleatic contrast between the one changeless Being (Truth) and the plural changing appearance (Opinion) structurally mirrors the [[philosophy-advaita-vedanta|Advaita Vedanta]] contrast between the non-dual, changeless *brahman* and the illusory plurality of *maya* — a comparison pressed since Paul Deussen but treated by the modern mainstream (and even by the comparativist McEvilley) as an **independent structural convergence**, with no attested 5th-century-BCE transmission channel. Second, Zeno's reductio method — dismantling motion, division, and plurality to defend a metaphysical thesis — parallels the dialectic of **[[philosophy-madhyamaka|Madhyamaka Buddhism]]**, where Nagarjuna deploys reductio against motion and *svabhava* (own-being) to establish emptiness. The parallel is one of **argumentative form**, again independent in origin.

## Disputes

- **Strict vs. modal monism.** Does Parmenides claim that *literally only one thing exists* (strict numerical monism — Burnet, older Kirk-Raven) or that *whatever genuinely is must be eternal and changeless* (a modal/predicational thesis compatible with more than one such being — Palmer 2009)? The reading chosen changes what "the Eleatic school" is committed to.
- **The status of the Way of Opinion.** Why does Parmenides append a detailed cosmology he calls untrue? The interpretive options (instructional foil, modal exercise, two partly-valid perspectives, appearance-only physics) are the same live puzzle catalogued in the [[parmenides]] node.
- **Whether Zeno's paradoxes are "solved."** Analytic reconstruction (Barnes 1982; the Vlastos/Salmon tradition) treats the paradoxes as still-instructive arguments about infinity, continuity, and the mathematics of limits, rather than mere sophisms disposed of by the calculus — the arrow and the plurality paradoxes in particular resist a purely mathematical dissolution.
- **Was there a unified "school" at all?** Some scholars stress that Zeno and Melissus develop Parmenides in incompatible directions (finite sphere vs. spatial infinite; the two even differ on whether Being is bodily), so that "Eleaticism" names a family of related doctrines rather than a single systematic teaching.
