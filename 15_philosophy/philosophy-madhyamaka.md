Madhyamaka ("Middle Way" school) is the philosophical tradition founded by [[nagarjuna]] (c. 150–250 CE) in his *[[phase-4-075-mulamadhyamakakarika|Mulamadhyamakakarika]]* (Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way). It was extended by Chandrakirti (c. 600–650 CE) in the Prasannapada commentary, and received its definitive Tibetan systematization by [[tsongkhapa]] (1357–1419 CE) in the Gelug school.

## Core Doctrine: Sunyata

**Sunyata** ([[emptiness-sunyata]]) — "emptiness" — is the claim that all phenomena lack inherent existence (svabhava): nothing exists from its own side, independently, as a self-sufficient substance. All things arise in dependence on causes, conditions, and conceptual designation. This is not nihilism — Nagarjuna explicitly distinguishes emptiness from nothingness. Emptiness is itself empty (it is not a super-substance); it is the precondition for change, relationship, and process. Things can arise and cease precisely because they have no fixed inherent nature.

The method of establishing emptiness is the **prasanga** (reductio ad absurdum): for any entity X claimed to have inherent existence, Nagarjuna demonstrates that this claim leads to logical contradiction. He does this systematically for motion, time, causation, the self, the Buddha, and nirvana itself.

The Two Truths: conventional truth (the level at which tables and persons and cause-and-effect function reliably) and ultimate truth (the level at which no phenomenon has inherent existence). Madhyamaka refuses to collapse these — the conventional world is not dismissed as illusion, but neither is it granted metaphysical solidity.

## Cross-Tradition Connections

### MASSIVE WIN — Nagarjuna / Wittgenstein / Derrida: Three Deconstructions

Nagarjuna's prasanga method deconstructs every possible metaphysical position by showing that any claim to a fixed, determinate referent leads to contradiction. Language cannot reach the real — it can only gesture toward it by failing consistently.

[[wittgenstein]]'s *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus* (1921): "What can be said at all can be said clearly, and what one cannot speak about one must pass over in silence." The limits of language are the limits of the world. The method of the *Philosophical Investigations* — showing that philosophical problems arise from language gone on holiday, dissolving pseudo-problems rather than solving them — is structurally prasanga applied to ordinary language.

[[derrida]]'s différance: meaning is never present but always deferred; no sign has a determinate referent outside the play of differences. Deconstruction undoes any text's claim to a stable metaphysical center.

Three philosophers, 1,800 years apart, three different traditions (Buddhist India, analytic Austria, French post-structuralism), the same operation: language cannot capture the real; the attempt to do so generates the problems we mistake for deep truths.

### Sunyata and the Apophatic Absolute

The Madhyamaka claim that ultimate reality cannot be described in positive terms — because positive description assumes inherent existence in the object described — places it in structural alignment with:

- **The One** ([[the-one-plotinus]], Neoplatonism): beyond being and thought; all positive attributes are denied
- **[[ein-sof]]** (Kabbalah): the limitless beyond all attributes
- **The Dao** (philosophical Daoism): "The Dao that can be named is not the eternal Dao"
- **Nirguna Brahman** (Advaita Vedanta): the unconditioned absolute without qualities

All five traditions arrive at the same apophatic terminus: ultimate reality cannot be captured in positive assertion. Whether this is a discovery about the structure of language, the structure of mind, or the structure of reality is the deepest open question in comparative philosophy.

### Madhyamaka and Pyrrhonian Skepticism

[[pyrrho-of-elis]] (c. 360–270 BCE), founder of Greek skepticism, achieved mental tranquility (ataraxia) by suspending judgment on all metaphysical questions. Nagarjuna achieves the same result by demonstrating that all metaphysical positions are logically untenable. Both move from deconstruction of fixed positions to a state of mental openness and equanimity.

Pyrrho reportedly encountered Indian "naked philosophers" (Gymnosophists — possibly Jainas or Ajivikas) during Alexander's Indian campaign. If Pyrrho brought back a methodological orientation toward suspension of judgment from this encounter, the structural parallel between Pyrrhonism and Madhyamaka may reflect actual philosophical contact — not later, but at the origin.

## Tradition-Internal Development

Madhyamaka split into two sub-schools: **Prasangika** (Chandrakirti's position: the prasanga method alone establishes emptiness; no autonomous inference is needed) and **Svatantrika** (Bhavaviveka: autonomous syllogisms can establish emptiness independently). [[tsongkhapa]]'s synthesis — the Gelug school's interpretation — holds the Prasangika position and integrates it with the Bodhisattva path, making Madhyamaka the metaphysical foundation of Tibetan Buddhism's ethical and soteriological program.
