# Yogācāra

Yogācāra ("the practice of yoga") — also called Vijñānavāda ("consciousness-doctrine") and *cittamātra* ("mind-only") — is one of the two principal classical Mahāyāna philosophical schools, alongside [[philosophy-madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]]. Founded in the 4th-5th c. CE by the brothers Asaṅga and Vasubandhu in Gandhāra (modern Pakistan/Afghanistan), it offers a phenomenological + epistemological treatment of consciousness that has been variously characterized as Buddhist idealism, Buddhist phenomenology, or (in more careful modern readings) a non-metaphysical analysis of cognitive process.

## The core doctrine

The principal Yogācāra claim — *vijñapti-mātra*, "consciousness-only" or "representation-only" — is that the objects of cognition are not directly given external things but cognitive representations, and that ordinary cognition systematically mistakes representations for external things. The strong reading takes this as metaphysical idealism: only mind exists. The weak/phenomenological reading (Lusthaus 2002 + others) takes it as the epistemological claim that what we have access to in cognition is always already cognitive, never the unmediated thing-in-itself.

The school developed an eightfold consciousness analysis:

- The five sense-consciousnesses (eye, ear, nose, tongue, body).
- The mental consciousness (*mano-vijñāna*).
- The afflicted consciousness (*kliṣṭa-manas*) — the consciousness that generates the false sense of a substantial self.
- The store-consciousness (*ālaya-vijñāna*) — the underlying continuum that carries karmic "seeds" (*bīja*) from past actions and produces future cognitive events; this is the distinctive Yogācāra contribution and is one of the most-discussed concepts in Buddhist philosophy.

## The three natures

Yogācāra analyzes phenomena under three modes (*trisvabhāva*):

- *Parikalpita* (the imagined): the cognitive misconstrual that takes representations as external substances.
- *Paratantra* (the dependent): the actual stream of dependently-arising cognitive events.
- *Pariniṣpanna* (the perfected): the dependent-arising stream cognized as it is, without misconstrual.

The path to liberation is the gradual transformation of dependent-arising cognition from being seen-as-imagined to being seen-as-perfected — without removing any phenomenon, but removing the misconstrual.

## Transmission to East Asia

Yogācāra entered China through Xuanzang's 17-year pilgrimage to India (629-645) and his return with the corpus of Yogācāra texts; the Chinese school he founded was Faxiang (法相). Through Korean intermediaries it reached Japan as Hossō (法相). The strand survives most actively in Tibetan Buddhism, where it is preserved both as a school and as a stage in the curricular philosophical progression (Madhyamaka being the higher stage in the Gelug-school sequence).

## Cross-tradition connections

- **[[philosophy-madhyamaka]]**: the rival classical Mahāyāna school; the two-truths-vs-three-natures debate is one of the principal axes of classical Mahāyāna philosophy.
- **[[tradition-tibetan-buddhism]]**: continuing institutional context.
- **[[tradition-chan]]**: Chan/Zen draws extensively on Yogācāra concepts.
- **[[philosophy-advaita-vedanta]]**: structural-parallel non-dual idealism (the comparison + the historical influence-question is much-discussed).
