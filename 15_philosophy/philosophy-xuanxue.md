# Xuanxue

Xuanxue (玄學, "Profound Learning", "Dark Learning", or "Learning of the Mystery"; conventionally **Neo-Daoism**) is the metaphysical current that dominated elite Chinese thought in the **Wei–Jin period** (3rd–4th c. CE). Its name is taken from the first chapter of the *[[phase-2-014-daodejing|Daodejing]]* — *xuán zhī yòu xuán* (玄之又玄, "mystery upon mystery, the gateway of all wonders") — and its canon was the **Three Mysteries** (三玄, *sān xuán*): the *[[phase-2-014-daodejing|Laozi]]*, the *[[phase-2-022-zhuangzi|Zhuangzi]]*, and the *[[phase-2-042-yi-jing-i-ching|Yijing]]*. Xuanxue is not a return to religious Daoism (*Daojiao*) but a philosophical revival of the classics of [[philosophy-daoism-philosophical|philosophical Daoism]], pursued by aristocratic scholars — many of them nominally Confucian officials — through **commentary** and through the salon-art of *qīngtán* (清談, "pure conversation"). Its two great figures are **Wang Bi** (王弼, 226–249), who died at twenty-three having written the commentaries on the *Laozi* and *Yijing* that became canonical, and **Guo Xiang** (郭象, d. 312), whose commentary on the *Zhuangzi* is the received recension of that text.

## Core doctrine

**You and wu (有 / 無, being and non-being).** The central metaphysical question of Xuanxue is the relation between determinate beings (*yǒu*, the "ten-thousand things") and non-being (*wú*). **Wang Bi** and **He Yan** (何晏, d. 249) took the position later labelled *guì wú* (貴無, "valuing/esteeming non-being"): all beings are grounded in *wu*, which is not sheer nothing but the **non-determinate** — because it has no properties of its own, it can be the undifferentiated source and "ruler" of everything determinate. Wang Bi glosses the Dao of the *Laozi* as exactly this productive nothingness.

**Benmo (本末, root and branch).** Wang Bi's signature schema pairs the **root** (*běn* = *wu*, the Dao, the One) with the **branches/tips** (*mò* = *you*, the phenomenal many). "The many cannot govern the many; what governs the many is the utterly solitary One" — so the sage "honours the root and puts the branches at rest" (*chóng běn xī mò*). The root/branch (benmo), one/many architecture is among Xuanxue's most influential legacies, feeding later the *lǐ* (principle) / *shì* (phenomena) vocabulary of Chinese Buddhism and Neo-Confucianism.

**Mingjiao versus ziran (名教 / 自然, moral nomenclature versus spontaneity).** The socio-ethical debate of the age: is the Confucian order of **names and roles** (*míngjiào* — the "teaching of names", ritual, hierarchy) compatible with Daoist **spontaneity / self-so-ness** (*zìrán*)? Wang Bi and He Yan sought a **harmony** in which *míngjiào* issues from *zìrán* as branch from root — Confucius, on this reading, was the greater sage precisely because he *embodied* wu rather than talking about it. The **Seven Sages of the Bamboo Grove** — especially **Ji Kang** (嵇康) and **Ruan Ji** (阮籍) — radicalized *zìrán* against a corrupt *míngjiào*, in Ji Kang's slogan "transcend the teaching of names and let the natural be your guide" (越名教而任自然). **Guo Xiang** then re-harmonized them from the other direction: the true sage can "roam within the bounds" of society and "roam beyond" them at once, so that *míngjiào* **is** *zìrán* correctly understood.

**Guo Xiang's dúhuà (獨化, "lone-transformation").** Against Wang Bi's *guì wú*, Guo Xiang mounts a *chóng yǒu* ("valuing being") counter-metaphysics (anticipated by **Pei Wei's** 裴頠 *Chóng yǒu lùn*, "Discourse on Esteeming Being"). Guo Xiang denies that *wu* can be a productive ground at all — "non-being is nothing, and nothing cannot produce being." Instead each thing **self-generates** (*zìshēng* 自生) and **lone-transforms** (*dúhuà*): there is no Creator, no single Dao behind the world, only the ten-thousand things spontaneously being self-so in mutual dependence. This is one of the most radically **immanent** metaphysics in the Chinese tradition.

**Yan and yi (言 / 意, words and meaning).** Xuanxue's apophatic epistemology. Adapting the *Zhuangzi*'s fish-trap image, Wang Bi argues "once you grasp the meaning, forget the words" (*déyì wàngyán* 得意忘言) — and, for the *Yijing*, forget even the hexagram-images once their meaning is grasped. This sides with "words do not fully express meaning" (言不盡意) against Ouyang Jian's "words *do* fully express meaning" (言盡意論): the nameless root exceeds all language, which is only a provisional pointer.

## Cross-tradition significance

**The Chinese elaboration of the Dao ([[dao]]).** Xuanxue is, in effect, the moment the Atlas's [[dao]] node describes philosophically. The nameless Dao of the *Daodejing* becomes, in Wang Bi's hands, an explicit **ontology of wu**: the non-determinate ground of all determinate being. Because the received *Laozi* text is the **Wang Bi recension**, the Dao that most readers have inherited for 1,700 years is a Dao already read through Xuanxue eyes. The "wu / non-being as the source of you / being" that the [[dao]] node names is Wang Bi's formulation.

**The scaffold for Chinese Buddhism ([[emptiness-sunyata]]) — a productive misreading.** Xuanxue is "the bridge between classical Daoism and the reception of Buddhism in China." When Prajñāpāramitā texts reached China with their doctrine of **emptiness** (*śūnyatā*), there was as yet no adequate Chinese vocabulary for them, and translators used *geyi* (格義, "matching concepts") to render Indian terms with Xuanxue ones — above all **śūnyatā ≈ wú** (non-being). The resulting **"Six Houses and Seven Schools"** (六家七宗) of 4th-century Chinese *prajñā* thought argued about emptiness in Wang Bi's terms — most literally the **"Original Non-being" (本無, *běnwú*) school**, which read *śūnyatā* as the productive *wu* of the root/branch scheme — and **Zhi Dun** (支遁, 314–366) reinterpreted the *Zhuangzi*'s "free and easy wandering" in a Buddhist key. This is a genuine **transmission** channel: borrowed vocabulary, not shared doctrine. It was a *misreading* — treating emptiness as a metaphysical ground rather than the negation of all grounds — and it was **corrected** within a century (see Disputes).

**A non-dual structure, twice over ([[non-duality]]).** Xuanxue supplies two distinct instances of the pan-traditional [[non-duality]] pattern. Wang Bi's *benmo* monism makes the phenomenal many not-other-than their single non-determinate root — a grounding non-dualism. Guo Xiang's *dúhuà* reaches non-duality from the opposite side: with **no** transcendent Dao behind things, the self-generating totality of the ten-thousand things is self-so with nothing outside it, an immanent, groundless non-dualism close to the *Zhuangzi*'s "all things and I are one." The parallel to the cross-tradition hub is **structural/typological**, not the product of contact.

**Downstream.** The root/branch (*benmo*) and one/many architecture of Xuanxue, filtered through Chinese Buddhism, resurfaces in the *lǐ*/*shì* metaphysics of [[philosophy-huayan|Huayan]] and, secularized, in the *lǐ*/*qì* system of [[philosophy-neoconfucianism-zhu-xi|Zhu Xi's Neo-Confucianism]] — the graph *lǐ* (理, principle) has, as the Huayan node's Disputes section notes, "an independent Confucian and Xuanxue prehistory."

## Disputes

- **Was Wang Bi "Confucian" or "Daoist"?** Xuanxue scholars were officials who venerated Confucius yet philosophized through the *Laozi* and *Zhuangzi*. Wang Bi's claim that **Confucius**, not Laozi, was the true sage of *wu* (because he embodied it silently rather than discoursing on it) makes the school's confessional label genuinely ambiguous — the Atlas files it under [[tradition-daoism|Daoism]] for its textual canon and its metaphysics of *wu*, but wires it to Confucianism, and the "Neo-Daoism" tag is a modern convenience the sources (Graham 1989) treat with caution.

- **Guì wú vs chóng yǒu — an internal fault line.** Xuanxue is not a single doctrine. Wang Bi's **valuing-non-being** and Guo Xiang's / Pei Wei's **valuing-being** are opposed metaphysics: one grounds the many in a productive *wu*, the other denies any ground and asserts spontaneous self-generation. Reading the school as a unity flattens this; Ziporyn (2003) treats Guo Xiang as a deliberate **refutation** of the guì-wú program, not its continuation.

- **How much of "Guo Xiang" is Xiang Xiu?** The received *Zhuangzi* commentary was long alleged (already in the *Shishuo Xinyu*) to have been substantially **plagiarized** from the earlier commentator **Xiang Xiu** (向秀, c. 223–300). Modern scholarship generally credits Guo Xiang with the decisive philosophical shaping while acknowledging a real debt to Xiang Xiu; the exact division is unrecoverable.

- **The *geyi* bridge was repudiated.** The Xuanxue reading of emptiness as *wu* was a **transitional error**. **Kumārajīva** (344–413) and his pupil **Sengzhao** (僧肇, 384–414), in the *Zhao Lun* (肇論), explicitly corrected the Daoist misconstrual: emptiness is neither *you* nor *wu* but the Middle Way that negates both. So the Xuanxue-Buddhist link is best framed as **scaffolding later removed** — historically decisive for the *reception* of Buddhism, doctrinally superseded once an accurate Madhyamaka vocabulary existed. Zürcher (1959) is the standard account.
