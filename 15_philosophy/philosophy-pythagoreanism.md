# Pythagoreanism (the philosophical school — number-metaphysics)

## Identity

Pythagoreanism, as a *philosophical school*, is the body of doctrine developed by [[pythagoras|Pythagoras of Samos]] (c. -570/-495) and the community he founded at [[tradition-pythagorean|Croton]] c. -530, and elaborated by [[philolaus|Philolaus]], [[archytas|Archytas]], and the later Neo-Pythagoreans ([[nicomachus|Nicomachus of Gerasa]], [[apollonius-of-tyana|Apollonius of Tyana]], [[numenius-of-apamea|Numenius]]). Its four load-bearing theses are: (1) **number is the archē** — the elements of number are the elements of things, and the cosmos is "a *harmonia* and a number" (Aristotle, *Metaphysics* 985b); (2) **harmonia** — consonant intervals, the planetary heavens, and the ordered soul all express the same integer ratios; (3) **metempsychōsis** — the immortal soul transmigrates across bodies until purified; and (4) the **akousmata** — the cryptic ritual-ethical precepts that governed the initiate's life.

This node is deliberately the *doctrinal* node, kept distinct from two others exactly as [[philosophy-neoplatonism]] is kept distinct from [[tradition-neoplatonism]]: [[tradition-pythagorean]] is the **community/lineage** (the brotherhood, its initiate-grades, its historical phases and political fortunes), and [[pythagoras]] is the **founder**. It is a sibling of the other classical-Greek school nodes in the Atlas — [[philosophy-platonism]], [[philosophy-stoicism]], [[philosophy-atomism-greek]], [[philosophy-pyrrhonian-skepticism]] — and, on the ancient tradition's own reckoning (Iamblichus), the school *upstream* of Platonism.

## Core doctrine

**Number as archē; the tetractys.** The Pythagoreans held that number is not a description laid over reality but its very constitution. The sacred figure of this thesis is the **tetractys** — the triangular array of ten points arranged 1-2-3-4 — whose rows contain the ratios of all the consonances (2:1, 3:2, 4:3) and whose sum is the perfect **decad**, 10, held to encode the whole of reality. See [[mathematics-pythagorean-sacred]].

**Harmonia and the harmony of the spheres.** The school's founding empirical result — that the octave, fifth and fourth correspond to the ratios 2:1, 3:2, 4:3 (the monochord demonstration) — is securely early-Pythagorean, though the experimental narratives (hammers, monochord) that dramatise it are later and partly legendary (Burkert 1972); see [[music-pythagorean-harmony]]. Extended to the heavens, it yields the **harmony of the spheres** ([[music-music-of-spheres]]): the planets, moving at speeds proportional to their distances, sound intervals we do not hear only because we have never known silence (Aristotle, *De Caelo* 290b).

**Metempsychōsis and purification.** The soul is immortal and divine; it transmigrates through successive lives — human, animal, possibly plant — and is released only through *katharsis*: vegetarianism (one might eat a reincarnated soul), ritual purity, and the mathematical study that attunes the soul to cosmic *harmonia*. This binds the school's [[reincarnation|metempsychōsis]] to its [[soul-immortality|soul-immortality]] and to its ethics.

**The akousmata and the two grades.** The community's practical rule was carried in the *akousmata* — cryptic sayings ("do not stir the fire with a knife," "abstain from beans") — and its members were divided into *akousmatikoi* ("listeners," who kept the precepts) and *mathēmatikoi* ("learners," who received the demonstrative mathematics), the oldest attested esoteric/exoteric institutional split.

**The Monad and the Dyad.** In the Neo-Pythagorean arithmetical theology (Nicomachus), the [[monad-pythagorean|Monad]] (the One) and the Indefinite Dyad are the twin principles from which number, and thence the cosmos, are generated — the seed of the Neoplatonic One-and-Intellect.

## Cross-tradition significance

Pythagoreanism sits at a junction where **documented transmission** and **independent convergence** must be told apart carefully — and where the honest account is one of *directional* flows in both directions.

**Downstream transmission (secure).** The school is the fountainhead of Western number-mysticism. Its harmonics pass through [[philolaus|Philolaus]] and [[archytas|Archytas]] to [[philosophy-platonism|Plato]] (the *Timaeus* World-Soul), then through Ptolemy and [[boethius|Boethius]] into the medieval *quadrivium*, into Islamic *falsafa* (al-Fārābī's *Kitāb al-Mūsīqā*), and are revived in Kepler's *Harmonices Mundi* (1619). Its number-theology is systematised by [[philosophy-neoplatonism|Neoplatonism]] (Monad → the One) and reaches, via the Neoplatonic-Arabic channel, the numerical cosmology of the *Sefer Yetzirah* and Kabbalah. That Plato "thinks in Pythagorean categories" is not a resemblance but an inheritance carried by named people (Archytas, Plato's host in Magna Graecia) and texts.

**Upstream / sibling transmission.** Two of the school's signatures are themselves *received*. Metempsychōsis and its purificatory regimen run parallel to — and are entangled with — the [[tradition-orphic|Orphic]] complex of the same time and place (Magna Graecia, 6th-5th C), documented in the Orphic Gold Tablets and the Derveni Papyrus; whether Pythagoreanism drew on Orphism, the reverse, or the two are parallel currents with shared participants is unresolved (Burkert 1972; West 1983). The Atlas therefore wires [[reincarnation]] to this school as a **contributor**, not an originator, and wires Orphism as a **parallel**.

**Independent convergence (typological, contested).** The most striking parallels lie eastward and are best read as convergence, not contact. Pythagorean metempsychōsis and Upaniṣadic/Jain/Buddhist *saṃsāra* are structurally identical and temporally overlapping (both Axial-Age, 6th-5th C), and the ancient biographers (Iamblichus, Porphyry) claim Pythagoras travelled to Persia and India — but the Indo-Greek contact zone opened only after Alexander (c. -323), so the mainstream verdict is shared Axial-Age development, not borrowing. Likewise the Pythagorean *harmonia*-cosmos belongs to the same functional family as Egyptian *ma'at*, Vedic *ṛta*, Avestan *aša* and Heraclitean *logos* ([[wisdom-as-cosmic-order]]) — a single right order at once mathematical and normative — but Pythagorean order is distinctively *mathematical-acoustic* rather than juridical, and the parallel is convergence within a family, not a documented line.

## Disputes

**How much of the "all is number" cosmology is early?** The central scholarly fault-line, opened by Walter Burkert (1972): the historical Pythagoras may have been primarily a religious-charismatic founder, with the full **number-metaphysics a later, Philolaic-and-Academic construction** retrojected onto him by Aristotle and the Old Academy. Leonid Zhmud (2012) mounts the major dissent, arguing for genuine early Pythagorean mathematics and science. The Atlas attributes the developed doctrines at the *school* level (secure) while flagging that the *founder*-level attribution of the cosmology is exactly what is contested — the one securely-earliest doctrine being metempsychōsis, fixed by Xenophanes' mockery c. -540.

**Plato-Pythagorean priority.** Whether Plato inherited the soul-doctrine and mathematical metaphysics of the *Phaedo* and *Timaeus* from the Pythagoreans or developed them largely on his own is an old debate; current consensus (Kahn 2001; Ebrey ed. 2022) accepts substantial Pythagorean inheritance plus Platonic elaboration — hence the "contributor-to" edges to [[philosophy-platonism]] and [[soul-immortality]].

**The theorem.** That Pythagoras "discovered" or first proved the right-triangle theorem is a late-antique legend; the proposition was known in Babylonian (Plimpton 322) and Indian (Baudhāyana's *Śulba-sūtra*) mathematics centuries earlier, and which early Pythagorean, if any, gave the first Greek proof is unknown (Burkert 1972).
