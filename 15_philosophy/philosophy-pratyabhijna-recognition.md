# Pratyabhijñā

The "Recognition" school of Kashmir Śaiva philosophy — the high-classical articulation of *monistic tantra* in 9th-11th c. Kashmir. The defining philosophical claim: liberation (*mokṣa*) consists not in attaining a new state but in *recognizing* (*pratyabhijñā*) one's pre-existing identity as the universal consciousness Śiva. The school articulates a sophisticated idealist metaphysics — all reality is the self-luminous play (*spanda*, "vibration") of consciousness (*cit*, *saṃvit*) — that runs structurally parallel to Advaita Vedānta but diverges sharply on the reality-status of the phenomenal world (Muller-Ortega 1989).

## Lineage

- **Somānanda** (c. 875-925) — *Śivadṛṣṭi*. Founder. Argues against the Vedāntic *brahman* and Buddhist Vijñānavāda alternatives that consciousness (Śiva) is the sole reality and the world is its genuine self-expression.
- **Utpaladeva** (c. 925-975) — *Īśvarapratyabhijñā-kārikā* + *Vṛtti*. The school's canonical *kārikā* text. Articulates the recognition argument: that liberation is the recognition of an already-existing fact, not the acquisition of a new state.
- **Abhinavagupta** (c. 975-1025) — *Īśvarapratyabhijñā-vimarśinī* + *Vimarśinī-vimarśinī* (commentaries on Utpaladeva); *Tantrāloka* (the encyclopedic synthesis of tantric ritual, philosophy, aesthetics). The school's principal systematizer.
- **Kṣemarāja** (c. 1000-1075) — *Pratyabhijñāhṛdaya* ("Heart of Recognition", 20 sūtras as a contemplative compendium); *Spandanirṇaya*; *Śivasūtravimarśinī*.

## Key positions

- **Idealist monism**: all reality is consciousness (*cit*). There is no extra-mental matter; what appears as object is the self-projection (*ābhāsa*) of the universal subject.
- **Spanda** ("vibration", "pulsation"): the dynamic self-expressive nature of consciousness. Unlike the static *brahman* of strict Advaita, the Śaiva absolute is intrinsically active — *prakāśa* (luminosity) + *vimarśa* (reflexive self-awareness).
- **Recognition** (*pratyabhijñā*): liberation is not attainment of a new state but *recognition* of what one already is. The *jīva*'s bondage is forgetfulness; liberation is anamnesis.
- **The 36 *tattvas*** (categories of manifestation): a more elaborated cosmogonic scheme than the Sāṃkhya 25 — adding 11 supra-mental levels above Sāṃkhya's *puruṣa* up to *Paramaśiva* at the apex.

## Cross-tradition resonance

The recognition-as-anamnesis structure shows striking convergence with Platonic *anamnesis* (*Meno* 81-86; *Phaedo* 72-77) — knowledge is recovery of what the soul already possesses, not new acquisition. The Plotinian (see [[philosophy-neoplatonism]]) doctrine of the One's self-reflexive *theoria* parallels *spanda* and *vimarśa* — both describe an absolute that is dynamically self-aware without being divided. The 14th-c. Christian apophatic mystic Meister Eckhart's *ground of the soul* (*Seelengrund*) doctrine matches *pratyabhijñā* almost line-for-line: the divine ground is found by recognizing it, not by acquiring it.

Among Buddhist parallels, the Mahāyāna [[philosophy-yogacara|Yogācāra]] "consciousness-only" position is structurally close — though Pratyabhijñā rejects Vijñānavāda *māyā* (illusion) and *kṣaṇikatva* (momentariness) doctrines. The Buddha-nature ([[philosophy-yogacara]]) traditions and the Dzogchen *rigpa* (innate awareness) doctrine independently articulate similar recognition-of-pre-existing-awareness structures. Heinrich Zimmer and Mircea Eliade noted these convergences mid-20th c.; David Lawrence (1999) formalizes Pratyabhijñā transcendental arguments in dialogue with German idealism ([[philosophy-german-idealism]]).
