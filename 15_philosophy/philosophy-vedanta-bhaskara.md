# Bhedābheda Vedānta

The *bhedābheda* ("difference-and-non-difference") school of Vedānta — the principal alternative to Śaṅkara's [[philosophy-advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] *strict non-dualism* in classical Indian philosophy. Where Advaita holds that *Brahman* alone is real and the phenomenal world is *māyā* (appearance / lower-truth), Bhedābheda holds that the world and individual souls (*jīvas*) are *simultaneously identical with and distinct from Brahman* — both relations are real, not one being a lower-truth illusion to be sublated by the other (Sarma 2003).

## Bhāskara and his successors

Bhāskara (active c. 750-800) writes the earliest extant *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya* defending a *bhedābheda* reading explicitly against Śaṅkara, whose commentary preceded his by roughly a generation. Bhāskara accepts the Upaniṣadic *mahāvākyas* ("That thou art", *tat tvam asi*) but reads the identity as one between modes of a single substance, not as the strict non-dualism Śaṅkara articulates. The school's later articulations include:

- **Yādava-prakāśa** (11th c.) — Rāmānuja's first teacher; Rāmānuja eventually breaks with him to formulate [philosophy-vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Viśiṣṭādvaita] ("qualified non-dualism").
- **Nimbārka** (13th c.) — *svābhāvika-dvaitādvaita* ("natural dualism-non-dualism"). Founder of the Nimbārkī sampradāya devoted to Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa.
- **Caitanya tradition** (16th-17th c.) — Jīva Gosvāmin formulates *acintya-bhedābheda* ("inconceivable difference-and-non-difference"): the relation between Brahman and the world is logically inconceivable but ontologically real. This becomes the philosophical foundation of Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism and (through Bhaktivinoda Ṭhākura + Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī) of modern ISKCON.

## Cross-tradition resonance

The *bhedābheda* logical structure — predicating both identity and distinction of the relation between an absolute and its modes — closely parallels Christian Trinitarian-formula tensions (one substance, three persons; not modalism, not tritheism), Cappadocian *hypostasis*/*ousia* distinctions, and Latin Trinitarian *circumincession*. The 14th-c. Meister Eckhart formula "God's ground is my ground" reads as a *bhedābheda* claim — and the [[philosophy-apophatic-mysticism|apophatic mysticism]] line through Eckhart shows independent convergence on a similar logical structure.

Among Islamic traditions, Ibn ʿArabī's *waḥdat al-wujūd* ("unity of being") shows a structurally analogous problem — the relation between Necessary Being and contingent beings — though Ibn ʿArabī moves toward a more thoroughgoing monism than Bhāskara permits. The Sufi school of *waḥdat al-shuhūd* ("unity of witness", Aḥmad Sirhindī d. 1624) responding to Ibn ʿArabī parallels the Vedāntic Advaita/Bhedābheda divide. Process theology ([[philosophy-process-theology]]: Whitehead, Hartshorne) recovers structurally similar both-and predication: God includes the world (panentheism) without being identical with it.
