# Muʿtazila

The principal early rationalist school of Islamic theology, active from the mid-8th century and dominant in court Islam during the Abbasid caliphates of al-Maʾmūn, al-Muʿtaṣim, and al-Wāthiq (-813 to -847). The school was named for its founder Wāṣil ibn ʿAṭāʾ's reported withdrawal (*iʿtazala*, "to separate oneself") from the circle of Ḥasan al-Baṣrī over the question of the moral status of the gravely-sinning Muslim — but the name later became a label for a coherent philosophical-theological program.

## The five principles

The mature Muʿtazilī program rests on five principles (*al-uṣūl al-khamsa*):

- **At-Tawḥīd** — *divine unity*. God's attributes are not separate entities distinct from God; the Quran's expressions of God's "speech", "hand", "face" are figurative. The implication for the controversial doctrine of the Quran: the Quran cannot be co-eternal with God (since that would compromise God's uniqueness), so the Quran is *created* (*makhlūq*). This doctrine — the *khalq al-Qurʾān* — was the principal Muʿtazilī position enforced by the Abbasid *miḥna* (-833 to -848).
- **Al-ʿAdl** — *divine justice*. God acts in accord with rational standards of justice; God cannot will what is unjust. Human beings have genuine free will (a position against the contemporaneous *Jabriyya* predestinarians); humans are accountable for their actions; God's punishments and rewards are deserved.
- **Al-Waʿd wa'l-waʿīd** — *the promise and the threat*. Divine promises (paradise for the righteous) and threats (hell for the unjust) are fixed; God will fulfill them without arbitrary modification.
- **Al-Manzila bayn al-manzilatayn** — *the position between the two positions*. The grave-sinning Muslim is neither a believer nor an unbeliever but in an intermediate moral status — the position Wāṣil reportedly took that occasioned the founder's "separation" from al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī.
- **Al-Amr bi'l-maʿrūf wa'l-nahy ʿan al-munkar** — *commanding the right and forbidding the wrong*. The active social-political obligation of the Muslim community to maintain just public conduct.

## The kalām program

Muʿtazilī theological method (*kalām*) used systematic rational argument modeled on Greek philosophical methods to defend and elaborate Islamic doctrine. This is the foundational Islamic *kalām* tradition; the later Ashʿarī and Māturīdī schools developed in critical response to Muʿtazila but adopted its *method* even as they rejected key conclusions.

## The miḥna and the reversal

Under al-Maʾmūn (-833) the Abbasid state enforced the Muʿtazilī doctrine of the created Quran by the *miḥna* — a formal inquisition requiring scholars + judges to affirm the doctrine on pain of removal from office or imprisonment. Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal (-780 to -855), the founder of the Ḥanbalī legal school, was the most-famous resister; his imprisonment and refusal made him the symbol of the traditionalist opposition. Under al-Mutawakkil (-847) state policy reversed, the miḥna ended, and Muʿtazila lost its institutional position. The school continued for several more centuries — particularly under Buyid and Zaydī patronage — but never recovered Sunni dominance.
