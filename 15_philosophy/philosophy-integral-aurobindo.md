# Integral philosophy (Sri Aurobindo)

## Identity

Integral philosophy is the evolutionary-panentheist metaphysics developed by [[sri-aurobindo|Sri Aurobindo]] (1872–1950) at Pondicherry, set out chiefly in *[[phase-7-027-life-divine|The Life Divine]]* (serialized in his review *Arya*, 1914–1919; definitive edition 1939–1940) and its practical companion *The Synthesis of Yoga* (1914–1921). It is among the most systematically ambitious metaphysical systems of 20th-century Hindu modernism — often read as the philosophical culmination of the reform lineage running from [[ram-mohan-roy|Ram Mohan Roy]] through [[dayananda-saraswati]] and [[swami-vivekananda|Vivekananda]] — and the doctrinal core of Integral Yoga. Its governing claim is that the entire cosmos is a real self-manifestation of **Saccidānanda** (*sat-cit-ānanda*, Being-Consciousness-Bliss), and that matter is Spirit self-concealed, evolving stage by stage back toward a fully divinized life on earth.

## The system: involution, evolution, and the Supermind

The architecture of *The Life Divine* rests on a double movement.

- **Involution**: the Divine (Saccidānanda) descends and self-conceals, deliberately veiling its own consciousness until, at the nadir, it appears as apparently inconscient **Matter**. Creation is thus not an error or an illusion (*māyā* in Śaṅkara's sense) but a genuine self-limitation of the Absolute — the One really *becoming* the many.
- **Evolution**: from inconscient Matter, consciousness re-emerges by graded ascent — Matter → Life → Mind — each level the surfacing of a power that was involved from the beginning. This is a spiritual, not merely biological, evolution: the arc is driven from within by the Divine seeking to recover itself in its own creation.
- **The Supermind** (the *supramental* consciousness, also the "Truth-Consciousness" / *vijñāna*): the missing evolutionary term above Mind. Mind, for Aurobindo, is a divided, groping faculty that can seek truth but not possess it whole; the Supermind is a unitary knowledge-by-identity, the Real-Idea by which Saccidānanda organizes the manifest world. The next evolutionary step is the **descent of the Supermind** into earthly life — a supramental transformation that would divinize Mind, Life, and even the body, yielding the "life divine" of the title.

The reciprocal rhythm of **ascent and descent** is central: the aspirant's consciousness ascends toward the higher planes while the higher force descends to transform the lower nature. *The Synthesis of Yoga* is the practical statement of this integral method, which refuses to specialize into a single traditional yoga (of knowledge, works, or devotion) and instead seeks the transformation of the whole being. The epic poem *Savitri* renders the same metaphysics in symbol.

## Cross-tradition significance

Integral philosophy's principal comparative role is as a **modern reform of [[philosophy-advaita-vedanta|Advaita Vedānta]]** from within. Aurobindo keeps the Advaitic non-duality of Brahman and the identity of Atman with Brahman, but rejects Śaṅkara's **māyāvāda** — the doctrine that the world is an unreal superimposition to be cancelled by knowledge. For Aurobindo the world is real precisely *because* Brahman has really become it; he calls his position a **"Realistic Advaita."** This is a **transmission relationship**: Aurobindo read Śaṅkara directly and consciously overturned the illusionist reading, re-interpreting *māyā* as the Divine's creative power of self-formation rather than as error. Stephen Phillips (1986) is the standard analytic reconstruction of this move. His world-affirming stance places him structurally beside [[philosophy-vishishtadvaita-vedanta|Rāmānuja's Viśiṣṭādvaita]] — the other great Vedāntic corrective to Advaita — though Rāmānuja's system is devotional-theistic and static where Aurobindo's is evolutionary.

The more striking, and more easily overstated, parallel is with **Western evolutionary theism** — [[philosophy-process-theology|Whiteheadian process theology]] and Teilhard de Chardin's convergent Omega. All three replace a static, wholly-actual classical Absolute with a reality in real becoming, and all three are panentheist. The Atlas frames this as **convergence, not transmission**. Aurobindo's *Life Divine* (1914–19) is contemporaneous with, and independent of, Whitehead's *Process and Reality* (1929) and Teilhard's writings (composed 1920s–1950s, published posthumously): each grows from its own soil — Vedānta for Aurobindo, mathematical physics and post-Kantian metaphysics for Whitehead, Catholic theology and paleontology for Teilhard. Beatrice Bruteau's *Evolution Toward Divinity* (1974) is the book-length comparison of Aurobindo and Teilhard, and treats the resemblance as convergence. The systems also **diverge sharply**: Aurobindo posits a determinate supra-rational goal-state (the Supermind) toward which evolution is teleologically drawn and which is destined to transform matter itself, whereas Whiteheadian becoming is open-ended and God acts only by persuasive lure. One should not read the shared late-modern vocabulary of "evolution" as evidence of contact.

There is a genuine **contemporaneity worth naming carefully**: Aurobindo was Cambridge-educated (King's College) and demonstrably read Western philosophy, so his evolutionism is not sealed off from the 19th-century European evolutionary climate (Darwin, Spencer, Hegel, Bergson). But the specific machinery — involution/evolution of Saccidānanda, the Supermind as the Truth-Consciousness of the Veda — is drawn from Vedic and Upaniṣadic sources, not borrowed from Whitehead or Teilhard, whose systems he did not use as sources.

## Disputes

- **How far from Advaita?** Whether Aurobindo's "Realistic Advaita" is a legitimate development *of* Vedānta or a substantially new system trading on Vedāntic prestige is debated. Phillips (1986) reads it as a philosophically serious but distinct metaphysics; more traditional Advaitins regard the rejection of māyāvāda as abandoning Śaṅkara's core.
- **Is the Supermind a philosophical concept or a report of experience?** Aurobindo presents the supramental planes as directly known in yogic experience (his own and the Mother's), not merely deduced. Critics question whether a claim grounded in private realization can do the argumentative work *The Life Divine* asks of it; defenders (McDermott 1987) treat the experiential basis as integral to the system's method rather than a defect.
- **The process/Teilhard parallel.** Because the resemblance to Whitehead and Teilhard is so clean, some popular and "integral-spirituality" literature (notably Ken Wilber's Integral Theory) treats the three as a single convergent movement or even a lineage. The scholarly caution is that the parallel is typological: independent evolutionary panentheisms, not a transmission chain. Framing them as one tradition flattens the real differences (teleological Supermind vs. open-ended process; earthly divinization vs. eschatological Omega vs. persuasive God).
