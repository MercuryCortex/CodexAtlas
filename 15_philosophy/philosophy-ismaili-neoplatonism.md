# Ismāʿīlī (Fatimid) Neoplatonism

## Identity

Ismāʿīlī Neoplatonism is the philosophical theology developed within the Ismāʿīlī Shīʿī daʿwa (mission) between roughly the early 10th and late 11th centuries, in which the emanationist metaphysics of the Arabic Plotinus and Proclus was fused with the Ismaili doctrines of the imamate, cyclical prophecy, and esoteric exegesis (*taʾwīl*). It is the sectarian, gnostic branch of the Arabic Neoplatonic reception — sibling to the demonstrative *falsafa* line of [[philosophy-neoplatonism-islamic|al-Fārābī and Ibn Sīnā]], and drawing on the same source texts (the *Theology of Aristotle*, the *Liber de Causis*), but wedded to a sacred history and a living hierarchy of teachers.

The founding figures are the Persian dāʿīs of the "Iranian school": **Muḥammad al-Nasafī** (d. 943), who first introduced Neoplatonic cosmology into Ismaili doctrine; **Abū Ḥātim al-Rāzī** (d. 934); and above all **Abū Yaʿqūb al-Sijistānī** (d. c. 971), whose *Kitāb al-Yanābīʿ* ("Wellsprings") and *Kashf al-maḥjūb* ("Unveiling of the Hidden") give the classical system. The encyclopedic **Rasāʾil Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ** ([[phase-5-011-rasail-ikhwan-al-safa|Epistles of the Brethren of Purity]], Basra, c. 950–980) belong to the same intellectual world. The system's most rigorous philosophical form is the *Rāḥat al-ʿaql* ("Repose of the Intellect") of **[[al-kirmani|Ḥamīd al-Dīn al-Kirmānī]]** (fl. 996–1021), the leading Fatimid dāʿī under the caliph al-Ḥākim; the Persian dāʿī **Nāṣir-i Khusraw** (1004–after 1070) carried the school east into Khurāsān and Badakhshān in his *Jāmiʿ al-ḥikmatayn* ("Reconciliation of the Two Wisdoms").

## The cosmology: origination, then emanation

The Ismaili thinkers face a problem the pagan Neoplatonists did not: how to hold an emanationist cosmos together with Qurʾānic *tawḥīd* (strict divine unity) and creation by Command. Their solution is a two-tier structure that is the school's signature:

1. **God (al-Mubdiʿ) is beyond being.** For al-Sijistānī, God is reached only by **double negation**: He is neither existent nor non-existent, neither described nor undescribed — and each negation is itself negated, lest the negation smuggle Him back into a category. Walker (1993) reads this double negation as a sharper apophaticism than Plotinus's One-beyond-being (see [[hidden-god]], [[philosophy-apophatic-mysticism]]).

2. **Ibdāʿ, not emanation, posits the First Intellect.** God does *not* emanate — for the world to overflow from God would make Him the source-genus of being and compromise His transcendence. Instead, by **ibdāʿ** (atemporal origination from nothing, the act of the divine Command / *amr*, the Qurʾānic *Kun*, "Be!") He originates the **First / Universal Intellect** (*al-ʿaql al-awwal*), the first originated being (*al-mubdaʿ al-awwal*) — complete, at rest, the fullness of being.

3. **Emanation begins below the Intellect.** From the Intellect proceeds, by emanation (*inbiʿāth*), the **Universal Soul** (*al-nafs al-kulliyya*), which is in motion and deficient, longing back toward the Intellect; and from Soul unfold Prime Matter, Nature, the Spheres, the elements, and the compound bodies of the sensible world. Human salvation is the *return* (return of the particular soul, through knowledge, to the Universal Soul and Intellect).

Al-Sijistānī's classical system is a **dyad** — Intellect and Soul as the two spiritual roots (*al-aṣlān*). Al-Kirmānī later replaced this with a **ten-intellect** scheme borrowed from [[philosophy-neoplatonism-islamic|al-Fārābī]], harmonizing the Ismaili cosmos with the falsafa cosmos; in the later Ṭayyibī development the tenth intellect becomes the "spiritual Adam" of a cosmic fall-and-recovery drama.

## Cyclical prophetology and taʾwīl

The cosmology is doubled by a philosophy of sacred history. Time unfolds in **seven great cycles** (*adwār*), each opened by a **nāṭiq** (speaker-prophet who brings a revealed Law): Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus, Muḥammad, and the seventh, the **Qāʾim** (the Resurrector / Mahdi), who abrogates the Law and discloses the pure inner reality. Each nāṭiq is completed by an *asās* / *waṣī* (foundation-legatee — ʿAlī for Muḥammad) and a series of imams who preserve the inner meaning.

The hinge between cosmos and history is **taʾwīl**: esoteric hermeneutics that lifts the *bāṭin* (hidden inner sense) out from beneath the *ẓāhir* (outward letter) of scripture, law, and nature. The prophet delivers the *ẓāhir*; the imam and the daʿwa hierarchy transmit the *bāṭin*. Knowledge (*ʿilm*, *ʿirfān*) received through this living chain is the vehicle of the soul's return — making the Ismaili system a **gnosis** (see [[gnosis-as-salvation]]) as much as a metaphysics, and tying its doctrine of the concealed and awaited restorer to the wider Shīʿī [[occultation-hidden-imam|occultation]] complex.

## Cross-tradition significance

The connection to Greek Neoplatonism is **transmission, not convergence, and it is textually secure**: the Intellect–Soul procession and the One-beyond-being reach the Ismaili dāʿīs through the *Theology of Aristotle* (the Arabic Plotinus, *Enneads* IV–VI under Aristotle's name) and the *Liber de Causis* / *Kalām fī maḥḍ al-khayr* (an Arabic abridgement of Proclus's *Elements of Theology*). What is original is not the emanation scheme but the **subordination of emanation to ibdāʿ** — the interposition of a creating Command between God and the first emanated being — which lets an emanationist metaphysics live inside a creationist monotheism. This is the distinctively Ismaili contribution to Neoplatonism (De Smet 1995; Walker 1993).

Outward, the school's afterlife runs in two directions. Doctrinally it feeds later Persian and Andalusian thought: the Rasāʾil influence Ibn Masarra and the Andalusian tradition, and the illuminationist ([[phase-5-018-suhrawardi-hikmat-al-ishraq|ishrāqī]]) and Akbarian ([[phase-5-021-ibn-arabi-fusus-al-hikam|Ibn ʿArabī]]) currents inherit the graded cosmos of Intellect and Soul. Structurally, al-Sijistānī's **double-negation** theology stands alongside the [[the-one-plotinus|Plotinian One]], the Kabbalistic **Ein Sof**, and Eckhart's **Gottheit** as one of the medieval world's most extreme negative theologies — a family of "God-beyond-being" positions that arise wherever a strict monotheism absorbs the Neoplatonic One. That resemblance is **typological**; the Ismaili instance's actual lineage is the specific Arabic Neoplatonic corpus, so it should be filed as a documented branch of that transmission, not as an independent convergence.

## Disputes

- **Is the Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ corpus Ismaili?** The affiliation of the Rasāʾil is the field's long-standing quarrel. Marquet (1975) and Daftary (2007) argue for a strong link to the early Ismaili daʿwa; Netton (1991) and earlier scholarship (Stern, Tibawi) are more cautious, placing the Brethren in a broader Shīʿī-leaning Basran rationalist milieu. The Atlas treats the Rasāʾil as adjacent to, not simply identical with, the Fatimid daʿwa's doctrine.
- **Dyad versus decad.** Al-Sijistānī's two-root (Intellect–Soul) cosmology and al-Kirmānī's ten-intellect cosmology are genuinely different systems. Some read the shift as a "philosophization" of Ismaili doctrine toward *falsafa*; others as an internal correction. It is a real doctrinal fault-line within the school, not a scholarly artefact.
- **Does ibdāʿ actually escape emanationism?** Whether interposing a creating Command between God and the First Intellect genuinely secures God's transcendence — or merely renames the first moment of an emanationist chain — is debated (De Smet 1995). The problem is the Ismaili form of the general tension between Neoplatonic procession and Abrahamic creation *ex nihilo* that also drove al-Ghazālī's attack on the *falāsifa*.
