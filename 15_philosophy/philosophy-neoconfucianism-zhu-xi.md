# Zhu Xi Neo-Confucianism

The systematic Song-dynasty (Southern Song, 1127-1279) reconstruction of the Confucian tradition (*lixue*, "study of principle"), synthesizing the 11th-c. Northern Song revival figures — Zhou Dunyi (1017-1073), Zhang Zai (1020-1077), Cheng Hao (1032-1085), Cheng Yi (1033-1107) — into a unified metaphysical-ethical-pedagogical program by Zhu Xi (1130-1200). The program centers on the metaphysics of *li* (理, principle / pattern) and *qi* (氣, vital energy / matter-stuff): every existing thing is constituted by both — *li* is the pattern that makes it the kind of thing it is; *qi* is the material substrate that individuates it. The school dominates East Asian intellectual life from the 14th to the 19th c. and is the principal target of the 20th-c. May Fourth-era critiques of "tradition" (Bol 2008).

## Key positions

- **Li and qi**: every existing thing is constituted by an inseparable union of *li* (principle, eternal pattern, what makes a thing the kind it is) and *qi* (matter-energy substrate that individuates instances). *Li* is one in the cosmic *taiji* (Supreme Ultimate); *qi* differentiates and embodies it. The classical analogy: the same *li* of "tree" exists in every tree, but *qi* makes this oak different from that oak.
- **The Great Ultimate** (*taiji*, 太極): the ontological summit of *li* — the principle of all principles. Zhou Dunyi's *Taiji-tu shuo* ("Explanation of the Diagram of the Supreme Ultimate") provides the cosmogonic schema; Zhu Xi makes it canonical.
- **The investigation of things** (*gewu*, 格物): the program of self-cultivation through gradual study and reflection on the principles inherent in the natural and moral order. The locus classicus is the *Great Learning* eight steps (investigate things, extend knowledge, sincere intentions, rectify heart, cultivate self, regulate family, govern state, bring peace to all under heaven).
- **The Four Books canon**: Zhu Xi establishes the curriculum that becomes the Chinese civil-service examination canon from 1313 to 1905 — *Analects*, *Mencius*, *Great Learning*, *Doctrine of the Mean* — with his commentaries as the official interpretation.
- **Original nature and physical nature**: every person possesses the "original nature" (*benran zhi xing*, identical with *li*) of perfect goodness, obscured to varying degrees by "physical nature" (*qizhi zhi xing*, conditioned by individuating *qi*). Self-cultivation polishes the qi to reveal the original goodness.

## Historical impact

Zhu Xi's commentaries on the Four Books became the Yuan-dynasty civil-service examination canon (1313) and remained so through the Ming and Qing, structuring six centuries of Chinese elite intellectual formation. The Korean Joseon dynasty (1392-1897) adopted Cheng-Zhu *lixue* as state orthodoxy under Yi Hwang (T'oegye, 1501-1570) and Yi I (Yulgok, 1536-1584). The Tokugawa shogunate (1603-1868) endorsed it through Fujiwara Seika and Hayashi Razan. Vietnamese Lê dynasty Confucianism likewise. The geographic-temporal reach of Cheng-Zhu Neo-Confucianism — from 13th-c. Hangzhou through to 19th-c. Edo and Hanoi — is one of the largest cultural-philosophical hegemonies in world history.

## Cross-tradition resonance

The *li*/*qi* polarity shows striking structural parallel to Aristotle's form/matter hylomorphism — *li* as the formal cause (what makes the thing what it is), *qi* as the material cause (the substrate that individuates). The convergence has been noted since the Jesuit-era encounters (Matteo Ricci, *Tianzhu shiyi* 1603) and is the subject of substantial comparative-philosophy literature (Mou Zongsan, Tu Weiming, Roger Ames). The Zhu Xi metaphysical synthesis runs parallel to the contemporaneous Latin scholastic synthesis (see [[philosophy-scholasticism]]): both 12th-13th-c. constructions of comprehensive metaphysical-ethical-pedagogical systems built on a recovered ancient canon (Aristotle for the Latins, the Five Classics + Four Books for Zhu Xi).

The *gewu* (investigation of things) program shares structural commitments with Aristotelian inquiry-into-nature and (more distantly) with the 17th-c. early modern scientific program; some Chinese scholars (Joseph Needham *Science and Civilisation in China*) trace the late-Ming "evidential learning" (*kaozheng*) movement to a *gewu* lineage. The Cheng-Zhu doctrine of the unity of *li* and *qi* in created things prefigures certain 17th-c. European debates on substantial forms. The 15th-c. challenge from [philosophy-neoconfucianism-wang-yangming|Wang Yangming] (1472-1529) parallels the late-medieval challenge to Aristotelian-scholastic synthesis from voluntarism and nominalism.
