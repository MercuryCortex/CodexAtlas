# Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta

The "qualified non-dualism" school of Vedānta founded by Rāmānuja (traditionally 1017-1137), the second of the three principal classical Vedāntic positions alongside Śaṅkara's [[philosophy-advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] and Madhva's *Dvaita*. The school's distinguishing thesis: reality is non-dual *in the qualified sense* — Brahman alone is real and includes the world and individual souls (*jīvas*) as its inseparable *modes* (*prakāra*), in the metaphysical relation of *body* (*śarīra*) to *embodied-self* (*śarīrin*). Where Advaita holds the world is *māyā* (lower-truth appearance) and the individual *jīvas* are non-different from Brahman in the highest truth, Viśiṣṭādvaita holds that the world and the souls are *real* — eternally distinct from but ontologically dependent on Brahman as a body is dependent on its embodied self (Carman 1974).

## Core doctrines

- **Brahman as personal**: Rāmānuja's Brahman is the Tamil Vaiṣṇava Lord — Nārāyaṇa with Śrī (Lakṣmī) as consort. Personal attributes (omniscience, mercy, accessibility) are not negated as Śaṅkara negates them; they are positive features of the highest reality.
- **The body-soul analogy** (*śarīra-śarīrin-bhāva*): the world and the souls are Brahman's *body*; Brahman is their *embodied-self*. The relation is asymmetric: Brahman pervades and sustains all; the world and souls do not pervade or sustain Brahman.
- **Real plurality without strict difference**: the souls are eternally distinct numerically — they are not Brahman in the strict Advaitic sense — but they are not metaphysically independent of Brahman.
- **Prapatti** (loving self-surrender) as the principal soteriological path: Rāmānuja and the Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition emphasize *bhakti* and *prapatti* (total self-surrender to the Lord's grace) over the *jñāna* (knowledge-yoga) emphasis of Advaita.
- **Apaurusheyatva of the Veda**: Rāmānuja inherits the [[philosophy-mimamsa-purva-ritual-metaphysics|Pūrva Mīmāṃsā]] doctrine of the eternity and authorlessness of the Veda but reinterprets it within a theistic framework — the Veda is eternal but rests on Brahman as its eternal ground.

## Cross-tradition resonance

The body-soul analogy as the structuring metaphor of the Brahman-world relation finds remarkable cross-tradition parallels. The Stoic doctrine of the cosmic *pneuma* as the rational fire that pervades and orders the world while remaining distinct from its parts is structurally analogous. The Christian *imago Dei* and Pauline *body of Christ* ecclesiology — the church as the *body of Christ* with Christ as the *head* — runs parallel in the relation of part-whole-dependence. The Sufi *waḥdat al-wujūd* ("unity of being") of Ibn ʿArabī articulates a similar both-personal-and-monistic position; the structural cousin in Sufi tradition has been recognized since the colonial-era comparative work of Toshihiko Izutsu.

The *prapatti* doctrine — total self-surrender to divine grace as the principal soteriological mode — runs structurally parallel to Lutheran *sola gratia* and to the Pure Land Buddhist (Jōdo Shū, Jōdo Shinshū) *tariki* ("other-power") position. The Tamil Śrīvaiṣṇava school's 13th-c. split into Vaḍakalai (combining grace with the soul's small "cat" effort — kitten-clinging-to-mother analogy) and Teṅkalai (total surrender; cat-mother-carrying-kitten analogy) replicates with striking precision the analogous Christian dispute between Catholic *cooperation with grace* and Calvinist *monergism*; the parallel is one of the standard cases in comparative-theology literature (Carman 1974).

The school's doctrine of the [[philosophy-vedanta-bhaskara|Bhedābheda]]-adjacent both-identity-and-distinction handling of the Brahman-world relation runs alongside the Christian Trinitarian formula's both-substantial-unity-and-personal-distinction articulation. Whether the parallels reflect structural inevitability or trace some historical contact (the Tamil coast had Syriac Christian + Jewish + Muslim contacts continuously from late antiquity) is debated.
