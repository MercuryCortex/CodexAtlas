# Sūrat Hūd (Quran 11 — Hūd)

## What it is
The densest of the Quran's "punishment-story" suras: one after another, prophets are sent to their peoples, rejected, and the peoples destroyed — Noah and the Flood, the Arabian prophet Hūd to the people of ʿĀd, Ṣāliḥ to Thamūd, Abraham's heavenly guests and the overthrow of Lot's city, Shuʿayb to Midian, and Moses to Pharaoh. Muhammad is reported to have said, "Sūra Hūd and its sisters have turned my hair grey," so heavy is its theme of communities annihilated for unbelief. Two details are theologically decisive: Noah's own son drowns because he is "not of your family — his conduct was unrighteous" (11:46), and the Ark comes to rest not on Ararat but on Mount al-Jūdī (11:44).

## Primary text

### Quran 11:44
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> Then it was said, "Earth, swallow up your water, and sky, hold back," and the water subsided, the command was fulfilled. The Ark settled on Mount Jūdī, and it was said, "Gone are those evildoing people!"

### Quran 11:46
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> God said, "Noah, he was not one of your family. What he did was not right. Do not ask Me about things you know nothing about. I am warning you not to be foolish."

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Meccan; the sura presses a single message — every prior rejection of a prophet ended in ruin, and Mecca is being warned.
- **Tradition.** Hūd and Ṣāliḥ are *Arabian* prophets with no biblical counterpart; the Quran sets a parallel sacred history of Arabia alongside the line of Israel.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The Ark's landing on al-Jūdī (in the Jazīra, near Mosul) became a fixed point of Islamic sacred geography.

## Themes and motifs
- The cycle of the destroyed nations — prophet, rejection, judgment, saved remnant.
- Faith over lineage — Noah's drowned son severs salvation from blood.
- The Arabian prophets — ʿĀd and Thamūd as a homegrown sacred past.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Three traditions, three mountains, one Flood — and an Arabian sacred history.** Sūra Hūd makes two moves that map the seams between scriptures. First, the Ark lands on **al-Jūdī** (11:44), not on Ararat (Genesis 8:4) and not on Mount Niṣir/Nimush (Gilgamesh XI 140–144): the same deluge, anchored to three different peaks by three different peoples, each claiming the geography of salvation for its own land. Second, alongside Noah and Abraham the sura places **Hūd** and **Ṣāliḥ**, prophets sent to ʿĀd and Thamūd — peoples with no place in the Bible but with real footing in Arabian memory (Thamūd is named in Assyrian royal annals and by classical geographers; Ṣāliḥ's she-camel and the rock-cut tombs of Madāʾin Ṣāliḥ anchor it in the Hijaz). The Quran thus asserts that God's warners reached Arabia directly, not only through Israel — and the drowning of Noah's unbelieving son (11:46) drives home the corollary the destroyed nations all ignored: kinship to a prophet saves no one; only submission does (Reynolds 2010 pp.59–70; Tottoli 2002 pp.46–58; Nasr 2015 pp.578–599).
