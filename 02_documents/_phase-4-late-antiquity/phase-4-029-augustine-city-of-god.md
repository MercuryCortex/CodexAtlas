# City of God

## What it is
A 22-book treatise written between 413 and 426 in response to the sack of Rome by Alaric's Visigoths in 410 — a catastrophe pagans blamed on the abandonment of the traditional gods. Books 1–10 attack pagan religion and the philosophical religions (including a long engagement with the Hermetic *Asclepius* and with Neoplatonism). Books 11–22 expound the doctrine of the *two cities* — the City of God (those loving God to the contempt of self) and the earthly city (those loving self to the contempt of God) — intertwined through history but separated at the Last Judgment. The work is the founding text of Christian political theology.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late Western Roman Empire in collapse; Alaric's 410 sack of Rome was a symbolic blow.
- **Religious milieu.** Augustine engages every major intellectual rival: Roman traditional religion (via Varro), Neoplatonism (Plotinus and Porphyry), Hermetism (the *Asclepius* lament passage), Manichaeism (briefly), Donatism, and Pelagianism.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-kingship]] — God as cosmic king; the earthly polity desacralized.
- [[end-times-judgment]] — eschatological separation of the two cities.
- [[chosen-people]] — the City of God as the true people of God across history.
