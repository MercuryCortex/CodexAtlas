# Ethiopian Sinodos (the Ethiopian Synodicon)

## What it is
The **Ethiopian Sinodos** (Geʼez *Sənodos*; sometimes called the *Ethiopian Synodicon* or *Ethiopian Apostolic Constitutions*) is the comprehensive apostolic-pseudonymous church-order corpus of [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]] — held as canonical-NT-adjacent scripture in the Ethiopian broader biblical canon. The text is a multi-layered compilation deriving from the Greek **Apostolic Constitutions** (~380 CE, Syrian-Antiochene Christian), the **Apostolic Canons** (the canonical-legal appendix of the Apostolic Constitutions), the **Apostolic Tradition of Hippolytus** (~215 CE Roman), and the **Didascalia Apostolorum** (~3rd c. Syriac), all reshaped by Ethiopian monastic redactors with additional Geʼez-redactional material.

The four principal sections of the Geʼez Sinodos:

1. **The Octateuch-Sinodos** — eight books of apostolic-pseudonymous teaching on the eight orders of the church.
2. **The Apostolic Canons** — the canonical-legal canons traditionally appended to the Apostolic Constitutions.
3. **The Apostolic Constitutions Book 8** material — liturgical and ordination regulations.
4. **The Statutes of the Apostles** — additional apostolic-pseudonymous regulations.

The four sections are sometimes printed separately and sometimes treated as a single corpus; canonical status varies slightly across recensions.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Aksumite-medieval Ethiopian church-state formation. The Sinodos as a canonical corpus stabilizes during the Solomonic period (1270 onward), when many distinctively Ethiopian canonical-and-legal texts crystallize.
- **Religious milieu.** Ethiopian Tewahedo's broader canon retains as scripture multiple texts the Greek and Latin Christian world held as *patristic-authoritative* but not *canonical-scriptural*. The Sinodos is one of the most consequential of these.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous-pseudonymous; the framing attributes the regulations to the apostolic college collectively. The historical compositional process spans 3rd–7th c. Greek-Syrian Christianity for the substrate texts, plus Ethiopian monastic redaction.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Ethiopian monastic-canonical use. Western scholarly recovery via Bausi's 1995 critical edition.

## Themes and motifs
- [[ecclesial-authority]] — detailed regulations of ordination, episcopal authority, liturgical discipline.
- [[ethiopic-canon-broad]] — exemplary case of the broader-Ethiopian canon: a substantial church-order corpus retained as scripture.
- [[liturgical-calendar]] — sacramental and ritual norms.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Greek Apostolic Constitutions (~380 CE) — *direct-translation* / *redaction-of*: the Greek text is the principal substrate for the Ethiopian Sinodos.
- → [[phase-4-061-didache]] — *parallel-form*, *ancestor-of*: the Didache is the earliest surviving text of the church-order genre.
- → [[phase-4-083-mashafa-kidan-testament-of-our-lord]] — *closely-related*: both are Ethiopian-canonical apostolic-pseudonymous church-order texts; sometimes grouped together in monastic manuscript transmission.
- → [[phase-5-037-fetha-nagast]] — *commentary-on* / *redaction-of*: the Solomonic-era Ethiopian legal code Fetha Nagast draws ecclesiastical-legal sections from the Sinodos.

## Disputes
- **Composition and recension history.** The Greek-to-Arabic-to-Ge'ez transmission chain is multi-staged and incompletely reconstructed; Bausi's critical-edition work continues.
- **Canonical status.** Ethiopian Tewahedo treats it as canonical-NT-adjacent; precise canonical-scriptural status varies across Ethiopian-monastic recensions.
