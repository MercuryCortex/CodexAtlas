# On the Holy Spirit

## What it is
Basil of Caesarea's 30-chapter Greek treatise defending the full divinity of the Holy Spirit, composed in 374–375 against the *Pneumatomachoi* ("Spirit-fighters") — those who, accepting Nicene divinity of the Son, balked at extending the same status to the Spirit. Addressed to Amphilochius of Iconium. The principal theological text behind the Niceno-Constantinopolitan Creed's expanded Spirit-clauses promulgated at Constantinople 381.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Basil is bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia from 370; the eastern empire under Valens (364–378) is Homoian-Arian-leaning; Basil and the other Cappadocians lead the Nicene resistance. The Spirit's divinity has become the chief unresolved theological question of the 370s.
- **Religious milieu.** The Eunomian / Anomoean radicals and the Pneumatomachoi (led by Eustathius of Sebaste, Basil's onetime ally) are the principal targets. The work also engages Greek philosophical vocabulary.
- **Who was behind it.** Basil personally; the work emerged out of liturgical controversy at Caesarea (Basil's use of the doxology "Glory to the Father *with* the Son *with* the Holy Spirit" was attacked as innovating; his treatise defends it).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Greek transmission.

## Themes and motifs
- [[trinitarian-formula]] — the foundational text of patristic pneumatology. Basil argues from baptismal formula, from doxological practice, from scriptural usage, and from the unity of divine operation that the Spirit shares the divine *ousia*.
- The work introduces and defends the distinction between *ousia* (essence, shared by the three) and *hypostasis* (the three subsisting modes) that becomes the Cappadocian and then catholic-orthodox Trinitarian framework.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[athanasius]] *Letter to Serapion* — *direct-precursor*: Athanasius had already extended the Nicene homoousios to the Spirit (~360); Basil systematizes the argument.
- → [[event-council-of-constantinople-381]] — *direct-influence*: the council's Spirit-clauses ("the Lord, the Giver of Life, who proceeds from the Father, who together with the Father and the Son is worshipped and glorified") encode Basilian theology.
- → [[gregory-of-nazianzus]] *Theological Orations* 5 (Or. 31) — *parallel*: Gregory's slightly later treatment of the Spirit is more philosophically adventurous than Basil's.
- → [[gregory-of-nyssa]] *Against Eunomius* — *parallel*.
- → Stoic *pneuma* doctrine — *substrate-counterpoint*: Greek philosophical *pneuma* / cosmic spirit is the lexical-conceptual substrate against which Christian *pneumatology* defines itself. Basil works the Greek philosophical vocabulary toward Christian Trinitarian conclusions; the Stoic-Greek substrate of the term is structurally unavoidable.
- → Origen's Trinitarian theology — *substrate-influence*: Basil's Trinitarian framework develops the Origenist *three hypostases* schema, modified to remove subordinationism.

## Disputes
- **The "economic vs. eternal" Trinitarian distinction.** Whether Basil's pneumatology is finally Nicene-coordinate or implicitly subordinationist has been debated (older Catholic critics); the catholic-orthodox tradition reads him as fully coordinate, and Constantinople 381 ratified this reading.
- **Authenticity of certain late chapters.** A few sections (esp. the doxological-history material in chs. 27–29) have occasionally been questioned; mainstream accepts them.
