# Against Heresies

## What it is
A five-book treatise by Irenaeus of Lyon, the most influential anti-Gnostic work of the second century. Book 1 catalogues Gnostic systems in detail — the Valentinians, the Marcionites, the Carpocratians, the BarBeloites (whose myth we now recognize as the Apocryphon of John), the Ophites — providing our richest external evidence for these movements. Books 2–5 refute them by philosophical argument, by appeal to apostolic tradition (the bishops in unbroken succession), and by appeal to a four-fold Gospel canon (Matthew, Mark, Luke, John — the first known statement of the four-Gospel canon as exclusive). Irenaeus' theology of "recapitulation" (Christ recapitulates Adam's history correctly) and his appeal to the rule of faith become foundational for proto-orthodox Christianity.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-second-century Roman Gaul. Irenaeus succeeded the martyr Pothinus as bishop of Lugdunum after the 177 persecution; he wrote *Against Heresies* a few years later, while Marcus Aurelius and Commodus reigned.
- **Religious milieu.** The full diversity of second-century Christianity is on display — Valentinian, Marcionite, Sethian, Carpocratian, Ophite, plus the proto-orthodox networks Irenaeus represents. Irenaeus' work is a major step in *constructing* what would become orthodoxy: by naming, listing, and rejecting alternatives.
- **Who was behind it.** Irenaeus, bishop of Lyon, a Greek from Asia Minor (born in Smyrna or thereabouts ~130) who heard Polycarp, who claimed to have heard the apostle John. The chain "John → Polycarp → Irenaeus" was used by Irenaeus to anchor apostolic tradition.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Latin transmission; Erasmus produced the editio princeps in 1526. The Armenian and Syriac fragments became known in the 19th century. Nag Hammadi (1945) confirmed Irenaeus' detail.

## Themes and motifs
- [[demiurge]] — Irenaeus is our richest source for the Gnostic demiurge concept; he repeats and refutes it.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — explicitly framed (the work's full title is *Refutation and Overthrow of Falsely So-Called Gnosis*).
- [[divine-feminine]] — Irenaeus reports the Valentinian and Sethian Sophia mythologies.

## Disputes
- **How fairly Irenaeus represents Gnostic systems.** Williams 1996, King 2003 have argued he homogenizes diverse movements under tendentious labels. But the Nag Hammadi finds substantially confirmed Irenaeus' specific factual claims, even where they don't share his evaluation.
- **The four-Gospel canon.** Whether Irenaeus is *establishing* or *witnessing* the four-Gospel canon (3.11.8); current consensus: witnessing an emerging proto-orthodox consensus and consolidating it polemically.
