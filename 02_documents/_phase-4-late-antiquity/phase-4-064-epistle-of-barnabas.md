# Epistle of Barnabas

## What it is
A 21-chapter Greek tractate-letter, pseudonymously attributed to the apostolic-era [[barnabas|Barnabas]] but actually composed in Alexandria ~+120/+132. Two main parts: (1) chapters 1–17 — an **allegorical-supersessionist exegesis** of the Hebrew Bible arguing that the Mosaic Law was *always* meant typologically and that the Jews fundamentally misunderstood their own scriptures (which Christians now correctly read), and (2) chapters 18–21 — the *Two Ways* moral catechesis, parallel to but framed differently from the [[phase-4-061-didache|Didache]]'s.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Alexandria in Hadrian's reign (117–138). Catastrophic Jewish-Alexandrian relations following the **Diaspora Revolt of +115/+117** (the *Trajanic War* — devastated Egyptian and Cyrenaican Jewish communities and ruptured Alexandrian Jewish-Christian-pagan relations), and on the eve of (or in immediate aftermath of) the **Bar Kokhba Revolt** (+132/+135). Barn. 16.4's reference to the Temple's potential rebuilding is the principal dating peg — most read it as anticipating a feared Hadrianic rebuilding before Bar Kokhba's defeat made the question moot.
- **Religious milieu.** Alexandrian Christianity is the heir of Alexandrian Jewish allegorical exegesis (Philonic). The break between (still substantially Jewish-Christian) Alexandrian Christianity and the rabbinic Jewish community is sharpening; Barnabas's polemic is part of that severance.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous Alexandrian Christian writer — possibly a teacher in the *didaskaleion* milieu that within decades produced Pantaenus and Clement of Alexandria.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Considered scriptural by Alexandrians (Clement of Alexandria quotes Barnabas as "the apostle Barnabas," with full canonical status; Origen knows and uses it). Included in Codex Sinaiticus. Excluded from the formed Western canon but never lost in the Eastern transmission stream.

## Themes and motifs
- [[allegorical-exegesis]] — load-bearing. Barnabas is the **earliest extended Christian allegorical-exegetical document**. The dietary laws, sabbath, circumcision, and Temple are all read as misunderstood typological prefigurations whose true (Christian) meanings the Jews missed. The hermeneutical method is recognisably **Philonic Alexandrian Jewish allegorical exegesis turned against Jews** — a hermeneutical inheritance from [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria|Philo]] weaponized against Philo's own community.
- **Christian supersessionism** — load-bearing. Barnabas advances the **strongest extant 2nd-c. claim that the Sinai covenant *never belonged to* the Jews** — they "lost it" when Moses smashed the first tablets (Barn. 4.6–8; 14.1–4). This is more radical than Justin and most subsequent *Adversus Judaeos* writers, who concede Israel *had* the covenant and then lost it. Barnabas claims Israel *never had it*.
- [[ethical-dualism]] / [[two-spirits-doctrine]] — chapters 18–21 present the Two Ways using a **two-angels** framing (the angel of light at God's side; the angel of darkness ruling the present age) — closer to **1QS 3:13–4:26** (Qumran's *Treatise of the Two Spirits*, with the Prince of Lights and Angel of Darkness) than the [[phase-4-061-didache|Didache]]'s plain *two-ways* schema. Critical substrate wire: Barnabas preserves the *angelic-cosmic* framing of the Jewish-derived Two Ways tradition more fully than the Didache.
- [[fulfillment-of-prophecy]] — heavy use of OT *testimonia* (combination texts) to prove that Christ's life and death were already-revealed in Jewish scripture — a foundational document for the *testimonia* hermeneutic that runs through Justin Martyr to the medievals.
- **Number symbolism**: famously the 318 servants of Abraham (Gen 14:14) interpreted as IH (= 18 = Jesus) + T (= 300 = the cross) — chapter 9.8. The earliest extant Christian *gematria*.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-061-didache]] — *parallel-recension*: the Two Ways material of Barn. 18–20 and Did. 1–6 derive from a **common Jewish-Christian source** (consensus: Niederwimmer 1998, van de Sandt & Flusser 2002, Kraft 1965). The shared substrate is Jewish; the Christian versions diverge.
- → [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]] — *direct-substrate* / *redirected-influence*: Barnabas's allegorical method *is* Philonic — the same hermeneutic Philo used to make Mosaic Torah respectable to Hellenistic readers is here used to make Mosaic Torah *belong to Christians, not Jews*. The Alexandrian allegorical tradition is the precondition.
- → [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — *parallel-motif* (two-angels): the two-angels framing of Barn. 18–20 is closer to 1QS 3:13–4:26 than to the Didache. Suggests Barnabas preserves a more archaic recension of the Jewish-Christian Two Ways tradition.
- → [[phase-4-025-clement-stromata]] — *direct-quote* / *commentary-on*: Clement of Alexandria quotes Barnabas as the apostle Barnabas with scriptural status repeatedly.
- → [[phase-4-026-origen-on-first-principles]] — *direct-influence*: Origen knows Barnabas; the Alexandrian allegorical tradition runs Barnabas → Clement → Origen.
- → Subsequent *Adversus Judaeos* literature (Justin's *Dialogue with Trypho*; Tertullian's *Adv. Judaeos*; Chrysostom's *Adv. Judaeos* homilies) — *founding-document*: Barnabas inaugurates the genre and its supersessionist core.

## Disputes
- **Date.** Two main positions: (a) ~+115/+120, immediately post-Diaspora Revolt (Hvalvik 1996); (b) ~+132/+135, just before Bar Kokhba (Prostmeier 1999, Kraft 1965). Barn. 16.4's "the very servants of the enemy will build it up again" is the decisive but ambiguous passage. Mainstream range: +120/+132.
- **Provenance.** Alexandria is the consensus on internal evidence (allegorical method; later Alexandrian reception); a Syrian-Palestinian alternative has been argued but is now minority.
- **Authorship.** Universally regarded as pseudonymous: the apostolic-era Barnabas of Acts (a Levite, presumably with respect for Mosaic Law) cannot be the author of a tract arguing the Mosaic covenant was always typological-and-Christian.
- **Relation to Didache.** Common-source hypothesis is now consensus (formerly: mutual literary dependence).
- **Severity of supersessionism.** Barnabas's "Israel never had the covenant" position is more radical than most subsequent *Adversus Judaeos*. Whether this reflects Alexandrian Christian-Jewish rupture specifically or a broader 2nd-c. trajectory is debated.
