# Mishnah

## What it is
The foundational legal text of rabbinic Judaism: a redacted compilation of oral-Torah traditions covering Jewish law in six orders (sedarim) and sixty-three tractates. The text is highly compressed — the basic Mishnaic genre is a brief paragraph of legal opinion attributed to named rabbis, often presenting multiple positions in dispute. Editorial work was done by Rabbi Judah ha-Nasi the Patriarch (~135–217) at Sepphoris and Beit She'arim ~200 CE; the material redacted goes back through the tannaim — including Akiva, Meir, Yehuda, Shimon, Yose — to Hillel and Shammai (~30 BCE – 30 CE).

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Two catastrophes precede the Mishnah's redaction: the destruction of the Second Temple (70 CE) and the failed Bar Kokhba Revolt (132–136). Judah ha-Nasi worked under reasonably peaceful Roman conditions ~200 CE as the recognized Patriarch (nasi) of Palestinian Jewry.
- **Religious milieu.** The Temple is gone; the priesthood as religious authority is replaced by the rabbis. The Mishnah is the *constitution* of post-Temple Judaism: it preserves law for sacrifices that can no longer be performed, codifying a religion centered on study and observance instead of cult.
- **Who was behind it.** A school of named rabbis — Hillel and Shammai (founding pair); the Five Pairs (zugot) before them; then ten generations of tannaim down to Judah ha-Nasi.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Jewish manuscript transmission.

## Themes and motifs
- [[chosen-people]] — the Jewish people defined now by Torah-observance rather than by Temple-cult.
- [[divine-kingship]] — God as cosmic king; the malkhut (kingdom) of God in legal-ethical form.
