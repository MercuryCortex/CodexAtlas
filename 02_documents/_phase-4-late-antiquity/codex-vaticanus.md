# Codex Vaticanus

## What it is
With [[codex-sinaiticus]], one of the two greatest **4th-century Greek uncial codices** of the Bible — and, in the judgement of much textual scholarship, the single finest witness to the New Testament text. *Vaticanus* (designated **B**, or 03) is written on superb vellum in three columns per page, in a disciplined hand. It contains most of the Greek Old Testament (Septuagint) and the New Testament, though the manuscript is now physically defective at the end: it breaks off in the middle of **Hebrews (9:14)** and so lacks the remainder of Hebrews, the Pastoral Epistles, Philemon, and Revelation. It has been in the Vatican Library since at least the 15th century, where its very accessibility long made it both famous and, until the 19th century, frustratingly under-published.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The 4th-century imperial church, in which large parchment Bibles became feasible artefacts of an established religion.
- **Religious milieu.** A church consolidating its scriptures; Vaticanus's text is notably "clean," reflecting a careful exemplar.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous professional scribes in a major scriptorium; the place of production (Alexandria, Caesarea, or Rome) is debated.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Unlike Sinaiticus, Vaticanus was never lost — it sat in the Vatican Library, catalogued there by the late 15th century, but the church restricted access, and a full reliable collation only came in the 19th century, feeding the critical work of Tischendorf, Tregelles, and above all Westcott and Hort.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[codex-sinaiticus]] — *sibling-witness*: the paired pillars of the Alexandrian New Testament text; their agreements and disagreements are central to textual criticism.
- → [[phase-3-006-septuagint]] — *manuscript-transmission*: Vaticanus is among the most important complete witnesses to the Greek Old Testament.
- → [[phase-4-095-new-testament-canon]] — *direct-evidence*: a primary witness to the 4th-century contents of the Christian Bible.
- → [[phase-4-108-peshitta]] — *parallel-witness*: a contemporary witness to the New Testament in the Syriac tradition.

## Disputes
- **Place of origin** (Alexandria, Caesarea, or Rome) is unresolved.
- **Relation to Constantine's "fifty Bibles."** Skeat (1999) argued Vaticanus may belong with the parchment Bibles Constantine commissioned from Eusebius; the thesis is debated.
- **Date.** Usually placed in the first half of the 4th century, slightly earlier than or contemporary with Sinaiticus.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The text behind the modern New Testament.** When Westcott and Hort built their epoch-making 1881 critical edition, they gave Vaticanus decisive authority as the purest witness to the early text — a judgement that, refined, still underlies the Greek New Testament editions from which essentially all modern Bible translations are made. The line from a 4th-century vellum codex to the New Testament on a present-day reader's shelf runs straight through Vaticanus (Westcott & Hort 1881; Metzger & Ehrman 2005).
- **Sibling evidence for a forming canon.** Read beside [[codex-sinaiticus]], Vaticanus lets scholars triangulate the contents, order, and text of the Christian scriptures at the moment the canon was crystallizing — concrete documentary anchor for [[phase-4-095-new-testament-canon]] (Metzger & Ehrman 2005).
