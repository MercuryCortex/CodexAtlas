# Allogenes the Stranger

## What it is
A Sethian Platonizing Gnostic apocalypse, companion text to *Zostrianos*. The visionary "Allogenes" ("Stranger" or "Of Another Race" — a standard Sethian designation for the spiritual seed of Seth) is instructed by Iouel ("She of the Glories") through a series of revelations, then ascends in vision through the layers of the Sethian Pleroma — the Triple-Powered One, the Barbelo aeon, ultimately to the Unknowable One who is beyond every predicate. Returning, Allogenes commands his disciple Messos to write the revelation down and to deposit the book on a mountain. The text is more philosophical-mystical than narrative; it has no Christology, no Jesus, no Genesis-rewriting; it is a *technical Platonist negative theology in Sethian mythological dress*.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Severan and post-Severan Roman Empire. *Allogenes*, like *Zostrianos*, circulates in the 3rd-c. multilingual cosmopolitan philosophical scene of Rome and the eastern Mediterranean.
- **Religious milieu.** Sethian Gnosticism in its Platonizing phase. Turner 2001 places *Allogenes*, *Zostrianos*, *Three Steles of Seth*, and *Marsanes* in a coherent subgroup — the Sethian Platonizing apocalypses — that drop the explicit Christian narrative elements of the earlier *Apocryphon of John*-type and operate in fully technical Middle Platonist philosophical vocabulary.
- **Who was behind it.** Sethian Gnostic schools, probably in Alexandria or Syria. The community is competing with — and partly *internal to* — the same 3rd-c. philosophical milieu that produces Plotinus, Porphyry, and Origen.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Single Coptic copy in Nag Hammadi Codex XI, alongside the Valentinian *Interpretation of Knowledge* and Hypsiphrone. Recovered 1945.

## Themes and motifs
- [[ascent-of-the-soul]] / [[apocalyptic-revelation]] — the central structural motif; Allogenes is instructed first, then ascends.
- [[via-negativa]] — the highest deity (the Unknowable One, the Pre-existent One) is described in strict apophatic terms. The treatment of the Unknowable as *more than One* (beyond unity, beyond predication) closely parallels Plotinus's treatment of the One in *Enneads* V.3 and VI.9; the relation between *Allogenes* and Plotinus on this point is the technical philosophical question of the text.
- [[aeon-as-emanation]] — the Pleroma's structure: the Triple-Powered One, the Barbelo aeon, the Autogenes, the Luminaries.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — salvation by *vision* of the Pleroma; the *form* of knowledge is itself the *content* of salvation.
- [[anthropos-myth]] — Allogenes-as-stranger / spiritual-seed-of-Seth: the salvific narrative of the divine race displaced into the world of darkness and now ascending home.
- *Mountain-deposit motif* — the closing instruction to deposit the book on a mountain echoes Jewish apocalyptic conventions (1 Enoch, Apocalypse of Abraham) and may signal the community's actual textual-burial practices (the Nag Hammadi codices themselves were buried).

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — *polemic-target*: Plotinus's *Enneads* II.9 "Against the Gnostics" targets, among other texts, *Allogenes*. Porphyry *Vita Plotini* 16 records that Amelius (Plotinus's senior student) wrote a long refutation of *Allogenes* specifically. Turner 2001, Burns 2014.
- → [[porphyry]] *Vita Plotini* 16 — *documentary-evidence*: the *Vita Plotini* 16 passage is the key external witness to the confrontation; *Allogenes* and *Zostrianos* are named directly by Porphyry as the Gnostic texts at issue.
- → Amelius's lost *Refutation of Allogenes* — *direct-refutation*: lost; known only from *Vita Plotini* 16.
- → [[phase-4-056-zostrianos]] — *parallel*: companion Sethian Platonizing apocalypse, theologically and structurally closely related. Both texts likely circulated together in the 3rd-c. Sethian community.
- → [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — *direct-source*: the *Parmenides* first hypothesis is the philosophical-technical foundation. *Allogenes*'s apophatic theology has direct verbal parallels with anonymous *Parmenides*-commentary fragments preserved in the *Anonymous Commentary on the Parmenides* (Hadot 1968 attributed to Porphyry; recent scholarship disputes the Porphyrian attribution — Bechtle 1999, Brisson 2002). The relation between *Allogenes*'s negative theology and the *Anonymous Parmenides Commentary* is one of the major technical-philosophical questions of late-antique Platonism.
- → Middle Platonist negative theology — *substrate-influence*: Eudorus of Alexandria, Moderatus of Gades, Numenius of Apamea.
- → [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]] — *shared-Sethian-milieu*: same Pleroma furniture (Barbelo, the Triple-Powered, the Autogenes), but in *Allogenes* the Christian-Genesis narrative is gone.
- → [[seth]] — *pseudepigraphic-figure*: Allogenes-as-Seth's-line is the Sethian community's claim to authentic visionary ancestry from the third son of Adam.

## Substrate and influence — cross-tradition wiring
- **The Plotinus-Sethian confrontation: load-bearing edge.** Together with *Zostrianos*, *Allogenes* is *the* documented case of mid-3rd-c. Sethian Gnostic theological literature operating in shared philosophical space with the Plotinian Neoplatonic school. The Plotinian circle's response is both polemical (the refutations by Porphyry against Zostrianos and Amelius against Allogenes) and substantive (Plotinus's own *Enneads* II.9). Porphyry's testimony in *Vita Plotini* 16 is *the* single passage in the patristic-philosophical corpus that names specific Gnostic texts being read and refuted in a philosophical school. Burns 2014 demonstrates how *Allogenes* operates a sophisticated Middle Platonist apophatic theology that Plotinus partially shares and partially rejects.
- **The Anonymous Parmenides Commentary and *Allogenes*.** Pierre Hadot's 1968 *Porphyre et Victorinus* argued that the *Anonymous Parmenides Commentary* (a partial commentary preserved in a Turin palimpsest) shares technical apophatic vocabulary with *Allogenes*, raising the possibility of common dependence on a pre-Plotinian Middle Platonist source — possibly the same source Plotinus himself drew on. Bechtle 1999 *The Anonymous Commentary on Plato's Parmenides* extends the analysis. The thesis would make *Allogenes* a direct witness to a pre-Plotinian Middle Platonist *Parmenides*-commentary tradition otherwise lost. This is one of the genuinely *productive* technical-philosophical contributions Gnostic studies has made to the broader history of late-antique philosophy.
- **Sethian community in dialogue with Greek philosophy.** *Allogenes* (and *Zostrianos*) shows that the Sethian community was not philosophically isolated; it was operating in the same philosophical conversation as the Plotinian school. The Sethian Platonizing turn is best understood not as Gnostic borrowing from Platonism but as parallel development out of shared Middle Platonist sources, with the Sethians eventually losing the philosophical argument (Plotinus's polemic gained traction) while the Plotinian Neoplatonists won institutional Platonist legitimacy.
- **The pseudepigraphic *foreigner*.** *Allogenes* — "of another race / stranger" — is a self-designation for the Sethian spiritual elite, the divine seed in the world of the archons. The motif of *being a stranger in this world* connects Sethian theology to the broader Hellenistic-Jewish topos of the *paroikia* (sojourning, exile) and to later Manichaean and Mandaean accounts of the soul as foreign to the cosmos. Hans Jonas's *The Gnostic Religion* (1958, 1963) made the *alien* motif central to his existentialist reading of Gnosticism; Burns 2014's title *Apocalypse of the Alien God* makes the connection explicit.

## Disputes
- **Direction of dependence with Plotinus.** *Allogenes* depends on Plotinus / Plotinus depends on a common source with *Allogenes* / both depend independently on Middle Platonist sources — all three positions are currently defended. Turner 2001 favors common-source dependence; Brisson 2002 has revisited.
- **Identity of Allogenes-as-figure.** Sethian texts identify Allogenes with Seth or one of Seth's descendants; whether *Allogenes* specifically means "the third son of Adam" or "the spiritual elect descended from Seth" varies in the texts.
- **The Anonymous *Parmenides* Commentary attribution.** Hadot 1968 attributed to Porphyry; recent work (Bechtle 1999) considers a pre-Plotinian author. If pre-Plotinian, it would significantly affect the dating and dependence-direction of *Allogenes*.
- **Whether *Zostrianos* and *Allogenes* were translated into Coptic together or independently.** Most likely together in the same Sethian community's 4th-c. Coptic edition, but not certain.
