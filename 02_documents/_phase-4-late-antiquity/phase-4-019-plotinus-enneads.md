# Enneads

## What it is
The 54 surviving treatises of Plotinus (~204/5–270), organized by his disciple Porphyry into six "sets of nine" (*enneades*). The system articulates a hierarchy of three primary hypostases: (1) the One (*to Hen*) — utterly simple, beyond being and thought, the source of all that is; (2) Nous (Intellect) — the first emanation, in which all the Platonic Forms exist as living thoughts thinking themselves; (3) the World Soul — the dynamic principle that produces nature and individual souls. From the soul descends matter, the indefinite formless limit of emanation. Human souls retain a connection to Nous and can, by philosophical purification and contemplation, achieve direct vision (*henōsis*) of the One. Plotinus reports four such unions in his own lifetime (Porphyry, *Life* 23). Ennead II.9 is an explicit polemic against the Gnostics — a key document for understanding the relationship between Neoplatonism and the Gnostic schools of Plotinus' Roman circle.

## Primary text — selected excerpts

The excerpts use Stephen MacKenna's *Plotinus: The Enneads* (1917–1930, Faber & Faber), now in the public domain in most jurisdictions and widely available at the Internet Archive and at the Sacred Texts site. MacKenna's English is rhythmical and beautiful; for current scholarly use consult A. H. Armstrong's Loeb edition (7 vols., 1966–1988), which offers a more literal English alongside the Henry-Schwyzer Greek. Ennead and treatise numbers follow the standard Porphyrian arrangement.

### Ennead V.1.1–2 — the soul's forgetting of its origin

The treatise *On the Three Primary Hypostases* opens with a diagnosis: souls have forgotten their divine source and become enamored of the body. The recovery is the path of the *Enneads* themselves.

> What can it be that has brought the souls to forget the father, God, and, though members of the Divine and entirely of that world, to ignore at once themselves and It? The evil that has overtaken them has its source in self-will, in the entry into the sphere of process, and in the primal differentiation with the desire for self-ownership... They have come to ignore even their own nature, like children separated from their family since birth and so long away from home that they recognize neither themselves nor their parentage.
>
> Let every soul recall, then, at the outset the truth that soul is the author of all living things, that it has breathed the life into them all, whatever is nourished by earth and sea, all the creatures of the air, the divine stars in the sky; it is the maker of the sun; itself formed and ordered this vast heaven and conducts all that rhythmic motion...

Citation: Ennead V.1.1–2, trans. Stephen MacKenna 1917–1930; cf. Armstrong Loeb vol. V, pp. 12–17.

### Ennead V.1.6 — the procession from the One

The classical Neoplatonist account of emanation. The One overflows precisely *because* it is perfect; what proceeds is not a part of it (the One is partless) but its overflow turning back toward it.

> Given this immobility in the Supreme, it can neither have yielded assent nor uttered decree nor stirred in any way towards the existence of a secondary. What happened then? What are we to conceive as rising in the neighbourhood of that immobility?
>
> It must be a circumradiation — produced from the Supreme but from the Supreme unaltering — and may be compared to the brilliant light encircling the sun and ceaselessly generated from that unchanging substance. All existences, as long as they retain their character, produce — about themselves, from their essence, in virtue of the power which must be in them — some necessary, outward-facing hypostasis continuously attached to them and representing in image the engendering archetypes...

Citation: Ennead V.1.6, trans. MacKenna; cf. Armstrong Loeb V, pp. 30–35.

### Ennead VI.9.4 — the One is beyond knowledge and being

The summit of Plotinian apophatic theology. The One is neither thinkable nor sayable, because thought introduces duality. The most influential ancient pagan formulation of negative theology, channeled through Proclus to Pseudo-Dionysius and through Dionysius to medieval Christian mysticism.

> Generative of all, The Unity is none of all; neither thing nor quantity nor quality nor intellect nor soul; not in motion, not at rest, not in place, not in time: it is the self-defined, unique in form or, better, formless, existing before Form was, or Movement or Rest, all of which are attachments of Being and make Being the manifold it is.
>
> But how, if not in movement, can it be otherwise than at rest? The answer is that movement and rest are states pertaining to Being, which necessarily has one or the other or both. Besides, anything at rest must be so in virtue of Rest as something distinct: Unity at rest becomes the ground of an attribute and at once ceases to be a simplex.

Citation: Ennead VI.9.3–4 (selection), trans. MacKenna; cf. Armstrong Loeb VII, pp. 312–317.

### Ennead VI.9.9–11 — the soul's union with the One

The closing chapters of the final treatise (in Porphyry's arrangement). Plotinus describes the union (*henōsis*) — wordless, beyond knowledge, like two centres coinciding — and ends the Enneads with the famous phrase about the "flight of the alone to the Alone."

> The soul that has known its likeness will be sound, will be in good and beauty; he will be one with the One, with no rift between God and self... There were not two; beholder was one with beheld; it was not a vision compassed but a unity apprehended. The man formed by this mingling with the Supreme must — if he only remember — carry its image impressed upon him: he is become the Unity, nothing within him or without inducing any diversity; no movement now, no passion, no outlooking desire, once this ascent is achieved; reasoning is in abeyance and all Intellection and even, to dare the word, the very self; caught away, filled with God, he has in perfect stillness attained isolation; all the being calmed, he turns neither to this side nor to that, not even inwards to himself; utterly resting he has become very rest...
>
> This is the life of gods and of the godlike and blessed among men, liberation from the alien that besets us here, a life taking no pleasure in the things of earth, the passing of solitary to solitary [*phygē monou pros monon*].

Citation: Ennead VI.9.10–11 (selection — closing of the *Enneads*), trans. MacKenna; cf. Armstrong Loeb VII, pp. 332–345.

### Ennead II.9.10 — against the Gnostics (selection)

The polemic Plotinus directs against the Gnostic circles in his Roman audience. He criticizes their reading of the cosmos as the contemptible work of a botched demiurge, defending the goodness and beauty of the visible universe.

> But this World — how can we deny it to be the image, ever fresh-fashioned, of the Intelligible Cosmos? The world is no cast-off image of a single faculty, but the work of one undivided Intellection whose every part is at once supreme and supremely conscious...
>
> Those who deny this fair design must speak ill of providence, and confound this universe with the worst of its parts. They despise the works of God — sun, earth, heaven — and call them lower than their own minds. Yet what artist has been seen to look at his work, find it nobly devised, and yet despise it? They have heard that the soul is divine, and on that account take themselves to be gods; but they have left out the steps that lie between, those steps which our own teachers know.

Citation: Ennead II.9.4, 9 (selection from the treatise *Against the Gnostics*), trans. MacKenna; cf. Armstrong Loeb II, pp. 240–253; Narbonne 2011 *passim* for the technical analysis.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Plotinus taught in Rome ~245–270 under Gallienus (r. 253–268), who admired him; Plotinus reportedly proposed a Platonopolis in Campania, never realized. The Crisis of the Third Century was the political backdrop.
- **Religious milieu.** Plotinus' Roman circle included Christians (some converts from Gnostic backgrounds), pagan philosophers, and Gnostics whom Plotinus criticized for "writing apocalypses of Zoroaster, Zostrianos, Nicotheus, Allogenes, and Mesus." Strikingly, Allogenes and Zostrianos are also titles of Nag Hammadi tractates (NHC XI,3; VIII,1) — Plotinus is attacking texts we now possess.
- **Who was behind it.** Plotinus the man, recorded and edited by Porphyry. Porphyry's *Life of Plotinus* (prefixed to the Enneads) is one of our most detailed ancient philosophical biographies.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Greek manuscript tradition; key witnesses are Byzantine. Ficino's 1492 Latin translation (commissioned by Cosimo de' Medici) made Plotinus a central authority for Renaissance Platonism.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hidden-god]] — the One is "beyond being," "beyond thought," apprehensible only by a non-cognitive direct contact; the most influential ancient statement of apophatic theology in non-Christian philosophy.
- [[soul-as-spark]] — the human soul retains a part that "has not descended"; salvation is the philosophical return.
- [[psychopomp]] — the philosopher's ascent through Soul to Nous to the One.
- [[mystery-initiation]] — explicit comparisons to the Eleusinian Mysteries.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — *commentary-on* / *direct-quote*: the Enneads are extended philosophical meditations on Plato (especially the Parmenides, Timaeus, Republic VI, Symposium). The first hypothesis of the Parmenides becomes the doctrine of the One.
- → Nag Hammadi Gnostic texts (Allogenes NHC XI,3; Zostrianos NHC VIII,1) — *polemic-against*: Ennead II.9 attacks these works by name (or by close cognate). One of the rare cases where an ancient philosopher and a surviving Gnostic text are in *direct* dialogue.
- → [[phase-4-020-iamblichus-on-the-mysteries]] — *commentary-on* (next generation): Iamblichus builds on Plotinus but adds theurgy, against Plotinus' more purely contemplative approach.
- → [[phase-4-021-proclus-elements-of-theology]] — *direct-influence*: Proclus systematizes Plotinian metaphysics into axiomatic form.
- → Augustine, *Confessions* 7 — *direct-influence*: Augustine credits "Platonist books" (almost certainly Plotinus via Marius Victorinus' Latin translation) with helping him overcome Manichaean materialism on the way to Christian conversion.
- → Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite — *direct-influence*: Christian apophatic theology channels Plotinian negative theology through Proclus.
- → [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] — *shared-milieu* / *parallel-motif*: both Plotinian and Hermetic systems are emanationist; whether Plotinus knew Hermetic literature is disputed (Festugière argued yes; others no).

## Disputes
- **Plotinus and Gnosticism.** Whether Plotinus' polemic was directed at specific known Sethian Gnostic communities in Rome (Turner, Tardieu, Narbonne) or at a broader Gnostic-philosophical posture. The Nag Hammadi parallels with Allogenes and Zostrianos make the specific-community reading strongly favored.
- **Plotinus and Mysticism.** Whether Plotinus' *henōsis* is properly "mystical" in the modern sense (Hadot, Bussanich for; Rist with qualifications).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **"Flight of the alone to the Alone" (*phygē monou pros monon*, VI.9.11) ↔ Sufi *fanāʾ* ↔ Hindu *samādhi* ↔ Dzogchen *rigpa*:** four traditions articulate the same phenomenological claim — subject/object collapse in unitive contemplation — arriving independently within a 1,000-year window. Plotinus' Rome (~270 CE) is the first Western philosophical statement of what comparative religion calls "mystical union"; the Islamic, Hindu, and Tibetan parallels are vault-crossable with sourced T1 citations at each node.
- **Plotinus names Allogenes and Zostrianos — both texts are in this vault (NHC XI,3; VIII,1):** Ennead II.9 attacks "apocalypses of Zoroaster, Zostrianos, Nicotheus, Allogenes" circulating in Plotinus' own Roman circle; Nag Hammadi recovered the attacked texts. We hold both sides of one of antiquity's sharpest metaphysical debates. Plotinus' charge — Gnostics despise the good cosmos and plagiarize Plato without credit — is now answerable from vault-resident documents (cf. [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]]).
- **Augustine, *Confessions* 7 → Latin Christianity:** "certain Platonist books" in Marius Victorinus' Latin translation (almost certainly Plotinus, *Enneads* I–V) freed Augustine from Manichaean matter-is-evil dualism. The *Enneads* are the direct intellectual bridge from pagan Neoplatonism into Nicene theology; Augustine's anti-Manichaean and anti-sceptical arguments both depend on Plotinian immateriality.
- **Enneads → Proclus → Pseudo-Dionysius → Eckhart — the Western apophatic descent:** Proclus axiomatized Plotinus; Pseudo-Dionysius Christianized Proclus (and was mistaken for Paul's Athenian convert for 900 years, granting apostolic authority); Eckhart's *Gottheit* ("Godhead beyond God, beyond being") is the One in Christian dress. Every Western mystic who says "God is beyond all names and predication" is downstream of Ennead VI.9.4 — the single most important transmission line in Western apophatic theology.
