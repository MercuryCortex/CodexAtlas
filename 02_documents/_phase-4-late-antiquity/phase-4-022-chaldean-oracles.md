# Chaldean Oracles

## What it is
A collection of hexameter Greek oracles attributed to two figures named Julian (father and son) under Marcus Aurelius (~150–180). The oracles articulate a theology of a transcendent Father, a Second Intellect, the goddess Hekate as cosmic Soul, and a complex of intermediary divinities. The fragments include practical theurgic instructions, ascetic disciplines, and ritual prescriptions. From Iamblichus onward, the Oracles are treated by Neoplatonists as a sacred scripture parallel to Plato's dialogues.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Late-antique pagan revelation literature; the "Chaldean" framing claims ancient Mesopotamian authority for what is in content Middle Platonist philosophical theology.
- **Discovery / transmission.** No continuous transmission. Fragments survive in Neoplatonist quotations.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hidden-god]] — the unknowable Father.
- [[mystery-initiation]] / theurgy — explicit ritual instructions.
- [[psychopomp]] — ascent of the soul through divine orders.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-020-iamblichus-on-the-mysteries]] — *commentary-on* / *direct-quote*: Iamblichus and his successors treat the Oracles as revelation; many fragments are preserved in their commentaries.
- → [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — *contemporaneous*: same milieu, somewhat different intellectual direction (Plotinus was reticent about ritual; Iamblichus made ritual central using the Oracles).
- → Mesopotamian astrological traditions — *claimed-influence*: the Oracles claim a Chaldean source; in fact they are Greek philosophical productions with astrological motifs.
