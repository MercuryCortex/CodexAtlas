# Roman History, Book 69 (Cassius Dio — the reign of Hadrian)

## What it is
The book of **Cassius Dio's** eighty-volume Greek *Roman History* that narrates the **reign of Hadrian** (117–138 CE). Written by a Roman senator of the Severan age and surviving chiefly through a later Byzantine epitome, Book 69 is the principal continuous ancient account of Hadrian's principate — his travels, building, administration, and, above all, the catastrophic **Bar Kokhba revolt** in Judaea. It also preserves an ancient (somewhat garbled) notice of the rebuilding of the [[pantheon-rome|Pantheon]] in Rome.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Composed under the Severans by a participant in imperial government, looking back a century to Hadrian.
- **Religious milieu.** Book 69 records the religious flashpoint of the era: Hadrian's refounding of Jerusalem as the colony *Aelia Capitolina* with a temple of Jupiter on the Temple Mount, and his ban on circumcision, which Dio gives as causes of the Jewish revolt.
- **Who was behind it.** Cassius Dio; the surviving text is mediated by the epitomist Xiphilinus.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The full book is lost; we read it through Byzantine excerpts and epitome.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-kingship]] — Dio's account of Hadrian, the travelling sacral emperor who founds cities and cults, exemplifies the Roman fusion of rule and religion.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[pantheon-rome]] — *primary-source*: Dio 69 is a chief literary witness to the Hadrianic Pantheon (and the source of the misattribution to Agrippa).
- → [[phase-3-024-suetonius-twelve-caesars]] — *genre-parallel*: imperial Roman history/biography of the same world.

## Disputes
- **Epitome distortion.** Because Book 69 survives through Xiphilinus's abridgement, separating Dio's own words and judgements from the epitomist's is a constant problem (Millar 1964).
- **Bar Kokhba casualty figures.** Dio's enormous numbers (985 villages destroyed, 580,000 killed) are debated as rhetoric versus record.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The chief account of Judaism's last great revolt.** Dio 69 is the primary narrative for the **Bar Kokhba war (132–135 CE)** — the crushing of which sealed the end of Jewish political autonomy in the land, the renaming of Judaea as *Syria Palaestina*, the barring of Jews from Jerusalem, and the decisive turn of Judaism toward a diaspora-and-rabbinic future. Few single sources so directly document a hinge in the history of a world religion (Schäfer 2003; Millar 1964).
- **A pagan's Pantheon.** Dio's confused notice that Agrippa built the Pantheon — when the standing building is Hadrian's reconstruction bearing Agrippa's reused inscription — is itself the classic case study in how ancient testimony and standing monument must be read against each other.
