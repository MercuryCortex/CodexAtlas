# Hypostasis of the Archons

## What it is
A Sethian Gnostic rewriting of Genesis 1–6, opening as a treatise by an unnamed author addressed to one "you, my friend," then sliding into mythological narrative. The archons attempt to capture Adam, fail to seize the spiritual Eve, rape the bodily Eve to engender Cain (a recurring Gnostic motif), and pursue Norea (the daughter / sister of Seth here) — who refuses them and is rescued by the angel Eleleth, who reveals the truth of the rulers' nature. The text shares core mythology with the Apocryphon of John but is more *narratively* coherent and centers feminine resistance.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Roman Egypt, second to third century.
- **Religious milieu.** Sethian Gnostic milieu, alongside ApocJohn and On the Origin of the World. The text is post-Christianized (it mentions "the Apostle Paul" by name in its opening), but the underlying myth has been argued by Pearson to derive from a Jewish-heretical substratum.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Nag Hammadi II (1945).

## Themes and motifs
- [[demiurge]] — Yaldabaoth named Samael ("blind god") here, with the variant name Sakla ("fool").
- [[divine-feminine]] — Norea as the female redeemer-figure; the spiritual Eve as awakener.
- [[fall-of-humanity]] — re-coded as the archons' rape of the material Eve, not human disobedience.
