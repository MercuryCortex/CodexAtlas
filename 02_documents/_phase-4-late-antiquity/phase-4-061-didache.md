# Didache

## What it is
Short Greek church manual in 16 chapters, the **earliest extant Christian community-rule document**. Four sections: (1) ethical catechesis on the *Two Ways* of life and death (chs. 1–6); (2) liturgical instructions on baptism, fasting, prayer (Lord's Prayer), and the Eucharist (chs. 7–10); (3) order on receiving traveling apostles, prophets, and teachers, and supporting resident clergy (chs. 11–15); (4) an apocalyptic conclusion (ch. 16). The text reads like a stratified rural-Syrian community handbook stitching together pre-existing Jewish catechetical material, agraphic Jesus-sayings, and emerging Christian liturgical practice.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Composed during Flavian Rome's continued post-+70 reorganisation of Judaea / Syria; Christianity is still organisationally indistinguishable from synagogue Judaism in many Syrian villages.
- **Religious milieu.** Syrian Jewish-Christian Christianity — the same milieu that produced [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] and that [[ignatius-of-antioch]] addresses a generation later. Wandering charismatic *prophets* and *apostles* (not the Twelve — itinerant Christian missionaries) still hold authority alongside emerging resident bishops and deacons; the *Didache* documents the friction between the two governance forms.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous community redactor(s) compiling earlier *Two Ways* catechesis (a Jewish-derived form attested independently at Qumran and in *Barnabas*), Jesus-sayings (esp. Matthean), and developing liturgical formulae.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Cited by Eusebius (*HE* 3.25.4 — among the "spurious / disputed"), Athanasius's *Festal Letter 39* (+367, useful for catechumen-formation but not canonical), and the *Apostolic Constitutions* (which absorbs much of it). Lost to Western view; **Bryennios 1873 rediscovery** in Constantinople made the text available.

## Themes and motifs
- [[ethical-dualism]] — the Two Ways framework. "There are two ways: one of life and one of death; and great is the difference between the two ways" (1.1). The *Two Ways* schema is the binding structural framework of chs. 1–6.
- [[two-spirits-doctrine]] — load-bearing substrate wire. The Two Ways recension here is the *Christian* form of the same Second-Temple Jewish moral-dualistic tradition expressed at Qumran in 1QS 3:13–4:26 ("Two Spirits") and in Barnabas 18–20 (two angels). The shared substrate is Jewish, not Christian; the Iranian-substrate question (whether ultimately mediated from Zoroastrian dualism) is live among scholars (Boyce, Hultgård, Frey).
- [[ecclesial-authority]] — the document is a critical witness to the **transition from charismatic itinerant authority to resident clergy** (apostles and prophets in chs. 11–13 vs. bishops and deacons in 15.1–2). Prophets who stay more than three days or ask for money "in the spirit" are exposed as false (11.5–6, 11.12).
- [[eschatological-imminence]] — ch. 16's apocalyptic conclusion: *Maranatha*, the world-deceiver, the resurrection of the saints. Strikingly close to Matthean-style apocalyptic discourse.
- Eucharistic liturgy (chs. 9–10) — the earliest extant Christian eucharistic prayer, with strikingly *Jewish* (*berakhah*-style) thanksgiving formulae over cup and bread and *no* atonement / body-and-blood language. This is the strongest internal evidence for an early date and a Jewish-Christian milieu.
- Baptismal practice (ch. 7) — running water preferred, still water acceptable; triple immersion in the triune name; fasting before baptism by candidate, sponsor, and community.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — *parallel-motif* / *shared-substrate*: the Didache *Two Ways* (chs. 1–6) and the Qumran *Treatise of the Two Spirits* (1QS 3:13–4:26) both express a moral-dualistic catechesis pre-existing in Second-Temple Judaism. Direct dependence cannot be shown; common Jewish source is the consensus (van de Sandt & Flusser 2002).
- → [[phase-4-064-epistle-of-barnabas]] — *shared-source* / *parallel-recension*: Barnabas 18–20 preserves a *parallel* Two Ways recension (with two-angels framing rather than two-ways framing). Three main scholarly positions: (a) Barnabas depends on Didache; (b) Didache depends on Barnabas; (c) both independently use a common Jewish-Christian source-tract — currently dominant (Niederwimmer 1998).
- → [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] — *direct-quote* / *shared-milieu*: heavy verbal and thematic dependence on Matthean tradition (Lord's Prayer at *Did.* 8 is the Matthean recension; "do not give what is holy to the dogs" at 9.5 quotes Matt 7:6). Likely shared Syrian-Jewish-Christian milieu.
- → [[phase-4-062-1-clement]] — *shared-milieu*: contemporary Christian community documents articulating early-2nd-c. emergent church order from different geographies.
- → [[phase-4-063-ignatius-letters]] — *parallel-development*: Ignatius's monepiscopal vision contrasts with the *Didache*'s still-fluid prophet/apostle/bishop economy, marking the trajectory toward consolidated episcopate.
- → [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]] — *deep-substrate*: the moral-dualistic choice-of-paths schema has been argued (Boyce, Hultgård, Shaked) to descend ultimately from Iranian *asha* / *druj* via Second-Temple Jewish mediation. Contested; the *Two Ways* per se is securely Jewish but the deeper question is open.

## Disputes
- **Date.** Range +50 to +110 is broad and load-bearing. **Early-date camp** (Audet 1958, Milavec 2003, van de Sandt 2002): mid-1st c., contemporary with or earlier than the Synoptics. **Late-date camp** (Niederwimmer 1998, Tuilier): early 2nd c., post-Matthean. Mainstream Hermeneia position: *final redaction* ~+100/+110, with substantially older Jewish-Christian source-material.
- **Unity.** Is this a redacted single work or a loose compilation? Consensus: stratified compilation with at least three redactional layers, but a single late-stage editor.
- **Authority.** Was it ever considered canonical? Athanasius (*Festal Letter 39*) lists it as useful for catechumens but not canonical; Eusebius marks it as disputed-spurious.
- **Relation to Barnabas's Two Ways.** Active debate; common-source hypothesis is currently dominant.
