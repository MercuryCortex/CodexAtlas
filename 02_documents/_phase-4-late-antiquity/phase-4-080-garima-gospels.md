# Garima Gospels (Abba Garima Gospels)

## What it is
Three Ge'ez illuminated codices of the Four Gospels — *Garima 1*, *Garima 2*, *Garima 3* — preserved continuously at the Abba Garima monastery, 5 km east of Adwa in highland Tigray, Ethiopia. Each codex contains the four canonical Gospels (Matthew, Mark, Luke, John) in Ge'ez translation, with full-page evangelist portraits, ornamental canon tables (Eusebian concordance grids set in *tempietto* architectural frames), and decorated incipits. Radiocarbon analyses of folio samples (Capelli, Bausi et al. 2013) returned 95-percent-confidence date ranges of 330–540 CE, 430–650 CE, and 530–660 CE for the three codices, decisively pushing them earlier than any previously-securely-dated illuminated Christian Gospel manuscript — *the oldest surviving illuminated Christian Gospel codices in the world*.

The codices are aesthetically and art-historically exceptional. The seated-evangelist portrait type (a frontal-facing seated evangelist holding the Gospel codex, framed architecturally) appears in the Garima Gospels in its earliest surviving form; this iconographic type dominates Byzantine, Latin, and Eastern medieval Gospel-book illumination for the next millennium. The canon-table architecture (Eusebian concordance grids set in classical arcaded *tempietti*) is also among the earliest extant.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Composed in the Aksumite Kingdom in the centuries after Christianization under [[ezana-of-aksum]] (~330 CE) and through the Nine-Saints / Miaphysite monastic consolidation (~480–540 CE). The codices materially anchor Aksum as a major late-antique Christian intellectual-artistic center.
- **Religious milieu.** Ethiopian Tewahedo Christianity in its formative centuries, with active Syriac-Coptic-Greek influence; the Garima monastery was a [[the-nine-saints]] foundation, embedding the codices in the cross-Mediterranean Syriac-Coptic-Miaphysite ecumene.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous monastic-scribal production at Abba Garima monastery. Traditional attribution to [[abba-garima]] himself (with the hagiographic legend that he wrote one in a single day with miraculous assistance) is canonical but cannot be sustained literally; the codices likely emerged from the monastery scriptorium across multiple hands and decades within the radiocarbon range.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuously preserved at Abba Garima monastery from production to present — over 1500 years of in-situ monastic custody. Bound and rebound multiple times; some folios are restorations. The 2013 radiocarbon study and the 2016 McKenzie-Watson publication were the first systematic external scholarly engagements; the monastery has historically restricted external access.

## Themes and motifs
- [[evangelist-portraits]] — earliest surviving examples of the iconographic type that dominates Christian gospel-book illumination for the next 1000 years.
- [[canon-tables]] — Eusebian concordance grids set in classical-architectural *tempietti*; among the earliest surviving examples.
- [[illuminated-manuscript-art]] — the founding monument of African illuminated-Christian-manuscript art.
- [[ethiopic-canon-broad]] — together with the Ge'ez Old Testament tradition (preserving 1 Enoch, Jubilees, Meqabyan, etc.), forms the textual-material substrate of the unique Ethiopian biblical canon.

## Disputes
- **Dating.** The Capelli/Bausi 2013 radiocarbon ranges are sometimes critiqued as too broad to be useful — Garima 1's 330–540 CE window spans two centuries. Subsequent analyses have narrowed the windows in some cases. The McKenzie-Watson 2016 art-historical assessment cross-validates the 5th-to-early-7th-c. range.
- **Authenticity of all illuminated folios.** Some folios are clearly later restorations (some 11th–13th-c. paint over the original ink-and-pigment). The original illumination program is preserved but partly obscured by restoration.
- **Relationship between the three codices.** Whether Garima 1, 2, 3 are siblings of one workshop or a chronologically-staggered group from a longer scriptorium tradition. Both readings are live.
