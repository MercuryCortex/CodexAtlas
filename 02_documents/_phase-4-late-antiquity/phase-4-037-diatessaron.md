# Diatessaron

## What it is
A second-century **harmonization of the four canonical Gospels** (plus possibly some non-canonical material) into a single continuous narrative of Jesus's life and ministry. Composed by [[tatian-the-syrian]] ~+170, probably in Greek or Syriac, the *Diatessaron* (Greek "through four") was the **standard gospel-text of Syriac Christianity for ~200 years** — until the 5th-c. effort by Rabbula of Edessa (and others) to enforce the separated four-Gospel canon, replacing the Diatessaron with the Peshitta. The original text is lost; reconstruction draws on Ephrem's 4th-c. commentary, the Arabic Diatessaron, the Codex Fuldensis Latin text, and various versional traditions.

## Context
- **Date.** ~+170, during or after Tatian's Roman period under [[justin-martyr|Justin]].
- **Place.** Composition likely at Rome (Eusebius implies as much, HE 4.29); dissemination in Mesopotamia where Tatian returned after Justin's martyrdom.
- **Author.** Tatian — well-attested (Eusebius, Epiphanius, Theodoret, etc.). The work follows in the genre of *gospel harmony* but predates all other extant attempts.
- **Cultural milieu.** Tatian's "Encratite" theology — rigorous sexual renunciation — is sometimes detected in editorial choices (e.g., omission of Jesus's Davidic descent in some witnesses; *Encratite Diatessaron* hypotheses by Petersen et al.).
- **Reception.** Universal in Syriac Christianity for two centuries; condemned by Rabbula and Theodoret in the 5th c. and systematically replaced.

## Themes and motifs
- **Single Jesus-narrative**: the harmony eliminates contradictions between the Gospels by selective omission, conflation, and reordering.
- **Encratite emphases**: where the Diatessaron's text varies from canonical readings, the variants often heighten ascetic/sexually-renunciatory themes — though Petersen treats this with appropriate caution.
- **Old Testament fulfillment**: the harmony preserves and amplifies fulfillment-of-prophecy framing, especially from Matthew.

## Connections (provisional)
- → all four canonical Gospels — *redaction-of*: the Diatessaron is a *literary* redaction integrating the four into one.
- → [[phase-3-014-q-source]] — the *Diatessaron's possible use of a fifth source* (variously identified as the *Gospel of the Hebrews*, the *Gospel of the Ebionites*, or non-Q Jewish-Christian material) is contested but well-attested in some readings.
- → Ephrem the Syrian's *Commentary on the Diatessaron* — *commentary-on*: 4th-c. continuous textual witness.
- → *Codex Fuldensis* (~+547) — *manuscript-transmission*: the Latin medieval gospel-harmony tradition derives partly from the Diatessaron.
- → Persian Diatessaron / Arabic Diatessaron — *manuscript-transmission*: derivative versional traditions, central to reconstruction.

## Disputes
- **Original language.** Syriac (older view), Greek (Petersen 1994's mainstream view), or possibly composed in both. Dura-Europos parchment 24 — a Greek fragment from ~+230 — was once accepted as Diatessaronic, but the attribution is now contested.
- **Fifth-source hypothesis.** Whether the Diatessaron used *Gospel of the Hebrews* or other non-canonical material — long-debated; some variants are striking but not conclusive.
- **Encratite editorializing.** Real but its scope is disputed.
- **Date of composition.** ~+170 is mainstream; some scholars argue earlier (~+155).
