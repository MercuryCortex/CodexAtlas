# Ecclesiastical History

## What it is
The foundational Christian history — ten books covering events from the apostolic age to Constantine's victory over Licinius (324). Eusebius establishes the genre of *ecclesiastical history* (distinct from civil history) and provides our principal evidence for the early Christian succession lists, the canon of scripture in formation (Book 3 lists disputed and rejected books, evidencing a fluid canon in the early 4th century), the persecutions and their martyrs, the rise and condemnation of various heresies, and the eventual Constantinian peace. Eusebius also preserves substantial quotations from earlier Christian writers whose works are otherwise lost — Papias, Hegesippus, Julius Africanus, Origen — making the *History* a major repository of fragmentary primary sources.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The work spans the period from the apostolic age through Diocletian's persecution (303–311) to Constantine's victory and the Council of Nicaea (325). Eusebius was bishop of Caesarea Maritima and a close ally of Constantine.
- **Religious milieu.** Eusebius writes the first *Christian-triumphalist* history; orthodoxy is now state-recognized; the persecutions are read providentially.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Greek transmission; Latin via Rufinus.

## Themes and motifs
- Canon formation — the explicit listing of accepted, disputed, and rejected New Testament books in HE 3.25.
- Apostolic succession — the bishop-lists ground orthodox claims.
- Providential history — Christian victory as divine vindication.
