# The Tripartite Tractate

## What it is
The longest and most systematic Valentinian theological treatise to survive. Three sections, untitled in the manuscript: Part I — the Pleroma (the unfolding of the Father, the Son, and the Aeons; the fall of the Logos in place of the Sophia of other Valentinian systems; the Pleroma's restoration through the Savior); Part II — Anthropogony (the creation of humanity in three classes — *pneumatic*, *psychic*, *hylic* — corresponding to the three orders of being); Part III — the descent of the Savior, the recapitulation of all three orders, and the eschatological reintegration of the pneumatic and psychic into the Pleroma while the hylic perishes. The treatise is uncharacteristically irenic for a Valentinian text: it gestures toward reconciliation with the wider catholic church, treats the psychic Christians (the catholics) as redeemable through faith, and frames the entire system in less mythological, more abstract-theological language than the *Apocryphon of John*-style Sethian texts.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-2nd / early-3rd-c. Roman empire; the Valentinian school is competing with the emerging proto-orthodox catholic church for institutional position.
- **Religious milieu.** Valentinian Christianity is at its theological-systematic peak. Where the *Apocryphon of John* tradition operates in narrative mythology, the Valentinian school under Heracleon and Ptolemaeus operates in scholarly-philosophical mode. The *Tripartite Tractate* is the most accomplished surviving example of Valentinian theological treatise-writing — closer in style to Origen than to *Apocryphon of John*.
- **Who was behind it.** A Valentinian school, possibly the same Eastern-Valentinian milieu as [[heracleon-valentinian]]; some scholars (Quispel, Kasser 1973) have proposed Heracleon as direct author. The substitution of *Logos* for *Sophia* in the standard Valentinian fall-narrative is one of the text's distinctive features and may indicate a particular Valentinian sub-school's theology.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Single Coptic copy in Nag Hammadi Codex I (the *Jung Codex*, acquired in 1952 for the C.G. Jung Institute in Zurich; returned to Cairo 1975). The Coptic translation is in Sub-Akhmimic dialect rather than the Sahidic of the other Nag Hammadi codices, suggesting a different translation tradition.

## Themes and motifs
- [[aeon-as-emanation]] — the Pleroma's structure, with thirty aeons in the canonical Valentinian arrangement.
- [[sophia-pleroma]] — but with the distinctive *Tripartite Tractate* feature: the falling figure is the *Logos*, not Sophia. This *Logos-substitution* alters the gender of the fall-figure and aligns Valentinian theology more closely with the masculine-Logos Johannine tradition.
- [[demiurge]] — the demiurge in *Tripartite Tractate* is not malevolent (as Yaldabaoth in the *Apocryphon of John*) but *ignorant and well-meaning*; he genuinely creates and orders the psychic world, unaware of the higher Pleroma. The *Tripartite Tractate* demiurge is closer to the Platonic *Timaeus* craftsman than to the Sethian malevolent archon.
- [[fall-of-humanity]] / [[anthropos-myth]] — three classes of humans corresponding to the three orders of being: pneumatic (spiritual, descended from the Pleroma's spirit), psychic (soulish, descended from the demiurge), hylic (material, the order that perishes).
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — the pneumatic class is saved by recognition; the psychic class (the catholic Christians) is saved by faith and obedience (a more inclusive soteriology than the Sethian).
- *Apokatastasis* — the eschatological reintegration of pneumatic and psychic into the Pleroma; only the hylic order perishes. This is the Valentinian *universal-restoration-minus-the-hylic* eschatology.

## Substrate and influence — cross-tradition wiring
- **Middle Platonist substrate.** The *Tripartite Tractate*'s three-orders cosmology (Father → Logos → Pleroma; pneumatic → psychic → hylic) is structurally Middle Platonist with Stoic-anthropological loading. Numenius of Apamea's three gods (the *Father*, the *Maker*, the *Made*) are the closest pre-Valentinian philosophical parallel. The text is one of the cleanest cases of Middle Platonist metaphysics being mythologized as Christian theology. Thomassen 2006, Stead 1980.
- **Stoic anthropology substrate.** The three-class division *pneumatic / psychic / hylic* draws on Stoic-physiological anthropology (spirit-soul-body); Valentinians turn the Stoic *parts of the human* into *types of humans*, with soteriological consequences. The substrate is well-documented in Stoic doxography (e.g., SVF II.770ff). Thomassen 2006 ch. 11.
- **Pauline pneumatic-psychic distinction.** Paul in 1 Corinthians 2:14–15 distinguishes the *pneumatikos* and the *psychikos*; the Valentinian three-class system is in part a development of this Pauline distinction. The *Tripartite Tractate* deploys Pauline vocabulary throughout. Pagels 1975 *The Gnostic Paul*.
- **The Logos-substitution as theological move.** Replacing the fallen Sophia with a fallen Logos aligns Valentinian theology with the Johannine Logos-Christology of the proto-orthodox tradition. This is one of the *Tripartite Tractate*'s irenic moves — bringing Valentinian theology closer to the catholic theological vocabulary while preserving the systematic core. Thomassen 2006 reads this as a Heraclean-Valentinian theological strategy.
- **The inclusion of the psychic order.** Unlike Sethian theology, which divides humanity into the saved spiritual and the lost material, the *Tripartite Tractate* affirms that the *psychic* Christians (the catholics) are also saved, by faith rather than gnosis. This is a Valentinian *theological-ecumenical* move toward the wider church — and partly accounts for why Valentinian Christianity could coexist with proto-orthodox Christianity for two centuries before being decisively suppressed.

## Disputes
- **Authorship.** Anonymous; Heracleon-attribution proposed by Quispel and Kasser is plausible but not provable.
- **Date.** ~200–270 CE; precise dating impossible.
- **Eastern vs. Italian Valentinian.** The Logos-substitution and the inclusive treatment of psychic Christians may indicate Eastern-school provenance (where Heracleon's *Tripartite Tractate*-type theology operated); but the standard Eastern-Italian distinction (per Hippolytus *Ref.* 6.35) may be overemphasized in modern scholarship.
- **Relation to the proto-orthodox church.** The *Tripartite Tractate*'s irenic treatment of psychic Christians has been read both as ecumenical bridge and as superior-condescension. Pagels 1975 reads the former; Wilson 1968 the latter.
