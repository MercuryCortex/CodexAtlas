# Corpus Hermeticum XIII (Secret Discourse on the Mountain — the Rebirth Dialogue)

## What it is
The thirteenth treatise of the Greek *Corpus Hermeticum*. A dialogue in which Hermes Trismegistus reveals to his son [[tat-hermetic-disciple|Tat]] the doctrine of *palingenesia* — spiritual rebirth into immortality through divine power. Tat reminds his father of the promise made earlier (in the closing of CH I and again in CH X): that the disciple, when ready, would be told the secret of being "born again." Hermes responds that rebirth is not transmitted in writing or in ordinary speech, but happens by the will of God when the twelve "tormentors" (the irrational vices binding the soul to the material zodiac) are expelled by ten divine "powers" (knowledge, joy, continence, perseverance, justice, generosity, truth, the Good, life, and light). The reborn person becomes *theou pais* — "child of God" — and joins the divine chorus singing the closing secret hymn (CH XIII.16–20). Among all the Hermetica it is the most explicitly *initiatory* (it claims to be a *logos apokryphos* — secret discourse) and it is the most *Christianity-adjacent* in its vocabulary of rebirth, divine sonship, and being "born of God."

## Primary text — selected excerpts

The excerpts use G. R. S. Mead's 1906 *Thrice-Greatest Hermes* (public domain, sacred-texts.com), checked against Copenhaver 1992. Section numbering follows Nock-Festugière.

### CH XIII.1–3 — Tat asks for the secret of rebirth

> Tat: "In the General Sermons, father, thou didst speak in riddles most unclear, conversing on Divinity; and when thou saidst no man could be saved before Rebirth, thy meaning thou didst hide. Further, when I became thy Suppliant, in coming up the Mount, after thou hadst conversed with me, and when I longed to learn the Sermon (Logos) on Rebirth, for this beyond all other things is just the thing I know not, thou saidst that thou wouldst impart it to me — 'when thou wilt have become a stranger to the world.' Wherefore I made me ready and have made the thought in me a stranger to the world-illusion. And now do thou fill up the things that fall short in me with what thou saidst would give me the tradition of Rebirth, setting it forth in speech or in the secret way. I know not, O Thrice-greatest one, from out what matter and what womb Man comes to birth, or of what seed."
>
> Hermes: "Wisdom that understands in silence — such is the matter and the womb from out which Man is born, and Truth the seed."

Citation: CH XIII.1–2, trans. Mead 1906; cf. Copenhaver 1992, pp. 49–50.

### CH XIII.7–10 — the twelve tormentors and the ten powers

The cosmological-soteriological mechanics: the twelve zodiacal "tormentors" (irrationality, grief, intemperance, lust, injustice, greed, deceit, envy, treachery, anger, rashness, and malice) which bind the embodied soul are driven out by ten divine powers (the second list above). Tat experiences this expulsion in real time during the dialogue and exclaims:

> Tat: "Father, I see the Universe and I see myself in Mind."
>
> Hermes: "This is Rebirth, my son — to be no longer fixed in body subject to three dimensions; because of this the Sermon on Rebirth, on which I did not comment — that no man might be told as a slanderer, and the God in part be slandered."

Citation: CH XIII.13–14, trans. Mead 1906; cf. Copenhaver 1992, p. 53.

### CH XIII.16–20 — the secret hymn

The dialogue closes with a hymn that the reborn Tat now joins. The hymn is striking for its triadic-monotheist cadence:

> Hermes: "Sing then with reverence, O Tat: All Nature in the cosmos give ear to this my hymn. Open, O Earth! Let every bolt of the Abyss be drawn for me. Stir not, ye Trees! I am about to hymn creation's Lord, both All and One. Ye Heavens open and ye Winds stay still; let God's deathless Sphere receive my word!"
>
> "I am about to sing creation's Lord, the All, the One. Open, ye Heavens; ye Winds, be still; let God's deathless Sphere receive my word!"
>
> "Holy art Thou, O Father of all that comes to be... Ye Powers that are within me, hymn the One and All; sing with my Will, Powers all that are within me. O blessed Gnosis, by thee illumined, hymning through thee the Light that mind alone can see, I joy in joy of Mind."

Citation: CH XIII.17–18, trans. Mead 1906; cf. Copenhaver 1992, pp. 55–57.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Roman Egypt, second to third century. Hadrianic to early-Severan period; Alexandria as the great syncretic capital.
- **Religious milieu.** Greco-Egyptian initiatory religion; the dialogue is set "on a mountain" — Mahé and Fowden read this against Egyptian temple-mountain (Hathor at Dendera, Thoth at Hermopolis) initiatory geography. Contemporary with the formation of [[tradition-christianity-canonical|canonical Christianity]], with whom it shares vocabulary — but the surviving Christian-Hermetic citation chain runs the *other* way (Lactantius and Augustine cite Hermes, not vice versa).
- **Who was behind it.** Egyptian-priestly intellectuals writing in Greek for an initiated audience. The teacher-disciple frame (Hermes-Tat) is an Egyptian *sebayet* ("instruction") genre form translated into Greek dialogue.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Byzantine transmission; Cosimo de' Medici acquires Greek mss 1460; Ficino translates 1471 (his Latin title *Sermo arcanus de regeneratione* — "Secret Discourse on Regeneration"); Casaubon 1614 places it in late antiquity.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hermetic-rebirth]] — the central doctrine: *palingenesia* as ontological transformation, not metaphor. The disciple becomes a different *kind* of being.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — explicit: "by thee illumined, hymning through thee the Light that mind alone can see."
- [[mystery-initiation]] — the dialogue is staged as an initiation; Tat experiences rebirth *during* the conversation.
- [[hermetic-piety]] — the closing hymn is the Hermetic *eusebeia* exemplar; Festugière calls it "le plus beau morceau de la spiritualité hermétique."
- [[apotheosis]] — the reborn becomes "child of God" (*theou pais*) and joins the divine chorus.
- [[soul-as-spark]] — the divine powers replacing the tormentors are not added to the soul but *uncovered* — the divinity was already there, occluded by the zodiacal vices.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] — *commentary-on*: CH XIII explicitly refers back to a promise made in CH I about future revelation of the rebirth secret. Internal Hermetic corpus cross-reference.
- → [[phase-4-013-discourse-on-the-eighth-and-ninth]] — *parallel-motif*: same initiatory genre (NHC VI,6 enacts a *visionary ascent*; CH XIII enacts a *rebirth*). Mahé 1982 reads them as cognate ritual transcripts of two adjacent Hermetic stages.
- → [[phase-4-012-asclepius]] — *shared-milieu*: the Latin Asclepius's "telesphoros" anthropology presupposes a doctrine of human deification compatible with CH XIII's rebirth.
- → [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — *parallel-motif*: John 3:3–8 (Jesus to Nicodemus: "Truly, truly, I say to you, unless one is born again [*gennēthē anōthen*] he cannot see the kingdom of God") is the locus classicus for Christian rebirth; the underlying *anagennēsis* / *palingenesia* vocabulary overlaps. Tröger 1971 documents the parallels and concludes against direct dependence in either direction; Dodd 1935 argues for shared Hellenistic-Jewish substrate. **Christian rebirth is not derived from Hermes, nor vice versa, but both arise in the same Greco-Roman religious-philosophical milieu — making CH XIII the single most useful comparandum for understanding the Johannine rebirth idiom in its non-Christian context.**
- → [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — *parallel-motif*: 2 Corinthians 5:17 ("if anyone is in Christ, he is a *new creation* — *kainē ktisis* — the old has passed away"); Galatians 6:15; Titus 3:5 (*loutron palingenesias* — "washing of regeneration"). The Pauline regeneration vocabulary is closer to the Hermetic than the Johannine — Tröger 1971 documents the lexical overlap.
- → [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]] — *parallel-motif*: Philo's *kainē genesis* and *anagennēthēnai* in *On the Special Laws* and *On the Cherubim*; the Alexandrian Hellenistic-Jewish substrate behind both Hermetic and Christian regeneration vocabulary (Runia 1993).
- → [[justin-martyr]] — *parallel-motif*: Justin's *1 Apology* 61 on baptismal regeneration uses *anagennēsis*; Justin's catechetical context is the closest Christian institutional setting that articulates the Hermetic-style rebirth-as-initiation parallel.
- → [[origen]] — *commentary-on* (indirect): Origen does not cite CH XIII by name but his *Commentary on John* engages Johannine rebirth via the same Alexandrian Platonist-philosophical vocabulary the Hermetica use.
- → [[lactantius]] — *direct-quote*: Lactantius's *Divine Institutes* (early 4th c.) cites Hermes Trismegistus 21+ times as a pagan-prophet predecessor; the Christian-Hermetic transmission edge for which CH XIII's vocabulary is one of the load-bearing pieces.
- → [[phase-6-001-ficino-pimander]] — *manuscript-transmission*: Ficino's 1471 Latin renders this as *Sermo arcanus de regeneratione*; widely read by Renaissance Christian readers as harmonizable with patristic regeneration doctrine.

## Disputes
- **Christian dependence?** 19th-century Reitzenstein argued Hermetic rebirth was Christianity-influenced. Tröger 1971, Festugière, and modern consensus reject this: shared Hellenistic-Jewish-Egyptian milieu, not direct dependence either way.
- **Genuine ritual or literary fiction?** Mahé 1982 (against Festugière) reads CH XIII as a literary transcript of a real Hermetic initiatory practice; Fowden 1986 supports this. Older view (purely literary fiction) is now minority.
- **Date.** Most place it 100–300 CE. Some place it as late as 300 (the systematizing-of-the-Corpus stage); Mahé argues for an Egyptian-priestly-initiation kernel earlier than the Greek redaction.
