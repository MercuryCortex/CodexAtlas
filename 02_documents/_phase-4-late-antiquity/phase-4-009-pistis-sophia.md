# Pistis Sophia

## What it is
A long Coptic Gnostic post-resurrection dialogue — Jesus, 11 years after the resurrection, returns to teach the disciples (notably Mary Magdalene, who is the dominant interlocutor) the mysteries of the Pleroma, the descent and repentance of the aeon Pistis Sophia, the soul's ascent through hostile powers, and the elaborate sacraments required to escape rebirth. Pistis Sophia falls into the chaos when the lion-faced power deceives her with false light; she repents in 13 long laments; Jesus ascends and rescues her. The astrological-sacramental sections in Book 4 read as practical magic / theurgy.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Late Egyptian Gnosticism, syncretized with astrology and Egyptian magical praxis (the *Books of Jeu* in the Bruce Codex belong to the same milieu).
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous; the elaborate ritual machinery suggests a Gnostic community with developed sacramental and astrological practice, not a purely intellectual school.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The Askew Codex is the *earliest major Gnostic text* to surface in Europe — purchased 1785, 160 years before Nag Hammadi, and was for that century-and-a-half nearly the only direct primary access to a Gnostic system (alongside the Bruce Codex).

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-feminine]] — Pistis Sophia's fall and redemption is the spiritual narrative.
- [[psychopomp]] — soul-ascent through astrological powers.
- [[mystery-initiation]] — elaborate sacraments (the three baptisms; the mystery of the ineffable name).
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — combined with sacramental practice; gnosis without ritual is insufficient here.
