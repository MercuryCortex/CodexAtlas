# Mashafa Kidan (Testament of Our Lord)

## What it is
The **Mashafa Kidan** (Geʼez "Book of the Covenant"; Latin *Testamentum Domini Nostri Iesu Christi*) is an early-Christian apocalyptic-liturgical pseudepigraphon, probably composed in Syriac in the 4th–5th centuries CE, framed as the *post-resurrection teaching of the Risen Christ to the apostles*. The text combines (a) an *apocalyptic discourse* by the Risen Christ on the signs of the end-times and the eschatological judgment, with (b) a detailed *church-order* section codifying liturgy, baptism, eucharist, ordinations of bishops/presbyters/deacons/deaconesses, the role of widows and virgins, prayers, and ecclesiastical discipline. The combination of *apocalypse* + *church-order* in a single pseudepigraphal frame is distinctive of late-antique Syrian Christianity.

The text is **canonical scripture in [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]]** (as part of the broader 81-book biblical canon), making it one of the texts whose continuous-canonical-scriptural status is unique (or near-unique) to Ethiopian Christianity. It is also held authoritatively (though not strictly canonical) in [[tradition-syriac-orthodox]] tradition.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-antique Christian Roman East. The text reflects a Syrian-Antiochene Christian church increasingly elaborate in ecclesiastical structure and apocalyptic-eschatological expectation.
- **Religious milieu.** Within the broader family of late-antique Christian church-order pseudepigrapha (Didache ~100 CE; Apostolic Tradition of Hippolytus ~215 CE; Apostolic Constitutions ~380 CE; Apostolic Canons; the *Didascalia Apostolorum*). The Mashafa Kidan is a later expansion of this genre with the distinctive added apocalyptic-frame and the pseudonymous-Risen-Christ-narrator device.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous Syrian Christian compiler; the pseudonymous-Christ frame is a 4th–5th-c. Christian apocryphal-literary device. The Ethiopian Geʼez recension is the most-developed extant form.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Ethiopian monastic-liturgical use. Western scholarly recovery began with Rahmani's 1899 Syriac edition and Cooper-Maclean's 1902 English translation.

## Themes and motifs
- [[apocalyptic-revelation]] — Christ's eschatological discourse to the apostles.
- [[ecclesial-authority]] — detailed church-order regulations.
- [[ethiopic-canon-broad]] — among the distinctive Ethiopian-canonical additions to the NT side of the broader canon.
- [[liturgical-calendar]] — sacramental and liturgical regulations.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-061-didache]] — *parallel-form*: the foundational ancestor of the church-order genre.
- → Apostolic Constitutions (~380 CE Syrian) — *closely-related*: the Mashafa Kidan is structurally cognate with this Syrian-Christian church-order compilation; shared textual material in some passages.
- → [[phase-4-084-ethiopian-sinodos]] — *constitutive-component*: the Mashafa Kidan is sometimes grouped with the Sinodos as part of the Ethiopian church-order corpus.
- → [[phase-4-065-shepherd-of-hermas]] — *parallel-form*: another canonical-Ethiopian early-Christian text with apocalyptic + ecclesial concerns.
- → [[tradition-syriac-orthodox]] — *manuscript-transmission*: Syriac origin and ongoing Syriac authority.

## Disputes
- **Date.** Most scholarship places composition between 350 and 500 CE; some scholars push earlier (4th c.) or later (early 6th c.). The relationship to the Apostolic Constitutions (~380 CE) suggests overlap or near-contemporary composition.
- **Original language.** Syriac is the consensus original; the early Ethiopian-Geʼez translation may have been mediated through Greek or Coptic.
- **Canonical status.** Ethiopian Tewahedo: canonical-scriptural. Syriac Orthodox: authoritative-liturgical but not strictly canonical. Western traditions: apocryphal.
