# Gospel of Truth

## What it is
A homiletic meditation, not a sayings-gospel or a narrative gospel — the title "Gospel" here means "good news / proclamation." Roughly 40 pages of dense, lyrical prose unfolding a Valentinian soteriology: the Father is unknowable; the Pleroma exists in ignorance of him; Error (Plane) personified arises and produces "matter in a fog"; Jesus comes as "Word from the Father" bringing knowledge that dispels Error; those who receive him return to the Father like the names written in the living Book of the Living. Distinguished from other Gnostic texts by its complete lack of the Sophia/Yaldabaoth myth; the cosmogonic drama is *epistemological* (ignorance vs. gnosis), not anthropomorphic.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Mid-second-century Rome under Antoninus Pius. Valentinus arrived in Rome ~136 from Alexandria, taught publicly, was reportedly a candidate for the bishopric and was passed over (per Tertullian, *Adv. Val.* 4).
- **Religious milieu.** Valentinian Christianity is the most intellectually sophisticated strand of second-century Christianity; it shares scriptures, Eucharist, and many practices with proto-orthodox Christians and only later was forced into separate communion. The Gospel of Truth pre-dates the Valentinian mythology's full elaboration (Ptolemaeus, Heracleon) — it reads almost as a Christian Platonist meditation that *implies* but doesn't narrate the myth.
- **Who was behind it.** Irenaeus (*Adv. Haer.* 3.11.9) notes that the Valentinians use a *Gospel of Truth* "different from those of the apostles" — almost certainly this text. Attribution to Valentinus himself is held by Quispel, Standaert, Thomassen against doubters who see it as later-school.
- **Discovery / transmission.** NHC I (the Jung Codex) was purchased by the C.G. Jung Institute in Zurich in 1952 (hence the name) before being returned to Egypt; the Gospel of Truth was its third tractate. Editio princeps by Malinine, Puech, Quispel, and van Unnik 1956.

## Themes and motifs
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — the most refined statement: salvation is the dispelling of ignorance by the Word; Jesus "becomes a fruit of the knowledge of the Father" — eating the fruit returns the eater.
- [[hidden-god]] — the Father is "incomprehensible, inconceivable, who is superior to every thought"; apophatic theology in early Christian register.
- [[the-pleroma]] — implied throughout; the aeons "lie within him" and seek him.
- [[fall-of-humanity]] — recoded as forgetfulness: humans are "as those whom sleep has overcome"; redemption is awakening into self-knowledge.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — *direct-influence* / *parallel-motif*: the Logos/Word Christology, the "I am" register, the light-coming-into-the-world frame all derive directly from Johannine grammar. The Gospel of Truth may be the earliest non-canonical text to do extended *exegesis of John*.
- → [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — *direct-influence*: Pauline categories of life/death, faith, and "the will of the Father" pervade; Valentinian exegesis of Paul is well-documented (Pagels, *The Gnostic Paul*).
- → [[phase-4-004-gospel-of-philip]] — *shared-milieu* (Valentinian corpus): same theological grammar; Philip is more ritually focused, Truth more meditative.
- → [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]] — *parallel-motif* (Gnostic salvation by knowledge) but *not* shared mythology; Truth presupposes the Pleroma without narrating Sophia's fall.
- → [[phase-4-023-irenaeus-against-heresies]] — *polemic-against* (reverse): Irenaeus 3.11.9 names the Gospel of Truth specifically as a Valentinian product to be rejected; one of the few cases where a heresiologist names the Nag Hammadi text he attacks.

## Disputes
- **Authorship by Valentinus.** Standaert, Quispel, Thomassen for; some (Williams) prefer "by Valentinus or by a very close disciple working in his lifetime." The text's literary quality is high enough that the question matters.
- **Genre.** Homily? Meditation? Festal address? No consensus, but most reject calling it a "gospel" in the Synoptic sense.
- **Relation to Sophia myth.** Whether the text *assumes* the developed Sophia-fall myth (and just doesn't narrate it) or *predates* its full elaboration. Thomassen: assumes it. Standaert: predates.
