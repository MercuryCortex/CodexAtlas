# Mandaean Book of John

## What it is
A late-antique / early-Islamic-period collection of 76 chapters of Mandaean teaching and narrative, centered on John the Baptist (Yahya / Yuhana) but ranging across mythological, cosmological, and ethical topics. John is presented as the great Mandaean prophet — opposed to Jesus, whom the text calls a false prophet who deceived people about the true baptism. The work also contains famous mythological materials including the descent of the redeemer Manda d-Hayyi into the lower world, the lament of the soul exiled in matter, and accounts of Ruha (the spirit of error/seduction).

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** The Book of John was probably redacted into its current form in the early Islamic period (~7th–8th century), when Mandaeans had to negotiate a religious identity under the dhimmī system. The emphasis on John the Baptist as *the* prophet positioned Mandaeans as a "people of the book" with their own prophet — a category Islam recognized for the *Sabians* (often identified with the Mandaeans in Islamic sources).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Mandaean manuscript tradition; Lidzbarski 1915 is the foundational Western scholarly edition.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-feminine]] — Ruha's ambivalent role; the female counterparts (uthras).
- [[soul-as-spark]] — the soul lamenting its imprisonment.
- [[mystery-initiation]] — baptism (masbuta) and the priestly initiation tradition.
