# Apocryphon of John

## What it is
A revelation-dialogue: the risen Jesus appears to John son of Zebedee after the crucifixion, mourning at the Temple, and discloses a complete cosmogony, anthropology, and soteriology. The text falls into four parts: (1) negative-theological description of the unknowable Monad and its self-unfolding into the Pleroma of aeons (Barbelo, Autogenes, the four Luminaries); (2) the fall of Sophia, who without consort emanates the misshapen Yaldabaoth — the ignorant demiurge who creates the material cosmos and the seven planetary archons, declaring "I am God and there is no other" (a quotation that brutally inverts Isaiah 45:5); (3) a rewriting of Genesis 1–7 in which the OT creator is unmasked as Yaldabaoth, Adam's spirit is a stolen spark from Sophia, and Eve and the Serpent become bearers of gnosis against the archons; (4) a soteriology in which Pronoia descends three times to awaken the sleeping divine spark. The long recension ends with the Pronoia hymn.

## Primary text — selected excerpts

The *Apocryphon of John* was published only after the Nag Hammadi find and the 1955 Berlin Codex publication; no truly public-domain English translation exists. The excerpts below are short fair-use selections, rendered with reference to the standard scholarly translations — Frederik Wisse's translation in *The Nag Hammadi Library* (Robinson ed., 1977), Bentley Layton's *The Gnostic Scriptures* (1987), and Marvin Meyer's *Nag Hammadi Scriptures* (2007) — and following the Waldstein-Wisse 1995 synoptic edition for codex/line references. The long recension (NHC II,1) is cited unless noted.

### The negative theology of the Monad (II,1 2.26–4.10)

The opening theology is one of the most rigorous apophatic passages of late antiquity, denying every predicate of the highest God even as it strains to name him. The structure is recognizably Middle Platonist (compare Plotinus on the One, written some decades later); the Monad's self-contemplation produces Barbelo, the first thought.

> The Monad is a monarchy with nothing above it. It is the God and Father of everything, the invisible one who is above everything, who is imperishable, who is in pure light into which no eye can look. He is the invisible Spirit. It is not right to think of him as a god, or something similar. For he is more than a god, since nothing is above him, and nothing rules over him. He is not in anything inferior to him, since everything exists in him. For it is he who establishes himself...
>
> He is illimitable, since there is no one prior to him to set limits to him. He is unsearchable, since there is no one prior to him to examine him. He is immeasurable, since there was no one prior to him to measure him. He is invisible, since no one saw him. He is eternal, since he exists eternally. He is ineffable, since no one was able to comprehend him to speak about him.

Citation: *Apocryphon of John*, NHC II,1 2.26–4.5; rendered with reference to Wisse in Robinson 1977 and Meyer 2007; Waldstein-Wisse 1995 synopsis.

### The fall of Sophia and the production of Yaldabaoth (II,1 9.25–10.30)

The originating catastrophe of Sethian cosmology. Sophia ("Wisdom"), the youngest aeon of the Pleroma, conceives a thought *without her consort's consent* and gives birth to a misshapen offspring: Yaldabaoth, the lion-faced serpent-shaped demiurge who is the cause of all subsequent cosmic disorder.

> Now Sophia, who is the wisdom of insight and who constitutes an aeon, conceived a thought from herself, with the reflection of the invisible Spirit and Foreknowledge. She wanted to bring forth a likeness out of herself without the consent of the Spirit — he had not approved — and without her partner and without his consideration...
>
> And she brought it forth. And because of the invincible power which is in her, her thought did not remain idle. And something came out of her which was imperfect and different from her appearance, because she had created it without her consort. And it was dissimilar to the likeness of its mother, for it has another form. When she saw the consequences of her desire, it had changed into the form of a lion-faced serpent. Its eyes were like lightning fires which flash. She cast it away from her, outside that place, that no one of the immortal ones might see it, for she had created it in ignorance. And she surrounded it with a luminous cloud, and she placed a throne in the middle of the cloud that no one might see it except the Holy Spirit who is called the mother of the living. And she called his name Yaldabaoth.

Citation: *Apocryphon of John*, NHC II,1 9.25–10.20; rendered with reference to Wisse and Meyer 2007.

### Yaldabaoth's boast — the inversion of Isaiah 45:5 (II,1 11.18–22 / 13.5–10)

The text's most iconic moment: Yaldabaoth, ignorant of the Pleroma above him, proclaims himself the sole god — citing what is, for any Hebrew Bible reader, a divine self-declaration from Isaiah and Deuteronomy. The recoding marks the entire Gnostic project: when the demiurge claims to be the only god, that very claim becomes proof of his blindness.

> And when he saw the creation which surrounds him and the multitude of the angels around him which had come forth from him, he said to them, "I am a jealous god, and there is no other god beside me." But by announcing this he indicated to the angels who attended him that there exists another god. For if there were no other one, of whom would he be jealous?

Citation: *Apocryphon of John*, NHC II,1 13.5–13 (parallel BG 44.9–15); rendered with reference to Wisse and Meyer 2007. The phrase echoes Isaiah 45:5–6, 46:9, and Exodus 20:5.

### The creation and animation of Adam (II,1 15.1–19.10, selection)

The archons fashion Adam after the heavenly Image they have glimpsed but cannot possess. The body is a corpse until — by a trick prompted from above — Yaldabaoth blows his stolen spirit into Adam, who thereby becomes more luminous than his makers.

> And the powers began [to create]: the first one, goodness, created a bone-soul; the second, providence, created a sinew-soul; the third, divinity, created a flesh-soul; the fourth, lordship, created a marrow-soul; the fifth, kingdom, created a blood-soul; the sixth, envy, created a skin-soul; the seventh, understanding, created a hair-soul... And [their] entire body [was] put together by them, and their angels...
>
> And the body moved and gained strength, and it was luminous. And in that moment the rest of the powers became jealous, because he had come into being through all of them and they had given their power to the man, and his intelligence was greater than that of those who had made him, and greater than that of the chief archon.

Citation: *Apocryphon of John*, NHC II,1 15.13–20.10 (selection); rendered with reference to Wisse and Meyer 2007.

### The Pronoia hymn — closing of the long recension (II,1 30.11–31.27)

The long recension closes with a first-person hymn in the voice of Pronoia ("Forethought") — she descends three times into the world of darkness to awaken the sleeping divine spark, the third descent reaching the deepest prison. The hymn is essentially the same material as *Trimorphic Protennoia*.

> I am the Pronoia of the pure light; I am the thought of the virginal Spirit, who raises you up to the honored place. Arise and remember that it is you who hearkened, and follow your root, which is I, the merciful one, and guard yourself against the angels of poverty and the demons of chaos and all those who ensnare you, and beware of the deep sleep and the enclosure of the inside of Hades.
>
> And I raised him up and sealed him in the light of the water with five seals, in order that death might not have power over him from this time on.

Citation: *Apocryphon of John*, NHC II,1 31.11–25 (long recension only); rendered with reference to Wisse and Meyer 2007.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Antonine and Severan Roman empire; Christianity is illegal but growing; intra-Christian theological warfare is intense.
- **Religious milieu.** The text is the *flagship* of what scholars now call Sethian Gnosticism. It rewrites Genesis using Platonist machinery (the Monad and its emanations look very like Numenius and Middle Platonist hypostatic schemes) and inverts the moral valuation of the OT creator. Whether this is "Christian theology of Genesis" (King) or "Jewish heretical inversion later Christianized" (older Pearson, partly Stroumsa) is the live debate.
- **Who was behind it.** A Sethian school; attribution to John son of Zebedee is pseudepigraphic. Irenaeus describes a "Barbeloite" group in Gaul ~180 whose myth is recognizably ApocJohn's core — proving the text or its source predates 180.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Three copies in the Nag Hammadi library (1945) plus the Berlin Codex 8502 (acquired by Carl Reinhardt in Egypt 1896, identified by Carl Schmidt; not published until Walter Till's 1955 edition). Together the four witnesses make this the *best-attested* Gnostic text from antiquity.

## Themes and motifs
- [[demiurge]] — the defining text: Yaldabaoth is the malevolent / ignorant craftsman, identified with the Genesis creator. Sets the template for all later demiurge speculation.
- [[divine-feminine]] — Sophia's tragic emanation drives the entire plot; Barbelo as the "first thought" of the Monad; Pronoia as the saving descender.
- [[dualism-spirit-matter]] — explicit and developed: material cosmos is a flawed copy fabricated by an ignorant power; the spirit-spark in human beings is foreign to it.
- [[soul-as-spark]] — Adam's "spirit" (pneuma) is divine residue stolen from Sophia; awakening it is salvation.
- [[fall-of-humanity]] — completely re-coded: the Fall is the fall of *Sophia*, not Eve; Eden's Serpent is a revealer of gnosis, not a tempter.

## Disputes
- **Origin.** Jewish-then-Christianized (Pearson, MacRae) vs. Christian-from-the-start (King, Williams). Hinges on whether the Christian frame (John + risen Jesus) is original or a later baptism of a Jewish revelation.
- **The Yaldabaoth–Yahweh identification.** Unambiguous in the text; whether the polemic is *anti-Jewish* or *anti-imperial-orthodox* in intent is contested. Williams (1996, *Rethinking "Gnosticism"*) argues against reading these texts as anti-Semitism.
- **Sethian as a coherent category.** Schenke proposed it in 1974; Williams (1996) and King (2003) have challenged "Gnosticism" itself as a useful category. Most still find Sethian useful for the ApocJohn cluster specifically.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Three vault documents in direct documented debate:** Plotinus *Enneads* II.9 attacks "Zostrianos, Allogenes, and others" — texts circulating in Plotinus' Roman circle; Irenaeus *Adv. Haer.* 1.29 (~180 CE) paraphrases the Barbeloite myth precisely in order to refute it; and the *Apocryphon of John* itself (NHC II,1) is the primary Sethian text both are attacking. The vault holds all three: the attacked document, the proto-orthodox refutation, and the philosophical-Platonist refutation. This three-way debate (~180–270 CE) is the most internally documentable theological controversy in the vault.
- **Yaldabaoth as the sharpest ancient critique of monotheism:** the ApocJohn's core move — quoting Isaiah 45:5 ("I am God and there is no other") *in Yaldabaoth's voice* as evidence of the creator's *ignorance* — turns the monotheist's proof-text into a confession of delusion. This is philosophically precise: the problem-of-evil is solved by relocating it to the creative act itself. Plato's *Timaeus* provides the demiurge; the ApocJohn provides the inversion; Plotinus provides the defense of the original; three texts, three positions, fully triangulated in the vault.
- **Barbelo/Pronoia ↔ Shekhinah ↔ Sophia ↔ Isis — the divine-feminine descent cluster:** Pronoia descends three times into the world of darkness to awaken the divine spark, is sealed in the water of five seals. Structurally: Isis gathering Osiris' scattered body; Shekhinah in Zoharic exile separated from Tiferet; Sophia's fall and recovery in Valentinian Pleroma. Four traditions (Egyptian, Jewish Kabbalist, Sethian Gnostic, Valentinian Gnostic) articulate the salvific drama as a *feminine divine figure's descent and recovery operation*. This is the vault's richest divine-feminine narrative cluster.
- **Post-mortem archon-navigation ↔ Tibetan Bardo ↔ Mithraic planetary ladder ↔ Hermetic seven spheres:** the soul's post-death journey through hostile archon-spheres in ApocJohn maps onto the Tibetan *Bardo Thodol*'s navigation through wrathful deities, the Mithraic seven-gate ladder (*Celsus in Origen, CC* 6.22), and CH I's seven-sphere ascent. This cross-tradition post-mortem-navigation cluster is one of the vault's most geographically and chronologically dispersed structural parallels — spanning Egypt, Syria, Babylon, and Tibet within 1,000 years.
