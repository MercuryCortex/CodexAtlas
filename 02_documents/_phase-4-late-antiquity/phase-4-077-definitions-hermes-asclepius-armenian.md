# Definitions of Hermes Trismegistus to Asclepius (Armenian)

## What it is
A collection of 10 sets of short gnomic propositions — *horoi* in Greek, *sahmank'* in Armenian — attributed to Hermes Trismegistus instructing Asclepius. Each "definition" is a single sentence or aphorism; together the ~123 propositions constitute a kind of **Hermetic catechism** covering: the unknowability of God, the structure of the cosmos, the nature of soul and body, the relation between intelligible and sensible reality, the principle that "as above, so below," human deification through gnosis, the proper religious practice (*eusebeia*), and the relation of man to god, animal, and matter. The format is closer to philosophical *gnomologia* (e.g., the *Sentences of Sextus*, the *Pythagorean Sentences*) than to the dramatic dialogue form of the Greek *Corpus Hermeticum* — and Mahé's central argument is that this *gnomic / catechetical* register is the **earlier, pre-Corpus** form of Hermetic teaching, with the dramatic dialogues of the Corpus a later literary development.

The text survives only in Armenian, in 10 manuscripts of a Christian monastic compilation (the *Galatk' Asorwoc'*) from the 13th century onward. The Greek Vorlage is lost. Cross-citations from the 5th-c. Christian patriarch [[cyril-of-alexandria]] (in his *Contra Julianum*) and from John of Stobi confirm that a Greek Hermetic *Definitions* circulated in the late-antique Mediterranean world; the Armenian preserves this lost Greek collection (probably via a Syriac intermediary in the 6th–8th c., during the early period of Armenian-church engagement with Syriac Christian and pagan literature).

## Primary text — selected Definitions

The selections use Salaman/van Oyen/Wharton/Mahé 2000 for English, checked against Mahé's 1982 French. Numbering follows Mahé's edition (Set Number / Definition Number).

### DH I.1–4 — God, cosmos, man

> 1. God is intelligible to himself; the cosmos is intelligible to itself; man is intelligible to himself.
> 2. God is in himself; the cosmos is in God; man is in the cosmos.
> 3. The cosmos is the image of God; man is the image of the cosmos.
> 4. The world is one through itself; the soul is one through itself; man is one through himself.

Citation: DH I.1–4, trans. Mahé/Salaman 2000, p. 105; cf. Mahé 1982, p. 379.

### DH III.4 — the famous correspondence statement

> The world is the visible god, man is the invisible cosmos.

Citation: DH III.4, trans. Mahé/Salaman 2000, p. 109. **This is one of the closest pre-Renaissance attestations of the "as above, so below" formula independent of the Tabula Smaragdina (which itself surfaces later in Arabic, ~6th–9th c.).**

### DH V.1 — the unknowability of God

> God is not Mind, but the cause of Mind being; not Spirit, but the cause of Spirit being; not Light, but the cause of Light being.

Citation: DH V.1, trans. Mahé/Salaman 2000, p. 114; cf. Mahé 1982, p. 391. **The grammar — *not X, but the cause of X being* — is the apophatic-causal formula later canonized in pseudo-Dionysian Christian apophasis (*The Mystical Theology* Ch. 5). The Definitions provide an explicit pre-Dionysian Greek-Hermetic source for this formula.**

### DH IX.5 — humanity as cosmic mediator

> Heaven is the channel of immortality; earth is the channel of mortality. The cosmos is the channel of generation; God is the channel of being. Man is the channel of nothing — but he becomes the channel of all by knowing himself.

Citation: DH IX.5 (paraphrase), trans. Mahé/Salaman 2000, p. 124.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** *Origin*: Roman Egypt 1st–3rd c. CE (alongside the rest of the Hermetica). *Preservation*: medieval Armenia under successive Bagratid, Cilician, and post-Cilician monastic-school conditions; the *Galatk' Asorwoc'* compilation is a high-medieval Armenian-monastic teaching anthology.
- **Religious milieu.** Original: Greco-Egyptian Hermetic schoolroom; gnomic *horoi* were the basic teaching unit before being expanded into dialogues. Preservation: Armenian Apostolic Christian monastic education, where pagan gnomic literature was rebadged as ethical-cosmological *paideia* (the same impulse that made Stobaeus copy pagan philosophy in Macedonia).
- **Who was behind it.** *Origin*: Egyptian-priestly Hermetic teachers writing for initiates. *Preservation*: Armenian Christian monastic compilers — the same milieu that translated and preserved many late-antique Greek pagan philosophical texts (Aristotelian commentary tradition, etc.) for Armenian Christian theological education.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Friedrich Conybeare 1895 noticed the Armenian Hermetic material but did not identify it; **Jean-Pierre Mahé in 1956–1982 identified the *Sahmank'* as the lost Greek Hermetic *Definitions* known from cross-citations in Cyril of Alexandria and Stobaeus**, and published the editio princeps with critical apparatus in 1982. This is — alongside the Nag Hammadi Coptic Hermetica recovered in 1945 — one of the two great 20th-century textual additions to the Hermetic corpus.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hermetic-piety]] — *eusebeia* as the practical orientation; many of the Definitions are practical-religious propositions on how to live in cosmos.
- [[hermetic-cosmogony]] — compressed schematic cosmology (God / cosmos / man as triadic).
- [[as-above-so-below]] — DH III.4 ("the world is the visible god, man is the invisible cosmos") is one of the clearest single attestations.
- [[hermetic-correspondences]] — pervasive: the relation of intelligible / sensible, divine / cosmic / human at every level.
- [[microcosm-macrocosm]] — DH I.3 ("the cosmos is the image of God; man is the image of the cosmos") is the locus classicus.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — DH IX.5 ("man is the channel of nothing, but he becomes the channel of all by knowing himself") is the Hermetic anthropology in compressed form.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] — *parallel-motif* + *shared-corpus*: the Definitions are a sister-form of Hermetic teaching to the dialogues of the Greek Corpus; Mahé argues they are *earlier* in compositional layer than CH I.
- → [[phase-4-012-asclepius]] — *parallel-motif* + *shared-corpus*: the Definitions are addressed to Asclepius (as the Latin Asclepius is); they preserve material thematically continuous with the Latin Asclepius.
- → [[phase-4-073-tabula-smaragdina]] — *parallel-motif*: DH III.4 ("the world is the visible god; man is the invisible cosmos") is **the most plausible Greek antecedent of the Tabula Smaragdina's "that which is above is from that which is below, and that which is below is from that which is above" formula**. The two texts are not in direct manuscript-genealogical relation — the Tabula surfaces in Arabic ~6th–9th c., the Definitions in Armenian ~13th c. but going back to a Greek 1st–3rd c. Vorlage — but both descend from the same Hermetic-correspondences tradition. Mahé 1982 makes this comparative case explicitly.
- → [[phase-4-013-discourse-on-the-eighth-and-ninth]] (NHC VI,6) — *shared-corpus*: both are non-Corpus Hermetic survivals (Coptic + Armenian) preserving the broader Hermetic literature.
- → pseudo-Dionysius (*The Mystical Theology*, ~500) — *parallel-motif*: DH V.1's apophatic-causal formula ("not X, but the cause of X being") prefigures the canonical pseudo-Dionysian apophatic formula. Whether pseudo-Dionysius knew Hermetic Definitions specifically is unprovable, but the Greek formula is attested in this Hermetic context centuries before pseudo-Dionysius.
- → [[cyril-of-alexandria]] *Contra Julianum* I (433 CE) — *direct-quote*: Cyril cites Hermes Trismegistus in defense of Christian theology; the citations overlap textually with the Armenian Definitions, providing the cross-confirmation Mahé used to identify the *Sahmank'* as the lost Greek Hermetic *Definitions*.
- → John of Stobi (*Anthologion*, c.500) — *direct-quote*: Stobaeus's *Hermetic Excerpts* include sentences also attested in the Armenian Definitions.
- → [[lactantius]] *Divine Institutes* — *direct-quote*: Lactantius cites Hermes Trismegistus 21+ times; some citations overlap material in the Definitions and the Asclepius.

## Disputes
- **Identification with the lost Greek Definitions.** Mahé's 1982 identification is now standard consensus (Fowden 1986, Brakke, van den Broek). A small minority of older Armenologists treated the *Sahmank'* as a genuinely Armenian Christian production with only superficial Hermetic dressing; that view is now untenable post-Mahé.
- **Date of the Greek original.** Mahé places the Greek Vorlage 100–200 CE — making the Definitions roughly contemporary with the earliest layers of CH (Poimandres). Some material may be earlier (1st c. BCE Egyptian-priestly Greek gnomic teaching).
- **Route of transmission to Armenia.** Mahé proposes a Syriac intermediary in the 6th–8th c. (when much pagan Greek philosophy entered Armenian via Syriac); direct Greek-to-Armenian translation is also possible. No surviving Syriac witnesses.
