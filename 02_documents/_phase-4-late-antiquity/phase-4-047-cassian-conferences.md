# Conferences

## What it is
Twenty-four Latin dialogues, presented as Cassian's transcripts of conversations he and his companion Germanus had with great Egyptian desert abbas during their ~15-year stay in Egypt (~385–399). Issued in three installments (1–10 by 426; 11–17 ~427; 18–24 by 429), dedicated to different Gaulish monastic patrons. Together with the earlier *Institutes* (in 12 books), the *Conferences* present a complete picture of Egyptian-monastic theory and practice for a Latin-speaking Gaulish monastic readership.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-Roman Gaul, with the Western empire crumbling; Cassian at his Marseille foundation, writing in the time of Augustine's late career.
- **Religious milieu.** Cassian transmits the Egyptian desert monastic tradition — which is itself partly Origenist-Evagrian — to a Latin readership uneasy about Origen (Jerome's anti-Origenist campaign of the 390s had poisoned the Latin reception). Cassian threads the needle: he transmits the substance of Evagrian-Origenist monastic psychology while never naming Evagrius (already a controversial figure) and translating the Greek vocabulary into safer Latin terms.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Latin transmission.

## Themes and motifs
- [[monastic-asceticism]] — the comprehensive theory: the goal (*skopos*) of monastic life is pure prayer; the end (*telos*) is the kingdom of God; the means is *puritas cordis* (purity of heart, Cassian's Latin for Evagrian *apatheia*).
- [[via-negativa]] — Conference 10 (on prayer) culminates in the "monologistic prayer" — short formula prayed continuously — that pre-figures the Jesus Prayer tradition.
- [[apokatastasis]] — Cassian's Evagrian-Origenist anthropology of soul-fall-and-restoration lies in the background of the system; he is careful not to make explicit the more controversial Origenist propositions.
- The principal practical themes: the eight *logismoi* / vices (Cassian's transmission of the Evagrian schema → the medieval Seven Deadly Sins), discretion, communal life vs. eremitism, friendship, scriptural exegesis as ascetic practice.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[evagrius-ponticus]] — *suppressed-direct-source*: extensive verbatim and paraphrastic borrowing from Evagrius (*Praktikos*, *On Prayer*, *Antirrhetikos*), without ever naming him. The eight-logismoi schema, the *praktike* / *theoria* / *theologia* progression, the meditation-method are all Evagrian. Stewart 1998: 36–47, 95–116.
- → [[antony-the-great]], [[pachomius]], and the desert tradition — *transmission-of*: the 15 abbas Cassian features are mostly historically real (Moses, Sarapion, Paphnutius, etc.); the *Conferences* are Cassian's literary construction transmitting their teaching.
- → [[benedict-of-nursia]] *Rule* — *direct-influence*: RB 73.5 enjoins reading Cassian; the Benedictine *cursus* of prayer, the eight-vices framework, the practical discretion-theme are all Cassianic.
- → Origenist Christian Platonism — *substrate-influence*: Cassian's anthropology (rational soul, fall into materiality, ascent through purification) is Platonist via Origen via Evagrius. The substrate-Platonism is preserved in the Latin tradition through Cassian even as the Greek-Origenist tradition is being suppressed by Justinianic anti-Origenism (553).
- → Pelagian / semi-Pelagian controversy — *polemic-against*: Conference 13 on grace and free will became the principal target of Prosper of Aquitaine's anti-Cassianic polemic (*Contra Collatorem*).

## Disputes
- **Authenticity of the reported dialogues.** Cassian's "15 abbas" are not transcripts; they are literary constructions transmitting Egyptian-monastic teaching through a stylized form. The substance is reliable; the dialogue-form is Cassian's literary device.
- **The semi-Pelagian question.** Conference 13 on the initial movement of the will toward God was attacked by Prosper as Pelagian. Cassian's position is more careful than the Pelagian charge admits, but it is not the strict Augustinian position.
- **Cassian's Evagrian dependence.** Quietly extensive — Stewart 1998 catalogues the textual parallels.
