# Alēthēs Logos / True Word

## What it is
A second-century **Middle Platonist philosophical polemic against Christianity** (~+178), authored by [[celsus]] (otherwise unidentifiable), the **first known systematic pagan attack on the Christian movement** from a learned philosophical perspective. The work is lost in its original form but **reconstructible to ~70–90% completeness** from [[origen]]'s ***Contra Celsum*** (~+248), which quotes Celsus line-by-line in order to refute him. The work's argumentative strategy: Christianity is a *derivative and degraded form* of older traditions (Jewish, Egyptian, Pythagorean, Platonic), socially recruited from the marginalized (slaves, women, the uneducated), philosophically incoherent (resurrection, anthropocentric providence), and politically destructive (rejecting the *patrios nomos*, the ancestral custom).

## Context
- **Date.** ~+177/+180 (late Marcus Aurelius).
- **Place.** Origen identifies Celsus tentatively with an Epicurean friend of Lucian; this seems incorrect — Celsus's positions are Platonist. Origin in Alexandria or Rome both proposed.
- **Author.** Otherwise unknown; the name is common.
- **Polemical structure.** The work opens with an *imaginary Jew* arguing against Christianity from a Jewish perspective; then transitions to Celsus's own *Platonist* arguments. The Jewish-source opening provides indirect evidence for late-2nd-c. Jewish anti-Christian polemic.

## Themes and motifs
- **Christianity-as-derivative**: borrows from Judaism (which is itself borrowed from Egypt), and degrades philosophy.
- **The Panthera tradition**: Jesus the illegitimate son of Mary and a Roman soldier named Panthera; learned magic in Egypt. The tradition is paralleled (independently?) in the [[phase-4-033-babylonian-talmud]] and Tertullian's *De spect.* 30 — suggesting a stock anti-Christian Jewish counter-narrative.
- **Anti-resurrection**: dead bodies decay; resurrection is philosophically impossible.
- **Anthropocentric vanity**: Christianity flatters humans with the conceit that God specifically cares about them.
- **Social-class critique**: Christianity attracts the marginalized — slaves, women, the uneducated. (This is sociologically valuable data, not just polemic.)
- **Patrios nomos**: ancestral custom is the framework of philosophical and political life; rejecting it is destructive.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[origen]] *Contra Celsum* — *commentary-on* (inverse): Origen preserves the *True Word* by quoting it; without his polemic the text would be lost.
- → [[porphyry]] *Against the Christians* (~+270) — *parallel-genre*: the next generation's pagan-philosophical attack.
- → [[phase-4-033-babylonian-talmud]] (the Yeshu-Pandera passages) — *parallel-source*: the Panthera tradition's transmission chain.
- → Middle Platonism — *philosophical-tradition*: Celsus's theology of the *prōton theos* aligns with Numenius-Albinus.

## Disputes
- **Identity of Celsus**: not the Epicurean Celsus addressee of Lucian.
- **Date**: ~+177/+180.
- **Reliability of Origen's quotations**: very high; Origen quotes verbatim for forensic purposes.
- **Reconstruction completeness**: Hoffmann's 1987 attempt is contested in detail but the general shape is secure.
