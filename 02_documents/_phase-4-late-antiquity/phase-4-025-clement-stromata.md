# Stromata

## What it is
Clement's "miscellanies" — seven books (a planned eighth survives only in fragments) of philosophical-theological reflections on Christian life, faith, knowledge, marriage, martyrdom, and the relationship of Christianity to Greek philosophy. Clement defends a "Christian gnostic" — by which he means the true gnostic, against the heretical (Valentinian, Basilidean) gnostics — who combines faith, philosophical learning, and ethical perfection. The work is the most sophisticated second-century engagement of Christian theology with Hellenistic philosophy.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Severan Alexandria; Clement fled the city during the persecution under Septimius Severus (~202) and never returned.
- **Religious milieu.** Alexandria as the great syncretic intellectual capital; Clement engages constructively with Greek philosophy (against Tertullian's "what has Athens to do with Jerusalem"), with Philonic biblical exegesis, and critically with Gnostic heterodoxies (Basilides, Valentinus, Carpocrates).
- **Who was behind it.** Clement, head of the Alexandrian catechetical school (succeeded by Origen).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Greek manuscript tradition.

## Themes and motifs
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — Clement *re-purposes* "gnosis" for orthodox use against the heretical gnostics; the orthodox Christian is the "true gnostic."
- [[hidden-god]] — Clement's apophatic theology absorbs Middle-Platonic negative-theological vocabulary.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-026-origen-on-first-principles]] — *direct-influence*: Origen was Clement's student or near-disciple.
- → [[phase-4-023-irenaeus-against-heresies]] — *parallel-engagement*: both confront Gnostic systems but from very different stances (Irenaeus refutes by tradition; Clement absorbs by philosophical synthesis).
- → Philo of Alexandria (Phase 3) — *direct-influence*: Clement is Philo's principal Christian inheritor.
- → Middle Platonism — *direct-influence*: Numenius, Albinus, the Platonist tradition more broadly.
