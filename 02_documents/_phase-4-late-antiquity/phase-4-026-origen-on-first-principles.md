# On First Principles

## What it is
The first systematic Christian theology — Origen's attempt to lay out the foundational Christian doctrines (God, Christ, the Holy Spirit, rational creatures, the cosmos, free will, scripture) in philosophical-theological order. Origen articulates positions that later orthodoxy would partly accept and partly condemn: the pre-existence of souls; the *apokatastasis* (final restoration of all rational creatures, including possibly the devil); allegorical interpretation of scripture in three senses (literal, moral, spiritual); the subordination of the Son to the Father (later seen as proto-Arian). The breadth and rigor of the work made it both the founding text of Christian systematic theology and the source of the long Origenist controversy.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Severan Egypt; later Caesarea Palaestinae (where Origen relocated after his conflict with Bishop Demetrius ~231).
- **Religious milieu.** Alexandria as the apex of Christian intellectual engagement with Platonism. Origen knew Numenius, Plotinus' teacher Ammonius Saccas (according to Porphyry, the two studied under the same teacher), and the Gnostic systems against which Clement had already written.
- **Who was behind it.** Origen — among the most prolific Christian writers of antiquity (Jerome credits him with ~6,000 works; certainly hundreds of volumes). His *Hexapla* (a six-column synopsis of Hebrew and Greek OT versions) and his many biblical commentaries set the agenda for Christian biblical scholarship.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Rufinus' Latin translation (~398) saved the work in compromised form. The Origenist controversies (399 in Egypt, 553 at Constantinople) condemned Origen's specific positions on pre-existence and apokatastasis.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hidden-god]] — Origen develops Middle Platonist apophatic theology in Christian register.
- [[soul-as-spark]] — pre-existence of rational souls; their fall into bodies; their return.
- [[end-times-judgment]] — but *apokatastasis*: the eventual restoration of all.

- → [[phase-4-025-clement-stromata]] — *direct-influence*: Origen's Alexandrian intellectual context.

- → Second Council of Constantinople (553) — *polemic-against* (posthumous): condemned Origen's pre-existence and apokatastasis doctrines.
