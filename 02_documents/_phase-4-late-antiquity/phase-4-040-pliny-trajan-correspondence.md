# Pliny–Trajan Correspondence on Christians (Epistulae 10.96–97)

## What it is
Two short letters from ~+112 between **[[pliny-the-younger]]** (Roman governor of Bithynia-Pontus, ~+111/+113) and **Emperor [[trajan-emperor|Trajan]]**, constituting the **earliest extant Roman administrative discussion of Christianity** and setting the legal-procedural framework for Roman handling of Christians for the next ~140 years. Ep. 10.96 is Pliny's report: he has been investigating Christians, applying test-of-sacrifice procedures, and is uncertain how to proceed. Ep. 10.97 is Trajan's brief rescript: do not seek them out; if denounced and convicted, punish them; sacrificing to the gods proves non-Christianity; anonymous denunciations are excluded.

## Context
- **Date.** ~+112, during Pliny's tenure as imperial legate to Bithynia-Pontus.
- **Author.** Pliny (the report) and Trajan (the reply, perhaps drafted by an imperial secretary).
- **Cultural context.** Pliny is acting in good faith — he is unfamiliar with the *cognitio extra ordinem* procedure for Christians and seeks imperial guidance. Trajan's reply is laconic and pragmatic.
- **Procedural significance.** Pliny describes his procedure: accused are asked if they are Christians; if they admit, asked again with threat; if they persist, executed. Christians are also tested by being asked to invoke the gods, offer wine and incense to the emperor's image, and curse Christ.

## Themes and motifs
- **The "name itself" question**: Pliny asks Trajan whether being a Christian is itself a punishable offense or only specific *flagitia* (crimes) committed by Christians. Trajan does not clearly answer — leaving the legal framework permanently incoherent.
- ***Carmen Christo quasi deo***: Pliny's earliest external attestation of Christian Sunday-morning hymnody, with Christ as the object of worship (Ep. 10.96.7).
- **Ethical practice**: Pliny reports Christians binding themselves by oath not to commit theft, adultery, fraud — a strikingly *exonerating* description.
- **Female deacons (*ministrae*)**: Pliny tortured two female slave-deacons to extract information — the earliest external attestation of female deacons in the Christian movement.
- **No Thyestean banquets / Oedipean intercourse**: Pliny finds Christian practice merely *superstitio prava et immodica* (depraved and excessive superstition), not criminal.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Trajan's legal precedent — *direct-influence*: governs Roman policy from 112 to 250.
- → [[hadrian-emperor]]'s rescript to Minucius Fundanus (~+125) — *direct-extension*: maintains Trajanic procedure.
- → [[tertullian]] *Apology* 2.6–8 — *commentary-on*: Tertullian famously exploits the logical incoherence of "don't seek them out but punish if found" — "if criminals, why not seek them out? if not criminals, why punish them?"
- → [[event-nero-persecution-64]] — *contrast*: the Pliny-Trajan procedure is *milder* than the Neronian arbitrary persecution.
- → all subsequent martyrdom narratives (Polycarp, Lyon martyrs, Perpetua) — *legal-framework*: the trial-procedure they describe is the Trajanic one.

## Disputes
- **Authenticity**: never seriously contested.
- **Date**: ~+112, mainstream.
- **Scope of Trajan's reply**: applied empire-wide or only to Pliny? Mainstream: treated as binding precedent across the Empire.
- **Pliny's information sources**: from the accused themselves, former Christians who recanted, and (per his testimony) the *libellus famosus* (informer's pamphlet).
