# Bundahishn (Book of Primordial Creation)

## What it is

The *Bundahishn* ("Original Creation" or "Primal Creation") is the principal Zoroastrian cosmogonic and cosmological text in Middle Persian (Pahlavi). It transmits, in prose commentary (*Zand*) form, the Avestan teaching on the structure of the universe, the origin of creation, the nature of time, and the eschatological end of history. The *Zand* tradition is a class of Pahlavi texts that translate and elaborate the original Avestan language scripture — the Bundahishn is the *Zand* of cosmological Avestan passages, systematized into a coherent encyclopedia of Zoroastrian cosmology.

## Versions

Two versions survive:

- **Iranian Bundahishn** (shorter; ~36 chapters): transmitted in Iranian manuscripts; first translated into English by E. W. West (1880).
- **Greater/Indian Bundahishn** (~33 longer chapters): preserved by the Parsi community in India; edited and translated by Anklesaria (1956). This is generally considered the more complete version for cosmological content.

The two versions overlap substantially but differ in chapter order and length of elaboration. The core cosmogonic and cosmological content is shared.

## Structure and content

### 1. Cosmogony: spiritual creation before material creation

Ohrmazd (Ahura Mazda) first creates the world in purely spiritual (*menok*) form — immaterial, ideal, incorruptible. Only then does he create the material (*getig*) world — visible, tangible, corruptible. This two-stage creation is distinctively Zoroastrian: the material world is not a fall or mistake; it is a necessary battleground. Ohrmazd needs the material realm to trap and defeat Ahriman (Angra Mainyu).

Creation takes 365 days (the cosmic year). Ahriman attacks and corrupts the material creation — introducing death, disease, cold, drought, and sin. This is the beginning of the mixed time (*gumezishn*) in which good and evil are intermingled in the world.

### 2. The primordial sacrifice: Gayomard and Gosh

The cosmic history of the Bundahishn pivots on two primal deaths:

- **Gayomard** (the primordial / first man) is killed by Ahriman's demonic assault. From his seed, sown in the earth, grows the first human pair: Mashya and Mashyane (the Iranian Adam and Eve). From his body come metals and minerals.
- **Gosh** (the primordial bull / the uniquely-created bull) is also killed. From his body come the animals, plants, and grains that sustain life.

This is the Zoroastrian version of the **cosmic sacrifice that generates the world** — structurally parallel to:

| Text | Primordial victim | World-generating substance |
|------|------------------|---------------------------|
| Bundahishn | Gayomard (man) + Gosh (bull) | Humans from seed; animals from body |
| Rigveda (*Purusha Sukta*) | Purusha (cosmic man) | All beings from dismembered body |
| Prose Edda (Norse) | Ymir (primordial giant) | Earth/sea/sky from body |
| Enuma Elish ([[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]]) | Tiamat (chaos-sea) | Heaven/earth from split body |

The structural logic — **the world is made from a primordial death** — appears across Indo-European traditions and is one of the most robust cross-tradition patterns in the vault.

### 3. Cosmic geography

The Bundahishn contains the most complete surviving description of Zoroastrian cosmological geography:

- **Seven continents (keshvars):** The world is divided into seven circular continental regions (keshvars), with the central region (*xwanirah*) as the human habitable world. The others are inhabited by other beings and are cut off from direct contact.
- **The cosmic mountain Hara Berezaiti** (*Alburz*): The world-axis mountain from which the sun, moon, and stars rise and set. Structurally the Zoroastrian [[axis-mundi]].
- **The Chinvat Bridge** (*Cinvat*; see [[cinvat-bridge-judgment]]): The judgment bridge crossed by souls after death; broad as a palace for the righteous, razor-thin for the wicked.
- **The cosmic tree Gaokerena**: The white *haoma* tree growing in the middle of the cosmic sea Vourukhasha. White haoma's fruit grants immortality; at the Renovation, Saoshyant will use it to resurrect the dead. Structurally parallel to Yggdrasil (Norse), the Kabbalistic Tree of Life (*Etz Chayyim*), and the Buddhist Bodhi Tree.
- **Rivers, seas, and winds** are catalogued in detail, each with a cosmological role in either sustaining creation or threatening it.

### 4. Time and eschatology: the three times

The Bundahishn articulates the Zoroastrian doctrine of three cosmic ages (*zaman*):

1. **Bundahishn** (Original Creation): the primordial state before Ahriman's attack — 3,000 years of spiritual creation.
2. **Gumezishn** (Mixture): the current age — 3,000 years of Ahriman's attack on the material world, in which good and evil are intermingled. This is the historical period of human life.
3. **Wizarishn** (Separation/Renovation): the final 3,000 years of progressive Zoroastrian triumph, culminating in **Frashkart** (the Renovation, the Final Renovation of the world). Total: **9,000 years of cosmic history.**

**Frashkart** (or *Frashokereti* in Avestan; see [[frashokereti-cosmic-renovation]]) is the Zoroastrian eschatological event: Ahriman is defeated permanently, the dead are resurrected, the molten metal river purifies all souls, and the cosmos is restored to its original *menok* (spiritual) perfection.

## Cross-tradition significance

### The dualism transmission

The Bundahishn is the clearest systematic statement of Zoroastrian cosmic **[[dualism-cosmic]]** — Ohrmazd/Ahriman, light/darkness, truth/lie (*asha/druj*). This dualism is the theological ancestor of:

- **Manichaeism**: Mani explicitly drew on Zoroastrian dualist cosmology, wrapping it in a Gnostic-Christian framework. The Manichaean Light/Darkness warfare is the Bundahishn's Ohrmazd/Ahriman conflict in new dress.
- **Gnosticism**: The evil demiurge of Gnostic texts (the creator who made the corrupt material world) is structurally Ahriman — the cosmic force that corrupts and debases material existence.
- **Catharism**: The Cathar doctrine of two principles (good God / evil creator) is the westernmost expression of the dualist transmission chain that begins in the Bundahishn.
- **Christian Satan**: The Christian doctrine of Satan as anti-cosmic adversary (not merely a rebellious angel but a genuine cosmic counter-principle in popular theology) carries the dualist imprint; the Bundahishn is the upstream source.

### The cosmic tree

The Gaokerena tree — cosmic, world-sustaining, bearing immortality fruit — mirrors:
- **Yggdrasil** (Norse): world-ash sustaining the nine worlds, with Odin's sacrifice and the cosmic mead
- **Etz Chayyim** (Kabbalah): the Tree of Life as the structure of divine emanation
- **Bodhi Tree** (Buddhism): the tree at the world-axis under which enlightenment occurs

### The eschatological template

**Frashkart (the Renovation/Universal Resurrection)** is the Zoroastrian eschatological template that influenced:
- **Jewish apocalypticism**: the resurrection of the dead and the end of history as a Zoroastrian concept transmitted to Second Temple Judaism through the Babylonian exile and the Persian-Jewish encounter (see [[phase-2-004-yasna-younger-avesta]] for the Avestan context of this transmission)
- **Christian resurrection theology**: the bodily resurrection, the last judgment, the new heaven and new earth — these are structural inheritors of Zoroastrian eschatology via Second Temple Judaism
- **Islamic judgment-day theology**: Yawm al-Din (the Day of Judgment), the Sirat bridge, the weighing of deeds, the final separation of saved and damned — each element has a Zoroastrian precursor

The transmission pathway is documented: Jewish communities in Babylonian exile (6th century BCE) were in sustained contact with Zoroastrian theology under Achaemenid Persian rule; Second Temple apocalyptic literature (1 Enoch, Daniel, 4 Ezra) shows clear Zoroastrian structural influence; early Christianity inherits from Second Temple Judaism; Islam inherits from both.
