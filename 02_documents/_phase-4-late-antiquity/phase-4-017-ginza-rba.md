# Ginza Rba

## What it is
The canonical "Great Treasure" — the principal scripture of Mandaeism, a gnostic baptizing religion that has survived continuously since late antiquity in southern Mesopotamia. The text is divided into Right Ginza (18 books, read right-to-left, treating cosmology, anthropogony, and ethics) and Left Ginza (3 books, the masiqta — the soul's ascent and the funerary liturgy, read by turning the book over). Content includes cosmogonies (multiple versions, not always reconciled), polemics against Jews, Christians, and Muslims, hymns, ethical instructions, ritual prescriptions, and the foundational myth of the descent of *manda* (gnosis) into the world.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Mandaeism crystallized in the late-antique Mesopotamian marshlands, surviving Parthian, Sasanian, Arab Muslim, Mongol, and modern Iraqi rule. Its survival has been precarious; the 21st-century Iraq War scattered the community.
- **Religious milieu.** Mandaeans are the *only* surviving Gnostic religion. Their origins are intensely debated: a western (Jordan Valley) origin among Jewish baptists who venerated John the Baptist (the Mandaean position; supported by Macuch, Rudolph, Lupieri), or an eastern Mesopotamian origin (older Lidzbarski-Säve-Söderbergh position). The Cologne Mani Codex's documentation of Elchasaite baptizing sects in the same southern Mesopotamian region strengthens the Mandaean-Elchasaite milieu argument.
- **Who was behind it.** A community of Nasoraeans (priests/initiates) and Mandaeans (laity), with a developed priestly hierarchy. The Ginza was redacted across centuries.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Unlike most Gnostic literatures, Mandaeism survives in a *living* community whose priests have copied and recopied the Ginza in unbroken succession. Western scholarly access began with Norberg's Latin edition (1815), Petermann's collation (1867), Lidzbarski's German translation (1925), and Drower's mid-20th-century fieldwork.

## Themes and motifs
- [[dualism-spirit-matter]] — the World of Light vs. the World of Darkness; the body is a prison of the soul.
- [[demiurge]] — Ptahil as the demiurge of the material world; problematic creator-figure.
- [[divine-feminine]] — Ruha as a complex feminine figure (sometimes redeemer, often the spirit of error/seduction); the female counterparts of the male Lights.
- [[soul-as-spark]] — souls are exiled portions of the World of Light.
- [[psychopomp]] — Manda d-Hayyi as the soul's guide; the Left Ginza's elaborate ascent-of-the-soul liturgy.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-018-mandaean-book-of-john]] — *shared-system*: same canon; Book of John focuses on John the Baptist material.
- → Elchasaite-Jewish-Christian baptists (attested by [[phase-4-014-cologne-mani-codex]], Hippolytus *Refutatio*) — *shared-milieu*: very likely the same religious environment in southern Mesopotamia.
- → [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]] (Sethian Gnosticism) — *parallel-motif*: similar dualism, descent of the redeemer, soul-spark; significant structural overlaps suggest shared late-antique Mesopotamian Gnostic milieu, despite different developments.
- → John the Baptist (canonical NT) — *polemic-relationship*: Mandaeans revere John as their prophet and polemicize against Jesus as a false Messiah; cf. Right Ginza Book 2 anti-Christian polemic.

## Disputes
- **Origin: Jordanian/Palestinian or Mesopotamian.** The dominant 21st-century position (Buckley, Lupieri, Häberl, McGrath) is that Mandaeism originated as a Jewish-Christian baptist movement in the Jordan Valley before migrating to Mesopotamia ~2nd century CE, drawing on John the Baptist material. Older scholarship (Lidzbarski) saw a Mesopotamian origin.
- **Date of the Ginza's redaction.** Strata range from ~200 to ~700; final redaction by the 7th century is widely accepted.
- **Connection to other Gnostic literatures.** Whether Mandaeism is part of a wider Gnostic family or a distinct development with parallel features remains debated.
