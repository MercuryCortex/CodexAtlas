# Asclepius

## What it is
A long dialogue between Hermes Trismegistus and his disciples Asclepius, Tat, and Ammon, covering the cosmos, the nature of God, the divinity of humanity ("a great miracle is man"), magic statues animated by daimones, the eschatological "Lament" prophesying the desolation of Egypt and the abandonment of its temples ("a time will come when it will seem that the Egyptians have honored the divine in vain"), and the final judgment. The Lament is among the most-quoted passages in Hermetic literature and was famously cited by Augustine to attack Hermetic theurgy (*City of God* 8.23–26).

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Late antique Egypt watching the rise of Christianity and the closure of pagan temples; the Lament is widely read as a literary response to that historical anxiety.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Latin version known throughout the Middle Ages (pseudo-Apuleius). The Coptic excerpt at NHC VI,8 (1945) is shorter but verifies the antiquity of the Lament.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hidden-god]] — apophatic / negative theology of the supreme God.
- [[creation-by-word]] — the Word and the Cosmos.
- [[end-times-judgment]] — the Lament's apocalyptic register.
- Theurgy / cult of statues — the animated statues passage is a key source for late-antique Hermetic theurgy.
