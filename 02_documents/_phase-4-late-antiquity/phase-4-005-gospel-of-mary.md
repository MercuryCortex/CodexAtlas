# Gospel of Mary

## What it is
A short revelation-dialogue and post-resurrection discourse. Two halves: (1) Jesus, after the resurrection, gives a brief discourse on matter, sin, and the law before departing — the first six pages are lost; (2) Mary Magdalene, who alone has not despaired, recounts a vision in which Jesus had taught her about the soul's ascent past four hostile powers (Darkness, Desire, Ignorance, Wrath) — again, four pages are lost in the middle. Peter and Andrew dispute Mary's authority; Levi defends her. Total surviving text is ~half of the original.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Roman provincial Egypt; second-century Christianity in formation, with active argument over apostolic authority and women's leadership.
- **Religious milieu.** The text reads as a *meta-comment* on the early-Christian conflict over who can authoritatively transmit revelation. Peter and Andrew represent the proto-orthodox tendency to dismiss visionary/revelatory authority — especially a woman's. Mary represents the charismatic-revelatory strand. The text is not Sethian, not Valentinian; it shares grammar with Gnostic ascent-of-the-soul motifs but lacks the demiurge myth.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous; very likely composed by a Christian community within which Mary Magdalene's apostolic status was taken seriously. The text *embeds* the very dispute that later silenced it.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The Berlin Codex was bought in Cairo in 1896 by Carl Reinhardt and remained largely unpublished until World War II and then Till's 1955 edition. The two Greek fragments were identified retrospectively. The text is *not* in the Nag Hammadi library — though it is in the same broad genre.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-feminine]] — Mary Magdalene as the apostle to whom Jesus reveals deepest mysteries; the structural inversion of the Petrine apostolic monopoly.
- [[psychopomp]] / soul-ascent — the surviving section describes the soul rising past the four powers; analogous to the ascent literature of later Hermetism, Sethianism, and Mithraism.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — "the Son of Man is within you. Follow after him." Salvation by interior knowledge, not by external orthodoxy.
- [[soul-as-spark]] — the soul has higher origins than matter; the powers attempt and fail to detain it.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-001-gospel-of-thomas]] — *parallel-motif*: both elevate a non-Petrine apostolic authority; Thomas 114 stages the Mary/Peter dispute differently (in Thomas, Jesus has to *defend* Mary to Peter; in Mary, Levi does).
- → [[phase-4-004-gospel-of-philip]] — *parallel-motif*: Mary Magdalene as Jesus' favored disciple / "companion."
- → [[phase-4-010-sophia-of-jesus-christ]] — *shared-milieu*: also in the Berlin Codex; same post-resurrection revelation-dialogue genre.
- → Canonical Gospels (Phase 3) — *parallel-motif* / *polemic-against*: Mary's exclusion from the apostolic core in Mark/Matthew/Luke is precisely what this text contests; the post-resurrection visions in John 20:11–18 may be a partly shared tradition.

## Disputes
- **Date.** King 2003 argues ~120 CE based on its lack of developed Gnostic mythology and engagement with the early-second-century leadership debate. Tuckett 2007 argues mid-to-late second century. Pasquier and others push toward early third. The 120–180 range here brackets the live debate.
- **"Gnostic" or not.** King and Tuckett argue *not* in any developed sense; the text precedes and stands apart from Sethian/Valentinian systems. Pagels treats it as Gnostic-adjacent.
- **The historical Mary Magdalene.** The text says nothing reliable about her; it is *evidence about second-century communities that claimed her*, not about the first-century woman.
