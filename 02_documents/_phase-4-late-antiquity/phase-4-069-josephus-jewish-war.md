# The Jewish War

## What it is
A 7-book Greek history of the **First Jewish Revolt** against Rome (+66 to +73/+74, with the fall of Masada as terminus) composed by **[[josephus|Flavius Josephus]]** ~+75/+79 in Rome under Flavian patronage. The single most important narrative source for late-Second-Temple Judaism's catastrophic end, the destruction of the Jerusalem Temple, the dispersion of the Judaean rebel factions (Sicarii, Zealots, the *John of Gischala* and *Simon bar Giora* factions), the siege of Jerusalem, and the fall of Masada. Composed by a defected Jewish aristocrat who personally commanded Jewish forces in Galilee in +66/+67, surrendered at Jotapata to Vespasian, and finished the war and the rest of his life in Roman service.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Flavian Rome under Vespasian (69–79) and Titus (79–81). The destruction of Jerusalem and the Judaea-victory triumph were *the* foundational propaganda-event of the Flavian dynasty (Arch of Titus; *Judaea Capta* coinage; the Colosseum, built with Judaean-war spoils per inscription). Josephus's *War* is officially commissioned-court-history.
- **Religious milieu.** Pre-rabbinic Judaism in shock-aftermath. The Pharisaic survivors at Yavneh under Yohanan ben Zakkai are reconstructing Judaism into post-Temple form; the apocalyptic-Zealot strands have been destroyed; Christianity is just beginning to define itself as separate from Judaism (Gospel of Mark contemporary with *War*; *Matthew* and *Luke-Acts* just after). Josephus's *War* sits exactly at this rupture.
- **Who was behind it.** Josephus himself, with **acknowledged court patronage** of Vespasian and Titus (and Vespasian's secretarial-Greek improving assistance — Josephus's Greek was non-native and a court-trained scribe likely polished the text; *Against Apion* 1.50 acknowledges this). The work was *commissioned for Greek-and-Roman elite consumption*, justifying Roman victory and (Josephus would say) *also* justifying the Jewish cause as a noble defeat at the hands of providence.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Christian transmission preserved the text. Eusebius (HE 3.5–10) treats Josephus as a near-canonical source for the destruction of Jerusalem (which Christian apologetic-theology read as divine judgment on the Jews for rejecting Christ — note Josephus himself does **not** make this connection, but later Christian readers did). Latin *Hegesippus* (4th c.), Slavonic Josephus (medieval), and *Sefer Yosippon* (10th-c. Hebrew) are major reception-tradition texts.

## Themes and motifs
- [[apologetic-historiography]] — load-bearing locus: the *War* is **the foundational text of Jewish-apologetic-historiography**, a genre Josephus essentially invents in Greek: *defending* Judaism to a Greek-Roman elite audience by writing Jewish history *in their idiom*. Cf. companion work [[phase-4-071-josephus-against-apion|Against Apion]].
- **Thucydidean historiography substrate-wire.** Load-bearing. Josephus deliberately models *War* on **Thucydides**: invented set-piece speeches (esp. Eleazar's speech at Masada in *BJ* 7), war-causation analysis (proems and digressions), plague-and-siege rhetoric, paired-speech debates. Polybius is the secondary model. Mason 2003 ch. 4 and Rajak 2002 demonstrate the Thucydidean patterning at length. **Josephus is self-consciously writing as a Greek historian; the Jewish material is in Greek historiographic dress.**
- [[apocalyptic-revelation]] — Josephus reports portents and oracles around the destruction of the Temple (*BJ* 6.288–315): the comet over Jerusalem; the heifer giving birth to a lamb in the Temple court; the eastern gate of the inner Temple opening of its own accord; the chariots and armies seen in the sky; the voice in the Temple announcing "Let us depart from here." Josephus reads these as portents that the divine presence has *abandoned* the Temple, transferring its favor to the Roman side. Critical datum: the same apocalyptic-portent vocabulary informs the contemporary [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark|Gospel of Mark]]'s Olivet discourse (Mark 13).
- [[diaspora-judaism]] — the *War* essentially ends the Judaean-Temple-centered phase of Jewish history and begins the rabbinic-diaspora phase.
- [[anti-roman-polemic]] — and its *opposite* — the *War* is both an *apology for Rome* (Roman victory is providential) and, **read closely, an apology for Jewish nobility in defeat** (the Masada suicide is presented with sympathy and Eleazar's speech is one of the great Stoic-noble-death speeches in ancient literature). This **double-voiced** quality is central to the work's literary character.
- **The Masada suicide as Stoic-philosophical noble death.** *BJ* 7.323–388: Eleazar ben Yair's two great speeches before the mass suicide are constructed Greek philosophical orations on the soul, on slavery vs. freedom, on the worthlessness of life under tyranny. Eleazar argues from Indian gymnosophist examples — i.e., Greek-philosophical comparative-religion. The Masada-suicide passage is the most extensively analyzed single set-piece in Josephus.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-070-josephus-antiquities-of-the-jews]] — *self-citation* / *redaction*: Antiquities 20 explicitly cross-references the War; the same author over ~20 years; some events re-narrated with changed emphases (notably the Galilean command — the two accounts conflict, generating the *Vita* as Josephus's later self-defense).
- → [[phase-4-071-josephus-against-apion]] — *companion-work*: same apologetic project.
- → [[phase-3-019-4-maccabees]] — *parallel-substrate*: both works deploy Hellenistic-Jewish noble-death philosophy for Jewish martyrs; Masada's Eleazar speech is in the same rhetorical-philosophical tradition as 4 Maccabees' Eleazar speech.
- → Thucydides — *literary-model*: deliberate emulation in form, structure, and rhetorical-techniques. Mason 2003 ch. 4. **The Hellenistic-Greek historiographic substrate that makes the *War* legible to its Greek-Roman audience.**
- → Polybius — *historiographic-model*: pragmatic-history method, universal-history scope.
- → [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — *shared-milieu* / *parallel-portents*: composed within years of one another; both deploy apocalyptic portent-vocabulary around the destruction of the Temple. Mark 13's portents parallel Josephus *BJ* 6.288–315's portents.
- → [[phase-4-027-eusebius-ecclesiastical-history]] — *direct-quote*: Eusebius treats the *War* as nearly canonical for the destruction of Jerusalem.
- → Later Jewish historical memory: *Sefer Yosippon* (10th-c. Hebrew paraphrase) is the principal medieval-Jewish reception of Josephus.

## Disputes
- **Reliability.** Josephus is **not a neutral observer**: he is an apologist for both himself (he commanded the Jewish forces he is now writing about), for Rome (he is in court patronage), and for Judaism (he is defending Jewish dignity to an alien audience). His own military conduct in Galilee is self-described in *War* and *Vita* with **substantial mutual contradiction**. Mainstream scholarship (Mason, Rajak) reads him as a sophisticated literary-political author, not as a transparent reporter.
- **Casualty figures.** Josephus's 1.1 million Jewish dead during the war (*BJ* 6.420) is widely judged a rhetorical exaggeration; modern estimates are dramatically lower.
- **Authenticity of the speeches.** Universally accepted as **Josephan composition** in the Thucydidean tradition, not transcript. The Masada-suicide speeches especially.
- **Historicity of the Masada-suicide.** Yadin's 1960s excavations broadly confirmed the siege archaeology but generated their own controversies (the *lots* found at the site, the precise number of bodies). Whether the mass-suicide happened *as Josephus narrates* or has been substantially shaped by his Stoic-noble-death literary patterning is actively debated (Cohen 1982).
- **The Slavonic Josephus's Christian-additions** (passages on Jesus, John the Baptist in the Slavonic version) — universally rejected as late Christian additions.
