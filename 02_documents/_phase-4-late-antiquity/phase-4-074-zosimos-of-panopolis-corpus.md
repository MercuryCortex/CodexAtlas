# Zosimos of Panopolis — Alchemical Corpus

## What it is
The surviving body of alchemical-philosophical-visionary writing by [[zosimos-of-panopolis|Zosimos of Panopolis]] (~290–310 CE), preserved fragmentarily through (a) Byzantine Greek alchemical compilations (Marcianus graecus 299 + companion mss), (b) Syriac translation (Cambridge Mm 6.29), and (c) Arabic citation in the Jabirian-Raziuan tradition. The corpus is **the earliest extant Western Greek-language alchemical literature with substantial direct preservation**, and the **foundational text-cluster of the visionary-spiritual reading of alchemy** that becomes definitive for the entire Western tradition through medieval Arabic, medieval Latin, Renaissance, and modern psychological receptions.

The corpus is organized as:
1. ***Cheirokmeta*** ("Things Done by Hand") — a large multi-book technical-philosophical work, ~28 books originally, organized by Greek letters, of which substantial fragments survive.
2. ***The Visions of Zosimos*** (sometimes treated as part of *Cheirokmeta*) — the **single most-influential text in the visionary-alchemical lineage**. Three or four dream-narratives in which Zosimos witnesses a priest-figure (variously named, sometimes called *Ion*) being dismembered, boiled, transformed, and reconstituted on a bowl-shaped sacrificial altar — interpreted as an allegory of the alchemical *opus*.
3. ***On the Letter Omega*** — mystical-cosmological-philosophical treatment of the Greek letter Ω as a cipher for Kronos / Saturn / the seventh planetary sphere.
4. ***Authentic Memoirs*** — additional fragments.
5. **Letters to Theosebeia** — substantial correspondence with a female alchemical colleague, in Hermetic teacher-student form.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Upper Egypt, late Roman period, immediately before the great Diocletianic and Constantinian transformations. Panopolis / Akhmim was a major Greek-Egyptian intellectual center. The region is the same one from which the [[event-nag-hammadi-discovery|Nag Hammadi]] Gnostic codices would later be buried (~370 CE).
- **Religious milieu.** Zosimos's contemporary religious environment includes: Greek paganism (still public, pre-Constantine); the Hermetic philosophical-religious literature of the *Corpus Hermeticum* tradition (also late-3rd / early-4th c.); the Sethian and Valentinian Gnostic schools (contemporary, regionally co-located); Manichaeism (rapidly expanding 270s+); Egyptian temple religion (declining but operative); early Egyptian Christianity (Origen's school in Alexandria a generation earlier; Antony's monasticism in Egypt contemporary). Zosimos engages all of these registers.
- **Who was behind it.** Zosimos as primary author; the corpus is preserved through subsequent Byzantine alchemists (esp. **Olympiodorus** and **Stephanos of Alexandria**, ~7th c.) who excerpt and gloss Zosimos.
- **Transmission.** Greek → Syriac → Arabic → Latin (the last only indirectly). **Marcianus graecus 299** (the standard Byzantine Greek alchemy anthology, ~10th-11th c.) is the principal extant witness. Berthelot 1888 published the first major critical edition; Mertens 1995 is the standard modern critical edition.

## Themes and motifs
- [[alchemy-as-spiritual-discipline]] — the Zosiman corpus IS the proto-text of this theme in Western alchemy.
- [[visionary-alchemical-allegory]] — the priest-dismemberment-and-reconstitution dream-form.
- [[as-above-so-below]] — Zosiman language about cosmic mirroring of micro and macro.
- [[soul-as-spark]] — the divine spark trapped in matter, awaiting alchemical-spiritual liberation, in language adjacent to Gnostic anthropogony.

## Disputes
- **Authenticity within the Marcianus 299 corpus.** Mertens 1995 distinguishes the authentic Zosimos from later attributions (Olympiodorus, Stephanos of Alexandria, Pseudo-Zosimos).
- **Gnostic positioning.** Whether Zosimos was a "Gnostic" in any strict school sense is debated; Mertens, Lindsay, Pearson treat his Gnostic-adjacent motifs as participation in shared Greco-Egyptian intellectual environment rather than school-membership.
- **Visions' source.** Whether the Visions are reports of genuine dreams, literary compositions, or initiatory-ritual descriptions is debated. Jung 1968 reads them as authentic visionary material; Principe 2007 is more agnostic.
