# Consolation of Philosophy

## What it is
Five books of alternating Latin prose and verse (*prosimetrum*), composed by Boethius while imprisoned at Ticinum (Pavia) awaiting execution in 523–525. Lady Philosophy (*Philosophia*) appears to the imprisoned Boethius and over five books leads him from grief through philosophical therapy to acceptance: Book I, the diagnosis of Boethius's despair; Book II, the unreliability of Fortune; Book III, the highest good; Book IV, providence and fate, evil's nature; Book V, divine foreknowledge and human free will. The most-read non-Biblical book of the medieval Latin West.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Ostrogothic Italy under King Theodoric. Boethius had been *magister officiorum* (head of the civil administration) in 522 — the year his two sons were consuls. In 523 he was accused of treason for defending Senator Albinus (charged with correspondence with the Byzantine emperor against Theodoric). Imprisoned, condemned, executed in 524 or 525.
- **Religious milieu.** Boethius was a Christian (the *opuscula sacra*, especially *De Trinitate*, are his). The *Consolation*, written when condemned to death, contains no overt Christianity — no Christ, no Bible, no Trinity. Lady Philosophy speaks in the Plotinian-Platonist register. The puzzle of the *Consolation*'s religious mode is one of the great problems of the work. The two most-defended solutions: (a) Boethius is writing Christian-compatible philosophical theology in a deliberately non-Christological register because he can speak that language with greater ecumenical authority (and perhaps because Theodoric's Arian regime did not welcome explicit Christology from a condemned Nicene aristocrat); (b) the *Consolation*'s philosophy is implicitly Christian and the form is a literary choice in the *prosimetrum* satirical-philosophical tradition.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous. Carolingian glosses (Remigius of Auxerre, Bovo of Corvey); Alfred's Old English translation (890s); Notker Labeo's Old High German (10th c.); innumerable medieval and Renaissance translations.

## Themes and motifs
- [[chain-of-being]] — the great Neoplatonist hierarchy from the One down through intelligences to bodies, with the world ordered by providence.
- [[apatheia-and-virtue]] — Lady Philosophy's therapy is Stoic-Platonist: dissolving the soul's attachment to Fortune, recognizing the *summum bonum* (highest good) as the only secure object of the soul's appetite.
- [[free-will-vs-determinism]] — Book V's celebrated discussion: divine foreknowledge does not destroy human free will, because God knows from outside time the eternal "now" in which all events are simultaneously present. (Influential on every subsequent Western treatment.)
- [[apotheosis]] / mystical ascent — Book III's metrum 9 (*O qui perpetua*) is a Latin verse paraphrase of Plato's *Timaeus* on the world-soul and the soul's return.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — *direct-source*: *Timaeus* (III.m.9 is its paraphrase), *Republic* IX (the geometric / arithmetic / harmonic happiness-arguments), *Phaedo* (the soul-ascent framework). Boethius read Plato in Greek; the *Consolation* shows continuous engagement.
- → [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — *direct-influence*: the highest good as the One; the providential ordering; the soul's ascent. Plotinian apophasis is present though not explicit. Chadwick 1981, Marenbon 2003.
- → [[phase-4-021-proclus-elements-of-theology]] — *direct-influence*: Proclean hierarchical metaphysics, transmitted via the late-Athenian Platonist school in which Boethius was trained (probably through Ammonius's Alexandrian circle).
- → Stoic ethics (Seneca, Cicero) — *direct-influence*: Lady Philosophy's therapy is heavily Stoic.
- → **Late-Antique Neoplatonist substrate** — *substrate-influence*: the *Consolation* is fundamentally a Latin Christian-compatible Neoplatonist work. It is the **principal channel by which late-antique Platonism reaches the medieval West in literary-philosophical form** (alongside the *Timaeus* in Calcidius's translation). Marenbon 2009. Without the *Consolation*, the medieval Western Platonist substrate is unintelligible.
- → Menippean satire / prosimetric form — *literary-form*: the alternation of prose and verse derives from the Hellenistic-Roman Menippean satire tradition (Varro, Petronius, Seneca's *Apocolocyntosis*).
- → [[sophia-pleroma]] — *structural-parallel*: Lady Philosophy as the divine-feminine wisdom figure who descends to the suffering prisoner is the same theological grammar as Valentinian Sophia descending to recover the wandering soul. Boethius did not know Gnostic texts; the parallel is structural convergence — the same theological need finding the same solution. The chain: Proverbs 8 *hokhmah* → Sirach 24 Sophia → Wisdom of Solomon → [[philo-of-alexandria]]'s Sophia-Logos → **Lady Philosophy** here.
- → [[beatrice-portinari]] — *direct-ancestor*: Dante knew the *Consolation* intimately (cited explicitly in *Convivio* II.xii as the book he turned to after Beatrice's death). Lady Philosophy's structure — divine feminine figure descending to the suffering sage, dialogue form, alternating prose and verse, ascent from grief through philosophical understanding to cosmic perspective — is the direct literary model for Beatrice as guide in the *Commedia*. This is not convergence but documented genealogy.
- → [[executed-divine-claimant]] — *instance-of*: written in prison awaiting unjust execution; the highest-ranking official of his civilization condemned without adequate due process; venerated as martyr; his death produces the most-transmitted philosophical text of the medieval West.
- → [[phase-5-024-aquinas-summa-theologiae]] and earlier Latin Christian Platonism — *direct-influence*: Boethius is everywhere in medieval Western theology and philosophy. Dante puts him in the Heaven of the Sun, *Paradiso* X.124–9.

## Disputes
- **Boethius's Christianity in the *Consolation*.** The absence of overt Christianity has been variously explained (see Context above). Most modern interpreters (Chadwick 1981, Marenbon 2003) accept Christian authorship working in a philosophical register.
- **Reason for execution.** Political (Theodoric's anti-senatorial reaction), religious-political (the Arian-Nicene tension), or actual treason — all defended. Marenbon 2003 reviews.
