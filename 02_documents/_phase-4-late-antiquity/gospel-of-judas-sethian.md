# Gospel of Judas (Sethian Gnostic, ~140–160 CE)

Sethian Gnostic gospel preserved in the Coptic Codex Tchacos, discovered in the 1970s in Middle Egypt, restored and first published in 2006 by Kasser-Meyer-Wurst (National Geographic Society edition). The text is mentioned and condemned by Irenaeus of Lyons in *Adversus Haereses* 1.31.1 (~180 CE), confirming the document existed and was known to heresiologists by the late 2nd century.

## The radical reframing

The Gospel of Judas inverts the canonical-Gospel framing of [[judas-iscariot]]: where the four canonical Gospels treat Judas as the betraying disciple whose handing-over of Jesus to authorities is the founding sin against the Christ, the Sethian Gospel of Judas treats Judas as Jesus's *privileged disciple* — the only one to whom Jesus reveals the deepest mysteries — and Judas's handing-over is interpreted as the *theologically-correct* act, helping Jesus shed his material body and return to the divine Pleroma.

This reframing exemplifies the Sethian-Gnostic theological framework:
- The material body is a prison made by the ignorant Demiurge
- The Christ-figure is a divine spark descended into matter to retrieve other sparks
- Liberation requires *gnōsis* — recognition of one's alien-divine origin
- The crucifixion is not a tragedy but a *liberation* (the spirit is freed from the body-prison)

Judas-as-privileged-disciple is the Gnostic-soteriological role demanded by this framework.

## Cross-tradition position

The Gospel of Judas anchors the cross-tradition pattern of *theological-inversion documents* — texts that take the central narrative of a parent tradition and systematically reverse its theological valuation. Parallel cases:
- Manichaean reading of Jesus + Buddha + Zarathustra as Apostles of Light persecuted by archonic powers
- Certain Cathar readings of the Old Testament Yahweh as the Demiurge
- The Naassene reading of the serpent as the Christ-figure (vs. canonical Christian serpent-as-tempter)

Each case: same texts, inverted theology, structural-Gnostic dualist framework. See [[theme-mesopotamian-gnostic-transmission]] for the broader Gnostic-substrate cluster.
