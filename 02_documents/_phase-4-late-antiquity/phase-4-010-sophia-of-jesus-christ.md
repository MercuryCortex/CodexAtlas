# Sophia of Jesus Christ

## What it is
A revelation-dialogue. The risen Jesus appears in Galilee to "twelve disciples and seven women," who pose cosmological questions; he discloses the unknowable Father, the Pleroma's emanations, and the origin of the cosmos through Sophia. The text is a Christianized rewriting of *Eugnostos the Blessed* — a non-Christian Gnostic philosophical treatise that argues from Greek philosophical premises against the philosophers' inadequate accounts of the highest principle. The comparison with Eugnostos is one of the cleanest cases of a Gnostic text being *Christianized by reframing*, with the philosophical content largely unchanged.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** A bridge between non-Christian philosophical-Gnostic speculation (Eugnostos) and Christian post-resurrection-dialogue Gnosticism (Sophia of Jesus Christ).
- **Who was behind it.** A Christian Gnostic redactor of an earlier philosophical-Gnostic source.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Two Coptic witnesses (NHC III; BG 8502) plus a Greek fragment (P.Oxy. 1081) confirming Greek original.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hidden-god]] — apophatic descriptions of the unknown Father derived from Eugnostos' philosophical critique.
- [[divine-feminine]] — Sophia's cosmogonic role.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — knowing the Father saves; the philosophers, knowing only the cosmos, are lost.
