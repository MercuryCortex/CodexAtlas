# Stobaean Hermetica (including Kore Kosmou)

## What it is
The 29 (or 40, with subdivisions) Hermetic excerpts preserved in the *Anthologion* of John of Stobi ("Stobaeus"), a learned Christian compiler in 5th-c. Macedonia who assembled a four-book commonplace-book of pagan philosophical-religious literature for his son. The excerpts are not a unified work — they are passages from now-lost Hermetic books that Stobaeus selected for their wisdom-content, treated as didactic material on a par with Plato, Aristotle, the Stoics, and the Pythagoreans. Without Stobaeus's compilation, this entire layer of the Hermetic corpus would be lost.

The single most important excerpt is **Stobaean Hermetic Excerpt XXIII = Kore Kosmou** ("Daughter of the Cosmos" — the Greek noun *korē* means both "girl/maiden" and "pupil of the eye," producing a deliberate double sense: the maiden-of-the-cosmos who is *also* the pupil-through-which-the-cosmos-sees). Unlike the *Corpus Hermeticum* (where Hermes instructs Tat or Asclepius), Kore Kosmou is a revelation of [[isis-egyptian]] to her son [[horus]] — placing it in the *Isis-Horus pedagogical-revelatory genre* attested in Egyptian temple-literature (the *Book of the Heavenly Cow*, etc.). It contains a long systematic cosmogony: Hermes Trismegistus himself, before becoming a man, served as scribe of the supreme God; the cosmos is created with multiple grades of being; souls pre-exist and are graded into 60 ranks; bodies are made for unruly souls; the gods of Egypt — Osiris, Isis, Horus — are sent down to civilize humanity; the kingship of Egypt is established as the earthly mirror of the divine order.

The other Stobaean Hermetic excerpts include **Definitions of Isis to Horus**, **Of the Soul and the Body**, **On Energy and Sense**, **On Truth**, **On the Decans and the Heavenly Bodies**, **Of Justice**, and **Of the Decans-Demons-Stars**. The excerpts on the **decans** (the 36 ten-degree divisions of the zodiac, each with its presiding god-figure) are the principal Hermetic source for late-antique astrology and survive nowhere else.

## Primary text — the Kore Kosmou opening (excerpts)

The excerpts use Mead 1906 / Kingsford-Maitland 1885 (public domain), checked against Festugière (Budé vol. 3, 1954). Section numbering follows Nock-Festugière SH XXIII.

### Kore Kosmou §1–6 — Isis begins her revelation to Horus

> So speaking, Isis pours forth first for Horus the sweet draught of immortality which souls receive from the Gods, and thus begins the most holy discourse:
>
> "Of the producing of all things, my son, the Sun-God's mighty bond to all... do thou give pious heed.
>
> "And inasmuch as it is necessary, my marvellous son, the heaven and earth and the all-bountiful æther of the cosmos to set forth before thy mind, behold I will reveal to thee in mystery a hidden doctrine, the most holy of holies — *the Pupil of the Eye of the Cosmos*. Hold thy thought silent within thee like one ravished, my son, and learn from me what was the constitution of all that came to be before all things came to be."

Citation: SH XXIII = Kore Kosmou §1–4, trans. Mead 1906; cf. Festugière Budé vol. 3 (1954), pp. 1–6.

### Kore Kosmou §16–17 — Hermes ascending after his earthly mission

> When Hermes had completed all of these things, my son Horus, and entrusted to his successors the safe keeping of the books, he ascended back to the stars, hymning his fellow-Gods. And Tat, his disciple-son, was at his side, and Asclepius the wise; for these were they whom he had begotten in his understanding, having transmitted to them the heaven-descended doctrine.

Citation: SH XXIII §16–17, trans. Mead 1906; cf. Festugière Budé vol. 3, pp. 12–14.

### Kore Kosmou §44–48 — Isis and Osiris are sent down to civilize humanity

> When the Souls were sent down into bodies, and Earth was disordered with weeping and lamentation, the Sovereign God beheld and took pity. He sent down Osiris and Isis, that the human race, savage and bestial, might receive piety and the laws. And these are the things they did: they founded sanctuaries; they appointed priests; they established mysteries and oracles; they taught agriculture; they laid down laws; they gave food to the bodies and reverence to the souls; and so the Earth was set in order.

Citation: SH XXIII §44–48 (paraphrase), trans. Mead 1906; cf. Festugière Budé vol. 3, pp. 26–30.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Original composition: Roman Egypt 1st–4th c. CE. Preservation: 5th-c. Macedonian Christian Byzantium (Stobaeus). The Hermetic material would have been lost without a Christian compiler willing to copy pagan religious texts as ethical-philosophical anthology.
- **Religious milieu.** Original Greco-Egyptian Hermetic milieu, distinguishable from the Hermes-Tat dialogues by its **Isis-Horus revelatory genre frame** — connecting it directly to Egyptian temple literature and to Hellenistic [[isis-hellenistic|Isiac mystery-religion]]. Festugière groups it with the "popular" Hermetica (cosmogony + practical religion), distinct from the "philosophical" Hermes-Tat dialogues.
- **Who was behind it.** Egyptian-priestly intellectuals; the Isis-Horus framing connects them to the Memphite-Saite theological tradition. Mahé and Fowden read Kore Kosmou as preserving genuine Late-Period Egyptian temple cosmology in Greek philosophical dress.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Stobaeus c.500 → Byzantine manuscript transmission → Conrad Gessner Latin 1543 → Francesco Patrizi Greek-Latin 1591 (printed as *fourth book* of his *Nova Philosophia*; Patrizi treated the Stobaean excerpts as canonical Hermetica equal to the Corpus) → Anna Kingsford-Edward Maitland 1885 English (the famous *Virgin of the World* edition, used widely in Theosophy and Western occultism through the 20th c.) → Nock-Festugière Budé 1954 (definitive critical Greek edition).

**Notable absence:** Brian Copenhaver's otherwise-standard Cambridge UP *Hermetica* (1992) **does not include the Stobaean fragments** — Copenhaver covers only the Greek Corpus and the Latin Asclepius. For English readers wanting the Stobaean excerpts the choice is still between Mead 1906 and Kingsford-Maitland 1885; a modern scholarly English of the Stobaean Hermetica is a real gap in the field.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hermetic-cosmogony]] — Kore Kosmou is the most systematic single Hermetic cosmogony, more developed than CH I (Poimandres) and ranking the souls and the elements with explicit hierarchy.
- [[divine-feminine]] — the Isis-Horus revelatory frame puts a *goddess* in the teacher-position (Hermes is a character *inside* the narrative, not the teacher of it); structurally distinct from every other Hermetic text.
- [[anthropos-myth]] — the descent of souls into bodies and the civilizing of Earth.
- [[ascent-of-the-soul]] — Hermes himself models the ascent ("ascended back to the stars, hymning his fellow-Gods").
- [[psychopomp]] — souls graded and assigned; the cosmos as a soul-economy.
- [[apokatastasis]] — souls eventually return to their ranks; the cosmos cycles.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — explicit revelation as soteriological vehicle.
- [[hermetic-piety]] — the laws, mysteries, and oracles given by Osiris-Isis civilize humanity.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] — *parallel-motif*: shared cosmogonic substrate (Nous, descent of soul, ascent through spheres) but Kore Kosmou is more systematic and uses Isis-Horus rather than Hermes-Tat.
- → [[phase-4-012-asclepius]] — *shared-milieu*: shared Egyptian-priestly material; the *Asclepius* "lament for the death of Egyptian religion" passage (§§24–26) overlaps thematically with Kore Kosmou's account of the founding of Egyptian religion by Isis-Osiris.
- → [[phase-4-072-plutarch-de-iside-et-osiride]] — *parallel-motif* + *shared-milieu*: Plutarch (early 2nd c.) provides the *Greek philosophical reading* of the Isis-Osiris-Horus mythology that Kore Kosmou presupposes; the two texts are within decades of each other and may share Egyptian-priestly informants. **Direct documentary edge: both texts read Egyptian theology Platonist-allegorically and assume a Hermes-Trismegistus / Thoth scribal-cosmological role.**
- → [[phase-3-021-hermetic-corpus-earliest]] — *shared-corpus*: belongs to the same broad Hermetic literature with overlapping themes and personnel.
- → [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] — *parallel-motif*: cosmogony as systematic divine-genealogy + ordering of cosmos; Mesopotamian comparandum (West 1997 traces the late-antique Greek cosmogonies' Near Eastern substrate).
- → Egyptian *Book of the Heavenly Cow* (New Kingdom) — *parallel-motif*: divine-mother revealing cosmogony to her child; Kore Kosmou structurally inherits this Egyptian temple-literature genre.
- → [[isis-egyptian]] / [[isis-hellenistic]] — *syncretic-identification*: Kore Kosmou is the textual locus where Isis is most explicitly given the *cosmocrator-and-revealer* role; the iconographic Isis-Horus pairing in Hellenistic-Egyptian art has a cognate textual scene here.
- → [[ethiopian-marian-devotion]] — *parallel-motif* (downstream): the Coptic-Ethiopian Marian iconographic tradition continues the Isis-Horus revealing-mother visual frame; the Plutarch / Hermetica → Coptic Mary chain runs through the same body of Greco-Egyptian material.
- → [[event-stobaeus-anthology-c500]] — *manuscript-transmission*: the Christian compilation that preserves the text.
- → [[phase-6-001-ficino-pimander]] — *manuscript-transmission* (sister edition): Ficino translated the Corpus Hermeticum from a manuscript that did NOT include the Stobaean fragments; Patrizi 1591 added them. The Renaissance reception of the Stobaean Hermetica is a generation behind the Corpus.

## Disputes
- **Egyptian vs. Greek substratum.** The same dispute as for the rest of the Hermetica. Festugière (Greek-philosophical) vs. Reitzenstein (Iranian-Babylonian) vs. Fowden / Mahé (genuinely Egyptian, in Greek dress). Kore Kosmou is the *strongest evidence* on the Egyptian side because of its Isis-Horus framing and its preservation of named Egyptian gods in clearly Egyptian cosmological roles.
- **Date.** Festugière placed the Stobaean material 200–300 CE; some elements may be earlier or later; the decan-astrology material may be late.
- **Why excluded by Copenhaver?** Copenhaver 1992 explains in his preface that he restricted himself to the Greek Corpus and the Latin Asclepius for textual-critical reasons; the Stobaean fragments would have required a separate volume. A consequence is that English-language scholarly access to the Stobaean Hermetica is significantly weaker than to the Corpus — a real gap in modern Hermetic studies.
