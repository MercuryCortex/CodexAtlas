# Trimorphic Protennoia

## What it is
A first-person revelation-aretalogy in three movements ("forms"): Protennoia ("First Thought" of the Father, identified with Barbelo) speaks as the Father's voice, then as a feminine Sound and Speech, then as the masculine Logos-Word descending into the world to awaken sleeping spirits. The third movement is so close in phrasing to the prologue of the Gospel of John that scholars have argued for either direct relationship or a shared liturgical hymn-tradition.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Late Sethian, Christianized overlay. The text seems built on an originally non-Christian Sethian Pronoia hymn (very close to the Pronoia hymn at the end of the long recension of the Apocryphon of John).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Nag Hammadi XIII (1945).

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-feminine]] — Protennoia / Barbelo speaks in the first person.
- [[hidden-god]] — apophatic descriptions of the Father.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — descent to awaken sleeping spirits.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]] — *direct-quote* / *shared-source*: the Pronoia hymn at the end of the long ApocJohn is a compressed Protennoia (or both depend on a common Pronoia liturgy).
- → [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — *parallel-motif* / *contested-influence*: Robinson (1981), Turner argue Protennoia preserves earlier non-Christian material that the Johannine prologue redacted; other scholars (Evans, Hill) argue the dependence runs the other direction.
- → Sethian Barbeloite texts (Three Steles of Seth, Zostrianos) — *shared-milieu*.

## Disputes
- **Direction of influence with John.** Three positions: (a) John depends on a Sethian Protennoia hymn-tradition (Robinson, Turner); (b) Protennoia depends on John (Evans); (c) both depend on a shared Hellenistic-Jewish wisdom hymn-tradition (Schenke). No consensus.
