# Babylonian Talmud

## What it is
The most extensive commentary on the Mishnah — produced by the Babylonian rabbinic academies under Sasanian rule. Sixty tractates; approximately 2.5 million words; structured as Mishnah passage → amoraic discussion (gemara) → stammaitic editorial framing. The Bavli is dialogical in form, comfortable with unresolved disputes, and notoriously digressive — legal discussion can yield to ethical, narrative (aggadic), or magical material at any point. Its prestige in subsequent Judaism exceeds the Yerushalmi's by a wide margin.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Sasanian Babylonia ~225–~650, where Jewish communities had autonomy under the *exilarch* (resh galuta) and the academies. Then the early Islamic period (Arab conquest 637, Abbasid period from 750) under which the Bavli's final redaction is sometimes placed.
- **Religious milieu.** Babylonian Jewish self-confidence; the two great academies of Sura (founded by Rav ~219) and Pumbedita (founded by Yehuda ben Yehezkel ~259) dominate intellectually. Contact with Zoroastrian theology and Sasanian legal categories is documented (Shai Secunda, Yaakov Elman, Shaul Shaked).
- **Who was behind it.** Generations of named amoraim (Rav, Shmuel, Yochanan, Rava, Abaye, Rav Ashi, Ravina) and anonymous stammaim who provided the dialectical framing.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous; Munich 95 (1342) the only complete medieval witness; Vilna Shas (1880–86) the standard print.

## Themes and motifs
- [[chosen-people]] — Jewish particularity worked out in legal-narrative-aggadic form.
- [[divine-kingship]] — recurring aggadic motif; God's kingship in halakhic detail.
