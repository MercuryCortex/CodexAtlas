# Gospel of Philip

## What it is
Not a narrative gospel and not a sayings gospel — a *florilegium* of Valentinian theological reflections strung loosely by association. ~110 paragraph-units cover sacramental theology (baptism, chrism, eucharist, redemption, the "bridal chamber"), Christological reflection, anti-Petrine and anti-orthodox jabs, etymological puns on Aramaic/Greek/Syriac terms (e.g., *Messias* = anointed = chrism), and the famously fragmentary passage about Jesus and Mary Magdalene as "companion" (*koinōnos*). The text is one of the chief witnesses for Valentinian sacramental practice — for some interpreters the "bridal chamber" is a literal ritual, for others a symbolic state.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-second/third-century Roman East; Valentinian Christianity is now a distinct, persecuted Christian school living in tension with the proto-orthodox church.
- **Religious milieu.** The text uses Valentinian categories (Pleroma, Sophia, the Spirit) as familiar furniture; it is a *use* of the system, not an exposition of it. The five Valentinian sacraments (baptism, chrism, eucharist, redemption, bridal-chamber) are listed only here (Logion 68/Saying 68).
- **Who was behind it.** A Valentinian compiler (or compilers); the loose structure suggests excerpts from larger lost works (some passages may derive from a Heracleonian commentary on John). Attribution to the apostle Philip is conventional — he speaks in the first person in only one passage.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Nag Hammadi II (1945); first English by Wesley Isenberg in Robinson's *Nag Hammadi Library in English* (1977), critical edition 1989. The Mary-Magdalene-as-companion passages received massive popular attention via *The Da Vinci Code* (2003).

## Themes and motifs
- [[sacred-marriage]] — the "bridal chamber" sacrament; sacramental union of the soul with its angelic counterpart. Whether ritually consummated or purely symbolic is disputed.
- [[divine-feminine]] — Sophia, the Holy Spirit (grammatically feminine in Syriac, exploited here), and Mary Magdalene as Jesus' *koinōnos* form a feminine theological thread.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — "Ignorance is the mother of all evils. Ignorance will result in death" (Saying 123).
- [[mystery-initiation]] — sacraments as initiations; explicit comparison to mystery cults.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-003-gospel-of-truth]] — *shared-milieu* (Valentinian corpus): same theological grammar but Philip is sacramental-meditative where Truth is homiletic-philosophical.
- → [[phase-4-005-gospel-of-mary]] / [[phase-4-001-gospel-of-thomas]] — *parallel-motif*: elevation of Mary against Peter; Saying 55 of Philip ("the companion of the [Saviour is] Mary [Magdalene]. [Christ loved] her more than [all] the disciples, and used to kiss her [often] on her [...]") parallels the Peter-vs-Mary tension in Mary 17.
- → [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — *direct-influence*: Johannine sacramental imagery (water/spirit, bread of life) is reused and Valentinianized.
- → Heracleon's *Commentary on John* (lost; Phase 4 background) — *direct-influence*: some sayings may derive from this Valentinian commentary, our first known Christian commentary on a NT text.

## Disputes
- **Date.** Layton, Isenberg: ~250 CE. Some Syrian-origin advocates push earlier (~200). The 200–300 range used here is mainstream.
- **Bridal chamber.** Literal ritual sex (Schenke originally, no longer mainstream) vs. baptismal-eucharistic ritual involving symbolic union (Thomassen, mainstream) vs. purely symbolic eschatological state (DeConick).
- **Jesus and Mary Magdalene.** The lacunae in the "kiss" passage are real (the manuscript is damaged); reconstructions vary from "on her mouth" (Meyer) to "on her cheek/forehead/feet." The popular claim that the text proves Jesus and Mary were married is far stronger than the text supports.
