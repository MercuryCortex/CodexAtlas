# Bṛhajjātaka

## What it is
The **classic Sanskrit textbook of horoscopic (natal) astrology**, composed in the 6th century CE by **Varāhamihira** of Ujjain — the most celebrated of India's astronomer-astrologers. The *Bṛhajjātaka* ("Great [book of] Nativities") organizes the *horā* branch of *jyotiṣa* — the Greek-derived horoscopic system first attested in the [[phase-3-041-yavanajataka]] — into the concise, authoritative, commentary-anchored form that has been the backbone of Indian astrology ever since. It is the Indian counterpart to Ptolemy's [[phase-3-038-ptolemy-tetrabiblos]] in both function (the canonical handbook) and ancestry (the same Hellenistic synthesis, naturalized into Sanskrit science).

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Gupta and post-Gupta India, with Ujjain (Avanti) as the prime meridian and capital of Indian mathematical astronomy.
- **Religious milieu.** Brahmanical Sanskritic learning that had fully absorbed and re-expressed the Greek-derived horoscopic system; Varāhamihira openly honours the *yavanas* (Greeks) as masters of this science even while it is now thoroughly Indian.
- **Who was behind it.** Varāhamihira, a polymath who also wrote the encyclopaedic *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* and the astronomical *Pañcasiddhāntikā* — the latter explicitly digesting a "Romaka" (Roman) and a "Pauliśa" (Paul-derived) siddhānta, fossils of Greco-Roman astronomy in Sanskrit.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuously transmitted with a major commentarial tradition (Bhaṭṭotpala, 966 CE); a living text of practising *jyotiṣa*.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divinatory-omen-reading]] — the canonical Sanskrit systematization of natal astrology.
- [[microcosm-macrocosm]] — the natal horoscope as the legible map of a life.
- [[wisdom-as-cosmic-order]] — astral science as honoured *śāstra* (systematic knowledge).

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-041-yavanajataka]] — *direct-development*: Varāhamihira systematizes the Greek-derived horā stratum the *Yavanajātaka* first introduced into Sanskrit.
- → [[phase-3-038-ptolemy-tetrabiblos]] — *parallel-form*: the Indian and the Greek canonical natal handbooks descend from the same Hellenistic synthesis.
- → [[tradition-jyotisha-indian-astrology]] — *foundational-text-of*: the *Bṛhajjātaka* is the spine of the classical Indian horoscopic curriculum.

## Disputes
- **Varāhamihira's dates** (c. 505–587) are fixed fairly securely by the epoch of the *Pañcasiddhāntikā* (505 CE) and the date of Bhaṭṭotpala's commentary (966 CE), but the precise lifespan is inferred.
- **Degree of Greek dependence.** Scholarship (Pingree, Plofker) treats the horā system as Greek-derived in origin while emphasizing its substantial Indian development; the balance is a matter of ongoing study.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The Indian arm of the spine made canonical.** With Varāhamihira, the Greek horoscopic astrology that crossed into India in the [[phase-3-041-yavanajataka]] becomes a permanent, self-standing classical science — the *jyotiṣa* still practised today. The *Pañcasiddhāntikā*'s "Romaka" and "Pauliśa" siddhāntas are named fossils of the Greco-Roman source, the clearest internal evidence that India's exact astral sciences absorbed a Mediterranean stratum (Neugebauer & Pingree 1970; Pingree 1981; Plofker 2009).
- **A parallel-but-independent canon.** Varāhamihira shows the spine forking: the same Hellenistic synthesis produces, in the Latin-Arabic west, [[tradition-western-astrology]], and in the Sanskrit east, the classical *jyotiṣa* of [[tradition-jyotisha-indian-astrology]] — two living traditions from one transmitted root.
