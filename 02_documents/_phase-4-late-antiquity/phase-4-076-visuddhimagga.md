# Visuddhimagga (Path of Purification)

## What it is
The Visuddhimagga is the foundational systematic synthesis of Theravāda Buddhism — the work that organizes the entire Theravāda doctrinal and meditative tradition into a single integrated path-structure. Composed by [[buddhaghosa]] at the Mahāvihāra monastery in Anurādhapura, Sri Lanka, ~+430, it is structured around the **three trainings** (*tisikkhā*) — *sīla* (ethical conduct), *samādhi* (concentration), *paññā* (wisdom) — articulated across 23 chapters and ~1,000 pages of carefully integrated doctrine and practice. The work has been the standard taught-text of Theravāda monastic education for ~1500 years, has provided the technical vocabulary of all subsequent Theravāda exposition, and is the principal classical Pali source behind the 20th-c. global *vipassanā* revival (Mahāsi Sayadaw's *vipassanā-ñāṇa* schema; S.N. Goenka's "Goenka method") and contemporary secular mindfulness (MBSR; Kabat-Zinn).

## Structure (23 chapters in three parts)

**Part I — Sīla (Ethical Conduct)** [chapters 1-2]:
1. The Description of Virtue
2. The Description of the Ascetic Practices

**Part II — Samādhi (Concentration)** [chapters 3-13]:
3. Taking a Meditation Subject
4. The Earth Kasiṇa
5. The Remaining Kasiṇas
6. Foulness as a Meditation Subject (cemetery contemplations)
7. The Six Recollections
8. Other Recollections as Meditation Subjects (including [[siddhartha-gautama-buddha|Buddha-recollection]], the four *brahmavihāra*s loving-kindness/compassion/sympathetic-joy/equanimity, and ānāpānasati breath-meditation)
9. The Divine Abidings (*brahmavihāra* in detail)
10. The Immaterial States
11. Concentration (*samādhi*) — defined and analyzed
12. The Supernormal Powers (*iddhi*)
13. Direct Knowledges

**Part III — Paññā (Wisdom)** [chapters 14-23]:
14. The Aggregates (*pañca-khandha*)
15. The Bases and Elements (*āyatana* + *dhātu*)
16. The Faculties and Truths (*indriya* + *sacca*)
17. Dependent Origination ([[pratitya-samutpada]] in 12-fold form)
18. Purification of View (*diṭṭhi-visuddhi*)
19. Purification by Overcoming Doubt
20. Purification by Knowledge and Vision of What Is and What Is Not the Path
21. Purification by Knowledge and Vision of the Way (**the *vipassanā-ñāṇa* sequence** — the progressive stages of insight; the most influential single chapter of the work)
22. Purification by Knowledge and Vision (the four supramundane paths: stream-entry, once-return, non-return, arahant)
23. The Benefits in Developing Understanding

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Sri Lanka in the early-to-mid 5th c. CE; the Anurādhapura kingdom under King Mahānāma (r. ~412-434) — the same king under whom the *Mahāvaṃsa* historical chronicle is also being composed. The Sinhalese state is patron to the Mahāvihāra, the principal Theravāda monastic institution; the rival Abhayagiri and Jetavana fraternities are also active. Buddhaghosa's work consolidates the Mahāvihāra orthodoxy against the rival Sinhalese fraternities.
- **Religious milieu.** The Pali canon is closed (~-29 BCE Aluvihāra writing-down ~450 years earlier); the Sinhalese-language commentaries (*Sīhalaṭṭhakathā*) are the established interpretive apparatus but are inaccessible to non-Sinhalese Theravāda communities. Buddhaghosa's project is to translate and systematize these commentaries into Pali, the international Theravāda lingua franca — re-canonizing the tradition for cross-regional use.
- **Who was behind it.** [[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa]] himself, working at the Mahāvihāra. His preface frames the work as a test-piece commissioned by the Mahāvihāra elders to demonstrate his competence before being entrusted with the full commentarial translation program. Internal evidence supports a single-author work with editorial assistance.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous transmission from composition to the present, with no significant interruption. The work has been the standard taught-text of Theravāda monastic education across Sri Lanka, Burma, Thailand, Cambodia, and Laos for ~1500 years.

## Primary text — selected excerpts

The excerpts below use Bhikkhu Ñāṇamoli's 1956 translation (Buddhist Publication Society, Kandy), the standard English version.

### Opening framing — the introductory verse

> "When a wise man, established well in virtue, develops consciousness and understanding, then as a *bhikkhu* ardent and sagacious, he succeeds in disentangling this tangle.
>
> "I shall expound the *Path of Purification* as told by the dwellers in the Great Monastery, who shed light upon the dispensation of the Conqueror..."

Citation: Visuddhimagga I.1-2, trans. Ñāṇamoli 1956.

### Chapter VII — Buddha-recollection (*buddhānussati*)

The Buddha-recollection meditation:

> "He recollects the Blessed One thus: 'That Blessed One is such since he is accomplished (*arahaṃ*), fully enlightened (*sammā-sambuddho*), endowed with [clear] vision and [virtuous] conduct (*vijjā-caraṇa-sampanno*), sublime (*sugato*), knower of the worlds (*lokavidū*), incomparable leader of men to be tamed (*anuttaro purisa-damma-sārathi*), teacher of gods and men (*satthā deva-manussānam*), enlightened (*buddho*), blessed (*bhagavā*)."

These are the **nine epithets of the Buddha** that became the standard liturgical formula of Theravāda Buddha-recollection practice, recited daily in monastic communities across the Theravāda world.

### Chapter VIII — ānāpānasati (mindfulness of breathing)

The Visuddhimagga's exposition of ānāpānasati is the most detailed classical Pali treatment of breath-meditation:

> "He, having gone to the forest or to the root of a tree or to an empty place, sits down; having folded his legs crosswise, set his body erect, established mindfulness in front of him, ever mindful he breathes in, mindful he breathes out... Breathing in long, he understands: 'I breathe in long'; breathing out long, he understands: 'I breathe out long.' Breathing in short, he understands: 'I breathe in short'; breathing out short, he understands: 'I breathe out short.'"

Citation: Visuddhimagga VIII.145-244 (the ānāpānasati treatment is one of the longest single meditation-expositions of the work).

This is the foundational classical source for breath-meditation in the modern global *vipassanā* and mindfulness traditions.

### Chapter XXI — the vipassanā-ñāṇa sequence

The most influential single chapter of the Visuddhimagga for the modern vipassanā revival. Buddhaghosa lists the progressive stages of insight (eight or sixteen depending on counting) through which the meditator passes en route to stream-entry:

1. Knowledge of comprehension (*sammasana-ñāṇa*)
2. Knowledge of rise-and-fall (*udayabbaya-ñāṇa*)
3. Knowledge of dissolution (*bhaṅga-ñāṇa*)
4. Knowledge of appearance as terror (*bhayatupaṭṭhāna-ñāṇa*)
5. Knowledge of danger (*ādīnava-ñāṇa*)
6. Knowledge of disenchantment (*nibbidā-ñāṇa*)
7. Knowledge of desire for deliverance (*muñcitukamyatā-ñāṇa*)
8. Knowledge of re-observation (*paṭisaṅkhā-ñāṇa*)
9. Knowledge of equanimity about formations (*saṅkhārupekkhā-ñāṇa*)
10. Knowledge of conformity (*anuloma-ñāṇa*)
11. Change-of-lineage (*gotrabhū*)
12. Path-knowledge (*magga-ñāṇa*)
13. Fruition-knowledge (*phala-ñāṇa*)
14. Reviewing-knowledge (*paccavekkhaṇa-ñāṇa*)

This sequence — derived by Buddhaghosa from earlier Pali commentarial material — is the operative map of insight-meditation development in Mahāsi Sayadaw's 20th-c. Burmese vipassanā method and its global descendants.

## Themes and motifs
- [[four-noble-truths]] — articulated systematically; chapter 16 ("The Faculties and Truths") is the standard Theravāda exposition.
- [[noble-eightfold-path]] — articulated in three-trainings structure (the three trainings *encompass* the eightfold path; sīla = right-speech/action/livelihood; samādhi = right-effort/mindfulness/concentration; paññā = right-view/intention).
- [[pratitya-samutpada]] — chapter 17 is the most detailed Theravāda treatment of dependent origination in 12-fold form.
- [[anatman-no-self]] — the anātman analysis pervades the work; chapters 14-15 articulate it in Abhidhamma technical detail.
- **Forty meditation subjects** (*kammaṭṭhāna*) — chapters 4-13 catalog and analyze the forty traditional meditation objects: ten *kasiṇa*s (colored disks), ten *asubha* (foulness contemplations), ten recollections, four *brahmavihāra*, four immaterial states, one perception (food-loathsomeness), one definition (four elements). Standard reference-list of Theravāda meditation.

## Connections (provisional)
- → **Pali Tipiṭaka** — *commentary-on*: the Visuddhimagga synthesizes the Tipiṭaka. The Sutta-piṭaka and Abhidhamma-piṭaka commentaries are companion works.
- → [[phase-2-016-early-buddhist-suttas]] — *commentary-on*: Buddhaghosa's *Sumaṅgalavilāsinī* (commentary on Dīgha Nikāya) and *Papañcasūdanī* (commentary on Majjhima Nikāya) are the companion commentaries to the early Pali suttas.
- → [[phase-2-029-dhammapada]] — *commentary-on*: Buddhaghosa's *Dhammapada-aṭṭhakathā* (the standard commentary, with the prose stories framing each verse) is another companion work.
- → ***Vimuttimagga* of Upatissa** (~1st c. BCE, preserved in Chinese as *Jietuo dao lun* 解脫道論) — *redaction-of*: the older Theravāda meditation manual that Buddhaghosa probably knew and significantly revised. The structural similarities are too close to be independent. Bapat 1937 is the foundational comparison.
- → 20th-c. global vipassanā movement — *influences*: Mahāsi Sayadaw's vipassanā method (Burma, 1940s onward), S.N. Goenka's "Goenka method" (1969 onward), the Insight Meditation Society's Western reception (Goldstein, Salzberg, Kornfield, 1975 onward), Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (Kabat-Zinn, 1979 onward) — all draw substantially from the Visuddhimagga's vipassanā-ñāṇa chapter and ānāpānasati chapter.

## Disputes
- **Date**: 410-450 is the consensus; precise year is unrecoverable.
- **Buddhaghosa as composer vs. compiler**: as noted at [[buddhaghosa]], Buddhaghosa's prefaces claim he is *translating* older Sinhalese commentaries; modern scholarship reads this as partly conventional modesty: he is also systematizing in distinctively new ways. The original Sinhalese commentaries are lost, so the precise mix is unrecoverable.
- **Relation to the *Vimuttimagga***: Bapat 1937 demonstrated the structural parallels; some Theravāda apologetic tradition has been uncomfortable with the implication that Buddhaghosa drew heavily on an earlier work; modern scholarship accepts the relationship without theological anxiety.
- **Status of the vipassanā-ñāṇa schema**: Buddhaghosa's 16-stage map is treated as authoritative by Mahāsi Sayadaw and the modern Burmese vipassanā lineage; it is treated more cautiously by some other Theravāda lineages (notably the Thai forest tradition) that emphasize the suttas over the commentarial schemas.
