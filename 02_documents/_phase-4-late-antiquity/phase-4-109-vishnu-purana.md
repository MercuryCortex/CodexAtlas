# Vishnu Purāṇa

The **Vishnu Purāṇa** is one of the 18 *Mahāpurāṇas* — the great Sanskrit encyclopedic texts that define classical Hinduism — and one of the oldest and most theologically systematic of the Vaishnava Puranas, composed in its present form c. 4th–5th century CE during the Gupta imperial period. It presents the complete cosmological and soteriological framework of Vaishnava theology: the nature of [[vishnu]] as supreme deity, the structure of cosmic time (*yugas*, *kalpas*, *manvantaras*), and the *avatara* doctrine (Vishnu's ten primary descents into the world to restore cosmic order).

## The avatars of Vishnu in the Vishnu Purāṇa

The text narrates several of Vishnu's descents (the *fixed* list of exactly ten — the *daśāvatāra* — is standardized only later, in the Bhāgavata, Garuḍa, and Agni Purāṇas; the Vishnu Purāṇa devotes its fifth book largely to Kṛṣṇa):
- [[matsya-avatar]] — the Fish: rescues Manu (the first man) and the Vedas from the cosmic flood
- [[kurma-avatar]] — the Tortoise: supports Mount Mandara during the churning of the cosmic ocean
- [[varaha-avatar]] — the Boar: rescues the earth goddess from the demon Hiraṇyākṣa
- Narasimha — the Man-Lion: destroys the demon Hiraṇyakaśipu, who had a boon protecting him from death by man, beast, day, night, indoors, or outdoors
- Vāmana — the Dwarf: recovers the three worlds from the demon king Bali in three steps
- Paraśurāma, Rāma, [[krishna]], Balarāma, Kalki

The avatara theology of the Vishnu Purāṇa is the Indic parallel to Incarnation theology in Christianity — the supreme deity taking a bounded form to act in history on behalf of the righteous. Both are structurally responses to the same theological problem: how does an omnipotent deity relate to a world in crisis?
