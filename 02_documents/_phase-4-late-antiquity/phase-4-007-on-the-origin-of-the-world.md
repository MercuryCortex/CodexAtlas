# On the Origin of the World

## What it is
An anonymous Gnostic cosmogonic treatise opening with an argument against the philosophical claim that chaos is primordial. The author argues that *Pistis* (Faith) is prior to chaos; Sophia emanates Yaldabaoth, who creates the seven archons and the material cosmos; Eros, Psyche, and the human pair are produced; the text expands with a long passage on the phoenix, the angels of the seven planets, the bestiary of the underworld, and an eschatological scenario ending with the destruction of the archons. Among the most ambitious Gnostic syntheses: pulls Sethian myth, Valentinian terminology, Hermetic cosmology, and Hellenistic astrology into one rolling narrative.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Late Sethian phase — the system has been opened up to absorb Hermetic and astrological materials.
- **Who was behind it.** A learned Alexandrian-style syncretist. The author engages Greek philosophy directly, naming "the philosophers" as opponents.

## Themes and motifs
- [[demiurge]] — Yaldabaoth's creation and reign.
- [[end-times-judgment]] — a developed apocalyptic finale, rare in Gnostic texts.
- [[divine-feminine]] — Pistis Sophia as cosmic-prior, distinct from the fallen Sophia.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-006-hypostasis-of-the-archons]] — *direct-quote* / *shared-source*: the two texts share verbatim passages on the archons' creation of Adam.
- → [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]] — *parallel-motif*: same broad Sethian narrative but with significant additions (Eros, phoenix, eschaton).
- → [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] — *shared-milieu*: Hermetic cosmological grammar borrowed.
- → Hellenistic astrology (Phase 3) — *direct-influence*: the seven archons mapped to planets.
