# Corpus Areopagiticum

## What it is
A late-5th / early-6th-century Christian corpus pseudonymously attributed to Dionysius the Areopagite, the Athenian convert of Paul in Acts 17:34. Four treatises: *Divine Names* (apophatic and cataphatic predicates of God), *Mystical Theology* (the unknowing approach to God beyond names), *Celestial Hierarchy* (the nine ranks of angels, the source of standard Christian angelology), *Ecclesiastical Hierarchy* (the corresponding earthly hierarchy of bishop, priest, deacon and the sacraments as initiatic mysteries). The corpus is the most thoroughgoing Christian appropriation of Neoplatonist (especially Proclean) metaphysics, often by direct verbatim borrowing.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Late-5th-century Syrian Christianity engaged in the Monophysite-Chalcedonian conflict. The corpus' first attestation is on the Monophysite side at the 533 colloquy.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Greek transmission, with Latin translation by Hilduin (~835) and the much better translation by John Scotus Eriugena (~860) bringing Dionysius into Western mysticism.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hidden-god]] — the most systematic Christian apophatic theology before Eckhart.
- [[psychopomp]] / hierarchical ascent — the soul rises through the celestial hierarchy to its source.
- [[mystery-initiation]] — sacraments treated as initiations.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-021-proclus-elements-of-theology]] — *redaction-of*: Koch and Stiglmayr 1895 demonstrated verbatim borrowing. Pseudo-Dionysius is, in significant part, Proclus Christianized.
- → [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — *indirect-influence*: Plotinian apophasis transmitted via Proclus and the Cappadocians.
- → Maximus the Confessor (~580–662) — *commentary-on* / *direct-influence*: Maximus' scholia were transmitted with the Dionysian corpus and made it authoritative for Eastern Christianity.
- → John Scotus Eriugena — *direct-influence*: Eriugena's translation (~860) and his own *Periphyseon* derive heavily from Dionysius.
- → Aquinas — *direct-influence*: Aquinas comments extensively on Dionysius, especially on the divine names.

## Disputes
- **Pseudonymous identity.** Until 1895 the corpus was widely accepted as apostolic; Koch and Stiglmayr's demonstration of Proclean borrowing settled it. Specific author still unknown — proposals include Peter the Iberian (Honigmann), Severus of Antioch (less plausible), an anonymous Monophysite (mainstream).
