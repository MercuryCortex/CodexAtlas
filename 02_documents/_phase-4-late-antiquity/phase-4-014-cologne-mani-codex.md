# Cologne Mani Codex

## What it is
A miniature Greek parchment codex giving an account of Mani's youth, prophetic call, and break with the Elchasaite Jewish-Christian baptist sect into which he was born. The work consists of testimonies — Mani speaking in the first person, and citations from his disciples (Salmaios the Ascetic, Innaios, Abjesus, Baraies the Teacher) — assembled into a chronological narrative. Topics include Mani's twin (his syzygos / heavenly counterpart) revealing himself at ages 4, 12, and 24; Mani's disputes with the Elchasaite elders over ritual washing, vegetable preparation, and the proper relation to Jesus; the divine command to leave the sect; and the beginning of his public mission.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The codex was composed in the Manichaean diaspora in the late Roman Empire (Egypt) but reaches back to events in third-century southern Mesopotamia under the Parthian and early Sasanian empires. Mani (216–276/277) lived under Ardashir I and Shapur I, who initially gave him protection.
- **Religious milieu.** Pre-Mani: the Elchasaites — a Jewish-Christian baptist sect with prophetic and dualistic features. Mani's transformation of his inherited Elchasaite religion into a universal world-religion (with explicit syncretic claims to absorb Jesus, Zoroaster, and the Buddha) is the central religious development the codex documents.
- **Who was behind it.** Mani's immediate disciples; the codex is a kind of *acts* of the prophet, modeled in part on Christian apostolic literature.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Acquired in Egypt; identified 1969 at the University of Cologne. The 1969–1988 publication sequence transformed Manichaean studies, providing for the first time near-contemporary autobiographical material on Mani's break with Jewish-Christianity.

## Themes and motifs
- [[dualism-cosmic]] — explicit Manichaean cosmic dualism (Light vs. Darkness, Father of Greatness vs. Prince of Darkness).
- [[mystery-initiation]] — the heavenly twin's repeated revelations to Mani.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — Mani's break with the Elchasaites is grounded in revealed knowledge against ritual practice.

## Disputes
- **Date of Mani's death.** Sasanian Christian sources give 274; Coptic and Iranian sources give 276 or 277. We carry the range here.
- **Mani's Elchasaite origin.** Confirmed by the codex against earlier doubt. Henrichs-Koenen's identification of *Alchasaios* in the codex with the Elchasai of the heresiologists settled the question.
