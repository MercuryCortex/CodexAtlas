# Confessions

## What it is
Thirteen books in which Augustine narrates his life from infancy through his mother Monica's death and his return to North Africa, framed as a continuous prayer to God. Books 1–9 are autobiography; Books 10–13 are an analysis of memory and time, and an allegorical commentary on Genesis 1. The work is the first major Western autobiography, the founding text of Christian introspective literature, and our principal source on Augustine's nine years (~373–382) as a Manichaean Hearer and his subsequent conversion via Neoplatonist reading and Ambrose's preaching to Catholic Christianity.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late Roman Empire under Theodosius and Honorius; Christianity is now the state religion (post-380 [[event-edict-of-thessalonica-380]]).
- **Religious milieu.** Augustine moves through three intellectual frameworks: Ciceronian philosophical aspiration; Manichaeism (~373–382) which solved the problem of evil through cosmic dualism; Neoplatonism (~384) which solved the problem of God's incorporeality through Plotinus' apophatic emanationism; and finally Catholic Christianity (baptized 387) which Augustine reads through both his Manichaean and Neoplatonic experiences.
- **Who was behind it.** Augustine, by then bishop of Hippo, writing in his early-to-mid forties.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Latin transmission; one of the most-copied texts of the Latin Middle Ages.

## Themes and motifs
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — Augustine's structure of conversion is *recognition* of who he already is in God; Manichaean and Gnostic resonances persist even as he refutes them.
- [[soul-as-spark]] — the soul as restless until it rests in God; the *Confessions*' famous opening.
- [[dualism-spirit-matter]] — Augustine rejects the Manichaean cosmic dualism but inherits an *anthropological* dualism (spirit/flesh) that pervades his later thought.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — *direct-influence*: Augustine reports reading "the Platonist books" (Confessions 7.9, 7.20) — almost certainly Plotinus through Marius Victorinus' Latin translation, plus possibly some Porphyry. The Plotinian framework was decisive for resolving his materialism about God.
- → [[phase-4-015-kephalaia-of-the-teacher]] / [[phase-4-016-shabuhragan]] — *polemic-against* (reverse) / *autobiographical-witness*: Augustine's nine Manichaean years make him one of our most informed external witnesses to late-antique Manichaean teaching. His later anti-Manichaean works (*Contra Faustum*, *De Natura Boni*, *Contra Fortunatum*) attack the system he had practiced.
- → [[phase-4-029-augustine-city-of-god]] — *same-author*: the autobiographical Confessions and the philosophical-political City of God are Augustine's two great works.
- → [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — *direct-influence*: Augustine's reading of Romans 7 (the divided self) is foundational for his theological anthropology.

## Disputes
- **Date of composition.** ~397–401, consensus.
- **Manichaean residue.** BeDuhn 2010–13 argues Augustine's anti-Manichaean polemic concealed continuing Manichaean structural assumptions (esp. on evil, on flesh); other scholars dispute the extent. Brown 2000 (revised epilogue) accepts a partial-residue position.
