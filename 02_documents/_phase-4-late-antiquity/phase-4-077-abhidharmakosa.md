# Abhidharmakośa

## What it is
The Abhidharmakośa ("Treasury of Abhidharma") is [[vasubandhu|Vasubandhu's]] systematic synthesis-and-critique of Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma philosophy — the most consequential single work of Indian Buddhist scholasticism after [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna's]] [[phase-4-075-mulamadhyamakakarika|MMK]] and one of the foundational curricular texts of East Asian + Tibetan + (in late-antiquity) Indian Mahāyāna monastic education. The structure is distinctive: ~600 *kārikā* (verse) propositions are followed by Vasubandhu's own prose *bhāṣya* (auto-commentary). The verses present the Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma system as canonized at the [[event-fourth-buddhist-council-kanishka-c-100|Fourth Buddhist Council under Kaniṣka]] (the *Mahāvibhāṣā* tradition); the auto-commentary often critiques the Sarvāstivāda position from a **Sautrāntika** standpoint, sometimes appearing to undermine the very system the verses present. This double-voiced structure — "I present Sarvāstivāda; I criticize it" — is the work's signature and the source of its enduring scholastic generativity.

## Structure (8 chapters)
1. *Dhātu* — analysis of the elements / categories.
2. *Indriya* — analysis of the faculties.
3. *Loka* — cosmology (the three realms; rebirth; the world-system).
4. *Karma* — action.
5. *Anuśaya* — the latent passions / underlying tendencies.
6. *Mārga* and *Pudgala* — path + person.
7. *Jñāna* — knowledge.
8. *Samādhi* — meditative absorption.
9. *Pudgalavāda-pratiṣedha* — refutation of the Pudgalavāda's "person"-doctrine (an appendix; sometimes counted as the 9th chapter).

## Context
- **Political milieu**: late Kushan / Gupta period India (~+4th-5th c.); the Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma school dominant in Kashmir-Gandhāra was reaching its scholastic apex.
- **Religious milieu**: Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma had become a vast technical literature centered on the *Mahāvibhāṣā* (the Sarvāstivāda magnum opus from the Kaniṣkan Fourth Council). The Sautrāntika "those-who-rely-on-sūtras" critical school had emerged as an internal challenger. Vasubandhu writes from the position of having studied Sarvāstivāda in Kashmir, then turned critical via Sautrāntika influences — and (in the traditional biography) eventually converted to Mahāyāna under his elder brother [[asanga|Asaṅga]]'s persuasion.
- **Who was behind it**: [[vasubandhu|Vasubandhu]] himself. The two-Vasubandhus hypothesis (Frauwallner 1951 — that there were two Vasubandhus, one Abhidharma-author and one Yogācāra-author) is now generally rejected; one Vasubandhu authored both Kośa and the Yogācāra works.
- **Discovery / transmission**: continuous transmission via Sanskrit (Nepal/Tibet) + Chinese (two translations) + Tibetan. The Sanskrit text was substantially lost in India after Nālandā's destruction (1193) and recovered from a Tibetan-monastery manuscript by Rāhula Sānkṛtyāyana in 1934-1938 at the Ngor monastery in Tibet (the foundational modern recovery of the Sanskrit Abhidharmakośa).

## Themes and motifs
- **The 75 dharma-categories** — Sarvāstivāda's taxonomy of ultimate-reals; Vasubandhu's signature systematic exposition.
- **Karma and rebirth (chapter 4)** — the most detailed Buddhist treatment.
- **The *dhātu-loka* cosmology (chapter 3)** — three realms (desire/form/formless), 31 planes of existence, the *kalpa*-cycles — the classical Buddhist cosmological system.
- **Refutation of the Pudgalavāda "person" doctrine (appendix)** — Vasubandhu's defense of strict [[anatman-no-self|anātman]] against the Pudgalavādin school's attempt to introduce a person-quasi-entity.
- ***Pratītyasamutpāda*** (chapter on conditions) — the twelvefold chain in technical Abhidharma form.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[vasubandhu]] — *author*.
- → [[event-fourth-buddhist-council-kanishka-c-100]] *Mahāvibhāṣā* — *redaction-of*: the Kośa summarizes (often critically) the Mahāvibhāṣā Sarvāstivāda tradition.
- → [[phase-4-076-visuddhimagga]] (Theravāda Buddhaghosa, ~+430) — *parallel-form*: near-contemporary systematic Abhidharma synthesis from the Theravāda side. The Kośa and the Visuddhimagga are the **two parallel summae of Buddhist scholastic Abhidharma** from the same generation: Vasubandhu in the north (Sarvāstivāda-Sautrāntika), Buddhaghosa in the south (Theravāda). They do not cite each other.
- → [[asanga]] — *family-connection*: Vasubandhu's elder brother; the traditional biographical account has Asaṅga converting Vasubandhu from Sarvāstivāda-Sautrāntika to Mahāyāna-Yogācāra.
- → [[xuanzang]] — *manuscript-transmission*: Xuanzang's 651-654 Chinese translation became the standard East Asian text and the curricular foundation of the Chinese Faxiang school.
- → Tibetan monastic education — *direct-influence*: the Kośa is the canonical preliminary curriculum in all four Tibetan schools.

## Disputes
- **The two-Vasubandhus hypothesis** (Frauwallner 1951): proposed there were two distinct authors named Vasubandhu, an earlier Abhidharma-author + a later Yogācāra-author. Now generally rejected (Gold 2015 + most modern Vasubandhu scholarship): one author, with a doctrinal trajectory from Sarvāstivāda-Sautrāntika to Mahāyāna-Yogācāra.
- **Date**: ~350-430. Precise dating ties to the broader Vasubandhu chronology, which is bound up with the date of Asaṅga and of the Yogācāra school's emergence.
- **Self-criticism in the *bhāṣya***: how to read Vasubandhu's frequent Sautrāntika critique of the Sarvāstivāda verses he himself presents? Modern consensus: this is the work's deliberate scholastic-dialectical method, not editorial inconsistency.
