# The Shepherd of Hermas

## What it is
A sprawling Christian apocalyptic-visionary work in three structural divisions — **5 Visions**, **12 Mandates**, **10 Similitudes** — composed in stages by a Roman Christian named [[hermas]] across ~+95 to ~+150. The narrative frame: Hermas, a former slave-turned-Roman-Christian-businessman, receives visions from an aged-woman figure (later identified as the *Church*) and then from the *Shepherd* (a guardian-angel figure of repentance). The dominant question is **whether sin committed after baptism can be forgiven**; the Shepherd's answer, controversial for its day, is *yes, but only once*. Around this question crystallize a moral catechesis, an angelomorphic Christology, and the most elaborate **church-building allegory** in early Christian literature (the great Tower-built-of-stones-of-various-shapes vision).

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Rome under Trajan / Hadrian / Antoninus Pius (composition span). Christians in Rome are a network of socially diverse household communities — wealthy benefactors, freedmen, slaves, traders. Hermas himself appears to be a freedman (or descendant) of trader status, struggling with business and family ethics.
- **Religious milieu.** Roman Christianity is *governance-fluid* — Hermas mentions presbyters and bishops in the plural (Vis. 2.4.3, 3.5.1, Sim. 9.27.2), confirming the same college-of-presbyter-bishops picture as 1 Clement, and quite different from the contemporaneous Ignatian monoepiscopacy of Antioch. Christian *prophecy* is still alive: Hermas writes as a *receiving prophet*, presenting his visions as scripture-quality revelation, in the same charismatic stream that within decades produces *Montanism*.
- **Who was behind it.** **Hermas** himself — known otherwise only through his own self-presentation in this text and the *Muratorian Fragment*'s notice. The *Muratorian Fragment* (~+170/+180) states: "The Shepherd was very recently in our own times written by Hermas, while his brother Pius was occupying the bishop's seat of the church of the city of Rome." This places the final form ~+140/+155.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Quasi-scriptural in 2nd–4th c.: Irenaeus, Clement of Alexandria, Origen, Tertullian (early period, before turning Montanist and rejecting it), Athanasius all treat or quote the Shepherd as edifying-scripture or scripture proper. Included in **Codex Sinaiticus** as canonical appendix. **Excluded from the formed Latin canon** by the *Muratorian Fragment* on explicit grounds that it was "very recently" written — the earliest extant *historical-recency* canonical criterion.

## Themes and motifs
- [[apocalyptic-revelation]] — load-bearing locus: the entire work is an apocalypse, but a strikingly **non-eschatological apocalypse** (Osiek 1999) — the visions concern *the church*, *moral discipline*, *post-baptismal repentance*, not the end of the world. Documents that Christian apocalyptic discourse could already by ~+140 redirect toward ecclesial-disciplinary purposes.
- [[ethical-dualism]] / [[two-spirits-doctrine]] — load-bearing **substrate wire**. *Mandate 6* presents **two angels accompanying each human being** — *the angel of righteousness* (delicate, modest, gentle) and *the angel of wickedness* (sharp-tempered, bitter, foolish). This is **the Christian moral-anthropological extension of the same Two-Spirits / Two-Angels tradition** attested in Qumran's 1QS 3:13–4:26 (Prince of Lights / Angel of Darkness) and in Barnabas 18 (two angels of light and darkness). Hermas is the **fullest extant Christian *internalization*** of the Two-Spirits anthropology into individual moral psychology. The substrate is Jewish (and behind that, contested Iranian); the Christian form here is internalized and ethicized.
- [[ecclesiology]] — **the great Tower of the Church** (Vis. 3, Sim. 9): stones of various shapes being built into a tower; some fit, some are rejected, some are reused after rounding-off. The most extensive ecclesial allegory in 2nd-c. Christian literature.
- **Post-baptismal repentance** (controversial doctrine): the Shepherd grants *one* post-baptismal opportunity for repentance — a position that becomes foundational for Roman penitential practice and is later loosened.
- **Christology**. Distinctive *angelomorphic* Christology — the Son is sometimes identified with or pictured alongside the Holy Spirit (Sim. 5, 9); the *glorious angel* figure appears in Christ-roles. A datum for the pre-Nicene Christological diversity of Roman Christianity.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — *parallel-motif* / *shared-substrate*: Mandate 6's two-angels moral psychology stands in the same tradition as 1QS 3:13–4:26's Two Spirits and **(via the substrate)** ultimately the Iranian *spənta* / *angra* dualism. The most internalized, individualized Christian extension of the substrate. Critical for tracking the Two-Spirits doctrine into Christian moral psychology.
- → [[phase-4-064-epistle-of-barnabas]] — *parallel-recension* (two-angels): Barn. 18 also uses two-angels framing. Both Hermas and Barnabas preserve the two-angels variant of the Jewish-Christian Two Ways / Two Spirits tradition.
- → [[phase-4-062-1-clement]] — *shared-milieu*: contemporary Roman Christian document; same picture of plural-presbyter governance.
- → Jewish apocalyptic-visionary tradition (4 Ezra, 2 Baruch, Enoch) — *shared-genre*: Hermas's visionary frame, his interlocutor-angels, his use of revelatory dreams and animal-symbolism all sit in this lineage. Osiek 1999.
- → Stoic moral psychology — *substrate-wire*: the *Mandates*' moral instruction (12 named virtues vs. opposite vices, each personified) is recognisably Stoic in form, Christianized. Reading the *Mandates* as Stoic-Christian compendium is the standard scholarly approach.
- → [[phase-4-023-irenaeus-against-heresies]] — *direct-quote*: Iren. *AH* 4.20.2 cites Hermas as scripture.
- → [[phase-4-027-eusebius-ecclesiastical-history]] — *discussion-of*: HE 3.3, where Eusebius records its disputed canonical status.

## Disputes
- **Date and unity.** Stratification is consensus: at least three composition layers across ~+95/+150, by a single author (Hermas) over decades. *Vis. 1–4* earlier; *Vis. 5, Mand., Sim.* later; final redaction under Pius (~+140/+155).
- **Canonical status.** Active scriptural reception ~+150/+325 in many Christian communities; never formally canonized in the Western tradition; Eastern reception more enduring. The Sinaiticus inclusion documents the *active* uncertainty in the 4th c.
- **Identification with the Hermas of Rom 16:14.** Origen's identification; modern scholarship: implausible on dating grounds.
- **Christology.** The angelomorphic-Christology readings (Bucur 2007, Henne 1992) reveal that pre-Nicene Roman Christology was substantially more diverse than later orthodox-construction admits.
- **Reading the 'one post-baptismal repentance' rule.** Whether *granting* one (a lenient innovation) or *limiting* it to one (a strict tightening) — debate hinges on what the prior practice was; mainstream: a *grant* against rigorist contemporaries (Tertullian's later position) who allowed no post-baptismal repentance.
