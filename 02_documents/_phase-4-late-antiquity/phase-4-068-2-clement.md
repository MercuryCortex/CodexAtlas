# 2 Clement

## What it is
A 20-chapter Greek paraenetic-homiletic text, pseudonymously transmitted as a "second letter" of Clement of Rome alongside 1 Clement, but in fact **not by Clement, not from Rome, and not a letter**. Internal evidence (esp. 2 Clem. 19.1 — "after the God of truth I am reading you an exhortation to pay attention to what was written") identifies it as a **sermon read aloud after a scripture-reading** — i.e., **the earliest extant Christian sermon / homily**. ~+130/+160. The opening is one of the strongest early Christological statements outside the New Testament: "we ought to think of Jesus Christ as we do of God — as the judge of the living and the dead."

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Mid-2nd c., probably Antonine.
- **Religious milieu.** Christianity faces its own laxity, perceived eschatological delay, and the practical problem of post-baptismal sin. The preacher is exhorting a community to *behavioral consistency* with the (high) Christology they profess. The sermon shows familiarity with a *Gospel of the Egyptians*-style sayings collection (2 Clem. 12.2 — "the Lord himself, asked by someone when his kingdom would come, said, 'When the two shall be one, and the outside as the inside, and the male with the female neither male nor female'" — a logion paralleled in *Gospel of Thomas* 22 and reported by Clement of Alexandria as from the *Gospel of the Egyptians*). This is one of the strongest data points for the **diversity of 2nd-c. Christian gospel-traditions** still circulating in mainstream-paraenetic contexts.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous community preacher. Pseudonymous attribution to Clement appears to have happened simply by manuscript-transmission alongside 1 Clement.
- **Provenance.** Genuinely uncertain. Proposals: Corinth (the address 2 Clem. 7's reference to athletic games suggests Corinthian Isthmian setting); Rome (the manuscript-transmission tradition); Alexandria (the apocryphal-gospel citation); Syria. Tuckett 2012 favors Corinth on internal grounds; Lindemann 1992 favors Rome on transmission grounds.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuously with 1 Clement; recovered in full through Bryennios's 1875 publication of the Hierosolymitanus.

## Themes and motifs
- [[ecclesiology]] — 2 Clem. 14: the famous *pre-existent Church* doctrine — "If we do the will of God our Father, we shall belong to the first Church, the spiritual one, which was created before the sun and moon." The Church as pre-existent heavenly reality, of which the visible church is a manifestation. Striking early ecclesiology.
- [[end-times-judgment]] — load-bearing throughout: the entire sermon is paraenetic-eschatological. Christians must *behave consistently with their confession now* because the *day of judgment* is imminent. Continues the eschatological-imminence concern through the mid-2nd c., demonstrating the imminent-parousia expectation had not yet diluted into ecclesial-routine.
- [[fulfillment-of-prophecy]] — heavy use of OT scripture as Christ-fulfilled prophecy; standard *testimonia*-style hermeneutic.
- [[stoic-virtue]] — moral exhortation echoes Stoic-Hellenistic virtue-vocabulary (athletic-contest metaphor at ch. 7; perseverance, self-control, faith) — Christianized.
- **High pre-Nicene Christology** — the opening declaration is among the strongest 2nd-c. articulations of Christ-as-God outside the Johannine corpus; data point for the *pre-existing* high-Christological strand in mainstream Christianity well before Nicaea.
- **Quoted unknown-gospel sayings** — 2 Clem. 12.2 is the *Gospel of the Egyptians* (or related) logion; 2 Clem. 4.5 contains another non-canonical Jesus-saying. Critical evidence that **non-canonical sayings-traditions** were still circulating in *mainstream* paraenetic Christian preaching mid-2nd c.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-062-1-clement]] — *manuscript-transmission* / *misattribution*: transmitted as a pair from antiquity; the misattribution is the *only* connection between them.
- → [[phase-4-001-gospel-of-thomas]] — *parallel-logion*: 2 Clem. 12.2's "when the two shall be one" logion is paralleled in *Gospel of Thomas* 22 and reported elsewhere as from the *Gospel of the Egyptians*. **2 Clem. is the earliest *mainstream-Christian* witness to the wide circulation of *Thomas-style sayings traditions* in proto-orthodox preaching contexts**.
- → [[phase-4-065-shepherd-of-hermas]] — *shared-milieu*: both address post-baptismal Christian moral failure with paraenetic-eschatological framing; both reflect mid-2nd-c. Christian *practical-moral* turn.
- → [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] / Synoptic Gospels — *direct-quote*: heavy quotation; documents the **growing functional canon** mid-2nd c.

## Disputes
- **Date.** ~+130/+160 mainstream; some scholars push earlier (~+100) or later (~+170). The *Gospel of the Egyptians*-type citation suggests Egyptian or Alexandrian connection but is not decisive.
- **Provenance.** Genuinely undecided; competing proposals all plausible.
- **Genre.** Was a *sermon* in original form; the epistolary opening (1.1, "Brothers and sisters") is sermonic-address vocabulary, not letter-opening vocabulary. Tuckett 2012 settles the homily-genre identification.
- **Authorship.** Anonymous; the Clement-attribution is a transmission-accident.
- **Status of the *Gospel-of-the-Egyptians*-style citations.** Whether the preacher considered them scriptural or simply traditional Jesus-sayings is contested.
