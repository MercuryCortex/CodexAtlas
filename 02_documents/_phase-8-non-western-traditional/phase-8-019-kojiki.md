# Kojiki (Record of Ancient Matters)

## What it is

The **Kojiki** (古事記, "Record of Ancient Matters," 712 CE) is Japan's oldest surviving chronicle and the primary written source for Shintō cosmology and myth. Compiled by **Ō no Yasumaro** from the oral memory of the court reciter **Hieda no Are** at the command of **Empress Genmei**, it was completed in 712 CE and presented to the empress as an official record of the imperial family's divine lineage. It is written in a hybrid script (Chinese characters used phonetically to represent Japanese sounds — Man'yōgana) that reflects the cultural frontier it occupies: Japanese oral tradition encoded by a Chinese writing system.

The Kojiki is not a unified composition but a compilation of distinct oral strands — creation myths, genealogies, songs, ritual narratives — stitched together by Yasumaro's editorial hand. The seams are visible, making it a document of enormous value for comparative mythology.

## Structure

Three volumes (maki):

1. **Kami-tsumaki (Age of the Gods)** — cosmogony, the creation of the Japanese islands, the generation of the major kami, the conflict of Amaterasu and Susanoo, Amaterasu's retreat into the cave, Susanoo's expulsion and his defeat of the eight-headed serpent Yamata no Orochi, the descent of Ninigi from heaven
2. **Naka-tsumaki** — the early legendary emperors from Jimmu (the first human emperor, great-great-grandson of Amaterasu) through the middle imperial period
3. **Shimo-tsumaki** — later imperial history through Empress Suiko (early 7th c. CE)

The cosmological material is overwhelmingly concentrated in Volume 1.

## The creation cosmology

In the beginning there were only heaven and earth-as-chaos (*konton*). The first three deities appeared spontaneously in the High Plain of Heaven and immediately concealed themselves. After further deity-generations, **Izanagi** (He-who-invites) and **Izanami** (She-who-invites) were commanded to create the drifting land. Standing on the Floating Bridge of Heaven, they stirred the brine with the Jeweled Spear of Heaven; the drops that fell became the island of **Onogoro**. They descended to the island, performed a marriage ritual around a heavenly pillar, and gave birth to the eight great islands of Japan and a succession of deities.

**Izanami dies** giving birth to Kagutsuchi (the fire deity); her death from burns is the entry of death into the world. **Izanagi descends to Yomi** (the underworld) to retrieve her. He lights a torch and sees her body covered in maggots and eight thunder deities — a rotting corpse. The taboo of looking is broken. Izanami, now ruler of the dead, sends the Shikome (hags of the underworld) and the thunder deities to pursue him. Izanagi escapes to the land of the living and rolls a great boulder across the entrance to Yomi. Izanami and Izanagi exchange curses: she will kill a thousand people per day; he will cause fifteen hundred births per day. This is the Kojiki's explanation of death and the balance of birth and death.

**Purification after Yomi**: Izanagi performs *misogi* (ritual ablution) in a river. From his left eye is born **Amaterasu** (Great Divinity Illuminating Heaven — the sun goddess); from his right eye, **Tsukuyomi** (the moon deity); from his nose, **Susanoo** (Valiant-Swift-Impetuous-Male — storm).

**Susanoo's expulsion and Amaterasu's cave**: Susanoo weeps to return to his mother in Yomi and causes destruction. Amaterasu, terrified, retreats into a rock cave — the world goes dark, evil spirits proliferate. Eight hundred myriad deities assemble and devise a plan: they hang a sacred mirror and jewels in a tree, perform rituals, and the goddess **Ame-no-Uzume** performs an ecstatic, sexually provocative dance that causes the assembled gods to laugh uproariously. Amaterasu opens the cave to look; she is seized and pulled out; the world is restored to light.

**The Three Imperial Treasures** (*sanshu no jingi*) — the sacred mirror (Yata no Kagami), the jewel (Yasakani no Magatama), and the sword (Kusanagi no Tsurugi) — are transmitted from Amaterasu to her grandson **Ninigi**, who descends to earth as her emissary. The first human emperor Jimmu (great-great-grandson of Ninigi) thus carries divine genealogy directly traceable to the sun goddess.

## Cross-tradition MASSIVE WIN parallels

**1. Izanagi's descent to Yomi = the katabasis cluster**
Izanagi descends to the underworld to retrieve the beloved dead, breaks a taboo (light/look), fails, and flees — pursued by death-powers. This is structurally identical to **Orpheus/Eurydice** (Greek), **Inanna's Descent** (Sumerian, ~2100 BCE), **Heracles and Cerberus**, and **Persephone/Demeter**. The universal katabasis grammar — love descends to death, taboo broken, partial success, return without the beloved — appears here in its Japanese instantiation with no possible transmission from the Mesopotamian or Greek versions. This is the katabasis cluster at its purest: structural identity without contact.

**2. Amaterasu's cave = the dying-and-rising solar deity universal**
The solar deity retreats (dies), the world enters darkness, the gods perform elaborate ritual to draw her back, light is restored. This is the **Ra in the Duat** narrative (Egyptian night-journey of the sun through the underworld), the **Persephone/Demeter** winter narrative, the **Baldr's death** Norse darkness, and the **resurrection of Christ** as cosmic light-restorer. The Ame-no-Uzume ecstatic dance as the instrument of divine revival has a precise parallel in the **erotic ritual** used to revive Osiris — divine sexuality as cosmological engine.

**3. Creation by stirring primordial sea = churning cosmogony**
Izanagi and Izanami stir the primordial brine with a spear and land solidifies from the drops. This is structurally parallel to the **Samudra Manthan** (Vedic churning of the ocean, with Mount Mandara as churning-stick and Vasuki serpent as rope) and to the Egyptian **Nun** (primordial waters from which all creation emerges). The Japanese version substitutes a spear for the cosmic mountain/serpent but the underlying grammar — ordered creation achieved by agitating the formless primal sea — is identical.

**4. Amaterasu → Ninigi → Jimmu = divine kingship transmission**
The Japanese imperial lineage is constructed as a direct genealogical chain from the sun goddess. This is structurally identical to the Egyptian pharaoh as Horus (son of Ra), the Mesopotamian *en/lugal* as chosen and installed by An/Enlil, the Chinese Son of Heaven as tianming-bearer, and the Christian king as consecrated anointed (christos). The Kojiki is Japan's installation of the universal divine-kingship legitimation structure.

**5. The Three Imperial Treasures = sacred regalia as cosmic mandate**
Mirror (divine vision/wisdom) + jewel (divine spirit/fertility) + sword (divine authority/power) transmitted from heaven to earth as proof of legitimate rule. Parallel to the **Ark of the Covenant** (Israel's portable divine presence and military legitimation), the **Hindu chakravartin's chakra** (wheel of righteous rule transmitted from divine realm), the **papal tiara** and Petrine keys, and the **Irish Lia Fáil** (Stone of Destiny, the coronation stone that cried out under the rightful king).

## Significance

The Kojiki is Japan's equivalent of the book of Genesis combined with a royal genealogy: it encodes cosmogony, theodicy (death's origin), the divine foundation of the imperial institution, and the structure of the Shintō ritual world. It was not widely studied for centuries after compilation — the Nihon Shoki in Chinese was the official public text — but from the 18th century **kokugaku** (National Learning) movement (Motoori Norinaga, 1730–1801) onward, the Kojiki became the nationalist foundation of Japanese cultural identity. Its wartime mobilization and postwar rehabilitation are a modern chapter in the reception of ancient scripture as political instrument.
