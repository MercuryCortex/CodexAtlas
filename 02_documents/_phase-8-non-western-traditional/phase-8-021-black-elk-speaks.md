# Black Elk Speaks (1932)

Heȟáka Sápa (Black Elk)'s account of his visions and spiritual life as recorded by poet John G. Neihardt in 1930–31 and published 1932. The central narrative is Black Elk's Great Vision received at age nine (1872) — a cosmological vision of the sacred hoop of the nation, the Tree of Life, the Six Grandfathers (the six directions as sacred powers), and the role Black Elk was given to restore the Lakota people. DeMallie's 1984 edition of the unedited transcripts (*The Sixth Grandfather*) is the Tier-1 scholarly source; it reveals that Neihardt significantly shaped the narrative for a literary audience, occasionally omitting or altering material and adding a tragic ending that Black Elk himself did not provide. The book became one of the most widely read accounts of Indigenous American religion in the 20th century and significantly shaped non-Lakota understanding (and misunderstanding) of Plains religion.
