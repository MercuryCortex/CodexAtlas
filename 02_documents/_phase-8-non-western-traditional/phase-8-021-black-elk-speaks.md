# Black Elk Speaks

## What it is
The life story and visions of **Heȟáka Sápa (Black Elk)**, an Oglala Lakota holy man, as recorded by the poet **John G. Neihardt** in 1930–31 and published in 1932 — one of the most widely read books on Indigenous American religion ever written. Its heart is Black Elk's **Great Vision**, received in a coma at about age nine: a cosmological revelation of the sacred hoop of the nation, the flowering tree at the center of the world, the **Six Grandfathers** (the powers of the six directions), and the charge laid on Black Elk to help his people walk the red road. The book then follows his life through the buffalo-hunting years, the wars, the killing of Crazy Horse, the Ghost Dance, and the massacre at Wounded Knee (1890).

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Reservation-era Pine Ridge, two generations after the destruction of the Plains buffalo economy and the suppression of the Ghost Dance.
- **Religious milieu.** Lakota visionary religion centered on *Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka* (the Great Mysterious) and the sacred pipe; Black Elk was also, by 1930, a long-serving Catholic catechist — a fact the book silently omits.
- **Who was behind it.** Black Elk as visionary source; Neihardt as the shaping author; Ben Black Elk as interpreter.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Initially little-read, the book was rediscovered in the 1960s–70s and became a counterculture and academic touchstone; DeMallie's 1984 transcript edition reframed all subsequent scholarship.

## Themes and motifs
- [[wakan-tanka]] — the Great Mysterious pervades the Great Vision; the whole revelation is an unveiling of *Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka*'s ordering of the world.
- [[white-buffalo-calf-woman]] — the bringer of the sacred pipe stands behind the Lakota ritual world Black Elk inherits.

## Connections (provisional)

## Disputes
- **Mediated authorship.** The central scholarly issue is how much of *Black Elk Speaks* is Black Elk and how much is Neihardt; DeMallie (1984) shows substantial literary reshaping, including the famous despairing ending.
- **The omitted Catholicism.** Black Elk's decades as a Catholic catechist, absent from the 1932 book, complicate every reading of him as a "last" traditional holy man (Holler 1995).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **A case study in the mediation of Indigenous religion.** Precisely because *Black Elk Speaks* is a collaboration across a vast cultural and literary gap, it is the textbook example of how Indigenous spirituality reaches a global audience already shaped by the outsider's hand — the gap between Black Elk's words and Neihardt's book (recoverable through DeMallie) is itself one of the most instructive documents in the study of how religions are transmitted and transformed (DeMallie 1984; Holler 1995).
