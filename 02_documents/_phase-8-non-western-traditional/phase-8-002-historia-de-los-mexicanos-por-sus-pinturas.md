# Historia de los Mexicanos por sus Pinturas

## What it is
A short but pivotal early-colonial text — its title means "**History of the Mexicans as told by their paintings**" — that records the **Aztec cosmogony** drawn directly from native pictorial books in the 1530s–40s. It is the single most systematic source for the Mexica creation myth: the primordial dual creator **Ometeotl / Tonacatecuhtli-and-Tonacacihuatl**; their four sons, the four **Tezcatlipocas** (among them the black [[tezcatlipoca]] and the white [[quetzalcoatl]]), who order the cosmos; the sequence of the **Five Suns** (world-ages), each created and destroyed in turn — by jaguars, by wind, by fiery rain, and by a great **flood** — until the present Fifth Sun is born through the gods' self-sacrifice at Teotihuacan. For the deep structure of Aztec religious thought, this brief text is foundational.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The first post-conquest generation, when Nahua painted books still survived and could be read aloud by native elders to Spanish recorders.
- **Religious milieu.** A Franciscan-mediated transcription of a cosmogony that was already, in the codices, a sophisticated theology of cyclical creation and divine sacrifice.
- **Who was behind it.** An anonymous Spanish recorder (plausibly Olmos), transmitting native pictorial-oral tradition.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Preserved through the 19th-century document collections of García Icazbalceta and made standard by Garibay's 1965 edition.

## Themes and motifs
- [[flood-motif]] — the destruction of the Fourth Sun by a world-flood is the Mesoamerican member of the cross-cultural flood pattern.
- [[divine-kingship]] — the cosmogony grounds Mexica rulership in the gods' ordering and re-creation of the world.

## Disputes
- **Authorship and date.** The attribution to Olmos and the precise date (1530s–40s) are inferred, not certain.
- **Reconstructing a lost pictorial.** Because the text translates now-lost painted books into Spanish prose, how faithfully it renders the original Nahua cosmogony is a matter of careful comparison with the Florentine Codex and the codices that survive.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **A Mesoamerican flood, independently grown.** The destruction of one world-age by a universal flood places Aztec cosmogony alongside the Mesopotamian, biblical, Greek, and Indian flood traditions the Atlas tracks — a structural parallel with no possible Old-World contact, the cleanest kind of evidence that the [[flood-motif]] recurs independently (Nicholson 1971; León-Portilla 1963).
- **The grammar of Aztec theology.** The dual creator and the four-Tezcatlipoca ordering of the cosmos, preserved here, is the framework on which the whole Mexica pantheon in this vault hangs — the cosmogonic *root* of the deity layer (Garibay 1965; Nicholson 1971).
