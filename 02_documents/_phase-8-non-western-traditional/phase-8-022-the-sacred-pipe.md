# The Sacred Pipe (1953)

The primary ethnographic source for the Seven Sacred Rites of the Lakota, as narrated by Heȟáka Sápa (Black Elk) to Joseph Epes Brown during the winter of 1947–48. The seven rites documented: (1) *Inípi* (Sweat Lodge), (2) *Haŋbléčheyapi* (Vision Quest), (3) *Wiwáŋyaŋg Wačhípi* (Sun Dance), (4) *Huŋkápi* (Making of Relatives), (5) *Išnáthi Awíčhalowaŋpi* (Keeping of the Soul), (6) *Tȟápa Waŋkáyeyapi* (Throwing of the Ball), (7) *Naǧí Glúhapi* (Coming of Age). Each rite is narrated with its origin myth (most involving [[white-buffalo-calf-woman]]) and ceremonial structure. The *čaŋnúŋpa* (sacred pipe) mediates every rite. This is a Tier-1 source — more reliable than *Black Elk Speaks* (1932) because Brown's recording was more literal than Neihardt's literary shaping. See [[tradition-lakota]].
