# The Sacred Pipe

## What it is
The principal documentary account of the **seven sacred rites of the Lakota**, narrated by the Oglala holy man **Black Elk** to Joseph Epes Brown over the winter of 1947–48 and published in 1953. Where *Black Elk Speaks* gives Black Elk's life and vision, *The Sacred Pipe* gives the ceremonial *system*: the seven rites brought by the **White Buffalo Calf Woman**, each with its origin story and ritual structure, all mediated by the *čhaŋnúŋpa*, the sacred pipe whose smoke joins the people to *Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka*.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Mid-20th-century Pine Ridge, when much Lakota ceremony had been driven underground by decades of suppression; Black Elk's account became a record for a tradition under pressure.
- **Religious milieu.** The pipe-centered ceremonial complex of the Lakota, framed by the gift of the White Buffalo Calf Woman.
- **Who was behind it.** Black Elk as ceremonial authority; Brown as a sympathetic recorder closer to the comparative-religion (Perennialist) school.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuously in print; foundational for later ceremonial revitalization and for outside study.

## The seven rites
1. *Inípi* — the sweat lodge (purification); 2. *Haŋbléčeyapi* — the vision quest ("crying for a vision"); 3. *Wiwáŋyaŋg Wačhípi* — the Sun Dance; 4. *Huŋkápi* — the making-of-relatives rite; 5. *Wanáǧi Yuhápi* — the keeping of the soul; 6. *Tȟápa Waŋkáyeyapi* — the throwing of the ball; 7. *Išnáthi Awíčhalowaŋpi* — the girl's coming-of-age rite. Most are traced to the gift of [[white-buffalo-calf-woman]].

## Themes and motifs
- [[white-buffalo-calf-woman]] — the culture-heroine who brought the pipe and the rites; her gift is the charter of the whole ceremonial order.
- [[wakan-tanka]] — every rite is directed toward, and the pipe-smoke ascends to, *Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka*.

## Disputes
- **Brown's framing.** Brown read Lakota religion partly through a Perennialist (Traditionalist) lens; how far that shaped the presentation is discussed in the scholarship (DeMallie & Parks 1987).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The charter of pipe religion, recorded under threat.** *The Sacred Pipe* preserved, at a moment of acute pressure on Lakota ceremony, the systematic account of the seven rites that would help anchor their 20th-century revival — a case of an outsider-mediated text feeding back into a living tradition's own continuity (DeMallie & Parks 1987; Holler 1995).
