# Ifá Divination Corpus (Odu Ifá)

## What it is
The literary-divinatory corpus of the Yoruba religion, structured as 256 *odu* — binary signs produced by manipulating sixteen palm nuts or an *opele* divining chain. Each of the 256 odu (16 "principal" *Oju Odu* generated by self-pairings, plus 240 "minor" combinations) carries an associated body of verses (*ese*) recounting precedent cases, mythological exemplars, ritual prescriptions (*ebọ*), and ethical commentary. The full corpus runs to tens of thousands of memorized verses; mastery requires decades of apprenticeship under a *babaláwo* ("father-of-secrets," Ifá priest). The system is operated by Òrúnmìlà, the orisha of wisdom and divination; the diviner consults Òrúnmìlà via the binary signature produced by the palm nuts, then recites relevant verses whose application the client interprets.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Yorùbáland (southwestern Nigeria, Benin, Togo) — multiple historical polities centered on Ile-Ifẹ̀ (sacred-mythic origin city), the Oyo Empire (~1300–1836), and other kingdoms. The diaspora was forced by the trans-Atlantic slave trade (~1500–1866), which carried millions of Yoruba people (especially after the collapse of Oyo) to Brazil, Cuba, Haiti, and Trinidad — where Ifá and the orisha cult continued as Candomblé, Lukumí/Santería, Vodou, Shango cult.
- **Religious milieu.** Yoruba religion is a layered system: *Olódùmarè* (the high god) is essentially deus otiosus; *Orisha* (lower deities) mediate between Olódùmarè and humans; *Egungun* (ancestors) and personal *Orí* (destiny / personal head-god) complete the system. Ifá is the diagnostic-divinatory apparatus by which the entire system is operationally consulted.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous, accumulated over centuries by the *babaláwo* lineages. Earliest textualization: Christian missionaries (Bishop Samuel Ajayi Crowther's Yoruba translation work, 1850s, codified Yoruba orthography that later enabled Ifá transcription). Scholarly textualization: William Bascom from 1937; Wande Abimbola from 1968; institutional Yoruba-academy publications from the 1970s onward.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The tradition was never lost — it is a continuous oral-priestly practice. What changed in the 20th century was *external scholarly access*: Bascom's anthropology, Abimbola's insider-scholar textualization, and the UNESCO 2005 declaration of Ifá as Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divinatory-systems]] — Ifá is one of three major world divinatory systems with comparable scope (Chinese *I Ching*, Mesopotamian *Enūma Anu Enlil*); binary signs generate retrievable narrative-precedent.
- [[oral-textuality]] — Ifá demonstrates that "scripture" need not be written: a memorized verse corpus larger than the Bible exists in trained priestly memory.
- [[orisha-pantheon]] — Ifá verses constantly invoke and explain orisha relations.
- [[destiny-doctrine]] — Yoruba *orí* doctrine (one's destiny is chosen before birth from a portion-of-being) is articulated through Ifá verses.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-8-016-haitian-vodou-liturgical-tradition]] — *manuscript-transmission* (oral): Haitian Vodou's *Rada* rite is the Dahomean and Yoruba liturgical inheritance transmitted via Middle Passage; many *lwa* (Vodou spirits) are recognizably orishas.
- → [[phase-8-017-santeria-lucumi]] — *manuscript-transmission* (oral): Cuban Lukumí is the closest diaspora preservation of Yoruba liturgy; the *Dilogún* (sixteen-cowries) oracle is a simplified-portable version of Ifá. Ref: Brandon 1993; Bascom 1980.
- → I Ching (Phase 1–2 Chinese) — *parallel-motif*: binary divination system with associated verse-corpus. No contact hypothesis; striking structural parallel of "world's two great binary-divinatory systems" (Bascom's framing).
- → Arabic geomancy (*ʿilm al-raml*) — *contested-parallel*: structural homology with West African Ifá is real (16 figures, sand-marking method). Direction of influence debated: Skinner (1980) argued African origin; Brenner argued Arab → African; most current scholarship treats them as historically related but with mutual elaboration. Ref: Brenner (2000), "Sufism in Africa."
- → [[phase-8-002-codex-borgia]] — *parallel-motif* (priestly divinatory technology): structurally analogous priest-divination apparatus across continents.

## Disputes
- **Pre-colonial date depth.** How old is the 256-odu structure? Bascom (1969) suggested it stabilized in the medieval period; some Yoruba sources claim much earlier antiquity. Definite documentary attestation only goes back to early-modern European-Yoruba contact.
- **Relation to Arabic geomancy.** See Connections above.
- **Canonical authority.** No central textual authority; each *babaláwo* lineage carries authoritative variants. Modern textual collections (Abimbola, others) are reference compendia, not canon.
