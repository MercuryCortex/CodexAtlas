# Walam Olum

## What it is
*Documented here as a contested document — Phase 8 explicit source-criticism case.*

The Walam Olum (claimed: "Red Score") is a 183-verse epic published by Constantine Samuel Rafinesque-Schmaltz in 1836 as a Lenape (Delaware Indian) historical-cosmogonic narrative recovered from inscribed wooden tablets. The text purports to recount Lenape origins through a creation account (a turtle-creator figure, primordial waters), a great-flood narrative, a migration from the west across "frozen waters" (read by 19th-c. interpreters as Bering Strait), and a tribal-historical chronicle of named *sakima* (chiefs) down to the time of European contact. Each verse is paired with a pictographic glyph; the package was for over 150 years taken as the principal surviving long Indigenous-North-American historical chronicle.

David Oestreicher's 1994 Rutgers dissertation and 1995–1996 publications demonstrated, through reverse linguistic and source-critical analysis, that the Walam Olum is **a fabrication** by Rafinesque, composed by back-translating English material into a Lenape vocabulary using contemporary Lenape grammars and dictionaries. The "wooden tablets" never existed; Rafinesque's working notebooks show the compositional method. The Delaware Tribe of Indians formally accepted Oestreicher's conclusions in 1997 and removed the Walam Olum from their official historical narrative.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Constantine Rafinesque (1783–1840) was an autodidact polymath active in Kentucky, Ohio, and Pennsylvania — botanist, malacologist, archaeologist, linguist. His career was marked by both genuine pioneering work (taxonomy of Ohio Valley flora) and self-aggrandizing fabrication; the Walam Olum is the most consequential of the latter. He published it as evidence for his pet theory of Indigenous American origin (Asian descent via Bering migration).
- **Religious milieu.** 19th-c. American Indigenous-origin theorizing: was a politically loaded enterprise. Mormon claims of Hebrew-descent Indigenous Americans (Book of Mormon 1830) competed with various "lost tribes" and "Bering migration" theories. Rafinesque's Bering-migration narrative was theologically nonpartisan but politically useful to those wanting to assimilate Indigenous Americans to Old World origins.
- **Who was behind it.** Rafinesque alone, drawing on real Lenape linguistic resources (Heckewelder, Zeisberger) and 19th-c. Romantic-comparative imagination.
- **Discovery / transmission.** *No physical source manuscript exists.* Rafinesque claimed to have received pictograph copies from "Dr. Ward of Indiana" in 1820 and a separate Lenape-language version through John Burns; neither Ward nor the tablets can be independently verified, and Oestreicher's analysis demonstrates the back-translation direction of composition.

## Themes and motifs
- [[disputed-historicity]] — the principal Phase-8 case study in how to treat a document whose authenticity is contested *and now resolved against authenticity*.
- [[forgery-and-fabrication]] — sits alongside the Hitler Diaries, the Donation of Constantine, the Protocols of the Elders of Zion, and various pseudepigraphal religious texts as a case in document criticism.
- [[indigenous-historiography]] — the painful irony: the Walam Olum was for over a century the principal extended example of "Indigenous historical chronicle," shaping non-Indigenous and even some Indigenous understandings of Lenape history. Its exposure forced rebuilding around actual Indigenous historical sources.
- [[bering-strait-migration]] — the Walam Olum was deployed to support what was independently a defensible archaeological claim; the migration is genuine, the chronicle is not.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Book of Mormon (Phase 7 NRM) — *parallel-motif* / *polemic-against*: both are 19th-c. American documents purporting to recover ancient Indigenous American religious-historical chronicles; both are fabricated; both have shaped American religious-historical imagination. Joseph Smith's 1830 Book of Mormon predates Rafinesque's 1836 Walam Olum; whether Rafinesque was influenced by Mormon precedent is unestablished but plausible. Ref: Givens 2002, *By the Hand of Mormon*.
- → Donation of Constantine (Phase 5 case) — *parallel-motif*: paradigm document-forgery exposed by source criticism (Lorenzo Valla 1440). The Walam Olum is the Donation of Constantine of Indigenous-American Studies — exposed late, decisive once exposed.
- → Genuine Lenape oral tradition — *contrast*: actual Lenape historical-religious tradition exists in 17th-c. Moravian missionary records (Heckewelder, Zeisberger), in mid-20th-c. fieldwork (Wallace), and in continuing tribal practice. None of this material has Walam Olum's epic structure; the Walam Olum's exposure required rebuilding around what is actually preserved.

## Disputes
- **Status of Oestreicher's case.** Now treated as settled. Voegelin's 1954 defense, the Indiana Historical Society's mid-20th-c. editions, and various older anthropological readings are methodologically superseded.
- **Whether to retain this entry at all.** Retained because: (a) the Walam Olum *historically* shaped the religious-historical map; (b) the source-criticism case is methodologically instructive; (c) the Delaware Tribe's formal repudiation (1997) is itself a moment in Indigenous historiography.
