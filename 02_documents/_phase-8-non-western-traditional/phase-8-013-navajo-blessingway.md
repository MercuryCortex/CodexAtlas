# Navajo Blessingway (Hózhǫǫjí)

## What it is
*Hózhǫǫjí* — "the way of *hózhǫ́*" — is the central ceremony of the Navajo (Diné) religious system. *Hózhǫ́* itself is the master concept of Navajo religion: a state of balance, beauty, blessedness, and right-relation in which the universe and human life ought to be. Blessingway is the ceremonial pathway by which *hózhǫ́* is established or restored. Unlike many other Navajo ceremonies (Enemyway, Nightway, Mountainway), which address specific kinds of disturbance, Blessingway is foundational — performed at births, marriages, blessing of new hogans, preparation for childbirth, and the initiation of medicine-singers — and is the framework to which other ceremonies are theoretically referred. The ceremony comprises chanted song-cycles (several hundred songs in a full performance), prayers (*tsodizin*), sandpaintings (made and destroyed during the ceremony), and a strict choreographic-ritual structure typically performed over two nights. The ceremony retells the Diné Bahaneʼ (Navajo origin/emergence narrative), in which the People emerged through four (or five, in some versions) successive worlds, were given Blessingway by the Holy People, and received Changing Woman (Asdzą́ą́ Nádleehé), who created the original Navajo clans.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Diné occupy a continuous homeland in the American Southwest; arrived as Athabaskan migrants ~1000–1500 CE based on linguistic evidence. Encountered Spanish (1540s onward), then Mexico, then the United States. The Long Walk of 1864 — forced relocation to Bosque Redondo by Kit Carson and the US Army — was a catastrophic event; the Treaty of 1868 returned a portion of the homeland. The Navajo Nation today is the largest U.S. tribal reservation by area.
- **Religious milieu.** Navajo religion is densely structured: Holy People (*Diyin Diné'é*), Earth-surface People (humans), specific Holy beings (Changing Woman, Spider Woman, Black God, Coyote, the Hero Twins Monster-Slayer and Born-for-Water), ceremonial complexes (Blessingway, Enemyway, Holyway, Lifeway, Evilway). Catholic and Protestant Christianity have made inroads (especially through Native American Church / Peyote Way, which is itself a Diné-internal twentieth-century development); the traditional ceremonial system continues.
- **Who was behind it.** *Hataałii* (medicine-singers) — specialists who undertake decade-or-longer apprenticeships to learn specific ceremonies. The Blessingway is held collectively by a *hataałii* lineage.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Navajo transmission; published ethnographic transcription begins with Washington Matthews (1880s onward), Father Berard Haile (Franciscan working with Navajo *hataałii* 1900s–1950s), Mary Wheelwright (1920s–1950s), Wyman (1950s–1980s). Faris (1990) provides critical historiography of how transcribers' selection biases and conditions of access have shaped what is publicly known.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hozho-balance]] — *hózhǫ́* / *Sa'ah Naagháí Bik'eh Hózhǫ́* ("walking in beauty / long-life / blessing"): the regulative ideal of Navajo religious life. Untranslatable; one of the field's defining concepts.
- [[creation-emergence]] — emergence through successive worlds rather than ex nihilo creation. The current Glittering World is the fourth or fifth, depending on tradition.
- [[ceremonial-restoration]] — religious work as restoring proper order, not as worshiping deities in the Mediterranean sense.
- [[changing-woman]] — *Asdzą́ą́ Nádleehé* — Changing Woman, the most beneficent of the Holy People, who creates the first Navajo clans from skin rubbed from her own body; the kinaaldá (puberty ceremony) celebrates her.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-8-009-dreaming-narratives]] — *parallel-motif*: both are Indigenous religious traditions whose textual existence is bound to landscape, where landscape *is* sacred narrative. Distinct traditions, structurally analogous relationship between religious knowledge and country.
- → Pueblo religious traditions (Hopi, Zuni emergence cosmogonies) — *shared-milieu*: Southwest-regional emergence-cosmogony complex; Navajo emergence narrative shares structure with Pueblo emergence narratives (different in detail, similar in shape) — clear regional cultural exchange between Athabaskan-arrival Navajos and long-established Pueblo peoples.
- → Native American Church / Peyote Way (Phase 7) — *commentary-on*: 20th-c. pan-tribal religion that incorporated Christian elements into Indigenous frame; many Diné practice both Blessingway and NAC. Cf. Aberle (1966), *The Peyote Religion among the Navaho*.

## Disputes
- **Pre-contact age of Blessingway in present form.** The ceremony in current form clearly post-dates Spanish contact (corn varieties, sheep references). The Athabaskan-religious substrate is older but how old is uncertain.
- **Access and publication ethics.** Faris (1990) and Diné scholars argue much published material on Navajo ceremony was obtained under conditions that today would not be considered ethical; ongoing debate within Navajo scholarship about repatriation, restriction, and revisiting publications.
