# Kumulipo

## What it is
The Hawaiian royal cosmogonic-genealogical chant — 2,102 lines in 16 *wā* (eras / sections). Cosmogony unfolds *evolutionarily* through paired species: each line names a male and female sea creature, then a land creature, in escalating complexity. The cosmos emerges from *Pō* (primordial night / darkness); the first six wā cover the time of *Pō*; the seventh marks the dawn of *Ao* (day, the realm of light, human time); subsequent sections move through the gods' generations to the ancestral genealogy of the chiefly lineage for which the chant was composed (Kalaninuiamamao, ~1700). The chant served as cosmogony *and* genealogical legitimation — the royal lineage's pedigree is woven directly into the creation of the cosmos.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Pre-unification Hawaiian Islands (~1700); composed for a specific *ali'i* (chiefly) lineage as legitimation. The Kamehameha unification (~1810) consolidated all the islands; the Hawaiian Kingdom (1810–1893) preserved the chant through the royal family. The 1893 overthrow of Queen Liliʻuokalani and 1898 US annexation politicized the chant's preservation; Liliʻuokalani's 1895 translation in prison is itself a sovereignty document.
- **Religious milieu.** Hawaiian religion: Kāne, Kū, Lono, Kanaloa as the four principal Akua; ancestral Aumakua; Pele's volcanic-divine domain; the *kapu* (sacred-prohibition) system regulated all of social life. The official *kapu* system was abolished in 1819 by Kamehameha II; Christianization followed rapidly under Calvinist missionaries (1820 onward). The Kumulipo persisted partly because it had royal-genealogical use that survived the *kapu* abolition.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous priestly composer(s) of the Keaweaheulu / Kalākaua royal lineage; David Kalākaua (reigned 1874–1891) preserved and first printed the chant; Liliʻuokalani translated it; Beckwith critically edited it.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Hawaiian royal-priestly transmission until print publication (1889). Anglophone scholarly access via Beckwith (1951).

## Themes and motifs
- [[primordial-darkness]] — *Pō*: the night-realm preceding day; cosmogony begins *in* darkness, not from chaos-water-combat. Compare Genesis 1:2 (*tohu wa-bohu* + darkness on the deep), but the structural emphasis differs.
- [[genealogical-cosmogony]] — creation *is* genealogy; cosmogony and royal pedigree are continuous.
- [[evolutionary-cosmogony]] — striking species-pair emergence pattern interpreted by some (provocatively) as proto-evolutionary; Beckwith dismisses the literal interpretation but the pattern is genuine.
- [[divine-kingship]] — *ali'i* are descended from the gods *as a direct genealogical fact* within the chant's logic.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-8-011-maori-cosmogonic-chants]] — *parallel-motif* / *shared-milieu*: Maori cosmogonic chants follow the *Te Kore / Te Pō / Te Ao* (Void / Night / Day) structure that the Kumulipo also follows. Common Polynesian cosmogonic substrate; shared Ancestral Polynesian Society religious vocabulary.
- → Theogony (Hesiod, Phase 2 Greek) — *parallel-motif*: cosmogonic genealogy; gods emerge through generative-pair sequences. Independent convergence; no contact.
- → Enuma Elish (Phase 1) — *parallel-motif* (cosmogony from primordial state); differs significantly in detail (no combat).
- → [[phase-8-009-dreaming-narratives]] — *parallel-motif*: Pacific Indigenous cosmogonic-chant traditions sharing structural features with continental Australian songline-cosmogony, though Polynesian and Aboriginal religious traditions are otherwise quite distinct.

## Disputes
- **Date of composition.** Beckwith places it ~1700 (Kalaninuiamamao). Earlier strata of Polynesian cosmogonic material clearly underlie the specific royal-genealogical framing.
- **"Evolutionary" reading.** 19th- and early-20th-century commentators (esp. after publication) attempted to read the species-pair sequence as proto-Darwinian. Beckwith treats this as a category error — the chant is genealogical, not biological. Current scholarship treats the reading as a culturally-revealing misappropriation, not as Hawaiian intent.
