# Māori Cosmogonic Chants (Te Kore / Te Pō / Te Ao)

## What it is
The Māori cosmogonic tradition, transmitted through chant and *whakapapa* (genealogical-narrative recitation). The canonical sequence: *Te Kore* (the Void, the not-yet) → *Te Pō* (the long Night, in many subdivisions — Te Pō nui, Te Pō roa, etc.) → *Te Ao mārama* (the World of Light). The principal cosmogonic event is the *separation of Ranginui (Sky Father) and Papatūānuku (Earth Mother)* by their child Tāne-mahuta — they had embraced too long; their children were crushed in the darkness; Tāne forced them apart by pushing with his legs, creating the lit-world space in which human life is possible. Tāne, Tangaroa, Tūmatauenga, Tāwhirimātea, Rongo, Haumia-tiketike are the principal *atua* (god-ancestors). Specific *iwi* (tribal) traditions differ — some traditions name Io (Io-matua-kore, "Io the parentless") as an even-prior supreme being, but the Io tradition's pre-contact authenticity is contested.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Polynesian settlement of Aotearoa ~1280 CE (Kirch's archaeological synthesis); pre-contact iwi structure (Tainui, Te Arawa, Mātaatua, Tākitimu, Kurahaupō canoe-traditions). European contact: Cook 1769; missionary period 1814 onward; Treaty of Waitangi 1840; New Zealand Wars 1845–1872; land alienation and cultural suppression; Māori Renaissance ~1970s onward; current bicultural / *tikanga*-recognition legal framework.
- **Religious milieu.** *Atua* (gods) related to humans through *whakapapa* (genealogy); *tapu* (sacred-restricted) / *noa* (free) duality regulates ritual practice; *mauri* (life-principle) inheres in beings and places; *mana* (spiritual-political authority) ranks people and lineages. Christianity arrived in 1814 (Samuel Marsden) and was syncretized in some iwi traditions (e.g., Te Kooti's Ringatū faith from 1860s); the cosmogonic *whakapapa* persisted alongside.
- **Who was behind it.** *Tohunga* (priest-experts) of specific iwi held cosmogonic and genealogical chants; transmission was through *whare wānanga* (houses of learning) attended by selected youths.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Sir George Grey *Polynesian Mythology* (1854), Edward Shortland *Maori Religion and Mythology* (1882), John White *Ancient History of the Maori* (1887–90), Elsdon Best *Maori Religion and Mythology* (1924). Best's work is foundational but has been corrected by 20th- and 21st-c. Māori scholarship (Mikaere, Mead, Royal) that returns interpretive authority to Māori voices.

## Themes and motifs
- [[primordial-darkness]] — *Te Pō* sequence; multiple Nights of increasing differentiation precede the World of Light.
- [[world-parent-cosmogony]] — Ranginui and Papatūānuku's forced separation by Tāne is the central cosmogonic event. Compare Egyptian Geb-Nut separation; Sumerian An-Ki separation; Hesiod's Ouranos-Gaia (similar separation, very different mechanism).
- [[genealogical-cosmogony]] — *whakapapa* makes cosmogony, genealogy, and identity continuous; human iwi descend genealogically from atua.
- [[evolutionary-cosmogony]] — emergence through phases of increasing differentiation, parallel structurally to the Kumulipo's species-pair emergence.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-8-010-kumulipo]] — *shared-milieu* / *parallel-motif*: Polynesian cosmogonic family — *Te Pō* (Māori) = *Pō* (Hawaiian); structural similarity reflects common Ancestral Polynesian religious vocabulary.
- → Egyptian Geb-Nut, Sumerian An-Ki separation — *parallel-motif*: sky-earth-separation cosmogonic type. Cross-continental motif convergence; no contact.
- → Hesiod's Theogony — *parallel-motif*: cosmogonic-genealogical structure (gods produced genealogically by gods); no contact.
- → Christianity (early-19th-c. mission encounter) — *shared-milieu*: post-1814 Māori religious experience is bicultural; syncretic movements (Pai Mārire, Ringatū, Rātana) integrate Christian and Māori elements.

## Disputes
- **Authenticity of Io tradition.** Some 19th-c. material (especially via Te Matorohanga / Whatahoro to Percy Smith) reports a supreme being *Io-matua-kore* as the original deity preceding even Ranginui and Papatūānuku. Critics (Simmons 1976; Mikaere 2011) argue the Io tradition is at minimum heavily shaped by post-contact attempts to present Māori religion as proto-monotheistic for European audiences; defenders (some Tūhoe traditions) maintain its pre-contact authenticity. Most current scholarship treats Io as *iwi-specific*, not pan-Māori, and not securely pre-contact in present detailed form.
- **Colonial selection bias.** Grey, Best, and others curated material for European audiences. Modern scholarship (Mikaere, Te Rangihīroa Buck, Royal) reframes the corpus from Māori perspectives.
