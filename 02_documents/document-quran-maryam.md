# Sūrat Maryam (Quran 19 — Mary)

## What it is
The only sura of the Quran named for a woman, and the fullest Quranic treatment of Mary and the birth of Jesus. It opens with the aged Zechariah's prayer and the miraculous birth of his son John (Yaḥyā), then turns to the annunciation to Mary by the Spirit, her withdrawal in shame, the virgin birth of Jesus (ʿĪsā) beside a palm tree, and the astonishment of her people — silenced when the infant Jesus speaks from the cradle to declare himself a prophet and servant of God. Tradition holds that the Muslim refugees recited this sura before the Christian Negus of Abyssinia to show how near the two faiths stood.

## Primary text

### Quran 19:30–33 (the infant Jesus speaks)
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> [The baby] said: "I am a servant of God. He has granted me the Scripture; made me a prophet; made me blessed wherever I may be... Peace was on me the day I was born, and will be on me the day I die and the day I am raised to life again."

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Meccan Islam articulating a Christology that honors Jesus and Mary while denying Jesus's divinity.
- **Sources.** The sura draws on Luke's infancy narrative and on apocryphal infancy traditions (the palm tree; the speaking infant).
- **Discovery / transmission.** A central text of Muslim Marian and Christological piety.

## Themes and motifs
- The virgin birth — Jesus born of Mary by God's word, "Be, and it is" (19:35).
- Prophetic kinship — John and Jesus as linked miracle-births.
- [[messianism]] — Jesus as messiah and prophet, explicitly not divine.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The Quran honors the virgin birth — and rewrites the Nativity.** Sūrat Maryam affirms what much of the modern world forgets is shared: Islam holds that Jesus was born of a virgin by the direct creative word of God ([[mary-mother-of-jesus]] is the only woman named in the Quran and is called the purest of all women). Yet it reframes the scene through apocryphal rather than canonical detail — Mary gives birth alone under a **palm tree** (as in the Latin Pseudo-Matthew), and the **infant Jesus speaks from the cradle** (as in the Arabic Infancy Gospel) — to make the newborn declare his own creaturely servanthood, pre-empting the divinity Christians would claim for him. The sura is the single best illustration of how the Quran absorbs Christian narrative while bending it to strict monotheism, and remains the favorite text of Muslim-Christian dialogue (Reynolds 2010 pp.130–147; Nasr 2015 pp.766–779).
