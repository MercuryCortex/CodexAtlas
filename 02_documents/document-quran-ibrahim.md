# Sūrat Ibrāhīm (Quran 14 — Abraham)

## What it is
A Meccan sura that turns on two images. The first is a parable (14:24–26): a good word is like a good tree, its root firm and its branches in the sky, giving fruit in every season; a bad word is a felled tree with no stability. The second is Abraham's prayer (14:35–41), in which the patriarch, having "settled some of my offspring in a barren valley by Your Sacred House," asks God to make hearts incline toward them and to keep him and his sons from idolatry. The sura thus roots Mecca itself — its sanctuary and its people — in Abraham, the common father.

## Primary text

### Quran 14:37
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> Our Lord, I have settled some of my offspring in a barren valley, near Your Sacred House, so that they may keep up the prayer. Make people's hearts turn to them, and provide them with produce, so that they may be thankful.

### Quran 14:24–25
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> Do you not see how God makes comparisons? A good word is like a good tree whose root is firm and whose branches are high in the sky, yielding constant fruit by its Lord's leave.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Meccan; Abraham is the proof that pure monotheism (ḥanīfiyya) is older than both Judaism and Christianity and native to Arabia's own sanctuary.
- **Tradition.** 14:37 is read with 2:125–129 as the charter of Mecca: Abraham and Ishmael raise the foundations of the Kaʿba.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The "good word" (kalima ṭayyiba) became a fixed term of Muslim devotion for the testimony of faith.

## Themes and motifs
- Abraham the founder — Mecca's sanctuary given an Abrahamic origin.
- The good tree — speech and faith that bear fruit, against the rootless word.
- Gratitude — "if you are thankful, I will give you more" (14:7).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The other son: how Mecca became Abrahamic.** Genesis sends Hagar and her son Ishmael into the wilderness of Beersheba, where God hears the boy and promises he too will father a nation (Genesis 21:9–21) — but the covenant runs through Isaac. Sūrat Ibrāhīm performs a momentous relocation: Abraham settles "some of my offspring" in *a barren valley by Your Sacred House* (14:37), and Islamic tradition reads this as Hagar and Ishmael at Mecca, with father and son later raising the Kaʿba's foundations (2:127). The effect is to make Islam's central sanctuary not a new institution but the restoration of Abraham's own, and to seat the Arab line through Ishmael at the head of sacred history — the same patriarch, the same God, a different chosen son. Layered onto this is the parable of the good tree (14:24), which shares its image with Psalm 1 and Jeremiah 17 (the righteous rooted by water) and with Jesus' test of a tree by its fruit (Matthew 7:17) — a single botanical metaphor for the durable spiritual life crossing all three traditions (Reynolds 2010 pp.71–79; McAuliffe 2006 pp.45–61; Nasr 2015 pp.621–633).
