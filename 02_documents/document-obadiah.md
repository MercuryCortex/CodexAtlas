# Book of Obadiah

## What it is
The shortest book in the Hebrew Bible — a single chapter of 21 verses — and a concentrated oracle of judgment against Edom, Judah's neighbor and kinsman (descended from Esau), for standing by, gloating, and looting during the Babylonian destruction of Jerusalem. It widens at the end into the prophetic "Day of the LORD" against all the nations and the restoration of a chastened Israel.

## Primary text

### Obadiah 10–11
*Trans. NRSV*
> For the slaughter and violence done to your brother Jacob, shame shall cover you, and you shall be cut off forever. On the day that you stood aside, on the day that strangers carried off his wealth... you too were like one of them.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The aftermath of -586: Edom's complicity in Jerusalem's fall provoked a lasting bitterness echoed in Lamentations 4 and Psalm 137.
- **Religious milieu.** The "brother" relationship of Israel (Jacob) and Edom (Esau) sharpens the betrayal into fratricide.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous canonical transmission within the Twelve.

## Themes and motifs
- The Day of the LORD against the nations — Edom's fate as a token of universal judgment (vv. 15–16).
- Fraternal betrayal — Esau/Edom against Jacob/Israel.
- [[divine-kingship]] — "the kingdom shall be the LORD's" (v. 21), the book's closing note.
