# Book of Zechariah

## What it is
The longest and most complex of the Twelve, in two distinct halves. Chapters 1–8 ("First Zechariah," 520–518 BCE) are eight angel-interpreted night visions that, with Haggai, drive the rebuilding of the Temple and envision a restored community led by two "anointed ones" — the priest Joshua and the governor Zerubbabel. Chapters 9–14 ("Deutero-Zechariah") are a later, darker, more apocalyptic collection that became the single richest quarry of the New Testament Passion narratives.

## Primary text

### Zechariah 9:9
*Trans. NRSV*
> Rejoice greatly, O daughter Zion!... Lo, your king comes to you; triumphant and victorious is he, humble and riding on a donkey, on a colt, the foal of a donkey.

### Zechariah 12:10
> And I will pour out a spirit of compassion and supplication on the house of David... so that, when they look on the one whom they have pierced, they shall mourn for him.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Persian Yehud under Darius I (chs. 1–8); a later, less certain horizon for chs. 9–14.
- **Religious milieu.** The angel-mediated visions of First Zechariah mark a hinge between classical prophecy and the full apocalyptic of Daniel and beyond.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous canonical transmission; chs. 9–14 saturate the Gospel Passion.

## Themes and motifs
- [[apocalyptic-revelation]] — the night visions, the cosmic final battle (ch. 14), and the imagery that flows into Daniel and Revelation.
- [[messianism]] — the humble donkey-riding king (9:9) and the pierced one (12:10), read messianically in the Gospels.
- The two anointed ones — priest and prince, the dyarchic restoration ideal (ch. 4).

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[document-haggai]] — *shared-milieu*: the paired prophets of the temple-rebuilding (520 BCE).
- → [[document-revelation]] / [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — *direct-influence*: the horsemen, lampstands, measuring line, and cosmic last battle feed the apocalyptic tradition.
- → [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] / [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — *direct-quote*: the Triumphal Entry (Matt 21:5), the thirty silver pieces (Matt 27:9), and the pierced one (John 19:37) all cite Zechariah.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Zechariah is the Passion narrative's deepest prophetic quarry.** No prophet outside Isaiah is drawn on more heavily by the Gospel accounts of Jesus's last week: the king entering Jerusalem "humble and riding on a donkey" (9:9 → the Triumphal Entry); "thirty pieces of silver" thrown to the potter (11:12–13 → Judas's betrayal, Matt 27:9–10); "strike the shepherd and the sheep will be scattered" (13:7 → Mark 14:27); and "they will look on the one whom they have pierced" (12:10 → John 19:37; Rev 1:7). A post-exilic prophetic collection supplied the script through which the evangelists narrated the crucifixion (Meyers & Meyers 1993, AYB 25C pp.43–53; Petersen 1995, OTL pp.30–38).
