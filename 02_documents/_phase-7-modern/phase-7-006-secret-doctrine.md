# The Secret Doctrine

## What it is
Blavatsky's magnum opus, two volumes totaling ~1,500 pages, published in London in October 1888. Subtitled "The Synthesis of Science, Religion, and Philosophy," the work is presented as a commentary on seven "Stanzas of Dzyan," an alleged ancient text from a Tibetan archive (no independent verification has ever been produced). Volume 1 ("Cosmogenesis") covers the unfolding of the cosmos from the unmanifest Absolute (Parabrahman) through cycles of cosmic days and nights (*manvantaras* and *pralayas*); Volume 2 ("Anthropogenesis") covers the evolution of humanity through seven "Root Races" inhabiting successive continents (Polarian, Hyperborean, Lemurian, Atlantean, Aryan, with two future races). Two further volumes were planned and never written (her death intervened 1891); a posthumous *Volume III* (1897, edited by Annie Besant) is rejected by some Theosophical branches as inauthentic.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late Victorian Britain at the height of empire; the British Theosophical Society had grown rapidly after the 1879 Olcott-Blavatsky relocation to India (Adyar headquarters from 1882).
- **Religious milieu.** Müller's *Sacred Books of the East* (begun 1879) was making primary Indian and Iranian texts available to English-reading audiences; Sinnett's *Esoteric Buddhism* (1883) had popularized the Mahatma-letter Theosophy. Blavatsky's *Secret Doctrine* claims a synthesizing position above the historians.
- **Who was behind it.** Blavatsky personally; she retreated to write in Würzburg (1885), then Ostend, then London (1887–1888). Annie Besant joined Theosophy in 1889 after reading the *Secret Doctrine* and reviewing it; she succeeded Blavatsky as international leader.
- **Discovery / transmission.** First edition 1888, London. Adyar edition (Jinarajadasa, 1938) altered the text substantially; the Pasadena Theosophical Press and the United Lodge of Theosophists publish the original 1888 text.

## Themes and motifs
- [[perennial-philosophy]] — same as *Isis Unveiled*, now systematized.
- **Cosmic cycles** — manvantaras and pralayas; long cosmic periods; multiple universes in temporal succession. Sanskrit-derived but with idiosyncratic elaboration.
- **Root races** — the most problematic doctrine. Seven cosmic-evolutionary races of humanity, with the present "Aryan" the fifth; this terminology, while in Blavatsky originally a spiritual-evolutionary category not biological, was downstream taken up by Ariosophists (List, Lanz von Liebenfels) and partially absorbed into Nazi racial mythology in mutated form. The original Theosophical doctrine is universalist and explicitly anti-discriminatory; the downstream uptake is morally serious and is noted in current Theosophical scholarship (Poewe 2006, Goodrick-Clarke 1992 *The Occult Roots of Nazism*).
- **Seven-fold cosmic structure** — sevenfold "principles" of the human (sthula sharira, prana, linga sharira, kama rupa, manas, buddhi, atma); sevenfold planetary chains; sevenfold "rounds."

## Disputes
- **Stanzas of Dzyan.** No independent attestation has ever surfaced. Most external scholarship treats Blavatsky as the author. Theosophical defense maintains genuine ancient origin.
- **Plagiarism and synthesis.** Coleman's plagiarism findings (originally on *Isis*) extend partially to *Secret Doctrine*, though *Secret Doctrine* contains more original synthesis.
- **Aryan root-race.** Mainstream scholarship documents the linguistic descent (Aryan as Sanskrit *ārya* via 19th-century philology) and Blavatsky's universalist intent, alongside the downstream racialized mutation. The two findings coexist in the literature.
