# The Gnostic Religion

## What it is
Hans Jonas's 1958 English-language synthesis (and substantial rewrite) of his German *Gnosis und spätantiker Geist* (1934–1954). The book treats the Gnostic movements of late antiquity (Sethian, Valentinian, Manichaean, Mandaean, Hermetic, plus selected Pauline, Marcionite, and other adjacent currents) as a unified religious phenomenon — "the Gnostic Religion" — with a distinctive *existential structure*: humans are stranded sparks of divine light in a hostile material cosmos created by a malevolent or ignorant demiurge; salvation comes through *gnosis* (knowledge of one's true origin); the religious mood is alienation, exile, and homesickness for the unknown God. Jonas's framing imports Heideggerian existentiality (thrownness, fallenness, anxiety, authenticity) to read Gnosticism, and in turn reads modern existentialist nihilism as a kind of secularized Gnosticism (the 1963 Epilogue).

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Jonas was a German Jewish refugee; he fled to Palestine 1934, served in the Jewish Brigade of the British Army in WWII (returning to Mönchengladbach to find his mother had died at Auschwitz), then taught in Canada and the US (New School from 1955). The *Gnostic Religion* was composed in this displaced-exilic position, which Jonas later acknowledged shaped his sense of Gnosticism's "alien God" theme.
- **Religious milieu.** Pre-Nag-Hammadi publication-wave Gnosticism studies relied heavily on Coptic-text excerpts (the Berlin Codex, the Pistis Sophia), heresiological reports (Irenaeus, Hippolytus, Epiphanius), and Mandaean texts (Lidzbarski's German translations). The Nag Hammadi find (1945) was known but its full publication was still ahead; the *Gnostic Religion* sits at this hinge moment.
- **Who was behind it.** Jonas alone; the book was widely adopted as the standard introduction for two decades.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Beacon Press 1958, 2nd ed. 1963; remained canonical until the 1977 Robinson Nag Hammadi Library publication and the subsequent (1980s+) revisionist scholarship (King, Williams, Brakke) complicated the unified-Gnostic-Religion thesis.

## Themes and motifs
- **Gnosticism as existential structure** — the master Jonasian move: treat Gnosticism as a *mood and stance* (alien God, hostile cosmos, divine spark, homesickness) rather than a doctrinal package.
- [[alien-god]] — the *unknown* / *unknowable* / *foreign* God beyond the demiurgic creator; Jonas's signature phrase.
- **Gnosis as redemptive knowledge** — knowledge of one's true origin and destination, not propositional knowledge.
- **Modern parallels** — the epilogue extends the framework to 20th-century existential nihilism (Heidegger especially), reading Gnosticism as the religious form whose secularized analogue is modern alienation.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-7-012-major-trends-jewish-mysticism]] — *parallel-development*: Jonas and Scholem (also a German Jewish refugee, Jerusalem-Hebrew-University colleague-at-distance) are doing structurally parallel recoveries — Jonas of late-antique Gnosticism, Scholem of medieval and early-modern Kabbalah — both treating the recovered material as central to (not marginal to) the religious history of the West.
- → [[phase-7-014-gnostic-gospels]] — *direct-influence*: Pagels (1979) inherits Jonas's general frame while moving toward more text-specific readings of the Nag Hammadi corpus.
- → [[event-nag-hammadi-discovery]] — *modern-reception edge*: Jonas's 1958 book is the *bridge generation* between the 1945 discovery and the 1977 Robinson Coptic-Library publication; the Phase-7 academic reception of Phase-4 Gnosticism is two-stage (Jonas frame, then Pagels-Robinson popularization).

## Disputes
- **"Gnostic Religion" as unified category.** Williams 1996 (*Rethinking "Gnosticism"*) and King 2003 (*What Is Gnosticism?*) systematically argued that "Gnosticism" is a heresiological construction and that Jonas's unifying existential reading projects a 20th-century mood onto heterogeneous late-antique materials. The post-1996 scholarship works mostly with subcategories (Sethian, Valentinian, Thomasine, Basilidean) rather than a unified "Gnostic Religion."
- **Heideggerian projection.** Whether Jonas saw Gnosticism through Heideggerian lenses to such a degree that he constructed rather than discovered the "existential structure" of Gnosticism.
