# Mensagem

## What it is

***Mensagem*** ("Message") is a **symbolist-modernist epic poem cycle** by [[fernando-pessoa|Fernando Pessoa]], composed across approximately 1913–1934 and published in Lisbon by Parceria António Maria Pereira in **October 1934**, in an edition of approximately 1,500 copies. It comprises 44 short poems in three sections, totalling roughly 1,200 lines, organized around the symbolic-mythological-historical figure of Portugal's imperial-spiritual destiny.

The three sections:

1. ***Brasão*** ("Coat of Arms" / "Heraldry"). 19 poems organized as the Portuguese national coat of arms — castles, escutcheons, crown, crests — each poem treating a founder-figure of Portuguese national identity (D. Afonso Henriques, D. Dinis, D. Sebastião, Vasco da Gama, Henrique the Navigator, etc.) as a heraldic-mythic figure.
2. ***Mar Português*** ("Portuguese Sea"). 12 poems on the Age of Discoveries — the oceanic-imperial expansion under the Order of Christ caravel-cross. Contains the most-quoted Pessoa lines in the Portuguese national cultural canon: *"Ó mar salgado, quanto do teu sal / São lágrimas de Portugal!"* (O salt sea, how much of your salt is tears of Portugal!).
3. ***O Encoberto*** ("The Hidden One"). 13 poems on the Sebastianist messianic-prophetic expectation — explicitly including a poem titled "Bandarra" (acknowledging the foundational [[goncalo-annes-bandarra|*Trovas*]] tradition), poems on Sebastian I, on the Fifth Empire, on the return of the Hidden King, and on Pessoa's own modernist-prophetic stance. Closes with the canonical final lines: *"É a Hora!"* (It is the Hour!).

***Mensagem* is the only Portuguese-language book of Pessoa's poetry published in his lifetime** — distinct from his earlier English chapbooks (*Antinous*, *35 Sonnets*, *English Poems I-II-III*, 1918–1921, Olisipo) and from his Portuguese periodical work in *Orpheu*, *Athena*, *Presença*. He submitted *Mensagem* to the **Salazarist Secretariado de Propaganda Nacional** prize competition; it won the **Antero de Quental Prize, Category B** (Category A — the larger prize, with a higher-budget submission requirement — was awarded to Vasco Reis's *Romaria*).

## Context

- **Political milieu.** The Salazar *Estado Novo* dictatorship was established 1933 (the year before *Mensagem*'s publication); Salazar's regime emphasized Catholic-imperial-traditionalist national identity; the *Mensagem* prize-submission context places Pessoa in direct interaction with the regime's cultural-propaganda apparatus, with substantial subsequent interpretive consequences (see Disputes).
- **Religious milieu.** Pessoa was deeply immersed in 1910s–1930s Lisbon esoteric circles: Theosophical, Rosicrucian, Hermetic, astrological. His correspondence with [[aleister-crowley]] (1929–1930) and his participation in the **September 1930 Boca do Inferno faked-disappearance hoax** at Cascais place him at the centre of the European post-Theosophical occult-revival scene during the immediate years before *Mensagem*'s completion.
- **Who was behind it.** Pessoa personally. *Mensagem* is composed entirely under his own name, not under any heteronym — an important distinction in the Pessoan œuvre. The book's authorship is *autographic* in a way most of his work is not.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous in print since 1934; canonized through the 1940s–1960s Portuguese national-cultural reception (especially via the 1956 João Gaspar Simões edition and the 1965 publication of Pessoa's preparatory notes); international through the late-20th-c. Pessoa boom triggered by the *Livro do Desassossego* integral publication (1982 Ática edition).

## Themes and motifs

- [[tradition-sebastianism|Sebastianismo]] — the principal subject; Section III is the central modernist literary articulation of the tradition.
- [[fifth-empire-quinto-imperio]] — Pessoa's renewal of the *Quinto Império* doctrine in modernist-poetic form, threading Daniel-2 + Joachim of Fiore + Bandarra + Vieira through 20th-c. modernist allegory.
- [[messianism]] — the *Encoberto* who returns to inaugurate the messianic kingdom.
- [[hidden-god]] — the *deus absconditus* in Portuguese-imperial-allegorical mode.
- [[apocalyptic-revelation]] — the moment of *"a Hora"* (the Hour) when the prophesied return occurs.
- **Heraldic symbolism** — the *Brasão* section reads Portuguese national-heraldic symbols (the castles, the quinas, the armillary sphere, the cross of Christ) as encoded-iconographic-prophetic statements.
- **Maritime symbolism** — the Atlantic, the sea-as-tears, the caravel, the navigator-as-mystical-pilgrim. The [[cross-order-of-christ|Cross of the Order of Christ]] on the caravel sails is the central iconographic anchor of Section II.

## Disputes

- **Is *Mensagem* Sebastianist-imperialist in earnest or as ironic-aesthetic re-presentation?** Open scholarly question. The Pessoa-scholarship spectrum:
  - *Sincere-Sebastianist reading* (Centeno 1985, Dalila Pereira da Costa, the Portuguese hermetic-circle): Pessoa was substantively committed to Sebastianist messianism; *Mensagem* is his most ideologically-committed work.
  - *Ironic-modernist reading* (Zenith 2021, Lopes 2008, much Anglophone scholarship): Pessoa was deeply read in Sebastianist material but maintained ironic-aesthetic distance; *Mensagem* is a modernist re-presentation that deploys the mythology without sincerely endorsing its messianic-political programme.
  - *Mendia 2023 mediation*: the question partly misframes the Pessoan project — the heteronymic discipline makes "did Pessoa really believe" a non-question; *Mensagem* is Pessoa-under-his-own-name engaging Sebastianism as an *exoteric* mode while the *esoteric* mode is distributed across his heteronymic and prose work.
- **Was the Salazar Secretariado de Propaganda Nacional prize submission a strategic ideological alignment, a financial necessity, or a private joke?** Pessoa needed money (he was in financial difficulty in 1934); the prize ratification gave the regime an apparent literary-cultural success; Pessoa's private notes (Espólio) include some sceptical remarks about Salazar that complicate simple readings either way. Best position (Zenith 2021): Pessoa submitted because the prize money was significant and the alignment was not so severe as to be intolerable; both sides extracted utility from the transaction.
- **What is the role of Crowley's Thelema in *Mensagem*'s late drafts?** Pessoa's 1929–1930 Crowley correspondence (BN Portugal preserved) shows substantive engagement with Thelemic vocabulary. Some readings (Patrício 2018) find Crowleyan inflection in Section III; others (Zenith 2021) treat it as peripheral. The 1930 Boca do Inferno hoax is undisputed but its weight in shaping *Mensagem*'s late drafts is contested.
