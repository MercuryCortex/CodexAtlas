# Kitáb-i-Íqán

## What it is
Bahá'u'lláh's principal doctrinal work, composed in two days and two nights in January 1861 (some scholars: late 1861) in response to questions from the Báb's maternal uncle who was investigating whether the Bábí movement's claims could be reconciled with traditional Shi'a Islamic eschatology. The book lays out the Bahá'í doctrine of **progressive revelation** — that all great religions are stages of one unfolding divine pedagogy, and that scriptural language about the "Day of Resurrection," "the coming of the Lord," "the seal of the prophets" must be read symbolically, as referring to the appearance of successive Manifestations of God (Adam, Noah, Abraham, Moses, Zoroaster, Krishna, Buddha, Christ, Muhammad, the Báb, and Bahá'u'lláh himself). Composed *before* Bahá'u'lláh's public declaration as the Promised One (1863); the *Íqán* lays groundwork without yet identifying its author as that Manifestation.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Bahá'u'lláh was in Baghdad (Ottoman territory) in his first exile after his 1852 release from the Síyáh-Chál dungeon in Tehran, where he had received the foundational mystical experience. The Bábí community in Baghdad was leaderless after the Báb's 1850 execution; Bahá'u'lláh emerged as its de facto stabilizer.
- **Religious milieu.** Twelver Shi'a Islam dominated Iran; the Bábí movement (1844 declaration of the Báb) had been violently suppressed (~3,000 Bábí dead by 1852). Sufi and Shaykhí esotericism provided the cosmological vocabulary; Sunni hadith and tafsir provided the textual battleground on which the *Íqán* fights for symbolic exegesis.
- **Who was behind it.** Bahá'u'lláh personally; the speed of composition (two days) is itself part of Bahá'í testimony to its revelatory status. Final form transcribed by his amanuenses.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Distributed in manuscript throughout the Bábí community 1862 onward; printed editions from late 19th century. Shoghi Effendi's English translation (1931) is binding for English-speaking Bahá'ís.

## Themes and motifs
- [[progressive-revelation]] — the *Íqán*'s central contribution: religious history is one continuous education from a single transcendent source.
- [[hidden-god]] — God in Bahá'í theology is utterly unknowable in essence; known only through Manifestations who are theophanies but not incarnations.
- [[messianism]] — the "return" of the Imam Mahdi, of Christ, of all the promised ones is unified into the single category of the Manifestation; the Báb is the return of the Mahdi, Bahá'u'lláh the return of Christ-and-others.
- **Symbolic exegesis of scripture** — phrases like "the heavens shall be cloven asunder," "the sun shall be darkened," and "the seal of the prophets" are de-literalized.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-7-003-kitab-i-aqdas]] — *direct-influence* (same author): the *Íqán* is doctrinal foundation; the *Aqdas* (1873) is legal code building on it.
- → [[phase-4-034-quran]] — *commentary-on*: extensive Quranic citation and re-interpretation; the *Íqán* is partly an exegetical argument that the Quran's eschatological passages have been *fulfilled* in the Báb/Bahá'u'lláh.
- → Shaykhí theology (Shaykh Ahmad al-Ahsa'i, Sayyid Kazim Rashti) — *direct-influence*: the cosmological vocabulary of the "Primal Will," the *hayákil* (temples / theophanic forms), and the symbolic-exegetical method are continuous with Shaykhí esoterism.

## Disputes
- **Date of composition** — January 1861 (most Bahá'í sources) vs. late 1861 (some external scholars). The text is internally dated "two years before" Bahá'u'lláh's Ridván declaration of April 1863.
- **Status vis-à-vis Islam.** Bahá'ís consider the *Íqán* a continuation and fulfillment of the Quranic revelation; Shi'a authorities consider Bahá'u'lláh's claim to post-Quranic prophecy heretical (the persistent legal basis for Iranian persecution of Bahá'ís).
