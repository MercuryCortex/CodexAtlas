# The Kybalion

## What it is
A 1908 pseudonymous American work, ~225 pages, presenting "the Hermetic Philosophy of Ancient Egypt and Greece" via seven "Hermetic Principles": (1) Mentalism — "The All is Mind; the Universe is Mental"; (2) Correspondence — "as above, so below"; (3) Vibration — "nothing rests; everything moves"; (4) Polarity — opposites are aspects of one thing; (5) Rhythm — everything has its cycles; (6) Cause and Effect — every effect has its cause; (7) Gender — masculine and feminine principles are in everything. The book claims to transmit an ancient esoteric Hermetic teaching from "The Kybalion," a (non-existent) ancient text. The actual provenance is the early-20th-century American New Thought movement; the work has no ancient sources beyond a few echoes of the Corpus Hermeticum and the Emerald Tablet.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Progressive Era US; New Thought publishing flourishing.
- **Religious milieu.** New Thought (Quimby, Eddy, Trine, Atkinson, Fillmore) was producing mass-market metaphysical-religious literature; Theosophy was at its post-Blavatsky height; American mass-print publishing reached a wide audience.
- **Who was behind it.** William Walker Atkinson, a Chicago lawyer-turned-occultist who wrote dozens of works under multiple pseudonyms (Yogi Ramacharaka, Theron Q. Dumont, Theodore Sheldon, Magus Incognito). Deslippe's 2011 introductory essay to the Tarcher centennial edition substantially established the Atkinson attribution from stylistic and biographical evidence.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Yogi Publication Society, Chicago, 1908. Continuously in print since; among the most widely-circulated 20th-century occult-Hermetic books.

## Themes and motifs
- **Seven Principles** — the book's organizing structure; the principles are presented as ancient but are in fact a New Thought synthesis dressed in ancient-Hermetic vocabulary.
- **"As above, so below"** — quoted in the Emerald Tablet tradition (Tabula Smaragdina); *The Kybalion*'s usage is its principal 20th-century mass-popular vector.
- **Mentalism** — "the universe is mental"; a New Thought / Christian-Science cousin doctrine in Hermetic costume.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] — *contested-influence (claimed, not actually)*: *The Kybalion* claims continuity with the *Corpus Hermeticum* but is not in fact descended from it; the relationship is loose vocabulary appropriation, not transmission.
- → American New Thought (Trine, Atkinson, Fillmore) — *direct-influence*: the real provenance.
- → 20th–21st century popular Hermeticism / "Law of Attraction" / Rhonda Byrne *The Secret* (2006) — *direct-influence*: *The Kybalion*'s "principles" become a recurring source for popular mass-market metaphysical literature.

## Disputes
- **Ancient-Hermetic claim.** The book's claim to transmit ancient teaching is rejected by all scholarly evaluation. Treated by sympathetic readers as "Hermetic in spirit" rather than "Hermetic in transmission."
- **Pseudonym attribution.** Atkinson is now consensus author; some earlier theories included Mabel Collins and others. Deslippe's evidence is the standard reference.
