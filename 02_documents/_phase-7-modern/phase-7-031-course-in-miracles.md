# A Course in Miracles

## What it is
*A Course in Miracles* (ACIM) is a 1,200-page work consisting of three parts: (1) a Text (~700 pages) presenting a non-dualist metaphysics in Christian vocabulary; (2) a Workbook (365 daily lessons, one per day for a year); (3) a Manual for Teachers. The work was received by Helen Schucman, a research psychologist at Columbia-Presbyterian Medical Center, beginning in 1965; she reported an inner voice (identified by her as Jesus) dictating the material. William Thetford, Schucman's department-head colleague, encouraged her to transcribe the dictation; the two worked together on the seven-year transcription (1965–1972). Kenneth Wapnick then edited the manuscript to publishable form; the Foundation for Inner Peace published the first edition in 1976.

The metaphysics: God is love; the physical-material world is a hallucination of the ego (the "tiny mad idea" that the Son separated from the Father); salvation is the recognition that the separation never happened; forgiveness is the practice that dissolves the ego's perceptual world and reveals the underlying oneness. The Christology: Jesus is *one* manifestation of the universal Sonship (which is all of us); the crucifixion is *not* a sacrificial atonement for sin (which does not exist in the metaphysics) but a demonstration of the unreality of death.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-1960s / early-1970s US; the "consciousness movement" (Esalen 1962+, Werner Erhard's est 1971+, the human potential movement) had created cultural infrastructure for non-traditional spiritual scriptures.
- **Religious milieu.** Christian Science was declining; Theosophy had passed its peak; American Vedanta and Zen Buddhism were growing; New Age "channeled" literature was a developing genre (Jane Roberts's *Seth Speaks* 1972; later Marshall Vian Summers's *Greater Community* materials; the Urantia Book, 1955, was already in print).
- **Who was behind it.** Schucman (the scribe), Thetford (collaborator), Wapnick (editor and principal exegete). Schucman herself was reportedly ambivalent about ACIM and remained a non-practicing Jewish-cultural-background psychologist throughout her life.
- **Discovery / transmission.** 1976 FIP edition. Marianne Williamson's 1992 *A Return to Love* (Oprah-promoted) made ACIM widely known to general American readers in the 1990s.

## Themes and motifs
- **Metaphysical non-dualism in Christian vocabulary** — the world is illusion (Vedantic *maya* re-coded); the ego is the principle of separation; reality is the Father-Son-Holy-Spirit oneness.
- **Forgiveness as the central practice** — not forgiving an actual offense but recognizing that the apparent offense was an illusion of the ego.
- **Inversion of atonement theology** — Christ's death does *not* pay for sin; sin (as a metaphysical reality) does not exist; the atonement is the *correction of the perception* that separation happened.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-7-004-science-and-health]] — *parallel-development*: Eddy's Christian Science (1875) and Schucman's ACIM (1965–1976) both produce metaphysical-idealist Christianity in which matter is illusion and healing is recognition of underlying spiritual reality. The parallel is structural; direct influence is not established.
- → Marianne Williamson — *direct-influence*: Williamson's 1992 *Return to Love* introduced ACIM to mainstream American audiences through Oprah Winfrey's show.
- → [[tradition-new-age]] — *central-document*: ACIM is one of the most-circulated New Age scriptures, alongside the Urantia Book and the Seth material.

## Disputes
- **Edition wars.** The FIP-authorized 1976 edition vs. the "Urtext" (earliest typed transcription, with material later edited out) vs. the "Hugh Lynn Cayce manuscript" vs. the "Sparkly Edition." Court cases (FIP v. Penguin etc., 1990s–2000s) established FIP's copyright in the standard text; whether the *true* ACIM is the edited or unedited version is debated among practitioners.
- **Authorship attribution.** Schucman's own attribution ("Jesus dictated this") vs. external-psychological reading (the work is Schucman's own composition in a dissociative-creative state). The former is the ACIM-practitioner position; the latter is the standard external-academic position. Schucman herself never publicly affirmed practice of the work.
