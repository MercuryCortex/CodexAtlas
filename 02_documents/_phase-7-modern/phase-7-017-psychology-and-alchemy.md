# Psychology and Alchemy

## What it is
Carl Gustav Jung's 1944 book establishing the alchemical-symbolic framework that would govern his late-period work. Three parts: (1) a long introduction on the religious-cosmological problem of the symbol; (2) an analysis of dream-series in a single patient (the so-called "Pauli dreams," in fact a series produced by the physicist Wolfgang Pauli during his 1932–34 analysis with Jung-analyst Erna Rosenbaum, with Jung as supervisor) read against alchemical symbolism; (3) "religious ideas in alchemy" — a thematic treatment of the alchemical *opus* as projection of the unconscious individuation process, with detailed readings of Mercurius, the *prima materia*, the *coniunctio*, the *rotundum*, the lapis-Christ parallel. The book argues alchemy is a Western parallel to Eastern yoga: a symbolic-meditative discipline for individuation, hidden under chemistry-experimental cover.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Switzerland during WWII; Jung had been involved in 1933–1934 with the editorship of the Zentralblatt für Psychotherapie under conditions that have generated persistent controversy regarding his stance toward the German psychotherapeutic establishment under Nazism (the dispute: was Jung accommodating, protective of Jewish colleagues, or sympathetic to the regime? scholarship is divided). The alchemical turn dates to the same broader period.
- **Religious milieu.** The Eranos Conferences at Ascona (Olga Fröbe-Kapteyn, founder; Jung's annual lecture base 1933–1951) brought together comparative-religion and esoteric scholars (Eliade, Scholem, Corbin, Otto, Heinrich Zimmer, Karl Kerényi). Jung's alchemical project developed in this context.
- **Who was behind it.** Jung personally, building on Marie-Louise von Franz's parallel work in alchemical-text editing and translation (the German *Theatrum Chemicum* collection at Jung's library, Bollingen, was substantially organized by von Franz).
- **Discovery / transmission.** First German edition 1944 (Rascher Verlag, Zürich); standard CW edition 1968.

## Themes and motifs
- **Alchemy as projected individuation** — Jung's master move: alchemy's external operations are unconscious projections of an inner psychological process; the alchemists were working on their own souls under chemical pretexts.
- [[individuation]] — the lifelong psychological process of integrating shadow, anima/animus, and Self.
- **Mercurius** — the dual / paradoxical figure of alchemical Mercury, read by Jung as a symbol of the unconscious and of the Self.
- **Lapis-Christ parallel** — Jung's controversial argument that the alchemical Philosopher's Stone (*lapis philosophorum*) is a "shadow" or compensating symbol for the Christ-figure; the Self is what the lapis and Christ both symbolize.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Phase 6 alchemical corpus (Khunrath *Amphitheatrum*, Maier *Atalanta Fugiens*, Mylius, Dorn) — *commentary-on*: Jung's primary source material. Jung's reception more or less *constructed* 20th-century esoteric interest in early modern alchemy.
- → [[phase-7-018-aion]] — *direct-influence* (same author): *Aion* (1951) extends the alchemy-individuation framework to the Christ-figure as Self-symbol across the astrological aeon of Pisces.
- → [[phase-7-019-mysterium-coniunctionis]] — *direct-influence* (same author): *Mysterium Coniunctionis* (1955) is Jung's final magnum opus on alchemical *coniunctio* as model for individuation.
- → [[phase-7-005-isis-unveiled]] and the broader Theosophical milieu — *parallel-development*: Jung's library held Theosophical works; the broader cultural recovery of alchemy as more than chemistry-prehistory was begun by Mary Anne Atwood (1850) and Theosophical writers, then made academically respectable by Jung.

## Disputes
- **Alchemy as psychology.** Whether alchemy is genuinely a psychological-symbolic discipline (Jung) or a proto-chemical experimental tradition with theological vocabulary (Lawrence Principe, William Newman, mainstream history of science from the 1990s onward). Jung's reading has been substantially complicated; alchemy is now treated as both experimental-chemical *and* theological-symbolic, not exclusively one.
- **Patient confidentiality / Pauli dreams.** The "Pauli dreams" used as central case material were not properly anonymized; Pauli's identity was reconstructable. The ethics-of-analysis-publication is now a topic in Jungian scholarship.
- **Jung's politics.** The 1933–1934 Zentralblatt period and Jung's later writings on "Jewish vs. Aryan psychology" generate continuing controversy. Noll 1997 is sharply critical; the Bair 2003 biography balances; the Jungian institutional position has shifted toward acknowledgment.
