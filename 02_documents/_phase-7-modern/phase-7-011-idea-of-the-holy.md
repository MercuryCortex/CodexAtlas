# The Idea of the Holy

## What it is
Rudolf Otto's 1917 *Das Heilige*, a 220-page essay positing that the core of religion is not doctrine, ethics, or social function but a distinctive *experience* of "the holy" — what Otto calls the **numinous**. The numinous is irreducible to rational categories; Otto coins the Latin formula *mysterium tremendum et fascinans* — a *mystery* that is *terrifying* (*tremendum*) and yet *fascinating/attractive* (*fascinans*). Examples are drawn from Hebrew Bible theophanies (the *kavod* of YHWH at Sinai), New Testament resurrection appearances, Islamic encounter with the majesty of God, Vedic and Buddhist meditative experience. The book argues that all genuinely religious texts and rituals are *schematizations* of an underlying numinous experience, and that comparative religion's task is to recover this experiential core through phenomenological description rather than evolutionary explanation (vs. Frazer) or sociological reduction (vs. Durkheim).

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Composed during World War I; Otto was a Marburg theology professor and a Reichstag deputy (Progressive party). Published 1917, the year of the Russian Revolution; a religiously consoling counterweight to the war's secular catastrophe.
- **Religious milieu.** German liberal Protestant theology (Schleiermacher's heir tradition); the dialectical-theology revolt against liberal theology (Barth, Brunner) would come in the 1920s and would partly displace Otto's experiential approach with a Word-of-God-centered Christology.
- **Who was behind it.** Otto personally; the book sits within his larger comparative project (he traveled to India and the Middle East 1911–12, published comparative volumes on Indian and Christian mysticism — *Mysticism East and West*, 1926).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Immediate and lasting impact; the term "numinous" entered general English (and German, Italian, French) intellectual vocabulary. C.S. Lewis adopted it in *The Problem of Pain* (1940); Jung used it; Eliade built the *Sacred and Profane* (1957) on Otto's foundations.

## Themes and motifs
- [[numinous]] — the *sui generis* religious experience; Otto's terminological coinage.
- **Mysterium tremendum** — awe, dread, the "creature-feeling" before the wholly Other.
- **Mysterium fascinans** — attraction, beatitude, the desire-toward.
- **The "wholly other" (*ganz andere*)** — God / the holy as radically discontinuous with the categories of ordinary experience.
- **Schematization** — the rational and moral categories of religion (justice, love, lawgiver, judge) are *schemata* under which the irrational numinous core is brought into religious-doctrinal-ethical form.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-7-022-sacred-and-profane]] — *direct-influence*: Eliade adopts Otto's experiential framework and extends it into the hierophany-history-of-religions program.
- → [[phase-7-010-elementary-forms-religious-life]] — *method-rivalry*: Durkheim's sociological-reductive sacred vs. Otto's phenomenological-experiential numinous are two of the three principal early-20th-century framings (with Frazer's evolutionist) of "what religion is."
- → C.G. Jung — *direct-influence*: Jung adopts *numinous* as a vocabulary term for archetypal experience; the term is now nearly inseparable from Jungian usage in popular reception.

## Disputes
- **Sui generis claim.** Whether religious experience is genuinely *sui generis* (Otto's claim) or analyzable into psychological/sociological/neurobiological components without remainder. The "sui generis" position has lost ground in contemporary religious studies (Proudfoot 1985 *Religious Experience* is the classical critique) but retains defenders.
- **Cross-cultural unity.** Whether the numinous is the same experience across traditions or whether Otto's category projects a Christian-mystical template onto heterogeneous experiences.
