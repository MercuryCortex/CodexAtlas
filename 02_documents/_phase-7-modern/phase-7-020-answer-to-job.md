# Answer to Job

## What it is
A short, polemical, deeply personal 1952 book in which Jung argues that the Yahweh of the Book of Job is morally inferior to Job; that the Book of Job marks a turning point in Yahweh's own psychological development (the Godhead becomes aware of its own shadow); that the Incarnation of Christ is Yahweh's attempt to become human in order to suffer what he subjected Job to; and that the 1950 papal proclamation of the Bodily Assumption of Mary (Munificentissimus Deus) is a major event in the continued *individuation* of the Christian Godhead — the integration of the feminine.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Postwar; Jung wrote *Answer to Job* in a single creative burst (~spring 1951) during a serious illness, calling it "a personal confession."
- **Religious milieu.** Pius XII's 1950 Assumption proclamation; the dialectical theology of Barth was still dominant in German Protestantism; Bultmann's demythologization program (1941 onward) provided one alternative framing.
- **Who was behind it.** Jung alone; the book was controversial within Jungian circles, with some (especially Christian-friendly Jungians) considering it ill-considered.
- **Discovery / transmission.** German 1952; English 1958 (CW 11).

## Themes and motifs
- **Yahweh's shadow** — Jung reads the Book of Job as exposing that Yahweh has a *Schattenseite* (shadow side), one Yahweh himself only partially recognizes. Job, by surviving the test with integrity, *forces* Yahweh into greater consciousness.
- **Incarnation as theodicy-impulse** — Christ's incarnation is Yahweh's project of becoming-human, motivated (in Jung's mythopoeic reading) by what was done to Job.
- **Mary's Assumption as feminine integration** — the 1950 dogma matters psychologically: the Trinity becomes a Quaternity by adding the feminine principle.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-7-018-aion]] — *direct-influence* (same author): *Aion*'s Christ-as-Self-symbol provides the framework that *Answer to Job* extends to the OT Yahweh.
- → Process theology (Whitehead 1929, Hartshorne 1948+, Cobb 1965+) — *parallel-development*: process theology and Jung independently developed accounts of a divine becoming-with-history.
- → Christian feminist theology — *indirect-influence*: Jung's foregrounding of the feminine-divine (Sophia, Mary) was read in late-20th-century Christian feminist work as evidence of suppressed feminine-divine currents.

## Disputes
- **Theological adequacy.** Whether Jung's reading is theologically serious or psychologistic projection. Roman Catholic and orthodox Protestant readers have generally been critical; sympathetic theological readings exist (Edinger, Stein, Lammers).
- **Job exegesis.** Jung's reading is *not* the consensus exegetical reading of Job (the prose-poetic structure, the multiple textual layers, the divine-speech theology are flattened in Jung's psychologizing).
