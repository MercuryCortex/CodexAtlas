# Kitáb-i-Aqdas

## What it is
The Bahá'í "Most Holy Book," Bahá'u'lláh's principal legal-doctrinal work, composed in Arabic ~1873 while exiled in the Ottoman penal colony of Akka. The *Aqdas* establishes Bahá'í law (prayer obligations, fasting in Bahá month of `Alá', laws of marriage and inheritance, prohibition of slavery, prohibition of war, abolition of clergy, abolition of religious mendicancy, requirements for universal education), institutes the **Houses of Justice** (local, secondary, and universal), abrogates earlier dispensational laws (including the Báb's *Bayán* and Islamic shari'a) in favor of the new Bahá'í dispensation, and addresses by name the world's rulers (Queen Victoria, Napoleon III, Nicholas II, Pope Pius IX, Wilhelm I, Násir al-Dín Sháh, the leaders of the American republics) summoning them to recognize the Manifestation and establish global peace under his teachings.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Bahá'u'lláh was held under house arrest in Akka 1868–1892, transferred there from Edirne after the Ottomans concluded he was politically dangerous. The *Aqdas* is composed in confinement; its addressees include the rulers responsible for his confinement.
- **Religious milieu.** The Bábí community was schisming between followers of Bahá'u'lláh and of his half-brother Subh-i-Azal (Azalis). The *Aqdas* implicitly settles the schism by issuing a comprehensive new law code that only Bahá'u'lláh's authority can ground.
- **Who was behind it.** Bahá'u'lláh personally, transcribed by amanuenses (especially Mírzá Áqá Ján).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Slow promulgation among the community 1873 onward; English translation was deliberately delayed until 1992 by `Abdu'l-Bahá and Shoghi Effendi (they preferred the community first absorb the *Hidden Words*, *Íqán*, and *Gleanings*).

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-kingship]] — Bahá'u'lláh as the supreme spiritual authority of the age, claiming to address kings and pontiffs as equals or subordinates.
- **Legal code as scripture** — the *Aqdas* is to Bahá'í what the Quran is to Islam: revelation containing law, not law derived from revelation by jurists.
- **Universalist humanism** — abolition of clergy, abolition of jihad, requirement of universal education, equality of women and men in education, single international auxiliary language, single world tribunal.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-7-002-kitab-i-iqan]] — *direct-influence* (earlier work, same author): the *Íqán* establishes the doctrinal framework that the *Aqdas* legislates.
- → [[phase-4-034-quran]] — *redaction-of* / *polemic-against* (in the Bahá'í dispensational frame): the *Aqdas* explicitly abrogates Islamic shari'a for the new dispensation while preserving the Quran's status as prior revelation.
- → Bábí *Bayán* (Persian and Arabic) — *redaction-of*: the Báb's *Bayán* announces a coming Manifestation ("He whom God shall make manifest") whose new law will abrogate the *Bayán*'s; the *Aqdas* performs that abrogation.

## Disputes
- **Translation timing.** Why did the authorized English translation wait until 1992? Walbridge 1996 and Cole 1998 discuss internal Bahá'í debates: legal applications to non-Eastern contexts required interpretive work by `Abdu'l-Bahá and Shoghi Effendi; some passages (corporal punishments, gender-asymmetric inheritance defaults) were considered prone to misunderstanding.
- **Application of laws.** Most *Aqdas* laws are stated as not yet binding pending decisions of the Universal House of Justice; the discretion this gives the Haifa-based institution is itself a topic in Bahá'í constitutional debate.
