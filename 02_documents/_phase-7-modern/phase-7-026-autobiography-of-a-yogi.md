# Autobiography of a Yogi

## What it is
Paramahansa Yogananda's 1946 spiritual autobiography, published a year after the end of WWII and a year after Yogananda's substantial Self-Realization Fellowship organizational consolidation in the US. The book recounts his Bengali childhood, his early spiritual experiences (visits to and from various reportedly enlightened sages), his discovery of his guru Sri Yukteswar (1855–1936) in 1910, his initiation into Kriya Yoga, his 1920 voyage to the US for the International Congress of Religious Liberals in Boston, and his subsequent thirty-year mission propagating Kriya Yoga in the West. The book contains numerous accounts of paranormal phenomena — bilocation, materializations, levitation, the *jiva-samadhi* state of the "deathless saint" Babaji (Yogananda's grand-grand-guru, alleged to be physically deathless and many centuries old) — presented as factual.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Postwar US; Indian independence imminent (1947); the first generation of Indian gurus to address Western audiences had been Vivekananda (1893 onward) and Yogananda himself (1920 onward).
- **Religious milieu.** American Theosophy was declining; American interest in Indian spirituality was growing (Vedanta Society from 1894, Self-Realization Fellowship from 1920); the broader counterculture-yoga wave of the 1960s–70s was a generation away but the cultural soil was preparing.
- **Who was behind it.** Yogananda personally.
- **Discovery / transmission.** 1946 publication; widely distributed; reportedly Steve Jobs read it annually and arranged for copies at his memorial service. Listed among "100 most-influential spiritual books of the 20th century" in multiple polls.

## Themes and motifs
- **Guru-disciple relationship** — the spiritual heart of the book; the lineage of Lahiri Mahasaya (1828–1895) → Sri Yukteswar → Yogananda.
- **Kriya Yoga** — a specific pranayama-meditation technique, taught by initiation; the technique is the practical-religious payload.
- **Paranormal as ordinary** — Yogananda presents miracles, materializations, telepathy, and physical immortality (Babaji) as factual elements of advanced spiritual life. Western audiences receive this in various registers.
- **Modern science / yoga reconciliation** — Yogananda repeatedly frames yogic findings in the vocabulary of 1940s physics, biology, and medicine, anticipating the later "quantum mysticism" register.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Vivekananda's American mission (1893 onward) — *direct-influence*: Yogananda is a direct heir of the Vivekananda-opened cultural channel.
- → [[phase-7-027-life-divine]] — *parallel-development*: Aurobindo (1939) and Yogananda (1946) are contemporary Hindu modernists offering systematic spiritual-philosophical works to Western and Indian English-reading audiences.
- → [[tradition-hindu-modernism]] / [[tradition-new-age]] — *direct-influence*: Yogananda's book is part of the cultural infrastructure that made yoga-as-spiritual-practice a 20th-century Western mass phenomenon.

## Disputes
- **Historicity of paranormal claims.** No independent verification of Babaji's existence or longevity; the bilocation and materialization accounts depend on Yogananda's testimony alone. Most external scholarship treats these as devotional-hagiographical content rather than factual claims.
- **SRF edition alterations.** Self-Realization Fellowship post-Yogananda editions (after his 1952 death) significantly altered the text in places, removing passages on other yogis (especially those who had broken with SRF) and softening some claims. The 1946 first-edition facsimile is preferred for textual research.
