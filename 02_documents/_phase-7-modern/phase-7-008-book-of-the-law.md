# Liber AL vel Legis (The Book of the Law)

## What it is
A short (~6,500-word) prose-poetic text in three chapters, dictated to Aleister Crowley over three successive days (8, 9, 10 April 1904) at noon in a Cairo flat, with his wife Rose Edith Kelly serving as oracular medium and the discarnate intelligence "Aiwass" as alleged dictator. The three chapters are spoken respectively by the Egyptian goddess Nuit (infinite space, all-possibility), the god Hadit (the dimensionless point of consciousness within Nuit), and Ra-Hoor-Khuit (a form of Horus, the lord of the announced "New Aeon"). The text declares (a) the "Aeon of Horus" has begun, replacing the Aeon of Osiris (the Christian-era dying-god religions); (b) the supreme ethical maxim is "Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law / Love is the law, love under will"; (c) the work of discovering one's "True Will" is the central task of the individual.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Pre-WWI Edwardian period; Crowley was a wealthy expatriate Englishman on a Mediterranean honeymoon. Egypt was a British protectorate; the Cairo Museum (visited by Crowley days before) housed Stele 666 of Ankh-af-na-khonsu, the priest whose stele furnished Thelemic iconography.
- **Religious milieu.** The Golden Dawn (founded 1888) was the principal British occultist organization; Crowley had joined 1898 and split with it. Theosophy was peaking. Late-19th-century Egyptology was generating the symbolic vocabulary the *Book of the Law* would deploy. Spiritualism's practice of mediumistic dictation was standard cultural equipment for any 1904 séance.
- **Who was behind it.** Crowley insisted he did not author the text; he distinguished himself (Aleister Crowley) from "The Beast 666" (his magical persona) from "Aiwass" (the dictator). External-source explanation: Crowley composing in trance-state on Theosophical, Egyptological, and Rabelaisian materials he had recently absorbed.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Crowley reportedly stored the manuscript and ignored it for years (1904–1909). After the 1909 "rediscovery" he treated it as the foundational text of a new religion. Founded the A∴A∴ (1907) and later took over the Ordo Templi Orientis (1912 onward) as institutional vehicles.

## Themes and motifs
- **The New Aeon** — replacing the Osirian Aeon (dying-and-rising gods, suffering-redemption religions, especially Christianity) with the Horian Aeon (each individual as their own crowned-and-conquering child).
- **True Will (Thelema = θέλημα, Greek "will")** — each person has a True Will distinct from surface desires; the discovery and execution of one's True Will is the central spiritual task.
- **"Every man and every woman is a star"** — radical individualism; non-interference ethic.
- **Provocative shock-content** — the third chapter contains passages (especially III.18–22 and III.49–51) celebrating violent imagery against rival religions; Thelemites typically read these as alchemical / symbolic rather than literal but they have been controversial since publication.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-7-016-magick-in-theory-and-practice]] — *direct-influence* (same author): Crowley's later technical magickal corpus operationalizes the *Book of the Law*.
- → [[phase-7-030-satanic-bible]] — *direct-influence*: LaVey's individualism, anti-Christian register, and several specific formulations descend from Crowley.
- → [[tradition-wicca-modern-pagan]] — *parallel-development with personal contact*: Gerald Gardner met Crowley in 1947; Wiccan ritual incorporates Crowleyan elements (Gardner is alleged to have purchased Crowley-authored rituals; precise dependence is contested in Wicca scholarship — Heselton 2000).
- → Rabelais' Abbey of Thélème (*Gargantua* I.57, 1534) — *direct-quote*: Crowley's "Do what thou wilt" is Rabelais' *Fay ce que vouldras* with Greek translation (*thelema*).

## Disputes
- **Authorship.** Whether the text was genuinely "received" (Crowley's framing, retained by Thelemites) or self-composed in altered-state (academic-historical standard view).
- **Reading of the violent passages.** Symbolic-alchemical interpretation (Crowley's later commentaries) vs. surface-literal reading (some critics, especially in the context of 20th-century occult-political extremism).
- **Ethics of the True-Will doctrine.** Whether Thelemic ethics functions as a moral framework, an anti-moral nihilism, or something in between is debated within and outside Thelema.
