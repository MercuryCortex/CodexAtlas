# The Life Divine

## What it is
Sri Aurobindo's principal philosophical work, ~1,100 pages, serialized in his journal *Arya* 1914–1919 and substantially revised for book publication 1939–1940. Aurobindo argues that the Vedantic Absolute (Sachchidananda — Being-Consciousness-Bliss) involves itself in matter via a descending series of planes (Supermind → Overmind → Intuition → Illumined Mind → Higher Mind → Mind → Life → Matter), and that evolution is the inverse-ascending process by which matter progressively recovers Spirit. The next evolutionary step, Aurobindo argues, is the descent of the Supermind into earthly existence — producing a "Supramental Being" beyond present humanity. The work integrates the Upanishadic-Vedantic tradition with 19th- and early-20th-century evolutionism (Hegelian, Bergsonian, Darwinian), producing a distinctive Integral metaphysics.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Aurobindo had been a leading revolutionary in the Indian independence movement 1906–1910, arrested for the Alipore Bomb Case (1908), acquitted (1909), then withdrew from political activity and moved to French Pondicherry in 1910, where he remained for the rest of his life. *The Life Divine* is the philosophical work of his ashram-period.
- **Religious milieu.** Vivekananda (d. 1902) had opened the Hindu modernist channel to the West; Tagore (Nobel 1913) provided literary prestige; Gandhi (active from 1915) was the political register. Aurobindo offered the systematic-philosophical register.
- **Who was behind it.** Aurobindo; Mirra Alfassa ("the Mother," 1878–1973) was his collaborator from her 1914 arrival in Pondicherry and led the Ashram after his 1950 death.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous since 1940; the Sri Aurobindo Ashram press; the *Complete Works* (37 volumes, 1997–2005) is the critical edition. Auroville (founded 1968) and Integral theory (Ken Wilber, *Sex, Ecology, Spirituality* 1995) are major 20th-century downstream developments.

## Themes and motifs
- **Sachchidananda** — the three-aspect Absolute: Being (Sat), Consciousness-Force (Cit), Bliss (Ananda); standard Vedantic, but Aurobindo emphasizes the *creative-evolutionary* dimension of Cit.
- **Supermind** — Aurobindo's term for the divine truth-consciousness; the next evolutionary stage beyond present human Mind.
- **Spiritual evolution** — the central Aurobindonian move: Darwinian evolution is the visible surface of an inner evolution of consciousness from Matter back to Spirit.
- **Integral Yoga** — Aurobindo's practical-discipline correlate: not retreat from the world but transformation of the world through descent of Supermind.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Upanishads — *commentary-on*: Aurobindo translated and commented on principal Upanishads; *Life Divine* is partly an extended Upanishadic exegesis.
- → 19th-century European evolutionism — *direct-influence (integrating)*: Hegel's Geist, Bergson's *Évolution créatrice* (1907), Darwin's biological evolution are integrated into a Vedantic frame.
- → Integral theory (Wilber 1995+) — *direct-influence*: Wilber's "AQAL" model explicitly inherits the Aurobindonian developmental-evolutionary frame.
- → [[phase-7-026-autobiography-of-a-yogi]] — *parallel-development*: Aurobindo (philosophical-Indian register) and Yogananda (devotional-popular-Western register) are contemporary projects of Hindu modernism reaching English-reading audiences.

## Disputes
- **Heehs biography controversy.** Peter Heehs's *The Lives of Sri Aurobindo* (2008), an academically standard biography, was suppressed in India after Ashram-affiliated groups sued; Heehs continues to face administrative consequences within the Ashram. The dispute is about *the genre of religious-founder biography* as much as about specific Heehs claims.
- **Supermind descent.** Aurobindo and the Mother claimed the Supermind descended into the earth-consciousness on 29 February 1956 (a leap day). The empirical-evidential status of the claim within and outside Integral Yoga is debated.
