# The Satanic Bible

## What it is
Anton LaVey's 1969 founding text of the Church of Satan (which LaVey had incorporated 1966, declaring the founding "year 1 *Anno Satanas*" on Walpurgisnacht 30 April 1966). The book consists of four parts: (1) "The Book of Satan" — short polemical-rhetorical excerpts (largely lifted from Ragnar Redbeard's 1896 *Might Is Right*, mostly unattributed); (2) "The Book of Lucifer" — LaVey's philosophical exposition; (3) "The Book of Belial" — ritual theory; (4) "The Book of Leviathan" — invocations and rituals, including the nineteen Enochian Keys lifted from Dee/Crowley with substituted Satanic vocabulary. The philosophy is a fusion of Nietzschean anti-Christianity, Randian individualist egoism, and ceremonial-magick aesthetics, presenting Satan as a *symbol* (not a literal being) of the natural, carnal, vital principle that Christianity has demonized.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-1960s San Francisco; the counterculture peaked; the "Summer of Love" had just ended. LaVey was a former carnival worker, organist, and police photographer who had built a small philosophical-occult circle around himself in his Black House at 6114 California Street.
- **Religious milieu.** The 1966 *Time* magazine cover "Is God Dead?" had asked the question; the parodic-theatrical inversion of Christian religious-form was particularly available as a cultural option. The Church of Satan grew rapidly in the early 1970s before plateauing; LaVey appeared in popular culture (Sammy Davis Jr. briefly joined; Jayne Mansfield was associated).
- **Who was behind it.** LaVey personally; the text's compilation from prior sources (especially Redbeard) is documented (Lewis 2003).
- **Discovery / transmission.** 1969 Avon mass-market paperback was a deliberate distribution strategy (LaVey wanted the book ubiquitous in airport bookshops); over 1 million copies sold. *The Satanic Rituals* (1972) and *The Satanic Witch* (1971) complete LaVey's principal-trilogy publication.

## Themes and motifs
- **Satan as symbol** — official LaVeyan position: there is no supernatural Satan; "Satan" is a vivid symbol of the carnal-vital-individualist principle.
- **Inversion of Christian ethics** — pride > humility, indulgence > abstinence, vital existence > spiritual pretense.
- **Nine Satanic Statements** — LaVey's famous opening: "Satan represents indulgence, instead of abstinence! Satan represents vital existence, instead of spiritual pipe dreams! ..."
- **Ritual as psychodrama** — LaVeyan ritual is explicitly framed as cathartic-theatrical-psychological, not supernatural-operative. Distinguishes LaVeyan Satanism from theistic Satanism.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-7-008-book-of-the-law]] — *direct-influence*: Crowley's individualism, anti-Christianity, and ceremonial-magick aesthetics are formative for LaVey; the Enochian material is sourced via Crowley.
- → Ayn Rand — *direct-influence*: Randian egoism is a major component of LaVey's ethical theory; LaVey called Satanism "Ayn Rand with trappings."
- → Temple of Set (1975) — *direct-influence (with schism)*: Michael Aquino split from the Church of Satan in 1975 to form the Temple of Set, retaining most of the ceremonial aesthetic while shifting to a theistic-Setian metaphysics.

## Disputes
- **Unattributed sourcing.** Lewis 2003 and Dyrendal et al. 2016 document the substantial unattributed lifting from Redbeard 1896 in "The Book of Satan."
- **LaVey biographical claims.** Many LaVey biographical claims (carnival lion-tamer, San Francisco police photographer, organist for various entertainers) have mixed documentary support. Barton 1990 (LaVey's intimate-partner-and-successor) is sympathetic-internal; some claims have been corroborated, others contested.
