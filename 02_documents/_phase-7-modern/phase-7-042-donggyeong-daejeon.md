# Donggyeong Daejeon (Eastern Classic)

**Donggyeong Daejeon** ("Great Scripture of the Eastern Learning") — composed by Choe Je-u (1824–1864) in 1861 and printed posthumously in 1880 — is the foundational text of the Donghak ("Eastern Learning") movement and its successor, Cheondogyo ("Religion of the Heavenly Way"). Choe Je-u, a failed Confucian scholar from a marginalized noble family in declining Joseon Korea, reported a revelatory encounter with a divine being (*Hanullim*, the Lord of Heaven) in 1860 and composed this text to systematize the resulting theology.

## Cross-tradition significance

**Deliberate counter-synthesis to Western Christianity**: The name "Eastern Learning" (*Donghak*) is an explicit counter to "Western Learning" (*Seohak*), the Korean term for Catholicism spreading from Jesuit sources. Choe Je-u rejected both the social hierarchy of Confucianism and the foreign origin of Christianity, synthesizing a Korean-rooted theology that absorbed elements from all three — Confucian ethics, Buddhist practice, Daoist cosmology — and added a new monotheistic revelation. The *Donggyeong Daejeon* is a rare case of a deliberate 19th-century syncretic canon-making: a founder who explicitly knew what he was synthesizing and why.

**Donghak → Korean independence**: The Donghak Peasant Revolution of 1894 — in which Donghak-organized peasant armies seized half of Korea before being crushed by Japanese and Chinese intervention — directly triggered the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95), which transferred Korea into Japan's sphere of influence. The same tradition, reorganized as Cheondogyo, produced Son Byong-hui, one of the 33 signatories of the Korean Declaration of Independence on March 1, 1919. No other new religious movement in the 19th century had this scale of geopolitical consequence.
