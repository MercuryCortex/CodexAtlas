# The Book of Mormon

## What it is
A 1830 English-language scripture, ~270,000 words, presented by Joseph Smith Jr. (1805–1844) as his translation of an ancient record engraved on golden plates in "reformed Egyptian," delivered to him by the angel Moroni at Cumorah (upstate New York) in 1827. The narrative recounts pre-Columbian Hebrew migrations to the Americas (the Lehite migration ~600 BCE; the earlier Jaredite migration from the Tower of Babel), centuries of righteous-and-apostate dynastic cycles among the Nephites and Lamanites, a post-resurrection visit of Jesus Christ to the Nephites in the New World (3 Nephi 11–28), and the final destruction of the Nephites at the hands of the Lamanites ~400 CE. Book divisions imitate biblical structure (1 Nephi, 2 Nephi, Jacob, Enos, Jarom, Omni, Mosiah, Alma, Helaman, 3 Nephi, 4 Nephi, Mormon, Ether, Moroni). The text claims to contain "the fullness of the gospel" and to be a *companion* to (not a replacement for) the Bible — the foundation of LDS restorationist claims.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Jacksonian-era United States; the "Burned-Over District" of western New York, a region swept by successive revivals during the Second Great Awakening (~1790–1840). Religious entrepreneurship was unusually permissive — the Cane Ridge revivals, Shaker communities, Hicksite Quakers, Campbellite restorationism, and Millerite adventism were nearby in time and space.
- **Religious milieu.** Restorationist Protestantism asked: which church was the original apostolic church, and how do we recover it? Smith's answer was radical: *all* churches were apostate; the original gospel had been lost and would be restored by new revelation. The Book of Mormon performs this restoration claim materially — a *new* scripture for a *new* dispensation.
- **Who was behind it.** Smith dictated the text to scribes (Martin Harris briefly; Oliver Cowdery for most of the surviving manuscript; Emma Smith and others incidentally), typically with his head in a hat into which he placed a seer stone (the same stone he had earlier used for treasure-seeking, 1822–1826). The 116-page "Book of Lehi" was lost by Harris in 1828; Smith re-dictated as the "Small Plates of Nephi" rather than retranslating, citing revelation. Total dictation period: ~April 1828–June 1829.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Smith reported finding the plates 22 September 1827 buried in the Hill Cumorah near Manchester, NY. The plates were never shown to the general public; Eleven Witnesses signed statements affirming they had seen them (Three Witnesses — Cowdery, Harris, David Whitmer — testified to angelic visitation; Eight Witnesses testified to physical handling). All eleven left the church at various points; most later reaffirmed their testimonies but interpretations of *what* they saw (literal or "spiritual eye") remain disputed. The plates were returned to Moroni after translation; no extant physical artifact.

## Themes and motifs
- [[restorationism]] — primitive Christianity has been lost; a *new* prophet receives a *new* revelation to restore it. The Book of Mormon is itself the proof-token of that restoration.
- [[chosen-people]] — the Nephites are a transplanted branch of Israel; the Americas are a promised land "kept from the knowledge of other nations"; a *second* chosen-people narrative geographically displaced from the first.
- [[messianism]] — Christ visits the Americas in 3 Nephi; Christology is fully developed, including a Trinitarian-Modalist register debated by LDS theologians (the 1830 text reads more Modalist; later doctrinal development moved toward a distinct Social-Trinitarian Godhead).
- **Apostasy and dispensational cycle** — repeated rise-fall-restoration cycles internal to the narrative prefigure the LDS reading of Christian history as one long apostasy ending with the Restoration of 1820–1830.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-7-002-doctrine-and-covenants]] — *direct-influence* (same author, same tradition): the D&C extends Smith's revelatory output into ecclesial governance.
- → King James Bible — *direct-quote* (extensive): ~27,000 KJV-cognate words; entire chapters of Isaiah (2 Nephi 12–24 = KJV Isaiah 2–14) appear with minor variants. Skousen's textual-criticism work shows that KJV italicized words (English glosses with no Hebrew/Greek counterpart) are sometimes silently incorporated into Book of Mormon "ancient" passages — a finding with disputed apologetic interpretations.
- → *View of the Hebrews* (Ethan Smith, 1823) — *parallel-motif* / *contested-influence*: B.H. Roberts (LDS general authority) drafted a private memorandum (*Studies of the Book of Mormon*, posthumous 1985) noting striking thematic and structural parallels; mainstream LDS scholarship disputes direct dependence, critical scholarship argues for influence.
- → [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — *parallel-motif*: Pauline soteriology (atonement, faith, grace) is fully present in Book of Mormon sermons (Alma 5; Mosiah 3) — an anachronism by the narrative's own dating (BC sermons preaching specifically Pauline categories) flagged by critical scholarship as evidence of 19th-century composition.

## Disputes
- **Historicity.** LDS scholarship (Sorenson, FARMS/Maxwell Institute) defends a "limited geography" model placing the Nephite civilization in Mesoamerica; mainstream archaeology, linguistics, and population genetics find no evidence of pre-Columbian Hebrew migrations, of "reformed Egyptian" script, of horses/chariots/steel/silk in the dated Pre-Columbian Americas, or of Lehite DNA in Native American populations (the 2014 LDS-published "Book of Mormon and DNA Studies" essay revised earlier "principal ancestors" language). [claim: contested — sect-internal vs. mainstream-academic].
- **Composition.** Critical scholarship considers it Smith's 1828–1829 composition, dictated extempore with KJV pastiche, biblical-typological structure, and reflexes of his contemporary religious environment (Vogel, Palmer, the Mormon Stories podcast scholarship). LDS scholarship treats it as ancient translation. The "loose translation" theory (Blake Ostler, others) within LDS scholarship grants Smith significant compositional involvement while retaining an ancient source.
- **Text-critical issues.** Skousen's *Earliest Text* edition reconstructs the Original Manuscript readings, recovering hundreds of variants edited out in 1837/1840/1981 LDS editions.
