# Exodus

**Exodus** (Hebrew *Šəmōt*, "Names"; Greek *Exodos*, "going out") is the second book of the **Torah** and the narrative heart of Israelite identity. It recounts Israel's **bondage in Egypt**, the call of **[[moses|Moses]]** at the burning bush and the revelation of the divine name **YHWH** (**[[yahweh|Yahweh]]**), the contest of the **ten plagues** with **Pharaoh**, the **Passover** and the deliverance through the **Sea of Reeds**, the theophany and **covenant at Mount Sinai**, the giving of the **Decalogue** and the **Covenant Code**, the apostasy of the golden calf, and the construction of the **Tabernacle**.

## Composition

Like the rest of the Pentateuch, Exodus is a **composite** of the J, E, and P sources redacted in the exilic/post-exilic period. Embedded within it is an older, self-contained legal text — the **Covenant Code** (Exodus 21–23) — which preserves the casuistic legal tradition of the ancient Near East.

## The cross-tradition wire

The **Covenant Code** stands in clear formal relation to the great Mesopotamian law collections, above all the **[[phase-1-007-code-of-hammurabi|Code of Hammurabi]]**: the same **casuistic "if a man… then…" structure**, overlapping case law (the goring ox, the seducer, debt-slavery, *lex talionis* "eye for eye"), and a shared conception of law as divine-royal justice. The differences are theologically pointed — Israelite law subordinates all jurisdiction directly to God rather than the king, and mixes apodictic command ("You shall not…") with the inherited casuistic form. The **Sinai covenant** itself is cast in the mould of the ANE **suzerain–vassal treaty**, with historical prologue, stipulations, and blessings/curses.

## Reach forward

Exodus supplies the master-paradigm of **redemption**: Passover and the Law anchor Jewish practice; the New Testament reads Jesus as a new Moses and the Last Supper as a Passover; the Qurʾān retells Mūsā against Firʿawn; and the "Exodus" became the enduring Western template for liberation from oppression.
