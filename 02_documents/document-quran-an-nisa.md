# Sūrat al-Nisāʾ (Quran 4 — Women)

## What it is
A long Medinan legal sura, named for its extensive legislation on women, orphans, inheritance, and marriage — much of it reforming pre-Islamic Arabian custom in the wake of the casualties at Uḥud. It is also the site of the Quran's most consequential Christological statement: the denial that Jesus was crucified.

## Primary text

### Quran 4:157–158
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> [They claimed] "We have killed the Messiah, Jesus, son of Mary, the Messenger of God." They did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, though it was made to appear so to them... God raised him up to Himself. God is almighty and wise.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Medinan law-giving alongside sharp theological boundary-drawing against both Jewish and Christian claims.
- **Christology.** 4:157 denies the crucifixion — "it was made to appear so" (shubbiha lahum) — and affirms Jesus's bodily assumption to God.
- **Discovery / transmission.** A central legal and creedal sura.

## Themes and motifs
- Family and inheritance law — orphans, women's shares, marriage (the foundation of Islamic personal law).
- [[messianism]] — Jesus the Messiah, raised by God rather than crucified.
- Justice and protection — the rights of the weak (orphans, women, the enslaved).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Quran 4:157 — the crucifixion denied, and its uncanny kinship with Christian docetism.** The single verse "they did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, but it was made to appear so to them" is the deepest theological rift between Islam and Christianity: it removes the cross — the very center of Christian faith — from the life of Jesus. Yet its logic is not new to Islam. Second-century Christian docetists and Gnostics (Basilides, per Irenaeus, had Simon of Cyrene crucified in Jesus's place; the Gnostic Apocalypse of Peter has the "living Jesus" laughing above the cross) taught exactly this "substitution" — that the divine Christ could not really suffer and only seemed to die. The Quran's shubbiha lahum ("it was made to appear so") thus stands in a long late-antique stream of docetic readings, making 4:157 a node where Islamic and heterodox-Christian Christologies converge against the orthodox cross (Lawson 2009; Reynolds 2010 pp.147–155).
