# Sūrat Ṭā Hā (Quran 20)

## What it is
A Meccan sura built around the most complete telling of the Moses story in the Quran: God's call to Moses at the burning bush, the gift of the staff and the shining hand, the confrontation with Pharaoh and the defeat of his magicians, the deliverance of Israel through the sea, and the apostasy of the golden calf. By tradition it is the sura whose recitation moved the future caliph ʿUmar to embrace Islam. Moses (Mūsā) is the Quran's most-frequently-mentioned prophet, and this sura is his fullest portrait.

## Primary text

### Quran 20:11–14
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> When he reached it, a voice called: "Moses! I am your Lord. Take off your shoes: you are in the sacred valley of Ṭuwā. I have chosen you, so listen to what is being revealed. I am God; there is no god but Me. So worship Me and keep up the prayer so that you remember Me."

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Meccan Islam; Moses serves as the great prophetic precedent for Muhammad's own mission against entrenched power.
- **Sources.** The narrative parallels Exodus closely, with characteristic Quranic compressions and one notable shift (the calf's maker).
- **Discovery / transmission.** A beloved sura of recitation and conversion.

## Themes and motifs
- The prophet against the tyrant — Moses before Pharaoh.
- Divine self-revelation — "I am God; there is no god but Me" at the bush (20:14).
- Apostasy and mercy — the golden calf and Moses's intercession.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The Quran's Moses — Exodus retold, with the golden calf reassigned.** Ṭā Hā follows Exodus through the burning bush ("I am God; there is no god but Me," 20:14, echoing "I AM," Exod 3:14), the staff-turned-serpent, the plagues and the sea, and the golden calf — making Moses the Quran's central biblical prophet and the model for Muhammad's confrontation with Mecca. The most theologically pointed change is the calf: where Exodus 32 has Aaron fashion it, the Quran exonerates the prophet Aaron and blames a single outsider, "the Sāmirī" (20:85–95) — a deliberate adjustment protecting prophetic sinlessness, and a small window onto how the Quran handles the moral problems its biblical sources leave open (Reynolds 2010 pp.121–129; Nasr 2015 pp.781–800).
