# Book of Malachi

## What it is
The last book of the Twelve and — in the Christian ordering — the final book of the Old Testament. Cast as a series of disputations between God and a complacent post-exilic community (each: a divine assertion, the people's skeptical objection, God's rebuttal), it indicts a corrupt priesthood, careless sacrifice, faithless divorce, and withheld tithes. It ends with the promise that God will send "the prophet Elijah" before the great Day of the LORD — the verse that, for Christians, opens the gap the New Testament fills.

## Primary text

### Malachi 3:1; 4:5–6
*Trans. NRSV*
> See, I am sending my messenger to prepare the way before me, and the Lord whom you seek will suddenly come to his temple. (3:1)
> Lo, I will send you the prophet Elijah before the great and terrible day of the LORD comes. He will turn the hearts of parents to their children and the hearts of children to their parents. (4:5–6)

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Persian Yehud, c. 460 BCE, in the same reforming milieu as Ezra and Nehemiah.
- **Religious milieu.** A polemic against priestly corruption and covenant infidelity; traditionally regarded as the last of the prophets, after whom "prophecy ceased" in rabbinic thought.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous canonical transmission; placed last among the Twelve.

## Themes and motifs
- The messenger / forerunner — the one who prepares the way (3:1) and the returning Elijah (4:5).
- [[messianism]] — the "Lord" and the "messenger of the covenant" who will come to the Temple.
- Covenant fidelity — right sacrifice, faithful marriage, honest tithing.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[elijah-prophet]] / [[john-the-baptist]] — *direct-influence*: Malachi's returning Elijah (4:5) is identified with John the Baptist in the Gospels.
- → [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] — *direct-quote*: Matthew 11:10, 14 and 17:10–13 cite Malachi 3:1 and 4:5 of John the Baptist as the forerunner.
- → [[document-zechariah]] — *shared-milieu*: the post-exilic prophetic close of the Twelve.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Malachi is the literary bridge from the Old Testament to the New.** The Christian Old Testament deliberately ends with Malachi's promise of a returning Elijah who will prepare the way before the Day of the LORD (4:5–6) — and all four Gospels open the New Testament by identifying that forerunner with John the Baptist (Mark 1:2 fuses Mal 3:1 with Isa 40:3; Matt 11:14 makes it explicit: "he is Elijah who is to come"). The canon's seam — the turn of the page from Old to New — was engineered around a single 5th-century-BCE oracle (Hill 1998, AYB 25D pp.363–393).
