# The Treatise on the Resurrection (The Letter to Rheginos)

## What it is
A short, elegant Valentinian letter instructing a pupil named Rheginos on the resurrection. Against any crudely literal hope for the raising of the flesh, it teaches that the resurrection is spiritual and, for the one who knows, already accomplished: the Savior "swallowed up death," and the elect have in truth "already risen" — they need only grasp it. Drawing on Paul's resurrection language while turning it inward, it is the clearest Valentinian statement of a realized, spiritual resurrection.

## Primary text

### On the present resurrection
*Trans. Meyer (2007)*
> Do not think the resurrection is an illusion. It is not an illusion, but it is truth. Indeed, it is more fitting to say that the world is an illusion, rather than the resurrection... Why not consider yourself as risen and already brought to this?

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Valentinian Christianity, with its spiritual anthropology and its reading of Paul.
- **Position.** Resurrection as a present, spiritual reality of the knowing self, not a future raising of corpses.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The Jung Codex of Nag Hammadi.

## Themes and motifs
- [[resurrection-of-the-dead]] — reinterpreted as spiritual and already realized.
- The illusory world — the cosmos, not the resurrection, is the unreal thing.
- Knowing as rising — salvation as the realization of one's already-accomplished resurrection.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-003-gospel-of-truth]] / [[phase-4-059-tripartite-tractate]] — *shared-source*: the Valentinian corpus and its spiritualizing theology.
- → [[document-1-corinthians]] — *commentary-on*: a Valentinian re-reading of Paul's resurrection chapter (1 Cor 15) toward a present, spiritual sense.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The heresy 2 Timothy names — now in its own words.** The Pastoral Epistle warns against Hymenaeus and Philetus, "who have swerved from the truth by saying that the resurrection is past already, and they upset the faith of some" (2 Tim 2:18). The Treatise on the Resurrection is precisely that teaching, stated positively and beautifully: the resurrection is spiritual and the elect have "already risen." For the first time the Nag Hammadi find lets us hear a position the New Testament had only let us hear condemned — a vivid case of the buried library giving voice to the losing side of an early-Christian argument (Peel 1969; Layton 1987 pp.316–324; Meyer 2007 pp.49–56).
