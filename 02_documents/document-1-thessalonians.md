# First Epistle to the Thessalonians

## What it is
The oldest book in the New Testament — indeed the earliest surviving Christian writing of any kind, predating the Gospels by a generation. Paul writes warmly to the young, persecuted church he had recently founded at Thessalonica, commending their faith, defending his conduct, and answering an acute pastoral worry: what becomes of believers who die before the Lord returns? His answer — that the dead in Christ will rise first and the living be caught up to meet him — is the earliest written Christian eschatology.

## Primary text

### 1 Thessalonians 4:16–17
*Trans. NRSV*
> For the Lord himself, with a cry of command, with the archangel's call and with the sound of God's trumpet, will descend from heaven, and the dead in Christ will rise first. Then we who are alive, who are left, will be caught up in the clouds together with them to meet the Lord in the air.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Earliest Christianity, gripped by the expectation of Christ's imminent return.
- **Authorship.** Undisputed Pauline; securely dated ~50 CE.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The earliest-written NT book, transmitted within the Pauline corpus.

## Themes and motifs
- The Parousia — the coming of the Lord and the fate of the dead (4:13–18).
- [[resurrection-of-the-dead]] — the dead in Christ rising at his return.
- Watchfulness — the day of the Lord coming "like a thief in the night" (5:2).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The oldest Christian text we possess — and the seed of the 'rapture'.** Written about 50 CE, 1 Thessalonians predates every Gospel and is the earliest window we have into Christian belief, barely two decades after the crucifixion. Its description of the living being "caught up (harpagēsometha) in the clouds... to meet the Lord in the air" (4:17) — Latin *rapiemur*, hence "rapture" — became, via 19th-century dispensationalism, the proof-text of an entire modern eschatology and a vast popular literature (Malherbe 2000, AYB 32B pp.62–73, 267–286).
