# Second Epistle to the Thessalonians

## What it is
A short letter in Paul's name addressing a community thrown into eschatological panic by the claim — perhaps spread by a forged letter — that "the day of the Lord is already here." It answers that the Day cannot come until "the rebellion" and the revealing of "the man of lawlessness" who exalts himself in God's temple; a mysterious "restrainer" currently holds him back. It closes by rebuking idleness with the famous rule that those unwilling to work should not eat.

## Primary text

### 2 Thessalonians 2:3–4
*Trans. NRSV*
> Let no one deceive you in any way; for that day will not come unless the rebellion comes first and the lawless one is revealed, the one destined for destruction. He opposes and exalts himself above every so-called god... declaring himself to be God.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** A church destabilized by over-realized eschatology; the letter reasserts a future, sequenced end.
- **Authorship.** Disputed: its eschatology (a timetable of signs) sits awkwardly beside 1 Thessalonians' "thief in the night" immediacy.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Within the Pauline corpus.

## Themes and motifs
- [[apocalyptic-revelation]] — the sequenced end: rebellion, the lawless one, the restrainer, the Parousia.
- The man of lawlessness — a key root of the Antichrist tradition.
- [[messianism]] — the coming of the Lord Jesus to destroy the lawless one "with the breath of his mouth."

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[document-revelation]] — *parallel-motif*: the "man of lawlessness" stands beside Revelation's beast in the Antichrist tradition.
- → [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — *direct-influence*: the self-exalting figure in the temple echoes Daniel's "desolating sacrilege" and the king who magnifies himself (Dan 11:36).
