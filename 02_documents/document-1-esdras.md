# First Book of Esdras

## What it is
A Greek alternative account of Judah's late monarchy, exile, and return — overlapping 2 Chronicles 35–36, Ezra, and Nehemiah 8 — but reordered and famous for one wholly original episode: the "Contest of the Three Guardsmen." Three young bodyguards of King Darius each argue for what is strongest in the world — wine, the king, women — until the third (identified as Zerubbabel) wins by proclaiming that strongest of all is Truth, earning the commission to rebuild Jerusalem.

## Primary text

### 1 Esdras 4:38, 41
*Trans. NRSV (Apocrypha)*
> But truth endures and is strong forever, and lives and prevails forever and ever... Great is truth, and strongest of all!

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Hellenistic-Jewish retelling of the restoration, blending Chronicler's history with court-wisdom.
- **Composition.** A free Greek rendering with the added guardsmen's debate.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Orthodox and Ethiopian canon; a Vulgate appendix; outside the Jewish, Catholic, and Protestant canons.

## Themes and motifs
- The supremacy of truth — the guardsmen's contest and its famous verdict (4:41).
- Restoration — return, Temple-rebuilding, and the reading of the Law.
- [[divine-kingship]] — God's purpose worked through the Persian kings.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[document-ezra]] / [[document-nehemiah]] — *shared-source*: 1 Esdras parallels and reorders the same restoration material.
- → [[document-2-chronicles]] — *shared-source*: it opens with Josiah's Passover from 2 Chronicles 35.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **"Great is Truth" — a deuterocanonical line that became a Western motto.** The climax of the Contest of the Three Guardsmen, *magna est veritas et praevalet* ("great is truth, and it prevails," 4:41), passed from this Greek-Jewish book into Latin proverb, into ecclesiastical and academic heraldry, and into English literature (it heads chapters and adorns mottos to this day). A book on the margins of every canon gave the West one of its most durable aphorisms (Myers 1974, AYB 42 pp.8–15, 52–57).
