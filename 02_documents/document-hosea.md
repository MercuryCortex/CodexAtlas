# Book of Hosea

## What it is
The first book of the Twelve, and the prophetic source of one of the Bible's most enduring metaphors: YHWH's covenant with Israel as a marriage, with Israel's worship of other gods as adultery. God commands Hosea to marry the promiscuous Gomer and to name their children "Not-Pitied" and "Not-My-People" — a living parable of broken and restored covenant. The book oscillates between searing indictment and tender pleading.

## Primary text

### Hosea 6:6 and 11:1
*Trans. NRSV*
> For I desire steadfast love and not sacrifice, the knowledge of God rather than burnt offerings. (6:6)
> When Israel was a child, I loved him, and out of Egypt I called my son. (11:1)

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The disintegrating northern kingdom in its last quarter-century before Assyria erased it.
- **Religious milieu.** A frontal contest with the Baal fertility cult; Hosea recasts YHWH as the true source of grain, wine, and oil (2:8).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous canonical transmission; the Hebrew text is unusually damaged.

## Themes and motifs
- Covenant-marriage — the metaphor of YHWH as faithful husband and Israel as faithless wife (chs. 1–3), the seed of the entire biblical "bride" theology.
- Ḥesed (steadfast love) over sacrifice — Hosea 6:6, the ethical kernel Jesus invokes.
- [[messianism]] — the promise of restoration and a reunited people under "one head" (1:11).

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-2-010-hebrew-bible-early-prophets]] — *shared-milieu*: the grouping node that has folded Hosea; retained as a lens.
- → [[document-jeremiah]] / [[document-ezekiel]] — *direct-influence*: both inherit and expand Hosea's marriage metaphor (Jer 2–3; Ezek 16, 23).
- → [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] — *direct-quote*: Matthew quotes Hosea twice — 6:6 (mercy not sacrifice) and 11:1 (out of Egypt), the latter applied to the infant Jesus's return from Egypt.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Hosea invents the divine-marriage metaphor that organizes biblical theology from the prophets to the Apocalypse.** YHWH-as-husband / Israel-as-bride passes from Hosea into Jeremiah and Ezekiel, into the allegorized Song of Songs, into the New Testament's "bride of Christ" and the wedding of the Lamb in Revelation 19–21. A single 8th-century northern prophet's scandalous marriage supplies the controlling image for the covenant across a thousand years of the canon (Andersen & Freedman 1980, AYB 24 pp.117–135).
