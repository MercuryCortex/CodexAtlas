# Sūrat al-Nūr (Quran 24 — Light)

## What it is
A Medinan sura combining communal law with one of the most luminous passages in scripture. Much of it legislates the life of the young community — the protection of reputation (the requirement of four witnesses, after the slander against the Prophet's wife ʿĀʾisha), the etiquette of gaze and dress, the privacy of households. At its centre stands the **Verse of Light** (24:35), a layered metaphor — God as the light of the heavens and earth, like a lamp in a niche, the lamp in glass like a shining star, kindled from a blessed olive tree "neither of the East nor of the West," light upon light — which became the seed of a thousand years of Islamic mysticism and philosophy.

## Primary text

### Quran 24:35
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> God is the Light of the heavens and earth. His Light is like this: there is a niche, and in it a lamp, the lamp inside a glass, a glass like a glittering star, lit from a blessed olive tree, neither of the East nor of the West, whose oil almost gives light even when no fire touches it — light upon light — God guides whoever He will to His Light.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Medinan; the sura builds the moral and legal fabric of the community while lifting the eye to God's own radiance.
- **Tradition.** al-Ghazālī's *Mishkāt al-Anwār* ("The Niche of Lights") is an entire treatise unpacking this one verse.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The niche-lamp-glass image entered Islamic architecture — the *miḥrāb* niche, the mosque lamp — as a visual theology of the verse.

## Themes and motifs
- Light upon light — God's radiance figured through niche, lamp, glass, and oil.
- The guarded community — laws of witness, modesty, and reputation.
- Guidance — light as the metaphor for being shown the way.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **"Light upon light" — the divine radiance across the mystical traditions.** The Verse of Light gave Islam its master-metaphor for God's immanent presence, and a whole philosophical lineage grew from it: al-Ghazālī's *Mishkāt al-Anwār* reads the niche, glass, lamp, tree, and oil as stages of the soul's faculties and the prophetic spirit; Suhrawardī's *ḥikmat al-ishrāq*, "the wisdom of illumination," makes graded light the very substance of reality, explicitly fusing the Quranic image with Neoplatonic emanation and the Zoroastrian light-cosmology of his Persian inheritance. That places 24:35 at a great confluence: the biblical God who is "my light and my salvation" (Psalm 27:1) and in whose light "we see light" (Psalm 36:9); the Johannine Christ as "the light of the world" (John 8:12); Plotinus's One overflowing as light; and the Iranian dualism of light against darkness. The Qur'an's distinctive contribution is the *nested vessel* — light mediated through niche and glass and oil, "neither East nor West" — an image of how the infinite radiance becomes bearable to finite sight, which Islamic art then built into the lamp-hung miḥrāb of every mosque (al-Ghazālī 1998 pp.1–53; McAuliffe 2006 pp.163–180; Nasr 2015 pp.873–879).
