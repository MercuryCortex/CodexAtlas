# Book of Judges

## What it is
The second book of the Deuteronomistic History, covering the turbulent pre-monarchic era through a gallery of "judges" — charismatic military deliverers like Deborah, Gideon, Jephthah, and Samson — raised up to rescue the tribes from successive oppressors. A recurring cycle structures the book: Israel does evil, is oppressed, cries out, and is delivered, only to relapse. It ends in near-anarchy, with the refrain that "there was no king in Israel; all the people did what was right in their own eyes."

## Primary text

### Judges 21:25
*Trans. NRSV*
> In those days there was no king in Israel; all the people did what was right in their own eyes.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** The Deuteronomistic cycle dramatizes the consequences of covenant infidelity; the closing chaos sets up the case for monarchy in Samuel.
- **Composition.** Older hero-legends framed by a late editorial theology.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous canonical transmission.

## Themes and motifs
- The deliverance cycle — apostasy, oppression, cry, rescue, relapse.
- Charismatic leadership — the Spirit-seized deliverer (Othniel to Samson).
- [[divine-kingship]] — the tension between "no king but YHWH" and the looming human monarchy.
