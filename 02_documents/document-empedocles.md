# Empedocles — On Nature and Purifications

## What it is
The fragments of Empedocles of Acragas — philosopher, physician, wonder-worker, and (by his own account) an exiled god. His cosmology answers Parmenides by positing four eternal "roots" — earth, air, fire, and water — which two opposed forces, **Love** and **Strife**, alternately unite into a perfect Sphere and tear apart, in an endless cosmic cycle. His *Purifications* sets out the soul's fall, its transmigration through plants, animals, and humans, and the path of purification back to the divine. Healer and mystic at once, Empedocles is the most flamboyant of the Presocratics.

## Primary text

### On the four roots and the two forces
*Trans. Wright (1981)*
> Hear first the four roots of all things: bright Zeus [fire], life-giving Hera [air], and Aidoneus [earth], and Nestis [water] whose tears are the source of mortal streams. ... At one time growing to be one alone out of many, at another again dividing to be many out of one.

## Context
- **Intellectual milieu.** Post-Parmenidean pluralism in the Greek West, fused with Orphic-Pythagorean soul-religion.
- **The two poems.** "On Nature" (physics) and "Purifications" (the soul's destiny) — perhaps a single work; the Strasbourg Papyrus blurred the old division.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Quoted fragments plus the 1999 Strasbourg Papyrus.

## Themes and motifs
- The four roots — earth, air, fire, water as the unchanging elements.
- Love and Strife — the cosmic forces of union and separation.
- Transmigration and purification — the fallen daimon's return to the divine.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[document-parmenides-poem]] — *commentary-on*: Empedocles preserves Parmenidean being by making the four roots eternal and indestructible while allowing change as their mixture.
- → [[document-heraclitus-fragments]] — *parallel-motif*: opposed cosmic forces and the strife/harmony of elements.
- → [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — *direct-influence*: the transmigration and purification of the soul feed Plato's Phaedo and Phaedrus.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The four elements — two thousand years of physics and medicine.** Empedocles's four roots — earth, air, fire, water — were taken up by Aristotle (who added a fifth, aether) and became the foundation of Western and Islamic natural philosophy, cosmology, alchemy, and Galenic medicine (the four humors) until the Scientific Revolution. At the same time his *Purifications* carries the Orphic-Pythagorean doctrine of the soul's fall and reincarnation into the mainstream of Greek thought, where Plato makes it central — a stream that flows on into Neoplatonism, and into Jewish and Christian debates over the soul's pre-existence (Wright 1981; Inwood 2001; KRS 1983 pp.280–321).
