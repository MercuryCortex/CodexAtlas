# Vāstu Śāstra

## What it is
**Vāstu Śāstra** ("science of dwelling/site") is the Sanskrit body of architectural theory governing the layout, orientation, proportion, and ritual fitness of built space — from a single house to a temple to a city. Its conceptual heart is the **Vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala**: a square grid laid over the building site, on which the cosmic "site-being" (Vāstu-puruṣa) is pinned down, with each cell assigned to a deity. To build correctly is to align the structure with cosmic order; orientation, module, and proportion are not aesthetics but ritual necessities. Like its sister corpus the [[shilpa-shastra]] (which governs the image), vāstu is dispersed across many texts — the *Bṛhat Saṃhitā* of Varāhamihira (~6th c.), the *Mānasāra*, the *Mayamata*, and the *Samarāṅgaṇa-sūtradhāra* attributed to King Bhoja (~11th c.).

## Significance
The Vāstu-puruṣa-maṇḍala is the clearest Indian instance of architecture as cosmology — the temple plan is literally a [[theme-mandala-as-architectural-programme]]. The same proportional-ritual logic underlies Hindu, Jain, and (via the Āgamic and Pāla worlds) much Buddhist building, a continuity captured in [[theme-hindu-buddhist-architectural-continuity]]. The tradition is also the source of the modern global "vastu" design movement.
