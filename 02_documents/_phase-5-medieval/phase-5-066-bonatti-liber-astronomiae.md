# Liber Astronomiae

## What it is
The **great compendium of Latin medieval astrology** — written c. 1277–1286 by **Guido Bonatti of Forlì**, the most renowned astrologer of 13th-century Europe. The *Liber Astronomiae* gathers the whole apparatus of judicial astrology (nativities, elections, interrogations/horary, mundane and weather astrology) into one authoritative Latin synthesis, drawing directly on the Arabic masters — above all Abū Maʿshar, but also Māshāʾallāh and al-Qabīṣī — newly available to the Latin West through the 12th-century Toledo translations. It is the practical handbook through which Arabic astrological science was naturalized into Christian Europe.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The Guelf–Ghibelline conflict of northern Italy; Bonatti served as a court and military astrologer (famously to Guido da Montefeltro at the siege of Forlì).
- **Religious milieu.** Judicial astrology flourished in Latin Christendom even as theologians debated its compatibility with divine providence and free will; Bonatti writes as a confident professional, and his prominence earned him a place among Dante's diviners in *Inferno* XX.
- **Who was behind it.** A single named master, the emblem of the medieval Latin astrologer.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuously copied, then printed (1491, 1550); a standard reference into the Renaissance.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divinatory-omen-reading]] — the comprehensive Latin systematization of judicial astrology.
- [[microcosm-macrocosm]] — the inherited Aristotelian-Arabic doctrine of celestial causation.
- [[wisdom-as-cosmic-order]] — astrology as a learned science within the Latin university world.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-5-064-abu-mashar-great-introduction]] — *direct-development*: Bonatti's chief authority; the *Liber Astronomiae* transmits Abū Maʿshar to Latin readers.
- → [[phase-3-038-ptolemy-tetrabiblos]] / [[phase-3-040-dorotheus-carmen-astrologicum]] — *transmission-of*: the Ptolemaic theory and the Dorothean horary/electional doctrine, received through the Arabic stream.
- → [[tradition-western-astrology]] — *foundational-text-of*: the medieval Latin anchor of the Western tradition between Firmicus and the Renaissance.

## Disputes
- **Composition date** (c. 1277–1286) and the work's exact structure (the "ten treatises") vary across the manuscript and print traditions.
- **Bonatti's biography** is partly legendary (the Montefeltro and Dante associations are embroidered in later tradition).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The Latin re-entry of the Arabic synthesis.** Bonatti is the node where the Islamic-Golden-Age astrology consolidated by [[phase-5-064-abu-mashar-great-introduction]] becomes the working science of Christian Europe, completing the arc Mesopotamia → Hellenistic → Islam → Latin West that [[tradition-western-astrology]] traces (Tester 1987; North 1986).
- **Astrology at the heart of medieval culture.** That Dante could place a named astrologer among the damned diviners — and that emperors and condottieri retained them — shows how fully this transmitted science had penetrated Latin Christendom (Smoller 1994).
