# Mashafa Berhan (Book of Light)

## What it is
The **Mashafa Berhan** ("Book of Light") is the major theological-pastoral-political treatise of Emperor [[zara-yaqob-emperor]] (r. 1434–1468), composed in Ge'ez during the 1450s–1460s and reflecting his comprehensive doctrinal-administrative consolidation of Ethiopian Christianity. The work is structured in four parts and addresses: (i) Trinitarian and Christological doctrine; (ii) Mariological theology and the calendar of Marian feasts; (iii) the Saturday-Sabbath / Sunday-Lord's-Day issue, resolved at the 1450 Council of Mitmaq; (iv) the suppression of dissident movements, especially the *Stephanite* (Estifanosite) movement, which refused prostration before icons and rulers and rejected the cult of the Cross.

The work is unusual in late-medieval-Christian literature in being authored *by the reigning emperor himself*. Comparison points are limited: Justinian's theological tracts (6th c.), Charlemagne's *Libri Carolini* (8th c., commissioned rather than authored personally), Henry VIII's *Assertio Septem Sacramentorum* (1521, ghostwritten). Zar'a Ya'qob's authorship — substantial, voluminous, theologically engaged across his reign — is closer to Justinian's than to any Western parallel.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Solomonic Ethiopia at its medieval apex under Zar'a Ya'qob. His name *Zär'a Yaʿǝqob* ("Seed of Jacob") and regnal name *Constantine I* both signal his self-understanding as the Solomonic-Davidic-Constantinian Christian-imperial figure.
- **Religious milieu.** The 1450 Council of Mitmaq was the resolution of the long Saturday-Sabbath controversy between the southern Takla-Haymanot network (Sunday-only) and the northern Ewostathian network (Saturday-and-Sunday). Zar'a Ya'qob, drafting the *Mashafa Berhan*, articulated the institutional settlement: dual Sabbath observance, with Saturday acknowledged as Sabbath of the Old Covenant and Sunday as Lord's Day of the New, both binding on the Ethiopian church. The work also institutionalizes the 33 Marian feasts of the Ethiopian liturgical calendar and the *Te'amre Maryam* (Marian miracle compendium) tradition.
- **Who was behind it.** Zar'a Ya'qob himself, with court-theological assistance. The doctrinal substrate is recognizably [[giyorgis-of-sagla]]'s framework (Mashafa Mistir, ~1390s–1420s); Giyorgis died 1425, before Zar'a Ya'qob's reign — so the influence is unidirectional and acknowledged.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Ethiopian monastic transmission; Conti Rossini-Ricci 1964–1965 critical edition.

## Themes and motifs
- [[tewahedo-christology]] — Cyrillian Miaphysite Christology in full systematic exposition.
- [[ethiopian-marian-devotion]] — codification of the 33 Marian feasts; integration of the *Te'amre Maryam* miracle-tradition into the liturgical year.
- [[sabbath-observance]] — articulation of the dual-Sabbath (Saturday + Sunday) settlement.
- [[anti-stephanite-polemic]] — refutation of the Stephanite movement and theological-political grounds for its suppression.

## Disputes
- **Authorship.** Confidently attributed to Zar'a Ya'qob; whether court theologians provided substantial drafting assistance is unrecoverable but probable. The voice and theological character are consistent across his attributed corpus.
- **Severity of anti-Stephanite content.** The text's institutional violence against dissent is real; modern scholarship treats it with care (Taddesse Tamrat 1972).
- **Relationship to other Zar'a Ya'qob works.** The *Mashafa Berhan* is the most ambitious; *Mashafa Milad* and *Mashafa Bahriy* are theologically more focused; *Tomarä Tǝsbǝʾt* is more pastoral. The bibliographic integration of his corpus is debated.
