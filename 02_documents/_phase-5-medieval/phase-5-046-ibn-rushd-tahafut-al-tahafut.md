# Tahāfut al-Tahāfut (The Incoherence of the Incoherence)

## What it is
**Averroes's point-by-point reply to al-Ghazālī's [[phase-5-045-al-ghazali-tahafut-al-falasifa|*Tahāfut al-Falāsifa*]]** — composed c. 1180 in al-Andalus / Maghrib, eighty-five years after Ghazālī's original polemic. Averroes structures his text by **quoting Ghazālī's *Tahāfut* in full, paragraph by paragraph**, then replying — so the work physically contains the entire Ghazālī text within it as quoted material. The 20 discussions of Ghazālī's *Tahāfut* are answered in order, with Averroes defending the falsafa tradition (and especially [[ibn-sina|Avicenna]]) against Ghazālī's anathemas while sometimes **partially conceding** Ghazālī-points where Avicennian emanationism departs from strict Aristotelian doctrine (e.g., Averroes is somewhat critical of the Avicennian emanational cosmology in favor of stricter Aristotelian first-mover metaphysics). The book's foundational dialectical move is that **Ghazālī's *Tahāfut* itself uses the philosophical tools it claims to be refuting** — Ghazālī cannot demonstrate the *incoherence* of falsafa without himself reasoning philosophically; therefore Ghazālī's polemic is self-undermining (*tahāfut* of *tahāfut*, "the incoherence of the incoherence"). The companion text — Averroes's ***Faṣl al-Maqāl*** (*Decisive Treatise on the Harmony of Religion and Philosophy*) — provides the **principled juridical-philosophical framework** for the *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut*: as a Mālikī *qāḍī*, Averroes argues that demonstrative philosophical inquiry is a religious obligation (Quranic *iʿtibār*), and that revealed religion and demonstrative philosophy cannot truly contradict because both are different routes to one truth.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Almohad-controlled al-Andalus and Maghrib. Averroes is at the height of his court influence under Caliph Abū Yaʿqūb Yūsuf and his successor Abū Yūsuf Yaʿqūb al-Manṣūr — serving as **qāḍī of Córdoba** and court physician. The *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* is composed in this favored-court period; ~15 years later Averroes will fall from favor and his books be briefly burned (c. 1195).
- **Religious milieu.** The Almohads were originally a North African Berber Islamic-reformist movement founded by Ibn Tūmart (d. 1130), with strong rationalist-theological commitments compatible with engagement with falsafa under the Almohad caliphs (especially the philosopher-caliph Abū Yaʿqūb Yūsuf). The *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* fits this institutional moment: Sunni Mālikī jurisprudence + state-level patronage of Aristotelian-Averroist falsafa.
- **Who was behind it.** Averroes himself, with strong implicit support of the Almohad caliphal court. The exposition is rigorously philosophical-juridical; the work is dedicated implicitly to the philosopher-caliph and the Almohad legal-philosophical reformist program.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Arabic and Hebrew manuscript transmission. **Latin translation by Calo Calonymos c. 1527** as ***Destructio Destructionis Philosophorum*** — published Venice 1527 paired with Ghazālī's *Destructio Philosophorum* in the great Giunta Averroes-omnia Latin edition. This is the **principal Renaissance-European format in which the Tahāfut controversy was encountered**: the Giunta Latin paragraph-by-paragraph Ghazālī-and-Averroes parallel was the standard reference work for 16th-17th-c. European Aristotelians engaging the falsafa-Christian controversy.

## Themes and motifs
- [[tahafut-controversy]] — the closing-bookend of the controversy.
- [[falsafa]] — Averroes's *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* is the keystone *defense* of falsafa.
- [[unmoved-mover]] — Averroes's Aristotelian first-mover metaphysics, partially in tension with the Avicennian Necessary-Being / emanational cosmology Ghazālī attacked.
- [[occasionalism]] — Averroes systematically refutes the Ghazālian occasionalism of the 17th discussion, defending natural causation against the habit-of-conjunction reading.
- [[resurrection-of-the-dead]] — Averroes carefully distinguishes the philosophical (rational-soul-survives-body) and theological (bodily-resurrection-as-revealed-doctrine) registers, arguing both are consistent within the *Faṣl al-Maqāl* harmony-framework.

## Disputes
- **Strict vs. mitigated rejection of occasionalism.** Averroes's 17th-discussion reply to Ghazālian occasionalism is robust — he defends necessary natural causation against habit-of-conjunction. But Averroes's overall theological framework allows for divine omnipotence acting through natural causation as the ordinary providential mode; the precise theological position is more nuanced than a flat rejection of occasionalism would suggest.
- **Position on Avicennian emanationism.** How critical is Averroes of Avicennian Neoplatonic emanationism? Modern scholarship divides: some (Davidson, Wolfson) read Averroes as fundamentally rejecting Avicennian emanationism in favor of strict Aristotelian first-mover metaphysics; others (Hourani, Leaman) read the *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* as defending the broader falsafa tradition including Avicennian emanationism with limited internal-criticism. The *Faṣl al-Maqāl* framework (philosophy and religion harmonize) is shared by both readings.
- **Effect on subsequent Sunni falsafa.** The traditional narrative: after Averroes, falsafa shrinks institutionally in Sunni learning (especially in the Maghrib / Andalus where the Almohad regime falls and Mālikī jurists return to a more anti-philosophy posture). The revisionist contemporary view (Griffel 2009 + Eichner 2020): falsafa continues in different institutional forms in the Sunni east, and is substantially absorbed into post-Razian kalām. Both readings agree that **Averroes is the last great Sunni Andalusi-Maghribi philosopher** in the classical falsafa form; the *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* is the closing-bookend of that tradition's classical period.
- **Latin reception. **The 1527 Calonymos *Destructio Destructionis* edition encountered Averroes already through the lens of Latin Averroism and the 1277 Paris condemnation; 16th-17th-c. European readers were primed to read the *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* polemically.
