# Chinon Parchment

## What it is
A single Latin parchment, dated 17–20 August 1308, recording the formal examination of [[jacques-de-molay|Jacques de Molay]] and four other senior officers of the [[tradition-knights-templar|Knights Templar]] by three cardinal-delegates of Pope [[pope-clement-v|Clement V]] at Chinon castle (Touraine, France). The document records the cardinals' **conditional absolution** of the Templar leadership from the charge of heresy: the examinees affirmed essential Catholic faith, retracted their earlier coerced confessions in their substantive heresy-content, and were absolved and received back into the Church and the sacraments.

The document is **load-bearing for modern reassessment of the [[event-trial-of-templars-1307-1314|Templar trial]]**. The older historical reading — that Clement V simply abandoned the Templars under French royal pressure — is substantially complicated by the Chinon Parchment, which shows Clement (through his cardinal-delegates) privately absolving the senior officers in 1308, four years before the Order's institutional suppression at the [[event-council-of-vienne-1311-1312|Council of Vienne]].

## Context
- **Political milieu.** August 1308: Clement V was at Poitiers, [[philip-iv-of-france|Philip IV]] was pressing publicly for the Templar suppression, the Order's senior officers had been imprisoned since the October 1307 mass-arrests. Clement dispatched three trusted cardinals (Frédol, de Suisy, Brancaccio) to Chinon to examine the senior officers — a move that Philip resented but could not block.
- **Religious milieu.** The papal-vs-royal jurisdictional contest over the Templar trial was at its peak. Clement's strategy: take procedural ownership of the trial to prevent Philip from unilaterally adjudicating it as a heresy case. The Chinon examination was part of this strategy.
- **Who was behind it.** The papal-curia chancery, the three cardinal-delegates, and Clement V personally.
- **Transmission.** The Chinon Parchment was filed in the Vatican curial archive; subsequently misclassified (catalogued among unrelated Avignon-period documents); essentially lost to scholarship for nearly seven centuries. In **September 2001**, Italian paleographer Barbara Frale identified the document in the Vatican Secret Archive while working on an unrelated project. Frale's 2004 *Journal of Medieval History* article published the discovery; her 2007 *Processus Contra Templarios* gave the full critical edition + facsimile.

## Disputes
- **What does the conditional absolution mean for the historical verdict on the Templars?** Frale 2004 and Barber 2006 ch. 4 agree that the Chinon Parchment substantially supports the position that **the Templars were not heretics in the technical canon-law sense**. The 1312 suppression was *per modum provisionis* (administrative provision) rather than *per modum condemnationis* (heretical condemnation). The traditional historical-popular framing of the Templars as "heretics burned by the Inquisition" is inaccurate at the canonical level; the heresy verdict was never formally entered.
- **Does the document settle the *Baphomet* question?** No — but its silence is suggestive. The cardinal-delegates' examination focused on the heresy charge generally (denial of Christ, idolatry, sodomy, illicit absolution) and the senior officers' explanations / retractions; the [[baphomet-controversy|Baphomet]] charge is treated as part of the general idolatry charge rather than as a separate matter requiring distinct doctrinal investigation. The cardinals found nothing requiring further heresy proceedings against the senior leadership.
- **Why was the document misfiled for seven centuries?** Probably accidental filing-error during a Vatican-archive reorganization (16th–17th c.), then catalogued under a misleading shelfmark. The Avignon registers were physically moved between archives multiple times during the early-modern period.
