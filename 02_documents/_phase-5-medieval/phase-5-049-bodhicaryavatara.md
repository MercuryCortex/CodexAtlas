# Bodhicaryāvatāra

## What it is
The Bodhicaryāvatāra ("Entering the Conduct of the Bodhisattva," conventionally rendered "Way of the Bodhisattva") is an 8th-c. Sanskrit verse treatise by [[santideva|Śāntideva]] of Nālandā — 10 chapters, 913 verses — that articulates the systematic ethical-meditative-philosophical practice of the Mahāyāna [[bodhisattva-vow|bodhisattva-path]]. It is the most-cited single Mahāyāna ethical work in any tradition, the canonical curriculum text in all four Tibetan schools, and the operational source of the modern *tonglen* (giving-and-taking) practice. The text is structured as the bodhisattva's progressive cultivation of the six (or in this case seven) *pāramitās* (perfections): generosity, ethics, patience, effort, meditation, **wisdom (the celebrated chapter 9 — a Madhyamaka-Prāsaṅgika philosophical-poetic dialectic)**, and dedication.

## Structure (10 chapters)

1. *Bodhicittānuśaṃsā* — The praise of *bodhicitta* (the mind of awakening).
2. *Pāpa-deśanā* — Confession of negative actions.
3. *Bodhicitta-parigraha* — Adopting the bodhicitta.
4. *Bodhicitta-apramāda* — Vigilant maintenance of bodhicitta.
5. *Samprajanya-rakṣaṇa* — Guarding awareness.
6. *Kṣānti-pāramitā* — Perfection of patience.
7. *Vīrya-pāramitā* — Perfection of effort.
8. *Dhyāna-pāramitā* — Perfection of meditation (contains the famous "exchange of self and others" verses, Bca 8.120-184).
9. *Prajñā-pāramitā* — Perfection of wisdom (Madhyamaka-Prāsaṅgika dialectic against svabhāva-realism).
10. *Pariṇāmanā* — Dedication.

## Context
- **Political milieu**: Pāla-period India (~700-1200); Nālandā at its peak as the principal Mahāyāna-Vajrayāna university; Indian Buddhism's last great institutional flourishing before the [[event-destruction-of-nalanda-1193|Nālandā destruction 1193]].
- **Religious milieu**: mature Mahāyāna integrating Madhyamaka philosophy (Nāgārjuna's MMK lineage), Yogācāra epistemology (Asaṅga/Vasubandhu), and tantric-Vajrayāna ritual. Śāntideva is firmly in the Madhyamaka-Prāsaṅgika philosophical stream and his chapter 9 is a verse-form of Candrakīrti's dialectic.
- **Who was behind it**: [[santideva|Śāntideva]] himself.
- **Discovery / transmission**: continuous transmission via Sanskrit (Nepal + Tibet) + Tibetan (8th-c. translation, revised by Rin-chen bZang-po and others) + Chinese (Tian Xizai 985 CE, less influential).

## Primary text — selected verses

### Bca 1.7-10 — the foundational bodhicitta praise

> 7. Just as a flash of lightning on a dark, cloudy night for an instant brightly illuminates all, so rarely, through the might of the Buddha, do people's minds turn, for an instant, to virtuous deeds.
>
> 8. ... So intermittent are the wholesome aspirations of the world. How few are those minds that take refuge in the Buddha-Dharma; how brief are they; from time to time...
>
> 9. The bodhicitta is to be known briefly to be of two kinds: bodhicitta as aspiration and bodhicitta as engagement.

(trans. Padmakara 1997, condensed)

### Bca 8.129 — the "exchange of self and others" foundation of tonglen

> Whoever wishes to quickly rescue both himself and others should practice the supreme mystery: the exchange of self and others.

### Bca 9.34 — the emptiness of phenomena

> When one analyzes a vase, dividing it into its parts, no real vase remains — only a designation. So no real self exists.

(trans. Crosby-Skilton 1995, abridged)

## Themes and motifs
- [[bodhisattva-vow]] — the systematic operational articulation; the text IS the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* of bodhisattva-conduct.
- [[emptiness-sunyata]] — chapter 9's Madhyamaka-Prāsaṅgika dialectic.
- [[anatman-no-self]] — the wisdom-chapter's no-self analysis extends from persons to all phenomena.
- **Bodhicitta** — the mind-of-awakening; aspiration + engagement.
- **Six pāramitās** — the standard Mahāyāna perfections (generosity / ethics / patience / effort / meditation / wisdom).

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[santideva]] — *author*.
- → [[bodhisattva-vow]] — *core articulation*: the Bodhicaryāvatāra is the canonical systematic-ethical articulation of the bodhisattva-vow.
- → [[phase-4-075-mulamadhyamakakarika]] (Nāgārjuna MMK) — *commentary-on*: chapter 9 is a verse-form Madhyamaka-Prāsaṅgika dialectic in Nāgārjuna's lineage.
- → [[phase-3-031-asthasahasrika-prajnaparamita]] — *commentary-on*: the Prajñāpāramitā corpus is the doctrinal substrate.
- → *Śikṣāsamuccaya* (Śāntideva's own anthology) — *parallel-form*: companion work; the Śikṣāsamuccaya provides the sutra-citations behind the Bodhicaryāvatāra's argument.
- → Modern *tonglen* meditation practice — *direct-influence*: Bca 8.120-184 is the operational source.

## Disputes
- **Date**: ~700-750 is the Tibetan-tradition window; some scholars place ~650-700.
- **Madhyamaka sub-school**: Prāsaṅgika (modern Tibetan-Gelug + Tibetan Mipham-school reading) vs. some Svātantrika-tinged interpretations of chapter 9. The text is not entirely unambiguous; the Padmakara translation follows the Mipham reading.
- **Authorship of the appended *vijñāna-vāda* (consciousness-only) refutation in chapter 9**: securely Śāntideva; not an interpolation per Williams 1998.
