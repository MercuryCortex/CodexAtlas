# Sefer Yetzirah (Book of Formation)

## What it is
A short Hebrew cosmogonic-mystical treatise — the foundational proto-Kabbalistic text. It presents creation as accomplished by God through "thirty-two paths of wisdom": the ten *sefirot belimah* (the *ten sefirot of-nothingness*) and the twenty-two letters of the Hebrew alphabet. The text divides these 22 letters into three "mothers" (אמש), seven "doubles" (בגדכפרת), and twelve "simples" (the remaining), each set correlated to cosmological, temporal, and bodily orders. The *sefirot* here are not the ten emanations of later Kabbalah but ten primordial *numbers* / dimensions (up, down, east, west, north, south, beginning, end, good, evil — or alternative schemes); the term *sefirah* derives from *sappīr*/sapphire and from *spr* (counting/recounting). The combinatorial mechanics by which the letters and *sefirot* generate the cosmos is the text's central content — God "engraved, hewed, weighed, exchanged, and combined" the letters to produce existence.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Wholly uncertain — the text bears no datable political references. Its later reception is in Geonic Iraq (~900) and then in 11th–12th-c. Spain and Provence.
- **Religious milieu.** Equally uncertain — the text sits typologically between the Hekhalot-Merkavah mystical literature (which it does *not* resemble in form: it has no throne-vision narrative) and the later medieval Kabbalah (which it does *not* resemble in content: no Adam Kadmon, no partzufim, no explicit emanationism). What the text *does* resemble is a Pythagorean-Neoplatonist cosmogonic mathematics with Hebrew-language combinatorics replacing Greek arithmology.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous. Pseudepigraphically attributed to the patriarch Abraham in the closing verse; medieval traditions attributed it variously to Rabbi Akiva and other tannaim. Genuine authorship and provenance remain disputed.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Earliest definite witness is Saʿadia Gaon's Judeo-Arabic commentary (~931). Earlier Geonic references (Dunash ibn Tamim, Shabbetai Donnolo) confirm circulation in the 9th–10th c. Manuscripts proliferate from the 10th c. onward. The text became central to two distinct medieval traditions: (1) the proto-Kabbalists of Provence and Spain who took its *sefirot* terminology and developed it into the ten-emanation Kabbalah; (2) the *Sefer Yetzirah*-as-practical-magic tradition (used in medieval Ashkenaz for creating a *golem*, as in the legends of R. Elijah Ba'al Shem of Chełm and the Maharal of Prague).

## Themes and motifs
- [[creation-by-word]] — radicalized: not merely "God said, let there be," but God *combinatorially manipulates* Hebrew letters to produce existence; the alphabet itself is the medium of creation.
- [[hidden-god]] — the *sefirot belimah* of-nothingness; the apophatic dimension of the primordial principles.
- [[divine-feminine]] — implicit in later reception via the *Shekhinah*-associated lower *sefirah* of the developed Kabbalah; not foregrounded in *Sefer Yetzirah* itself.
- [[soul-as-spark]] — the *sefirot* and letters constitute the human microcosm as well as the macrocosm; the human is structurally a re-folded creation.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Hekhalot-Merkavah literature — *parallel-motif* / *shared-milieu*: both are pre-medieval Jewish mystical texts, but the relationship is structural (esoteric mysticism, Hebrew-language operative power) rather than literary.
- → [[phase-5-026-sefer-ha-bahir]] — *direct-influence*: *Bahir* is the first text to take *Sefer Yetzirah*'s ten *sefirot* and reinterpret them as divine emanations / hypostases, the move that constitutes Kabbalah proper.
- → [[phase-5-027-sefer-ha-zohar]] — *direct-influence*: *Zohar*'s elaborated sefirotic tree is the developed form of the seed in *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- → Pythagoreanism / Neoplatonism — *parallel-motif* / *direct-influence?*: the structural similarity to Pythagorean numerology and Plotinian / Procline hypostatic schemes is striking; whether this is influence (via Greek-language texts circulating in Late-Antique Palestine) or independent invention is disputed.

## Disputes
- **Date.** This is the single most controversial dating in Jewish-mystical text criticism. Estimates range from 2nd c. CE (Scholem's "early stratum") to 9th c. CE (Wasserstrom; some of Hayman). Hayman 2004 favors composition in the 8th–9th c., possibly in an Islamicate cultural environment, with the work then "discovered" as a pseudepigraph; Scholem favored ~200–400 CE; Liebes 2000 argues an even earlier (1st–2nd c.) Jewish-Pythagorean origin. *We carry the range 200–900*.
- **Provenance.** Palestine vs. Babylonia vs. Mesopotamia generally; some have argued a Mediterranean (Italian or Aegean) origin via Greek-Pythagorean substrate.
- **Connection to Kabbalah.** Scholem famously read *Sefer Yetzirah*'s ten *sefirot* as the seed of all later Kabbalah; Idel argues a more discontinuous relationship — the *Bahir*'s reinterpretation is the genuine creative leap, and *Sefer Yetzirah*'s *sefirot* are not yet emanations.
