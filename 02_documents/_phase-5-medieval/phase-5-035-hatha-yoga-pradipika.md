# Haṭhayoga-Pradīpikā

## What it is
The canonical late-medieval Sanskrit treatise on *Haṭha Yoga* — the body-discipline tradition of postural, respiratory, energetic, and meditative practices that emerged in the medieval Nath sampradaya and that constitutes the historical-textual root of modern global postural yoga. 389 Sanskrit verses in four chapters: (1) *āsanas* (postures: ~15 in the HYP itself, expanded substantially in later Hatha texts), (2) *prāṇāyāma* and the six *ṣaṭkarmas* (cleansings: dhauti, basti, neti, trāṭaka, nauli, kapālabhāti), (3) *mudrās* and *bandhas* (energetic seals and locks: mahā-mudrā, mahā-bandha, viparīta-karaṇī, vajrolī, śakti-cālanā, etc.), and (4) *samādhi* (meditative absorption, the goal). The text positions itself as a complement and prerequisite to *Rāja Yoga* — the higher mental-meditative discipline (associated with Patañjali) — with Haṭha as the bodily-energetic preparation: *na rāja-yogaḥ vinā haṭhaḥ* / "no Raja Yoga without Hatha." The work cites Matsyendranath as Adi-Nath (foundational teacher) and lists a parampara of yogic siddhas including Gorakhnath; the textual lineage is firmly Nath-sampradaya.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-medieval pre-Mughal India (Vijayanagara empire in the south; Delhi Sultanate / Bahmani in the north; the Lodi dynasty in late-Delhi-Sultanate north India). The Nath sampradaya was a major medieval renouncer tradition with wide geographical reach across pre-modern India.
- **Religious milieu.** A field of converging late-medieval religious-yogic developments: Nath sampradaya yogis as a major institutional renouncer tradition, the Sant-Bhakti movement (Kabir, Ravidas, Dadu) emerging alongside, Sufi Chishti and Suhrawardi institutional networks, the bhakti devotional movements (Vaishnava, Shaiva) intensifying. The Nath tradition's *kānphaṭa* yogis interacted with all these.
- **Who was behind it.** Svātmārāma, a Nath-sampradaya yogi of the 15th c., compiled and systematized older Nath-sampradaya material from a tradition stretching back to Gorakhnath (~11th–12th c.) and Matsyendranath (~10th c.). The compositional layer is fairly tight ~1400–1500; the materials are older.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Nath-sampradaya and broader Sanskrit-yogic transmission. The text became globally famous in the 19th–20th c. through the modern yoga revival (Theos Bernard, Krishnamacharya, Iyengar, Pattabhi Jois) and is one of the principal medieval Sanskrit texts cited in modern global yoga.

## Themes and motifs
- [[ascent-of-the-soul]] — the *kuṇḍalinī* ascent through the *suṣumnā-nāḍī* (the central energetic channel) from the *mūlādhāra-cakra* at the base of the spine to the *sahasrāra-cakra* at the crown — the *Haṭha Yoga* signature contribution to Indian soteriological cosmology.
- [[apatheia-and-virtue]] — the *yamas* and *niyamas* prerequisite to practice; the discipline of bodily and energetic restraint.
- [[mystery-initiation]] — the practices are explicitly transmitted *guru-mukhāt* (from the teacher's mouth); textual reading alone, the HYP warns, is insufficient.
- [[microcosm-macrocosm]] — the subtle-body system: the human body as microcosm of the universe, with seven *cakras*, three principal *nāḍīs* (iḍā / piṅgalā / suṣumnā), and the *kuṇḍalinī* śakti. The microcosm-macrocosm parallel is one of the foundational moves of Hatha Yoga.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-023-yoga-sutras-of-patanjali]] — *commentary-on* / *complement-to*: the HYP positions itself as the bodily-energetic preparation for the meditative *aṣṭāṅga-yoga* of Patañjali; the relation is complement-and-prerequisite, not contradiction.
- → [[phase-5-006-vijnana-bhairava-tantra]] — *parallel-motif* / *shared-milieu*: the HYP draws on the Tantric subtle-body cosmology developed in Kashmir and Bengal Tantric literature; the Kaula Tantric and Shaiva sources of Hatha Yoga are extensive.
- → Matsyendranath / Gorakhnath lineage — *direct-influence*: the HYP names both as foundational figures of the Nath lineage and the textual tradition.
- → Modern global postural yoga (Krishnamacharya, Iyengar, Pattabhi Jois, Bikram Choudhury, the global gym-yoga industry) — *direct-influence*: Singleton 2010 has shown that modern postural yoga is principally a 19th–20th-c. transformation of the medieval Hatha Yoga tradition under influences including 19th-c. European gymnastics, the Indian YMCA physical-culture movement, and the Mysore Palace yoga reconstruction. The HYP and the *Gheraṇḍa-Saṃhitā* are the principal pre-modern textual anchors; modern postural yoga reads them selectively.
- → Western occult subtle-body literature — *direct-influence*: [[helena-blavatsky]]'s Theosophical Society made the Indian subtle-body cosmology (chakras, *kuṇḍalinī*, the seven planes) one of the principal contributions of Theosophy to Western esotericism. Arthur Avalon (Sir John Woodroffe)'s *The Serpent Power* (1919) made the Hindu Tantric subtle-body system available to the Anglophone world in scholarly form. Modern Western "chakra" culture, with its 7-chakra rainbow color-system (a 20th-c. Western innovation, not classically Hindu), is downstream.
- → Tibetan Vajrayāna Buddhist *tsa lung* (channel-and-wind) tradition — *parallel-motif* / *shared-milieu*: structurally similar subtle-body cosmology with three central channels and the energetic-ascent soteriology. Sanderson 2009 and Samuel 2008 argue for substantial cross-pollination between Hindu Nath Tantric and Tibetan Buddhist Tantric subtle-body literatures in medieval Tibet-Nepal-Bengal contact zones.
- → Sufi *laṭāʾif al-sittah* (the six subtle centers) — *parallel-motif*: medieval and early-modern Sufi (especially Naqshbandi and the Indian *muraqaba* tradition) developed a six-center subtle-body cosmology with structural-functional parallels to the Hatha Yoga *cakra* system. The historical-causal question (direct borrowing in the Indo-Persian Sufi-Yogic contact zone of medieval North India? independent development with structural convergence?) is contested; Carl Ernst's work on Sufi-Yogic interaction in the *Amṛtakuṇḍa* corpus is one of the principal documentary studies of explicit Sufi-Hatha Yogic cross-pollination.

## Disputes
- **Date**: 1400–1500 is the standard placement; some scholars argue for slightly earlier or slightly later.
- **Svātmārāma**: known principally through this text; biographical details are sparse.
- **The HYP's relation to modern yoga**: substantially documented (Singleton 2010); modern postural yoga is a substantial transformation of the medieval Hatha Yoga corpus, not a direct continuation.
- **Sufi-Yogic *Amṛtakuṇḍa* tradition**: the Persian and Arabic translations of a Sanskrit Hatha Yoga / Kaula text (the *Amṛtakuṇḍa*) in the medieval Indian Sufi corpus is a documented case of explicit Sufi reception of Hindu Tantric-Yogic material (Ernst 2003, 2016).
