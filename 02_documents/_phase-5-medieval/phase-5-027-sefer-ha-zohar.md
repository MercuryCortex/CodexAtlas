# Sefer ha-Zohar (The Book of Splendor)

## What it is
The classical text of medieval Kabbalah and the third — with the Tanakh and the Talmud — most-influential sacred text in post-Temple Jewish tradition. Presented as a mystical midrash on the Torah by the 2nd-c. tanna R. Shimon bar Yochai (Rashbi) and his circle of disciples wandering through the Galilean countryside discussing the inner meaning of Scripture, the *Zohar* is actually a Castilian Jewish work of the 1280s. Its content systematically develops a fully articulated theosophic Kabbalah: the *Ein Sof* (the Infinite, beyond all attributes) emanates the ten *sefirot* — *Keter* (Crown), *Chokhmah* (Wisdom), *Binah* (Understanding) at the top; *Chesed* (Lovingkindness), *Gevurah* (Severity), *Tiferet* (Beauty) in the middle; *Netzach* (Eternity), *Hod* (Glory), *Yesod* (Foundation) below; and *Malkhut* (Kingdom) / *Shekhinah* (Divine Presence) at the bottom. The sefirot are configured as a tree with three columns (right of mercy, left of judgment, middle of compromise); they interact dynamically; their balance and imbalance is the inner content of Torah, of history, and of cosmic events. The lower sefirah, Shekhinah, is feminine and is in tragic separation from her divine masculine partner Tiferet; the human's performance of the commandments (*mitzvot*) effects *tikkun* — the repair of the cosmos — by reuniting Shekhinah with Tiferet. The text introduces an unprecedented intimacy of language — including frank sexual symbolism — for describing the inner life of God. The work is, alongside the Tanakh and Talmud, the third pillar of post-medieval Jewish religious imagination.

## Primary text — selected excerpts

The *Zohar* has no fully reproducible English translation in the public domain. The Sperling-Simon Soncino translation (5 vols., 1931–1934) — the standard English translation for most of the 20th c. — is not yet in the public domain in all jurisdictions and is widely regarded as paraphrastic and often misleading. The reference scholarly edition is now Daniel C. Matt's *The Zohar: Pritzker Edition* (12 vols., Stanford UP, 2003–2017). The excerpts below are short fair-use renderings paraphrased with reference to the Sperling-Simon English and to Matt's Pritzker text; for any serious interpretive work, consult Matt directly.

### Zohar I.1a–2a — the Rose (opening of the *Sitrei Torah*)

The Zohar opens not with creation but with a parable of the Rose — Israel as a rose among thorns, the thirteen petals of mercy. The image is unsettlingly intimate from the first line: this is a midrash on Song of Songs 2:2 made into a key to the inner structure of the Godhead.

> Rabbi Hizkiah opened, "Like a rose among thorns, so is my beloved among the maidens" (Song of Songs 2:2). Who is "a rose"? This is the Assembly of Israel. For there is a rose, and there is a rose. Just as a rose among thorns is colored red and white, so is the Assembly of Israel comprised of judgment and mercy. Just as a rose has thirteen petals, so the Assembly of Israel has thirteen attributes of mercy which surround her on every side...
>
> At the hour she is about to give scent, she conjoins above, then conjoins below: "Let God say, 'Let there be light' — and there was light." This is the light that was hidden and stored away. This is the light that comprises all the colors. This is the light because of which the world was created and stands.

Citation: Zohar I.1a (opening of the commentary on Genesis), paraphrased with reference to Sperling-Simon 1931 and Matt 2003, Pritzker vol. 1, pp. 1–7.

### Zohar I.15a — the cosmogony of the Hidden Point

The Zohar's account of creation: the Ein Sof, the Infinite, withdraws into itself and a hidden point (the sefirah Keter, or in some passages Chokhmah) emerges. The image of the first dot becomes the formal beginning of *all* differentiated reality.

> In the beginning. At the head of potency of the King, He engraved engravings in luster on high. A spark of impenetrable darkness flashed within the concealed of the concealed, from the head of Infinity — a cluster of vapor forming in formlessness, thrust in a ring, not white, not black, not red, not green, no color at all. As a cord surveyed, it yielded radiant colors. Deep within the spark gushed a flow, splaying colors below, concealed within the concealed of the mystery of Ein Sof.
>
> It split and did not split its aura, was not known at all, until under the impact of splitting, a single, concealed, supernal point shone. Beyond that point, nothing is known. So it is called *Reshit* (beginning) — first command of all.

Citation: Zohar I.15a, paraphrased with reference to Matt 2003, Pritzker vol. 1, pp. 107–110 — Matt's rendering is the standard for this notoriously dense passage.

### Zohar III.288a (Idra Zuta) — the Holy Ancient One

The *Idra Zuta* ("Lesser Assembly") describes the death of Rabbi Shimon and contains the densest theosophical sections of the Zohar. The "Ancient of Days" / "Holy Ancient One" / *Arikh Anpin* is the long-faced sefirotic configuration of Keter — the merciful aspect of God beyond the structures of judgment.

> The Ancient of all the Ancients, the Concealed of all the Concealed, has a form, and yet has no form. It has a form, by which the universe is preserved; it has no form, because it is not contained in any thing... When it began to be shaped, it produced nine lights that shine from It, from Its form. And those lights shine from It, and emanate flames, and they spread forth on every side, as the light of the lamp spreads its rays on every side. And those rays which spread, when one approaches to look at them, are not found — only the lamp itself...
>
> All those lights are linked together in this Holy Ancient One, the Concealed of all the Concealed; they are linked one in another, and they shine into all.

Citation: Zohar III.288a (Idra Zuta), paraphrased with reference to Sperling-Simon Soncino and Matt 2017, Pritzker vol. 9.

### Tikkunei Zohar, opening (Tikkun 1) — the mystery of *Bereshit*

The Tikkunei Zohar (a closely associated stratum, possibly post-Moses-de-León) opens with seventy interpretations of the first word of Genesis, *Bereshit* ("In the beginning"). The text is a sustained celebration of multiple-valenced reading: the same word opens onto Chokhmah, onto the Torah, onto the King, onto Wisdom-the-Mother.

> In the Beginning. Rabbi Shimon opened and said: It is a time to act for the Lord; they have annulled Thy Torah (Psalm 119:126). It is a time to act for the Lord — what is this "time"? Rabbi Shimon wept and said: Woe if I reveal! Woe if I do not reveal!
>
> *Bereshit* — *Be-Reshit* — "with the Beginning." Reshit is Chokhmah (Wisdom). And with Wisdom, He created. The "Beginning" is the supernal Chokhmah; with it, all was made.

Citation: Tikkunei Zohar, Tikkun 1 (opening), paraphrased with reference to the standard printed editions; cf. Matt 2003 Pritzker introduction, vol. 1.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late 13th-c. Castile under Alfonso X the Wise (r. 1252–1284) and his successor Sancho IV. The relative tolerance and intellectual flourishing of Castilian Jewish life was already showing strain: the Disputation of Barcelona (1263) had been a coercive theological confrontation; anti-Jewish sentiment was rising. The 1391 Spanish-Jewish pogroms and the 1492 Expulsion would dramatically transform Sephardic life — the *Zohar*'s eschatological intensity would be read with new urgency after these traumas.
- **Religious milieu.** Spanish Kabbalah was the inheritor of the Provençal Kabbalah of Isaac the Blind and the Geronese Kabbalists (Naḥmanides, Azriel of Gerona, the brothers Isaac and Jacob ha-Cohen of Castile). The *Zohar* synthesizes the Geronese theosophy, the Cohen brothers' demonology and mystical letter-speculation, the Iyyun circle's apophatic emanationism, and the *Bahir*'s mythic-feminine symbolism into a single literary monument. The contemporary Abraham Abulafia's "prophetic Kabbalah" — based on letter-permutation meditation rather than sefirotic theosophy — provides the alternative mystical stream of the same generation.
- **Who was behind it.** Moses de León of Guadalajara (later Avila) is the historically attested distributor of the *Zohar* manuscripts in Castile in the late 1280s and early 1290s. He sold copies to wealthy patrons claiming to have transcribed them from an ancient manuscript of R. Shimon bar Yochai. After his death (1305), his widow reportedly confessed that her husband had composed the work himself. Scholem 1941 demonstrated through philological analysis of the Aramaic that the language is artificial — post-Talmudic neologisms and grammatical patterns betray the medieval composition. Whether de León worked alone or with a circle (the Castilian Kabbalist colleagues David ben Yehudah he-Hasid, Joseph Gikatilla, the Cohen brothers' tradition) is a debated point — Liebes 1993 favors circle authorship; Scholem favored sole authorship.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Distributed by Moses de León in the 1290s; took ~50 years for full acceptance as authoritative; cited by the post-1391 generation of Spanish Kabbalists; printed Mantua 1558 (where rabbinic authorities had publicly debated whether to permit print); became fully canonical Jewish text by ~1600. Lurianic Kabbalah at Safed (~1570) is *the* commentary tradition on the *Zohar*. The work entered modern Christian intellectual culture through the Christian Cabala of Pico della Mirandola (1486 onward) and reaches the modern academic period through Scholem.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-feminine]] — Shekhinah as the lower sefirah, *both* indwelling presence (Talmudic) *and* a hypostatic feminine divine partner-in-tragic-separation (new). The most powerful divine-feminine theology in medieval Judaism.
- [[hidden-god]] — *Ein Sof* (the Infinite) is utterly beyond all attributes; the sefirot are God's relational self-disclosure; the strict distinction permits Kabbalah's bold inner-divine descriptive language without compromising apophatic transcendence.
- [[non-duality]] — but as a graded structure; the sefirot are *not* a polytheism but the inner articulation of the one Godhead; the resolution of the apparent multiplicity is in the *Ein Sof*.
- [[soul-as-spark]] — souls preexist in the sefirotic structure and descend into bodies for tikkun; reincarnation (*gilgul*) develops further from the *Bahir*.
- [[sacred-marriage]] — the union of Tiferet and Shekhinah, of masculine and feminine within the Godhead, is the central inner-divine event the *mitzvot* are designed to effect.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-5-026-sefer-ha-bahir]] — *direct-influence*: the *Zohar* is the systematic, monumental, literary development of the *Bahir*'s proto-Kabbalistic seed.
- → [[phase-5-012-sefer-yetzirah]] — *direct-influence*: the *Zohar*'s ten sefirot derive from *Sefer Yetzirah*'s primordial-number list through the mediating reinterpretation of the *Bahir*.
- → [[phase-5-013-hekhalot-literature]] — *direct-influence*: angelic hierarchies, esoteric registers, palaces of the heavens — all inherited.
- → [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]] / Sethian Gnostic emanationism — *parallel-motif*: structural parallels (emanative pleroma; divine feminine in tragic separation; the human's role in cosmic restoration) have been at the center of the Scholem-Idel debate on Kabbalah origins. The *Zohar*'s Shekhinah-Tiferet drama is the strongest single point of structural parallel to Sethian Sophia-Christ syzygies. *Whether* this is historical transmission across centuries or independent emergence from rabbinic-aggadic ingredients remains the foundational dispute.
- → Lurianic Kabbalah (Phase 6) — *direct-influence*: Isaac Luria's entire system (the *tzimtzum*, the breaking of the vessels, *tikkun olam*) is read as a re-systematization of the *Zohar*'s themes.

## Disputes
- **Authorship.** Scholem 1941 established Moses de León as principal author through philological analysis of the artificial Aramaic. Liebes 1993 argues for *circle* authorship — Moses de León + collaborators in the Castilian Kabbalist milieu. A small minority (Yehuda Liebes in some moods; Mordechai Tisby) entertains the possibility that some material strata are older. Modern consensus: Moses de León is the principal author, with possible collaborators and some inherited materials.
- **Antiquity claim.** The *Zohar* itself attributes composition to R. Shimon bar Yochai (2nd c.). This attribution is now universally rejected by academic scholarship and was rejected by some medieval critics (Elia del Medigo 1496) but defended by mystics into the modern period.
- **Gnostic-influence thesis.** Scholem's claim of structural parallels with Sethian Gnostic Sophia myths (esp. the Shekhinah-Tiferet syzygy and the human role in cosmic restoration) is the largest single comparative-religion question in medieval Jewish mysticism. Idel argues continuity with rabbinic aggadic materials; Wolfson argues complex Christian-influenced symbolism (esp. on the gendered ten-sefirot structure).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Shekhinah-Tiferet ↔ Isis-Osiris ↔ Sophia-Christ — the divine-marriage axis:** the Zohar's central liturgical drama — feminine Shekhinah in tragic exile from masculine Tiferet, reunited by Sabbath observance and correct performance of *mitzvot* — is structurally identical to the Egyptian Isis-Osiris cosmic reunion and to the Sethian Sophia-Christ syzygy in the Apocryphon of John (both sides in the vault). Three traditions, spanning 2,500 years, deploy the same inner-divine sacred-marriage to ground human ritual action as cosmically restorative.
- ***Ein Sof* — the vault's apophatic absolute, western-esoteric axis:** *Ein Sof* ("without end, beyond all predication") is the Zohar's contribution to the cross-tradition cluster that includes Plotinus' One (Enn. VI.9.4), the Daodejing's unnamed Dao (ch. 1), Ibn ʿArabī's *aḥadiyya*, and Eckhart's *Gottheit*. The *Zohar*'s distinctive move is to pair the apophatic *Ein Sof* with the ten *sefirot* as God's relational self-disclosure — resolving the tension between the God-beyond-all-names and the God-who-speaks-in-Torah without collapsing either pole.
- **Pseudepigraphy as legitimation — the vault's canonical case study:** Moses de León attributes composition to R. Shimon bar Yochai (2nd c.) to secure rabbinic authority. The same move: Pseudo-Dionysius claims to be Paul's Athenian convert; the Corpus Hermeticum claims Hermes Trismegistus; the *Bahir* claims ancient tannaitic material. The *Zohar* is the most extensively documented instance of esoteric-text legitimation by false antiquity, and the critical tools that exposed it (Scholem 1941, philological Aramaisms) became the model for modern critical analysis of all pseudepigraphic religious literature.
- **Zohar → Luria → Christian Kabbalah → Freemasonry — the long transmission chain:** Lurianic Kabbalah (Safed, ~1570) systematized the *Zohar* into the *tzimtzum/tikkun* framework; Pico della Mirandola (1486) made Kabbalah a Christian intellectual tool; Reuchlin, Agrippa, and Paracelsus Christianized the sefirot; this stream fed Rosicrucianism (~1614), early Speculative Freemasonry (~1717), and the entire modern Western esoteric tradition documented in the atlas. The *Zohar* is the most consequential single text for understanding how Jewish mystical structures entered secular Western culture.
