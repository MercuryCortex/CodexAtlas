# Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim (al-Musnad al-Ṣaḥīḥ)

## What it is
**The second of the two canonical Sunni hadith collections** — *al-Ṣaḥīḥān* ("the Two Sound Books") with [[phase-5-007-sahih-al-bukhari|al-Bukhārī]] — compiled by **Abū al-Ḥusayn Muslim ibn al-Ḥajjāj al-Naysābūrī (~822–875 CE)** in Nishapur, Khurasan. The full title is *al-Musnad al-Ṣaḥīḥ al-Mukhtaṣar bi-Naql al-ʿAdl ʿan al-ʿAdl ilā Rasūl Allāh* ("The Concise Authentic Musnad Transmitted from Trustworthy to Trustworthy to the Messenger of God"); the work contains **approximately 7,500 hadith (with repetitions; ~3,000-4,000 distinct hadith)**, organized into 54 thematic *kitāb*-books covering belief (*īmān*), purity (*ṭahāra*), prayer (*ṣalāt*), zakat, fasting, hajj, marriage, commerce, jihad, hudūd, kingship, eschatology, ethics, etc.

Muslim's distinctive methodological contribution is the **substantial *Muqaddima* (Introduction)** in which he lays out his *isnād*-criticism principles in systematic form — the foundational document of the 9th-c. Sunni hadith methodology. Muslim's principal innovations over Bukhārī: (1) **organization of hadith by thematic-juristic chapter** with all variant chains-of-transmission of the same hadith grouped together (whereas Bukhārī sometimes splits the same hadith across multiple thematic chapters with different *fiqh* deployment); (2) **stricter focus on *muttaṣil* (continuous) *isnād*** chains; (3) **less *fiqh* interpolation** by Muslim's own juristic commentary (whereas Bukhārī's *Ṣaḥīḥ* includes substantial Bukhārī-juristic chapter-titles framing each hadith for *fiqh* deployment). Sunni hadith-tradition conventionally accords Bukhārī higher overall authority but **acknowledges Muslim's superior** *isnād*-methodology** in certain technical respects (the formulaic *taḥdīth* / *akhbara* / *anbaʾa* distinctions; the *mutābaʿāt* / parallel-chain documentation).

Together the two *Ṣaḥīḥān* constitute the **foundation of Sunni hadith methodology and the highest-authority hadith corpus**; they were canonized over the 10th-12th centuries as the canonical paired collection (Brown 2007). The four *Sunan* collections — **Abū Dāwūd al-Sijistānī's *Sunan* (~880), al-Tirmidhī's *Sunan* (~890), al-Nasāʾī's *Sunan al-Kubrā* (~900), and Ibn Mājah's *Sunan* (~890)** — complete the canonical *Kutub al-Sitta* (Six Books) of Sunni hadith. (Brown 2007; Brown 2009.)

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-9th-c. Khurasan under the Saffārid and then Sāmānid Persian dynasties, on the Persianate eastern frontier of the Abbasid caliphate. The post-*miḥna* Sunni-traditionalist restoration period — al-Mutawakkil (r. 847-861) had rolled back the Muʿtazilī-rationalist state-theology and restored Sunni-traditionalist (especially Ḥanbalī) primacy.
- **Religious milieu.** The high period of Sunni hadith-criticism (Yaḥyā ibn Maʿīn, ʿAlī ibn al-Madīnī, Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal in Baghdad; Bukhārī in Bukhara and the Khurasan circuit; Muslim in Nishapur). The Mutazilī-traditionalist conflict over the createdness-of-the-Quran has just settled in favor of the traditionalists; hadith-criticism is the methodological centerpiece of Sunni-traditionalist orthodoxy. The *Ṣaḥīḥān* are the **institutional fruit of this victory**: a canonical paired collection that fixes the hadith corpus as the foundational source for Sunni doctrine and practice.
- **Who was behind it.** Muslim himself, with extensive travel to collect hadith from teachers across the Islamic world (Khurasan, Iraq, Hijaz, Egypt). His teachers include Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal (the founder of the [[tradition-islam|Ḥanbalī]] *madhhab*) and Bukhārī himself. Muslim's school of disciples (the Nishapur hadith school) preserved and transmitted the *Ṣaḥīḥ*.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Sunni-Islamic manuscript transmission. The *Ṣaḥīḥ* with al-Nawawī's *Sharḥ Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim* (~1270 — the canonical Sunni commentary) is the standard reading-format in the medieval and modern Sunni tradition.

## Themes and motifs
- [[ecclesial-authority]] — Sunni hadith methodology establishes the authority of the prophetic Sunna as foundational alongside the Quran; *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim* is one of the two foundational textual deposits of that authority.
- [[oral-tradition-transmission]] — Muslim's *isnād*-criticism is a sophisticated methodology of vetting oral-tradition-transmission for reliability.
- [[commentary-as-genre]] — the al-Nawawī *Sharḥ* commentary tradition demonstrates the Islamic commentary-genre at its highest classical level.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-5-007-sahih-al-bukhari]] — *parallel-form* / *paired-canonical-text*: the two *Ṣaḥīḥān* are the foundational paired hadith collections; conventionally cited together (e.g., *muttafaq ʿalayhi* — "agreed-upon" by both Bukhārī and Muslim — is the highest-authority hadith-attribution).
- → [[phase-5-043-ibn-ishaq-sirat-rasul-allah]] — *complementary-genre*: Ibn Isḥāq's *Sīra* and Muslim's *Ṣaḥīḥ* are complementary primary sources for the early Islamic period — the *Sīra* for biographical-narrative material, the *Ṣaḥīḥ* for prophetic-Sunna doctrinal-legal material.
- → [[phase-4-034-quran]] — *commentary-on*: the Quran is the foundational text; the hadith corpus including *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim* provides the Sunna alongside it.
- → [[phase-5-024-aquinas-summa-theologiae]] — *structural-parallel*: the Sunni hadith canon (*Kutub al-Sitta*) provides Sunni Islam with its post-Quranic textual-doctrinal foundation in a way structurally parallel to the role of the Patristic + Council corpus in Christianity. The Aquinas-Summa engagement with the Christian tradition's Sunna-equivalent (Augustine, Damascus, the Councils) is the structural-parallel role.

## Disputes
- **Bukhārī vs. Muslim authority-ranking.** Mainstream Sunni: Bukhārī higher overall, Muslim superior on *isnād*-methodology in some technical respects. Various sub-traditions have nuanced positions.
- **Authenticity of specific hadith.** Sunni-classical: ~all *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim* hadith are *ṣaḥīḥ*. Modern critical scholarship: substantially accepts the Sunni-classical authentication for hadith with strong *isnād*-criticism; some hadith are partly contested even within Sunni tradition.
- **Inclusion of some politically-sensitive hadith.** Both *Ṣaḥīḥān* include hadith that became politically controversial in subsequent intra-Muslim sectarian dispute (especially around the ʿAlī / Muʿāwiya / First Fitna period); the inclusion-choices reflect 9th-c. Sunni-traditionalist editorial decisions.
