# Sinaitic Palimpsest (Codex Syriacus Sinaiticus)

## What it is
One of the two surviving witnesses to the **Old Syriac version of the gospels** — the *Evangelion da-Mepharreshe* — preserved as the erased lower writing of a **palimpsest**. The original Syriac gospel text, copied around the late 4th century, was scraped off and overwritten in 778 CE with a collection of lives of female saints; the gospel text beneath was recovered photographically after the manuscript's discovery. Older than and independent of the standard Syriac **Peshitta**, the Sinaitic palimpsest is a major early witness to the gospel text in the language closest to that of Jesus.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The Syriac-speaking Christianity of the late-antique Near East, between the Roman and Persian spheres.
- **Religious milieu.** The Old Syriac "separated gospels" represent the move away from Tatian's gospel-*harmony* (the Diatessaron) toward the four distinct gospels that the church came to require — a key episode in the formation of the gospel canon in the East.
- **Who was behind it.** An anonymous early Syriac translator; the manuscript was preserved for centuries at St Catherine's, Sinai.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Identified in 1892 by Agnes Smith Lewis and Margaret Dunlop Gibson — self-taught Scottish twin scholars — in the same monastery library that had yielded the Greek [[codex-sinaiticus|Codex Sinaiticus]] half a century earlier.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-108-peshitta]] — *superseded-by*: the Old Syriac of this palimpsest is the older layer that the Peshitta later standardized and replaced.
- → [[codex-sinaiticus]] — *sibling-find*: both are foundational biblical manuscripts recovered from St Catherine's Monastery on Sinai (the Greek codex by Tischendorf, 1844; this Syriac palimpsest by Lewis, 1892).

## Disputes
- **Date of the underwriting.** The gospel text is generally placed in the late 4th century, but estimates range; it is clearly pre-Peshitta.
- **Relationship to the Curetonian and the Diatessaron.** How the two Old Syriac witnesses relate to each other and to Tatian's harmony is a long-standing problem of Syriac textual scholarship (Burkitt 1904).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The gospel in a sister-language of Jesus.** As a witness to the Old Syriac — Syriac being a dialect of the Aramaic that Jesus spoke — the Sinaitic palimpsest carries some of the earliest and most distinctive non-Greek readings of the gospels, an independent line in the textual transmission that the Atlas can set beside the Greek [[codex-sinaiticus]] and [[codex-vaticanus]] (Burkitt 1904; Metzger 1977).
- **Two Sinai discoveries, one monastery.** That both the greatest Greek Bible (Sinaiticus) and a greatest Syriac gospel witness emerged from the single library of St Catherine's — recovered by Tischendorf and by the Lewis-Gibson sisters — makes the monastery a documented nexus of the modern recovery of the biblical text across languages.
