# Latin Rule of the Templars

## What it is
The original Latin Rule of the [[tradition-knights-templar|Order of the Temple]], promulgated at the [[event-council-of-troyes-1129|Council of Troyes]] (13 January 1129) — 72 articles regulating the combined monastic-military life of the Order. Modeled structurally on the Benedictine Rule but adapted to the unprecedented combination of monastic vow and military profession. [[bernard-of-clairvaux|Bernard of Clairvaux]] was the principal drafter, working from [[hugues-de-payens|Hugues de Payens']] decade of operational experience with the founding Jerusalem confraternity (~1119–1129).

The Latin Rule was later expanded by French additions in two distinct strata:
1. The ***Retraits*** or **Statutes** (Old French) — operational rules added incrementally through the 12th–13th c., regulating military procedure, hierarchical authority, daily life, and the conduct of the Order's preceptories.
2. The ***Penances*** (Old French) — disciplinary rules with case-precedents for offenses and their sanctions.

Together, the Latin Rule (1129) + the Retraits + the Penances constitute the operational corpus of the Order's internal law from 1129 to 1312.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The Council of Troyes was a French regional church council; the papacy (Honorius II) was in temporary withdrawal during a contested Roman political moment. Champagne was the operational base of the Templar Western-fundraising tour 1127–1129. France was the principal recruiting and donor zone.
- **Religious milieu.** The Cistercian movement, under Stephen Harding (Cîteaux) and Bernard (Clairvaux), was the most influential reformist current in Latin monasticism. Bernard's drafting of the Rule produces a recognizably Cistercian-inflected document.
- **Who was behind it.** The Cistercian-Bernardine reform party + the founding Templar leadership + the French episcopate, with papal legate authorization.
- **Transmission.** The Latin text survives in multiple medieval mss; the French additions (Retraits, Penances) survive in their own ms tradition. Curzon 1886 is the standard critical edition of the full French Rule.

## Themes and motifs
- [[monastic-asceticism]] — Templars take poverty, chastity, obedience.
- [[ecclesial-authority]] — direct subordination to the Master and through him to the Pope.
- [[martyrdom-theology]] — death in defense of the Holy Land carries the soteriological status of martyrdom.
- [[crusade-theology-of-malecide]] — the Bernardine doctrine that combat against *malefactores* in defense of pilgrims and the Holy Land is not *homicide* but *malecide* — sanctioned killing. This is theologically operationalized in the companion text [[phase-5-037-bernard-de-laude-novae-militiae]].

- → [[phase-5-037-bernard-de-laude-novae-militiae]] *companion-text*: the theological-rhetorical companion to the juridical-disciplinary Rule, both Bernardine.

## Disputes
- **How much was Bernard's drafting vs. Hugues' input?** Probably a collaborative document: Hugues supplied the operational framework, Bernard the theological language. Barber 1994 ch. 1.
- **The "Primitive Rule" vs. later additions.** The 72 Latin articles are the foundation; the Retraits and Penances are later. Older scholarship occasionally treated the whole French corpus as "the Rule"; modern scholarship distinguishes the strata. Hiestand 1980, Barber 1994.
