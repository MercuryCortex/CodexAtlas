# Brahma-Sutra-Bhasya (Śaṅkara)

## What it is
Śaṅkara's commentary (*bhāṣya*) on Bādarāyaṇa's *Brahmasūtra* (also called *Vedāntasūtra*), the aphoristic summa of Vedānta. The commentary is the foundational systematic statement of Advaita Vedānta — the doctrine that Brahman (the ultimate) and Ātman (the self) are non-different (*advaita*), that the apparent plurality of the world is *māyā* (cosmic illusion superimposed by ignorance, *avidyā*), and that liberation (*mokṣa*) consists in the direct realization "I am Brahman" (*aham brahmāsmi*). Śaṅkara also wrote commentaries (*bhāṣyas*) on ten principal Upaniṣads and on the *Bhagavadgītā*, plus the introductory treatise *Upadeśasāhasrī*. The *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya* organizes the Vedāntic texts into a coherent system, polemicizes against rival schools (Sāṃkhya, Vaiśeṣika, Buddhists, Jains, Pūrva Mīmāṃsā ritualists, theistic Vedāntins of his time), and establishes the "two-truths" distinction (*pāramārthika satya* — ultimate truth, *vyāvahārika satya* — empirical truth) that allows the world to be both real (empirically) and unreal (ultimately).

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late 8th / early 9th century India, post-Gupta regional kingdoms; the Pāla dynasty in the east, Pratīhāra in the north, Rāṣṭrakūṭa in the Deccan, Pāṇḍya and Pallava in the south. Buddhist Mahāyāna and Tantric institutions were still strong; Brahmanical revival was underway.
- **Religious milieu.** Indian intellectual culture organized as inter-school disputation (*saṃvāda* / *vāda*). Buddhism — especially Madhyamaka and Yogācāra — was the most sophisticated metaphysical opponent; Śaṅkara was repeatedly accused by his opponents of being a "crypto-Buddhist" (*pracchanna-bauddha*) for his use of *māyā*. The same period saw the rise of devotional (*bhakti*) movements in Tamil country (Āḻvārs, Nāyaṉārs) which would feed later Vedānta of Ramanuja and Madhva.
- **Who was behind it.** Ādi Śaṅkarācārya: South Indian Brahmin, organizer of the four *Daśanāmi* monastic orders, founder of the four cardinal *maṭhas* (Sringeri, Dwarka, Badrinath, Puri). His biographical *Śaṅkara-Digvijaya* (hagiography of his country-wide debating tour) is largely legendary, but his dates are placed by most scholars 788–820 CE (traditional) or 700–750 CE (revisionist; Hacker).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Indian transmission; the canonical organizing text of Advaita Vedānta. The text passed into European awareness via Anquetil-Duperron's Latin Upaniṣad translations (via Persian, 1801) which Schopenhauer cherished, and via Max Müller's *Sacred Books of the East* series (Thibaut translation, 1890–1904).

## Themes and motifs
- [[non-duality]] — the foundational text of *advaita* (non-duality) as a technical philosophical doctrine.
- [[hidden-god]] — Brahman is *nirguṇa* (without qualities); the personal *Īśvara* with qualities is *saguṇa* Brahman, a lower-level concession for empirical knowers.
- [[soul-immortality]] — but radicalized: there is no plurality of souls; the apparent multiplicity of *jīvas* is *māyā*; liberation is the realization that there is *only* the one self.
- [[creation-by-word]] — Vedic creation is reinterpreted as the *appearance* of multiplicity in the one Brahman, not real production.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-2-012-brihadaranyaka-upanishad]] / [[phase-2-013-chandogya-upanishad]] — *commentary-on*: Śaṅkara's exegesis is centrally an interpretation of the Upaniṣadic *mahāvākyas* ("great sayings": *tat tvam asi*, *aham brahmāsmi*, *prajñānam brahma*, *ayam ātmā brahma*).
- → [[phase-5-016-ramanuja-sribhasya]] — *polemic-against* (reverse direction): Ramanuja's *Śrībhāṣya* (~1100) is composed explicitly to refute Śaṅkara's interpretation of the same *Brahmasūtra*.
- → [[phase-5-022-madhva-brahma-sutra-bhasya]] — *polemic-against* (reverse direction): Madhva's commentary (~1240) is a third front in the same Vedānta debate, attacking *both* Śaṅkara and Ramanuja.
- → Buddhist Madhyamaka (Nāgārjuna) and Yogācāra — *parallel-motif* / *polemic-against*: Śaṅkara polemicizes against Buddhists at length but adopts much of their dialectical method; the "crypto-Buddhist" charge has been a perennial scholarly question (Bhattacharya 1925; Ingalls; Hacker; Halbfass).

## Disputes
- **Dates.** Traditional 788–820 CE (championed by K.A. Nilakanta Sastri, Hacker variant); revisionist 700–750 CE (Mayeda 1965; Hacker's later position). Range -700 to -820 in YAML.
- **Buddhist debt.** *How much* of Śaṅkara's apparatus derives from Madhyamaka? Bhattacharya (1925) argued essentially everything; later scholars (Ingalls, Mayeda, Hacker, Halbfass) qualify but acknowledge significant methodological borrowing.
- **Authorship of attributed works.** Many texts attributed to Śaṅkara (e.g., *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi*, much devotional poetry) are now widely held to be later. The *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya*, *Upadeśasāhasrī*, and the major Upaniṣad-bhāṣyas are securely his (Mayeda 1979).
