# Tiruviḷaiyāṭal Purāṇam (The Sacred Games of Śiva)

## What it is
The Tamil *sthala-purāṇa* of **Madurai** — the collection of the **sixty-four sacred games (*tiruviḷaiyāṭalkaḷ*)** that Śiva, in his Madurai form as **Sundareśvara**, is said to have played in and around the city for the sake of his devotees. Composed in its standard form by **Parañcōti Munivar** in the 17th century, the work narrates miracle after miracle: Śiva selling bangles, turning stones to gold, teaching grammar, sending rain, and — at the cycle's heart — his **marriage to the fish-eyed goddess Mīnākṣī**, the warrior-princess of the Pandya line who is the city's sovereign deity. It is the charter-text of the immense Mīnākṣī–Sundareśvara temple and the script for its festival life.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Madurai, ancient Pandya capital and, by the time of the standard text, a great Nayaka-period temple city.
- **Religious milieu.** Tamil Śaiva devotion (the bhakti world of the Nāyaṉārs) centred on the local form of Śiva and on the goddess Mīnākṣī, who in Madurai *precedes* and outranks her divine husband in the city's devotion.
- **Who was behind it.** Parañcōti Munivar (standard version), drawing on earlier Tamil and Sanskrit māhātmya material.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuously transmitted as the temple's own sacred history; its episodes are re-enacted in the temple's annual festivals.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-feminine]] — Mīnākṣī, the fish-eyed warrior-goddess and ruling deity of Madurai, whose marriage to Sundareśvara is the festival centerpiece.
- [[divine-kingship]] — Śiva as Sundareśvara rules Madurai as its divine king; the Pandya kings rule as his vassals, fusing temple and throne.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[meenakshi]] / [[sundareswarar]] — *foundational-myth*: the Tiruviḷaiyāṭal is the sacred biography of these two deities and their marriage, the core of Madurai religion.
- → [[silappatikaram]] — *shared-place*: the same sacred city of Madurai is the setting of the Tamil epic and of these divine games.

## Disputes
- **Recensions and date.** The relation among Parañcōti Munivar's 17th-century standard text, the earlier Perumpaṟṟap Puliyūr Nampi version, and the Sanskrit *Hālāsya Māhātmya* is a matter of textual scholarship.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **A city's god in sixty-four scenes.** The Tiruviḷaiyāṭal is one of the richest *sthala-purāṇa* corpora in India, binding a specific sacred geography, a living temple, and a festival calendar into a single mythic cycle — the model case of how a place becomes a god's biography and that biography becomes ritual (Harman 1989).
- **The goddess who outranks the god.** That Mīnākṣī, not Sundareśvara, is the sovereign deity of Madurai — a warrior-queen whom Śiva marries into the city — is a striking instance of the South Indian elevation of the local goddess, a pattern the Atlas tracks across the [[divine-feminine]] traditions (Harman 1989).
