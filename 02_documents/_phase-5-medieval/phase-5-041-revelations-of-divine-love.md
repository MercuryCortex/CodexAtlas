# A Revelation of Love (Revelations of Divine Love)

## What it is
Julian of Norwich's vernacular Middle English mystical-theological treatise, in two stages — the **Short Text** (~1373–1388, 25 chapters) and the much-expanded **Long Text** (~1395–1410, 86 chapters). The Long Text is a sustained theological elaboration on the **sixteen showings** Julian received during a near-fatal illness on 8 May 1373. The work is **the first surviving book in English by an identifiable woman** and one of the high points of late-medieval Western mystical theology.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Late-14th-c. English mystical efflorescence — contemporary with [[phase-5-031-cloud-of-unknowing|*Cloud of Unknowing*]], Walter Hilton's *Scale of Perfection*, Richard Rolle's English works, and the Wycliffite vernacular biblical translation project (1380s). Julian writes vernacular theology in a moment when vernacular theology was institutionally suspect (Archbishop Arundel's *Constitutions* 1407–1409 will restrict vernacular religious writing).
- **Anchoritic setting.** Julian's cell at St. Julian's Conisford in Norwich was a permanent walled enclosure attached to the church — typical of the English anchoritic tradition the *Ancrene Wisse* (~1220) had codified for women a century earlier.
- **Theological-pastoral horizon.** The Long Text is structured for **pastoral use** — Julian wants to communicate her showings as theology that other Christians can hold. She is not writing a private journal.
- **Transmission.** The work survived through **Catholic recusant** channels (Benedictine and Carthusian) after the English Reformation. First printed edition: Serenus Cressy 1670 (Catholic recusant Paris). The modern recovery begins with Grace Warrack 1901.

## Themes and motifs
- [[apophatic-mysticism]] — Julian uses both apophatic and kataphatic registers.
- [[bridal-mysticism]] — soul-bride imagery.
- [[apokatastasis]] — *'al shal be wel'* — universal-restoration tendencies (Turner 2011).
- [[atonement-reinterpreted]] — Julian's atonement-theology is non-satisfactionist in important ways; she re-frames Anselm.
- [[divine-feminine]] — Christ-as-Mother (chapters 58–63 of the Long Text).
- [[soul-as-bride]] — explicit in the Long Text.

## Disputes
- **Universalist or not?** Turner 2011: yes, in substance — Julian's logical position requires apokatastasis. McGinn 1998: more cautious, since Julian explicitly says some are saved and some are not.
- **Maternal Christology as foundational or as part of broader 12th-15th-c. tradition?** Bynum 1982: part of broader tradition; Julian is the most developed instance.
