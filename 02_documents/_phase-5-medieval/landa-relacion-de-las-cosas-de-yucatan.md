# Relación de las cosas de Yucatán (Landa)

## What it is
The single most important early source for **Yucatec Maya religion, calendar, and script** — and the work of the very man who did the most to destroy them. Fray **Diego de Landa**, Franciscan and later Bishop of Yucatán, presided over the notorious 1562 *auto-da-fé* at Maní, where he had Maya hieroglyphic books burned and Maya people tortured to extirpate "idolatry." Recalled to Spain to answer for his severity, he composed (c. 1566) the *Relación* — a defence that is also an ethnography, describing the Maya gods ([[itzamna]], [[kukulkan]], [[chaac]]), the festivals, the *haab* calendar and day-signs, and offering a garbled "Maya alphabet." The paradox is total: the destroyer of the books left the key to reading what remained of them.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The brutal early decades of Spanish Yucatán, with the Franciscans running a campaign of forced conversion.
- **Religious milieu.** Landa writes as an extirpator justifying himself, yet his Maya informants (from the Xiu and Cocom elites) make the *Relación* a dense record of a living tradition.
- **Who was behind it.** Landa, transmitting Maya testimony through a hostile evangelizing frame.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The original is lost; a 17th-century abridgement, rediscovered in 1862 and edited by Tozzer (1941), is the text we have.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-kingship]] — Landa records the fusion of Maya rulership, calendar, and the cults of [[kukulkan]] and [[itzamna]].

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-8-005-chilam-balam]] / [[phase-8-001-popol-vuh]] — *parallel-source*: the indigenous Maya literatures (in Latin-script Yucatec and K'iche') that Landa's external account complements.
- → [[phase-8-004-florentine-codex-sahagun]] — *shared-genre*: the friar-ethnography of a conquered Mesoamerican religion.

## Disputes
- **Reliability (tier note).** A T1 *document* but deeply compromised: Landa's data on calendar and ritual are foundational, yet his frame is self-exculpatory and his understanding of Maya religion partial and hostile (Tozzer's 1941 notes catalogue the distortions).
- **The "alphabet."** Landa wrongly assumed Maya glyphs were alphabetic; the resulting list looked like a dead end for centuries.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The key that cracked Maya writing.** Landa's flawed "alphabet" — long dismissed — turned out to record *syllabic* values, and it was precisely this list that the Soviet linguist **Yuri Knorozov used in 1952** to begin the phonetic decipherment of Maya hieroglyphs, the breakthrough that recovered a whole literate civilization's own voice (Coe 1992; Thompson 1950). The man who burned the books supplied the cipher to read them.
- **The colonial channel for Maya religion.** As with Durán for the Aztecs, Landa is the transmitting *wire* through which Yucatec Maya religion — the *home* tradition — reaches modern scholarship (Tozzer 1941).
