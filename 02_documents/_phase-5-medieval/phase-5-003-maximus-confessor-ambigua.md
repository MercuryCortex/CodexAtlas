# Ambigua and Mystagogia (Maximus the Confessor)

## What it is
Two related theological corpora by the 7th-century Byzantine monk and martyr Maximus. The *Ambigua* are extended commentaries on "difficult passages" (*aporiai*) of Gregory of Nazianzus and Pseudo-Dionysius: the *Ambigua ad Iohannem* (71 passages, ~628–630) and the *Ambigua ad Thomam* (five passages, ~635). They are Maximus's most sophisticated metaphysical work: a synthesis of Cappadocian theology, Pseudo-Dionysian apophaticism, and Origenist cosmology recoded through Chalcedonian Christology — most famously his doctrine of the *logoi* (the divine ideas/intentions of each created thing) gathered into the one *Logos* incarnate, and the cosmos as a unfolding "five mediations" (uncreated/created, intelligible/sensible, heaven/earth, paradise/inhabited world, male/female) all reconciled in Christ. The *Mystagogia* (~628–630) is a theological-symbolic exposition of the Byzantine Divine Liturgy as a re-enactment of cosmic salvation: church-as-cosmos, liturgy-as-cosmogenesis, the believer's interior life as the same drama on the microcosmic scale.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The Roman/Byzantine empire under Heraclius (610–641) and his successors, in collapse: Persian invasions, then the Islamic conquests beginning 634. Maximus fled Palestine for Carthage and Rome. The empire was trying to reunify with the Miaphysite churches of Egypt and Syria (lost after Chalcedon 451) by negotiating Monenergism (one *energy* of Christ) and then Monothelitism (one *will*). Maximus became the doctrinal leader of the resistance.
- **Religious milieu.** Late patristic. Pseudo-Dionysius's corpus (~480–520) had recently entered circulation and required Chalcedonian reception — Maximus's *Ambigua* is the major work performing that reception. Origen's cosmological speculations had been formally condemned (Constantinople II, 553); Maximus needed to rescue Origen's metaphysical apparatus while purging the condemned doctrines (preexistence of souls, universal restoration in its strict form).
- **Who was behind it.** Maximus himself: aristocratic Constantinopolitan civil servant turned monk; secretary briefly to Emperor Heraclius; theologian-in-residence at the Lateran Synod of 649 (which condemned Monothelitism with Pope Martin I). For this, Constantinople's regime had Maximus's tongue cut out and his right hand cut off ~662; he died in exile in Lazica that same year. The Sixth Ecumenical Council (Constantinople III, 680–681) vindicated him posthumously.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuously transmitted in Byzantine monasticism. The decisive Western reception came when John Scotus Eriugena translated the *Ambigua* and *Mystagogia* into Latin (~862–864) for Charles the Bald — this is the manuscript transmission that made Eriugena's own *Periphyseon* possible.

## Themes and motifs
- [[logos-cosmic-reason]] — Maximus's doctrine of the *logoi* (the divine intentions / archetypes of every existent) and their unity in the one *Logos* who is Christ; the most developed Christian counterpart to the Stoic and Plotinian *logos*.
- [[creation-by-word]] — creation as the *Logos*'s unfolding of the *logoi*; salvation as their gathering back.
- [[soul-immortality]] — defended in a non-Origenist form: the soul does not pre-exist the body, but is created with it and through Christ attains *theosis* (deification) — bodies included.
- [[mystery-initiation]] — *Mystagogia* makes the Eucharistic liturgy the structural enactment of cosmogenesis and deification.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-030-pseudo-dionysius]] — *commentary-on*: the *Ambigua ad Iohannem* directly explicates difficult passages of Dionysius; Maximus is the *normalizer* who makes Dionysian apophaticism safe for Chalcedonian orthodoxy.
- → [[phase-4-026-origen-on-first-principles]] — *redaction-of* / *polemic-against*: Maximus inherits Origen's cosmic framework while explicitly correcting the points condemned at Constantinople II 553.
- → [[phase-4-021-proclus-elements-of-theology]] — *direct-influence* (via Dionysius): the Procline triadic procession/return structure is structurally retained.
- → [[phase-5-008-eriugena-periphyseon]] — *direct-influence*: Eriugena translates Maximus and Dionysius before writing his own system; *Periphyseon* is unthinkable without this translation work.
- → [[phase-5-030-palamas-triads]] — *direct-influence*: Maximus's distinction between divine essence and divine *energeiai* / *logoi* is the conceptual ancestor of the Palamite essence/energies distinction.

## Disputes
- **Origenism.** How much of Origen's strict eschatology (universal *apokatastasis*) survives in Maximus is a long debate (von Balthasar 1941 vs. later correctives). Maximus uses the word *apokatastasis* in qualified senses but explicitly affirms eternal punishment for the obstinate.
- **Date of *Ambigua ad Iohannem*.** Generally dated 628–630 (Sherwood, Louth); some place earlier or split into multiple stages.
