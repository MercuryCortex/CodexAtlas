# Engishiki (Procedures of the Engi Era)

## What it is
A vast 10th-century **code of Japanese government regulations**, named for the Engi era and presented to the throne in 927 CE. While its fifty volumes cover the whole administrative apparatus of the Heian state, its **first ten volumes are the foundational documentary source for institutional Shinto**: they preserve the **norito** — the formal liturgical prayers spoken to the *kami* — the calendar and procedures of the state's annual rites, the regulations of the great Ise Shrine, and a comprehensive **register of officially-recognized shrines** and the deities enshrined in them. For the actual practice of early Shinto, no other single text is so important.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The mature Heian *ritsuryō* state under Fujiwara regents, codifying every procedure of court and cult.
- **Religious milieu.** The court's management of *kami* worship as an arm of government: the Engishiki fixes which shrines the state recognizes, ranks, and provisions, and how the great purification and harvest rites are performed.
- **Who was behind it.** A court commission under the Fujiwara, completing earlier *shiki* (procedural codes).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuously transmitted as the authoritative liturgical and administrative reference; central to the later study and revival of Shinto.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-kingship]] — the Engishiki binds the emperor's ritual role, the *kami*-cult, and the state into one sacral-administrative order, with the sovereign as chief priest of [[amaterasu]].

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-8-019-kojiki]] / [[phase-8-020-nihon-shoki]] — *complement*: the imperial chronicles narrate the myths of the *kami*; the Engishiki regulates their worship.
- → [[tradition-shinto]] — *foundational-document*: the institutional charter of state Shinto.

## Disputes
- **Date of enforcement.** Compiled 927, but enforced only from 967; the relationship of its norito to actual older practice is studied carefully.
- **Representativeness.** The Engishiki records the *court's* official cult; how far it reflects popular *kami* worship is debated.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The institutional skeleton of Shinto.** Where the [[phase-8-019-kojiki|Kojiki]] and [[phase-8-020-nihon-shoki|Nihon Shoki]] give Shinto its myths, the Engishiki gives it its *institutions* — the ranked shrine network, the liturgical texts, and the ritual calendar that structured state *kami*-worship for a millennium and were revived as the template of modern State Shinto (Hardacre 2017; Breen & Teeuwen 2010).
- **The norito as living archaic Japanese.** The Engishiki's prayers preserve some of the oldest continuous Japanese religious language, a liturgical register still echoed in shrine ritual today (Bock 1970).
