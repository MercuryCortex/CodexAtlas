# Picatrix (Ghāyat al-Ḥakīm)

## What it is
The most important medieval manual of **astral magic** — the Arabic *Ghāyat al-Ḥakīm* ("The Goal of the Sage"), compiled in 10th-century al-Andalus, and far better known by the name of its Latin translation, **Picatrix**. Where the astrological texts of the spine *read* the heavens, the Picatrix seeks to *operate* on them: it is a vast compendium of talismans, planetary images, suffumigations, invocations, and the philosophy of drawing down celestial *spiritus* into matter at astrologically elected moments. It fuses Hermetic, Sabian-Harranian, and Hellenistic material into the most systematic theory and practice of "natural magic" available to the Middle Ages.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Composed in the brilliant scientific culture of Umayyad/post-Umayyad al-Andalus; translated into Castilian (and thence Latin) at the court of **Alfonso X of Castile** c. 1256, the great Iberian translation enterprise that moved Arabic learning into Christian Europe.
- **Religious milieu.** The text draws openly on the star-worship of the Ṣābians of Ḥarrān and on Hermetic theology, making it religiously transgressive from orthodox standpoints — which is exactly why it circulated semi-clandestinely yet enormously in Latin Europe.
- **Who was behind it.** Traditionally al-Majrīṭī; reassigned by Fierro (1996) to Maslama ibn Qāsim al-Qurṭubī (d. 964), a scholar with esoteric (*bāṭinī*) interests.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Arabic → Castilian (1256) → Latin; the Latin Picatrix is the channel by which astral-magical theory reached Ficino, Agrippa, Bruno, and Dee.

## Themes and motifs
- [[microcosm-macrocosm]] — the operative core: the magus binds the powers of the macrocosm into terrestrial images and bodies via correspondence and astrological timing.
- [[divinatory-omen-reading]] — astrological election (choosing the moment) governs every operation.
- [[wisdom-as-cosmic-order]] — magic presented as the highest *ḥikma* (wisdom), knowledge of the cosmic sympathies.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[al-kindi]] — *direct-influence*: al-Kindī's *De Radiis* (the doctrine that every star radiates rays that structure the sublunary world) is the philosophical engine of the Picatrix's magic.
- → [[phase-5-064-abu-mashar-great-introduction]] — *shared-milieu*: the same Arabic astrological science, here turned to operative ends.
- → [[phase-3-021-hermetic-corpus-earliest]] — *direct-influence*: the Hermetic theology of cosmic sympathy underwrites the talismanic programme.
- → [[marsilio-ficino]] / [[cornelius-agrippa]] / [[tradition-renaissance-hermeticism]] — *direct-influence*: the Picatrix is a principal source of Renaissance natural magic.

## Disputes
- **Authorship and date.** The al-Majrīṭī attribution is now generally rejected in favour of al-Qurṭubī (Fierro 1996); the Arabic compilation is dated to the mid-10th to early-11th century.
- **How much is "Sabian."** The degree to which the Picatrix preserves genuine Harranian star-ritual versus literary Hermeticism is debated (Pingree 1980).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The operative branch of the spine, Arabic to Latin.** The Picatrix is the clearest single conduit by which the *practical* astral tradition — talismanic magic grounded in astrological timing — crossed from Islamic al-Andalus into Latin Christendom via Alfonso X's translators, and from there into the Renaissance magic of Ficino, Agrippa, Bruno, and Dee (Pingree 1986; Ritter & Plessner 1962). It is the magical sibling of the doctrinal transmission carried by [[phase-5-064-abu-mashar-great-introduction]].
- **A Hermetic-Sabian-Hellenistic confluence.** Few texts so densely braid the threads the Atlas traces — Mesopotamian celestial substrate, Hellenistic astrology, Egyptian Hermetism, and Harranian star-religion — into one operative system (Pingree 1980).
