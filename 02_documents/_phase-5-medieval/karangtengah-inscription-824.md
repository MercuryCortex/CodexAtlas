# Karangtengah Inscription (824 CE)

## What it is
The **Karangtengah inscription** (also called the Kayumwungan inscription), dated to **824 CE**, is one of the most important dated epigraphic sources for the great Buddhist monuments of the Central Javanese **Śailendra** dynasty. Bilingual — a Sanskrit eulogy followed by an Old-Javanese dating-and-donation section — it records a royal Buddhist religious foundation, including a monument that many scholars connect to the consecration of **[[place-borobudur]]** (the inscription's reference to a *jinālaya* / "abode of the Jina" and a sacred foundation is read by de Casparis and others against the Borobudur and Mendut complex). It thus provides a rare fixed date within the otherwise sparsely-documented period when the colossal stūpa-mountain of Borobudur — whose galleries carve the [[gandavyuha-sutra]] in stone — was being built.

## Significance
Because Borobudur itself bears no foundation inscription, dated Śailendra epigraphs like Karangtengah are the principal external evidence for placing the monument in history. The inscription anchors the Śailendra Buddhist building programme to the early ninth century and is a standard reference in the long scholarly debate over Borobudur's date, patrons, and meaning — analysed at length in de Casparis's foundational editions of the Central Javanese inscriptions.
