# Tahāfut al-Falāsifa (The Incoherence of the Philosophers)

## What it is
**Al-Ghazālī's principal anti-falsafa polemic** — a systematic refutation of 20 theses he attributes to the falāsifa (especially [[ibn-sina|Avicenna]], with [[al-farabi|al-Farabi]] as secondary target), composed in Baghdad c. 1094-1095 during al-Ghazālī's tenure as head professor at the Niẓāmiyya madrasa. Of the 20 theses refuted, **al-Ghazālī classifies three as constituting unbelief (*kufr*)** — meaning that holding them puts the holder outside Islam:
1. **The eternity of the world** (the falsafa view that the world has no temporal beginning; the Ashʿarī kalām and Quranic creation-doctrine require a temporal beginning of the world);
2. **Denial that God knows particulars** (the falsafa view that God knows particulars *in a universal mode*; al-Ghazālī reads this as effectively denying particular providence);
3. **Denial of bodily resurrection** (the falsafa view that the rational soul survives death without bodily reconstitution; the Quranic eschatology requires bodily resurrection).

The remaining 17 refuted theses are heretical (*bidʿa*) without rising to unbelief. The book is **deliberately destructive** in its method — al-Ghazālī claims to refute the falāsifa using their own logical-philosophical tools, demonstrating that their conclusions are *incoherent* (*tahāfut* — literally "collapse," "falling-apart-over-each-other") rather than building positive Ashʿarī kalām alternatives. The companion text, the ***Maqāṣid al-Falāsifa*** (*Aims of the Philosophers*, composed shortly before the *Tahāfut*), is al-Ghazālī's sympathetic-expository summary of the falsafa positions he then refutes in the *Tahāfut* — a deliberate dialectical-pedagogical structure (*"first I show you what they say, then I show why it is incoherent"*).

The most enduring philosophical content is the **17th discussion**, on **causality**: al-Ghazālī argues that the falāsifa cannot demonstrate any *necessary* connection between cause and effect (e.g., the burning of cotton when fire touches it). Conjunction of cause and effect is *habit* (*ʿāda*) — God could equally well dis-conjoin them (e.g., fire could not burn cotton — as the Quranic narrative of Abraham in the fire of Nimrod, 21:69, attests). This is the locus classicus of **Islamic occasionalism** ([[occasionalism]]) and structurally anticipates Hume's later sceptical arguments against necessary causal connection by ~700 years.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-11th-c. Seljuk-controlled Abbasid Baghdad. Al-Ghazālī is appointed head professor at the Niẓāmiyya madrasa in 1091 by the vizier Niẓām al-Mulk (founder of the Niẓāmiyya network of madrasas explicitly to teach Ashʿarī Sunni orthodoxy against Ismāʿīlī Shīʿī threats). The Niẓāmiyya is a Sunni-Ashʿarī state institution; the *Tahāfut* fits its institutional purpose.
- **Religious milieu.** Sunni Ashʿarī kalām facing two intellectual threats: (1) Falsafa — the Greek-Arabic philosophical tradition culminating in Avicenna, seen as importing pagan-Greek doctrines incompatible with Islam (eternity of the world, denial of particular providence, etc.); (2) Ismāʿīlī esoteric Shīʿīsm — the Bāṭinī tradition reading the Quran as encoded esoteric truth accessed through the Imam-hierarchy. Al-Ghazālī attacked both: the *Tahāfut* against falsafa, the *Faḍāʾiḥ al-Bāṭiniyya* (*Infamies of the Bāṭinīs*, ~1095) against Ismāʿīlī esotericism. Both are state-Sunni intellectual-policing works.
- **Who was behind it.** Al-Ghazālī himself, at the height of his Niẓāmiyya teaching career. The companion *Maqāṣid* was composed shortly before; the *Tahāfut* in 1094-95. Shortly after the *Tahāfut*, al-Ghazālī underwent his celebrated spiritual crisis (1095, retirement from Baghdad, ~11 years of Sufi-mystical retreat in Damascus / Jerusalem / Mecca / home Khurasan, during which he composes *Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn* — [[phase-5-015-al-ghazali-ihya]]). The *Tahāfut* is the **last work of his pre-crisis institutional period**.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Islamic manuscript transmission. The Latin translation by **Calo Calonymos** (Calo ben David, Italian-Jewish convert) c. 1527 made the *Tahāfut* available in Latin Renaissance Europe as ***Destructio Philosophorum***, published Venice 1527 paired with Averroes's reply in the Giunta-edition Averroes-omnia. **Aquinas's misclassification of Ghazālī** — as a falsafa author alongside Avicenna and Averroes — stems from Aquinas reading the *Maqāṣid* (Ghazālī's sympathetic-expository summary) under Ghazālī's name as if it were Ghazālī's own falsafa position, without knowing the *Tahāfut* refutation. The historical misreading is corrected only with the 16th-c. Calonymos Latin *Destructio* edition.

## Themes and motifs
- [[tahafut-controversy]] — the bookend opening; the *Tahāfut* sets up the controversy that Averroes will reply to in [[phase-5-046-ibn-rushd-tahafut-al-tahafut|*Tahāfut al-Tahāfut*]] ~85 years later.
- [[falsafa]] — al-Ghazālī's *Tahāfut* is the keystone *anti-falsafa* polemic; reading the *Tahāfut* alongside the *Maqāṣid* gives a complete late-11th-c. snapshot of what falsafa was understood to be.
- [[occasionalism]] — the 17th-discussion treatment of causation is the locus classicus of Ashʿarī occasionalism: God is the only true cause; "natural" causation is habit / regularity sustained by divine convention, not necessary connection.
- [[free-will-vs-determinism]] — Ghazālī's defense of God's free creative will against the falsafa-Avicennian quasi-necessitarian emanationism.
- [[resurrection-of-the-dead]] — bodily resurrection (Quranic eschatology) against the falsafa-Avicennian rational-soul-only afterlife.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-5-044-ibn-sina-kitab-al-shifa]] — *polemic-against*: the *Tahāfut*'s 20 refuted theses target Avicennian theses concentrated in the *Shifāʾ* Metaphysics. The *Tahāfut* is essentially a refutation of the *Shifāʾ*.
- → [[phase-5-046-ibn-rushd-tahafut-al-tahafut]] — *direct-reply*: Averroes's *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* is the canonical 12th-c. point-by-point reply. The two together form the Tahafut controversy.
- → [[phase-5-015-al-ghazali-ihya]] — *shared-author*: al-Ghazālī's mature post-crisis Sufi-theological *summa*. The *Iḥyāʾ* presents the constructive religious-mystical alternative to the destructive *Tahāfut*.
- → [[occasionalism]] (theme) — the 17th-discussion treatment of causation is the locus classicus.
- → Hume on causation — *structural-parallel*: Ghazālī's habit-of-conjunction argument anticipates Hume's 1739 *Treatise* sceptical argument against necessary causal connection. **Whether there is a direct historical transmission** (via the Calonymos Latin *Destructio* → 17th-c. European reception) **or only structural parallel** is contested in modern scholarship; Griffel 2009 notes the parallel without committing on historical transmission.

## Disputes
- **Ghazālī's actual position on the 20 theses.** Frank 1994 + Griffel 2009 argue: al-Ghazālī's later writings (especially *Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn*, *Munqidh min al-Ḍalāl*, and the highly Avicennian late *Mishkāt al-Anwār*) show that al-Ghazālī **personally absorbed substantial Avicennian theses** even while polemicizing against them publicly. The *Tahāfut* is a polemical-pedagogical exercise targeting *certain* falsafa positions in their Ghazālī-reconstructed forms; it is not Ghazālī's complete private epistemology. The standard "Ghazālī killed falsafa" narrative is being substantially revised by contemporary scholarship.
- **The three "*kufr*" charges.** Whether al-Ghazālī's three unbelief-charges (eternity of world, God's knowledge of particulars, bodily resurrection) accurately characterize Avicenna's actual positions is contested. Avicennian defenders (then and now) argue Ghazālī mis-states the Avicennian positions.
- **The Aquinas-Ghazālī misclassification.** Aquinas cites Ghazālī as a *falāsifa* author on the strength of the *Maqāṣid* alone; this is a known scholarly issue in medieval Latin philosophy. The *Destructio Philosophorum* Latin translation (1527) corrects the misclassification but only late, well after Aquinas.
- **Did Ghazālī "kill" falsafa in Sunni learning?** The traditional narrative (Goldziher; older Western Islamicists): yes, after Ghazālī falsafa shrinks institutionally in Sunni learning. The contemporary revisionist view (Griffel 2009; Eichner 2020): falsafa continues vigorously in the post-Ghazālī period, in different forms and institutional locations, especially in the Persianate East (Suhrawardi, the post-Avicennian commentaries) and substantially as *naturalized into kalām itself* (the post-Razian Ashʿarī tradition is heavily Avicennian).
