# Platform Sutra of the Sixth Patriarch

## What it is
The foundational text of Chan / Zen Buddhism — paradoxically a *sūtra* (a designation otherwise reserved for words of the Buddha) attributed to a Chinese patriarch. It narrates Huineng's biography (illiterate woodcutter awakened by overhearing the Diamond Sūtra; secret transmission of the patriarch's robe and bowl from the Fifth Patriarch Hongren over the head of the senior disciple Shenxiu; the famous "mind-mirror" verse contest), then presents his teaching: *sudden awakening* (*dunwu* 頓悟), the identity of meditation (*ding*) and wisdom (*hui*), the rejection of formal sitting-meditation as a means to awakening (because the self-nature is already enlightened), and a polemical contrast between the "Southern School" of sudden awakening and the "Northern School" of gradual awakening. The text contains the locus classicus formulas: "Bodhi originally has no tree, the bright mirror has no stand; originally there is not a single thing — where can dust alight?"

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Mid-to-late Tang dynasty, the period of Chan's institutionalization. The post-An-Lushan-Rebellion (755–763) court was patronizing different Chan factions; sectarian competition for imperial favor and monastic patronage produced the Sutra's polemics.
- **Religious milieu.** Chinese Buddhism in its sectarian classical phase. The text's polemical opponent — the "Northern School" (Shenxiu, ~606–706 and successors) — was not actually doctrinally crude. McRae 1986/2003 demonstrates that the Northern School's "gradualism" is largely a strawman constructed by Huineng's disciple Shenhui (~684–758) in his 730s public campaigns against Shenxiu's lineage. The Platform Sutra is the literary monument of that campaign's success.
- **Who was behind it.** Layered authorship: (1) Huineng himself — historicity attested but biographical details largely legendary; (2) Fahai, his disciple, the traditional editor; (3) Shenhui's school, which inserted polemical material; (4) Song-dynasty editors who produced the polished standard recension. The Dunhuang witness shows the text already in roughly its present shape by ~780, but considerably rougher and shorter than the standard version.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous transmission of the long recension in East Asia. The decisive modern event was Paul Pelliot's and Aurel Stein's 1907–1908 recovery from the Dunhuang Cave 17 library (sealed ~1002 CE) of the short recension (mss. dated ~830). D.T. Suzuki and Hu Shih in the 1930s used the Dunhuang find to reconstruct early Chan history; Yampolsky's 1967 edition translates the Dunhuang text.

## Themes and motifs
- [[non-duality]] — the unity of meditation and wisdom; the rejection of any practice/attainment duality.
- [[emptiness-sunyata]] — Heart-Sūtra-style negation chain redeployed as an awakening pedagogy.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — "seeing one's own nature" (*jianxing* 見性) as the operative event; analogous structurally (not historically) to the Gnostic "knowing").

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-5-002-heart-sutra]] — *direct-influence*: Huineng's awakening is triggered by the Diamond Sūtra in the narrative, but the Sutra's negation rhetoric draws constantly on the Heart Sūtra; Chan liturgy chants the Heart Sūtra daily.
- → [[phase-2-014-daodejing]] — *shared-milieu* / *parallel-motif*: Chan's anti-textual, anti-conceptual rhetoric ("a separate transmission outside the scriptures") and its valuing of spontaneity / wu-wei has a clear Daoist substrate; Chan is the East Asian Buddhist tradition most thoroughly Sinified.
- → Northern School (Shenxiu) — *polemic-against*: the entire narrative apparatus is constructed against the Northern School.
- → later Zen lineages (Linji, Caodong / Soto; Korean Seon; Vietnamese Thien; Japanese Rinzai and Soto) — *direct-influence*: the Platform Sutra is the constitutional text of all of them.

## Disputes
- **Huineng's historicity.** Real but the biography in the Sutra is largely constructed. The illiterate-woodcutter framing is now widely regarded as a literary device.
- **Dating of the text.** Dunhuang witness ~830, composition ~780 (Yampolsky). The standard long recension is Yuan-dynasty (1291).
- **Sudden vs. gradual.** Whether this binary describes anything actual in 8th-c. Chan practice or is purely Shenhui's polemical fiction is debated (McRae argues the latter).
