# Rasāʾil al-Ḥikma (Epistles of Wisdom)

**Rasāʾil al-Ḥikma** ("Epistles of Wisdom") — composed 1017–1043 CE by Hamza ibn ʿAlī and his associate Bahāʾ al-Dīn al-Muqtanā — is the closed scriptural canon of the Druze, a syncretic religious community that emerged in Fatimid Cairo around the figure of the caliph al-Ḥākim bi-Amr Allāh (r. 996–1021). The corpus consists of 111 epistles (also organized as the "Books of Wisdom"), transmitted exclusively within the initiated minority (*ʿuqqāl*, "the knowers"); the uninitiated majority (*juhhāl*, "the ignorant") participate in communal life but not in the text's esoteric content.

## Contents and doctrine

The epistles establish the core Druze theological structure:
- **Divine unity through negation**: God is so absolutely one that no positive attribute applies; the Druze *tawḥīd* (monotheism) is more radical than even Islamic *tawḥīd*
- **Cosmic hierarchy**: Five divine principles (*ḥudūd*) mediate between the absolute divine and the world, structuring the spiritual cosmos — paralleling the Ismaili emanation hierarchy and Neoplatonic hypostases
- **Al-Ḥākim as theophany**: The Fatimid caliph al-Ḥākim bi-Amr Allāh is presented as the most recent theophanic manifestation of the divine principle in human history — a deification of a historical ruler structurally parallel to Roman imperial apotheosis
- **Transmigration of souls** (*tanāsukh*): Souls reincarnate exclusively among Druze; the total Druze population is fixed, ensuring a stable relationship between souls and community
- **Taqiyya** (precautionary dissimulation): The community may outwardly conform to surrounding religion for self-protection

## Cross-tradition significance

### The clearest case of reincarnation entering an Abrahamic-adjacent tradition
The Druze doctrine of metempsychosis is the most unambiguous instance of transmigration belief entering a tradition derived from Islam. The transmission route passes through [[tradition-ismailism|Ismaili]] Neoplatonism: Fatimid court theology had absorbed Plotinian soul-doctrine via the Arabic Neoplatonic corpus (*Theology of Aristotle* — [[phase-5-048-theology-of-aristotle-arabic]]) and Pythagorean mathematical metaphysics. The Rasāʾil takes this and produces a fully operational rebirth cosmology — souls recycling within the community — which is structurally identical to Hindu/Buddhist transmigration but arrived at through an entirely different philosophical lineage.

### Divine kingship in an Abrahamic context
Al-Ḥākim's deification in the Rasāʾil is the vault's primary example of the divine-ruler theophany pattern within an Abrahamic framework. The same structure appears in Egyptian pharaonic divine kingship ([[tradition-egyptian-religion]]), Roman imperial apotheosis, and Japanese imperial Shinto divinity — but the Druze case is uniquely persistent: the community has maintained al-Ḥākim's theophanic status for a millennium, including the expectation of his eschatological return.

### Closed-canon survival strategy
The restriction of the Rasāʾil to initiated members (*ʿuqqāl*) is a textual parallel to the Mandaean restriction of the *Ginza Rba* to initiated priests and the Yazidi restriction of the *qewls* to hereditary reciters. The closed-esoteric institutional survival strategy — text restricted to the inner circle, outer community maintains communal but not doctrinal life — is an alternative to the universal accessibility of mainstream Abrahamic scripture.
