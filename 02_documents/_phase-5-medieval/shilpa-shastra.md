# Śilpa Śāstra

## What it is
**Śilpa Śāstra** ("treatise on the arts/crafts") is not a single book but the broad Sanskrit technical literature governing the visual and constructional arts of India — sculpture, painting, iconography, image-casting, and the design of temples and divine images. It overlaps with the architectural [[vastu-shastra]] but centres on *making the image*: the canonical proportions (*tāla-māna*, iconometry), the postures, attributes, and *mudrās* by which each deity must be represented, and the ritual of image-creation and consecration. Major texts and strata include the *Mānasāra*, the South-Indian *Mayamata*, the *Śilparatna*, and the relevant sections of the Āgamas and Purāṇas. By fixing the measurements and attributes of divine images, the Śilpa Śāstra made it possible for the icon of a god carved in one region to be recognisably the same god a thousand miles away — it is the codified grammar of Hindu sacred art.

## Significance
The iconometric canon standardised the depiction of [[shiva]], [[vishnu]], and the goddesses across the subcontinent and, through the Āgamic and Pāla traditions, shaped Buddhist and Jain image-making as well — a continuity captured in [[theme-hindu-buddhist-architectural-continuity]]. The treatises treat the temple and image as a [[theme-mandala-as-architectural-programme]]: a diagram of the cosmos in which proportion is theology.
