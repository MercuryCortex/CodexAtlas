# Sūrya Śataka

## What it is
The **Sūrya Śataka** ("Hundred Verses to the Sun") is a Sanskrit devotional poem of one hundred ornate verses in the *sragdharā* metre, addressed to the sun-god [[surya]], composed by the seventh-century court poet **Mayūra**. It is one of the most celebrated *śataka* (century) hymns in Sanskrit and a key literary monument of the **Saura** (sun-worship) tradition. A famous legend — recorded with the rival hymns of his contemporary Bāṇabhaṭṭa — holds that Mayūra, stricken with leprosy, composed the hymn and was cured by Sūrya's grace, making the poem itself a story of the sun's healing power. The hymn praises the sun's rays, his horses and charioteer Aruṇa, and his cosmic sovereignty, weaving theology, astronomy, and elaborate ornament (*alaṃkāra*) into a single devotional performance.

## Significance
The Sūrya Śataka is a high-water mark of Sanskrit *stotra*-*kāvya* and a primary literary witness to the once-major Saura cult of sun-worship in early-medieval India — the same devotional current that would later raise the great sun-temple of Konārak. Its framing of Sūrya as cosmic sovereign also reflects the early-medieval fusion of solar theology with royal ideology ([[divine-kingship]]). The standard scholarly edition and translation is Quackenbos (1917).
