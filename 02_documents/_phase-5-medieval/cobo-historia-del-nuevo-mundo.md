# Cobo, Historia del Nuevo Mundo

## What it is
The **Historia del Nuevo Mundo** ("History of the New World"), completed in 1653 by the Spanish Jesuit **Bernabé Cobo**, is one of the richest single sources for the religion of the **Inca**. Though written more than a century after the conquest by a European missionary, Cobo drew on now-lost sixteenth-century investigations — above all the report of the colonial official Polo de Ondegardo — to compile an unusually systematic account of Andean belief and practice. His books on Inca religion describe the supreme creator [[viracocha]], the sun-god [[inti]] and the imperial solar cult, the *huaca* shrines, mummified royal ancestors, and — most famously — the **ceque system**: the network of forty-one sight-lines radiating from the Coricancha (the Sun temple) in Cusco, along which some 328 shrines were arranged and tended by the city's kin-groups. This is the home tradition of the Andes recorded through, but not authored by, the colonial church.

## Significance
Because so much Inca religious knowledge was destroyed or driven underground, Cobo's compilation is a primary key to reconstructing the pre-Columbian Andean sacred landscape, especially the ceque system that ordered Cusco as a ritual and astronomical diagram — an Andean expression of sacred kingship and cosmic order ([[divine-kingship]]). The standard English editions are Roland Hamilton's translations.
