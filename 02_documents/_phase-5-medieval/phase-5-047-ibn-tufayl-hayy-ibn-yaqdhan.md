# Risālat Ḥayy ibn Yaqẓān (The Epistle of Ḥayy ibn Yaqẓān — "Alive, son of Awake")

## What it is
**Ibn Ṭufayl's philosophical novel** — a short Arabic prose treatise narrating the autodidact ascent of a child raised by a gazelle on a desert island in the Indian Ocean. The work is structured in **three septenary phases** (0-7 / 7-21 / 21-49 years), with each seven-year phase elevating Ḥayy through observation, induction, philosophical reasoning, and mystical contemplation to a higher rung of intelligible reality. By age 49 Ḥayy has reasoned his way to:
- the natural order (Phase 1, ages 0-7: physical observation and survival);
- the structure of natural causation, the four-elements cosmology, the soul-body distinction, animal vs. rational soul (Phase 2, ages 7-21);
- the metaphysical structure of being, the Necessary Being / God whose essence is existence, the emanational cosmology, the immortality of the rational soul, and mystical *fanāʾ* (annihilation in the divine) — the Avicennian intellect-Active-Intellect conjunction (Phase 3, ages 21-49).

In the final third of the work, an inhabitant of a nearby populated island arrives on Ḥayy's island. **Absāl** (sometimes vocalized Asāl) has been retreating from the literal-revealed religion of his island's population to practice *taʾwīl* (esoteric-allegorical interpretation) of the revealed scriptures. Absāl teaches Ḥayy human language; Ḥayy and Absāl discover that the inner-philosophical truth Ḥayy has reached by unaided reason and the esoteric content of the revealed religion Absāl has reached by *taʾwīl* are **the same truth**. They travel together to Absāl's populated island intending to teach the people the philosophical-esoteric truth; they find the multitude **cannot be moved beyond the literal-revealed form** of religion; they return to Ḥayy's desert island to continue their philosophical-mystical practice in solitude.

The political-theological lesson — **philosophical truth is the inner content of revealed religion, accessible to the unaided intellect, but the multitude cannot be moved beyond the exoteric literal-revealed form** — is the keystone esoteric falsafa position, articulated in narrative form rather than direct treatise form. The Avicennian intellect-theory framework is explicit; the Sufi-mystical *fanāʾ* terminology is present; the result is a synthesis of Aristotelian-Avicennian falsafa with Sufi mystical-union vocabulary that **structurally anticipates simultaneously: (a) the philosophical-novel form; (b) the *deus sive natura* Enlightenment natural-religion trope; (c) the desert-island-autodidact narrative form that descends into Locke and *Robinson Crusoe*.**

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Almohad-controlled al-Andalus / Maghrib, ~12th-c. Ibn Ṭufayl is at the court of Almohad caliph Abū Yaʿqūb Yūsuf as physician and vizier. The "philosopher-prince" character of the Almohad court is the political background that makes the philosophical-novel form possible.
- **Religious milieu.** Sunni Mālikī under the Almohad reformist Islamic regime; substantial state-level patronage of Aristotelian-Averroist falsafa under Abū Yaʿqūb Yūsuf. Sufi mystical traditions (Ibn ʿArabī's Andalusi family generation just below Ibn Ṭufayl's) provide the *fanāʾ*-vocabulary context.
- **Who was behind it.** Ibn Ṭufayl himself; the work is dedicated to a disciple/friend who has asked about the inner content of Avicennian philosophy.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Arabic / Hebrew manuscript transmission. The **1671 Pococke Latin** translation at Oxford is the watershed reception-history moment: the *Philosophus Autodidactus* enters the European Enlightenment debate on natural religion. Quaker English version (George Keith, 1674); Simon Ockley fuller English version (1708); reception in Locke, Defoe, Spinoza, the Quaker / Behmenist natural-religion movements. **Modern critical Arabic edition Léon Gauthier 1900 / 1936** — the Algiers Arabist-edition.

## Themes and motifs
- [[falsafa]] — the *Ḥayy* is the most accessible Andalusi-falsafa exposition; its narrative form translates Avicennian-Aristotelian metaphysics into a parable readable by non-specialists. The Avicennian intellect-theory, emanational cosmology, and Necessary-Being argument all appear in narrative form.
- [[ascent-of-the-soul]] — the three-septenary-phase ascent of Ḥayy through observation → reasoning → mystical contemplation is the canonical Avicennian-Sufi soul-ascent in narrative form.
- [[henosis]] / mystical union — the climactic *fanāʾ* of Ḥayy in conjunction with the Active Intellect / Necessary Being is the Avicennian-Sufi *henosis*.
- [[via-negativa]] — the divine attributes are reached by negation (eternal, unmoving, unchanging, beyond category).
- [[allegorical-exegesis]] / *taʾwīl* — Absāl represents the esoteric-allegorical reading of revealed religion; Ḥayy represents the unaided-natural-reason route; their identity-of-result is the falsafa thesis that philosophy and revealed religion converge.
- [[perennial-philosophy]] — the *Ḥayy*'s thesis (that the inner-philosophical truth is universal and accessible to natural reason without reliance on revealed scripture) is one of the keystone medieval sources for the later perennialist tradition.

## Disputes
- **The Active-Intellect / Sufi-mystical-union ending.** Is the climactic *fanāʾ* a Sufi-mystical event or an Avicennian Active-Intellect conjunction or both? Corbin 1960 reads it as fundamentally Avicennian-Illuminationist; Goodman 1972 and Hourani read it as more Sufi-inflected. Modern consensus: probably both — the Avicennian framework with Sufi-mystical vocabulary is characteristic of late-12th-c. Andalusi falsafa.
- **The esoteric political-philosophical message.** Does the *Ḥayy* recommend strict separation of philosophical truth from the multitude (Andalusi-peripatetic *mutawaḥḥid* "solitary" position), or careful esoteric / exoteric pedagogical structure? Strauss-Mahdi readings lean esoteric-political; mainstream readings see Sufi-mystical individual-ascent as the principal message.
- **Strength of direct influence on Locke / Defoe.** Documented (Ben-Zaken 2011); the *strength* of the influence is contested. Cautious modern view: *Ḥayy* is one of several inputs into 17th-c. European natural-religion / desert-island-autodidact forms; strongest direct influence on the Quaker / Behmenist reception.
