# Periphyseon (On the Division of Nature)

## What it is
A five-book Latin theological-cosmological *summa* in dialogue form (Master and Disciple) by the Irish scholar John Scotus Eriugena at the Carolingian court. The work's organizing schema is a fourfold division of "Nature" (*natura* = totality of what is and is not): (1) Nature that creates and is not created — God as origin; (2) Nature that is created and creates — the primordial causes / divine ideas; (3) Nature that is created and does not create — the spatiotemporal cosmos of effects; (4) Nature that neither creates nor is created — God as final term, all things returning into God. The system is a Christian Neoplatonic procession-and-return synthesizing Pseudo-Dionysius's apophatic theology, Maximus's *logoi*-doctrine, Gregory of Nyssa's anthropology, and Augustine's exegesis. Eriugena's negative theology is so radical that God is described as *Nihil* — *nothing*, because beyond every predicable category — and the procession of creation is itself described as the *theophany* (manifestation) of the otherwise-incomprehensible God. The system's apparent tendency toward identifying God and creation got the work condemned in 1225 as pantheist.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Carolingian court of Charles the Bald (West Francia, r. 843–877), grandson of Charlemagne. Eriugena was the leading intellectual of the court's *schola*, brought there ~847.
- **Religious milieu.** Latin Christianity in the early scholastic prehistory. The 9th-c. predestination controversy (with Gottschalk of Orbais) drew Eriugena into theological battle — his *De Praedestinatione* (851) tried a Greek-philosophical solution that was itself condemned at the synods of Valence (855) and Langres (859). The decisive event was Charles the Bald's commission for Eriugena to translate the Greek corpus newly arrived in 827 as a gift from Byzantine Emperor Michael II to Louis the Pious: the works of Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite. Eriugena completed the Dionysius translation ~860–862, the Maximus *Ambigua* ~862–864, and then composed the *Periphyseon* as the systematic Latin theology those translations made possible.
- **Who was behind it.** John Scotus Eriugena ("John the Irishman the Irish-born"): the only first-rate metaphysician of the Latin West between Augustine (~430) and Anselm (~1080), and the *only* Carolingian-period Latin who could read Greek competently. Origins in Ireland (Iona-Bobbio learning tradition), career entirely on the Continent.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Three Carolingian-period mss; the work was read in 9th–12th c. monastic schools (especially Laon) and influenced the 12th-c. School of Chartres. Amalric of Bena (d. ~1206) and his disciples (the Amalricians) were accused of drawing pantheist heresies from *Periphyseon*; this drove the 1225 condemnation under Honorius III. Surviving copies were rediscovered in the 17th c.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hidden-god]] — God is *superessentialis* (beyond being); strictly speaking, *God does not exist* in the sense that creatures exist; God *is Nothing* by hyper-apophatic excess.
- [[creation-by-word]] — creation as theophany: God *manifests* God's self in the procession through the primordial causes into spatiotemporal creatures.
- [[non-duality]] — radical: the difference between Creator and creation is real only from the creature's perspective; from the divine perspective, creatures *are* God-manifesting-as-creature.
- [[logos-cosmic-reason]] — the *primordialiter causae* are Christ-as-Logos's interior content, an explicit Maximian inheritance.

## Disputes
- **Pantheism.** The 1225 condemnation called the work pantheist. Modern scholarship (Moran 1989; McGinn) reads Eriugena as panentheist or as deploying a hyper-apophatic vocabulary that orthodoxy should not have found problematic. The debate continues.
- **Eriugena's dates.** Birth ~800–815 generally accepted; death sometimes given as ~877 (court) or ~880 (Malmesbury legend of being stabbed by his own students with their pen-styluses is a 12th-c. embellishment).
