# Tablet of the Cross (Palenque)

## What it is
The principal hieroglyphic tablet of the **Temple of the Cross** at Palenque, carved for King **K'inich Kan Bahlam II** and dedicated in 692 CE. Its central image is the Maya **world-tree** — a foliated cosmic axis topped by the celestial Principal Bird Deity — flanked by the standing king and a smaller figure of his deceased father, the great K'inich Janaab Pakal. Its long text reaches back into deep mythological time to narrate the **birth of the Palenque Triad gods** and the city's divine progenitors, then steps forward through the historical dynasty to Kan Bahlam's own accession — binding the living king to the cosmic order.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-7th-century Palenque at the height of its power under Kan Bahlam II, son of Pakal, in the great age of western-lowland Maya kingship.
- **Religious milieu.** The Cross Group's three temples each house one of the **Palenque Triad** — three patron gods born in mythic time — and the tablets together form a coordinated cosmological-dynastic statement.
- **Who was behind it.** Kan Bahlam II's court scribes and sculptors.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Recorded by 18th–19th-century explorers (who supplied the misleading "Cross" name) and central to the 20th-century decipherment of Maya writing.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-kingship]] — the tablet's entire program legitimates the king by anchoring his reign in the births of the gods and the unbroken descent of the dynasty.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[palenque-tablet-of-the-foliated-cross]] / [[palenque-tablet-of-the-sun]] — *set*: the three Cross Group tablets are a single coordinated program, mapping the three Triad gods onto three temples (creation, sustenance, war).
- → [[palenque-triad]] — *primary-source*: the tablet is a chief source for the Triad gods' mythology.

## Disputes
- **Mythological chronology.** The relation of the deep-time "births" (the First Mother / progenitor and the Triad) to actual ritual and dynastic events is intensively debated in Maya epigraphy.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **A Maya scripture in stone.** The Cross Group tablets are among the fullest Classic Maya religious texts — a pre-Columbian civilization's own written cosmology and dynastic theology, independent of any Old-World contact, decipherable in the Maya's own language (Schele & Mathews 1998). The "world-tree as cosmic axis" they depict is the Mesoamerican member of the cross-cultural axis-mundi pattern the Atlas tracks (the Norse Yggdrasil, the Indian world-pillar, etc.) — a striking independent parallel.
