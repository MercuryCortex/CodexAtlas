# Iḥyāʾ ʿUlūm al-Dīn (The Revival of the Religious Sciences)

## What it is
Al-Ghazālī's monumental synthesis of Sunni Islamic ethics, ritual practice, and Sufi spirituality. The work is structured as 40 books in four "quarters": *Acts of Worship* (purification, prayer, almsgiving, fasting, pilgrimage, recitation, supplication, litanies, the order of personal devotions, daily-life ordering); *Norms of Daily Life* (eating, marriage, earning, lawful/unlawful, friendship, retreat, travel, music, commanding right and forbidding wrong, conduct of the Prophet); *Ways to Perdition* (wonders of the heart, disciplining the soul, breaking the two desires of belly and sex, the diseases of the tongue, blame and envy, greed, love of status, pride and vanity, self-deception); *Ways to Salvation* (repentance, patience, fear and hope, poverty and asceticism, divine unity and trust, love and longing, intention and sincerity, self-examination, contemplation, remembrance of death and the afterlife). The literary genre is *adab* (ethical-spiritual instruction) elevated to systematic theological discourse. The fourfold structure mirrors the Sufi maps of the spiritual path while remaining strictly grounded in Qur'an, hadith, and Sunni jurisprudence.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The Seljuk Empire under the great vizier Niẓām al-Mulk (assassinated 1092) and Sultan Malik Shāh (d. 1092), succeeded by political fragmentation. Niẓām al-Mulk founded the great Niẓāmiyyah madrasas; al-Ghazālī was appointed to the Niẓāmiyyah of Baghdad in 1091, becoming the most prominent academic theologian in the Muslim world at age 33.
- **Religious milieu.** Sunni Islam was consolidating its post-Miḥna traditionalist-Ashʿarite synthesis. Ismā'īlī Shi'ism was at its peak influence under the Fatimids of Cairo and the Nizārī "Assassins" of Alamut, against whom al-Ghazālī wrote polemics. Avicennan Aristotelianism was the dominant philosophical school; al-Ghazālī's *Tahāfut al-Falāsifa* (Incoherence of the Philosophers, ~1095) systematically attacked Avicenna and Fārābī on 20 points including the eternity of the world and bodily resurrection — a famous (and famously double-edged) refutation that paradoxically *secured* the Aristotelian framework as the implicit horizon of Sunni theology going forward.
- **Who was behind it.** Al-Ghazālī himself. The biographical context for the *Iḥyāʾ* is his celebrated 1095 spiritual crisis (described in *al-Munqidh min al-Ḍalāl*): at the height of academic success in Baghdad, he became literally unable to speak in lecture, abandoned his post under the cover of a Mecca pilgrimage, and entered a decade of Sufi practice (Damascus, Jerusalem, Hebron, Medina, Mecca; later home in Ṭūs). The *Iḥyāʾ* was composed during this period of withdrawal and synthesizes everything he had been: jurist, theologian, philosopher, and now Sufi practitioner.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Islamic transmission; one of the most read books in the post-medieval Sunni world. Translated into many Islamicate languages; brought into the medieval Latin West indirectly (Aquinas knew of al-Ghazālī as "Algazel" but read primarily his *Maqāṣid al-Falāsifa*, an exposition of Avicenna, mistaking Ghazālī for a straight Aristotelian). Hostile contemporary reactions: Ibn Taymiyyah (~1300) attacked the *Iḥyāʾ* for its alleged philosophical errors; the work was publicly burned in Almoravid Cordoba in 1109 under Mālikī jurist agitation.

## Themes and motifs
- [[ethical-dualism]] — the *Iḥyāʾ*'s third and fourth quarters are organized around vice/virtue (*muhlikāt* — destroyers; *munjiyāt* — savers); the moral binary structures the work.
- [[soul-immortality]] — Ghazālī defends bodily resurrection against the Aristotelians while sophisticating the eschatology with Sufi-psychological depth.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — the experiential heart of religion is *maʿrifa* (gnosis, direct knowing); the work's pedagogy moves the believer from external observance through internal disciplines toward direct experience.
- [[afterlife-judgment]] — Book 40 *Remembering Death and the Afterlife* (*Dhikr al-Mawt*) is the work's eschatological capstone, much read independently.
- [[mystery-initiation]] — graded structure of practice and stations (*maqāmāt*) and states (*aḥwāl*), inherited from the Sufi tradition (Qushayrī, Junayd) and made central.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-034-quran]] / [[phase-5-007-sahih-al-bukhari]] — *direct-quote*: extensively cited throughout; the *Iḥyāʾ* takes legal-doctrinal Sunni Islam as foundation, *adds* Sufi depth, never subtracts.
- → Avicenna (Ibn Sīnā) — *polemic-against* / *direct-influence*: Ghazālī's relationship is famously double — he wrote *Tahāfut al-Falāsifa* against Avicenna (and the *Maqāṣid* summarizing him for the *Tahāfut*), yet the *Iḥyāʾ*'s philosophical apparatus (faculty psychology, theory of the soul) is deeply Avicennan-inflected.
- → [[phase-5-018-suhrawardi-hikmat-al-ishraq]] — *direct-influence*: Suhrawardī's Illuminationism takes Ghazālī's spiritual ascent psychology and converts it into a systematic metaphysics of light.
- → [[phase-5-021-ibn-arabi-fusus-al-hikam]] — *direct-influence*: Ibn ʿArabī's ontology builds on the Ghazālian foundation though with much more radical metaphysical claims.
- → [[phase-5-025-rumi-masnavi]] — *direct-influence*: Rūmī cites Ghazālī repeatedly; the *Iḥyāʾ*'s ethical-spiritual program is the doctrinal backdrop of the *Masnavi*'s poetry.

## Disputes
- **Ghazālī and philosophy.** Did Ghazālī genuinely refute Avicenna or merely refine him? Griffel 2009 argues the *Iḥyāʾ* is far more philosophically informed than its anti-philosophical surface suggests; Marmura, Wisnovsky, and others differ on degree.
- **Effect on Islamic science.** A long-running thesis (popularized by Goldziher and later by Pervez Hoodbhoy) that al-Ghazālī's anti-philosophical polemic single-handedly stalled Islamic science is now largely rejected by historians of Islamic science (Saliba, Ragep) — Islamic science continued and flourished for centuries after Ghazālī.
