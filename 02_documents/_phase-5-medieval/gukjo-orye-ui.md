# Gukjo Oryeui

## What it is
The **Gukjo Oryeui** ("Five Rites of State") is the official ritual code of **Joseon Korea**, begun under King Sejong and completed and promulgated under King Seongjong in **1474**. It systematizes the entire ceremonial life of the Confucian state under the canonical **Five Rites**: the *gillye* (auspicious rites — sacrifices to Heaven, the royal ancestors, and the gods of soil and grain), *hyungnye* (funerary/inauspicious rites), *gullye* (military rites), *binnye* (guest/diplomatic rites), and *garye* (felicitous rites — coronations, weddings, investitures). For each it prescribes the participants, vessels, music, vestments, and choreography in minute detail. Together with the legal code *Gyeongguk Daejeon*, it forms the constitutional backbone of the Joseon order — the ritual half of a state conceived on Neo-Confucian principles.

## Significance
The Gukjo Oryeui is a primary document of one of history's most thoroughgoing projects of Confucianization: the deliberate reconstruction of an entire society's ritual life along Confucian-classical lines, the subject of Martina Deuchler's classic study. Because correct ritual was held to constitute political and cosmic order, the code is the clearest expression of Joseon [[divine-kingship]] — the king as ritual mediator — and of the [[tradition-joseon-confucianism]] that defined Korea for half a millennium. Its rites descend from the classical Confucian ritualism rooted in the world of the [[phase-2-015-analects-of-confucius]].
