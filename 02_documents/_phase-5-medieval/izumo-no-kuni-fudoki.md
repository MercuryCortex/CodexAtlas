# Izumo no Kuni Fudoki

## What it is
The **Izumo no Kuni Fudoki** is the only one of the Nara-period *fudoki* (imperially-commissioned provincial gazetteers, ordered in 713) to survive in nearly complete form. Submitted to the court in 733, it records the geography, products, place-name etymologies, and local traditions of **Izumo Province** — and, crucially, its distinctive **myths**. Where the court chronicles [[phase-8-019-kojiki]] and [[phase-8-020-nihon-shoki]] center the sun-goddess [[amaterasu]] and the Yamato imperial line, the Izumo Fudoki preserves the parallel mythology of the Izumo region, dominated by the storm-god [[susanoo]] and his descendants, including the famous **kunibiki** ("land-pulling") myth in which a god enlarges Izumo by hauling surplus land across the sea with ropes. It is the single most important witness to a regional Japanese mythology that the centralizing Yamato narrative partly absorbed and partly suppressed.

## Significance
The Izumo Fudoki is indispensable for reconstructing pre-unified, local Japanese religion: it shows that the "national" mythology of the Kojiki was assembled out of competing regional cycles, of which the Izumo tradition (centered on the great shrine of Izumo Taisha) was the most powerful rival to the Yamato-Amaterasu line. The standard English translation and study is Aoki (1971/1997).
