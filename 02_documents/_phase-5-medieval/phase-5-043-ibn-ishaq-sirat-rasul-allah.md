# Sirat Rasul Allah (Life of the Messenger of God)

## What it is
**The foundational Islamic-tradition biography of the Prophet Muhammad** — originally composed by **[[ibn-ishaq|Ibn Isḥāq]] (~767 CE)** in Medina and Iraq, surviving in canonical form as the **recension by ʿAbd al-Malik ibn Hisham (~833 CE)**, with substantial Ibn Isḥāq material additionally preserved in Ṭabarī's *Tārīkh* (~915). The *Sīra* is **the single most influential narrative source for Muhammad's life** and is the founding work of the Arabic *sīra*-genre. The Ibn Hisham recension is structured in three principal parts: **(1) *al-Mubtadaʾ*** (The Beginning — abbreviated in Ibn Hisham; Ibn Isḥāq's original began with creation, the prophets through Adam, and the Arab prophetic genealogy); **(2) *al-Mabʿath*** (The Sending — Muhammad's birth, the Year of the Elephant, ʿAbd al-Muṭṭalib and Banu Hāshim genealogy, the rise of the Quraysh, Khadīja's marriage to Muhammad, the first revelation at Mount Ḥirāʾ, the Meccan persecution period, the [[satanic-verses-incident|*gharānīq* episode]], the first hijra to Aksum, the boycott and Year of Sorrow, the Isrāʾ wa-l-Miʿrāj (Night Journey and Ascension), the Hegira to Medina); **(3) *al-Maghāzī*** (The Campaigns — the Medinan period: the Battle of Badr 624, Uḥud 625, the Trench 627, the slander of ʿĀʾisha 627, treaty of Hudaybiyya 628, conquest of Mecca 630, expedition to Tabūk 630, the *ridda* / apostasy context, Muhammad's death 632). The work integrates **biographical narrative, *Asbāb al-Nuzūl* (Quranic revelation-occasions), early Arab poetry as historical attestation, treaty-texts (most famously the Constitution of Medina), and battle-narratives with detailed troop-counts and casualty lists**.

The Ibn Hisham recension **edits out material the editor regarded as theologically unfit** — most consequentially the *gharānīq* / Satanic Verses narrative, certain materials Ibn Hisham regarded as embarrassing or polemically-sensitive, and substantial portions of Ibn Isḥāq's original *Mubtadaʾ* (Beginning) section on pre-Muhammad prophetic genealogy. The fuller Ibn Isḥāq corpus is partly recoverable through **Ṭabarī's independent preservation** in the *Tārīkh* (~915), which preserves the *gharānīq* narrative in its original Ibn Isḥāq form as part of the **early Islamic-tradition memory before the post-13th-c. orthodox rejection** of the Satanic Verses material (Ahmed 2017 documents).

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Original composition: late-Umayyad and early Abbasid (Ibn Isḥāq c. 704-767, working in Medina and Iraq under Abbasid caliphal patronage c. 754-767). Ibn Hisham recension: ~833, late-Abbasid Egypt (Fusṭāṭ).
- **Religious milieu.** The mid-8th c. Abbasid-Mawlā milieu in which Persian-mawlā and Medinan-Arab scholars are systematizing the Islamic-tradition memory in Arabic literary form for the first time on this scale. The *Sīra* is part of the broader 8th-9th-c. project of foundational Islamic-tradition systematic literature (Quranic canonical text under ʿUthmān ~650; hadith collections starting ~8th c.; jurisprudence schools forming; *Sīra* providing biographical narrative; Ṭabarī's universal history ~915 integrating all of these).
- **Who was behind it.** Ibn Isḥāq (Mawlā of the Banu Hāshim) for the original; Ibn Hisham for the canonical recension. Ibn Isḥāq drew on the early Medinan biographical tradition (ʿUrwa ibn al-Zubayr, Wahb ibn Munabbih, al-Zuhrī) and his own hadith-collection across the Hijaz / Iraq circuit.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The Ibn Hisham recension is preserved in multiple Arabic manuscript traditions and is the standard form by which the *Sīra* is read. Modern Arabic critical editions: Wüstenfeld 1858-60 (the foundational European edition); Cairo Saqqā-Abyārī-Shalabī 1955; multiple subsequent. English Guillaume 1955 (Oxford UP) — the standard translation with Ṭabarī supplementation in footnotes.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hanif-monotheism]] — the *Sīra* preserves the Islamic-tradition memory of the pre-Islamic *ḥunafāʾ* (Waraqa ibn Nawfal, Zayd ibn ʿAmr ibn Nufayl, Khalid ibn Sinan, Quss ibn Saʿida) and frames the Quranic *millat Ibrāhīm* / Abrahamic-monotheism in pre-Islamic Arabian context.
- [[satanic-verses-incident]] — the Ibn Isḥāq original preserves the *gharānīq* narrative (preserved by Ṭabarī through Ibn Isḥāq); Ibn Hisham edited it out of the canonical recension.
- [[oral-tradition-transmission]] — the *Sīra* is methodologically the foundational example of Arabic-Islamic systematic oral-tradition-into-textual-recording. The *isnād*-criticism Ibn Isḥāq applied is less rigorous than the subsequent classical hadith-criticism school (Bukhārī, Muslim), leading to Mālik ibn Anas's classical criticism of Ibn Isḥāq's *isnāds*.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-4-034-quran]] — *complementary-source*: the *Sīra* provides the biographical and historical context for the Quranic *Asbāb al-Nuzūl*. The two together form the foundational sources for the Quranic period.
- → [[phase-5-007-sahih-al-bukhari]] / [[phase-5-042-sahih-muslim]] — *complementary-genre*: the hadith collections provide the prophetic-Sunna doctrinal-legal material; the *Sīra* provides the biographical-narrative material. The classical Sunni distinction between *sīra* and *ḥadīth* is institutional (the *Sīra* is biographical-historical; the *Ṣaḥīḥān* are doctrinal-legal).
- → [[phase-5-009-tabari-tafsir]] / Ṭabarī's broader *Tārīkh al-Rusul wa-l-Mulūk* — *direct-source* and *preservation*: Ṭabarī's *Tārīkh* preserves substantial Ibn Isḥāq material not in the Ibn Hisham recension.
- → [[phase-4-018-mandaean-book-of-john]] — *contemporary-religious-context*: the *Sīra* references the *Sabians* (the Quranic *ahl al-kitāb* protected religious community); the late-antique Mandaean / Sabian religious context is reconstructible via the *Sīra* + early Islamic-tradition material.

## Disputes
- **Source-critical reliability.** Mainstream contemporary view (Watt, Donner): reliable historical core + late-7th-and-8th-c. literary-historical formation. Revisionist (Wansbrough 1977; Crone-Cook 1977): substantially later construction. Subsequent manuscript and inscriptional evidence has constrained the most radical revisionist positions.
- **Ibn Hisham's editorial decisions.** Ibn Hisham removed material on theological-orthodoxy grounds (the *gharānīq* narrative; some early-prophetic-genealogy material; some politically-controversial reports). The fuller Ibn Isḥāq corpus partly recoverable via Ṭabarī.
- **Mālik ibn Anas's criticism of Ibn Isḥāq's *isnād*s.** Classical Mālikī school criticism of Ibn Isḥāq's hadith-methodological standards (insufficient *isnād*-rigor for the *Sīra*'s narrative material) is one of the principal reasons the *Sīra* is canonically read as a **biographical-historical** source rather than as a **hadith** source per se.
- **Sectarian use.** Sunni reception treats the *Sīra* as the foundational biographical source. Shīʿī reception is more selective (the *Sīra* gives more attention to Sunni-canonical figures and events than to ʿAlī-Fāṭima material; Shīʿī tradition supplements the *Sīra* with independent Imāmī biographical-historical material).
