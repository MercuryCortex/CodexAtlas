# Sinkessar (Ethiopian Synaxarium)

## What it is
The **Sinkessar** (Ge'ez *Sänkəsar*) is the Ethiopian Orthodox hagiographic-liturgical calendar — a comprehensive collection of saints' *Lives*, martyrologies, and liturgical commemorations organized by date according to the Ethiopian liturgical year (which has 12 months of 30 days + the *Pagumē* short month of 5–6 days). Each day's entry contains short prose accounts of the saints commemorated that day, with associated readings, hymns, and liturgical references. The work has been read daily in Ethiopian monastic and parish liturgical practice for nearly six centuries.

The Ge'ez Sinkessar is a composite work derived from (a) the **Coptic-Arabic Synaxarium of Michael of Athrib and Malig** (~13th c., Cairo) translated into Ge'ez in the early-to-mid 15th century, and (b) **substantial Ethiopian-original hagiographic additions** — entries for Ethiopian saints not present in the Coptic original: [[frumentius]] (Abba Salama), [[the-nine-saints]], [[abba-garima]], [[yared-the-deacon]], [[kaleb-of-aksum]], [[lalibela-king]], [[takla-haymanot]], [[ewostatewos]], [[giyorgis-of-sagla]], [[zara-yaqob-emperor]], and others. The Marian-feast expansions (the 33 Marian feasts of the Ethiopian calendar) and the integration of the *Te'amre Maryam* (Marian miracle compendium) into the daily-reading scheme are products of the [[zara-yaqob-emperor]] period.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Solomonic Ethiopia, mid-15th c., under Zar'a Ya'qob's reign of theological-liturgical consolidation. The work crystallizes the canonical hagiographic register of Ethiopian Christianity at this point and becomes definitive.
- **Religious milieu.** Marian-devotional intensification under Zar'a Ya'qob (33 Marian feasts), integration of indigenous-Ethiopian sanctity (Lalibela, Takla Haymanot, Ewostatewos) with the inherited Coptic-Mediterranean martyrological tradition, daily liturgical reading practice in monasteries and parishes.
- **Who was behind it.** Michael of Athrib for the Coptic-Arabic compilation; anonymous Ge'ez translator-redactors for the Ge'ez version; Zar'a Ya'qob and his court theologians for the Marian-feast expansions and Ethiopian-saint additions.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Ethiopian liturgical use to present. Budge 1928 English translation; Colin 1986–2002 critical Ge'ez edition with French translation.

## Themes and motifs
- [[hagiography]] — the foundational Ethiopian hagiographic-liturgical compendium; many Ethiopian saints' *Lives* are known primarily through their Sinkessar entries.
- [[liturgical-calendar]] — defines the structure of the Ethiopian liturgical year and the daily reading practice.
- [[ethiopian-marian-devotion]] — codifies the 33 Marian feasts.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Coptic-Arabic Synaxarium of Michael of Athrib — *direct-translation* / *redaction-of*: the Ge'ez Sinkessar is a heavy redaction with substantial additions.
- → Greek Synaxarion of Constantinople — *parallel-form*: parallel hagiographic-calendar tradition; both descend from earlier Greek martyrological-calendrical traditions.
- → [[zara-yaqob-emperor]] / [[phase-5-038-mashafa-berhan]] — *shared-milieu*: the Marian-feast and Ethiopian-saint expansions belong to Zar'a Ya'qob's liturgical-theological consolidation.
- → Individual Ethiopian saints' Gadla literature — *redaction-of*: the Sinkessar entries condense fuller *gadl* (acts/hagiography) sources.

## Disputes
- **Date of the initial Ge'ez translation.** Most likely early 15th c., with substantial elaboration through Zar'a Ya'qob's reign. The 1400–1450 window is consensus; finer dating is unrecoverable.
- **Extent of Ethiopian-original vs. Coptic-translated material.** The Marian-miracle and indigenous-Ethiopian-saint additions are extensive; the proportions are debated.
- **Status of variant recensions.** Multiple recensions exist (the *Vulgate* recension, the various monastic recensions); the Colin edition treats the textual situation systematically.
