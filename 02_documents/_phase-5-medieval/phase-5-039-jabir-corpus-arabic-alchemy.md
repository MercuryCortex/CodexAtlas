# Jabirian Corpus — Arabic Alchemical Literature

## What it is
The substantial body of Arabic-language alchemical-natural-philosophical-Hermetic writing attributed to [[jabir-ibn-hayyan|Jabir ibn Hayyan]] but actually authored by a 9th-10th-c. school of Arabic-speaking philosophers writing under his name — **the principal Arabic-language transmission vehicle of Hermetic-alchemical material from late antiquity into the medieval Islamic world and from there into medieval and early-modern Latin Europe**. The corpus is conventionally divided into the *112 Books*, the *70 Books* (*Kitāb al-Sabʿīn*), the *144 Books of the Balance* (incorporating the *Kitāb al-Mīzān*), and additional thematic clusters; Ibn al-Nadim's 10th-c. *Fihrist* lists hundreds of attributed titles.

The corpus integrates four prior streams:
1. **Late-antique Greco-Egyptian alchemy** — Zosimos, Maria the Jewess, Pseudo-Democritus, the Hermetic Greek tradition. Jabir cites these explicitly.
2. **Aristotelian natural philosophy** — the four-elements / four-qualities framework, providing the theoretical infrastructure.
3. **Galenic medicine** — humoral theory and pharmacology.
4. **Indian alchemical and metallurgical material** — transmitted via Persian intermediaries.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Abbasid Caliphate (founded 750 CE), at its early-medieval intellectual high-water — Baghdad under Harun al-Rashid (786–809) and al-Maʾmūn (813–833), the **Bayt al-Ḥikma** (House of Wisdom) translation movement. The Arabic-language scientific synthesis is the defining intellectual project of the 8th-10th c. Islamic world.
- **Religious milieu.** The Jabirian corpus emerges in a context that is *officially* Sunni-Abbasid but intellectually engaged with **Ismaili / proto-Ismaili philosophical circles**. Kraus 1942 demonstrated the corpus's intellectual adjacency to the [[phase-5-011-rasail-ikhwan-al-safa|Ikhwān al-Ṣafāʾ]] (Brethren of Purity) — a 9th-10th-c. Ismaili-adjacent encyclopedist circle. This Shia-esoteric adjacency feeds back into European reception of "Arabic alchemy" as inherently esoteric.
- **Who was behind it.** The 9th-10th-c. Jabirian school — multiple anonymous authors writing under the eponymous attribution. The historical 8th-c. Jabir is a probable nucleus but most of the corpus is later.
- **Transmission to Latin Europe.** Beginning in the 12th c., key Jabirian texts were translated into Latin (often through Toledo or Sicily) and entered Latin scholastic-alchemical literature as 'Geber' material. The medieval Latin alchemical canon — through Albertus Magnus, Roger Bacon, Pseudo-Lull, Paul of Taranto's *Summa Perfectionis* (which Newman 1991 demonstrated is by Paul, not by the Arabic Jabir), Petrus Bonus' *Pretiosa Margarita Novella* — is built on the Jabirian-Geberian substrate.

## Themes and motifs
- [[alchemy-as-spiritual-discipline]] — the Jabirian corpus combines technical-chemical procedure with philosophical-religious framing.
- [[hermetic-correspondences]] — the mīzān theory's quantitative-qualitative correspondences instantiate Hermetic analogical-cosmological thinking.

## Disputes
- **How much of the corpus is authentically 8th-c.?** Kraus 1942: a small nucleus; most is 9th-10th c.
- **Ismaili affiliation specifics.** Kraus identified Ismaili / Qarmati intellectual adjacency; the precise institutional connection is debated.
- **Relation of Arabic Jabir to Latin Geber.** Newman 1991 made the key distinction: the Latin "Geber" of the *Summa Perfectionis* is by Paul of Taranto, not by the Arabic Jabir, though it appropriates Jabir's name. Earlier Latin Geber-attributed texts (12th-c. translations) are closer to authentic Arabic Jabir.
