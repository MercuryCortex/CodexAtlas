# The Theology of Aristotle (Uthūlūjiyā Arisṭū)

## What it is
**The single most consequential pseudepigraphic text in the history of medieval philosophy** — an Arabic paraphrase, produced in the al-Kindi circle at the Abbasid Bayt al-Ḥikma in Baghdad c. 840-870 CE, of **Plotinus *Enneads* IV-VI** (the latter half of the Plotinian corpus, covering the soul, the intellect, and the One), **falsely attributed to Aristotle**. The Arabic text is a paraphrase rather than a literal translation, reorganizing the Plotinian material into ten *maymar*-treatises with substantial editorial interventions, expansions, and explanatory glosses. The translator was the Syrian Christian ʿAbd al-Masīḥ ibn Nāʿima al-Ḥimṣī; the text was revised and possibly framed for circulation by [[al-kindi|al-Kindi]] himself.

The misattribution to Aristotle was not a deliberate fraud but a transmission-history accident: the al-Kindi circle was processing the Plotinian Greek material as part of the broader Aristotelian project of recovering the Stagirite's complete corpus, and the *Theology* came to be read as the long-lost "theological" / "metaphysical" capstone of Aristotle's program — the content of *Metaphysics Λ* (Lambda) expanded and elaborated. **The misattribution was uncritically accepted by the entire falsafa tradition** from al-Kindi through Avicenna, Averroes, and into medieval Latin scholasticism, and is the principal reason that **Avicennian and Averroist "Aristotle" is in substantial part Plotinus**. The Neoplatonic emanational cosmology, the doctrine of the One above being, the soul's mystical ascent — all the Plotinian themes the falsafa tradition took as Aristotelian — derive from this Arabic Plotinus.

**The correction took 700 years.** Cardinal Bessarion in the 15th c. noted the Plotinian character of the text; **Petrus Nicolaus Castellani (1519) firmly identified the *Theology of Aristotle* as paraphrase of Plotinus**; **Ficino's 1492 separate Latin translation of Plotinus *Enneads* in the original** retroactively let European readers see that the *Theology of Aristotle* had been Plotinus all along. The correction is part of the broader **Casaubon-style critical reidentification movement** (Casaubon 1614 on the Hermetic Corpus — [[event-casaubon-redates-hermetica-1614]]; Castellani 1519 on the *Theology of Aristotle*; broader 15th-17th-c. critical reidentification of late-antique pseudepigrapha as Renaissance philological scholarship matured).

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Abbasid Baghdad under the caliphs al-Maʾmūn, al-Muʿtaṣim, and al-Wāthiq — the high point of the Graeco-Arabic translation movement at the Bayt al-Ḥikma. The al-Kindi circle is the principal translation-supervisory team, with Christian-Syrian, Sabian-Harranian, and Muslim translators producing Arabic versions of Greek philosophy, science, medicine, and astronomy.
- **Religious milieu.** Muʿtazilī-supported rationalist Abbasid official theology during the *miḥna* period; the broader Bayt al-Ḥikma cosmopolitan-intellectual environment includes Christian, Jewish, Sabian, Zoroastrian, and Muslim scholars working in collaborative translation programs.
- **Who was behind it.** Christian-Syrian translator ʿAbd al-Masīḥ ibn Nāʿima al-Ḥimṣī; revision and framing by al-Kindi; circulation supervised by the al-Kindi circle as part of the broader Greek-philosophy-into-Arabic project. The pseudepigraphic attribution is an accident of late-antique-Greek transmission catalogs that the al-Kindi team inherited, not a deliberate fraud.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Arabic manuscript transmission through the medieval falsafa tradition. Latin partial reception via the broader Avicennian-Aristotelian Toledo-school translations. Modern critical study begins with F. Dieterici 1882; the canonical contemporary study is Adamson 2002 *The Arabic Plotinus*.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-emanation]] — the Plotinian emanational cosmology: the One → Intellect → Soul → World-Soul → individual souls. The Arabic paraphrase preserves the Plotinian emanational structure and transmits it to falsafa.
- [[the-one-plotinus]] — the One above being, the apophatic divine principle, the source from which Intellect proceeds. Foundational for Avicennian Necessary-Being theology.
- [[henosis]] / mystical union — the soul's mystical ascent to union with the One. Foundational for the Avicennian intellect-Active-Intellect conjunction doctrine and for Sufi mystical-union vocabulary.
- [[soul-immortality]] — the rational soul's separability from body and survival of death.
- [[ascent-of-the-soul]] — the Plotinian soul-ascent through purification, dialectical reasoning, and final mystical conjunction.
- [[falsafa]] — the *Theology of Aristotle* is the foundational pseudo-Aristotelian text of Arabic Neoplatonism; "what falsafa thought Aristotle taught" can be substantially read off this text.

## Disputes
- **Long vs. short recension.** Two Arabic recensions exist: a "Long Recension" structurally close to the Plotinian source, and a "Vulgate" (shorter version) circulated more widely. Modern scholarship debates which is the original al-Kindi-circle production and which is the later excerpting / abbreviation. (Adamson 2002.)
- **Deliberate vs. accidental misattribution.** The mainstream contemporary view (Adamson 2002, Gutas 1998): the misattribution is an inherited transmission-history accident, not deliberate fraud. The al-Kindi circle was working with Greek source-catalogs that had already misattributed the Plotinian material in late-antique transmission. The al-Kindi team is editorial-cooperative with the misattribution but did not create it.
- **Why the misattribution was uncritically accepted.** Because the falsafa tradition expected Aristotle to have a theological-metaphysical capstone (the missing portion of *Metaphysics Λ*), and the *Theology* fits that expected slot. The Neoplatonic content of the *Theology* was read as legitimate-Aristotle's-mature-metaphysics rather than as foreign Plotinian content.
- **Impact of the 1519 Castellani identification.** Once Castellani correctly identifies the *Theology* as Plotinian, the falsafa tradition's "Aristotle" is retrospectively unveiled as Aristotle-plus-Plotinus. The 17th-c. European response is to read Avicennian-Averroist Aristotelianism through the corrected Plotinian frame (Ficino had separately translated Plotinus in 1492, making this correction practically possible). Modern Aristotle scholarship treats the falsafa "Aristotle" as a substantially Neoplatonized reading rather than as direct Aristotle.
