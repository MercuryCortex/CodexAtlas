# Shoku Nihongi

## What it is
The **Shoku Nihongi** ("Chronicle of Japan Continued") is the second of Japan's **Six National Histories** (*Rikkokushi*), compiled in Chinese annalistic style and presented to the court in 797. Continuing where the [[phase-8-020-nihon-shoki]] left off, it covers the years **697–791** — essentially the whole **Nara period** — recording the reigns of the emperors from Monmu through Kanmu, the building of the capitals at Nara and (briefly) Nagaoka, the Taihō legal codes, the great Buddhist projects (the Tōdaiji Great Buddha under Emperor Shōmu), and the political crises of the era, including the extraordinary career of the monk **Dōkyō** under Empress Kōken/Shōtoku. It is notable for preserving sixty-two **Senmyō** — imperial edicts recorded in Old Japanese rather than Chinese — which are priceless evidence for the early language and for Shinto-Buddhist royal ideology.

## Significance
The Shoku Nihongi is the single most important narrative source for the Nara period, the formative era of the Japanese imperial state and of state Buddhism. Its Senmyō edicts fuse Shinto sacral kingship with Buddhist and Chinese political theology, a key document of Japanese [[divine-kingship]]. The standard study of the Rikkokushi tradition it belongs to is Sakamoto (1991).
