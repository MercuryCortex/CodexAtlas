# Śiva Sūtras

## What it is
The foundational scripture of non-dualist Kashmir Shaivism — 77 short Sanskrit aphorisms in three sections (*āṇavopāya*, *śāktopāya*, *śāmbhavopāya* — three paths of liberation: individual-effort, energy-based, and direct-recognition-of-Śiva), revealed (per tradition) to the early-9th-c. Kashmiri Vasugupta. The text is among the most condensed and oracular Sanskrit philosophical-religious sūtras; it is the root scripture of the Trika and Pratyabhijñā philosophical schools and the foundational text from which the medieval Kashmir Shaiva tradition (culminating in Abhinavagupta ~1000 CE) develops.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Kashmir Valley under the Karkota dynasty (later Utpalas), early 9th c. — a period of state patronage of Shaiva, Vaishnava, and Buddhist institutions. The Lalitāditya-period (~8th c.) Kashmir intellectual culture had been at a peak; the 9th c. saw the Tantric textualization phase from which the Śiva Sūtras and the subsequent Pratyabhijñā philosophy emerge.
- **Religious milieu.** Tantric Buddhism (Vajrayāna), Tantric Shaivism (Krama, proto-Trika), Shaiva Siddhānta dualism, and Vaishnavism were all intensely active in 9th-c. Kashmir. The Śiva Sūtras articulates the radical non-dualist position within Tantric Shaivism — that Śiva is identical with the individual self's consciousness — against both dualist Shaivism and against the broader Brahmanical tradition.
- **Who was behind it.** Vasugupta — the early-9th-c. Kashmiri Shaiva master who received the sūtras (per tradition) in a divine vision after meditating on Śiva. The "revealed scripture" framing is theologically standard for Tantric texts; the historical Vasugupta is a documented compiler-redactor.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Kashmir manuscript transmission. The principal commentary (Kṣemarāja's *Śivasūtra-Vimarśinī*, ~early 11th c.) is virtually inseparable from the base text in subsequent reception. Modern Western reception substantially mediated by Lakshman Joo (1907–1991) → Sanderson, Dyczkowski, Singh, Muller-Ortega.

## Themes and motifs
- [[non-duality]] — radical: Śiva is identical with the individual self's consciousness; the apparent multiplicity of beings and worlds is the play (*spanda*) of the singular consciousness.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — liberation is *recognition* (the Pratyabhijñā doctrine that develops out of these sūtras): the realization that "I am Śiva" — the supreme consciousness.
- [[divine-feminine]] / [[divine-immanence]] — Śakti as the dynamic-energetic aspect of Śiva-consciousness; the cosmos is the immanent self-expression of the divine.
- **The three *upāyas* (paths)**: *āṇavopāya* (individual-effort path with ritual, mantra, posture), *śāktopāya* (energy/imagination-based path of contemplation), *śāmbhavopāya* (direct recognition of Śiva-consciousness — accessible only to those of strong *śaktipāta* / descent of grace). The three-path structure becomes a central organizing scheme in Abhinavagupta's *Tantrāloka*.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-5-014-abhinavagupta-tantraloka]] — *commentary-on* / *direct-influence*: the Śiva Sūtras is the root scripture; Abhinavagupta's *Tantrāloka* is the supreme medieval elaboration. The three-*upāya* organization of the *Tantrāloka* derives from the Śiva Sūtras.
- → [[phase-5-006-vijnana-bhairava-tantra]] — *parallel-motif* / *shared-milieu*: contemporary non-dualist Kashmir Tantric text; the VBT supplies meditative practices (*dhāraṇās*) within the same non-dual Shaiva framework.
- → [[utpaladeva]]'s *Īśvara-Pratyabhijñā-kārikā* (~900–950) — *direct-influence*: the Pratyabhijñā doctrine that liberation is *recognition* of one's identity with Śiva is the philosophical development of the Śiva Sūtras's *śāmbhavopāya*.
- → Mahāyāna Tantric Buddhism (Vajrayāna) — *parallel-motif* / *shared-milieu*: 9th-c. Kashmir was a major Vajrayāna center; the structural parallels (deity-yoga, mantra, mūdra, the recognition-of-Buddha-nature as one's own awareness) are extensive. Sanderson 2009 ("The Śaiva Age") argues the direction was substantially Śaiva → Buddhist (a contested thesis).
- → [[phase-2-012-brihadaranyaka-upanishad]] — *parallel-motif*: the Upaniṣadic *tat tvam asi* / *ahaṃ brahmāsmi* identification of the self and ultimate reality finds a Tantric-Shaiva instantiation in the Śiva Sūtras. The Upaniṣadic tradition is one acknowledged source; the Tantric framing is distinctive.

## Disputes
- **Vasugupta's historicity**: documented; the revelation-narrative is theological framing.
- **Date**: ~800–850 is the standard placement, constrained by Kallaṭa (Vasugupta's disciple) → Somānanda → Utpaladeva genealogy.
- **The "Vimala" commentary tradition**: there are two distinct commentary traditions on the Śiva Sūtras — Kṣemarāja's *Vimarśinī* (the dominant one) and Bhāskara's *Vārttika*. The two traditions interpret the sūtras differently in places.
