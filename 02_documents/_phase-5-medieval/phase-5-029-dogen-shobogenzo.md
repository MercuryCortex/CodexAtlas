# Shōbōgenzō (Dōgen)

## What it is
The **Treasury of the True Dharma Eye** — the masterwork of **Eihei Dōgen** (1200–1253), founder of Japanese **Sōtō Zen**, and one of the most original works of religious philosophy in the East Asian tradition. Composed in difficult, luminous **vernacular Japanese** (a striking departure in an age when serious Buddhist writing was done in Sino-Japanese), its fascicles meditate on Buddha-nature, practice, and language with extraordinary depth. Its central teachings are **shikantaza** ("just sitting") and the unity of **practice and realization** (*shushō-ittō*) — the insistence that zazen is not a means to enlightenment but enlightenment enacted. The fascicle **Uji** ("Being-Time") advances a radical philosophy in which time and existence are identical, each moment a complete and dynamic whole.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Kamakura Japan, the era of the new "Kamakura Buddhism" (Pure Land, Nichiren, Zen) reaching beyond the aristocracy.
- **Religious milieu.** Dōgen brought the Caodong (Sōtō) lineage of Chinese Chan to Japan after training under **Tiantong Rujing**, and wrote to establish an authentic transmission against what he saw as a lax Zen establishment.
- **Who was behind it.** Dōgen himself, lecturing to his community at Kōshōji and Eiheiji; the work was assembled from these teachings.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Left unfinished and transmitted in competing recensions; long an in-house Sōtō text, it became, in the 20th century, a touchstone of world philosophy.

## Themes and motifs
- [[anatman-no-self]] — Dōgen radicalizes the no-self teaching ("to study the self is to forget the self," *Genjōkōan*) into a philosophy of total dynamic presence.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-5-004-platform-sutra-huineng]] — *develops*: Dōgen stands in the Chan lineage the Platform Sutra mythologizes, but transforms its sudden-awakening rhetoric into the practice-realization of just sitting.
- → [[tradition-zen]] / [[dogen]] — *founding-text-of*: the Shōbōgenzō is the doctrinal foundation of Sōtō Zen.

## Disputes
- **The recensions.** The relationship among the 75-, 60-, 12-, and 95-fascicle versions, and Dōgen's own final intentions, are central textual problems (Bielefeldt 1988).
- **Philosophy vs. religion.** Whether the Shōbōgenzō is best read as systematic philosophy (the Kyoto School, Heidegger comparisons) or strictly as religious instruction is debated (Kim 2004).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **A medieval Zen master in the conversation of world philosophy.** Dōgen's *Uji* ("Being-Time") so closely engages the problems of time, being, and presence that 20th-century thinkers placed it beside Heidegger; the Shōbōgenzō is the clearest case of a classical East Asian religious text entering the Western philosophical canon on its own terms (Kim 2004).
- **The Chan-to-Zen transmission, documented.** Dōgen's record of his Chinese training under Rujing and his polemics for authentic transmission make the Shōbōgenzō a primary witness to how the Chinese Chan of the [[phase-5-004-platform-sutra-huineng|Platform Sutra]] lineage became Japanese Sōtō Zen — a transmission-spine the Atlas traces (Bielefeldt 1988).
