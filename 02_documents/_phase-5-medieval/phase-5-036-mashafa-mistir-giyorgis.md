# Mashafa Mistir (Book of the Mystery)

## What it is
The **Mashafa Mistir** (literally "Book of the Mystery" / "Book of the Hidden Things") is the major systematic-theological work of medieval Ethiopian Christianity, composed in Ge'ez by [[giyorgis-of-sagla]] (Abba Giyorgis II, 1365–1425) in the late 14th to early 15th century, completed by 1424. It consists of **30 *dərsanat* (discourses)** organized around (a) refutation of ten named heretical positions and (b) systematic exposition of Tewahedo Trinitarian and Christological doctrine. The work cites extensively from the Old and New Testaments (especially Pauline epistles and Johannine literature), from Cyrillian and Athanasian patristics, and from earlier Ethiopian theological literature — making it both a doctrinal summa and a witness to the patristic-textual ecology of medieval Ethiopia.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Solomonic Ethiopia under emperors Dawit I (r. 1382–1413) and Yeshaq I (r. 1414–1429). Giyorgis served at court; he was briefly imprisoned over theological disputes but rehabilitated. The work is composed in the long lead-up to the [[zara-yaqob-emperor]] theological-centralization era (1434–1468), and Giyorgis's framework provides the doctrinal scaffolding Zar'a Ya'qob will build on.
- **Religious milieu.** Medieval Ethiopian Christianity in active engagement with heretical movements (real or perceived): the *Stephanites* / Estifanosites, the Saturday-Sabbath Ewostathians, and ongoing internal-Trinitarian and Christological disputes. The 14th–15th c. is one of the most theologically productive periods of Ethiopian Christianity.
- **Who was behind it.** [[giyorgis-of-sagla]] in his own systematic exposition. The work is undisputedly his — multiple manuscripts attribute it, the style and theological framework are consistent across his other works (*Arganona Maryam*, *Saatat*, *Wəddase Mäsqäl*), and the post-mortem hagiographic tradition is unanimous.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Ethiopian monastic transmission. Critical-edited Yaqob Beyene 1990–1993 (CSCO, 4 vols, Ge'ez text + Italian translation).

## Themes and motifs
- [[tewahedo-christology]] — Giyorgis's central concern: the Cyrillian *mia physis* formulation against (a) Chalcedonian dyophysitism and (b) Eutychian extremism.
- [[trinitarian-formula]] — Athanasian-Cappadocian Trinitarian theology in Ge'ez systematic form.
- [[ethiopian-systematic-theology]] — the founding work of the genre in Ge'ez.
- [[orthodoxy-vs-heresy]] — Giyorgis names ten heresies and refutes each: Arian, Sabellian, Macedonian (Pneumatomachian), Nestorian, Eutychian, Manichaean, Marcionite, *Anjur* (the Christological position of those Giyorgis identifies as in error within the Ethiopian church itself), and others.

## Disputes
- **Date of completion.** Most sources date to before Giyorgis's death (1425); the precise composition window is c. 1390s–1420s.
- **Identification of the "Anjur" heresy.** Variously interpreted as a specific Ethiopian Christological dissent group, as a label for a strand of opposition Giyorgis faced personally, or as a generic-rhetorical heretical placeholder. Bausi 2005 surveys.
- **Extent of original composition vs. anthology.** The work cites extensively from earlier sources; some scholarship has framed it as substantially florilegium-like; the consensus is that it is genuine systematic composition with extensive scriptural/patristic citation.
