# Śrībhāṣya (Ramanuja's Brahma-Sūtra Commentary)

## What it is
Rāmānuja's systematic commentary on the same *Brahmasūtra* that Śaṅkara had commented on three centuries earlier, but reading it to defend an entirely different metaphysics — *Viśiṣṭādvaita*, "qualified non-dualism" — in which Brahman is real, personal (= Viṣṇu / Nārāyaṇa), and ontologically prior, while individual souls (*jīvas*) and matter (*prakṛti*) are real modes (*prakāra*) or "body" of Brahman. The world is not *māyā* (illusion to be transcended) but the body of God, dependent on God yet ontologically distinct. The decisive theological move is the doctrine of the *aprthak-siddhi* (inseparable existence) of souls and matter from Brahman — non-dualism that nevertheless preserves real difference. Liberation is not realization "I am Brahman" but loving service (*kainkarya*) to Viṣṇu, attained through devotion (*bhakti*) culminating in self-surrender (*prapatti*). The *Śrībhāṣya* is the foundational systematic statement of bhakti-theistic Vedānta — the philosophical backbone of the entire Vaiṣṇava theological tradition.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The Cōḻa Empire dominated Tamil South India; Rāmānuja was active in the Cōḻa heartland (Śrīraṅgam, Kāñcīpuram). The Vaiṣṇava and Śaiva sects competed for royal patronage; Rāmānuja was at one point exiled to the Hoysala territory in Karnataka (where he is credited with the conversion of the Hoysala king Bittideva → Viṣṇuvardhana) and returned to Śrīraṅgam later in life.
- **Religious milieu.** The Tamil bhakti movement of the Āḻvārs (~600–900) had produced the *Divya Prabandham* — 4,000 Tamil devotional verses to Viṣṇu and his consorts — which formed the experiential ground that Rāmānuja's Sanskrit theology systematized. Śaṅkara's Advaita Vedānta had been the dominant intellectual position for 300 years; Rāmānuja's project is the systematic theistic Vedāntic alternative, drawing on the Pāñcarātra Āgamas (the Vaiṣṇava Tantric literature) and the *Bhagavadgītā* as scriptural anchors against Advaita.
- **Who was behind it.** Rāmānuja: born Śrīperumbudūr near Madras; studied initially with the Advaita teacher Yādavaprakāśa, broke with him over the interpretation of the *Chāndogya* passage about the Lord's nature; succeeded Yāmuna (his unmet teacher who died before they could meet, having left three undelivered instructions) as the head of the Śrīraṅgam sampradāya. His traditional dates 1017–1137 give an implausibly long lifespan (120 years); some scholars argue the dates are hagiographic compression of multiple figures or simply round-numbered.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous within Śrī Vaiṣṇavism. After Rāmānuja, the school split (~14th c.) into the Vaḍakalai (Northern, more Sanskritic, emphasizing the *kitten*-model of salvation by effort) and Teṅkalai (Southern, more Tamil, emphasizing the *monkey*-model of salvation by grace). Both branches treat the *Śrībhāṣya* as authoritative.

## Themes and motifs
- [[non-duality]] — but qualified: *Viśiṣṭādvaita*. Brahman is one without a second, *but the One has internal articulation* — souls and matter as God's body. Rāmānuja accepts the *advaita* word while rejecting Śaṅkara's reading of it.
- [[creation-by-word]] — Brahman creates the cosmos through volitional thought; the world is real, not illusory; creation is a real act, not the mere appearance of multiplicity in unchanging Brahman.
- [[chosen-people]] — Rāmānuja's universalism is famous (the legend that he shouted Tirumantra from the temple roof to all castes), but operationally Śrī Vaiṣṇavism is a sampradāya: bhakti is mediated through teacher-lineages and ritual community.
- [[soul-immortality]] — individual souls are *eternally distinct* from Brahman, *eternally dependent on* Brahman, *eternally* in some relation of love-service.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-5-005-shankara-brahma-sutra-bhasya]] — *polemic-against*: the *Śrībhāṣya* is composed *explicitly* to refute Śaṅkara's *Bhāṣya* on the same root text. Both commentaries comment on Sūtra 1.1.1: Śaṅkara reads it Advaita; Rāmānuja reads it *Viśiṣṭādvaita*. The polemic is sustained throughout.
- → [[phase-5-022-madhva-brahma-sutra-bhasya]] — *polemic-against* (reverse direction): Madhva (~1240) refutes Rāmānuja from the dualist side — against both Advaita and Viśiṣṭādvaita.
- → the *Bhagavadgītā* — *direct-quote*: Rāmānuja also wrote a *Gītā-Bhāṣya*, and the *Gītā*'s synthesis of jñāna, karma, and bhakti is the textual ground of *Viśiṣṭādvaita*.
- → Tamil Āḻvārs (esp. Nammāḻvār's *Tiruvāymoḻi*) — *direct-influence*: the experiential devotional content of Śrī Vaiṣṇavism comes from the Āḻvārs; Rāmānuja sanskritizes and systematizes.
- → Pāñcarātra Āgamas — *direct-influence*: Rāmānuja defends the orthodoxy of the Pāñcarātra (rejected by some smārta Brahmins) and integrates its ritual theology.

## Disputes
- **Dates.** Traditional 1017–1137 (120 years); modern scholars often shorten to ~1077–1157 or accept the longer dates with caution.
- **Relationship to bhakti vs. prapatti.** Whether Rāmānuja taught *bhakti* (active loving devotion as the means) or *prapatti* (self-surrender to grace as the means) as the primary path is the foundational issue dividing Vaḍakalai and Teṅkalai sub-schools.
