# Dunhuang Manuscripts

## What it is
In 1900 a sealed chamber — **Cave 17**, the "library cave" — was discovered in the **Mogao** Buddhist grottoes at **Dunhuang** on the Silk Road. It contained roughly **50,000 manuscripts and printed documents**, sealed around the year 1000 CE and preserved by the desert climate for nine centuries. The overwhelming majority are Buddhist scriptures in Chinese and Tibetan, but the cache also holds Daoist, Manichaean, and Nestorian-Christian texts, as well as contracts, letters, school primers, and divination manuals — a complete cross-section of medieval Silk-Road life and religion. Among its treasures is the **Diamond Sūtra of 868 CE** ([[phase-5-002b-diamond-sutra]]), the world's oldest dated, complete printed book. Large portions were removed to London and Paris by Aurel Stein and Paul Pelliot after 1907, and the manuscripts are now dispersed across the world's libraries.

## Significance
The Dunhuang manuscripts are one of the greatest manuscript discoveries in history — they transformed the study of medieval Chinese Buddhism, recovered lost texts and vernacular literature, and pushed back the documented history of printing. Because the cache preserves Buddhist, Daoist, Manichaean, and Christian writings side by side, it is also a uniquely direct witness to the religious pluralism of the Silk Road, and the foundation of the modern discipline of **Dunhuang studies** (*Dunhuangxue*).
