# Emunot ve-Deot

## What it is
The first systematic philosophical theology in Judaism, written in Judeo-Arabic in 933 by Saʿadia ben Yosef, the Gaon of the Sura academy. The work has ten treatises: (1) creation — proves the world was created ex nihilo, refuting eternalist positions; (2) God's unity — proves divine oneness, refutes anthropomorphism, dualism, and trinitarianism; (3) revelation and command; (4) human moral capacity and divine justice; (5) merit, demerit, and afterlife consequence; (6) the soul and its powers; (7) resurrection of the dead; (8) the messianic age; (9) the world to come; (10) ethics and life-conduct. The method is broadly that of Muʿtazilite Kalām, the rationalist Islamic theology then dominant in Baghdad: arguments from reason are deployed to defend received tradition, with reason and revelation framed as parallel and mutually reinforcing paths to the same truths.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late Abbasid Caliphate; the Caliph al-Muqtadir (r. 908–932) and his successors. Geonic-period Jewry was concentrated in Iraq under Babylonian-academy authority (Sura, Pumbedita) and was beginning to lose centrality as the diaspora developed competing centers (Andalusia, Kairouan, Italy, Rhineland).
- **Religious milieu.** The Karaite-Rabbanite controversy was at its peak — Karaite Jews (founded ~760 by Anan ben David) rejected the Oral Torah and the Talmud. Saʿadia was the foremost Rabbanite polemicist against Karaism. The Islamic intellectual environment was the Muʿtazilite Kalām (rationalist theology defending divine justice and the createdness of the Qur'an) — Saʿadia's method is openly Muʿtazilite-influenced, against later Sunni traditionalism's eventual victory inside Islam.
- **Who was behind it.** Saʿadia Gaon: born Egypt, educated in Tiberias and Palestine, eventually appointed Gaon (head) of the Sura academy in Iraq (928). His career was politically turbulent — he was deposed and later restored. Translated the Hebrew Bible into Arabic (the *Tafsīr*); wrote a Hebrew grammar (the first), a prayerbook (*Siddur*), polemical works against Karaism, halakhic responsa.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Hebrew translation by Yehudah ibn Tibbon (1186) at Lunel in Provence brought the work into European Jewish thought; cited extensively by Abraham ibn Daud, Maimonides (qualifiedly — Maimonides considers Saʿadia philosophically unsophisticated by his standard), Naḥmanides, and later commentators. First printed Constantinople 1562.

## Themes and motifs
- [[creation-by-word]] — Saʿadia argues creation *ex nihilo* by philosophical proofs (four cosmological-style arguments), defending the traditional Jewish reading of Genesis against Aristotelian eternalism.
- [[soul-immortality]] — treatise 6 develops a substance-dualist account drawing on Galen and Kalām discussions.
- [[afterlife-judgment]] — treatises 5, 7–9: divine reward and punishment, bodily resurrection, the messianic age, and the world-to-come distinguished as separate stages.
- [[messianism]] — Saʿadia's rationalist messianism distinguishes natural/historical and supernatural redemptive moments; he calculated a messianic date which passed without event.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-5-019-maimonides-guide-for-the-perplexed]] — *direct-influence* (qualified): Maimonides knew Saʿadia thoroughly and built on his project of philosophical theology, but treated his philosophy as superseded by Aristotelianism. Maimonides occupies the Saʿadian slot of "the philosopher-Gaon" in later memory.
- → [[phase-4-033-babylonian-talmud]] — *commentary-on*: Saʿadia operates within rabbinic-Talmudic Judaism but defends it with non-Talmudic philosophical apparatus.
- → Muʿtazilite Kalām (al-Jubbāʾī, etc.) — *shared-milieu* / *direct-influence*: the technical apparatus of *kalām al-tawḥīd wa-l-ʿadl* (theology of unity and justice) is openly borrowed.
- → Karaite anti-Rabbanism — *polemic-against*: Saʿadia's defense of the Talmud and the Oral Torah was decisive in containing Karaite influence within the Rabbanite mainstream.

## Disputes
- **Saʿadia's philosophical originality.** Was he merely transposing Muʿtazilite Kalām, or developing a distinctively Jewish philosophical theology? Both views have defenders.
- **Messianic calculation.** Treatise 8 attempts a date for the messiah's coming (~968 CE). The failure of this prediction is a perennial topic in critical discussions of Saʿadia.
