# Brahma-Sūtra-Bhāṣya (Madhva)

## What it is
Madhva's commentary on the same *Brahmasūtra* commented on three and four centuries earlier by Śaṅkara (Advaita) and Ramanuja (Viśiṣṭādvaita), reading it now from the position of strict theistic dualism (*Dvaita*). Madhva's metaphysics affirms *five eternal differences* (*pañca-bheda*): God–soul, God–matter, soul–soul, soul–matter, matter–matter — all real and irreducible. Brahman = Viṣṇu is supremely real, supremely independent (*svatantra*); everything else is dependent (*paratantra*) but no less real. There is no *māyā* hiding a unity behind a plurality (against Śaṅkara); souls and matter are not modes / *prakāra* of Brahman (against Ramanuja). Souls are eternally distinct, and crucially, *eternally differentiated in capacity*: some are eligible for liberation (*muktiyogya*), some are eternally bound (*nityasaṃsārin*), and some are eternally damned (*tamoyogya*). This last position — predestinarian theology of three distinct soul-classes including eternal damnation — is unique in Indian philosophy and structurally parallel to Calvinist double predestination, a parallel often noted in comparative theology. The *Bhāṣya* and Madhva's other 36 works constitute the foundational corpus of the third great Vedāntic school.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Hoysala and early Vijayanagara South India. Karnataka under the late Hoysalas was a center of religious patronage; the founding of Vijayanagara in 1336 — *after* Madhva's death — would inaugurate a great age of Mādhva intellectual flourishing (Vyāsatīrtha was a guru of Krishnadevarāya).
- **Religious milieu.** Madhva positions himself against Advaita (Śaṅkara's school) as the dominant philosophical position and Viśiṣṭādvaita (Ramanuja's) as a half-measure. His tone is markedly polemical — he calls the Advaitins *māyāvādins* (illusionists) and argues their position is essentially crypto-Buddhist. The contemporaneous emergence of Tamil Śaiva Siddhānta also articulates a dualist theological position, but Madhva's school operates within Vaiṣṇavism.
- **Who was behind it.** Madhva (also known as Ānanda Tīrtha, Pūrṇa Prajña, Vāsudeva): from the South Kanara coast of Karnataka; took saṃnyāsa young; debated his way to fame across South India; founded the Udupi Krishna temple (still the principal Mādhva pilgrimage center); claimed to be an incarnation of Vāyu (the wind god), the third incarnation in a series — Hanumān (in the Rāmāyaṇa), Bhīma (in the Mahābhārata), and now Madhva. His traditional dates 1238–1317 are widely accepted, with some scholars proposing 1199–1278.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous within the Mādhva sampradāya. The eight Udupi *maṭhas* and the broader Mādhva monastic network preserve the corpus. The school's intellectual peak came with Vyāsatīrtha at Vijayanagara (~1500); the Haridāsa devotional poets (Purandara Dāsa, Kanaka Dāsa) brought Mādhva theology into Kannada-language popular religion.

## Themes and motifs
- [[ethical-dualism]] — *Pañca-bheda*: five eternal differences. Real plurality is metaphysically irreducible.
- [[chosen-people]] / *three soul classes* — the doctrine of *tāratamya* (hierarchical gradation) extends across souls; some are eternally destined for liberation, others for eternal bondage, others for eternal damnation. This is the most striking doctrine in Madhva's system and the one that most sharply differentiates Dvaita from all other Indian theologies.
- [[soul-immortality]] — individual souls are eternally distinct, eternally real, and (for the elect) eternally in service relation to Viṣṇu in liberation.
- [[afterlife-judgment]] — eternal heaven *and* eternal hell for the *tamoyogya*.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-5-005-shankara-brahma-sutra-bhasya]] — *polemic-against*: extensive and sharp refutation; Madhva treats Śaṅkara's position as functionally Buddhist nihilism with Vedāntic cosmetics.
- → [[phase-5-016-ramanuja-sribhasya]] — *polemic-against*: Madhva refutes Ramanuja's *Viśiṣṭādvaita* for being a half-measure that fails to acknowledge the irreducible reality of difference.
- → the *Brahmasūtra*, *Upaniṣads*, *Bhagavadgītā* — *commentary-on*: all interpreted to support Dvaita; Madhva is famously creative (or capricious, depending on the critic) in his philology, finding alternative root meanings to support his readings.
- → Pāñcarātra and Bhāgavata traditions — *direct-influence*: Madhva's theological focus on Viṣṇu / Nārāyaṇa and on the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* connects him to broader Vaiṣṇava devotional currents.
- → Calvinist double predestination — *parallel-motif* (no causal influence): the structural parallel between Madhva's three soul-classes and Calvinist predestination is consistently noted in comparative theology; the two doctrines develop independently in distinct continents and traditions.
- → Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Chaitanya, ~1500) — *direct-influence?* (disputed): Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism claims descent from a Mādhva sub-lineage; modern Mādhva scholarship treats this claim cautiously.

## Disputes
- **Madhva's lost-source citations.** Mesquita 2000 demonstrates philologically that many citations Madhva attributes to "lost Vedic" texts cannot be securely traced to any extant or attested source; the question is whether Madhva had access to lost recensions or was creatively constructing authorities. The work is controversial within Mādhva scholarship.
- **Dates.** Traditional 1238–1317 is generally accepted; older alternative dating (1199–1278) is occasionally defended.
- **Eternal damnation.** Whether Madhva's *nitya-naraka* doctrine is unique or has Indian antecedents (some Tantric texts contain hierarchical-eternal categories) is occasionally argued; the Mādhva position is by far the most explicit.
