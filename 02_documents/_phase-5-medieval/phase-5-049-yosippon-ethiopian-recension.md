# Yosippon / Joseph ben Gurion (Zena Ayhud — Ethiopian recension)

## What it is
**Yosippon** (also *Sefer Yosippon*, "Book of Yosippon") is a medieval Hebrew chronicle of Second-Temple-period Jewish history composed in 10th-c. Byzantine southern Italy (probably Naples or Capua), pseudonymously attributed to **Joseph ben Gurion ha-Kohen** — a literary conflation of the Hellenistic-Jewish historian Josephus Flavius and the Hasmonean-era Joseph ben Gurion. The text recapitulates and reworks Josephus's *Antiquities* and *Jewish War*, the Latin Pseudo-Hegesippus *De Excidio Hierosolymitano* (a 4th-c. Latin paraphrase of Josephus), and 1–2 Maccabees (LXX), producing a Hebrew-language history of the Hasmonean revolt, Herodian period, and the Roman war of 66–73 CE. It became one of the most influential medieval Jewish historical works.

The **Geʼez Zena Ayhud** ("History of the Jews") is the Ethiopian recension of Yosippon, translated from Arabic (which was translated from Hebrew) in approximately the 15th century. It is **canonical scripture in [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]]** — making the Ethiopian tradition the *only Christian tradition to elevate Yosippon to canonical-scriptural status*. The Geʼez recension is a substantially elaborated and reshaped form of the Hebrew original, with Ethiopian-specific theological framing.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** 10th-c. Byzantine southern Italy for the Hebrew composition (a Greek-Jewish-Latin cultural intersection that produced multiple consequential medieval Jewish texts); 15th-c. Solomonic Ethiopia for the Geʼez recension (the era of canonical-textual consolidation under [[zara-yaqob-emperor]]).
- **Religious milieu.** Medieval Hebrew historical writing developing during a period when continuous Jewish access to Josephus's Greek text had been lost; Yosippon reconstructs Second-Temple history for Hebrew-speaking medieval Jews. The Ethiopian reception treats this Hebrew chronicle as canonical-historical-scripture — an unusual move within Christian canon-formation.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous 10th-c. Italian-Jewish compiler. The pseudonymous attribution to *Joseph ben Gurion* combines Josephus's name with a Hasmonean general's name. The Geʼez translator-redactor is anonymous.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Ethiopian-canonical use for the Geʼez Zena Ayhud. Western scholarly engagement with the Hebrew original began with Münster's 1541 Latin translation; the Geʼez recension was first systematically studied by van Donzel 1969.

## Themes and motifs
- [[ethiopic-canon-broad]] — among the most distinctive Ethiopian-canonical additions to the broader OT, particularly because it is *medieval* (10th c.) rather than late-Second-Temple-era in composition.
- [[martyrdom-theology]] — the Maccabean martyrs (heavily elaborated in Yosippon, drawing on 2/4 Maccabees) are theologically central.
- [[messianic-revelation]] — the Hasmonean-restoration narrative carries Davidic-messianic resonance.

## Disputes
- **Date of the Hebrew original.** Most scholarship places it in the 10th c. (Flusser 1978); some scholars argue for slightly earlier or later. The general 10th-c. Italian-Jewish dating is consensus.
- **Date and source-history of the Geʼez recension.** Approximately 15th c. (Solomonic-era canonical consolidation), with Arabic-language intermediation. The exact translator and translation-context are not securely recoverable.
- **Canonical status in Ethiopia.** Zena Ayhud is canonical-scriptural in Tewahedo; precise canonical-scriptural status varies slightly across recensions.
