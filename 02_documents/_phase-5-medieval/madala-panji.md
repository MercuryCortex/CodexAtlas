# Mādalā Pañji

## What it is
The **Mādalā Pañji** is the running chronicle of the **Jagannātha temple** at Puri in Odisha — a continuously-maintained record, kept on palm-leaf by the temple's hereditary record-keepers, of the temple's rituals, endowments, renovations, and above all the deeds of the kings who served the god [[jagannath]] as his earthly deputies. Combining temple administration with dynastic history, it narrates the reigns of the Gajapati and earlier dynasties of Odisha, the periodic renewal of the wooden images (*Nabakalebara*), invasions and desecrations of the shrine, and the ritual calendar of the cult. Its layered, accreted character makes its early sections legendary and its later sections increasingly documentary — much like the Sri Lankan [[mahavamsa]] to which it is often compared as a temple-centred regional chronicle.

## Significance
The Mādalā Pañji is the central indigenous source for the history of the Jagannātha cult and the medieval kingdoms of Odisha, and a striking example of a temple itself functioning as a state archive: the chronicle assumes that the true sovereign of Odisha is the god Jagannātha, with the human king ruling as his servant — a theology of [[divine-kingship]] in which the temple, not the palace, is the seat of legitimacy. It is analysed at length in the standard scholarly study of the Jagannātha tradition (Eschmann, Kulke & Tripathi, eds., 1978).
