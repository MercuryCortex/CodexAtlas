# Book of Nehemiah

## What it is
The companion to Ezra, built around the vivid first-person memoir of Nehemiah, a Jewish cupbearer to the Persian king Artaxerxes who secures a commission to rebuild the ruined walls of Jerusalem. Against fierce local opposition he completes the walls in fifty-two days, then joins Ezra in a great public reading of the Torah and a covenant renewal that re-founds the community's discipline.

## Primary text

### Nehemiah 8:8
*Trans. NRSV*
> So they read from the book, from the law of God, with interpretation. They gave the sense, so that the people understood the reading.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Persian Yehud; Nehemiah governs as an imperial appointee, his memoir a rare ancient first-person political document.
- **Religious milieu.** The covenant-renewal assembly of Nehemiah 8–10 — Torah read aloud and interpreted to the assembled people — is a foundational moment for what becomes synagogue and scriptural Judaism.
- **Discovery / transmission.** One book with Ezra in the Hebrew tradition.

## Themes and motifs
- Rebuilding and boundary — the walls as the physical sign of a reconstituted community.
- Torah read and interpreted — Nehemiah 8 as the charter of scriptural exposition.
- [[divine-kingship]] — YHWH's providence operating through Persian imperial sanction.
