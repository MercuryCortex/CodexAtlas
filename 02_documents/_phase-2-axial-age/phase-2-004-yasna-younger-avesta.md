# Yasna and Younger Avesta

## What it is
The Younger Avestan corpus — composed and accreted around the older Gathic core over several centuries — comprises (1) the **Yasna**, a 72-chapter liturgy that enwraps the Gathas as its central holy text; (2) the **Yashts**, 21 hymns each addressed to an individual *yazata* (worship-worthy being — Mithra, Anāhitā, Verethraghna, Tištrya, etc.); (3) the **Visperad**, extension prayers used in major festivals; and (4) the **Vendidad** (Vidēvdād, "law against the daēvas"), a priestly code of purity and demonology. These texts elaborate the Gathic dualism into a full theological system: seven Amesha Spentas around Ahura Mazda, a hierarchy of yazatas, a structured demonology, and explicit eschatology.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Composition spans the late second through mid first millennium BCE, crossing the rise of the Median and especially the Achaemenid (550–330 BCE) Persian empire. By the Achaemenid period Zoroastrianism (in something like its developed form) is the religion of the imperial dynasty (Darius's Bisotun inscription invokes Ahura Mazda).
- **Religious milieu.** The Younger Avesta re-admits, in the Yashts, several inherited Indo-Iranian deities that the Gathas had elided — most importantly Mithra (Yt. 10), Anāhitā (Yt. 5, "Ābān Yašt"), and Verethraghna (Yt. 14). These are subordinated to Ahura Mazda as yazatas, not co-equal gods; the move is integrative rather than reverting to polytheism.
- **Who was behind it.** Generations of priestly redactors of the *mōbed* lineages. The Vendidad in particular reads as a priestly handbook for the management of pollution (corpse-handling, menstruation, dogs, fire).
- **Discovery / transmission.** The Sasanian dynasty (224–651 CE) compiled the Great Avesta (21 *nasks*), most of which was lost after the Islamic conquest of Iran (~651 CE). The surviving liturgical texts were carried by Zoroastrian priestly diaspora into India (Parsis from ~9th c. CE onward). Modern Western scholarship begins with Anquetil-Duperron (1771).

## Themes and motifs
- [[dualism-cosmic]] — the structured opposition of Ahura Mazda + the Amesha Spentas + yazatas vs. Angra Mainyu + the daēvas, formalized in the Younger Avesta.
- [[ethical-dualism]] — extended into ritual purity: the Vendidad codifies physical/moral purity as the right-hand alignment with Ahura Mazda.
- [[end-times-judgment]] — the Chinvat Bridge, Saoshyant, frashokereti, resurrection, and final renewal of the world are explicit in the Younger Avesta and Pahlavi books.
- [[afterlife-judgment]] — Hadhōkht Nask preserves the soul's journey after death and its encounter with its own *daēnā* (conscience embodied as maiden).
- [[divine-council]] — Ahura Mazda surrounded by the Amesha Spentas (Vohu Manah, Aša Vahišta, Xšaθra Vairya, Spənta Ārmaiti, Haurvatāt, Amərətāt) as cosmic and ethical aspects.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]] — *commentary-on* / *redaction-of*: the Younger Avesta enshrines, surrounds, and develops the Gathic teaching; the Yasna liturgy literally encloses the Gathas as its sacred center.
- → [[phase-2-001-rig-veda-family-books]] — *shared-milieu*: Mithra (Yt. 10) ↔ Vedic Mitra; Haoma ↔ Soma; *yasna* ↔ *yajña*. The Avestan re-admission of Mithra as yazata mirrors structures preserved in Vedic religion.
- → Second Temple Jewish texts (e.g., 1 Enoch, Daniel, sectarian Qumran — Phase 3) — *shared-milieu*: organized angelology (Michael/Gabriel/Raphael/Uriel paralleling Amesha Spentas), dualistic Sons-of-Light vs Sons-of-Darkness (Qumran's War Scroll), final judgment, bodily resurrection. The Persian period (539–332 BCE) provides the historical contact zone. Ref: Boyce & Grenet 1991; Shaked 1984; Barr 1985.
- → Achaemenid royal inscriptions (Bisotun, Persepolis) — *shared-milieu*: Darius and Xerxes invoke Ahura Mazda in formal contexts congruent with Younger Avestan theology. Ref: Kent 1953, *Old Persian*.
- → Manichaean cosmogony (Phase 4) — *direct-influence*: Mani's dualism explicitly draws on Zoroastrian categories (Ahura Mazda / Ahriman → Father of Greatness / King of Darkness). Ref: Asmussen 1975, *Manichaean Literature*.

## Disputes
- **Dating internal layers.** The relative chronology of Yashts is contested; Yt. 10 (Mithra) and Yt. 5 (Anāhitā) contain demonstrably old material (Mithra's Indo-Iranian inheritance) packaged within a later frame. Skjærvø, Hintze, Kellens differ on stratification.
- **Vendidad's date.** The Vendidad is often dated very late within the Younger Avestan period; some scholars treat it as Arsacid (~250 BCE–224 CE) on linguistic grounds. Boyce defends a substantially earlier core.
- **Were the Achaemenids Zoroastrian?** Strict definitions (Gathic) vs. loose definitions (Mazdayasnian) yield different answers. Most scholars say yes in a broad sense; some (Henkelman) emphasize continuity with older Iranian polytheism.
