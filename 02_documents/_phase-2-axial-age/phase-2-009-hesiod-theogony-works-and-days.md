# Hesiod — Theogony and Works and Days

## What it is
Two short Archaic Greek hexameter poems by Hesiod of Ascra (Boeotia). The **Theogony** (~1,022 lines) gives the systematic genealogy of the Greek gods, from primordial Chaos through Gaia, Uranus, Kronos, and the Olympians — including the succession-myth narrative of Uranus castrated by Kronos, Kronos overthrown by Zeus, and Zeus securing cosmic kingship by defeating the Titans and Typhoeus. The **Works and Days** (~828 lines) is didactic wisdom-poetry addressed to Hesiod's brother Perses, containing the Pandora myth, the myth of the Five Ages (Gold, Silver, Bronze, Heroic, Iron), and agricultural / ethical / calendrical advice. Together with Homer, Hesiod is the foundational textual source for the Greek pantheon.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late 8th / early 7th c. BCE Boeotia, a rural agricultural region of mainland Greece. The Works and Days describes the world of a small landowning farmer locked in dispute with his brother over inheritance, complaining about corrupt "bribe-swallowing kings".
- **Religious milieu.** Pre-philosophical Greek polytheism. Hesiod systematizes inherited oral mythology into a coherent succession-and-genealogy structure. His sources are partly Greek (Mycenaean residues, ongoing oral tradition), partly Near Eastern (Hittite Kumarbi cycle, Mesopotamian/Levantine cosmogony, via Anatolian and Phoenician contact).
- **Who was behind it.** Hesiod names himself (Theog. 22–34), describes his Muses' commissioning on Mount Helicon, and gives autobiographical detail in Works and Days. He is generally regarded as a real historical poet, slightly later than (or contemporary with) the monumental Homer.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Greek scholarly transmission. Significantly informed by 20th-century discoveries: the Hittite Kumarbi cycle (deciphered from Boğazköy tablets in the 1930s–40s) revealed close structural parallels to Hesiod's Uranus-Kronos-Zeus succession.

## Themes and motifs
- **Succession myth** — Uranus (Sky) is castrated by his son Kronos with a sickle Gaia provides; Kronos is overthrown by his son Zeus; Zeus defeats the Titans and Typhoeus to secure cosmic kingship. This three-generation displacement is the structural backbone.
- [[divine-kingship]] — Zeus's enthronement as cosmic king after the Titanomachy and Typhonomachy.
- [[primordial-waters]] — though Hesiod's primal entity is Χάος (Chaos, "gap"), waters appear with Ocean and Tethys among the early divine generations.
- [[chaoskampf]] — the Typhonomachy (Zeus vs. Typhoeus, Theog. 820–880) is Greek chaoskampf — clearly modeled on, or genealogically related to, the Hittite Storm-god vs. Illuyanka and Marduk vs. Tiamat traditions.
- [[divine-council]] — assemblies of gods on Olympus; Zeus distributing honors.
- **Five Ages / decline** — Works and Days's myth of Golden, Silver, Bronze, Heroic, Iron ages: a moralized history of decline (with the heroic age inserted out of strict order to accommodate the Trojan generation).
- **Pandora** — first woman, opened jar releasing evils — a Greek "fall" myth.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] — *parallel-motif* (succession myth + chaoskampf): the structural parallel (older generation displaced, primordial monster defeated, young storm-god enthroned) is striking. Likely mediated via Hittite/Hurrian intermediaries rather than direct Mesopotamian transmission. Ref: West 1997, *East Face of Helicon*.
- → **Hittite Kumarbi cycle** (Phase 1, Anatolian; Song of Going Forth + Song of Ullikummi) — *parallel-motif* / *redaction-of*: Kumarbi castrates Anu by biting off his genitals and swallowing them (cf. Kronos castrating Uranus); Kumarbi is then overthrown by Teshub the storm-god (cf. Zeus). The Hittite-Hesiod connection is the gold-standard demonstration of Bronze Age Eastern Mediterranean → Greek myth transfer. Ref: Güterbock 1948; West 1997 chs. 5–6.
- → **Phoenician cosmogony of Sanchuniathon / Eusebius's Praeparatio Evangelica** — *parallel-motif*: Phoenician theogony (transmitted via Philo of Byblos and Eusebius) shares motifs of Sky castrated by son, providing a likely Levantine intermediary. Ref: Baumgarten 1981.
- → [[phase-2-008-homeric-epics]] — *shared-milieu*: Homer and Hesiod together canonize Greek mythological reality (Herodotus 2.53).
- → Pre-Socratic cosmogony (e.g., Pherecydes of Syros) and later Orphic theogonies — *direct-influence*: develop and transform the Hesiodic theogonic schema.

## Disputes
- **Date relative to Homer.** Most scholars now place Hesiod slightly after Homer (~700–680 BCE); some classical authorities (Herodotus 2.53) treat them as roughly contemporary. Linguistic evidence (Janko 1982) tends to support Hesiod later.
- **Authorship of the "Hesiodic" corpus.** Theogony and Works and Days are widely accepted as genuine; the Shield of Heracles, Catalogue of Women, and other attributions are mostly later. The disputed *Megalai Ehoiai* and other fragments remain marginal.
- **Mechanism of Near Eastern influence.** That there is influence on the succession myth is established (West 1997). Mechanism: through Greek-Phoenician contact (Cyprus, Levant, Crete); through Anatolian neighbors of Ionia; through Bronze Age direct contact preserved in Mycenaean memory. All three operate.
