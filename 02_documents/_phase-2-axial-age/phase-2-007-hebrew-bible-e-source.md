# Hebrew Bible — E (Elohist) Source

## What it is
E, the "Elohist", is the second of the four classical reconstructed Pentateuchal sources, identified by (a) consistent use of *Elohim* for God prior to the revelation of the name *YHWH* to Moses at the burning bush (Exod 3:13–15), at which point the name shifts; (b) a northern Israelite geographic horizon — Shechem, Bethel, Shiloh, Joseph and the Ephraimite tribes are central; (c) dream- and angel-mediated theophany rather than direct anthropomorphic encounter; (d) a less anthropomorphic, more transcendent deity. E supplies (in Friedman's reconstruction) the binding of Isaac (Gen 22 — mostly E), the Joseph cycle's E layer, the E version of the burning bush, parts of Sinai, and assorted patriarchal material in dialogue with parallel J doublets.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Northern Kingdom of Israel (922–722 BCE), with capital eventually at Samaria, from secession from Judah after Solomon to the Assyrian destruction of Samaria in 722 BCE. The northern kingdom housed the cult centers at Bethel and Dan (the golden calves of 1 Kings 12) and Shiloh; E reflects this northern milieu. After 722 BCE, refugees from the north carry the E tradition south to Judah, where it is eventually combined with J.
- **Religious milieu.** Northern Yahwism, often less Jerusalem-centric and more accommodating of Canaanite forms — though E itself is theologically critical of polytheism. The prophets Amos and Hosea operate in this milieu (~-760 to -722).
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous northern scribes, plausibly associated with the priesthood of Shiloh (Friedman 1987 argues for the Mushite / Mosaic priestly line, displaced from Shiloh to provincial Anathoth, where it surfaces again in Jeremiah).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Reconstructed source-critically. The hypothesis that a distinct E document existed is more contested than J or P; some scholars treat what is called "E" as merely a stratum within the JE composite.

## Themes and motifs
- **Elohim / mediated theophany** — God is "Elohim" until the burning bush; theophanies are mediated by angels (Hagar's angel, Gen 21:17; angel staying Abraham's hand, Gen 22:11), dreams (Jacob's ladder, Gen 28:12; Joseph's dreams), or fire/cloud.
- [[divine-council]] — E preserves some of the "Elohim" plural ambiguity (the *bĕnê hāʾĕlōhîm* in Job, the Elohim of Ps 82) — a residual divine-council framework where multiple *ʾelōhîm* exist under YHWH or a high god.
- [[henotheism-monolatry]] — Exclusive Yahweh-worship demanded; existence of other gods of other nations not always explicitly denied.
- **Joseph cycle prominence** — Joseph and his sons Ephraim and Manasseh dominate the genealogy in ways consonant with northern political identity.
- **Binding of Isaac (Akedah)** — Gen 22 is largely E; one of the highest theological/dramatic peaks of the Pentateuch.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]] — *parallel-motif* / *redaction-of*: J and E supply parallel and often doubled material (two versions of the wife/sister story, two of God's covenant with Abraham, two of the Joseph-sold incident). A redactor (often called RJE) combined them, likely after 722 BCE when northern survivors brought E to Judah.
- → [[phase-2-010-hebrew-bible-early-prophets]] — *shared-milieu*: Amos and Hosea operate in the northern kingdom in the 8th c. BCE, the same milieu as E's late stratum. Hosea's emphasis on northern history (the wilderness wandering, idolatry at Baal-Peor) overlaps E's interests.
- → [[phase-2-011-hebrew-bible-d-source]] — *influences*: The Deuteronomic / Deuteronomistic tradition shares many features with E (covenantal theology, anti-idolatry, prophet-centered religion) and is often argued to descend from the same northern stream after 722.
- → [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]] — *shared-milieu* / *polemic-against*: the northern setting was where Baal worship was most contested; E and the northern prophets engage Baal religion directly.

## Disputes
- **Whether E exists as a distinct source.** This is the single most contested point in Pentateuchal source-criticism today. Friedman, Baden, Stackert, Schwartz defend the classical four-source view including a separate E. Many continental scholars (Schmid, Blum) treat "E" passages as redactional or as part of JE composite. The minority view (Volz/Rudolph) denied E from early on.
- **Date.** ~900–750 BCE (Friedman/Baden) vs. ~750 BCE post-Amos (some others) vs. denial as a separate source.
- **Geographic placement.** Northern provenance is widely held but some recent scholars (Schniedewind) push for Judahite provenance with northern interests.
