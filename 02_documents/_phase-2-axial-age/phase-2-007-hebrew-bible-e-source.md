# Hebrew Bible — E (Elohist) Source

## What it is
E, the "Elohist", is the second of the four classical reconstructed Pentateuchal sources, identified by (a) consistent use of *Elohim* for God prior to the revelation of the name *YHWH* to Moses at the burning bush (Exod 3:13–15), at which point the name shifts; (b) a northern Israelite geographic horizon — Shechem, Bethel, Shiloh, Joseph and the Ephraimite tribes are central; (c) dream- and angel-mediated theophany rather than direct anthropomorphic encounter; (d) a less anthropomorphic, more transcendent deity. E supplies (in Friedman's reconstruction) the binding of Isaac (Gen 22 — mostly E), the Joseph cycle's E layer, the E version of the burning bush, parts of Sinai, and assorted patriarchal material in dialogue with parallel J doublets.

## Primary text — selected passages

### Genesis 22:1–13 — The Binding of Isaac (Akedah) — E stratum
*Trans. NRSV; Friedman, R.E. (2003). The Bible with Sources Revealed. HarperOne.*
> After these things God tested Abraham. He said to him, "Abraham!" And he said, "Here I am." He said, "Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love, and go to the land of Moriah, and offer him there as a burnt offering on one of the mountains that I shall show you."... Then Abraham reached out his hand and took the knife to kill his son. But the angel of the LORD called to him from heaven, and said, "Abraham, Abraham!" And he said, "Here I am." He said, "Do not lay your hand on the boy or do anything to him; for now I know that you fear God, since you have not withheld your son, your only son, from me."

*The Akedah is the E document's theological peak — the near-sacrifice resolved by angelic mediator (characteristic E theophany: angel rather than direct encounter). "Your only son... whom you love" is the first occurrence of the word "love" in the Bible. The Akedah is the shared paradigmatic test-narrative in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam (where it is traditionally associated with Ishmael in later Islamic exegesis — Quran 37:99–113).*

### Genesis 28:12–17 — Jacob's ladder at Bethel — E stratum
*Trans. NRSV; Jenks, A.W. (1977). The Elohist and North Israelite Traditions. Scholars Press.*
> And he dreamed that there was a ladder set up on the earth, the top of it reaching to heaven; and the angels of God were ascending and descending on it... And Jacob woke from his sleep and said, "Surely the LORD is in this place — and I did not know it!" And he was afraid, and said, "How awesome is this place! This is none other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven."

*The dream-theophany at Bethel is the paradigmatic E sequence: a dream-vision (not direct encounter), angelic mediators on the ladder, divine voice from above. The site — *Beit-El*, "house of God" — is the northern cult center whose prestige E reinforces; Jacob's proclamation functions as the founding etiological narrative for the Bethel sanctuary.*

### Exodus 3:13–15 — The divine name at the burning bush — E stratum
*Trans. NRSV; Baden, J.S. (2012). The Composition of the Pentateuch. Yale UP.*
> But Moses said to God, "If I come to the Israelites and say to them, 'The God of your ancestors has sent me to you,' and they ask me, 'What is his name?' what shall I say to them?" God said to Moses, "I AM WHO I AM." He said further, "Thus you shall say to the Israelites, 'I AM has sent me to you.'" God also said to Moses, "Thus you shall say to the Israelites, 'The LORD, the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you': This is my name forever, and this is my title for all generations."

*The formula *'Ehyeh asher 'ehyeh* ("I AM WHO I AM") is a grammatically ambiguous self-referential construction — possibly "I am what I am," "I will be what I will be," or "I cause to be what I cause to be." The LXX renders it *Egō eimi ho ōn* ("I am the Being"), a Greek ontological statement that drives Philo, Aquinas, and the entire tradition of Western philosophical theology.*

*Citations: NRSV; Friedman, R.E. (2003), HarperOne; Jenks, A.W. (1977), Scholars Press; Baden, J.S. (2012), Yale UP.*

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Northern Kingdom of Israel (922–722 BCE), with capital eventually at Samaria, from secession from Judah after Solomon to the Assyrian destruction of Samaria in 722 BCE. The northern kingdom housed the cult centers at Bethel and Dan (the golden calves of 1 Kings 12) and Shiloh; E reflects this northern milieu. After 722 BCE, refugees from the north carry the E tradition south to Judah, where it is eventually combined with J.
- **Religious milieu.** Northern Yahwism, often less Jerusalem-centric and more accommodating of Canaanite forms — though E itself is theologically critical of polytheism. The prophets Amos and Hosea operate in this milieu (~-760 to -722).
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous northern scribes, plausibly associated with the priesthood of Shiloh (Friedman 1987 argues for the Mushite / Mosaic priestly line, displaced from Shiloh to provincial Anathoth, where it surfaces again in Jeremiah).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Reconstructed source-critically. The hypothesis that a distinct E document existed is more contested than J or P; some scholars treat what is called "E" as merely a stratum within the JE composite.

## Themes and motifs
- **Elohim / mediated theophany** — God is "Elohim" until the burning bush; theophanies are mediated by angels (Hagar's angel, Gen 21:17; angel staying Abraham's hand, Gen 22:11), dreams (Jacob's ladder, Gen 28:12; Joseph's dreams), or fire/cloud.
- [[divine-council]] — E preserves some of the "Elohim" plural ambiguity (the *bĕnê hāʾĕlōhîm* in Job, the Elohim of Ps 82) — a residual divine-council framework where multiple *ʾelōhîm* exist under YHWH or a high god.
- [[henotheism-monolatry]] — Exclusive Yahweh-worship demanded; existence of other gods of other nations not always explicitly denied.
- **Joseph cycle prominence** — Joseph and his sons Ephraim and Manasseh dominate the genealogy in ways consonant with northern political identity.
- **Binding of Isaac (Akedah)** — Gen 22 is largely E; one of the highest theological/dramatic peaks of the Pentateuch.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]] — *parallel-motif* / *redaction-of*: J and E supply parallel and often doubled material (two versions of the wife/sister story, two of God's covenant with Abraham, two of the Joseph-sold incident). A redactor (often called RJE) combined them, likely after 722 BCE when northern survivors brought E to Judah.
- → [[phase-2-010-hebrew-bible-early-prophets]] — *shared-milieu*: Amos and Hosea operate in the northern kingdom in the 8th c. BCE, the same milieu as E's late stratum. Hosea's emphasis on northern history (the wilderness wandering, idolatry at Baal-Peor) overlaps E's interests.
- → [[phase-2-011-hebrew-bible-d-source]] — *influences*: The Deuteronomic / Deuteronomistic tradition shares many features with E (covenantal theology, anti-idolatry, prophet-centered religion) and is often argued to descend from the same northern stream after 722.
- → [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]] — *shared-milieu* / *polemic-against*: the northern setting was where Baal worship was most contested; E and the northern prophets engage Baal religion directly.

## Disputes
- **Whether E exists as a distinct source.** This is the single most contested point in Pentateuchal source-criticism today. Friedman, Baden, Stackert, Schwartz defend the classical four-source view including a separate E. Many continental scholars (Schmid, Blum) treat "E" passages as redactional or as part of JE composite. The minority view (Volz/Rudolph) denied E from early on.
- **Date.** ~900–750 BCE (Friedman/Baden) vs. ~750 BCE post-Amos (some others) vs. denial as a separate source.
- **Geographic placement.** Northern provenance is widely held but some recent scholars (Schniedewind) push for Judahite provenance with northern interests.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges

- **The Akedah (Gen 22) across three faiths — the single narrative most-contested between Abraham's children**: The E document's near-sacrifice of Isaac (Gen 22 — attributed to E by the near-universal consensus of source critics) became the shared paradigmatic text for Jewish, Christian, and Islamic theology. Judaism reads it as the foundational test of faith in High Holiday liturgy; Christianity reads it as a type of the crucifixion (beloved son, mountain sacrifice, substitutionary ram); Islam reads a parallel narrative in Quran 37:99–113, traditionally identifying the son as Ishmael — the most consequential single narrative split between sibling traditions (Vermes, G. 1961. *Scripture and Tradition in Judaism*. Brill. pp.193–227; Spiegel, S. 1969. *The Last Trial*. Jewish Publication Society. pp.1–17; Fitzmyer, J.A. 1981. AYB 28. pp.512–513).

- ***'Ehyeh asher 'ehyeh* (Exod 3:13–15) — most philosophically consequential divine name revelation in Western religion**: E's burning bush disclosure of *'ehyeh* ("I am" / "I will be") produced, via the LXX's rendering *Egō eimi ho ōn* ("I am the Being"), one of the most philosophically significant phrases in world religion. This drove Philo's equation of YHWH with Plato's self-subsisting Good, Aquinas's *ipsum esse subsistens* in *Summa Theologiae* I.3.4, Meister Eckhart's *esse est deus*, and the entire tradition of *negative theology* and *onto-theology* in the West (Gilson, É. 1952. *Being and Some Philosophers*. PIMS. pp.40–53; Marion, J.-L. 1991. *God Without Being*. Chicago UP. pp.73–107; Hadot, P. 1968. *Porphyre et Victorinus*. Études Augustiniennes. pp.221–234).

- **E's dream-theophany theology — template for all prophetic and apocalyptic mediational structures**: E's consistent use of angel-mediated and dream-mediated theophany (angelic stay of Abraham's hand, Gen 22:11; Jacob's ladder, Gen 28:12; Joseph's dream-interpretation) created the mediational theology of prophetic communication that runs through Daniel's angelic visions, Zechariah's night visions, and John's Revelation. The move from direct to mediated divine encounter is the axial theological step separating apocalyptic literature from older prophetic forms (Collins, J.J. 2016. *The Apocalyptic Imagination*, 3rd ed. Eerdmans. pp.19–25; Hanson, P.D. 1975. *The Dawn of Apocalyptic*. Fortress. pp.11–31; Gnuse, R. 1996. *Dreams and Dream Reports in the Writings of Josephus*. Brill. pp.12–18).

- **RJE redaction preserving contradictions — the most productive editorial decision in canonical history**: The Redactor's choice to preserve both J and E versions of doublets (two wife-sister stories, two covenants with Abraham, two accounts of Joseph sold) rather than harmonizing them created the *contradictions* within the Pentateuch that drove 2500 years of exegesis — allegorical reading, typological reading, Midrash, and ultimately modern historical-critical method. The preserved contradictions are the generative engine of Jewish and Christian hermeneutics (Friedman, R.E. 1987. *Who Wrote the Bible?* HarperOne. pp.100–121; Kugel, J.L. 1998. *Traditions of the Bible*. Harvard UP. pp.1–36; Childs, B.S. 1979. *Introduction to the Old Testament as Scripture*. Fortress. pp.128–135).
