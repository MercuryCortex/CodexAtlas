# Yi Jing (I Ching / Book of Changes)

## What it is

The **Yi Jing** (易經, "Classic of Changes," also romanized I Ching) is simultaneously the oldest of the Chinese Five Classics, the foundational text of Chinese divination, the cosmological framework for all classical Chinese thought, and — through the **Cantong Qi** (~142 CE) — the theoretical backbone of Chinese alchemy. Its influence on Chinese civilization is comparable to that of the Bible on Western civilization: every major intellectual tradition (Confucian, Daoist, Buddhist, Legalist, alchemical, medical) operates within a conceptual universe the Yi Jing shaped.

The text has two distinct layers:

- **The core** (*Zhouyi*, "Changes of Zhou"): 64 hexagrams (六爻, each composed of six broken or solid lines) with a brief judgment (*gua ci*) for each hexagram and a brief statement (*yao ci*) for each of the six lines. This is the practical divination manual, probably compiled from Zhou dynasty court divination archives (~-1000 to -800 BCE).
- **The Ten Wings** (*Shiyi*): ten commentary texts traditionally attributed to Confucius, actually composed by Warring States and Han scholars (~-350 to -150 BCE). The Wings transform the divination manual into a cosmological-philosophical treatise.

## The 64 hexagrams: binary cosmology

Each hexagram is composed of six lines, each either **solid** (⚊, Yang) or **broken** (⚋, Yin). Six binary digits = 2⁶ = **64** possible combinations. Every state, situation, process, and transition in the cosmos is mapped onto one of these 64 configurations.

The cosmological claim: **all change** (*yi* 易) in the universe follows the logic of yin-yang transformation. A hexagram describes a present configuration; the changing lines (transitioning from yin to yang or yang to yin) show the direction of movement; the resulting hexagram shows the future configuration. The Yi Jing is a map of all possible cosmic transitions.

### The eight trigrams (Bagua 八卦)

Two trigrams stacked = one hexagram. The eight primary trigrams are:

| Trigram | Symbol | Name | Element | Direction | Quality |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ☰ | ⚊⚊⚊ | Qian | Heaven | Northwest | Creative, strong |
| ☷ | ⚋⚋⚋ | Kun | Earth | Southwest | Receptive, yielding |
| ☳ | ⚋⚋⚊ | Zhen | Thunder | East | Arousing, movement |
| ☵ | ⚋⚊⚋ | Kan | Water | North | Abysmal, danger |
| ☶ | ⚊⚋⚋ | Gen | Mountain | Northeast | Keeping still |
| ☴ | ⚊⚊⚋ | Xun | Wind | Southeast | Gentle, penetrating |
| ☲ | ⚊⚋⚊ | Li | Fire | South | Clinging, luminous |
| ☱ | ⚋⚊⚊ | Dui | Lake | West | Joyous, open |

The 8 × 8 matrix of stacked trigrams = the 64 hexagrams.

## The Ten Wings: cosmological transformation

The **Great Treatise** (*Dazhuan*, Wings 5–6) is the most philosophically significant of the Ten Wings — the text that transforms the Yi Jing from a divination manual into a cosmological system:

> The Book of Changes contains the measure of Heaven and Earth... Therefore it enables us to comprehend the Tao of Heaven and Earth. In the beginning there was the Supreme Ultimate (*Taiji*). The Supreme Ultimate produced the two elementary forms (*liangi* — Yin and Yang). The two elementary forms produced the four emblems (*sixiang*). The four emblems produced the eight trigrams (*bagua*).

This is the Yi Jing's **cosmogony**: from undivided unity (Taiji) → binary division (yin/yang) → quaternary structure → 8 trigrams → 64 hexagrams → the ten thousand things. It is the same logical structure as the Daodejing's cosmogony ("the Dao gives birth to one; one gives birth to two; two gives birth to three; three gives birth to the ten thousand things") — but specified in combinatorial-mathematical form.

## The Yi Jing and Chinese alchemy

The connection between the Yi Jing and Chinese alchemy is not metaphorical — it is structural and direct. The **Cantong Qi** (~142 CE, the foundational alchemical text) explicitly builds its framework on Yi Jing hexagram theory:

- The **64 hexagrams** are mapped onto the **lunar month**: each hexagram governs a specific period; the alchemical operations must be timed to the hexagram-phase of the moon
- **Kan** (☵, Water hexagram) = Lead = Yin alchemical substance; **Li** (☲, Fire hexagram) = Mercury = Yang alchemical substance
- The alchemical *opus* is the controlled transformation of Kan and Li — making the water-substance release its inner yang (the true lead within) and the fire-substance its inner yin — until the two merge into the supreme undivided (the gold elixir)

The Yi Jing is therefore the **mathematical-cosmological language** in which Chinese alchemy is written. This makes the Yi Jing an indirect ancestor of the sulphur-mercury theory that later entered Arabic and European alchemy via the Cantong Qi.

## Leibniz and binary mathematics

In 1701, the Jesuit missionary **Joachim Bouvet** sent **Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz** a diagram of the 64 hexagrams. Leibniz, who had independently developed binary arithmetic in 1679, immediately recognized the structural identity:

> I find in this arrangement proof of an ancient science of numbers... I believe this book [the Yi Jing] to be the most ancient monument of science in the world.

Leibniz's binary system: 0 and 1 → 2¹ = 2 → 2² = 4 → 2³ = 8 → 2⁶ = 64. The I Ching's broken/solid line system: Yin(0) and Yang(1) → 2 liangi → 4 sixiang → 8 bagua → 64 hexagrams. **Identical mathematical structure, independent development (~2,000 years apart).**

Leibniz interpreted this as confirmation that the Yi Jing encoded a universal divine logic accessible to both Chinese and European minds — the same Logos (his word) discoverable by reason regardless of cultural context. The modern reading: both Leibniz's binary system and the Yi Jing's hexagram system are independent discoveries of the **combinatorial logic of binary sequences** — the mathematics that underlies all modern computation.

## Jung and synchronicity

**Carl Jung** wrote the preface to the Wilhelm-Baynes English translation (1950) and used the Yi Jing as the primary case study for his concept of **synchronicity** (acausal connecting principle):

> The Chinese mind, as I see it at work in the I Ching, seems to be exclusively preoccupied with the chance aspect of events... The moment under actual observation appears to the ancient Chinese mind a far more reliable index of... the cosmic situation than are the various causally connected elements.

Jung's I Ching usage: the hexagram obtained by chance (coin-toss or yarrow-stalk method) is meaningful because it reflects the *total* configuration of the moment — the enquirer's state, the cosmic state, and their resonance — not because it *causes* anything. This is the divination logic underlying all oracle systems: meaningful correspondence rather than mechanical causation.

## Cross-tradition MASSIVE WIN parallels

**1. Yi Jing divination ↔ universal oracle systems — independent convergence on binary/lot divination**
The Yi Jing's method (random-chance generation of a symbol from a limited set → interpretation of the symbol's meaning in context) is structurally identical to:
- **Babylonian extispicy / hepatoscopy**: reading the liver of a sacrificed animal (random pattern → cosmic meaning)
- **Urim and Thummim** (Hebrew Bible, Exodus 28:30): two stones giving binary yes/no divine answers
- **Greek oracle** (Delphi): question posed; ecstatic utterance from the Pythia; interpretation required
- **Roman augury**: bird flight patterns as random cosmic signals interpreted by the augur
- **Islamic istikhara**: random Quran-opening for divine guidance
- **Tarot** (14th c. onward): random card-draw interpreted through symbolic correspondence

All oracle systems share the same deep logic: **randomness as the entry-point for cosmic communication**, because the human will cannot control what is random — therefore a random outcome reflects the non-human (cosmic/divine) order. The Yi Jing is the most mathematically systematized and long-lived of these systems.

**2. Taiji → Yin/Yang → 10,000 things = universal emanationist cosmogony**
The Yi Jing's cosmogonic sequence (undivided unity → binary opposition → multiplicity) is structurally identical to:
- **Daodejing** ch. 42: "Dao gives birth to One; One gives birth to Two; Two gives birth to Three; Three gives birth to the ten thousand things"
- **Plotinian Neoplatonism**: The One → Nous (Mind) → Soul → Matter — descending emanation from unity to multiplicity
- **Kabbalistic Tzimtzum**: Ein Sof (undivided infinite) → contraction → first point → sefirot → creation
- **Hermetic Poimandres**: undivided Nous → Light/Darkness separation → four elements → the material world

All four are independent instantiations of the same cosmogonic logic: **primordial unity generates multiplicity through sequential differentiation**. The Yi Jing's version is notable for being the most explicitly *mathematical* (binary combinatorics) and the earliest to generate a complete combinatorial map of all possible states.

**3. 64 hexagrams ↔ binary mathematics ↔ DNA codons — convergent binary coding of universal information**
The structural parallel Leibniz noticed has a modern extension: **DNA's genetic code** operates through four bases (A, T, G, C) read in triplets (codons) = 4³ = 64 possible codons. The Yi Jing: 2 elements (yin/yang) in sequences of 6 = 2⁶ = 64 hexagrams. The biological universe encodes all life information in 64 units; the Yi Jing encodes all cosmic-change information in 64 units. Both are binary-combinatorial systems that cover a complete state-space. The parallel is typological (not transmission) — but it is the kind of structural resonance the Atlas exists to document.

**4. Yi Jing ↔ Cantong Qi ↔ Jabir = the alchemy transmission spine's cosmological root**
The Yi Jing's yin-yang hexagram system → Cantong Qi's alchemical hexagram calendar → Jabirian sulphur-mercury polarity theory → European sulphur-mercury-salt → Lavoisier's critique → modern chemistry. The Yi Jing is the conceptual origin of a transmission chain that reaches Isaac Newton's alchemical notebooks.

**5. Divination as pre-scientific probability management ↔ Pascal's Wager ↔ Bayesian reasoning**
The Yi Jing's divinatory logic — using a structured randomness protocol to navigate genuine uncertainty — is the ancient Chinese parallel to the Western tradition of formal decision-making under uncertainty. Pascal's Wager (1670): structured reasoning about unknown outcomes under uncertainty. Bayesian probability (1763): updating probability estimates based on evidence. The Yi Jing: structured randomness protocol + interpretive tradition to navigate unknowable futures. All three address the same human problem (radical uncertainty about future outcomes) with structurally analogous moves (formalize the uncertainty, work within it rather than pretending it away).
