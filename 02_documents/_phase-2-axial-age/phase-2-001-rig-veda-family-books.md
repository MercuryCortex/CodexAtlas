# Rig Veda — Family Books (Mandalas 2–7)

## What it is
The Rig Veda is the oldest stratum of the Sanskrit Veda: ~1,028 sūktas (hymns) of ~10,600 verses, distributed across ten maṇḍalas. The "family books" — Maṇḍalas 2 through 7 — are by consensus the oldest core, each attributed to and likely curated within a specific ṛṣi family lineage. The hymns are addressed to a pantheon of devas, supplicating them in the context of the śrauta sacrificial ritual (especially the Soma rite), and constitute the earliest surviving Indo-Aryan literature. The collection survives orally with negligible textual variation, then in manuscript from the medieval period.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Pastoral / semi-nomadic tribal society in the Sapta Sindhu (seven-rivers Punjab) region. The hymns reference cattle raids, clan conflict (the *dāśarājña*, "Battle of the Ten Kings", Maṇḍala 7), and a society organized around chariot-warrior aristocracy and sacrificial priesthood. No states; lineages and tribes (Bharata, Pūru, Yadu).
- **Religious milieu.** Indo-Iranian inheritance still visible: the Vedic *devas* are cognate with Avestan *daēvas* (though Zoroastrian reform inverts their valence), Soma cognate with Haoma, Mitra with Mithra. Rituals center on *agni* (sacrificial fire), *soma* (pressed ritual drink), and recited *mantra*. Possibly substrate contact with Indus Valley and Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex (BMAC) populations.
- **Who was behind it.** Hereditary priestly lineages (Bharadvāja, Vasiṣṭha, Viśvāmitra, etc.) composed and memorized hymns within ritual contexts. The texts are *śruti* — "what was heard" — held by tradition to be beginningless and revealed to seers, but text-historically authored within the family lineages.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous oral tradition. Brought to European scholarly attention by Henry Thomas Colebrooke (early 19th c.); first complete edition by Max Müller (1849–1874) with Sāyaṇa's medieval commentary. Witzel (e.g., 1995, 2007) has reconstructed the relative chronology and geographic layering.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divine-kingship]] — Indra is the kingly warrior-god par excellence; many Maṇḍala 2–7 hymns enthrone him as overlord of the devas after his combat with Vṛtra.
- [[chaoskampf]] — the slaying of Vṛtra (the obstructing serpent who pens the cosmic waters) is the Vedic instance: Indra splits Vṛtra with the vajra, releasing the waters that nourish the world.
- [[primordial-waters]] — the cosmic waters (*āpaḥ*) penned by Vṛtra; released, they animate creation. Cognate framework to the Mesopotamian/Canaanite chaoskampf.
- [[divine-council]] — assemblies of devas decide, deliberate, and act collectively; Varuna and Mitra co-sovereign the cosmic and moral order (*ṛta*).
- **Ṛta (cosmic order)** — the deep Vedic concept of cosmic / ritual / moral truth; cognate with Avestan *aša*.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]] — *shared-milieu* / *parallel-motif*: Avestan and Vedic share inherited Indo-Iranian deities (Mitra/Mithra, Soma/Haoma), ritual vocabulary (*yajña*/*yasna*, *hotṛ*/*zaotar*), and cosmic-order concepts (*ṛta*/*aša*). The Gathic reform *inverts* the daēva/deva valence — what is divine in the Veda is demonic in Zoroaster. Ref: Boyce 1975, *A History of Zoroastrianism* vol. I; Skjærvø 2011.
- → [[phase-2-003-atharva-veda]] — *parallel-motif* / *redaction-of*: shares hymns and deities with the Rig Veda but with magical/medical orientation; the AV preserves a partly distinct popular religion of charms and household rite alongside the priestly RV.
- → [[phase-2-006-brahmanas-aranyakas]] — *commentary-on*: the Brāhmaṇas are prose ritual exegesis explicitly tied to RV hymns; the Āraṇyakas extend the move toward speculative interpretation.
- → [[phase-2-012-brihadaranyaka-upanishad]] / [[phase-2-013-chandogya-upanishad]] — *commentary-on*: the Upaniṣads internalize and philosophize the sacrificial system the RV grounds.
- → Hittite / Mitanni treaty oaths (Boğazköy, 14th c. BCE) — *manuscript-transmission* of the Indo-Iranian deities Mitra, Varuṇa, Indra, the Nāsatya named as divine guarantors in the Mitanni-Hittite treaty — strong philological evidence for the Indo-Iranian pantheon predating both the Veda and the Avesta. Ref: Thieme 1960, JAOS.

## Disputes
- **Date and the "Indo-Aryan migration".** The RV's terminus ad quem is firm (~1000 BCE, before the Brāhmaṇas); the terminus a quo is contested. Mainstream scholarship (Witzel, Jamison, Bryant) places the family-book core at ~1500–1200 BCE, correlated with the Indo-Aryan migration into the Indian subcontinent from the steppe via the BMAC. "Indigenous Aryan" / "Out-of-India" theorists (some Indian scholars, notably Talageri) argue for earlier and indigenous composition; this view is rejected by the philological mainstream.
- **Soma identification.** What plant is Soma? Wasson (1968) proposed *Amanita muscaria*; others propose *Ephedra* (favored by current Iranian Haoma scholarship, e.g., Falk 1989), *Peganum harmala* (Syrian rue), *Cannabis*, or a now-extinct mountain plant. Unresolved.
- **Relative ordering of family books.** Witzel's reconstruction (2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 3) is widely cited; alternatives exist.
