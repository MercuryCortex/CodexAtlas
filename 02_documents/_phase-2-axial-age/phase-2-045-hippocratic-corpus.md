The Hippocratic Corpus is the founding document of Western medicine — a collection of 60+ medical texts attributed to [[hippocrates]] of Kos (c. 460–370 BCE), though written by many authors across several centuries. It was collected and attributed to Hippocrates in the library of Alexandria (3rd century BCE), establishing the "Hippocratic" tradition as a coherent school.

## The Foundational Texts

**On the Sacred Disease** (c. 400 BCE): the single most radical text — the first systematic argument that disease has natural causes, not divine ones. Epilepsy (the "sacred disease") is explained through brain dysfunction, not divine intervention. Opens the possibility of secular medicine.

**Epidemics I and III** (c. 410 BCE): the oldest surviving clinical case records. Patient-by-patient observations over seasons — names, locations, daily symptom records, outcomes. Many patients die; the physician records this without claiming to have saved them. The founding gesture of evidence-based medicine: honest clinical documentation.

**Hippocratic Oath**: the founding document of medical ethics. Divine witnesses; teacher-student obligation; confidentiality; prohibition on harm, abortion, and sexual exploitation. The structural model for every subsequent medical ethics code.

**On Airs, Waters, Places**: the first environmental medicine text — systematic argument that disease correlates with climate, water quality, and seasonal patterns. Wrong causal mechanism (miasma), correct epidemiological observation. The ancestor of public health.

**Prognostics**: the first treatise on medical prognosis as a professional skill — the physician who can predict the course of disease demonstrates genuine diagnostic authority.

**On Ancient Medicine**: a philosophical defense of empirical medicine against theoretical speculation — an epistemological argument for clinical observation over abstract theorizing that reads as remarkably modern.

## The Humoral Theory

The Hippocratic tradition's central theoretical framework — elaborated by Galen into canonical form:
- Four humors: blood (hot/moist), yellow bile (hot/dry), black bile (cold/dry), phlegm (cold/moist)
- Health = balance; disease = imbalance
- Constitutional types correlate with humor dominance
- Treatment restores balance: diet, exercise, bloodletting, purging

Wrong physiology, productive methodology. The framework drove systematic clinical observation for 2,400 years.
