# Great Learning (大學) / Daxue

## What it is
The *Daxue* (大學, 'Great Learning' or 'University Learning') is one of the shortest but most structurally influential texts in the Confucian tradition. Originally embedded in the *Liji* (Book of Rites, one of the Five Classics), it was extracted by the Cheng brothers in the 11th century CE and canonized by [[zhu-xi]] (~1190 CE) as the first of the Four Books (*Sishu*) — the pedagogical sequence through which all educated persons in East Asia from the Song dynasty through the 19th century were initiated into Confucian learning. It sets out an eight-step program of self-cultivation that moves from individual moral formation to social transformation to universal governance.

## Context
The Daxue presents the Confucian conviction that ethical self-cultivation and political governance are continuous — the same process operating at different scales. The eight-step program (*bamu*, 'eight items') is its central contribution:

1. **Investigation of things** (*gewu* 格物) — attentive inquiry into the nature of things in the world
2. **Extension of knowledge** (*zhizhi* 致知) — expanding understanding through that inquiry
3. **Making the will sincere** (*chengyi* 誠意) — purifying intention, eliminating self-deception
4. **Rectifying the mind** (*zhengxin* 正心) — stabilizing the heart-mind free from distorting passions
5. **Cultivating the self** (*xiushen* 修身) — the hinge step; the cultivated self is both the product of the first four and the foundation for the next three
6. **Regulating the family** (*qijia* 齊家) — ordering one's household as the first social extension
7. **Governing the state** (*zhiguo* 治國) — from ordered household to well-governed state
8. **Bringing peace to all under Heaven** (*pingtianxia* 平天下) — the ultimate political extension: universal human flourishing

This sequence encodes a fractal logic: the inner disciplines (1-5) scale outward to social and political order (6-8). The cultivated self is not opposed to the political world but is its origin.

## Neo-Confucian significance
[[zhu-xi]]'s controversial decision to place the Daxue first in the Four Books sequence (before the Analects) made it the entry point for all Confucian learning in the Song-Ming-Qing period. His supplementary chapter on *gewu* (investigation of things) — which he inserted because he believed the original commentary was missing — became one of the most debated passages in Chinese intellectual history. Wang Yangming (1472-1529), the major Ming-period challenger to Zhu Xi's authority, offered a radically different reading of *gewu*: where Zhu Xi read it as attentive inquiry into external things (an empirical-analytical process), Wang Yangming read it as the extension of innate moral knowledge (*liangzhi*) — turning the investigation inward. The debate between Zhu Xi's 'investigation of external things' and Wang Yangming's 'extension of innate knowing' structured Neo-Confucian philosophy for four centuries.
