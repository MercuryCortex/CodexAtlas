# Book of Proverbs

## What it is
A collection of Hebrew wisdom poetry and moral instruction, attributed to Solomon by tradition but composed over several centuries (~950–400 BCE) and likely compiled in post-exilic Jerusalem. The canonical 31-chapter book contains multiple distinct collections: the "Proverbs of Solomon" (chs. 10–22, 25–29), the "Words of the Wise" (22:17–24:22), a section attributed to "Agur son of Jakeh" (ch. 30), and the enigmatic "Capable Wife" acrostic poem (ch. 31). The most theologically consequential section is the prologue (chs. 1–9), which personifies [[hokmah-hebrew]] as a divine feminine figure who was present at creation.

## Primary text — selected passages

### Proverbs 8:22–31 — Wisdom's self-presentation (pre-existence and presence at creation)
*Trans. NRSV; Fox, M.V. (2000). Proverbs. Anchor Bible. Doubleday.*
> The LORD possessed me at the beginning of his work,
>     the first of his acts of long ago.
> Ages ago I was set up,
>     at the first, before the beginning of the earth.
> When there were no depths I was brought forth,
>     when there were no springs abounding with water.
> Before the mountains had been shaped,
>     before the hills, I was brought forth...
> when he established the heavens, I was there,
>     when he drew a circle on the face of the deep...
> then I was beside him, like a master workman,
>     and I was daily his delight,
>     rejoicing before him always,
> rejoicing in his inhabited world
>     and delighting in the children of man.

*The key term at v. 22 is קָנָנִי (qānānī): "possessed/acquired" in MT; LXX reads ektisen me = "created me." The LXX reading directly fueled the Arian controversy — Arius cited it to argue the Son/Logos was God's first creature, not co-eternal.*

### Proverbs 22:17–20 — Words of the Wise (direct parallel to Egyptian Amenemope)
*Trans. Fox 2000 (Anchor Bible)*
> Incline your ear and hear my words,
>     and apply your mind to my teaching;
> for it will be pleasant if you keep them within you,
>     if all of them are ready on your lips.
> So that your trust may be in the LORD,
>     I have made them known to you today — even to you.
> Have I not written for you thirty sayings
>     of admonition and knowledge?

*Fox (2000, Anchor Bible, pp. 718–722): at least 17 of these "thirty sayings" directly parallel chapters of the Egyptian Instruction of Amenemope (~1200–1000 BCE) — the most explicitly documented case of foreign literary borrowing in the Hebrew Bible.*

*Citation: Fox, M.V. (2000). Proverbs. Anchor Bible. Doubleday. Von Rad, G. (1972). Wisdom in Israel. Abingdon.*

## Context
Proverbs belongs to the broader Ancient Near Eastern wisdom tradition — close parallels exist with the Egyptian *Instruction of Amenemope* (which may have been a direct source for Proverbs 22:17–23:11), the Mesopotamian *Counsels of Wisdom*, and the Aramaic *Words of Ahikar*. The uniquely Israelite contribution is the personification of Wisdom as a female figure — Lady Wisdom — who calls from the streets, builds her house, and was God's delight before creation.

## Themes and motifs
- The two paths: Wisdom vs. Folly, life vs. death — dualistic moral cosmology
- The "fear of the LORD" as the beginning of wisdom — theodicy and virtue
- [[hokmah-hebrew]] personified as a pre-existent feminine divine principle

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges

- **Proverbs 8:22 → Arian controversy → Nicene Creed — one word ignited the defining battle of Christian theology**: Arius (~318 CE) cited LXX Proverbs 8:22 (*ektisen me* = "he created me") to argue that the Son/Logos was God's first creature, not co-eternal with the Father. Athanasius spent 45 years and four exiles defending the counter-reading. The entire architecture of the Nicene Creed — "begotten not made, of one substance [*homoousios*] with the Father" — is a direct refutation of Arius's reading of ten Hebrew words in Proverbs. The political fallout ran through four ecumenical councils (Nicaea 325, Constantinople 381, Ephesus 431, Chalcedon 451) and split Christianity into Nicene and Arian branches, the latter surviving in Germanic kingdoms through the 6th century (Hanson, R.P.C. 1988, *The Search for the Christian Doctrine of God*, T&T Clark, pp. 203–207; Williams, R. 2001, *Arius: Heresy and Tradition*, Eerdmans; → [[phase-3-005-stoic-foundational-texts]]).

- **Egyptian Amenemope → Proverbs 22:17–23:11 — most explicitly documented foreign borrowing in the Hebrew Bible**: The Egyptian *Instruction of Amenemope* (~1200–1000 BCE) was unknown until E.A. Wallis Budge's 1922 British Museum publication. Fox (2000, Anchor Bible, pp. 718–722) documents that at least 17 of Amenemope's 30 chapters are paralleled in Proverbs 22:17–23:11, often with near-verbatim vocabulary overlap. "Have I not written for you *thirty* sayings" (Prov 22:20) directly references Amenemope's 30-chapter structure. Israel's "revealed wisdom" is partly Egyptian royal-court wisdom — the most explicit foreign source relationship in the entire Hebrew canon (Whybray, R.N. 1994, *The Composition of the Book of Proverbs*, JSOT Press; Ruffle, J. 1977, *Tyndale Bulletin* 28:29–68).

- **Lady Wisdom ↔ Asherah — the suppressed Canaanite feminine divine substrate**: Lady Wisdom (Prov 1–9) and the rejected Lady Folly correspond structurally to the Canaanite divine feminine pairing that Israelite religion actively suppressed. Hadley (2000, *The Cult of Asherah in Ancient Israel and Judah*, Cambridge UP) and Keel & Uehlinger (1998, *Gods, Goddesses, and Images of God*, Fortress, pp. 210–248) argue Lady Wisdom preserves a transformed survival of Asherah-theology — the same goddess Jeremiah 7:18 depicts Judean women worshipping as "Queen of Heaven" at the very time Proverbs was compiled. Proverbs simultaneously absorbs and sublimates the rival female divinity into a theologically safe figure (→ [[inanna-sumerian]], → [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]] for the Canaanite context).

- **Proverbs 8 → Sirach 24 → Wisdom of Solomon 7 → Philo → John 1 → Nicene Creed — most consequential 10-verse chain in Western theology**: The Sophia/Logos development runs step by step: Proverbs 8 (pre-existent Wisdom as creator's companion) → Sirach 24 (Wisdom identified with Torah, dwelling in Israel) → Wisdom of Solomon 7 (Sophia as breath of God, 21 Stoic attributes) → Philo (Logos as God's firstborn, mediating principle) → John 1 (Logos incarnate) → Nicaea 325 (Logos co-eternal). Each step is documentable and each builds on the prior. The 10 verses of Proverbs 8:22–31 launched a trajectory that shaped Trinitarian Christianity, the *Hokhmah* sefirah of Kabbalah, and Islamic *kalima* (divine word) theology (Fox 2000; Brown 1966, *The Gospel According to John I–XII*, Anchor Bible 29; von Rad 1972, *Wisdom in Israel*; → [[phase-3-007-sirach]], → [[phase-3-012-wisdom-of-solomon]], → [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]], → [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]]).
