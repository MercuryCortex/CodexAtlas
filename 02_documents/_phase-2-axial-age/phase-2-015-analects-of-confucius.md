# Analects of Confucius

## What it is
The *Lúnyǔ* ("Selected Sayings") is a compilation of dialogues, aphorisms, anecdotes, and short narratives attributed to Confucius (Kǒngzǐ, ~551–479 BCE) and his immediate disciples. Twenty books contain ~500 short units, most under fifty characters. The text is the principal source for Confucius's teaching and the founding scripture of Confucianism. Its central concepts: *rén* (仁, humaneness / benevolence), *lǐ* (禮, ritual propriety), *jūnzǐ* (君子, "exemplary person" / "gentleman"), *xiào* (孝, filial piety), the *Way* (*dào*, the proper course of human life and government), and the ideal of moral cultivation through study, ritual practice, and self-examination.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late Spring-and-Autumn period (Confucius's lifetime ~551–479 BCE) in the small state of Lu and other east-Zhou principalities. The Zhou royal house was a ceremonial shell; competing aristocratic clans dominated weakened ducal states. Confucius taught and traveled seeking a ruler willing to put proper governance into practice; he never found one. The compilation of the Analects spans several generations of disciples and second-generation followers, extending into the Warring States.
- **Religious milieu.** Old Zhou cult of *tiān* (Heaven) and the ancestors remained in place; popular religion of nature spirits and divination coexisted. Confucius is largely reticent about cosmological and afterlife questions ("Not yet able to serve the living, how can one serve the dead?", 11.12) but accepts Heaven (*tian*) as a moral order and refers to a divine mandate (*tian ming*).
- **Who was behind it.** Confucius's immediate disciples (Zigong, Zilu, Yan Hui, Zengzi) and second-generation followers (especially the followers of Zengzi and of Confucius's grandson Zisi) compiled and accreted the material. The Brookses (1998) propose a chapter-by-chapter chronological reconstruction; Slingerland is skeptical of the precision but accepts layered composition.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Chinese transmission. Standardized as one of the Four Books by Zhu Xi (~1190 CE); thereafter foundational to the imperial examination system. The Dingzhou bamboo-slip Analects (excavated 1973 from a Han prince's tomb, sealed -55 BCE) is the oldest physical witness.

## Themes and motifs
- ***Rén*** (仁, humaneness / benevolence) — the central virtue. Difficult to define univocally; rendered variously as benevolence, humaneness, co-humanity. "Overcoming self and returning to ritual is *rén*" (12.1).
- ***Lǐ*** (禮, ritual propriety) — the formal patterns of conduct that, when internalized, shape moral life. Not empty form; the channel through which virtue is cultivated.
- ***Jūnzǐ*** (君子, exemplary person) — the ideal of moral cultivation, contrasted with the *xiǎorén* (the small / petty person).
- **Filial piety (*xiào*)** — the rooting of all virtue in family relations; the parent-child bond as the primary moral arena.
- **The mandate of Heaven (*tian ming*)** — Heaven's moral order; rulers who lose virtue lose the mandate.
- **Self-cultivation through study** — the opening line: "To learn and to apply at the proper time — is this not a pleasure?" (1.1).
- **Skepticism about the supernatural** — Confucius avoids speculation about spirits and afterlife; pragmatic this-worldly ethics.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-2-014-daodejing]] — *polemic-against* / *shared-milieu*: the DDJ ridicules the Confucian project of ritual cultivation; Confucianism counters by treating Daoism as quietist abdication. Both are Warring States responses to the same political-ethical crisis.
- → [[phase-2-025-mozi]] — *polemic-against* (within China): Mozi attacks Confucian particularistic care (the family-first ethic) in favor of universal love. Confucians counter.
- → [[phase-2-023-mengzi]] — *direct-influence* / *commentary-on*: Mencius systematizes Confucian thought, develops the doctrine of innate moral goodness.
- → [[phase-2-024-xunzi]] — *direct-influence* (with internal disagreement): Xunzi develops a more austere Confucianism arguing human nature is bad and must be shaped by ritual.
- → [[phase-2-026-han-feizi]] — *polemic-against*: Han Fei's Legalist tradition treats Confucian moralism as the principal obstacle to functional governance.
- → Greek Socratic / Platonic ethics — *parallel-motif*: roughly contemporaneous emergence of ethics-centered philosophy in two civilizations; the famous Jaspers "Axial Age" thesis treats this convergence as significant. No direct contact.

## Disputes
- **Compositional layers.** Brookses (1998) propose precise chronological strata, attributing different books to different disciple-groups across two centuries. Slingerland and others doubt the precision while accepting that the text is layered.
- **Historical Confucius vs. school Confucius.** What in the Analects goes back to the historical figure vs. what is school accretion remains debated.
- **Religious vs. philosophical character.** Confucianism's status as "religion" or "philosophy" has been disputed for centuries; the *tian* / mandate of Heaven element gives it religious dimensions, while its this-worldly focus gives it philosophical ones.
- **Confucius's view of Heaven.** Whether *tian* in the Analects is a personal moral deity, an impersonal moral order, or both, is debated.
