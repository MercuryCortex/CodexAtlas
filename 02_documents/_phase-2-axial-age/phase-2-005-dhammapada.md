# Dhammapada

The **Dhammapada** ("Verses on the *Dhamma*," the path or teaching) is a canonical anthology of **423 verses in Pāli**, part of the **Khuddaka Nikāya** of the Sutta Piṭaka — and probably the **single most widely read Buddhist text in the world**. Arranged into 26 chapters by theme (the Twin Verses, Heedfulness, the Mind, Flowers, the Fool, the Sage, the Arahant, Craving, and so on), it distils the Buddha's ethical and contemplative teaching into terse, memorable, often strikingly beautiful aphorisms: "All that we are is the result of what we have thought"; "Hatred is never appeased by hatred; it is appeased by love alone."

## Composition

The Dhammapada is an **anonymous compilation**, drawing together a stratum of **older oral gnomic verse** attributed to the Buddha and assembled by the early monastic community (c. 3rd–1st c. BCE). It belongs to the pan-Indian genre of **wisdom-verse (subhāṣita)**, and survives in several parallel recensions — most importantly the **Gāndhārī Dharmapada**, among the oldest surviving Indic manuscripts — which let scholars trace how the collection grew and travelled.

## The cross-tradition wire

As a compact distillation of the path of liberation through ethical discipline, mental cultivation, and the conquest of craving, the Dhammapada sits within the broad **Indian dharma-and-wisdom literature** alongside texts like the **[[phase-2-027-bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gītā]]** — both axial-age South Asian works on duty, the disciplining of the mind, and release — and it anchors the Theravāda tradition that the later **[[phase-4-102-avatamsaka-sutra|Mahāyāna sūtras]]** would build upon and reinterpret.

## Reach forward

Carried with Theravāda Buddhism throughout Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia, and among the first Buddhist texts translated into European languages, the Dhammapada became the **global gateway to Buddhist thought** — the verses most often memorized, chanted, and quoted wherever the Dhamma is taught.
