# Oresteia (trilogy: Agamemnon, Libation Bearers, Eumenides)

## What it is
The ***Oresteia*** is **the only surviving complete Greek tragic trilogy** — three connected plays composed by [[aeschylus]] and produced at the **City Dionysia of -458** in Athens, winning the first prize. **Three plays plus a now-lost satyr play (*Proteus*)**:
1. **Agamemnon** — Agamemnon returns to Argos from Troy with Cassandra as captive concubine; his wife Clytemnestra (avenging Agamemnon's sacrifice of their daughter Iphigenia ten years earlier) murders both.
2. **Libation Bearers** (*Choephoroi*) — Orestes, Agamemnon's son, returns from exile and, **commanded by Apollo at Delphi**, murders his mother Clytemnestra in revenge for his father.
3. **Eumenides** — the **Erinyes** (Furies of blood-vengeance) pursue Orestes for matricide; he flees to Apollo, then to Athens; **the goddess [[athena]] establishes the Areopagus court of Athens** to try him; the human jury votes 6-to-6; **Athena's tie-breaking vote acquits Orestes**; the Erinyes, enraged, are persuaded by Athena to accept honored status as the **Eumenides** ("kindly ones") in the civic religion of Athens.

**The trilogy is the foundational dramatic articulation of Greek theology of cosmic justice** — the historical movement from **archaic blood-vengeance** (the Erinyes pursuing matricide with implacable chthonic-vendetta logic) **to civic-rational law** (the Areopagus court, the jury vote, the rule of *dikē* as juridical-political principle). And it is **the most influential single Greek tragic work** in the entire Western religious-political tradition.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Athens at the height of the Athenian Empire's early phase — the Delian League had been founded 19 years earlier; democracy was 50 years into its development; the conservative-aristocratic Areopagus court had just been **stripped of its political functions** by Ephialtes's reform (-462) and reduced to homicide jurisdiction only — a major political-religious controversy at the time. **The *Oresteia*'s climactic Areopagus-foundation scene** is read by many scholars as Aeschylus's **theological-political vindication** of the Areopagus's reduced (but sacralized) civic function: the court that judges Orestes is the same Areopagus that, under Ephialtes's reform, judges only homicide — and Aeschylus presents this as **religiously foundational** for Athens.
- **Religious milieu.** Athenian state-religious life is at its classical peak. The Eleusinian Mysteries are operative ~22 km away; the Dionysia at which Aeschylus is performing is itself a Dionysian-religious festival; the Areopagus court the play sacralizes is a real Athenian institution. **Religion and politics are not separable** at this period; Aeschylus's drama is religious-political-juridical theater simultaneously.
- **Who was behind it.** Aeschylus himself — Marathon-and-Salamis veteran, born at Eleusis, probable Eleusinian initiate — at age 67, two years before his death.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Byzantine "select" manuscript tradition. The trilogy as a whole is the gold standard of preserved Greek drama.

## Themes and motifs

- [[ethical-dualism]] — **blood-vengeance vs. civic-rational law** as the two opposed cosmic-juridical principles, dramatized as **the Erinyes vs. the Areopagus court**.

- [[final-judgment]] — the Areopagus court's trial of Orestes is the **dramatic prototype of judgment-court theology**, comparable in form (though not in content) to the Egyptian Hall of Two Truths ([[osiris]]'s afterlife court) and to Christian Last Judgment imagery.

- [[divine-kingship]] — Agamemnon's return from Troy as king-and-sacrificer-of-daughter; his murder as **religiously-juridically ambiguous act** (Clytemnestra's "righteous" revenge is itself crime requiring further punishment); the entire trilogy stages the **religious-political ambiguity of sacred kingship**.

- **The transformation of chthonic into civic.** The Erinyes (primordial Earth-born goddesses of blood-vengeance, predating the Olympians) are not destroyed or denied by the new Athenian-civic order; they are **integrated as the Eumenides** with a cult-shrine on the Areopagus slopes. This is **deep Eleusinian theology** — old chthonic mother-powers are integrated, not overcome, by the higher-order civic-religious cosmos. **This is the foundational Western articulation of how religious tradition can be transformed without being destroyed** — a structural pattern that Augustine's *City of God* receptions for Christian political theology.

- **Apollonian vs. Erinyean.** Apollo's command for matricide vs. the Erinyes's punishment for matricide — **the two divinities articulating opposed cosmic-juridical principles**. The play forces both to be reconciled by Athena's civic-juridical intervention.

## Connections (provisional)

### MASSIVE-win edges into Christianity

- → **The Areopagus court → Paul at the Areopagus (Acts 17).** **Paul preaches the gospel to the philosophers at the very Areopagus where Athena established the court of justice in the *Eumenides***. Acts 17:22–34 — Paul's speech at the Areopagus — is set in the **same physical location** Aeschylus had sacralized 5 centuries earlier as **the seat of civic-religious justice**. The structural-thematic continuity (a divine messenger arriving at the Areopagus to announce a new religious-juridical order, met with skeptical-civic response) is a **MASSIVE-win edge** that mainstream Acts-commentary rarely connects to Aeschylus, but the geographic-religious-juridical coincidence is uncontested. **Paul's Areopagus speech is, structurally, a Christian-eschatological reframing of the Aeschylean Areopagus-justice-founding.**

- → **Civic-justice-as-religious-cosmos → Augustinian *civitas Dei*.** Augustine *City of God* 2.19 and 19.21 engages classical-political-theology including Aeschylean material; Augustine's contrast of *civitas Dei* and *civitas terrena* is **structurally a Christian reframing of the Aeschylean transformation of chthonic-vendetta into civic-juridical justice**, with the Christian move adding the eschatological-transcendent dimension.

- → **The integration of old religious tradition rather than its destruction.** The *Oresteia*'s climactic move — Erinyes transformed into Eumenides, dark chthonic powers integrated into the new civic order — is **structurally the pattern Christian theology applies to its appropriation of pagan religious-philosophical material** (Justin: pagan logos was Christian logos in dispersed form; Origen: pagan philosophy is praeparatio evangelica; Augustine: Platonism's truths are Christian truths). **The Aeschylean integration-not-destruction model is foundational for Christian-pagan reception**.

### Within the vault

- → [[phase-2-008-homeric-epics]] — *commentary-on*: Aeschylus is reworking and theologically deepening Homeric material on the House of Atreus.
- → [[phase-2-030-euripides-bacchae]] — *parallel-motif* + *contrast*: both are tragic-religious-dramatic works at the Dionysia; Aeschylus's articulates confident archaic-into-classical religious cosmos; Euripides's stages religious-experiential rupture and divine irrationality. Together they map the range of Greek-tragic-religious vision.

## Disputes
- **Date.** -458 is uncontested (the *didascalia* — official Athenian production records — preserve it).
- **Political readings.** Whether the *Oresteia* is (a) pro-democratic (the Ephialtic reform of -462 vindicated through the Areopagus's religious sanctification of its reduced but sacralized function), (b) conservative-aristocratic (Aeschylus's lament for the old Areopagus dressed in religious praise), or (c) politically-balanced (showing the religious cost of any settlement) is debated. Goldhill 1992 favors (a) with caveats; older scholarship more often (b).
- **The Athena-vote interpretation.** Whether Athena's vote *breaks* a 6-6 tie or *creates* a 6-5 deadlock (the trial vote-counting is textually ambiguous) is a famous scholarly question. Most scholars accept the 6-6 + Athena-tie-breaker reading.
- **Religious vs. literary intent.** Whether Aeschylus's primary intent was religious (theological-juridical articulation) or literary (dramatic-poetic achievement) is anachronistic — both are simultaneously and inseparably the case in 5th-c. Athenian dramatic-religious culture.
