# Huangdi Neijing

## Identity

The **Huangdi Neijing** (黄帝内经, "Yellow Emperor's Classic of Internal Medicine") is the foundational theoretical text of Traditional Chinese Medicine, the Chinese medical tradition's equivalent of the Hippocratic Corpus. Composed c. 200 BCE–100 CE during the Han dynasty and attributed in the text to dialogues between the Yellow Emperor (Huangdi) and his divine physician-sage Qibo, it establishes the complete theoretical framework of TCM: qi, yin-yang, five phases, meridians, acupuncture, moxibustion, pulse diagnosis, and the body-cosmos correspondence that underlies all subsequent Chinese medical theory.

The text survives in two parts:
- **Suwen** (Basic Questions, 素問) — 81 chapters on fundamental theory, diagnosis, and treatment principles
- **Lingshu** (Spiritual Pivot / Divine Pivot, 靈樞) — 81 chapters on acupuncture and meridian theory

## Structure: Dialogue as Medical Canon

The Neijing is structured as a dialogue between the Yellow Emperor (Huangdi) and his physician Qibo — the sovereign asking questions, the sage answering. This form is not incidental: it encodes the text's authority-claim (the Yellow Emperor as the sage-ruler who systematized civilization asks; the divine physician as the highest medical authority answers), and it establishes medicine as a form of cosmological knowledge accessible to governance.

The same dialogue structure appears in the **Charaka Samhita** (Atreya and Agnivesha) and has analogues in Platonic dialogues on medicine — independent literary solutions to the problem of encoding authoritative medical knowledge.

## Five Phases ↔ Four Humors: The Cross-Tradition Parallel

The Neijing's five-phase (wuxing) system — wood/fire/earth/metal/water governing organs, seasons, emotions, flavours, and disease patterns — is the most structurally precise parallel to the Hippocratic four-humour system in ancient medicine. Both emerged c. 400–200 BCE with no documented contact (Lloyd 1996).

| Five Phases | Organ | Season | Emotion | Hippocratic Humour |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wood | Liver/Gallbladder | Spring | Anger | Blood (sanguine) |
| Fire | Heart/Small Intestine | Summer | Joy | Yellow bile (choleric) |
| Earth | Spleen/Stomach | Late summer | Worry | — |
| Metal | Lung/Large Intestine | Autumn | Grief | Black bile (melancholic) |
| Water | Kidney/Bladder | Winter | Fear | Phlegm (phlegmatic) |

Both systems encode: body participates in the cosmic element-cycle; disease is elemental imbalance; diagnosis identifies which element is excess or deficient; treatment restores elemental balance.
