# Psalms (the Psalter)

The **Psalms** (Hebrew *Təhillīm*, "Praises") are the **hymn- and prayer-book of ancient Israel** — 150 poems of praise, lament, thanksgiving, royal liturgy, wisdom, and trust, gathered into a five-book collection that became the devotional heart of both Judaism and Christianity. Traditionally ascribed to **David** (73 carry his name), the Psalter is in fact a **composite anthology** of many authors and centuries, redacted from the monarchic period through the Second Temple era. They are organized into genres — individual and communal laments, hymns of praise, royal psalms, songs of Zion, thanksgivings — that map onto the worship-life of the **Jerusalem Temple**.

## The cross-tradition wire (the MASSIVE-WINS)

The Psalms are among the richest sites of cross-tradition parallel in the whole Bible:

- **Psalm 104 ↔ the [[phase-1-011-great-hymn-to-aten|Great Hymn to the Aten]]** (Akhenaten, 14th c. BCE): the two share so much — God/the sun providing for all creatures, the lion seeking its prey by night, ships and Leviathan/sea-monsters at play, humans going out to labour by day — that a literary relationship (likely mediated) is widely argued.
- **Psalm 29 ↔ the [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle|Baal Cycle]]** and **[[phase-1-014-ugaritic-ritual-texts|Ugaritic]]** storm-poetry: Psalm 29's "voice of YHWH" thundering over the waters and shattering the cedars is, many scholars hold, a **Canaanite storm-god hymn (to Baal) re-attributed to YHWH** — and the recurring motif of YHWH defeating **Yam (Sea)** and **Leviathan** mirrors Baal's combat with Yam.
- The patriarchal-god **[[el-canaanite|ʾĒl]]** and the storm-god **[[baal-hadad|Baal]]** stand visibly behind the Psalter's language for **[[yahweh|YHWH]]**, who absorbs their titles, imagery, and combat-myths.

## Reach forward

The Psalms became the shared prayer-book of the West: the backbone of the Jewish liturgy, the **Divine Office** of Christian monasticism, the **most-quoted Old Testament book in the New Testament** (Psalms 2, 22, 110 frame the Passion and early Christology), the **Zabūr** given to Dāwūd in the Qurʾān, and the wellspring of a thousand years of hymnody, poetry, and music.
