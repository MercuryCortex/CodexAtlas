# First Epistle to Timothy

## What it is
The first and longest of the three Pastoral Epistles — letters of church order addressed to Paul's delegates rather than to congregations. 1 Timothy instructs Timothy, left in charge at Ephesus, on sound teaching against speculative false teachers, on the qualifications of overseers and deacons, on the conduct of worship and of various groups in the household of God, and on the dangers of wealth. It pictures a church becoming an ordered, enduring institution.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** The post-apostolic generation: structures (offices, qualifications, succession of teaching) replace the founding charisms.
- **Authorship.** With 2 Timothy and Titus, the leading case for pseudonymous "testamentary" composition in Paul's name.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Within the Pauline corpus, transmitted as a sub-group with Titus and 2 Timothy.

## Themes and motifs
- Church order — bishops/overseers, elders, deacons and their qualifications (ch. 3).
- Sound doctrine — guarding the "deposit" against "what is falsely called knowledge" (6:20).
- [[messianism]] — the confession "there is one God; there is also one mediator... Christ Jesus" (2:5).
