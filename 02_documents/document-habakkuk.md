# Book of Habakkuk

## What it is
A short prophetic book structured as a dialogue: the prophet twice complains to God — first that violence and injustice go unpunished in Judah, then that God's chosen instrument of punishment, the brutal Chaldeans (Babylonians), are worse than those they punish — and God answers. It closes with a magnificent theophany-psalm (ch. 3). Out of this wrestling with divine justice comes one of the most consequential single verses in the Bible: "the righteous shall live by his faith" (2:4).

## Primary text

### Habakkuk 2:4
*Trans. NRSV*
> Look at the proud! Their spirit is not right in them, but the righteous live by their faith.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Judah caught between dying Assyria and rising Babylon; the Chaldeans sweep westward after Carchemish (-605).
- **Religious milieu.** A frank theodicy: Habakkuk does not merely lament but cross-examines God about the moral order of history.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous canonical transmission; intensively reinterpreted at Qumran in the Habakkuk Pesher.

## Themes and motifs
- Theodicy — the problem of why God tolerates and even uses the wicked.
- Faith / faithfulness — 2:4, living by ʾemûnâ (steadfast trust), the verse with the longest downstream reach.
- [[chaoskampf]] — the closing theophany (ch. 3) depicts YHWH as the storm-warrior splitting earth and sea.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[document-jeremiah]] — *shared-milieu*: the same Babylonian-crisis horizon.
- → [[document-romans]] / [[document-hebrews]] — *direct-quote*: Hab 2:4 is quoted at Romans 1:17 (Paul's thesis) and Hebrews 10:38.
- → [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — *commentary-on*: the Habakkuk Pesher (1QpHab) reads the book verse-by-verse as a coded prophecy of the Qumran sect's own history.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Habakkuk 2:4 is the single verse on which both the Reformation and the Qumran sect built their identities.** Paul makes "the righteous shall live by faith" the thesis of Romans (1:17) and the engine of his gospel; Luther's rediscovery of this Pauline reading launched the Protestant Reformation. Independently, the Qumran community's Habakkuk Pesher (1QpHab) interprets the same verse of those who keep faith with the Teacher of Righteousness — "their faith in the Teacher" delivering them at judgment. One late-7th-century theodicy verse generated two of the most consequential faith-communities in Western history, each reading it as the key to salvation (Andersen 2001, AYB 25 pp.205–221; Roberts 1991, OTL pp.111–116).
