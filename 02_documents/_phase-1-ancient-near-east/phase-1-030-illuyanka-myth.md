# The Illuyanka Myth

## What it is
The Illuyanka Myth (CTH 321) is the Hittite chaoskampf text: the storm-god's combat with the serpent-dragon Illuyanka. Two distinct recensions survive on Boğazköy cuneiform tablets, both framed as ritual narratives recited at the *Purulli* festival — the Hittite spring new-year and agricultural-renewal feast. The text expressly names a priest, Kella, as the narrator-transmitter. This is one of the clearest cases in the Hittite corpus of a myth preserved *because* it was a ritual recitation, and of a chaoskampf integrated into seasonal cult.

**Old version.** Illuyanka defeats the Storm-God in an initial combat. The Storm-God's daughter Inara prepares a banquet of food and drink and persuades a mortal man named Hupasiya to help her — promising to be his (lover, wife — the term is ambiguous) in return. Illuyanka and his offspring come up from their hole, eat and drink to excess, are unable to return underground; Hupasiya binds Illuyanka with a rope; the Storm-God arrives and slays him. Inara installs Hupasiya in a house on a cliff, with the stipulation that he not look out the window at his wife and children; he does; she destroys him.

**Late version.** Illuyanka defeats the Storm-God in initial combat and takes his heart and eyes. The Storm-God begets a son by the daughter of a poor man and raises him; the son comes of age and marries Illuyanka's daughter; the Storm-God instructs his son to ask his father-in-law Illuyanka for the heart and eyes as a bride-price; restored, the Storm-God comes against Illuyanka. The son, having sworn loyalty to Illuyanka's house, insists his father slay him alongside his Illuyanka in-laws. The Storm-God does.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Old Hittite to imperial periods (~-1650 to -1180). The ritual was performed in the Hattusa cult-center.
- **Religious milieu.** Hattic-substrate dragon-slaying material in Hittite-imperial liturgical use. The *Purulli* festival celebrated the storm-god's renewed kingship over the cosmos — i.e., the chaoskampf is *re-enacted annually* to secure the world.
- **Who was behind it.** Hittite cult; transmitted by the priest Kella (named in the text); preserved in royal archives at Hattusa.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Boğazköy excavations from 1906; Hittite cuneiform deciphered by Hrozný in 1915; the Illuyanka myth was among the earliest Hittite religious texts identified and edited. Modern editions: Beckman 1982; Hoffner 1998.

## Themes and motifs
- [[chaoskampf]] — Anatolian instance. Storm-god vs. serpent-dragon; the storm-god's victory re-establishes cosmic order. Annual ritual re-enactment at Purulli.
- **Indo-European serpent-slaying formula.** Watkins (1995) showed that the Hittite phrase *MUŠilluyankan kuenta* "he slew the serpent" instantiates a PIE poetic formula *gʷhen- (ogʷhim)* "to slay (the serpent)," cognate to the Vedic *áhann áhim* used of Indra slaying Vritra. The Illuyanka myth is the Anatolian Indo-European cognate of the Vedic Indra-Vritra combat.
- **Ritual-myth integration.** Like the Telipinu myth, Illuyanka is a *ritual narrative* — it functions liturgically, not just narratively.
- **The folk-tale insertion (Old version).** Hupasiya-Inara is a folk-tale layer (the mortal helper rewarded with a goddess-mistress, then destroyed for breaking the taboo) embedded in the cosmological combat.
- **The tragic son (Late version).** The hybrid Storm-God-son who dies in his Illuyanka in-laws' house is a moral-narrative complication of the bare chaoskampf — perhaps reflecting a later, more reflective stage of the cult.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] — *parallel-motif* (chaoskampf): Marduk vs. Tiamat is the Mesopotamian instance of storm-warrior vs. chaos-serpent / chaos-sea. Annual ritual re-enactment at the Akitu festival in Babylon corresponds to the Anatolian re-enactment at Purulli — both are *new-year cosmic-renewal* uses of chaoskampf.
- → [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]] — *parallel-motif*: Baal vs. Yam (Sea) and Baal vs. Lotan (seven-headed serpent, KTU 1.5 I 1–3) — the Ugaritic Levantine instances of the same type.
- → [[phase-2-009-hesiod-theogony-works-and-days]] — *parallel-motif* / *direct-influence* (West 1997): Zeus's combat with Typhon / Typhoeus (Theog. 820–880) shares motifs with Illuyanka and is plausibly transmitted via Anatolian intermediaries. West argues direct transmission; Watkins argues a deeper PIE substrate ALSO operates, so both Anatolian transmission and inherited Indo-European formula reinforce each other in the Greek text.
- → [[phase-1-028-kumarbi-cycle]] — *parallel-motif* within the Hittite corpus: the Storm-God's combats with Hedammu and Ullikummi (in the Kumarbi cycle) are typologically continuous with the Illuyanka combat.
- → Hebrew Bible chaoskampf residues — *parallel-motif*: Yahweh's combat with Leviathan / Rahab / Yam (Job 26:12, Ps 74:13–14, Isa 27:1, 51:9–10) preserves the same chaoskampf type.
- → Vedic Indra-Vritra (Rigveda) — *parallel-motif* / *cognate*: PIE serpent-slaying formula (Watkins 1995).
- → Apollo-Python at Delphi, Heracles-Hydra, Perseus-Cetus — *parallel-motif*: later Greek instances of the dragon-slaying type.

## Disputes
- **PIE inheritance vs. Bronze Age transmission to Greece.** Both operate. Watkins's PIE formula is solid; West's transmission argument adds Anatolian-specific motifs (the heart-and-eyes theft, the daughter's marriage in the Late version) that recur in Hesiod's Typhonomachy. Most current scholars accept both inputs.
