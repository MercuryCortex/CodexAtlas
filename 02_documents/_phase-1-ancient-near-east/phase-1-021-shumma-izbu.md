# Šumma Izbu

## What it is
*Šumma izbu* ("If a malformed birth...") is the canonical Babylonian teratological-omen series — 24 tablets of omens drawn from anomalous human and animal births. Tablets 1–4 read malformations in human newborns; tablets 5–24 read malformations in animal newborns (especially sheep and goats, the principal sacrificial animals). Each omen specifies a particular anomaly (a lamb born with two heads, a lamb born without ears, a child born with extra fingers, etc.) and a paired political or agricultural prognosis aimed primarily at the king and the state. With [[phase-1-019-enuma-anu-enlil]] (celestial omens) and [[phase-1-020-shumma-alu]] (terrestrial omens), Šumma izbu rounds out the canonical Babylonian divinatory corpus.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Like the other omen series, Šumma izbu functions in royal-scholarly orbit. The court scholars (*barû*, *ṭupšar Enūma Anu Enlil*) reported anomalous-birth omens to the king alongside celestial signs; the State Archives of Assyria letter corpus preserves examples. Ritual countermeasures (*namburbi*) addressed ominous births.
- **Religious milieu.** The Babylonian view: anomalous birth is a divine sign, a *tāmītu* — the gods speaking through unusual nature. The *izbu* (the anomalous newborn) is treated not as a freak of nature but as a meaningful text. This is the deepest case of the Mesopotamian commitment that the natural world is divinely inscribed.
- **Who was behind it.** Babylonian scribal-scholarly class (*ṭupšarrū*); the series circulates in the canonical *iškaru* curriculum alongside the other great omen series.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Like the other omen series, recovered piecemeal from 19th- and 20th-century excavations of Nineveh, Assur, Sultantepe, and Babylon. Leichty 1970 (TCS 4) was the first comprehensive English-language edition; De Zorzi 2014 substantially expands and revises it.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divinatory-omen-reading]] — the teratomantic application of the canonical Babylonian omen form; anomalous births as divine inscription.
- [[wisdom-as-cosmic-order]] — even the most apparently chaotic biological event (a malformed birth) is legible to the trained scholar as ordered divine speech.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-1-019-enuma-anu-enlil]] / [[phase-1-020-shumma-alu]] — *shared-milieu*: the three great Babylonian omen series constitute a coherent scholarly system: sky (EAE), earth (Šumma ālu), anomalous birth (Šumma izbu). Same form, same scribal class, same curriculum. Refs: Maul 2018; De Zorzi 2014.
- → Hittite teratomantic texts at Boğazköy — *manuscript-transmission*: Šumma izbu material in Hittite-language adaptation appears in the Boğazköy archives (~-1400 to -1200), confirming Late Bronze Age circulation of the series beyond Mesopotamia.
- → Roman *prodigium* / *ostentum* literature (Julius Obsequens's *Prodigiorum Liber*, Pliny *Naturalis Historia* book 7) — *parallel-motif*: Roman teratomancy shares the conceptual core (anomalous birth as state-relevant divine sign) but the genealogical connection — whether via Etruscan haruspicy traditions or independent development — is unsettled. Briquel 1986 argues for indirect Mesopotamian-Etruscan-Roman descent.
- → Medieval European *monstrum* literature (Ambroise Paré's *Des monstres et prodiges*, 1573) — *parallel-motif*: the late descendants of the prodigium tradition in early modern medicine and demonology. Daston & Park, *Wonders and the Order of Nature* (1998), surveys the long history.

## Disputes
- **Genealogical relation to Roman prodigy literature.** Whether the Babylonian-Etruscan-Roman transmission is genealogical (Briquel's view) or whether Roman teratomancy is a parallel development is contested; the philological links Briquel identifies are suggestive but not coercive.
- **OB forerunner integration.** Leichty 1986 and De Zorzi 2014 differ in how much of the OB forerunner material can be securely fitted into the canonical 24-tablet sequence.
