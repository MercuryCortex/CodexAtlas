# The Telipinu Myth

## What it is
The Telipinu Myth (CTH 324) is the longest and best-preserved of a sub-genre of Hittite mythological-ritual texts known as the "vanishing-god" myths. Telipinu, the agricultural-vegetation son of the Storm-God of Hatti, becomes enraged for unspecified reasons, puts his shoes on the wrong feet in his haste, and storms off into the steppe and the marshland. His absence causes universal sterility: the hearths smoke but do not warm, the ewes refuse their lambs, the cows refuse their calves, the gods at their tables and the humans at theirs starve. The gods convene; envoys are sent — first the swift eagle, who searches the high mountains and the deep waters but does not find him; then the bee, who, despite the Storm-God's contempt for so small a messenger, finds Telipinu sleeping in a meadow and stings his hands and feet to wake him. Telipinu returns in worse anger than he left, threatening to destroy the gods and humans. Only the rituals of the goddess Kamrušepa — incantations of healing-magic accompanied by the symbolic objects of cedar resin, fig, olive, malt, beer-bread, honey, and butter — appease him. He returns to his temple; fertility returns; the king is blessed.

The text is partly *myth* and partly *ritual instruction*: the narrative of Telipinu's withdrawal is interleaved with the explicit ritual actions and incantations that re-enact and effect his recall. This is one of the clearest ancient examples of ritual-myth integration — the myth is told *in order to* perform the rite that brings the god back.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Old Hittite to early imperial period (~-1600 to -1400). Telipinu's cult is rooted in the Hattic-substrate vegetation religion of central Anatolia.
- **Religious milieu.** Hattic-substrate vegetation cult preserved and adapted into the Hittite imperial religion. The vanishing-god type is distinctively Anatolian (though typologically connected to Mesopotamian Dumuzi-descent traditions and Levantine Baal-descent narratives) — Anatolian religion preserves multiple instances (vanishing of the Sun-God, of the Storm-God of Nerik, of Hannahanna, of Inara, etc.). Telipinu's is the central, fullest version.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous Hittite scribal-priestly transmission; the text was used in ritual practice, not as detached myth.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Excavated at Boğazköy from 1906 onward. Edited by Laroche in the CTH catalog (1971); English translation in Hoffner's *Hittite Myths* (1990; 2nd ed. 1998); definitive modern monograph Mazoyer (2003).

## Themes and motifs
- [[dying-rising-god]] — Telipinu is the canonical Anatolian instance of the *disappearing-and-returning* god, the broader myth-type of which "dying and rising" is one variety. Note Mettinger (2001) cautions that the strict "dying and rising" category should be applied carefully; Telipinu vanishes and returns, but the text does not assert literal death. The motif-cluster is real and distinct from chaoskampf.
- **Cosmic infertility on the god's absence.** Universal sterility — agricultural, animal, even divine — follows the god's withdrawal. The cosmos is integrated; the god's presence is the world's fertility.
- **Ritual recall.** Kamrušepa's healing-magic with named substances (cedar, fig, olive, honey, etc.) is performative — the myth and ritual are coextensive.
- **Bee-as-finder.** The smallest envoy succeeds where the largest fails — a motif also found in folk-tale tradition broadly.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-1-017-descent-of-inanna]] — *parallel-motif*: Dumuzi's descent in Inanna's place; his return brings agricultural-seasonal restoration; the Tammuz cult (still attested in Ezekiel 8:14, where women weep for Tammuz at the Jerusalem temple gate) is the Mesopotamian descendant. Telipinu and Dumuzi belong to the same broad Bronze Age Near Eastern myth-cluster.
- → [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]] — *parallel-motif*: Baal descends into Mot's gullet and is later restored; same vanishing-and-returning vegetation-god pattern in the Ugaritic Levantine context. Anatolian and Ugaritic are sister-cousins in this type.
- → Later Adonis / Attis / Osiris myth-cluster — *parallel-motif*: the Hellenistic-period dying-and-returning gods are downstream cultural descendants of the Bronze Age Anatolian-Levantine type. (Osiris is older as Egyptian but functionally cognate.)
- → Persephone-Demeter Eleusinian myth — *parallel-motif*: Greek seasonal-agricultural variant of the same myth-type, with gender reversed; the Hellenistic-era reception sometimes treats them together.

## Disputes
- **"Dying and rising" classification.** Mettinger 2001 (*The Riddle of Resurrection*) cautioned against the over-broad category of "dying and rising gods" inherited from Frazer's *Golden Bough*; Telipinu's text does not say he dies, only that he withdraws. The "disappearing-and-returning" formulation is more accurate to the text. The motif-cluster is, however, genuine and pre-Hellenistic.
- **Dating layers.** The text combines older Hattic-substrate material with Hittite-period reworking; the strata are not always cleanly separable.
