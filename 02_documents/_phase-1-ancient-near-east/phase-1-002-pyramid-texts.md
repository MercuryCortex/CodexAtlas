# Pyramid Texts

## What it is
The oldest known corpus of Egyptian religious literature, carved on the interior walls of late Old Kingdom royal pyramids (Dynasties 5–6, ~2345–2150 BCE). Roughly 800 distinct "utterances" (PT 1–759 in Sethe's numbering, plus subsequent additions) of varying genre: offering rituals, resurrection spells, protective incantations against snakes and demons, sky-ascent texts, identifications of the deceased king with [[ra]], [[osiris]], and [[horus]], and short cosmogonic hymns. The texts are exclusive to royal burials in this period (their later, broader use is the [[phase-1-009-coffin-texts]]). They aim to secure the king's transformation into an *akh* (effective spirit) and his ascent to join the imperishable stars and the sun god.

## Context

800 spells carved on the walls of five Old Kingdom pyramids (Unas, Teti, Pepi I, Merenre, Pepi II); discovered 1880–1881 by Gaston Maspero. The texts are simultaneously: royal funerary spells enabling the dead pharaoh's resurrection and ascent to the stars; the earliest documentation of the Osiris/Isis/Set mythology in sustained narrative form; the source document for the Nut/Geb/Shu/Tefnut cosmogony; and the basis for all subsequent Egyptian funerary literature. No single pyramid contains all ~800 utterances — each pyramid has its own selection.

- **Political milieu.** Late Old Kingdom Egypt — the apex of pharaonic centralization at Memphis. Unas was the last king of Dynasty 5; the 6th Dynasty saw the corpus expanded under Teti, Pepi I, Merenre, and Pepi II. The corpus stops being inscribed in pyramids with the collapse of the Old Kingdom (~2150 BCE).
- **Religious milieu.** The royal mortuary cult is the central institution of Old Kingdom religion. Two cosmologies braid through the texts: a solar/celestial program centered on [[ra]] (the king ascends to the sun) and an Osirian/chthonic program centered on [[osiris]] (the king is identified with Osiris in a death-and-revivification pattern). The Heliopolitan creation sequence (Atum → Shu and Tefnut → Geb and [[nut]] → Osiris, Isis, Set, Nephthys) is structurally assumed.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous priestly composition by the temple staff of the royal mortuary cult, very likely drawing on much older oral ritual that had previously been performed at burials but never inscribed. Some scholars (Hays 2009, 2012) argue the texts directly preserve performance scripts; others read them as literary distillations.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The pyramid of Unas was opened in 1880 by Gaston Maspero, who identified the inscriptions as religious texts; Sethe's 4-volume edition (1908–1922) gave the corpus its scholarly form. Excavations of subsequent 6th Dynasty pyramids continued through the 20th century, with major additions still being published (e.g., the queens' pyramids of Pepi II's wives).

## MASSIVE WIN cross-tradition edges

### 1. Oldest religious literature in the world — the baseline for all subsequent tradition

The Pyramid Texts represent the world's earliest surviving sustained theological literature. Comparative chronology:

| Text | Earliest date | Distance from Pyramid Texts |
|------|--------------|----------------------------|
| **Pyramid Texts** | ~2345 BCE (inscription of Unas) | — baseline — |
| Gilgamesh Epic | ~2100 BCE (written; oral older) | ~245 years later |
| Rigveda (earliest hymns) | ~1500–1200 BCE | ~850–1145 years later |
| Hebrew Bible (earliest strata) | ~900–750 BCE | ~1445–1595 years later |
| Iliad | ~700 BCE composition | ~1645 years later |
| Upanishads (earliest) | ~700–500 BCE | ~1645–1845 years later |

The Pyramid Texts predate the Rigveda by roughly 1,000 years and the Hebrew Bible by roughly 1,500 years. They constitute the oldest surviving record of: an afterlife theology, a resurrection narrative (Osiris), a cosmogony in sustained literary form, and divine-human identification language. This makes the Egyptian theological tradition the *baseline* against which all subsequent ancient Near Eastern and Mediterranean religious developments must be measured. Wire to [[tradition-egyptian-religion]], [[phase-1-009-coffin-texts]], [[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]].

### 2. The stellar vs. solar vs. Osirian afterlife — Egyptian religion's internal development

One of the most revealing features of the Pyramid Texts is the presence of **three competing afterlife theologies** within a single corpus:

**a. Stellar afterlife (circumpolar theology)**: The earliest and perhaps oldest stratum. The dead pharaoh ascends to join the *akhw* ("imperishable stars") — the circumpolar stars that never set below the horizon. He becomes an *akh* (transfigured effective spirit) in the sky. Key utterances: PT 245–246 (the deceased crosses to the far side of the sky where the stars do not set), PT 262–264 (ascending to heaven on the wings of the divine bird).

> *"O you great one who sees his father, keeper of the book of Thoth, behold, I have come to you... that I may see my father, that I may see Re."* (PT 216)

**b. Solar afterlife (Ra-union theology)**: The deceased joins [[ra]] in his solar barque; the king sails across the sky with the sun. He is reborn each morning with the sun.

**c. Osirian afterlife (death-resurrection theology)**: The deceased is identified with [[osiris]] — murdered by [[set]], mourned and restored by [[isis]], revivified and justified. The deceased *is* Osiris in death.

These three theologies are in tension throughout the corpus and are never synthesized into a fully coherent system. The Amduat ([[phase-1-036-amduat]]) later achieves a partial synthesis: the Ra-Osiris midnight union brings solar and Osirian theologies together. The [[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]] inherits all three strands simultaneously. The tension between stellar, solar, and Osirian models is one of Egyptian religion's most interesting internal developments. Wire to [[osiris]], [[ra]], [[theme-graduated-afterlife]].

### 3. The Nut vignette — cosmic digestion/gestation as the template of resurrection

[[nut]], the sky goddess, swallows the sun each evening (*zam-mutef*: "he who seizes the sun's disk at the western horizon") and gives birth to it each morning at dawn. In the Pyramid Texts, this same cosmic process is applied to the dead pharaoh: Nut swallows the king and gives birth to him as a star. The resurrection of the dead king is explicitly made structurally identical to the sun's daily rebirth — the same physical process (cosmic ingestion, gestation, and parturition) applies to both celestial bodies and to human souls.

> *"O my mother Nut, spread yourself over me, so that I may be placed among the imperishable stars which are in you, and that I may not die."* (PT 825)

This is theologically distinctive: resurrection is not a miraculous exception to nature but an **application of nature's most fundamental cycle** (celestial birth and death) to the human soul. Wire to [[nut]], [[ra]], [[osiris]].

### 4. Utterance 600 and the Memphite theology in seed form

The Pyramid Texts contain the earliest seeds of what will become the **Memphite Theology** (fully articulated in the [[phase-1-027-memphite-theology-shabaka-stone]], ~700 BCE but almost certainly preserving Old Kingdom material). Utterance 600:

> *"O Atum-Khepri, you were high as the hill; you rose up as the benbenet stone in the Benben-precinct in Heliopolis; you spit out Shu, you expectorated Tefnut, and you put your arms around them..."*

Present here in embryonic form: the **self-created primordial deity** (Atum rising from [[nun]]/primordial waters on the primordial mound), the **benben stone** as the original sacred object (the apex of creation, later the obelisk-tip and pyramid-apex), and the **creative act by bodily emission** (spitting/masturbating → the first divine pair). The Memphite Theology around [[ptah]] develops this into a fully articulated **creation-by-speech** doctrine (*Ptah conceived in his heart and spoke with his tongue*). The Pyramid Texts contain the Heliopolitan physical-creation version; the Shabaka Stone contains the Memphite mental-and-verbal creation version. Together they form the complete Egyptian **creation-by-word** theology. Wire to [[phase-1-027-memphite-theology-shabaka-stone]], [[creation-by-word]], [[ptah]], [[atum]].

### 5. The Cannibal Hymn — archaic power-incorporation theology

Utterances 273–274, the **Cannibal Hymn**, describe the dead pharaoh hunting, killing, and *eating* the gods to absorb their divine power:

> *"The sky is overcast, the stars are darkened, the celestial expanses quiver, the bones of the earth-gods tremble... when they see the King appearing in power as a god who lives on his fathers and feeds on his mothers... The King is the bull of the sky who conquers at will, who lives on the being of every god, who eats their entrails."*

This is not metaphor in any attenuated sense — it is an archaic **power-incorporation theology** in which the king's immortality is literally grounded in the consumption of divine substance. It stands in striking contrast to the later, more ethical Osirian theology of *maa-kheru* (being "true of voice" = justified). The Cannibal Hymn is a window into a pre-classical strand of Egyptian religion that predates the moralizing of the afterlife (which culminates in the Negative Confession of [[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]] Chapter 125). Comparative note: similar **power-incorporation** logics appear in the Vedic *soma* ritual (priest ingests divine substance), the Christian Eucharist (consuming the body and blood of Christ), and certain Tantric rites — though direct transmission is not claimed. Edge type: *parallel-motif*.

## Themes and motifs
- [[afterlife-judgment]] — earliest large-scale articulation of an Egyptian afterlife program: the king ascends, joins the gods, and is justified before them. The fully developed weighing-of-the-heart scene crystallizes later (in the [[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]]), but the lexicon of "justification" (*maa-kheru*) is already present.
- [[divine-kingship]] — the king *is* a god in death (identified with Horus in life, with Osiris in death). Pharaonic divinity is sealed in his funerary texts.
- [[primordial-waters]] — [[nun]] as the primordial ocean from which Atum self-emerges (PT 600 and elsewhere); the king is reborn out of Nun in some spells.
- [[creation-by-word]] — Atum creates the first gods by uttering / spitting / masturbating them forth (the variants exist within the corpus); naming is creative.
- **Sky-ladder ascent** — the king climbs to the sky on a ladder, on the sunbeams, on the wings of Thoth as ibis, on the bull of heaven. Stellar destiny is as important as the solar.
- **Cannibal Hymn** (PT 273–274) — the king devours the gods and absorbs their powers — a striking glimpse of an archaic, pre-classical strand within Egyptian religion.

## Connections

- → [[phase-1-009-coffin-texts]] — *redaction-of* / *direct-quote*: many Coffin Text spells are direct adaptations of Pyramid Text utterances, democratized to non-royal elite use. Ref: Allen 2005; Faulkner, *The Ancient Egyptian Coffin Texts*.
- → [[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]] — *redaction-of*: the New Kingdom Book of the Dead is a further democratization of the Coffin Texts, with continuous spell-lineage traceable back to the Pyramid Texts. Ref: Allen 2005; Hornung, *The Ancient Egyptian Books of the Afterlife*.
- → [[phase-1-036-amduat]] — *parallel-motif* / *shared-milieu*: the Amduat synthesizes and reorganizes PT imagery (solar barque, underworld geography, Ra-Osiris theology) into the first coherent narrative geography of the Duat. The PT are the raw theological material; the Amduat is the narrative synthesis.
- → [[phase-1-027-memphite-theology-shabaka-stone]] — *parallel-motif* → *ancestor-of*: the PT's Heliopolitan creation cosmogony (Atum self-creation, benben stone) is the older stratum that the Memphite Theology develops and reformulates as creation-by-speech via [[ptah]]. Allen (1988) *Genesis in Egypt* traces the relationship. Edge: the PT → Memphite Theology is a case of *ancestor-of* within Egyptian tradition.
- → [[creation-by-word]] — *ancestor-of* (within tradition): PT Utterance 600's creation-by-speech/physical-emission is the seed of the full Memphite *Logos* theology.
- → [[osiris]], [[ra]], [[nut]], [[ptah]], [[atum]] — core deities whose mythologies are first attested in sustained literary form in the PT.
- → Hebrew Bible afterlife and resurrection language — *parallel-motif* (loose): the Osirian-justification vocabulary is part of the wider Ancient Near Eastern lexicon from which later Jewish resurrection ideas drew, though most direct influence is on Greek mystery religion and Hellenistic Judaism rather than the Hebrew Bible itself. Ref: Smith 2017.
- → Vedic soma ritual, Christian Eucharist — *parallel-motif* (loose, cross-cultural): the Cannibal Hymn's power-incorporation theology structurally parallels divine-substance-consumption in later traditions; no transmission claimed.

## Disputes
- **Date of composition vs. date of inscription.** Inscriptions begin in Unas (~2345 BCE), but the consensus is that the texts are much older oral compositions; how much older is contested (Sethe argued Predynastic; current scholarship is more conservative).
- **Performance vs. text.** Hays (2009, 2012) argues many utterances are direct ritual-performance scripts; older scholarship treated them more as literary deposits. Both positions remain active.
- **Royal vs. broader audience.** Whether the program reflects narrowly royal theology or a wider Egyptian afterlife belief is debated — the parallel democratization in the Coffin Texts suggests broader uptake was latent.
