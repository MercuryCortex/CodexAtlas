# Yijing (Book of Changes)

## What it is
A divinatory-cosmological manual originally consisting of 64 hexagrams — six-line figures constructed of broken (yin) and unbroken (yang) lines, each accompanied by a hexagram statement and six line statements. The original *Zhou Yi* (Western Zhou, ~-900 to -700) was a working oracular handbook: a diviner would generate a hexagram by manipulating yarrow stalks and read the resulting statements as guidance. From the 4th c. BCE onward, the Yijing was overlaid with the *Ten Wings* — seven cosmological-philosophical appendices that re-interpreted the hexagrams as a complete cosmology of yin-yang interaction and constant transformation (*yi* 易, "change"). The combined work became one of the Five Classics, and from the Han period onward functioned both as a continuing oracle and as the symbolic substrate for Chinese natural philosophy, medicine, statecraft, alchemy, and cosmology. In the modern era it became (via Leibniz, Jung, and the Wilhelm/Baynes translation) the most globally influential single Chinese book.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Western Zhou court of the late 9th-7th c. BCE for the Zhou Yi core; Warring States and early Han for the Ten Wings. Hexagram statements bear marks of Western Zhou language and historical reference (Shaughnessy 1996 reconstructs); the Ten Wings reflect the cosmological synthesis of Warring States and early Han under the rising yin-yang and Five Phases (*wuxing*) speculation.
- **Religious milieu.** The Yijing originated in continuity with the Shang oracle-bone tradition (a different technology — milfoil rather than scapulae — but the same divinatory imagination). By the Warring States and Han, the Yijing was being read cosmologically rather than purely oracularly; it became the canonical text of an emerging Chinese natural philosophy.
- **Who was behind it.** Western Zhou court diviners for the hexagram and line statements; later Confucian-influenced cosmological writers for the Ten Wings. The traditional attribution chain (King Wen authored hexagram statements while imprisoned by Shang king Zhou; Duke of Zhou authored line statements; Confucius authored Ten Wings) is now rejected as legend. Mawangdui-silk and Shanghai-slip discoveries provide direct evidence for the layered composition.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Chinese transmission as a Five-Classic. Major modern recensions: Wang Bi (~250 CE) for the received text; Mawangdui silk mss (excavated 1973) for the earliest substantial physical witness; Shanghai Museum bamboo slips (Warring States) for a pre-Mawangdui divinatory-manual recension. European reception: Jesuit translations from the 17th c.; Leibniz's 1703 letter to Bouvet identifying the hexagram sequence with binary arithmetic; Legge 1882; Wilhelm 1924 (Baynes English 1950 with Jung preface); the I Ching's massive American counter-cultural reception 1960s onward.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divinatory-omen-reading]] — the Zhou Yi core is one of the world's most influential divinatory-manual genres. Cf. the Mesopotamian *šumma* corpora (*Enuma Anu Enlil*, *Shumma Alu*, *Shumma Izbu*) which use a closely parallel "if X then Y" omen-syntax.
- [[divinatory-calendar]] — by the Han, the 64 hexagrams were correlated with the year's 24 solar nodes, the 12 months, and various calendrical schemata; the Yijing became a key vehicle of Chinese calendrical-cosmological thought.
- **Yin-yang and the eight trigrams (*bagua*)** — the 8 trigrams (Qian, Kun, Zhen, Xun, Kan, Li, Gen, Dui) representing Heaven, Earth, Thunder, Wind, Water, Fire, Mountain, Lake; their doubling produces the 64 hexagrams. Cosmological reading: every configuration of yin and yang corresponds to a phase of cosmic process.
- [[microcosm-macrocosm]] / [[as-above-so-below]] — the hexagram as a model of cosmic process; the diviner's situation mirrors the cosmos; "human" and "Heaven" share a single pattern.
- [[cosmic-cycles]] — change (*yi*) as the supreme reality; all states give way to their opposites; the cosmos is in continual transformation.
- **Sage as reader of patterns** — the Ten Wings present the sage (especially Confucius) as the one who can read cosmic configurations and align with them.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-1-019-enuma-anu-enlil]] / [[phase-1-020-shumma-alu]] / [[phase-1-021-shumma-izbu]] — *parallel-motif*: cross-tradition substrate of ancient divinatory-omen literature, syntactic and functional parallel ("if X is observed, then Y will happen / the situation is Y"). No transmissional claim; the parallel is structural. The substrate is so widespread (cf. Mesoamerican *tonalpohualli*, Vedic *muhūrta*, the Etruscan haruspex tradition) that it is fairly characterized as a cross-tradition substrate genre.
- → [[phase-2-014-daodejing]] — *shared-milieu*: the Daodejing and the Ten Wings emerge from the same Warring States cosmological-philosophical milieu; both presume change, polar complementarity (yin-yang), and the sage's ability to align with cosmic rhythm. The Ten Wings can be read as in part a Confucian appropriation of Daoist cosmological vocabulary.
- → [[phase-2-015-analects-of-confucius]] — *attributed-to* (rejected): the traditional ascription of the Ten Wings to Confucius is the foundation of the Yijing's canonical authority in Confucian tradition; modern scholarship rejects the attribution but acknowledges that the Ten Wings are deeply Confucian in tone.
- → [[shangdi]] / [[tian-heaven]] — *substrate*: the Yijing emerges from a religious universe presupposing Heaven as the source of cosmic order.

## Cross-tradition substrate
- **Divinatory-calendar genre.** The Yijing belongs to a cross-tradition genre of divinatory cosmologies that correlate symbolic units (hexagrams, omens, day-signs) with calendar, weather, fortune, and ritual. Mesopotamian *Enuma Anu Enlil* (celestial omens), *Shumma Alu* (terrestrial omens), and *Shumma Izbu* (birth omens) are syntactically and functionally parallel. Vedic *muhūrta* (astrologically auspicious moments) and the Etruscan haruspex tradition share the structural assumption. Mesoamerican *tonalpohualli* (the 260-day divinatory calendar) and the Maya calendar's day-sign system likewise correlate symbolic units with destiny. Cross-tradition substrate: the human universal of treating chance-procedures as patterned access to cosmic order. (Smith 2008 treats this comparatively; Rochberg 2004 *The Heavenly Writing* for the Mesopotamian side; Aveni 2002 for the Mesoamerican side.)
- **Yi Jing and binary arithmetic (Leibniz).** Leibniz's 1703 *Explication de l'arithmétique binaire* identified the hexagram sequence (in the *Fu Xi* order) with binary numerals 0-63. This was the first European recognition of a non-European system as mathematically interesting on its own terms — a milestone of cross-tradition intellectual exchange. (Mungello 1985, *Curious Land*; Smith 2008 ch. 7.)
- **Jung and synchronicity.** Jung's 1949 foreword to Wilhelm/Baynes used the Yijing as the empirical reference for his concept of synchronicity (acausal meaningful coincidence). Jung's reception of the Yijing through Wilhelm is foundational for the modern Western reception of Chinese thought as relevant to psychology.

## Disputes
- **Authorship of Ten Wings.** Traditional: Confucius (and earlier, King Wen and Duke of Zhou for the core). Modern: the Wings are 4th-2nd c. BCE Confucian-influenced compositions; Confucius did not write them. Nylan 2001 ch. 5 surveys.
- **Date of the Zhou Yi core.** Probably late Western Zhou / early Spring-and-Autumn (~-900 to -700). Some specific line statements appear to reference 9th c. BCE events (Shaughnessy 1996).
- **Mawangdui vs. received text.** The Mawangdui recension has a substantially different hexagram order and includes five commentaries not in the received text. Whether the Mawangdui order or the received order is older is debated.
- **Oracle vs. wisdom-text.** Whether the Yijing should be read fundamentally as an oracle, as a philosophical-cosmological text, or as both, is a long-running debate within Chinese tradition.
- **European reception.** The "I Ching" as received in mid-20th-c. Western culture (via Wilhelm-Baynes-Jung) emphasizes the Ten Wings' cosmological reading; the *Zhou Yi* core's divinatory function is sometimes obscured. Recent translations (Shaughnessy, Field) deliberately recover the divinatory-manual character.

## Cross-tradition MASSIVE WIN parallels

**1. Yi Jing divination ↔ universal oracle systems — randomness as cosmic communication**
The Yijing's method (random-chance generation of a symbol from a limited set → interpretation of the symbol's meaning in context) is structurally identical to every major oracle system:
- **Mesopotamian extispicy / hepatoscopy**: reading the liver of a sacrificed animal — random pattern → cosmic meaning
- **Urim and Thummim** (Hebrew Bible, Exodus 28:30): two stones giving binary yes/no divine answers
- **Greek oracle** (Delphi): question posed; ecstatic utterance from the Pythia; interpretation required
- **Roman augury**: bird flight patterns as random cosmic signals interpreted by the augur
- **Islamic istikhara**: random Quran-opening for divine guidance
- **Tarot** (14th c. onward): random card-draw interpreted through symbolic correspondence

All oracle systems share the same deep logic: **randomness as the entry-point for cosmic communication**, because the human will cannot control what is random — therefore a random outcome reflects the non-human (cosmic/divine) order. The Yijing is the most mathematically systematized and long-lived of these systems.

**2. Taiji → Yin/Yang → 10,000 things = universal emanationist cosmogony**
The Ten Wings' cosmogonic sequence (undivided unity → binary opposition → multiplicity) is structurally identical to:
- **Daodejing** ch. 42: "Dao gives birth to One; One gives birth to Two; Two gives birth to Three; Three gives birth to the ten thousand things"
- **Plotinian Neoplatonism**: The One → Nous → Soul → Matter — descending emanation from unity to multiplicity
- **Kabbalistic Tzimtzum**: Ein Sof (undivided infinite) → contraction → first point → sefirot → creation
- **Hermetic Poimandres**: undivided Nous → Light/Darkness separation → four elements → the material world

All four are independent instantiations of the same cosmogonic logic: primordial unity generates multiplicity through sequential differentiation. The Yijing's version is notable for being the most explicitly *mathematical* (binary combinatorics) and the earliest to generate a complete combinatorial map of all possible states.

**3. MASSIVE WIN: 64 hexagrams ↔ 64 DNA codons — convergent binary coding of universal information**
The structural parallel Leibniz noticed (hexagrams = binary sequences) has a modern extension: **DNA's genetic code** operates through four bases (A, T, G, C) read in triplets (codons) = 4³ = 64 possible codons. The Yijing: 2 elements (yin/yang) in sequences of 6 = 2⁶ = 64 hexagrams. The biological universe encodes all life information in 64 units; the Yijing encodes all cosmic-change information in 64 units. Both are binary-combinatorial systems that cover a complete state-space. The parallel is typological (no transmission) — but it is the kind of structural resonance the Atlas exists to document.

**4. Yi Jing → Cantong Qi → Jabir = the alchemy transmission spine's cosmological root**
The Yijing's yin-yang hexagram system → [[phase-4-096-cantong-qi]]'s alchemical hexagram calendar (~142 CE) → Jabirian sulphur-mercury polarity theory (~780-950 CE) → European sulphur-mercury-salt → Lavoisier's critique → modern chemistry. The Yijing is the conceptual origin of a transmission chain that reaches Isaac Newton's alchemical notebooks. The key move: Cantong Qi maps the 64 hexagrams onto the lunar month to time alchemical operations, making the Yijing the mathematical backbone of Chinese alchemy.

**5. Divination as pre-scientific probability management ↔ Pascal's Wager ↔ Bayesian reasoning**
The Yijing's divinatory logic — using a structured randomness protocol to navigate genuine uncertainty — is the ancient Chinese parallel to the Western tradition of formal decision-making under uncertainty. Pascal's Wager (1670): structured reasoning about unknown outcomes under uncertainty. Bayesian probability (1763): updating probability estimates based on evidence. The Yijing: structured randomness protocol + interpretive tradition to navigate unknowable futures. All three address the same human problem (radical uncertainty about future outcomes) with structurally analogous moves.
