# The Kumarbi Cycle

## What it is
The Kumarbi cycle is a sequence of Hurrian-language religious-epic compositions (designated SÌR, "song," in the Hittite scribal tradition), preserved on cuneiform tablets from the Hittite imperial archive at Hattusa in both Hurrian originals and Hittite translations. The cycle narrates the succession of cosmic kingships among the gods: Alalu, Anu, Kumarbi, and finally Teshub the storm-god. The three principal surviving compositions are:

- **Song of Going Forth (Song of Kumarbi / Song of Emergence) — CTH 344.** The succession proper: Alalu rules heaven for nine years; Anu deposes him; nine years later Kumarbi attacks Anu, who flees skyward; Kumarbi seizes him by the feet, drags him down, and *bites off Anu's genitals*, swallowing them. Kumarbi thereby becomes pregnant with five "terrible gods" — including the storm-god Teshub, the Tigris (a river-god), and Tašmišu. The text breaks off shortly after the birth of Teshub from Kumarbi's body, but the narrative arc (continued in the Hedammu and Ullikummi songs) is Teshub's overthrow of his father Kumarbi to assume cosmic kingship.
- **Song of Hedammu — CTH 348.** Kumarbi, deposed, fathers a sea-monster Hedammu on the daughter of the Sea, in order to retake the throne. Teshub's sister Šauška (the Hurrian Ishtar) lures Hedammu out of the sea by appearing naked on the shore, gets him drunk, and the Storm-God's faction destroys him.
- **Song of Ullikummi — CTH 345.** Kumarbi fathers a stone-monster Ullikummi on a great rock; Ullikummi grows in the sea, blinds the Sun-God, drives the gods from heaven; the wise Ea descends and severs Ullikummi from the shoulder of the giant Ubelluri (the world-supporting Atlas-figure) on whom he stands, using the very cutting-tool the older gods used to separate heaven and earth; with Ullikummi cut off from his support, Teshub defeats him and reclaims kingship.

Additional partly preserved compositions include the *Song of LAMMA*, *Song of Silver*, and *Song of the Sea*, all belonging to the same Hurrian theogonic-succession cycle.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late Bronze Age Hittite Empire (~-1400 to -1180). The Hurrian-Hittite religious-political system: the Hittite Great Kings adopted significant Hurrian theology, especially under the dynasty descending from Tudhaliya II / Suppiluliuma I and the Hurrian queen-mothers (Puduhepa being the most prominent). The cycle's preservation at Hattusa is partly an artifact of this imperial integration of Hurrian cult.
- **Religious milieu.** A genuinely Hurrian theology preserved in Hittite-imperial archives. Kumarbi is the Hurrian "father of the gods" displaced by his storm-son; the succession is the central Hurrian theogonic narrative. The cycle was performed orally before being committed to writing — the Hittite term SÌR indicates a "song" (a sung composition) of a chanter who would have been trained in Hurrian-language religious-epic recitation.
- **Who was behind it.** The Hurrian compositional tradition; copied by Hittite scribes at Hattusa. The 1946 Güterbock edition (*Kumarbi*) brought the cycle to scholarly visibility and immediately raised the comparative question: was Hesiod's Theogony derivative? Subsequent scholarship has overwhelmingly answered yes — that the structural and motivic parallels are too specific to be independent.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Excavations at Boğazköy (ancient Hattusa) by Hugo Winckler and Theodor Makridi for the German Oriental Society, starting 1906. Bedřich Hrozný deciphered Hittite cuneiform in 1915 (identifying Hittite as an Indo-European language). Editions and translations of the cycle began with Forrer and Güterbock in the 1930s–40s; Güterbock's 1946 *Kumarbi* monograph and Hoffner's 1990 (2nd ed. 1998) *Hittite Myths* are the keystone modern editions.

## Themes and motifs
- [[chaoskampf]] — Teshub's combats with Hedammu (the sea-monster) and Ullikummi (the stone-giant) are Anatolian instances of the storm-warrior's combat with chaos-monsters, generically cognate with Marduk vs. Tiamat ([[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]]), Baal vs. Yam ([[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]]), and Zeus vs. Typhon ([[phase-2-009-hesiod-theogony-works-and-days]]).
- [[divine-kingship]] — the cycle's spine is the *transfer of cosmic kingship* across generations; the Hittite king's earthly kingship is grounded in the Storm-God's cosmic kingship as established here.
- [[divine-council]] — the gods deliberate, send envoys, assign tasks throughout the cycle (especially in the Ullikummi song where Ea is summoned to consult).
- **Succession by violence on the father.** Anu castrated by Kumarbi (by biting); Kumarbi overthrown by Teshub. This is the *succession motif* that becomes Hesiod's Ouranos castrated by Kronos (with the sickle) and Kronos overthrown by Zeus.
- **Swallowing and birth from the body.** Kumarbi swallows Anu's genitals and is impregnated; the gods are born from him (and from a rock he spits out). In Hesiod, Kronos swallows his children whole. Same motif element, recombined: the divine swallowing.
- **Stone-Atlas.** Ubelluri, who holds up heaven and earth and on whose shoulder Ullikummi grows, is the Anatolian Atlas — direct iconographic-functional cognate of Greek Atlas.

## MASSIVE WIN: The Bachvarova Transmission Argument — Best-Documented ANE→Greek Source Text

The Kumarbi Cycle is **the vault's single best-documented non-Greek source for Hesiodic theogony** — the one case where the fact of transmission from the Ancient Near East to Greece is treated as established in mainstream classical scholarship, with debate limited to *mechanism* and *route*, not *whether*.

### The Transmission Chain

**Step 1: Hurrian oral composition (~-1600 BCE)**
The succession-myth tradition — Alalu → Anu → Kumarbi → Teshub — originated in Hurrian-language religious epic, probably in the Kizzuwatna / North Syrian zone where Hurrian theological culture was concentrated. The genre (*SÌR*, "song") implies oral-performance origin: a chanter (*kaluti* in Hurrian) who sang these myths in a ritual context.

**Step 2: Hittite imperial archive at Hattusa (~-1300 BCE)**
The cycle was translated into Hittite and written on cuneiform tablets for the Boğazköy archive. The surviving tablets (CTH 344–348) are the physical witnesses, but they encode material at least 300 years older. The Hittite scribal context also explains Hurrian-to-Hittite adaptation: certain Hurrian theonyms (Anu, Kumarbi) are preserved even in Hittite-language passages, reflecting the Hurrian theological prestige.

**Step 3: Late Bronze Age Aegean-Anatolian itinerant singer networks (~-1300 to -800 BCE)**
Bachvarova (2016) reconstructs the transmission mechanism: *itinerant singers* circulating across the Aegean-Anatolian zone carried mythological material orally. The Late Bronze Age trade networks (Ugarit ↔ Cyprus ↔ Mycenae ↔ Anatolia) provided the highways. Bachvarova argues this network was *continuous from the Bronze Age forward*, meaning the political collapse of ~-1200 BCE did not sever the transmission — it continued through smaller-scale cultural contact, Phoenician trade, and the Iron Age successor states.

**Step 4: Phoenician intermediary layer (~-1000 to -800 BCE)**
The critical gap-filling witness is the **Phoenician History of Sanchuniathon**, preserved in Philo of Byblos (~100 CE, in Eusebius *Praeparatio Evangelica* I.10). Philo records a Phoenician theogony with a recognizable succession structure:
- Elioun ("Most High") → Epigeios / Uranos (Sky) — first generation
- Epigeios/Uranos **castrated by his son El-Kronos** — second generation
- El-Kronos / Demarous (Hadad the storm-god) inherits kingship — third generation

This is the Kumarbi Cycle's three-generation succession transposed into Phoenician divine names. Burkert (1992) argues this Phoenician layer was the *direct* intermediary through which the succession myth reached archaic Greece — Phoenician traders and cultural brokers in the 8th-7th c. BCE "Orientalizing" period. Bachvarova (2016) accepts this as one mechanism among several (not mutually exclusive with Bronze Age continuity).

**Step 5: Hesiod's Theogony (~-700 BCE)**
The Ouranos → Kronos → Zeus succession in Hesiod's Theogony is the Greek end-product of this transmission chain. The correspondences are too specific to be coincidental:
- **Ouranos / Anu**: sky-god who rules as the first divine king; castrated by the next generation
- **Kronos / Kumarbi**: castrates/attacks the sky-father, swallows divine substance (children in Hesiod; Anu's genitals in the Hurrian text), is eventually overthrown by his storm-god son
- **Zeus / Teshub**: storm-warrior who defeats the usurping father and establishes permanent cosmic kingship; also fights monsters (Typhon / Ullikummi, Typhon / Hedammu)
- **Typhonomachy / Ullikummi Song**: Zeus's fight with Typhon (Theogony 820–880) has specific structural and motivic parallels with Teshub's fight with Ullikummi — including the role of the wise god (Hermes / Ea) in resolving the crisis

West (1997, pp. 276–305) lists more than a dozen specific motivic correspondences. The fact that Hesiod's theogony agrees with the Hurrian-Hittite cycle in structure *and* in multiple specific details is the core evidential argument for transmission rather than independent invention.

## Disputes
- **Mechanism of transmission to Hesiod.** The fact of transmission is uncontested. The mechanism divides scholarship:
  - **Direct Bronze Age literary/oral dependence** (West 1997, with significant precursors in Walcot 1966 *Hesiod and the Near East*). On this view, the Kumarbi cycle's textual fingerprints — including specific phrasings — survive into Hesiod via Mycenaean-Anatolian oral-poetic continuity.
  - **8th-/7th-c. Phoenician intermediation** (Burkert 1992). On this view, the Bronze Age is too remote; transmission is via Phoenician singers and scribes in the Orientalizing period, with Phoenician cosmogony (Sanchuniathon) as the intermediate witness.
  - **Continuous Aegean-Anatolian itinerant-singer transmission** (Bachvarova 2016). On this view, both mechanisms operate; the question is poorly framed as either-or; itinerant singers carry material across the Aegean-Anatolian zone from the LBA forward, and Phoenician contact is one input among many.
- **Dating of the original Hurrian composition.** The Hittite-language tablets are mostly ~-1300 to -1200, but they translate Hurrian originals plausibly going back to ~-1600. Whether the cycle is a Hurrian original ab initio or a Hurrian reworking of Mesopotamian succession material (the Anzu cycle, the Ninurta cycle) is debated.
- **Whether Kumarbi corresponds to Kronos in detail.** Lucian (*On the Syrian Goddess*) and Philo of Byblos (via Eusebius) explicitly identify the Phoenician El (Elioun's son) with Kronos in the succession-myth role; whether Kumarbi specifically lies behind this identification, or whether Kumarbi and El converged on a shared role via independent paths, is a finer question.
