# Shijing (Book of Songs / Classic of Poetry)

## What it is
The oldest extant anthology of Chinese poetry — 305 poems in four divisions, ranging from short folk-song stanzas to long royal-genealogical hymns. The folk-song *Guo Feng* sections preserve courtship songs, work songs, soldiers' laments, and political complaints from the eastern Zhou principalities; the *Ya* and *Song* sections preserve Zhou court ritual hymns, including a sequence of dynastic genealogies (the founding of the Zhou by Hou Ji, the conquest of the Shang by King Wu, the regency of the Duke of Zhou) that constitute the earliest layer of Chinese self-narration. The Shijing is the first of the *Wujing* (Five Classics) and the most-quoted text in pre-Qin philosophical writing; the *Analects* quotes from it constantly, and Confucius's reputation as the text's editor — though traditional and disputed — itself attests its canonical authority by the Warring States.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Western Zhou (-1046 to -771) and Spring and Autumn (-770 to -476). The court hymns derive from the early Zhou royal cult; the *Guo Feng* folk songs reflect the Spring and Autumn principalities. The Zhou conquest of Shang (~-1046) is celebrated in *Da Ya* poems such as *Wen Wang* and *Da Ming*; the Zhou founders are mythologized as recipients of the *Mandate of Heaven* (*tian ming*).
- **Religious milieu.** Zhou court religion: the cult of Heaven (*tian*) and the high ancestor (*Shangdi* in earlier strata; *tian* increasingly in later strata), royal ancestor sacrifice, ritualized agricultural festivals. The hymns presume an elaborate sacrificial economy in which the king's offerings to Heaven and the ancestors maintain cosmic order.
- **Who was behind it.** Zhou court ritual musicians and bards; regional folk traditions for the *Guo Feng*. Traditional account: Confucius edited the corpus from a larger collection of ~3,000 poems down to 305 (already a Warring States legend, doubted by modern scholars). Four Han schools (Lu, Qi, Han, Mao) competed to transmit and interpret the text; only the Mao recension survives. The Mao prefaces, attributed to a "Wei the Elder" and Mao Heng / Mao Chang in the early Han, give each poem a moralizing political interpretation that became canonical for two millennia.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Chinese transmission; the Mao recension stabilizes the received text. Major recent reshapings come from excavated manuscripts: the Fuyang slips (~-165), the Shanghai Museum bamboo slips (~-300, including a Confucius-discussion-of-the-Shijing text, *Kongzi Shilun*), the Tsinghua slips (Warring States), and the Anhui University slips have shown variants and added context for how the late Warring States and early Han read the poems.

## Themes and motifs
- [[royal-hymn]] — the Zhou genealogical hymns (Wen Wang, Da Ming, Sheng Min) are the closest Chinese parallel to ancient Near Eastern royal hymnody (Enheduanna; Pyramid Texts royal sequences).
- [[divine-kingship]] — the Zhou king as recipient of the *tian ming* (Mandate of Heaven); the king's virtue sustains cosmic order.
- [[sacred-king]] — the king as ritual intermediary between Heaven, the ancestors, and the people.
- **Mandate of Heaven** — *Da Ya* poems articulate the doctrine: Heaven gave the Mandate to the Zhou because the Shang lost virtue. Foundational for two millennia of Chinese political theology.
- **Hou Ji birth-narrative** — *Sheng Min* (Mao 245) tells of the miraculous birth of the Zhou agricultural ancestor Hou Ji from Jiang Yuan, who stepped in a divine footprint; abandoned and miraculously protected. A founding-ancestor type-scene with cross-cultural parallels in Mesopotamian birth-of-Sargon and Egyptian royal-conception narratives.
- **Lament and political critique** — many *Guo Feng* and *Xiao Ya* poems criticize corrupt officials and complain of the heaviness of labor; this established the precedent for the Confucian use of the Shijing as a vehicle of moral remonstrance.

- → [[shangdi]] / [[tian-heaven]] — *deities-mentioned*: the Shijing is one of the earliest textual witnesses to the Zhou transition from Shangdi to Tian as supreme cosmic principle.

## Cross-tradition substrate
- **Ancient royal-hymn genre.** The Zhou court hymns belong to a global Bronze-Age type — Mesopotamian temple hymns, Egyptian royal sequences, Vedic praise hymns. The shared genre is *parallel-motif*: not transmission, but a common social technology by which Bronze-Age polities used formulaic poetic praise to bind king, deity, and cosmos. (Owen 1996 introduction; Nylan 2001 ch. 2; for the Mesopotamian side, Foster 2005 *Before the Muses*.)
- **Founder-birth miracle.** The Hou Ji birth narrative (Mao 245) belongs to a cross-tradition type-scene of the miraculous birth of culture-heroes (Sargon-of-Akkad legend, the Moses-in-the-bulrushes type, the Krishna birth-narrative). No claim of borrowing; the type is sufficiently widespread to count as a substrate phenomenon. (Doniger has discussed the cross-cultural type at length.)

## Disputes
- **Confucius's editorial role.** The traditional claim that Confucius selected 305 from 3,000 poems is rejected by most modern scholars; the corpus was likely stable in roughly its present shape before Confucius. Nylan 2001 surveys.
- **Mao prefaces.** The moralizing interpretations attached to each poem in the Mao tradition are not original to the poems; reading the *Guo Feng* folk-songs as straightforward courtship and complaint (Karlgren, Waley, Owen) rather than encoded political allegory (Mao-Zheng) is now standard but contested in traditional Chinese scholarship.
- **Date of compilation.** Earliest poems may go back to ~-1000 (early Western Zhou royal hymns); folk poems are mostly Spring-and-Autumn; the *corpus* probably stabilizes by ~-600 to -500.
