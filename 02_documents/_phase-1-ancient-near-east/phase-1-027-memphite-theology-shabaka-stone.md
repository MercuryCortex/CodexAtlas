# The Memphite Theology (Shabaka Stone)

## What it is
The "Memphite Theology" is the religious-philosophical text carved on the basalt slab known as the Shabaka Stone (British Museum EA 498), commissioned by the 25th-Dynasty Nubian pharaoh Shabaka (~-716 BCE). The text is presented as a re-cutting of an older worm-eaten papyrus that Shabaka claims to have found in the temple of Ptah at Memphis. The legible portions contain: (a) a brief prologue describing Shabaka's commissioning of the inscription; (b) a theological-cosmogonic exposition in which Ptah, the god of Memphis, is identified as the supreme creator who brings the cosmos into being *through the thought of his heart and the speech of his tongue*; (c) a narrative of the Osirian-Horus succession that locates the seat of Horus's coronation at Memphis (thereby anchoring Memphis's political-cultic centrality in mythic geography).

The text's central theological claim is that *Ptah created all things by thinking them in his heart and then speaking them with his tongue*. This is the **earliest surviving extended exposition of creation-by-word as a developed cosmogony** anywhere in the ancient record — though the dating of the *theological content* (vs. the physical inscription) is heavily contested (see Disputes).

## Primary text — selected passages

### Shabaka's colophon — the prologue
*Trans. Lichtheim, M. (1973). Ancient Egyptian Literature, vol. 1. UC Press, pp. 51–57.*
> His Majesty copied it anew in the house of his father Ptah-South-of-his-Wall,
> for His Majesty found it as a work of the ancestors
> which was worm-eaten, so that it could not be understood from beginning to end.
> His Majesty wrote it anew, so that it became better than it had been before.

*The colophon presents the inscription as a faithful copy of an ancient text found on a worm-eaten papyrus. Allen (1988) argues this is a Late-Period archaizing fiction; the colophon is itself a theological claim — the Nubian pharaoh as restorer of pure Egyptian tradition.*

### The heart-and-tongue creation theology (columns 48–61, partially reconstructed)
*Trans. Lichtheim 1973*
> Sight is to the eyes, hearing to the ears, breathing air to the nose —
> they report to the heart.
> It is the heart that brings forth every completed concept,
> and the tongue that announces what the heart thinks.
> Thus were created all the gods, Atum and his Ennead.
> For every divine word came about
> through what the heart devised and the tongue commanded.

*The creation-by-word passage at the heart of the text — Ptah conceives in his heart, then commands with his tongue. This is the clearest statement of the two-stage interior-cognitive cosmogony: thought precedes speech, and speech creates. The central columns of the stone were destroyed by millstone reuse.*

### Ptah as the universal ground (columns 53–57)
*Trans. Lichtheim 1973*
> Ptah — the Great, who is the heart and tongue of the Ennead...
> who gave birth to the gods,
> from whom every thing came forth: food and provisions,
> divine offerings and all good things.
> For it was discovered and understood
> that his strength is greater than that of the other gods.

*The Heliopolitan Ennead (Atum's nine gods) are here identified as Ptah's "teeth and lips" — made modes of his creative speech. This is the sub-sumptive theological move: absorbing rival pantheons into the attributes of the supreme deity.*

*Citation: Lichtheim, M. (1973). Ancient Egyptian Literature, vol. 1. UC Press, pp. 51–57. Allen, J.P. (1988). Genesis in Egypt. Yale Egyptological Studies 2.*

## Context
- **Political milieu.** 25th Dynasty, Nubian / Kushite rule of Egypt (~-744 to -656). Shabaka was the second Kushite pharaoh to rule a unified Egypt. His commissioning of the Memphite stone is part of a 25th-Dynasty archaizing program — the Kushite pharaohs presented themselves as restorers of pure Egyptian tradition, reaching back over the disordered Third Intermediate Period to the Old Kingdom.
- **Religious milieu.** Late Period Egyptian theological reflection. Memphis had been a major cult-center of Ptah throughout Egyptian history; the Memphite Theology is the systematic doctrinal exposition of Ptah's primacy, in competition with the Heliopolitan (Atum-centric, Ennead-based) and Hermopolitan (Ogdoad-based) cosmogonies. The Memphite text *appropriates* the Heliopolitan Ennead by making Atum and the eight other gods of the Ennead manifestations of Ptah — "Ptah-Naunet," "Ptah-Nun," etc.
- **Who was behind it.** The Memphite priesthood of Ptah; the inscription was commissioned by Shabaka.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The stone was used as a millstone for centuries (which explains the central worn-away portion). Acquired by the British Museum in 1805 (by way of the British consul-general Henry Salt). Translated by James Henry Breasted (1901) and Adolph Erman (1911); foundational philological editions Sethe (1928) and Junker (1939–40), both of whom argued for an Old Kingdom (~2400 BCE) origin of the underlying composition. Lichtheim (1973) presented the older view in standard English form. Allen (1988) decisively revised the dating downward to the 25th Dynasty itself, arguing on philological and historical grounds that the "old papyrus" framing is a Late-Period scribal archaizing fiction.

## Themes and motifs
- [[creation-by-word]] — Ptah creates by *the thought of his heart and the speech of his tongue*. The text describes how Ptah's heart conceives and his tongue commands; the gods come into being *as the teeth and lips* of Ptah, and creation proceeds from speech. This is the FIRST attested extended cosmogony of the creation-by-word type — predating Genesis 1's "And God said, Let there be..." by centuries even on the latest (Allen 1988) dating of the Memphite text, and by *much* longer on the older (Sethe / Junker) dating that put it in the Old Kingdom.
- [[divine-kingship]] — the Osirian-Horus succession narrative places Memphis at the cosmic center of legitimate kingship.
- **Heart-and-tongue theology.** A specifically *interior* theology of creation — Ptah thinks first, then speaks. The heart is the locus of conception; the tongue is the locus of utterance. This is a developed cognitive-cosmological theology, not a merely mythological narrative.
- **Pantheistic-monotheizing move within polytheism.** The Memphite text absorbs other gods into Ptah's modalities (Ptah-Naunet, Ptah-Nun, Ptah-Atum) — structurally similar to the absorbing-of-other-gods move in [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]]'s Fifty Names of Marduk.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-2-018-hebrew-bible-p-source]] (Genesis 1) — *parallel-motif* / contested *direct-influence*: Genesis 1's "And God said, Let there be..." is the textbook creation-by-word account; the Memphite text precedes it on any reasonable dating. Direct dependence is unestablished but the Memphite cosmogony is the *earliest attested* member of the family. Mechanism: Egyptian-Israelite contact during the Late Bronze and Iron Ages is documented (Amarna correspondence; Solomon-Egyptian diplomacy; Israelite slavery / exodus narratives; 26th-Dynasty contact with Judah); whether theological content moved with it is a separate question. Most recent comparative scholarship treats the cases as instances of a *shared Near Eastern cosmogonic grammar* with no need to posit direct dependence (Walton 2011; Hoffmeier).
- → [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] (Johannine Prologue) — *parallel-motif* in the deep history of *logos theology*: "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God ... All things came into being through him." John 1 is the Hellenistic Jewish + Christian endpoint of the creation-by-word tradition stream of which the Memphite text is the earliest extant source.
- → Quranic *kun fa-yakun* ("Be! and it is" — Q 2:117, 3:47, 16:40, 36:82) — *parallel-motif*: the Quran's creation-by-divine-imperative continues the same theological tradition. Allah commands; reality complies. Latest member of the creation-by-word family in the Abrahamic textual chain.
- → Hellenistic Logos speculation (Philo, the Hermetic Corpus, the Gospel of John's Prologue) — *parallel-motif* via Egyptian-Greek syncretism in Alexandria: the Hellenistic Logos tradition develops in an Egyptian environment where Memphite-style Ptah theology is the local substrate. Direct lineage from the Memphite text to Philo's Logos is not provable but is the *natural cultural-geographical line of descent*.
- → [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] — *shared-milieu*: earlier Egyptian cosmogonic material (Heliopolitan Ennead) which the Memphite text appropriates.
- → [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] — *parallel-motif* (creation cosmogony): contrast — Babylonian creation is by *combat* (Marduk vs. Tiamat) and *divine body splitting*; Memphite creation is by *thought and speech*. The two represent opposed cosmogonic modes coexisting in the Bronze Age Near East.

## Disputes
- **Dating of the theological content.** This is the major scholarly question:
  - **Old view (Breasted, Erman, Sethe 1928, Junker 1939, Lichtheim 1973):** the inscription preserves a genuinely Old Kingdom (~2400 BCE or earlier) Memphite theological composition, with Shabaka faithfully reproducing an ancient text. Lichtheim's 1973 translation still presents this view as the standard.
  - **Current scholarly consensus (Allen 1988 and successors):** the text is a 25th-Dynasty (~-716) archaizing composition. The "found on an old worm-eaten papyrus" framing is a *Late Period scribal back-projection* — an archaizing claim of antiquity made to lend authority to a contemporary theological composition. Linguistic features (some Late Egyptian usages, archaized Middle Egyptian style typical of 25th-Dynasty inscriptions) and historical-theological context (the 25th-Dynasty restoration program; Memphite-Heliopolitan rivalries of the Late Period) support this dating.
  - **Either way**, the Memphite Theology is the earliest extended creation-by-word cosmogony preserved in the ancient record. On the Allen 1988 dating it still precedes Genesis 1 P (post-exilic, ~-550 to -450) by a century or more. On the older Sethe / Junker dating it precedes Genesis 1 by ~1800–2000 years.
- **Whether Genesis 1 depends on the Memphite Theology.** Direct dependence is unestablished. Most current scholars (Walton 2011, Currid, Smith 2010 for Enuma Elish-comparison) prefer a "shared cosmogonic grammar" model — the ANE was an interconnected theological zone, and creation-by-word was one of several available cosmogonic options that surfaced in multiple traditions.
