# Baal Cycle

## What it is
The Baal Cycle is the principal mythological composition of Late Bronze Age Ugarit, narrating the storm god [[baal-hadad]]'s combats and cosmic kingship across six cuneiform tablets in three connected episodes: (1) **Baal vs. Yam** (the Sea, also called Nahar, the River) — Yam is exalted by El as a candidate for kingship; Baal contests; the craftsman-god Kothar-wa-Hasis forges him magical clubs ("Driver" and "Chaser"); Baal defeats and disperses Yam; (2) **Baal's palace** — Baal, lacking a palace befitting his new status, lobbies through Anat and Asherah for El's permission to build; the palace is constructed by Kothar with cedar and gold, and a window of cloud is installed (the channel through which Baal sends rain); (3) **Baal vs. Mot** (Death) — Mot summons Baal to the underworld; Baal descends and dies; the cosmos withers; Anat slaughters Mot, threshes him, scatters him as grain; Baal revives and returns. The cycle's climax is Baal's restored cosmic kingship and the seasonal pattern of his death-and-revival tied to Levantine rainfall and vegetation. Ilimilku the scribe is named in colophons.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late Bronze Age Ugarit (~1400–1185 BCE) — a wealthy port kingdom on the northern Syrian coast (modern Ras Shamra), within the orbit of Egyptian, then Hittite, suzerainty. Niqmaddu II (~1350 BCE), under whom Ilimilku worked, was an Egyptian vassal during the Amarna period. Ugarit was destroyed in the Late Bronze Age collapse (~1185 BCE) and never reoccupied, preserving its cuneiform archives in situ — an extraordinary archaeological stroke of luck.
- **Religious milieu.** Standard Late Bronze Canaanite polytheism with El as the high god (the divine "father of years"), Baal-Hadad as the active storm and warrior god, Anat as warrior goddess, Asherah as El's consort and divine mother, Mot as Death, Yam as the Sea, Shapash the sun-goddess, Yarikh the moon. This pantheon is structurally identical (in many key features) to the Canaanite religion presupposed and polemicized against in the Hebrew Bible — Ugarit gives us the unmediated, non-polemicized Canaanite original.
- **Who was behind it.** Ilimilku the Šubʿanite (so the colophons), scribe of the royal court of Niqmaddu II — the named tradent, though the underlying mythological material is centuries older oral tradition. He also wrote out the *Kirta* and *Aqhat* legends.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Discovered in 1929 by a peasant plowing at Ras Shamra; Claude F.-A. Schaeffer led the French archaeological mission that excavated Ugarit (1929 onward). The cuneiform alphabet was deciphered by Hans Bauer, Édouard Dhorme, and Charles Virolleaud working independently within months in 1930. The Baal Cycle tablets were edited from the 1930s onward; the modern critical edition is Smith (1994) and Smith & Pitard (2009), ongoing.

## Themes and motifs
- [[chaoskampf]] — **the foundational Canaanite chaoskampf**: Baal vs. Yam is the storm-god-vs.-sea-monster combat in its purest Levantine form. Direct typological cousin of Marduk vs. Tiamat in [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] and Yahweh vs. Leviathan/Rahab/Yam in residual Hebrew Bible poetry.
- [[dying-rising-god]] — Baal's descent to Mot, death, mourning by Anat, and revival is the textbook Canaanite dying-rising god pattern. Tied to the Levantine agricultural cycle: Baal's death is the dry season, Anat's threshing of Mot is the harvest, Baal's return is the rainy season.
- [[divine-council]] — Mount Lel / the assembly of El is the meeting place of the gods; key decisions (Yam's exaltation, the palace, Baal's status) are taken in council. Structurally parallel to the Hebrew Bible's *bĕnê ʾēlîm* and divine-council passages (Ps 82; 1 Kings 22:19–23; Job 1–2).
- [[divine-kingship]] — Baal's whole arc is the *establishment* of his kingship: from challenge by Yam through combat through palace-building through validation by death-and-revival. The cycle ritualizes royal-divine kingship.
- [[primordial-waters]] — Yam is both the sea (literal cosmic water) and the chaotic primordial principle. The chaoskampf reduces him; cosmos is ordered.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] — *parallel-motif*: Marduk-Tiamat and Baal-Yam are structurally identical chaoskampf-and-kingship narratives, with shared deep structure (young storm-warrior overthrows old cosmic threat, attains kingship, builds palace/cosmos). Whether by shared Near Eastern inheritance or by cross-cultural diffusion is debated; Day (1985) treats both as instances of a common Bronze Age Eastern Mediterranean mythic complex. Ref: Day 1985; Smith 1994 vol. 1 intro.
- → [[phase-1-014-ugaritic-ritual-texts]] — *shared-milieu*: the Ugaritic ritual corpus presupposes and operationalizes the Baal Cycle mythology. Ref: Pardee, *Ritual and Cult at Ugarit* (2002).
- → Hebrew Bible chaoskampf residues — *parallel-motif* / *common-tradition*: Yahweh's combats with Leviathan, Rahab, and Yam (Ps 74:13–14; Job 26:12–13; Isa 27:1, 51:9–10) reproduce the Canaanite Baal-vs.-Yam structure with Yahweh substituting for Baal. Day (1985) is the standard treatment. Ref: Day 1985; Cross, *Canaanite Myth and Hebrew Epic* (1973).
- → Hebrew prophetic anti-Baal polemic (Hosea, Jeremiah, Elijah cycle in 1 Kings 17–19) — *polemic-against*: the Hebrew prophets attack Israelite Baal worship; that worship presupposes the Baal-cult-complex of which the Baal Cycle is the mythological backbone. Ref: Smith 2002, *Early History of God*; Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan* (2000).
- → Hesiod's Theogony (Phase 2) — *parallel-motif*: Zeus vs. Typhon shares the chaoskampf structure (storm god vs. chaotic monster); West, *The East Face of Helicon* (1997), traces specific Eastern Mediterranean diffusion. Ref: West 1997.

## Disputes
- **Order of the tablets.** KTU 1.1–1.6 is the modern reconstructive order; ancient compositional sequence is partly inferred. Some scholars (Pardee) argue for alternative orderings.
- **Dying-rising god status of Baal.** Mettinger (2001, *The Riddle of Resurrection*) defends Baal as a genuine dying-rising god; older scholarship (Lambert, Smith in early work) was more cautious. Current consensus: Baal is genuinely such a god in the Ugaritic texts, though the Frazerian generalization of the category has been heavily critiqued.
- **Direct Hebrew dependence.** Whether Hebrew Bible chaoskampf passages are *direct* Canaanite inheritance or independent shared-milieu poetic conventions is debated in detail; consensus is that the Canaanite tradition is the proximate source for the Hebrew imagery.
- **Authorship attribution to Ilimilku.** Whether Ilimilku composed, redacted, or merely copied is unrecoverable; colophons say "scribe" (spr), which is ambiguous.
