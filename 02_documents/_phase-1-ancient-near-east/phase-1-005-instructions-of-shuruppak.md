# Instructions of Shuruppak

## What it is
A Sumerian wisdom composition presented as the testamentary instructions of Šuruppak (eponym of the city of Šuruppak, son of Ubar-tutu) to his son Ziusudra — the Sumerian flood hero. The text consists of a long series of practical, ethical, and prudential admonitions in short couplets ("Do not buy a donkey that brays too much"; "Do not insult the downtrodden"; "Do not curse strongly: it rebounds on the curser"; "A loving heart maintains a family; a hateful heart destroys a family"). Three framing sections (each beginning "Šuruppak gave instructions to his son...") segment the proverbs. Among the **very oldest extant works of human literature**, with copies from ~2600 BCE at Abu Salabikh; transmitted in Sumerian and Akkadian for nearly two millennia.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The archaic Abu Salabikh and Fara (= Šuruppak) tablets emerge from the Early Dynastic IIIa city-state network (~2600 BCE). The OB recension (~1800 BCE) is curricular: a fixture of the scribal-school *edubba* alongside the [[phase-1-001-kesh-temple-hymn]] and other classical Sumerian texts.
- **Religious milieu.** The frame is paradigmatically antediluvian — Šuruppak instructs his son Ziusudra *before the flood*. The Mesopotamian flood tradition (later codified in [[phase-1-006-atrahasis]] and the Sumerian Eridu Genesis and the SB Gilgamesh XI) is presupposed: wisdom is transmitted from before the cosmic break to after it, like the *me* preserved by Ziusudra/Ut-napishtim/Atrahasis.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous scribal composition with literary attribution to Šuruppak. The figure of a venerable king-father instructing his son is a stable Near Eastern and Egyptian genre (cf. the Egyptian *Instruction of Ptahhotep*, ~2400 BCE; the *Instruction for Merikare*, ~2100 BCE).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Excavated from Tell Abū Ṣalābīkh (1963–65) and Fara (= Šuruppak; German Oriental Society excavations 1902–1903). The OB tablets known from Nippur and elsewhere. Bilingual Sumerian-Akkadian recensions continue into the first millennium; copies in Hittite, Hurrian, and Akkadian from Boğazköy demonstrate the text's reach.

## Themes and motifs
- **Practical wisdom** — proverbs covering family, work, friendship, women, slaves, hospitality, debt, speech, and self-control. The horizon is overwhelmingly thisworldly and prudential.
- [[divine-kingship]] — peripheral: the gods (Enlil, Enki) are named but not central; this is wisdom *for* a (future) king rather than wisdom *of* the gods.
- [[flood-motif]] — *frame-only*: the text is the antediluvian father's words to the flood-hero son; the flood itself is not narrated here but presupposed as the horizon-event that the wisdom outlives.
- **Speech ethics** — strong emphasis on guarding the tongue ("a heart fled is hard to bring back"; "do not curse strongly"). Comparable to later Egyptian, Israelite, and Greek speech-wisdom.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-1-006-atrahasis]] — *shared-milieu* / *frame-coherence*: Ziusudra is the Sumerian name for the flood hero who appears as Atrahasis ("Exceeding-Wise") in Akkadian and Ut-napishtim in the SB Gilgamesh. Shuruppak's instructions are framed as the wisdom that passes through the flood. Ref: ETCSL t.5.6.1; Alster 2005; Lambert 1960.
- → [[phase-1-004-gilgamesh-old-babylonian]] — *shared-milieu* / *parallel-motif*: Ut-napishtim is called "the man of Shuruppak" in the SB Gilgamesh; the alewife Šiduri's advice to Gilgamesh in the OB Sippar tablet is structurally similar to Shuruppak's wisdom genre. Ref: George 2003.
- → Egyptian *Instruction of Ptahhotep* (~2400 BCE) — *parallel-motif* / *shared-milieu*: contemporary instances of the father-to-son instruction genre across two Bronze Age literate cultures. No direct dependence claimed; common Near Eastern genre. Ref: Lichtheim, *Ancient Egyptian Literature* vol. 1 (1973).
- → Hebrew Bible Proverbs — *parallel-motif*: the proverb-collection genre, the prudential-ethical horizon, the "my son" framing, and several specific topoi (speech-control, the dangerous wife, just dealing) connect the Sumerian and Hebrew traditions through the broader ANE wisdom continuum. Direct dependence not argued; shared genre. Ref: Lambert 1960; Fox, *Proverbs* (Anchor Bible, 2000–2009).

## Disputes
- **Date of the archaic recension.** Abu Salabikh tablets are ~2600 BCE on palaeographic grounds, but whether the *composition* is exactly contemporary or already older is undecidable; Alster (2005) treats the archaic version as effectively contemporaneous with composition.
- **Single composition vs. anthology.** Whether the proverbs constitute a unified composition with intentional structure or a loose anthology assembled in the OB period from older proverb-stock is debated.
- **Authorial fiction.** The attribution to Šuruppak instructing Ziusudra is universally treated as literary frame, not as historical claim; whether the frame is original to ~2600 BCE or developed in the OB period is contested.
