The Ebers Papyrus (c. 1550 BCE) is the most extensive surviving medical text from ancient Egypt — 110 pages containing 877 prescriptions covering the full range of medicine: internal diseases, eye conditions, skin complaints, gynecology, dentistry, mental illness, and surgical procedures. Named for Egyptologist Georg Ebers who purchased it in Luxor in 1872, it is now held at the University of Leipzig.

## The Organization

The papyrus is organized by body system and symptom type — an ancient analogue of modern clinical categorization:
- **Incantations and magical remedies** at the beginning (establishing divine authorization)
- **Internal diseases** (the core: digestive, cardiac, respiratory)
- **Eye diseases** (34 prescriptions)
- **Skin diseases** (including early descriptions of skin cancer)
- **Limb disorders**
- **Gynecological conditions**
- **Dentistry**
- **Surgical treatments**

## Key Medical Achievements

The Ebers Papyrus contains the world's oldest known description of **depression** — a condition called "the heart is weary" with systematic treatment protocols. It also contains an early anatomical understanding of the heart's centrality in the body's circulatory system ("the heart speaks in the vessels of every limb").

The pharmacopoeia includes:
- **Castor oil** as purgative
- **Aloe vera** for wound healing
- **Garlic and onion** as antimicrobials
- **Willow bark** (salicin) for pain and inflammation
- **Poppy** (opium) for pain relief
- **Honey** for wound dressing
- **Beer** as pharmaceutical vehicle
