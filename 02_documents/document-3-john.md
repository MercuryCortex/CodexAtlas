# Third Epistle of John

## What it is
The shortest book in the New Testament: a personal note of fifteen verses from "the elder" to a certain Gaius, praising him for hosting traveling missionaries "for the sake of the name" and contrasting him with Diotrephes — a local leader who "likes to put himself first," refuses to welcome the elder's envoys, spreads false charges, and even expels those who would. It is a rare, unvarnished snapshot of an authority struggle in a first-century church.

## Primary text

### 3 John 9–10
*Trans. NRSV*
> I have written something to the church; but Diotrephes, who likes to put himself first, does not acknowledge our authority... He refuses to welcome the friends, and even prevents those who want to do so and expels them from the church.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** A conflict between itinerant authority ("the elder" and his missionaries) and a self-asserting local leader — a fault line at the birth of church order.
- **Authorship.** "The elder," the voice of 2 John.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The least-cited NT book in antiquity; among the antilegomena.

## Themes and motifs
- Hospitality — supporting traveling co-workers as a Christian duty.
- Church authority — the clash of charismatic and local-resident leadership (Diotrephes).
- Imitating good — "do not imitate what is evil but imitate what is good" (v. 11).
