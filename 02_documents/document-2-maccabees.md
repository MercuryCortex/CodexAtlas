# Second Book of Maccabees

## What it is
Not a sequel to 1 Maccabees but an independent, theologically charged account of the same crisis, condensed from a lost five-volume work by Jason of Cyrene. Where 1 Maccabees is a dry dynastic chronicle, 2 Maccabees is emotive "pathetic" history, full of heavenly apparitions, vivid martyrdoms, and explicit doctrine — above all the resurrection of the body, creation out of nothing, and atoning prayer for the dead.

## Primary text

### 2 Maccabees 7:28, 14
*Trans. NRSV (Apocrypha)*
> I beg you, my child, to look at the heaven and the earth and see everything that is in them, and recognize that God did not make them out of things that existed. (7:28)
> One cannot but choose to die at the hands of mortals and to cherish the hope God gives of being raised again by him. (7:14)

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Hellenistic-Jewish theology with a developed afterlife: resurrection, angelic aid, and the merit of the martyrs.
- **Composition.** An epitome of Jason of Cyrene, in Greek, prefaced by letters urging the diaspora to keep Hanukkah.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Deuterocanonical; its 12:43–45 became a key proof-text for purgatory at the Council of Trent.

## Themes and motifs
- [[resurrection-of-the-dead]] — the seven martyred brothers confess bodily resurrection (ch. 7), with Daniel 12 the clearest pre-Christian statement.
- Creation ex nihilo — 7:28, the earliest explicit "out of nothing."
- Martyrdom and atonement — the noble death of Eleazar and the seven; prayer for the dead.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[document-1-maccabees]] — *parallel-motif*: the same revolt narrated with opposite theology and genre.
- → [[antiochus-iv-epiphanes]] — *key-figure*: the persecutor whose tyranny produces the martyrs.
- → [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — *parallel-motif*: the twin Second-Temple sources for the resurrection hope (Dan 12; 2 Macc 7).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **2 Maccabees 7 is, with Daniel 12, the birthplace of resurrection faith — and 7:28 the first "creation out of nothing."** The martyrdom of the mother and her seven sons turns on their confident hope that "the King of the universe will raise us up to an everlasting renewal of life" (7:9) — the most explicit pre-Christian statement of bodily resurrection, feeding directly into Pharisaic, rabbinic, and Christian eschatology. In the same chapter the mother urges her son to see that God made heaven and earth "not out of things that existed" (7:28) — the verse later theologians made the proof-text of *creatio ex nihilo*. And 2 Macc 12:43–45's prayer for the dead became Trent's scriptural basis for purgatory. A deuterocanonical book thus seeds three major doctrines (Goldstein 1983, AYB 41A pp.291–315; Schwartz 2008, CEJL).
