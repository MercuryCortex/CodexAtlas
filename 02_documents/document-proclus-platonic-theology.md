# Proclus — Platonic Theology and the Commentaries

## What it is
The mature theological system of Proclus Diadochus, last great head of the Athenian Academy and the most systematic mind of ancient Neoplatonism. Beyond his austere *Elements of Theology* (a Euclid-like deduction of metaphysics), his *Platonic Theology* and his vast commentaries on Plato's Timaeus and Parmenides build reality into an immense, ordered procession: from the ineffable **One**, through ranks of "henads" (the gods), divine intellects, and souls, down to the material world — and back again in universal return. Treating Plato's dialogues and the Chaldean Oracles as revealed scripture, Proclus produced the most complete surviving theology of paganism on the eve of its extinction.

## Context
- **Intellectual milieu.** The Athenian Academy in its final, embattled, intensely religious phase under a Christian empire.
- **Method.** Geometry-like deduction (the Elements) plus scriptural commentary (Plato as sacred text).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Greek manuscripts; partly transmitted to the Latin and Arabic worlds in abridged form.

## Themes and motifs
- The procession and return — all things flow from the One and revert to it.
- The henads — the gods as the first determinations of unity below the One.
- Plato as scripture — the dialogues expounded as revelation.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The last pagan system became the secret skeleton of Christian and Islamic theology.** Proclus built the most complete philosophical theology of antiquity just as paganism was dying — and it outlived its religion three times over. Through **Pseudo-Dionysius**, his graded hierarchy of being entered Christianity as orthodox mysticism and angelology. Through the **Liber de Causis** — an Arabic abridgement of his Elements of Theology, mistaken for Aristotle — he shaped Islamic philosophy and was expounded by Thomas Aquinas. And through Marsilio Ficino he fed the Renaissance revival of Platonism. The intellectual architecture of the Abrahamic theologies of God, being, and the angelic orders is, to a remarkable degree, the pagan system of a 5th-century Athenian who spent his life defending the old gods (Dodds 1963; Saffrey & Westerink 1968; Tarrant & Baltzly 2007).
