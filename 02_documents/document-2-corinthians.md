# Second Epistle to the Corinthians

## What it is
The most personal and emotionally raw of Paul's letters, written after a bruising conflict with the Corinthian church and the arrival of rival missionaries who disparaged him. Paul defends his apostolic integrity, redefines authentic ministry as power perfected in weakness and suffering, presses the great collection for Jerusalem, and — in the "fool's speech" — boasts paradoxically of his hardships and his "thorn in the flesh." Its sharp shifts of tone lead many to read it as a stitching-together of several letters.

## Primary text

### 2 Corinthians 12:9
*Trans. NRSV*
> But he said to me, "My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness." So, I will boast all the more gladly of my weaknesses, so that the power of Christ may dwell in me.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** A reconciliation letter amid wounded authority and rival "super-apostles."
- **Authorship.** Undisputed Pauline; likely a composite of letter-fragments.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Within the Pauline corpus.

## Themes and motifs
- Strength in weakness — "power made perfect in weakness" (12:9); "treasure in clay jars" (4:7).
- The new-covenant ministry — the surpassing glory of the Spirit over the letter (ch. 3).
- Generosity — the theology of the Jerusalem collection (chs. 8–9).
