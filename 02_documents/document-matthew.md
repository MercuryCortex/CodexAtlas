# Gospel of Matthew

The **Gospel of Matthew** is the first book of the New Testament and the most influential Gospel in church history — the most quoted, the most used in liturgy and catechesis, and traditionally placed at the head of the canon. Written for a **Jewish-Christian** community (probably at **Antioch**, c. 80–90 CE), it presents Jesus of Nazareth as the **Messiah and the fulfillment of the Hebrew Scriptures**, structuring his teaching into five great discourses — above all the **Sermon on the Mount** (chs. 5–7) — that deliberately echo the five books of the Torah, with Jesus as a **new Moses** giving a new law from a mountain.

## Composition

Matthew is a Greek work built on two earlier sources (the **two-source hypothesis**): the narrative framework of the **[[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark|Gospel of Mark]]**, and the sayings-source **Q** shared with **[[phase-3-018-luke-acts|Luke]]** (its sapiential register overlapping the **[[phase-4-001-gospel-of-thomas|Gospel of Thomas]]**). Its hallmark is the **"fulfillment formula"** ("this happened to fulfil what was spoken by the prophet…"), repeatedly tying events of Jesus' life to the **[[phase-3-006-septuagint|Septuagint]]** text of **[[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah|Isaiah]]**, the Psalms, Hosea, and Micah.

## The cross-tradition wire

Matthew is the great hinge between the **Hebrew Bible and the Christian movement**: it opens with a genealogy from **Abraham** ([[document-genesis|Genesis]]), models Jesus' temptation on Israel's wilderness testing (Jesus answers Satan with three quotations from **[[document-deuteronomy|Deuteronomy]]**), and reads the whole life of Jesus as the recapitulation and fulfilment of Israel's story. Forward, its infancy narrative (the virgin birth, the magi) and miracle traditions feed directly into the **Qurʾānic** portrait of **ʿĪsā** (Jesus). Matthew thus wires the deity-and-scripture layers of all three Abrahamic traditions into a single chain.

## Reach forward

The Sermon on the Mount, the Lord's Prayer, the parables of the kingdom, and the Great Commission ("make disciples of all nations") made Matthew the foundational catechetical text of Christianity, shaping its ethics, liturgy, and missionary self-understanding for two millennia.
