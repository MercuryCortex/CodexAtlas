# Porphyry — Isagoge, Life of Plotinus, and Against the Christians

## What it is
The most consequential works of Porphyry of Tyre, Plotinus's pupil and editor and late antiquity's great scholar. Three matter most: the **Isagoge**, a short "Introduction" to Aristotle's logic that became the universal first textbook of philosophy for a millennium; the **Life of Plotinus**, prefaced to Porphyry's edition of the Enneads and our chief source for Plotinus's life and circle; and the lost fifteen-book **Against the Christians**, the most learned pagan assault on Christianity ever written — so dangerous that emperors ordered every copy burned.

## Context
- **Intellectual milieu.** The school of Plotinus at Rome, harmonizing Plato and Aristotle into a single Neoplatonic curriculum.
- **The editor.** Porphyry's six-by-nine arrangement gave the Enneads their name and form.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The logical and biographical works survive; Against the Christians was destroyed and survives only in the quotations of its opponents.

## Themes and motifs
- The harmony of Plato and Aristotle — logic as the gateway to philosophy.
- Critical scripture-scholarship — Porphyry's startlingly modern dating arguments against the Bible.
- The defense of paganism — philosophy contra the new faith.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **A twelve-word question that ran the Middle Ages — and the pagan critique that read Daniel like a modern.** Porphyry's Isagoge poses, then declines to answer, the question of whether genera and species (like "animal", "human") exist in reality, in the mind, or only in words. Translated into Latin by **Boethius**, that question became the **problem of universals**, the defining controversy of medieval scholastic philosophy from Abelard to Ockham. Meanwhile his *Against the Christians* anticipated modern biblical criticism by 1,500 years — arguing on internal evidence that the Book of Daniel was written not in the 6th century BCE but during the Maccabean crisis of the 160s, the same conclusion critical scholarship reaches today. The most influential pagan philosopher of his age shaped both the form of medieval thought and the questions of modern scripture-scholarship (Barnes 2003; Berchman 2005; Edwards 2000).
