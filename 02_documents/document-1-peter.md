# First Epistle of Peter

## What it is
A circular letter of encouragement to scattered, socially-marginalized Christian communities across Asia Minor, framing their experience of hostility as a noble participation in the sufferings of Christ. Rich in baptismal and Old-Testament imagery, it calls believers "exiles" and "resident aliens" yet also a "chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation," and grounds their hope in Christ's resurrection.

## Primary text

### 1 Peter 2:9
*Trans. NRSV*
> But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, God's own people, in order that you may proclaim the mighty acts of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Diaspora Christian communities under social pressure; the letter reframes their alien status as election.
- **Authorship.** Disputed between Petrine and deutero-Petrine; "Babylon" (5:13) signals a Roman provenance.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Among the undisputed Catholic Epistles.

## Themes and motifs
- Suffering as participation — sharing Christ's sufferings now, his glory later.
- Exile and election — resident aliens who are nonetheless God's chosen people.
- [[messianism]] — Christ the rejected "living stone" who becomes the cornerstone (2:4–8).

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[document-romans]] — *shared-milieu*: shared early-Christian paraenesis and Septuagintal Christology.
- → [[document-ephesians]] — *parallel-motif*: household codes and the church as a holy, elect people.
