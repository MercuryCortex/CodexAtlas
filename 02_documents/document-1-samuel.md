# First Book of Samuel

## What it is
The account of Israel's fateful turn from a tribal confederacy to a monarchy. The prophet Samuel — last of the judges — reluctantly anoints first Saul and then, secretly, the young David, whose rise (the defeat of Goliath, his service and flight from the jealous Saul) fills the second half of the book. One book with 2 Samuel in the Hebrew tradition (divided in the Greek as 1–2 Kingdoms), it is a profound meditation on power, election, and rejection.

## Primary text

### 1 Samuel 16:7
*Trans. NRSV*
> The LORD does not see as mortals see; they look on the outward appearance, but the LORD looks on the heart.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** The Deuteronomistic ambivalence toward kingship: monarchy as both rejection of YHWH's rule (ch. 8) and the vehicle of his chosen anointed.
- **Composition.** Older David and Saul traditions edited into the Deuteronomistic History.
- **Discovery / transmission.** A textually difficult book; 4QSamᵃ is a key Qumran witness.

## Themes and motifs
- The founding of monarchy — its dangers (ch. 8) and its divine sanction.
- Election and rejection — Saul rejected, David anointed.
- [[messianism]] — David as the anointed (māšîaḥ) prototype of the Messiah.
