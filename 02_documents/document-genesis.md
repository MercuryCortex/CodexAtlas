# Genesis

**Genesis** (Hebrew *Bərēʾšīt*, "In the beginning") is the first book of the **Torah** and of the entire Bible — and, for the Codex Atlas, the **root of the root**: the text from which the largest share of the cross-tradition wiring radiates. It falls into two movements. The **Primeval History** (chs. 1–11) narrates creation, the garden of Eden and the human fall, Cain and Abel, the genealogies of the antediluvians, the **Flood** of Noah, and the Tower of Babel; the **Ancestral History** (chs. 12–50) follows the patriarchs — Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, and Joseph — and the promise of land and progeny that constitutes Israel as a people.

## Composition

Genesis is not a single-author work but a **composite**: the classical Documentary Hypothesis (Wellhausen; Friedman) distinguishes interwoven **J (Yahwist)**, **E (Elohist)**, and **P (Priestly)** strata, redacted into the present form in the **Persian period** (c. 5th c. BCE). The seams are visible — two creation accounts (the P cosmogony of 1:1–2:4a and the J garden narrative of 2:4b–25), doubled flood-chronologies, and divine names alternating between *YHWH* (**[[yahweh|Yahweh]]**) and *Elohim* (**[[el-elohim-hebrew|Elohim]]**). The patriarchal god ʾĒl (**[[el-canaanite|El]]**) surfaces in the ancestral epithets *El Shaddai*, *El Elyon*, *El Olam*.

## The cross-tradition root (the MASSIVE-WINS)

Genesis 1 opens over the *təhôm*, **"the deep"** — cognate to **Tiamat**, the salt-water chaos-ocean split by Marduk in the Babylonian **[[phase-1-008-enuma-elish|Enūma Eliš]]** — and the Egyptian primeval **Nun** and Norse **Ginnungagap** belong to the same world-picture of order carved from a primal sea (the canonical READ-surface parallel of the Atlas). The **Flood** narrative stands in direct relation to the Mesopotamian tradition — **[[phase-1-006-atrahasis|Atraḫasis]]**, the **[[phase-1-004-gilgamesh-old-babylonian|Gilgamesh]]** flood-tablet (Utnapishtim), and the Sumerian **[[phase-1-016-eridu-genesis-flood|Eridu Genesis]]** — sharing the divine decision to destroy humanity, the warned survivor, the ark, the bird-scouts, and the post-flood sacrifice. These are not borrowings of doctrine but a shared Near Eastern narrative inheritance that Genesis re-theologizes around a single sovereign God.

## Reach forward

As the foundational origin-narrative, Genesis seeds the whole of later scripture: the rewritten-Bible literature of the Second Temple (Jubilees, the Genesis Apocryphon), the New Testament's Adam–Christ typology, and the Qurʾānic stories of Ādam, Nūḥ, and Ibrāhīm. It is the single most generative text in the Atlas's corpus.
