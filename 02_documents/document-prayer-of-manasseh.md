# Prayer of Manasseh

## What it is
A brief, beautiful penitential prayer of fifteen verses, composed to supply the words that 2 Chronicles 33 says the wicked King Manasseh prayed when, carried captive to Babylon, he humbled himself and repented — and was restored. It is a model act of contrition: a confession of God's mercy, an admission of sins "more in number than the sand of the sea," and a plea for forgiveness from the "God of those who repent."

## Primary text

### Prayer of Manasseh 13–14
*Trans. NRSV (Apocrypha)*
> And now I bend the knee of my heart, imploring you for your kindness. I have sinned, O Lord, I have sinned, and I acknowledge my transgressions... do not let me perish in my sins.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Second Temple reflection on repentance: even Judah's most notorious idolater finds mercy when he turns.
- **Composition.** A synagogue-style penitential psalm filling the gap in the Chronicler's narrative.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Preserved among the Septuagint Odes and in Syriac; Orthodox-liturgical, a Vulgate appendix, and in the KJV Apocrypha.

## Themes and motifs
- Repentance (metanoia) — contrition as the door to mercy.
- Boundless divine mercy — God as "the God of those who repent."
- The penitent king — even Manasseh, the worst, is forgiven.
