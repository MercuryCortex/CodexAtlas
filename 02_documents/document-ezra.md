# Book of Ezra

## What it is
The narrative of the return from Babylonian exile and the restoration of the Jerusalem cult under Persian patronage. It falls in two movements: the first return under Sheshbazzar/Zerubbabel and the rebuilding of the Temple (chs. 1–6, with Aramaic Persian-archive documents), and the later mission of Ezra the priest-scribe to teach and enforce the Torah, climaxing in the controversial dissolution of marriages with foreign women (chs. 7–10).

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The Achaemenid Persian empire, whose policy of restoring subject peoples' cults frames the whole book; royal rescripts are quoted verbatim in Aramaic.
- **Religious milieu.** The refounding of post-exilic Judaism on Torah and Temple; Ezra makes the written Law the community's binding constitution.
- **Discovery / transmission.** One book with Nehemiah in the Hebrew tradition; divided in the Christian Bibles.

## Themes and motifs
- Restoration — return, rebuilding, and the re-establishment of the cult.
- Torah as constitution — the public promulgation and enforcement of the Law.
- [[divine-kingship]] — YHWH working through the Persian kings (Cyrus is even called his "anointed" in Isaiah 45).
