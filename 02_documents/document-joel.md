# Book of Joel

## What it is
A short prophetic book of the Twelve in which a devastating locust plague — described with mounting, almost apocalyptic intensity — becomes the harbinger of the cosmic "Day of the LORD." After a call to communal repentance and God's promise to restore the years the locusts have eaten, the book rises to its famous promise of a universal outpouring of God's Spirit, and ends with judgment on the nations and the vindication of Zion.

## Primary text

### Joel 2:28–29
*Trans. NRSV (Heb. 3:1–2)*
> Then afterward I will pour out my spirit on all flesh; your sons and your daughters shall prophesy, your old men shall dream dreams, and your young men shall see visions. Even on the male and female slaves, in those days, I will pour out my spirit.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Post-exilic Persian Yehud, a temple-centered community.
- **Religious milieu.** The locust catastrophe is read liturgically: a summons to fasting and return, and a window onto the eschatological Day.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous canonical transmission within the Twelve.

## Themes and motifs
- The Day of the LORD — the plague as its omen; cosmic portents of sun darkened and moon to blood (2:31).
- [[apocalyptic-revelation]] — the locust-armies and cosmic signs feed the apocalyptic tradition (cf. Rev 9).
- The outpouring of the Spirit — 2:28–29, the promise democratizing prophecy across age, gender, and status.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[document-amos]] — *shared-milieu*: the Twelve's "Day of the LORD" tradition (Joel is editorially paired with Amos).
- → [[document-revelation]] — *parallel-motif*: the demonic locust-cavalry of Revelation 9 reworks Joel's locust-army imagery.
- → [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] — *direct-quote*: Peter's Pentecost sermon (Acts 2:17–21) quotes Joel 2:28–32 in full as fulfilled in the gift of the Spirit.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Joel 2 is the charter text of Pentecost and the Christian doctrine of the Spirit.** When the Spirit descends on the disciples at Pentecost, Peter explains it by quoting Joel: "this is what was spoken through the prophet Joel" (Acts 2:16–21). Joel's promise that God would pour out his Spirit "on all flesh" — sons and daughters, young and old, slave and free — becomes the scriptural foundation of the church's self-understanding as a Spirit-filled, prophesying community, and the proof-text of every later charismatic and Pentecostal movement (Crenshaw 1995, AYB 24C pp.164–172).
