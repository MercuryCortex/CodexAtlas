# Book of Joshua

## What it is
The opening book of the Deuteronomistic History, narrating Israel's entry into Canaan under Joshua: the crossing of the Jordan, the fall of Jericho, the campaigns of conquest, the allotment of the land among the tribes, and a closing covenant-renewal at Shechem. Long read as straightforward history, it is now understood as an idealized, theologically-driven account; archaeology finds little support for a swift military conquest, pointing instead to a gradual and largely internal emergence of Israel in the highlands.

## Primary text

### Joshua 24:15
*Trans. NRSV*
> Now if you are unwilling to serve the LORD, choose this day whom you will serve... but as for me and my household, we will serve the LORD.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Deuteronomic covenant theology: the land is YHWH's gift, conditional on exclusive loyalty; the ban (ḥerem) enacts that exclusivity in narrative form.
- **Composition vs. setting.** Set at the "conquest" (~1200 BCE) but composed centuries later as part of the exilic Deuteronomistic History.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous canonical transmission; significant Hebrew/Greek textual divergence.

## Themes and motifs
- Conquest and land — the promised land taken and divided.
- Covenant fidelity — the Shechem assembly's "choose this day" (24:15).
- [[divine-kingship]] — YHWH as the true conqueror who "fights for Israel."
