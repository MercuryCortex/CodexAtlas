# Second Epistle to Timothy

## What it is
The most personal and moving of the Pastorals, cast as the apostle Paul's last words from a Roman prison as he faces execution: a farewell "testament" charging Timothy to guard the gospel, endure suffering, and hand the sound teaching on to faithful successors. It contains the canon's central statement on the inspiration of scripture and the famous valediction of the finished race.

## Primary text

### 2 Timothy 3:16–17 and 4:7
*Trans. NRSV*
> All scripture is inspired by God and is useful for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness. (3:16)
> I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, I have kept the faith. (4:7)

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** The passing of the apostolic generation; the urgent transmission of "sound teaching" to successors.
- **Authorship.** Part of the Pastoral sub-corpus; the prison-testament frame is its distinctive feature.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Within the Pauline corpus.

## Themes and motifs
- The inspiration of scripture — 3:16, the cornerstone proof-text of later doctrines of biblical inspiration.
- Faithful endurance — the athlete/soldier finishing the course.
- Succession — entrusting the message "to faithful people who will be able to teach others" (2:2).
