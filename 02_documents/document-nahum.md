# Book of Nahum

## What it is
A short, ferocious poem celebrating the impending destruction of Nineveh, the capital of Assyria. It opens with a hymn to YHWH as an avenging divine warrior (a partial alphabetic acrostic) and proceeds to a vivid, almost cinematic depiction of the city's fall — galloping cavalry, flashing swords, heaps of corpses. Where Jonah shows mercy toward Nineveh, Nahum shows judgment: the two books frame the Assyrian capital from opposite poles.

## Primary text

### Nahum 1:2–3a
*Trans. NRSV*
> A jealous and avenging God is the LORD, the LORD is avenging and wrathful; the LORD takes vengeance on his adversaries and rages against his enemies... The LORD is slow to anger but great in power, and the LORD will by no means clear the guilty.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The collapse of the Assyrian empire; Nineveh fell to the Medes and Babylonians in -612. Nahum writes in the charged interval before that fall, having already seen Assyria itself sack Egyptian Thebes (3:8).
- **Religious milieu.** A theology of YHWH as the just avenger of imperial cruelty — comfort to a Judah long brutalized by Assyria.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous canonical transmission; reinterpreted at Qumran (4QpNah) against later enemies.

## Themes and motifs
- [[chaoskampf]] — the opening theophany casts YHWH as the storm-riding divine warrior who dries up sea and rivers (1:4), the combat-myth idiom turned against a historical empire.
- Divine vengeance on empire — Nineveh's fall as the just recompense for Assyrian atrocity.
- [[divine-kingship]] — YHWH's sovereignty over the great powers.
