# Sūrat al-ʿAlaq (Quran 96 — The Clinging Form)

## What it is
By the consensus of the tradition, the first revelation. Muhammad, in retreat in the cave of Ḥirāʾ above Mecca, is seized by the angel Gabriel and commanded "Iqraʾ!" — "Recite!" He protests that he cannot, is gripped until he can bear no more, and then the opening five verses come: God who created humankind from a mere clinging clot is the same God who teaches by the pen what was unknown. The remainder of the sura (a slightly later addition) rebukes a man — traditionally Abū Jahl — who tries to stop the Prophet from praying.

## Primary text

### Quran 96:1–5
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> Read! In the name of your Lord who created: He created man from a clinging form. Read! Your Lord is the Most Bountiful One who taught by [means of] the pen, who taught man what he did not know.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Meccan, c. 610 CE; the founding moment of Islam as a revealed religion.
- **Tradition.** The root q-r-ʾ ("recite/read") gives the Qur'an its very name — "the Recitation."
- **Discovery / transmission.** The episode at Ḥirāʾ — the terror, the threefold embrace, the descent — is the master-narrative of Islamic prophetology.

## Themes and motifs
- The pen — knowledge as God's gift, literacy as sacred.
- Creation from a clot — humankind's lowly origin against God's bounty.
- The call resisted — the prophet who cannot, made to recite.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **"Recite!" — the reluctant prophet and the heavenly book.** The scene at Ḥirāʾ joins one of the oldest patterns in scripture: the unwilling messenger overwhelmed by a commission he did not seek. Moses pleads that he is slow of speech (Exodus 4:10); Isaiah cries "I am a man of unclean lips" until a coal touches them (Isaiah 6:5–7); Jeremiah objects that he is only a youth and God touches his mouth (Jeremiah 1:6–9); Ezekiel is made to *eat* a written scroll (Ezekiel 3:1–3). Muhammad's threefold "I cannot recite," broken by Gabriel's crushing embrace, belongs unmistakably to this family — but the Qur'an gives it a distinctive emphasis: the first thing revealed is a command to *recite/read* and a celebration of **the pen** as the instrument by which God teaches. Where the Hebrew prophets receive a *word* to speak, Muhammad receives a *Recitation* to deliver, and Islam's founding image is of God as the teacher who writes — seeding the civilization's reverence for the book, the pen, and the act of reading (Lings 1983 pp.43–47; McAuliffe 2006 pp.35–40; Nasr 2015 pp.1543–1549).
