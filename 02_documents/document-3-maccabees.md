# Third Book of Maccabees

## What it is
A short Hellenistic-Jewish deliverance novella whose title is a misnomer: it has nothing to do with the Maccabees or Antiochus. Instead it tells how Ptolemy IV Philopator, enraged at being barred from the Holy of Holies, orders the Jews of Egypt rounded up to be trampled by 500 drunken war-elephants — only for God to send angels who turn the maddened elephants back on the king's own troops, delivering the Jews, who institute a commemorative festival.

## Primary text

### 3 Maccabees 6:18
*Trans. NRSV (Apocrypha)*
> Then the most glorious, almighty, and true God revealed his holy face and opened the heavenly gates, from which two glorious angels of fearful aspect descended, visible to all but the Jews.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Alexandrian Jewry's experience of vulnerability and miraculous deliverance under the Ptolemies.
- **Composition.** Ornate Greek; a festal etiology in the manner of Esther.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Canonical for the Eastern Orthodox; outside the Jewish, Catholic, and Protestant canons.

## Themes and motifs
- Deliverance from genocide — divine rescue through angelic intervention.
- Festival etiology — a commemorative feast like Esther's Purim.
- [[divine-kingship]] — God's protection of his people against an imperial tyrant.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[document-2-maccabees]] — *parallel-motif*: a fellow Hellenistic-Jewish narrative of persecution and divinely-aided deliverance (the title links them, though the events differ).
