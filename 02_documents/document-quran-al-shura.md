# Sūrat al-Shūrā (Quran 42 — Consultation)

## What it is
A Meccan sura (one of the Ḥā-Mīm group) that takes its name from a single praised quality of the believers: they conduct "their affairs by mutual consultation" (shūrā, 42:38). Around it the sura makes two large claims. First, that the religion now revealed is not new but the *same* faith "enjoined on Noah… Abraham, Moses, and Jesus" — one straight path running through all the prophets (42:13). Second, in a verse of rare theological precision, it lays out the three modes by which the transcendent God communicates with a mortal: by inspiration cast into the heart, from behind a veil, or through a sent angelic messenger (42:51).

## Primary text

### Quran 42:51
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> It is not granted to any mortal that God should speak to him except through revelation or from behind a veil, or by sending a messenger to reveal by His command what He will: He is exalted and wise.

### Quran 42:38
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> [Those who] conduct their affairs by mutual consultation and give to others out of what We have provided for them.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Meccan; the sura asserts continuity with the older prophets against the charge of novelty.
- **Tradition.** 42:38 became the proof-text for *shūrā*, the consultative principle invoked across Islamic political thought.
- **Discovery / transmission.** 42:51 gave theology its standard map of revelation — heart-inspiration, veiled speech (as to Moses at the bush), and the angel (as to Muhammad).

## Themes and motifs
- One religion of all the prophets — Noah to Jesus on a single path.
- Consultation — decision by counsel as a mark of faith.
- The modes of divine speech — how the unseen God reaches the human.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **How God speaks — and the one religion of all the prophets.** Sūrat al-Shūrā contains the Qur'an's most analytic statement of revelation itself (42:51): God speaks to a mortal in exactly three ways — *waḥy* cast directly into the heart, speech *from behind a veil*, or *a sent messenger* (an angel). This neatly classifies the very episodes the Bible narrates without theorizing: God speaking "face to face, as a man speaks to his friend" yet hidden in cloud to Moses (Exodus 33:11, 33:20 — the "veil"); the angel of the Lord sent to Abraham and the prophets (the messenger); the word that "came to" Jeremiah and Ezekiel (heart-inspiration). The sura pairs this with the claim of 42:13 that the *same* religion was "enjoined on Noah, Abraham, Moses, and Jesus" — a strong statement of the Islamic view that the prophets form one continuous tradition and Islam restores, rather than replaces, the faith of all of them. And from the half-verse on *shūrā* (42:38), Muslim political thought across the centuries built its case that legitimate authority rules by consultation, not by caprice — a thread reaching to modern debates on Islam and democratic counsel (Reynolds 2010 pp.211–220; McAuliffe 2006 pp.163–180; Nasr 2015 pp.1191–1205).
