# Sūrat Yūnus (Quran 10 — Jonah)

## What it is
A Meccan sura built around the signs of God in creation, the certainty of resurrection, and the Quran's own inimitability — including the famous challenge (10:38) to anyone who doubts its origin: "then produce a sura like it." Although named for Jonah, his story is told in a single arresting verse (10:98), where his people become the one exception to the Quran's iron rule that rejected prophets bring destruction: Nineveh believed, and was spared.

## Primary text

### Quran 10:98
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> If only a single town had believed and benefited from its belief! Only Jonah's people did so, and when they believed, We relieved them of the punishment of disgrace in the life of this world, and let them enjoy life for a while.

### Quran 10:38
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> Or do they say, "He has invented it"? Say, "Then produce a sura like it, and call on anyone you can beside God if you are telling the truth."

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Meccan; the sura answers the charge that Muhammad invented the Quran by staking the revelation's truth on its irreproducibility.
- **Tradition.** Jonah's people are the theological counter-case: divine punishment is conditional, and sincere repentance can avert it.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Jonah's ordeal in the fish is not told here but at 21:87–88 and 37:139–148, where he becomes *dhū al-nūn*, "the man of the fish."

## Themes and motifs
- The signs (āyāt) of God in sun, moon, and the alternation of night and day.
- The inimitability of the Quran — the challenge to match even one sura.
- Repentance accepted — the one city that turned back in time.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The one prophet whose city was spared.** Every other warner in the Quran — Noah, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Lot, Shuʿayb — preaches to a people who refuse and are destroyed. Jonah's Nineveh is the deliberate exception (10:98): they believed, and the punishment was lifted. This is exactly the theological burden of the biblical Book of Jonah, where God's readiness to relent on Nineveh's repentance is the lesson the reluctant prophet cannot accept (Jonah 3–4). The Quran preserves the lesson — mercy answers repentance — while quietly omitting Jonah's angry protest at that mercy, so that the bitter, ironic prophet of the Hebrew book becomes, in Islam, a clean sign of God's clemency. The fish episode, detached from this sura, generates the enduring epithet *dhū al-nūn* and a whole devotional tradition of the prayer from the belly of the fish (21:87: "There is no god but You, glory be to You, I was wrong") — recited across the Muslim world as the prayer of those in distress (Reynolds 2010 pp.117–125; Tottoli 2002 pp.124–130; Nasr 2015 pp.567–571, 591).
