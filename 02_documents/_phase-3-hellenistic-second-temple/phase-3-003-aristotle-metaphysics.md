# Aristotle — Metaphysics and De Anima

## What it is
Two interlocking treatises from the corpus of Aristotle's school-internal ("esoteric") works.
- **Metaphysics** — fourteen books (Α, α, Β, Γ, Δ, Ε, Ζ, Η, Θ, Ι, Κ, Λ, Μ, Ν) of first philosophy: being qua being, substance, potentiality and actuality, and culminating in Book Λ (Lambda)'s theology of the *unmoved mover* — a self-thinking thought (*noēsis noēseōs*) that moves all things by being the object of cosmic desire without itself moving.
- **De Anima** (*Peri Psychēs*) — three books on the soul as the form of a living body: vegetative (nutrition, growth), sensitive (perception), and rational (intellect, *nous*) capacities. Book III's *active intellect* (Α 5) becomes one of the most contested passages in the history of philosophy.

Together they constitute Aristotle's mature theology and psychology.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Aristotle's life spans the rise of Macedon: he was tutor to the young Alexander (~-343) at Pella, then returned to Athens and founded the Lyceum (~-335). After Alexander's death (-323), anti-Macedonian feeling in Athens forced Aristotle to flee ("lest the Athenians sin twice against philosophy" — referencing Socrates); he died at Chalcis the next year.
- **Religious milieu.** Aristotle accepted civic religion outwardly but his theology is philosophical: the *unmoved mover* is impersonal, contemplative, and has no providential care for the world. This is the divinity later Christian and Islamic thinkers had to *modify* heavily to make it congruent with revealed religion.
- **Who was behind it.** Aristotle himself, working from his lecture pulpit at the Lyceum. The treatises read as edited lecture notes, not polished prose; this is one reason the texts were called *esōterika* (inside-the-school) versus the lost polished dialogues (*exōterika*).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Strabo (*Geography* 13.1.54) tells the famous story: after Theophrastus's death, Aristotle's library passed to Neleus, was hidden in a cellar to keep it from the Attalid book-collectors, and rotted there for over a century until being rediscovered, sold, and brought to Rome where Andronicus of Rhodes produced the first proper edition (~-50). Whether literally true or not, the corpus was indeed lost from circulation in the early Hellenistic period and recovered late.

## Themes and motifs
- [[unmoved-mover]] — *Metaphysics* Λ 7–9: a being of pure actuality (*energeia*), pure thinking thinking itself, the final cause toward which the cosmos eternally moves. The first philosophical "God of the philosophers".
- [[forms-and-archetypes]] — Aristotle's *hylomorphism* (form-matter compound) is his answer to Plato's separated Forms; form is internal to substance, not transcendent.
- [[soul-immortality]] — controversial: Aristotle says the *active intellect* "alone is immortal and eternal" (*DA* III.5, 430a23), but this is read variously as personal survival, impersonal divine Nous shared by all minds (Alexander of Aphrodisias's reading; Averroes's), or as something in between.
- [[teleology]] — every natural process is directed toward an end (*telos*); this teleological grammar persists into Christian providence and only collapses with the 17th-c. mechanical revolution.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — *commentary-on* / *polemic-against*: *Metaphysics* Α 9 systematically critiques Plato's Forms. Yet the framework (substance, form, soul, divine intellect) is fundamentally Platonic in inheritance.
- → [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]] — *direct-influence*: Philo absorbs Aristotelian terminology (potency/act, the categories) into his Middle-Platonist Logos theology.
- → [[phase-3-005-stoic-foundational-texts]] — *shared-milieu*: Stoic physics develops in dialogue with both Plato and Aristotle; Aristotle's *nous* and the Stoic *logos* are competing visions of cosmic intelligence.
- → Patristic theology (Origen, Augustine, John of Damascus — Phase 4+) — *direct-influence*: the Christian God-as-Pure-Act formulation is Aristotelian.
- → Islamic philosophy (al-Farabi, Avicenna, Averroes — Phase 5) — *direct-influence*: Aristotle as "the First Teacher"; the *active intellect* doctrine becomes central to falsafa.

## Disputes
- **Date of Metaphysics composition.** Multiple authorial layers and an editorial composite (Andronicus): different books may date from different periods of Aristotle's career (Jaeger's developmental hypothesis vs. unitarian readings).
- **Active intellect.** Personal-individual (Aquinas) vs. shared-divine (Averroes) readings have been live for two millennia; modern scholarship is divided.
- **Theology proper.** Whether Metaphysics Λ's unmoved mover is "the only god" or one of many (the discussion of fifty-five movers in Λ 8 raises this) — and whether Aristotle was *truly* a theist or a philosophical naturalist using theological vocabulary.
