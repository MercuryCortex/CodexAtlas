# Q Source (Quelle)

## What it is
A **hypothetical** lost sayings collection, postulated to explain the ~235 verses of material common to Matthew and Luke but absent from Mark — the so-called "Double Tradition". Reconstructed Q consists almost entirely of *sayings* (with a few narrative frames: the Temptation, the centurion at Capernaum). Major Q material includes the Sermon on the Plain / Mount (Beatitudes, Lord's Prayer, Golden Rule), the mission discourse, woes against the Pharisees, eschatological sayings (Son of Man coming), and Wisdom-Christology sayings (Q 7:35 "Wisdom is vindicated by her children"; Q 11:49 the "Wisdom of God" sending prophets). The reconstructed corpus is conventionally cited by Lukan chapter and verse (e.g., Q 6:20–23 = the Beatitudes).

**[claim: contested]** — Q's *existence* is the majority position (Two-Source Hypothesis: Mark + Q as the two sources of Matthew and Luke), held by Streeter, Kloppenborg, Robinson, the International Q Project. The minority **Farrer-Goulder-Goodacre hypothesis** dispenses with Q, arguing Luke directly used Matthew. We carry the Q hypothesis as the working scholarly default while flagging the live dispute.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Roman Palestine in the period following Jesus's death (~+30/+33), under Roman procurators with client-king fragments. The Galilean villages and the southern Syrian / Decapolis region are the most-argued composition zone.
- **Religious milieu.** A Jewish-Christian movement carrying Jesus's teachings orally, then in initial written collections. Q presupposes an itinerant prophetic-charismatic group practicing radical discipleship (Q 9:57–62, Q 10:2–16 mission discourse). The community is still within Judaism and views Jesus through prophetic and Wisdom categories — not yet the high Christology of the later epistles and Gospels.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous. Kloppenborg's three-stratum model: Q1 = a wisdom-instruction core (Beatitudes, mission discourse, prayer); Q2 = apocalyptic / prophetic-judgment expansion (woes, Son of Man comings, judgment on "this generation"); Q3 = a late narrative-frame layer (Temptation). The three layers track movement from village-wisdom-school to apocalyptic crisis.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Q was a literary hypothesis from the 1830s (Schleiermacher, then C.H. Weisse 1838) before being decisively formalized by B.H. Streeter (1924). No manuscript has ever been found. Its plausibility was reinforced by the 1945 discovery of the Gospel of Thomas — a Coptic sayings-gospel of analogous form — at Nag Hammadi.

## Themes and motifs
- [[wisdom-personified]] — Q's Christology presents Jesus as Wisdom's emissary or *embodied* Wisdom: Q 7:35 (Wisdom vindicated), Q 10:21–22 (the Father-Son revelation), Q 11:49–51 (Wisdom sends prophets). This Sophia-Christology is one of Q's distinctive features.
- [[apocalyptic-revelation]] — Q 17:23–37 the Son-of-Man-day sayings; judgment language; "this generation" denunciations.
- [[son-of-man]] — Q is one of the principal sources for Synoptic "Son of Man" sayings.
- [[itinerant-discipleship]] — Q 9:57–62 ("no place to lay his head"), Q 10:2–11 (mission charge, no staff, no purse). Reflects a charismatic itinerant lifestyle that has been compared to Cynic philosophers' beggar-discipleship.
- [[reign-of-god]] — the *basileia* of God as imminent and present (Q 11:20, Q 13:18–21 the mustard seed and leaven).

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] — *direct-influence* / *redaction-of*: Matthew incorporates Q material extensively, often organized into discourse blocks (Sermon on the Mount).
- → [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] — *direct-influence* / *redaction-of*: Luke incorporates Q material, generally in a different order (Sermon on the Plain), often with greater preservation of original phrasing per Kloppenborg.
- → [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — *parallel-motif*: Q and Mark are two separate early streams of Jesus tradition; their independent agreements increase confidence in older traditions.
- → Gospel of Thomas (Phase 4) — *parallel-motif*: a Greek-then-Coptic sayings collection whose form-class is identical to Q. Many sayings overlap (Q 12:22–31 worry / Thomas 36; Q 11:33 lamp / Thomas 33). Whether Thomas used Q, drew on parallel Jesus-tradition, or both is debated.
- → Jewish wisdom literature ([[phase-3-007-sirach]], Proverbs) — *parallel-motif*: Q's wisdom-sayings genre is Jewish-wisdom form-class extended.

## Disputes
- **Existence.** Two-Source Hypothesis (Q-affirming) is majority; Farrer-Goodacre (Q-denying) is significant minority. Streeter, Kloppenborg, Robinson defend Q; Farrer, Goulder, Goodacre, Watson reject it.
- **Stratification.** Kloppenborg's Q1/Q2/Q3 model is influential but not universally accepted; Goodacre argues stratification adds layers of hypothesis to an already hypothetical document.
- **Galilean vs. Syrian provenance.** Kloppenborg favors Galilee; some prefer the Syrian-Antiochene region.
- **Relationship to historical Jesus.** Whether Q preserves the *earliest* and *most authentic* Jesus tradition (Robinson, Mack) or is an early Christian theological construction less than 30 years from Jesus (Kloppenborg) is contested.
