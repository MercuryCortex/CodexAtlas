# Sirach

## What it is
A book of wisdom instruction in 51 chapters composed in Hebrew around 190–175 BCE by Yeshua ben Eleazar ben Sira, a Jerusalemite sage and scribe. The grandson translated it into Greek in Alexandria a generation later (after -132, dated by the translator's preface mentioning his arrival "in the thirty-eighth year of Euergetes"). The book combines (a) classical Hebrew proverb-style wisdom on family, friendship, prudence, work, and conduct; (b) hymnic passages on the personified figure of Wisdom (24:1–22, identifying Wisdom with the Torah); (c) the famous "Praise of the Ancestors" (chs. 44–50), surveying biblical heroes from Enoch to the contemporary high priest Simon II.

## Primary text — selected passages

### Sirach 24:1–9, 23 — Wisdom speaks: her origin and identification with Torah
*Trans. Skehan, P.W. and Di Lella, A.A. (1987). The Wisdom of Ben Sira. Anchor Yale Bible 39. Yale University Press.*
> Wisdom praises herself,
>   and tells of her glory in the midst of her people.
> In the assembly of the Most High she opens her mouth,
>   and in the presence of his hosts she tells of her glory:
> "I came forth from the mouth of the Most High,
>   and covered the earth like a mist.
> I dwelt in the highest heavens,
>   and my throne was in a pillar of cloud.
> Alone I compassed the vault of heaven
>   and traversed the depths of the abyss.
> Over waves of the sea, over all the earth,
>   and over every people and nation I have held sway."
> [...]
> All this is the book of the covenant of the Most High God,
>   the law that Moses commanded us.

*Sir 24:3's "from the mouth of the Most High" anticipates John 1:1's Logos theology — both make the divine word the origin-point of cosmic Wisdom/Logos. Sir 24:23's explicit Wisdom = Torah equation becomes the foundation of all subsequent rabbinic theology of Torah-study as mystical union.*

### Sirach 44:1–7 — Praise of the Ancestors: the catalogue-of-heroes form
*Trans. Skehan & Di Lella 1987*
> Let us now sing the praises of famous men,
>   our ancestors in their generations.
> The Lord apportioned to them great glory,
>   his majesty from the beginning.
> There were those who ruled in their kingdoms,
>   and made a name for themselves by their valor;
> those who gave counsel because they were intelligent;
>   those who spoke in prophetic oracles...
> All these were honored in their generations,
>   and were the pride of their times.

*Sir 44–50's literary form — sustained heroic praise of a sequence of named exemplars — is the template for Hebrews 11 ("the cloud of witnesses") and for Christian hagiographic biography. Ben Sira invented the "sacred hall of fame" as a literary genre.*

### Sirach 50:1, 11–13 — Simon the High Priest in the Temple
*Trans. Skehan & Di Lella 1987*
> The leader of his brothers and the pride of his people was the high priest Simon son of Onias, who in his life repaired the house, and in his time fortified the temple...
> When he put on his glorious robe and clothed himself in perfect splendor, when he went up to the holy altar, he made the court of the sanctuary glorious. When he received the portions from the hands of the priests, as he stood by the hearth of the altar with a garland of brothers around him, he was like a young cedar on Lebanon...

*This description of Simon officiating in the Temple cult is almost certainly an eyewitness account — giving Sir 50 unique status as a direct contemporary portrait of Second Temple Temple worship in action.*

*Citations: Skehan & Di Lella 1987, AYB 39.*

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Judea is a Seleucid province; the Tobiad-Oniad rivalry that will erupt under Antiochus IV is already simmering. Ben Sira writes within Jerusalem's priestly-scribal establishment under High Priest Simon II ("Simon the Just"), whom he celebrates in chapter 50 in shining detail — almost certainly from personal observation of him officiating in the Temple.
- **Religious milieu.** Pre-sectarian Second Temple Judaism. Ben Sira represents *establishment* wisdom Judaism: Torah-centered, Temple-loyal, sapiential. He polemicizes against an unnamed group some scholars identify with proto-Enochic apocalypticists (Sir 3:21–24 against "speculating about hidden things") — possibly a counter-current to the contemporary 1 Enoch.
- **Who was behind it.** Ben Sira himself, named in 50:27 — one of the few Second Temple Jewish authors who *signs* his work, as Egyptian and Greek sages did but Hebrew biblical writers generally did not.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Original Hebrew vanished from circulation in Late Antiquity, surviving in Christian translation only (Greek in LXX, Latin in Vulgate). The 1896 Cairo Genizah discovery (Solomon Schechter) recovered the largest run of Hebrew fragments; Qumran and Masada added more — now we have about two-thirds of the original Hebrew text.

## Themes and motifs
- [[wisdom-personified]] — Sir 24 has Wisdom (Hokhmah / Sophia) speak in the first person: "I came forth from the mouth of the Most High... I took root in an honored people, in the portion of the Lord, his heritage." She is identified with the Torah of Moses. This *personified Wisdom* is a critical bridge: from Proverbs 8 → Sirach 24 → Wisdom of Solomon → Philo's Logos → Johannine Christology.
- [[fear-of-the-lord]] — the formal program ("the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom", inherited from Proverbs) governs the whole.
- [[torah-and-wisdom-identified]] — Sir 24:23 explicitly equates Wisdom with the "book of the covenant of the Most High God, the law that Moses commanded us." This identification becomes axiomatic for Hellenistic and rabbinic Judaism.
- [[honor-of-fathers]] — Praise of the Ancestors (chs. 44–50) is a literary precedent for Hebrews 11 and influences Christian "lives of the saints" hagiography.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-012-wisdom-of-solomon]] — *parallel-motif* / *direct-influence*: Wisdom of Solomon expands Ben Sira's personified Wisdom (Sophia) with Stoic and Platonist categories.
- → [[phase-3-006-septuagint]] — *manuscript-transmission*: Sirach is part of the LXX canon (and thus the Christian deuterocanon) though its Hebrew form was eventually excluded from the Jewish canon at Yavneh.
- → [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — *polemic-against* (probable): Sir 3:21–24's prohibition of speculation about "what is too hard for you" reads as a critique of Enochic apocalyptic curiosity. Argued by Argall (1995), Boccaccini.
- → New Testament epistle of James — *parallel-motif*: James's practical wisdom in tone and structure closely echoes Sirach (and Proverbs).
- → Synoptic "wisdom-of-Jesus" sayings — *parallel-motif*: the proverbial-form material common to Q and Sirach reflects shared Second Temple wisdom culture, not necessarily literary dependence.

## Disputes
- **Canonical status.** Accepted as scripture in the Septuagint and by Catholic and Orthodox churches (as *Ecclesiasticus*); excluded from the rabbinic Hebrew canon by the late 1st c. CE; quoted as "Ben Sira" in rabbinic sources but with non-scriptural status.
- **Critique-of-Enochic-Judaism reading.** Argall's reading that Sirach polemicizes against Enochic apocalyptic wisdom is influential but contested.
- **Original Hebrew text.** Reconstruction from Genizah + Qumran + Masada is incomplete; ~30% of the Hebrew is still lost, and the Greek/Syriac/Latin versions show some doctrinal expansion (especially Sir 24 in the Greek).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges

- **Sirach 24 — the critical bridge in the Prov 8 → Sir 24 → Wis 7 → Philo → John 1 Logos chain, the single most documentable intellectual genealogy in Christian theology**: Proverbs 8 has Wisdom present at creation; Sirach 24 has Wisdom speaking "from the mouth of the Most High" and pitching her tent in Israel; Wisdom of Solomon 7:25 has Sophia as "breath of the power of God, a pure emanation of the glory of the Almighty"; Philo of Alexandria (~-20 to +50) identifies Sophia with the divine Logos (the rational structure of creation); John 1:1–14 identifies the Logos as the pre-existent divine principle who "became flesh." The verbal link between each stage is traceable in the Greek text. Sirach 24 is the hinge-point: it takes Proverbs' poetic personification and gives it a liturgical-hymnic form ("I came forth from the mouth of the Most High") that the Wisdom of Solomon and Philo then elevate into hypostasis. Without Sir 24, the chain from Proverbs to John 1 is broken (Skehan & Di Lella 1987, AYB 39 pp.328–334; Brown 1966, *The Gospel According to John*, Anchor Bible vol. 1 pp.519–524; Witherington 1994, *Jesus the Sage*, Fortress pp.69–78).

- **Sirach's explicit Torah-Wisdom identification (24:23) — the theological equation that generates the rabbinic bet midrash tradition and Jewish mystical Torah-theology**: Sir 24:23 ("All this is the book of the covenant of the Most High God, the law that Moses commanded us") is the first explicit written equation of personified Wisdom with the Torah. Before Ben Sira, Wisdom and Torah were parallel but distinct categories in Jewish thought. After him, they are one — which means studying Torah is communing with the divine Wisdom present at creation. This equation becomes foundational to: rabbinic Judaism's elevation of Torah-study as the highest religious act; the institution of the bet midrash (house of study); the medieval Kabbalistic theology in which Torah = the name of God = the first *sefirah* (Hokhmah / Wisdom in the *Zohar*); and Ben Sira's counter to Hellenistic philosophy — Israel already has Wisdom, embodied in Torah. This is the most consequential identification in the history of Jewish intellectual life (von Rad 1972, *Wisdom in Israel*, Abingdon pp.240–262; Urbach 1975, *The Sages*, Magnes pp.286–295; Neusner 1991, *Torah: From Scroll to Symbol*, Scholars Press).

- **The Praise of the Ancestors (Sir 44–50) — invention of the "catalogue of heroes" form, generating Hebrews 11 and the entire Western hagiographic tradition**: Sir 44–50's sustained encomium of biblical heroes (Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Moses, David, Solomon, culminating in the high priest Simon II) is the literary template for Hebrews 11 ("by faith Abel... by faith Noah... by faith Abraham..." — the most direct structural borrowing from Sirach in the NT), the early Christian *martyrologia*, the medieval *Acta Sanctorum*, and ultimately the Western biographical commemorative tradition. Ben Sira invented the "sacred hall of fame" as a literary genre — the idea that the exemplary dead form a "cloud of witnesses" (Heb 12:1) guiding the living. This genre then generates: Julian of Norwich's list of holy women, Dante's Paradiso (concentric circles of saints), hagiographic biography from Bede to Foxe's *Book of Martyrs*, and secular equivalents (Plutarch's *Parallel Lives* is a near-contemporary parallel) (Skehan & Di Lella 1987 pp.495–516; deSilva 1996, *The Jewish Teachers of Jesus*, Scholars Press; Lane 1991, *Hebrews*, WBC 47 pp.319–330).

- **The Cairo Genizah rediscovery of Ben Sira's Hebrew (Schechter 1896) — the most dramatic pre-Qumran recovery of a lost biblical text, founding modern genizah studies**: Sirach's original Hebrew had been considered irrecoverably lost for over 1,400 years — only the Greek and Latin translations survived. Solomon Schechter's 1896 discovery of Hebrew Ben Sira fragments in the Cairo Genizah's "chamber of secrets" (eventually recovering approximately two-thirds of the book) is the closest 19th-century precedent for the 1947 Dead Sea Scrolls discovery: ancient Hebrew religious texts retrieved from a sealed repository. The Genizah discovery restructured understanding of the LXX translation process (the Greek is sometimes expansionist), of Second Temple Hebrew usage, and of the canon's formation (Ben Sira was clearly read and valued in Hebrew-speaking communities long after the rabbinic canon excluded it). Schechter's find also founded the field of genizah studies, enabling later Cambridge scholars to recover hundreds of thousands of medieval Jewish documents (Schechter & Taylor 1899, Cambridge UP; Reif 2000, *A Jewish Archive from Old Cairo*, Curzon; Bar-Ilan 1985, "The Date of Ben Sira," *HUCA* 56).
