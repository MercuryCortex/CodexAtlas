# Carmen Astrologicum (Pentateuch)

## What it is
A five-book **didactic astrological poem** ("Pentateuch"/"Pentabiblos") composed in Greek hexameters by **Dorotheus of Sidon** in the 1st century CE. It is the earliest systematic Hellenistic treatment of **electional astrology** (choosing auspicious moments to begin undertakings) and **inceptional/horary** technique, alongside natal doctrine. Its enormous historical importance is partly intrinsic and partly accidental of survival: the Greek original is lost, and the *Carmen* reaches us through a **Sasanian Pahlavi translation that was then rendered into Arabic around 800 CE** — so the very text-object is a fossil of the Greek-to-Persian-to-Arabic transmission that carried Hellenistic astrology into the Islamic world and back to Europe.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Composed under the early Roman Empire; transmitted across the Sasanian Persian and then the early ʿAbbāsid worlds — three successive imperial translation cultures.
- **Religious milieu.** Originally Greco-Egyptian Hellenistic astrology (Dorotheus invokes the Nechepso–Petosiris charter); later naturalized into Zoroastrian Sasanian court science and then into the astrology of the Islamic Golden Age.
- **Who was behind it.** Dorotheus, a named but shadowy 1st-century figure; then the anonymous Pahlavi translators of Sasanian Iran; then the named ʿAbbāsid translator **ʿUmar ibn al-Farrukhān al-Ṭabarī** (c. 800).
- **Discovery / transmission.** The relay Greek → Pahlavi → Arabic is the headline. From the Arabic, Dorothean electional doctrine entered the work of Māshāʾallāh and the Baghdad astrologers, and through them medieval Latin Europe.

## Themes and motifs
- [[divinatory-omen-reading]] — the systematization of prognostic and elective technique.
- [[wisdom-as-cosmic-order]] — choosing the right moment presumes a lawful, legible cosmic order.
- [[microcosm-macrocosm]] — the sky's configuration at an inception governs the outcome of the act begun under it.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-038-ptolemy-tetrabiblos]] / [[phase-3-039-vettius-valens-anthologiae]] — *contemporary-parallel*: with Ptolemy and Valens, Dorotheus completes the trio of foundational 1st–2nd-century Hellenistic astrologers; Dorotheus is the electional/horary specialist.
- → [[phase-1-019-enuma-anu-enlil]] — *transmission-of*: the inherited zodiacal and prognostic substrate is Babylonian.
- → [[phase-3-041-yavanajataka]] — *parallel-transmission*: Dorothean and related Greek material is among the Hellenistic astrology that crossed into India.
- → [[al-kindi]] / [[tradition-western-astrology]] — *direct-influence*: the Arabic *Carmen* shaped early ʿAbbāsid astrology and, through it, the Latin West.

## Disputes
- **How much of the Arabic *Carmen* is genuinely Dorothean.** The Pahlavi-to-Arabic relay introduced updates and interpolations (e.g. later horoscope examples); Pingree's edition distinguishes layers, but the boundary between original Dorotheus and Sasanian/Abbasid accretion is not everywhere certain.
- **Dorotheus's exact date** (1st c. CE) rests on the datable example horoscopes embedded in the text.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **A text that *is* its own transmission story.** Few documents so literally embody the Atlas thesis: a Greek poem, lost in Greek, preserved only because it was translated into Pahlavi under the Zoroastrian Sasanians and then into Arabic under the Muslim ʿAbbāsids — the same relay (Pingree's "the transmission node") by which the whole of Hellenistic astrology reached Islam and, via 12th-century Toledo, Latin Christendom (Pingree 1976, 1997).
- **The source of Western electional and horary astrology.** The doctrine of choosing auspicious moments and of answering questions from the chart of the moment they are asked — central to medieval Arabic and Latin practice and alive in modern astrology — descends in large part from Dorotheus (Tester 1987; Barton 1994).
