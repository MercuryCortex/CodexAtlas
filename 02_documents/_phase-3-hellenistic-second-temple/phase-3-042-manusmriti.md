# The Laws of Manu (Mānava-Dharmaśāstra)

## What it is

The *Manusmṛti* — "The Laws of Manu," more precisely the *Mānava-Dharmaśāstra* ("the Dharma treatise of Manu") — is the most authoritative and most-cited work of the Hindu **Dharmaśāstra** genre: the Sanskrit legal-ethical literature that codifies *dharma*, the ordered duty binding cosmos and society. Composed as roughly 2,685 metrical verses (*ślokas*) across twelve chapters (*adhyāyas*) between about 200 BCE and 200 CE, it is *smṛti* ("that which is remembered" — authored tradition), ranked below the revealed *śruti* of the Vedas but treated in later Brahmanical thought as their practical extension into law and conduct.

The text is framed as a teaching of **[[manu]]** — Manu Svāyambhuva, the primordial man and first lawgiver, "born of the Self-existent" — delivered to the assembled sages and transmitted to humanity through the sage Bhṛgu. It opens (chapter 1) with a cosmogony, moves through the stages and duties of the twice-born life (*saṃskāras*, studentship, marriage, householdership), lays out the duties of the king and the administration of justice (*rājadharma* and *vyavahāra*), treats permitted occupations, purity and pollution, penance (*prāyaścitta*), and closes (chapter 12) with *karma*, rebirth, and the paths to liberation (*mokṣa*).

## Primary text — the opening cosmogony

The Manusmṛti is in the public domain. The vault's reader carries **Manu 1.1–13** (text id `laws-of-manu-1`) in Georg Bühler's translation (*Sacred Books of the East* vol. 25, 1886); short fair-use quotation below.

> The great sages approached Manu, who was seated with a collected mind, and, having duly worshipped him, spoke as follows: "Deign, divine one, to declare to us precisely and in due order the sacred laws of each of the (four chief) castes (varna) and of the intermediate ones."
> …
> He, desiring to produce beings of many kinds from his own body, first with a thought created the waters, and placed his seed in them. That (seed) became a golden egg, in brilliancy equal to the sun; in that (egg) he himself was born as Brahman, the progenitor of the whole world.

Citation: Manu 1.1–2, 1.8–9; trans. Bühler (1886). The golden egg (*hiraṇyagarbha*) and the seed-in-the-waters are the Manusmṛti's reworking of the Vedic cosmogonies of [[phase-1-031-rigveda]] (RV 10.121 and RV 10.129).

## Historical context / composition

The Manusmṛti did not spring from a single author. Patrick Olivelle's critical work (2005) shows it as a **redaction of an earlier metrical Dharmasūtra tradition** — the prose-and-verse law codes of Āpastamba, Gautama, Baudhāyana, and Vasiṣṭha — reworked into a unified versified treatise and retro-attributed to the mythic Manu to claim the highest possible authority. Its consolidation belongs to the post-Mauryan / early classical period, when Brahmanical orthodoxy was defining itself against the heterodox movements (Buddhism, Jainism) and the recent memory of Aśoka's Buddhist-inflected *dhamma* (see [[phase-3-030-asokan-edicts]]).

Its later career is extraordinary. Medieval commentators (Medhātithi ~9th c., Kullūka ~13th c.) built a vast juristic apparatus on it. In 1794 **Sir William Jones** translated it as the *Institutes of Hindu Law*, and the British colonial administration adopted it as the authoritative code of "Hindu law" — giving a single Brahmanical text a legal reach across the subcontinent it had never historically possessed. In the twentieth century the same text became a symbol of caste oppression: **B.R. Ambedkar publicly burned the Manusmṛti on 25 December 1927** during the Mahad Satyagraha, repudiating its sacralization of the *varṇa* hierarchy and its strictures on women and *śūdras*.

## Defining contributions

**1. *Dharma* as cosmic-social order.** The Manusmṛti is the classical crystallization of *dharma* as the ordering law that binds cosmos and society together — right conduct is the maintenance of an order established *at creation*, not human convention. This is the Vedic branch's mature "law-from-cosmic-order" doctrine, descended from Vedic *ṛta*; see [[moral-rta-cosmic-order]] and [[wisdom-as-cosmic-order]].

**2. The codification of *varṇāśrama-dharma*.** It is the fullest classical statement of the four-*varṇa* social order and the four *āśramas* (life-stages), grounding both in the cosmogony: Manu 1.31 derives the four estates from the body of the creator — the direct reworking of the Ṛgvedic Puruṣa Sūkta (RV 10.90) into social-legal doctrine. See [[cosmic-body-cosmogony]].

**3. The reference-text of the Dharmaśāstra genre.** Of all the Dharmaśāstras it became the most authoritative and most-commented, the practical anchor of Hindu jurisprudence for two millennia and the lens through which colonial and modern audiences first encountered "Hindu law."

## Relations to neighbors

- → [[phase-1-031-rigveda]] — *source-cosmogony*: Manu chapter 1 reworks the Vedic golden-egg (RV 10.121 Hiraṇyagarbha) and the cosmic-body (RV 10.90 Puruṣa Sūkta) cosmogonies into its own creation account.
- → [[cosmic-body-cosmogony]] — *reworked-parallel*: Manu 1.31 (varṇas from the creator's body) is the classical Indian instance of the cosmos/society-from-a-primordial-body pattern.
- → [[moral-rta-cosmic-order]] — *canonical-source*: the mature Vedic-branch witness to dharma-as-cosmic-law, cognate with Egyptian *ma'at* and Greek *logos*.
- → [[phase-1-007-code-of-hammurabi]] — *parallel-genre* (**cross-tradition**): both are sacralized law-codes that ground human justice in a divinely-established order — Hammurabi's stele receives the law from Shamash; Manu receives dharma from the Self-existent. Independent instances of "law from cosmic/divine order."
- → [[phase-3-030-asokan-edicts]] — *contemporary-counterpoint*: Aśoka's Buddhist *dhamma* of the edicts is the near-contemporary rival articulation of righteous order against which Brahmanical *dharma* consolidated.
- → [[phase-2-027-bhagavad-gita]] — *dharma-neighbor*: the Gītā's *sva-dharma* ethic and the Manusmṛti's *varṇa-dharma* belong to the same classical discourse on duty.

## Disputes

- **Composition and unity.** Whether the received text has a coherent single-author design (Olivelle argues for a strong redactorial hand) or is a looser accretion remains debated; Olivelle's critical edition also distinguishes an earlier recoverable text from the fuller medieval *vulgate*.
- **Historical legal force.** How far the Manusmṛti actually governed real Indian legal practice — versus functioning as an idealized Brahmanical prescription later *made* into positive law by colonial adoption — is a central question in the history of South Asian law.
- **Caste, gender, and reception.** Its statements on *śūdras*, untouchability, and women are the focus of enduring moral and political controversy, from Ambedkar's repudiation to present-day debate.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges

- **Dharma ↔ ṛta ↔ ma'at ↔ logos ↔ natural law: the Manusmṛti as the Vedic-branch node in the vault's cosmic-order-as-law cluster.** The Manusmṛti gives classical doctrinal form to the idea that human law is the earthly face of a cosmic order fixed at creation — the same conviction the vault tracks through Egyptian *ma'at*, the Greek and Stoic *logos*, and the later natural-law tradition that feeds modern human-rights discourse. Manu is the mature Indian witness to this trans-civilizational conviction. Ref: Olivelle 2005; Doniger & Smith 1991. See [[moral-rta-cosmic-order]], [[wisdom-as-cosmic-order]].

- **Manu 1.31 ↔ Puruṣa Sūkta (RV 10.90): cosmic-body cosmogony carried from Vedic hymn into social law.** The Puruṣa Sūkta creates the four social estates from the body-parts of the sacrificed cosmic Man (mouth→priest, arms→ruler, thighs→commoner, feet→servant). Manu 1.31 restates exactly this mapping, transposing a hymnic cosmogony into a legal charter for the *varṇa* hierarchy — a rare, precisely-documented case of a cosmogonic myth being deployed to sacralize a social order. This links Manu to the vault's three-tradition cosmic-body cluster (Puruṣa Sūkta / Enuma Elish–Tiamat / Norse Ymir). Ref: Doniger & Smith 1991; Bühler 1886. See [[cosmic-body-cosmogony]], [[phase-1-031-rigveda]].
