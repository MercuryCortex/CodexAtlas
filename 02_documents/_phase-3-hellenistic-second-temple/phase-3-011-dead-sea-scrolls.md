# Dead Sea Scrolls — Sectarian Core

## What it is
This node focuses on the **sectarian core** of the Qumran library — the writings produced *by* the community itself, not the broader Hebrew Bible and pseudepigraphal material also stored there. Three texts anchor the corpus:
- **Community Rule (Serek ha-Yahad, 1QS)** — the constitutional document of the *Yahad* ("Unity"): admission procedure, hierarchy, communal meals, annual covenant renewal, the famous Two Spirits Treatise (1QS 3:13–4:26).
- **War Scroll (Milhamah, 1QM)** — a battle plan for the final eschatological war of the "Sons of Light" (the community + the angel Michael) against the "Sons of Darkness" (led by Belial), occupying forty years of culminating world history.
- **Hodayot / Thanksgiving Hymns (1QHᵃ)** — first-person psalmic compositions, some attributed by scholars to the Teacher of Righteousness himself; rich in dualistic, predestinarian, and self-abasing vocabulary.

Together they constitute the theological program of the Qumran sect.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Hasmonean and early Roman Judea. The community originated in priestly opposition to the Hasmonean usurpation of the high priesthood (Jonathan Maccabee, ~-152, is one candidate for the "Wicked Priest" of the pesharim). They withdrew to Qumran by the Dead Sea, where archaeological evidence shows habitation from ~-100 to the Roman destruction of the site in -68 CE.
- **Religious milieu.** A Jewish sectarian community organized as a (probably celibate, certainly hierarchical) covenantal society practicing extreme ritual purity, communal property, and intensive scriptural study. Most scholars identify them with the **Essenes** described by Philo, Josephus, and Pliny — though some prefer "Yahad" as the self-designation actually attested.
- **Who was behind it.** Founded under the leadership of an unnamed **Teacher of Righteousness** (Moreh ha-Tzedek) — a priestly figure of charismatic interpretive authority who broke with the Jerusalem establishment and founded the sect. He authored or inspired some of the Hodayot.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Bedouin shepherds discovered the first cave in late 1946 or early 1947; ten more caves yielded scrolls by 1956. The texts had been stored in jars in caves on the limestone cliffs above the community's compound, hidden as Roman forces advanced in the First Jewish Revolt. Publication was scandalously slow (1955–2010) due to a small original editorial team and lapses in oversight; full release of all texts came only with the digital facsimiles in the 1990s–2010s.

## Themes and motifs
- [[dualism-cosmic]] — the universe is divided into the dominion of light and darkness, ruled respectively by the Prince of Light (= Michael) and the Angel of Darkness / Belial. The most thoroughgoing dualism in Second Temple Judaism.
- [[two-spirits-doctrine]] — 1QS 3:13–4:26 ("The Treatise of the Two Spirits"): God created two spirits, of truth and of perversion; every human is allotted portions of each; the eschaton will purify the elect of the spirit of perversion. This is the doctrinal heart of the community.
- [[predestination]] — humans are determined to their portions: "From the God of knowledge comes all that is and shall be... He laid down for them their entire design... and all the visitations" (1QS 3:15–16). Compatible with vigorous moral exhortation.
- [[eschatological-war]] — 1QM's 40-year campaign against Kittim (= Greeks → Romans), the climactic angelic-human alliance.
- [[priestly-purity]] — Damascus Document, Temple Scroll, MMT (4QMMT) and many other texts: strict purity halakhah marking the community off from the Temple establishment.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — *shared-milieu* / *manuscript-transmission*: 11 Aramaic Enoch mss at Qumran; Enochic angelology and dualism feed into Qumran sectarian theology.
- → [[phase-3-009-jubilees]] — *shared-milieu* / *direct-influence*: ~15 Jubilees mss at Qumran; the 364-day solar calendar of Jubilees is the Qumran calendar; Damascus Document cites Jubilees by name.
- → [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — *shared-milieu*: 8 Daniel mss; Daniel's apocalyptic and Son-of-Man vocabulary resonates with Qumran's eschatology.
- → [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]] — *manuscript-transmission*: 1QIsaᵃ, the Great Isaiah Scroll, is the single most important pre-Masoretic Hebrew Bible witness.
- → Early Christianity — *shared-milieu*: John the Baptist's wilderness baptism, his use of Isa 40:3 (also Qumran's foundational text, 1QS 8:13–14), shared dualistic vocabulary in the Gospel of John ("sons of light" — cf. 1 Thess 5:5, John 12:36), and the structural overlap with apocalyptic sectarianism point to a shared milieu, not literary dependence. Argued by Charlesworth, Frey, Brooke.
- → Persian / Zoroastrian dualism — *shared-milieu* / *parallel-motif*: the Two Spirits doctrine has been compared since Kuhn (1952) to the Avestan twin spirits (Yasna 30); whether through Persian-period contact or independent development is debated.

## Disputes
- **Identification of the community.** Essene (majority view since Sukenik and Cross); Sadducean (Schiffman); Enochic-priestly (Boccaccini); or a unique *Yahad* not reducible to any classical category.
- **The Teacher of Righteousness's identity and date.** Multiple candidates proposed; no consensus beyond *priestly opposition figure*, mid-2nd c. BCE.
- **John the Baptist / Jesus and Qumran.** Direct contact (some popular and a few scholarly claims) is unsupported by evidence; *shared milieu* and *parallel sectarian vocabulary* is consensus.
- **Persian-Zoroastrian influence on the Two Spirits.** Kuhn (1952), Boyce, Frey: substantial; others (Barr): generic apocalyptic parallel, not dependence.
