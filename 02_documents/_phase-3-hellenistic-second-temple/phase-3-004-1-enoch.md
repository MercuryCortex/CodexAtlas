# 1 Enoch

## What it is
A composite of at least five originally separate Aramaic compositions, redacted and translated into Greek and finally into Ge'ez:
1. **Book of the Watchers** (chs. 1–36; ~-300 BCE) — expansion of Gen 6:1–4: two hundred angelic Watchers led by Semyaza and Azazel descend on Mount Hermon, take human wives, beget the Giants, teach forbidden arts (metallurgy, cosmetics, sorcery, astrology), and corrupt the earth. God dispatches the archangels (Michael, Gabriel, Uriel, Raphael) to bind them. Enoch is taken up and shown heaven and Sheol.
2. **Book of Parables / Similitudes** (chs. 37–71; ~-50/+50) — three "parables" featuring the "Son of Man / Chosen One" enthroned beside the Head of Days. Not found at Qumran.
3. **Astronomical Book / Book of the Luminaries** (chs. 72–82; ~-300) — solar 364-day calendar polemic; very old.
4. **Book of Dream Visions** (chs. 83–90; ~-165) — the "Animal Apocalypse" allegorizing Israelite history with animal characters; ends with the Maccabean revolt.
5. **Epistle of Enoch** (chs. 91–108; ~-150/-100) — the "Apocalypse of Weeks" dividing history into ten weeks of judgment.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Composition straddles the post-Alexander Hellenistic settlement (Ptolemies then Seleucids in Judea), Antiochus IV's persecution (-167), the Maccabean revolt, and the Hasmonean state. The Animal Apocalypse explicitly references this crisis.
- **Religious milieu.** Apocalyptic Judaism — a sectarian, often anti-establishment current that reframed Israel's troubles as cosmic conflict between God's elect and oppressive earthly + angelic powers. Enochic Judaism developed an alternative *priestly* theology centered on the antediluvian Enoch (rather than Sinai-Moses), with its own 364-day solar calendar opposed to the Jerusalem Temple's lunisolar calendar.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous scribal-priestly circles. Strong overlap with the Qumran community: 11 Aramaic copies were found there, more than copies of any other non-canonical work. Some scholars (Boccaccini) speak of an "Enochic Judaism" as a distinct stream.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The Ethiopian Orthodox Church preserves 1 Enoch as scripture (uniquely among Christian bodies) — they likely received it from Egyptian Christian missionaries (Frumentius, 4th c.). It was unknown in Europe until James Bruce returned from Ethiopia in 1773. The Aramaic original was recovered only from Qumran (Cave 4) in the 1950s.

## Themes and motifs
- [[watchers-and-fallen-angels]] — the elaborate fall-of-angels narrative; the source of all later Christian and Islamic demonology and the "fallen-angel-as-Satan" complex.
- [[origin-of-evil]] — evil enters the world through the Watchers' transgression, not (or not only) Adam's. This is a competing explanation to Genesis 3 and remains a Jewish alternative until rabbinic Judaism backgrounds it.
- [[apocalyptic-revelation]] — knowledge from heaven via visions, angelic mediators, and otherworld tours.
- [[heavenly-tour]] — Enoch journeys through the seven heavens, sees the storehouses of winds, judgment chambers, the tree of life.
- [[final-judgment]] — explicit eschatological judgment of humans *and* angels; resurrection language.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-009-jubilees]] — *direct-quote*: Jubilees 4:17–24 explicitly cites Enoch's heavenly writings and incorporates Watchers material.
- → [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — *shared-milieu* / *manuscript-transmission*: 4Q201–212 are the only Aramaic Enoch fragments extant; Qumran's community lived with Enoch as functional scripture.
- → [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — *parallel-motif*: Daniel 7's "one like a son of man" coming on the clouds is conceptually linked to 1 Enoch 46's "Son of Man" enthroned beside the Ancient of Days; both are second-century apocalyptic responses to imperial oppression.
- → [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] / [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — *parallel-motif* + (*direct-quote* in Jude): the NT "Son of Man" Christology has 1 Enoch 37–71 in its background. Jude 14–15 quotes 1 Enoch 1:9 by name.
- → Christian demonology — *direct-influence*: the names Azazel, Semyaza, and the fall-of-angels framework feed into Tertullian, Origen, Augustine, and ultimately *Paradise Lost*.
- → Manichaean Book of Giants (Phase 4) — *direct-influence*: Mani composed a *Book of Giants* directly modeled on the Aramaic Enochic *Book of Giants* fragments preserved at Qumran.

## Disputes
- **Date of Similitudes (chs. 37–71).** Absent from Qumran. Milik proposed a Christian origin (~+270); Nickelsburg, Collins, Stuckenbruck now place it ~-50 to +50, pre-Christian Jewish, on internal grounds.
- **Canonical status.** Quoted as scripture by Jude; cited by patristic writers (Justin, Athenagoras, Tertullian); rejected by Jerome and Augustine; survived only in Ethiopia. The Ethiopian Orthodox include it; no other Christian or Jewish body does.
- **"Enochic Judaism".** Boccaccini's model of Enochic Judaism as a distinct, anti-Zadokite stream is influential but not unanimous.
