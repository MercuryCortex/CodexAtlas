# Res Gestae Divi Augusti

The autobiographical inscription of [[caesar-augustus|Augustus]] (b. -63, d. +14), composed across his life and inscribed posthumously on bronze pillars at his Mausoleum in Rome (now lost) and on stone copies across the eastern provinces. The most complete surviving copy is incised on the walls of the **Temple of Augustus and Rome at Ancyra** (modern Ankara) — the **Monumentum Ancyranum** — with a parallel Greek translation. Fragmentary additional copies survive from Apollonia and Antioch (Pisidia). The text is a structured first-person catalog of Augustus's offices, military victories, building projects, religious restorations, public benefactions, and political settlements — the canonical self-portrait of the Augustan settlement and the founding document of Roman imperial-autobiographical genre.
