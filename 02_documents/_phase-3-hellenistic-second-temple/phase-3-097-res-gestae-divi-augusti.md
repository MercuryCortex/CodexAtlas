# Res Gestae Divi Augusti

## What it is
The **official first-person account of his own reign left by Augustus** — the *Res Gestae Divi Augusti*, "the Deeds of the Divine Augustus." Composed across his long life and inscribed after his death in 14 CE on bronze pillars at his Mausoleum in Rome (now lost) and on stone in the provinces, it is a structured catalogue of his offices and honours, his military victories, his vast building and religious-restoration programme, his public benefactions, and the constitutional settlement by which he claimed to have "restored the Republic" while in fact founding the monarchy. It is the canonical self-portrait of the Augustan order and the founding document of the imperial-autobiographical genre.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The consolidation of one-man rule behind republican forms; the *Res Gestae* is propaganda of the highest craft, recording everything Augustus wished posterity to remember and silently omitting the civil wars' victims.
- **Religious milieu.** Its very title — *Divi* Augusti, "of the *deified* Augustus" — announces the **imperial cult**: Augustus was enrolled among the gods after death, and the text documents his restoration of temples and priesthoods that made the *princeps* the hinge of Roman religion.
- **Who was behind it.** Augustus himself.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Recovered for scholarship from the Ankara temple walls (the *Monumentum Ancyranum*) and supplementary provincial copies.

## Themes and motifs
- [[apotheosis]] — the document's framing as the deeds of the now-*deified* Augustus is the charter of imperial deification.
- [[divine-kingship]] — the *Res Gestae* is the supreme Roman statement of sacralized sole rule masked as restored republic.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-024-suetonius-twelve-caesars]] — *counterpoint*: Augustus's polished self-account against Suetonius's later, less flattering biography of the same emperor.
- → the imperial cult and its architecture — *foundational*: the text is preserved *on a temple to Augustus himself*, fusing document and cult.

## Disputes
- **Truth and omission.** The *Res Gestae* is studied as much for what it suppresses (the proscriptions, the defeated) as for what it records; reading it requires constant control from other sources (Cooley 2009).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The god-emperor in his own words.** The *Res Gestae* is the cleanest primary document of the Roman **imperial cult** — a living ruler restoring the gods' temples and, at death, joining the gods himself — the western imperial form of the [[divine-kingship]] the Atlas traces from Egypt and Mesopotamia through to Rome (Cooley 2009; Goldsworthy 2014).
- **The original of the ruler's autobiography.** As the founding instance of the sovereign's official self-record, the *Res Gestae* stands behind every later monarchical and imperial autobiography in the Western tradition (Brunt & Moore 1967).
