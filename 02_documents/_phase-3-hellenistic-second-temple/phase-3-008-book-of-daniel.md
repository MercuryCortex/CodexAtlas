# Book of Daniel

## What it is
A twelve-chapter book in two halves:
- **Chapters 1–6** — court tales: Daniel and his three companions (Hananiah, Mishael, Azariah / Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego) navigate Babylonian and Persian royal courts. Famous scenes: the fiery furnace (3), Nebuchadnezzar's madness (4), Belshazzar's feast and the writing on the wall (5), Daniel in the lions' den (6). These tales likely circulated as a Jewish diaspora wisdom-cycle from ~-300 onward.
- **Chapters 7–12** — apocalyptic visions: the four beasts and the Son of Man (7); the ram and the goat (8); the seventy weeks (9); the angelic explanation of the Greek wars and the desolating sacrilege (10–12). These were composed during the Antiochene crisis (-167/-164), pseudonymously presented as Daniel's 6th-c. visions but transparently encoding events of the early 2nd century.

The book is bilingual: 2:4b through 7:28 is in Aramaic; the rest (1:1–2:4a; 8–12) in Hebrew. This bilingual sandwich is one of the most studied textual puzzles in the Hebrew Bible.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Antiochus IV Epiphanes, Seleucid king (-175/-164), attempted forced Hellenization of Jerusalem culminating in the **"desolating sacrilege"** of -167 (installation of an altar to Zeus Olympios in the Temple, sacrifice of swine on the altar; described in Dan 9:27, 11:31, 12:11; corroborated in 1 Maccabees and Josephus). The Maccabean revolt (Judah Maccabee, -167/-160) ejected the Seleucids; the Temple was rededicated -164 (Hanukkah).
- **Religious milieu.** Apocalyptic Judaism crystallizing. Daniel and 1 Enoch's Animal Apocalypse are contemporary responses to the same crisis. The book endorses *passive endurance* and divine deliverance over (apparently) the Maccabean military path: 11:34 disparages Maccabean help as "little help".
- **Who was behind it.** The *maskilim* ("wise") of Daniel 11:33–35; 12:3 — a circle that endured persecution and looked for vindication via resurrection and angelic deliverance. Possibly an antecedent to the Qumran community; possibly an apocalyptic-priestly stream parallel to it.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Jewish canonical transmission (in the *Ketuvim* / Writings, not among the Prophets — a clue to its late date); Greek translation in two competing forms (OG and Theodotion, with the Church preferring Theodotion); confirmed at Qumran from ~-125.

## Themes and motifs
- [[apocalyptic-revelation]] — Daniel is one of the two foundational apocalypses (with 1 Enoch's parts); revelation by angelic interpreter, pseudonymous antiquity, symbolic visions.
- [[four-kingdoms-schema]] — Dan 2 (statue of metals) and Dan 7 (four beasts) lay down the *four-empire* template (Babylon → Media → Persia → Greece; later Christian readings substituted Rome for Greece). This periodization governs medieval and early modern historiography.
- [[son-of-man]] — Dan 7:13–14: "one like a son of man" comes on the clouds of heaven, receives kingship from the Ancient of Days. **This is the Christological *locus classicus*** behind the Gospel "Son of Man" sayings.
- [[resurrection-of-the-dead]] — Dan 12:2: "many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth will awake, some to everlasting life, others to shame and everlasting contempt." This is the **first unambiguous resurrection text in the Hebrew Bible**.
- [[dualism-cosmic]] — Michael as Israel's angelic prince fighting the princes of Persia and Greece (Dan 10) introduces a cosmic dimension of conflict.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — *parallel-motif*: contemporary apocalypses with overlapping vocabulary (Ancient of Days, Son of Man, angelic mediators). Whether Daniel 7 *draws on* Enochic Similitudes or shares a tradition is debated.
- → [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — *shared-milieu* / *manuscript-transmission*: eight Daniel manuscripts at Qumran (one of the best-represented biblical books there); Daniel motifs pervade the War Scroll's eschatology.
- → [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — *direct-quote*: Mark 13:14 ("the desolating sacrilege") and Mark 13:26 (the Son of Man coming on the clouds) explicitly invoke Daniel 7 and 9:27 / 11:31.
- → New Testament book of Revelation — *direct-influence* / *commentary-on*: structurally modeled on Daniel; the beast imagery, the kingdoms, the resurrection-judgment all derive.
- → Zoroastrian apocalyptic milieu — *shared-milieu*: world-ages schemata, final battle of light and darkness, resurrection, are paralleled in Avestan and Pahlavi sources; the direction and degree of influence is contested (Hultgård, Boyce).

## Disputes
- **Date.** A near-consensus since Porphyry (3rd c. CE, *Against the Christians*) places the visions chs. 7–12 at -167/-164 because the "prophecies" track Seleucid history with extreme precision down to Antiochus IV and then become inaccurate (Antiochus's death is mis-described in 11:40–45). Traditional / conservative Christian readings retain a 6th-c. Daniel; mainstream critical scholarship since Driver (1900) is unanimous on the Maccabean dating.
- **Bilingual puzzle.** Why is the book half-Hebrew and half-Aramaic? Theories include (a) two separate sources joined; (b) deliberate code-switching (Aramaic for the "international" tales, Hebrew for the Israel-centric visions); (c) an Aramaic original partially Hebraized. No consensus.
- **Influence of Persian eschatology.** Hultgård, Collins argue substantial; others (Barr) more cautious.
