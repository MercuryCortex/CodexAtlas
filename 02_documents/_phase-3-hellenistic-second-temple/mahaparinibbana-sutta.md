# Mahāparinibbāna Sutta

## What it is
The **longest discourse in the Pali canon** (Dīgha Nikāya 16), narrating the **last months, death, and *parinibbāna* (final nirvāṇa) of the Buddha**. It follows him on his final journey northward through the Gangetic towns, records his last teachings and his refusal to appoint a personal successor ("let the Dhamma and the Vinaya be your teacher"), his last meal (Cunda the smith's offering) and his falling ill, his lying down between two sal trees at **Kusinārā**, his final exhortation — *"vayadhammā saṅkhārā, appamādena sampādetha"* ("all conditioned things are subject to decay; strive on with diligence")* — his passing, and the cremation and the **eightfold division of his bodily relics** among contending kingdoms. It is at once the Buddhist passion narrative and the charter of the relic cult.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Set against the rivalrous monarchies and republics of the 5th-century-BCE Gangetic plain; the relic-division episode reflects the political prestige attached to possessing the Buddha's remains.
- **Religious milieu.** A foundational text of Theravāda piety and of the institution of the *stupa*: the relics, divided into eight portions and enshrined, become the focus of a cult that Aśoka would later expand by redistributing them into thousands of stupas.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous reciters of the Dīgha tradition; fixed in writing in Sri Lanka c. 29 BCE.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Preserved in the Pali Tipiṭaka and in parallel versions in other early schools' canons (Sanskrit *Mahāparinirvāṇa-sūtra* of the Mūlasarvāstivāda, Chinese Āgama parallels), which Bareau (1971) compared source-critically.

## Themes and motifs
- [[anatman-no-self]] — the Buddha's composure in the face of his own dissolution embodies the teaching that all conditioned things, the body included, are impermanent and not-self.
- [[bodhisattva-vow]] — the long path that began in the [[jataka-tales|Jātaka]] births here reaches its term in the final nirvāṇa.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-030-asokan-edicts]] — *direct-influence*: Aśoka's programme of opening the original relic-stupas and redistributing the relics presupposes the relic-division of this sutta.
- → [[sanchi-great-stupa]] / [[borobudur-stupa-mandala]] — *foundational-text-of*: the stupa form and its relic cult derive from the events this discourse narrates.
- → [[phase-2-029-dhammapada]] — *shared-corpus*: a companion text of the Khuddaka/early Sutta tradition.

## Disputes
- **Strata and parallels.** Bareau and others reconstruct an older core overlaid with miracle-expansions; the relationship among the Pali, Sanskrit (Mūlasarvāstivāda), and Chinese versions is a major source-critical question.
- **Do not conflate with the Mahāyāna sūtra.** The Pali *Mahāparinibbāna Sutta* (this text) is distinct from the later **Mahāyāna *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra***, which uses the deathbed setting to teach the universal Buddha-nature.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The origin-text of the stupa — Buddhism's defining sacred form.** The eightfold relic-division narrated here is the charter of the entire stupa tradition, from the original reliquary mounds through Aśoka's thousands of stupas to the grand mandalic monuments of [[sanchi-great-stupa]] and [[borobudur-stupa-mandala]]; the architecture of Buddhist Asia begins in this discourse (Bareau 1971; von Hinüber 1996).
- **A shared deathbed, two Buddhisms.** That the same parinirvāṇa event generated *both* the sober Pali account *and* the later Mahāyāna *Mahāparinirvāṇa Sūtra*'s Buddha-nature theology is a clean illustration of how one narrative kernel is re-read across traditions — the kind of divergence the Atlas tracks as a wire, not a merger (Walshe 1995).
