# Book of Jubilees

## What it is
A rewriting of Genesis 1 – Exodus 14, framed as a revelation given to Moses by the **Angel of the Presence** on Mount Sinai during the 40-day stay (Exod 24). The angel dictates a history of the world divided into "jubilees" — 49-year cycles, each composed of seven sabbatical weeks. The book covers from Creation through the Exodus, providing:
- a chronological framework in jubilees, sabbaths-of-years, and years
- a rigorous **364-day solar calendar** (12 months of 30 days + 4 quarter-day intercalations; weeks fall on the same date every year)
- extensive halakhic expansion: Sabbath rules, festival dates, the law of nudity, prohibition of intermarriage with Gentiles
- the Watchers narrative (drawing from 1 Enoch)
- the figure of **Mastema**, the prince of evil spirits, who is permitted to retain a tenth of the demonic spirits after the Flood and is the source of subsequent temptation (4:30; 10:8–13; 17:16; 48:2)
- testimony that the patriarchs (Noah, Abraham, Levi) already knew and practiced the Torah

## Primary text — selected passages

### Jubilees 1:27–29 — The heavenly tablets: Torah as pre-existent eternal law
*Trans. VanderKam, J.C. (1989). The Book of Jubilees. CSCO 511. Peeters.*
> [The Angel of the Presence speaks to Moses:]
> "Write the complete history of the creation, how in six days the LORD God finished all his works and everything that he created. On the seventh day he rested and sanctified it for all ages... Write all of these words which I have commanded you. For Moses wrote all of these words..."
> The angel of the presence said to Moses: "We have written everything which we were commanded concerning everything that exists on the heavenly tablets..."

*The book's framing device — an angel dictating from "heavenly tablets" to Moses during the 40 days on Sinai — transforms the Torah from a Mosaic document into an eternal pre-existent cosmic scripture. What Moses receives is a copy of what was written in heaven before creation.*

### Jubilees 6:32–38 — The 364-day solar calendar polemic
*Trans. VanderKam 1989*
> There will be people who carefully observe the moon with lunar observations, for that one [the moon] corrupts the set times and comes out a year before its time each year... Those who depend on it are confused and will mistake the appointed time and the year. For it is the nature of the sun to make each year exactly 364 days. [...]
> Command the Israelites to keep the years in this number: 364 days constitute a complete year.

*Jubilees' fierce 364-day solar calendar polemic is a sectarian identity marker of the highest order — calendar determines when one celebrates Passover, Pentecost, and the Day of Atonement. Those on the wrong calendar are celebrating at the wrong time and thus are cut off from covenant blessing.*

### Jubilees 17:15–18:2 — Mastema's test of Abraham
*Trans. VanderKam 1989*
> During the seventh week, in the first year, in the first month — on the twelfth of this month — in this jubilee, there were words in heaven regarding Abraham, that he was faithful in everything which he told him and that he loved the Lord and was faithful in all tribulation. Prince Mastema came and said before God: "Abraham does indeed love his son Isaac and finds him more pleasing than everything. Tell him to offer him as a burnt offering on the altar. Then you will see whether he performs this command."

*Jubilees rewrites the Genesis 22 Akedah explicitly making it a temptation instigated by Mastema (the prince of evil) — parallel to Job 1 (the satan's challenge before God). This is the most developed pre-NT "devil instigating a divine test" narrative, and a bridge toward the NT Satan who tempts Jesus in Matthew 4.*

*Citations: VanderKam 1989, CSCO 511; VanderKam 2018, Hermeneia.*

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Post-Maccabean Judea (~-160/-140). The Hasmonean state has been established but its priestly-political hybridity (the Hasmoneans as both kings and high priests) was controversial, generating sectarian alternatives.
- **Religious milieu.** A sectarian Jewish stream emphasizing (a) the 364-day solar calendar against the Temple's lunisolar reckoning; (b) the eternity and pre-existence of the Torah ("written on the heavenly tablets"); (c) strict purity and separation from Gentiles. This stream substantially overlaps with the Qumran community.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous; almost certainly priestly-scribal circles overlapping with the early Essenes / Qumran community's predecessors. The number of Qumran manuscripts (~15 separate copies) is exceeded only by Psalms, Deuteronomy, Genesis, Isaiah, and Exodus — Jubilees was *functional scripture* at Qumran.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Lost in mainstream Judaism by Late Antiquity. Preserved canonically only in Ethiopia (along with 1 Enoch). Latin fragments rediscovered in 1861 (Ceriani); Greek lost; Hebrew fragments recovered from Qumran from 1947 on.

## Themes and motifs
- [[heavenly-tablets]] — a recurring motif: Torah, calendar, history of Israel, names of the righteous and wicked are all inscribed on pre-existing "heavenly tablets". Theologically: Torah is *eternal* and *cosmic*, not merely Mosaic.
- [[364-day-solar-calendar]] — Jub 6:32–38 ferociously polemicizes against any calendar of 354 days (lunar) or other variants. Calendar identity is salvation-identity.
- [[watchers-and-fallen-angels]] — Jub 4:15–22, 5:1–11, 7:21–25 retells the Watchers story largely from 1 Enoch's Book of the Watchers, but tightens it: the demons after the Flood are bound by Michael except for one-tenth, who serve Mastema.
- [[torah-as-pre-existent]] — the patriarchs from Noah on already keep Torah-like halakhah (circumcision, festivals, food laws); Sinai is a *renewal* of pre-existing eternal law.

- → [[phase-3-006-septuagint]] / Genesis — *commentary-on*: Jubilees is a rewriting/expansion of Genesis–early Exodus.
- → Ethiopian Christian canon — *manuscript-transmission*: Jubilees survives as scripture only in Ge'ez. Its theology of pre-existent Torah and Mastema-as-demon-chief influences Ethiopian Christian thought.
- → Christian demonology — *parallel-motif*: Mastema is conceptually parallel to / a precursor of the later Christian Satan (cf. Jub 17:16's tempting of Abraham, paralleling Job 1).

## Disputes
- **Date.** VanderKam argues for ~-160/-150 on internal evidence (no awareness of Antiochene crisis or its outcome in some readings; others see it). Some place it slightly later (~-130). Pre-Qumran-community in either case.
- **Relationship to Qumran community.** Is Jubilees a *product* of the proto-Qumran community, or a sister-text the community adopted? VanderKam: produced before the community split; Boccaccini: parallel Enochic-Mosaic synthesis.
- **Mastema and Satan.** Whether Mastema directly underlies the NT Satan or both descend from an earlier Jewish "satan-figure" tradition (Job, Zechariah 3) is debated.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges

- **The 364-day solar calendar (Jubilees 6) → Qumran calendar → the Jaubert Last Supper date hypothesis — a cross-tradition calendar dispute that may explain the Gospel chronological contradiction**: Jubilees' 364-day solar calendar is adopted by the Qumran community, which is why the Damascus Document identifies Jubilees' "Book of the Divisions of the Times." Annie Jaubert's 1957 hypothesis (*La date de la cène*, Gabalda) proposes that Jesus celebrated the Last Supper on Tuesday night (14 Nisan by the Jubilees/Qumran solar calendar) while the official Temple calendar placed Passover on Friday. This would explain the chronological contradiction between the Synoptic Gospels (Last Supper = Passover meal) and John (Jesus dies as the Passover lambs are slaughtered, before the Passover meal). The hypothesis remains contested but is one of the most creative cross-tradition resolutions of a longstanding NT puzzle: a 2nd-century BCE sectarian calendar dispute may hold the key to reconciling the Gospel narratives of Holy Week (Jaubert 1957, *La date de la cène*, Gabalda; VanderKam 1998, *Calendars in the Dead Sea Scrolls*, Routledge pp.112–128; Strobel 1958, *Untersuchungen zum eschatologischen Verzögerungsproblem*, Brill).

- **Mastema (Jubilees 17) as the most developed pre-NT "adversary" figure — the bridge from Job's ha-Satan to the NT's personal devil**: The figure of Mastema (from Hebrew *maśṭemāh*, "enmity/hostility") in Jubilees is the most developed pre-NT adversary-deity in any Jewish text. Where Job 1's *ha-satan* (the accuser) is a member of the divine council with a specific legal function, and Zechariah 3:1's satan is a prosecutor, Jubilees' Mastema is a *prince* with a retinue of demonic spirits who leads the post-Flood evil order, instigates divine tests of the righteous (Jubilees 17:16 — testing Abraham, parallel to Job 1), and commands the Egyptian plagues (Jub 48:2). Mastema's role as an independent prince of evil who operates within God's permission but against humanity is the direct conceptual ancestor of Matthew's "Satan" in the temptation narrative (Mt 4) and Paul's "god of this age" (2 Cor 4:4). Jubilees is the text where the satan-figure becomes a proper noun (*Mastema*) and an independent cosmic antagonist (VanderKam 2018, Hermeneia vol. 1 pp.493–507; Russell 1977, *The Devil: Perceptions of Evil*, Cornell UP pp.187–196; Forsyth 1987, *The Old Enemy: Satan and the Combat Myth*, Princeton UP pp.183–190).

- **Jubilees' survival only in Ge'ez (Ethiopian Orthodox canon) — the most dramatic example of canonical divergence between Jewish and Christian traditions, and Ethiopia's unique textual custodianship**: Jubilees is canonical scripture in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church — one of the very few texts that is in no other Christian canon and in no Jewish canon (except as a manuscript find). Its preservation only in Ge'ez, while the Hebrew original and Greek translation were lost, makes Ethiopia the sole custodian of this text for 1,400+ years (from Late Antiquity to the 1947 Qumran discoveries). This custodianship is not accidental: the Ethiopian Orthodox canon (81 books, the largest Christian canon in the world) also preserved 1 Enoch in Ge'ez, making Ethiopia uniquely important for the history of both Second Temple Judaism and early Christianity. The discovery of the Hebrew Jubilees fragments at Qumran (4Q216 etc.) confirmed what only the Ethiopian canon had preserved — validating the Ge'ez tradition against a scholarly establishment that had treated Jubilees as marginal (VanderKam 1989, CSCO 511; Knibb 1999, "The Ethiopic Book of Enoch," in *The Ethiopic Book of Enoch*, Oxford UP; Cowley 1988, *Ethiopian Biblical Interpretation*, Cambridge UP).

- **Jubilees' "rewritten Bible" technique — the template for Jewish and Christian narrative midrash, generating centuries of scriptural expansion**: Jubilees is the oldest surviving complete example of a literary genre that scholars now call "rewritten Bible" (Vermes) or "retold scripture" (Alexander): a text that retells and expands an existing canonical narrative to clarify theological ambiguities, fill narrative gaps, and enforce the author's halakhic positions. The technique: follow the canonical text closely enough that the reader knows the story, but insert explanatory angels, expanded speeches, additional characters, and halakhic rulings at every gap. Jubilees rewrites Genesis–Exodus; the Genesis Apocryphon (Qumran) rewrites Genesis; the NT Gospels rewrite the Jesus story in each other's terms; the Quran retells biblical narratives with theological correction. The genre Jubilees exemplifies is one of the most productive in the history of religion — every tradition produces its own rewritten sacred text (Vermes 1961, *Scripture and Tradition in Judaism*, Brill pp.67–126; Segal 2007, *The Book of Jubilees*, Mohr Siebeck; Bernstein 2005, *Rewritten Bible after Fifty Years*, Brill).
