# Septuagint (LXX)

## What it is
The Greek translation of the Hebrew Scriptures produced in stages in Alexandria from the mid-3rd century BCE through the 1st century BCE. The **Pentateuch** (Genesis–Deuteronomy) was translated first (~-280 under Ptolemy II), followed by Prophets, Psalms, and Writings over the next two centuries. The legendary account in the *Letter of Aristeas* describes seventy-two Jewish elders sent from Jerusalem who in seventy-two days produced miraculously identical translations on the island of Pharos — the source of the name "Septuagint" (Greek for "seventy"). The actual translation history was a multi-generation Alexandrian Jewish project. The corpus also includes books composed *in* Greek (Wisdom of Solomon, 2–4 Maccabees) or expanded in Greek (Daniel, Esther, Jeremiah) — these are absent from the later rabbinic Hebrew canon.

## Primary text — selected passages (translation divergences)

### Genesis 1:1–3 (LXX) — The creation formula in Greek
*LXX: Septuaginta (Rahlfs ed. 1935, revised Hanhart 2006); trans. NETS (Pietersma & Wright 2007), Oxford UP.*
> In the beginning God (*Elohim* → *ho Theos*) made the heaven and the earth.
> But the earth was invisible and unformed [*aoratos kai akataskeuastos*],
> and darkness was over the abyss [*abyssos*],
> and God's spirit [*pneuma theou*] was being carried along over the water.
> And God said [*kai eipen ho Theos*], "Let there be light," and there was light.

*The LXX's *aoratos kai akataskeuastos* ("invisible and unformed") renders the Hebrew *tohu wa-vohu* in a more Platonic register — "invisible" (*aoratos*) resonates with the Platonic world of invisible Forms. The *abyssos* translation becomes the NT and early Christian theological term. The *pneuma theou* ("spirit of God") is the seed of Christian pneumatology.*

### Isaiah 7:14 — The pivotal translation: *almah* → *parthenos*
*Hebrew MT: "The young woman [*almah*] is pregnant and shall bear a son." LXX: "Behold, the virgin [*parthenos*] shall conceive in the womb and shall bear a son..." NETS (Pietersma & Wright 2007).*
> MT: "Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign: Look, the young woman [*ha-ʿalmāh*] is with child and shall bear a son, and shall name him Immanuel."
> LXX: "Therefore the Lord himself will give you a sign: Look, the young woman [*hē parthenos*] will conceive in the womb and bear a son, and you shall call his name Immanuel."

*The Hebrew *almah* ("young woman", not specifically "virgin" — the word for "virgin" is *betulah*) becomes the Greek *parthenos* ("virgin") in the LXX — a translation choice that Matthew 1:23 reads as a prophecy of the virginal conception of Jesus. This single translation word generates the entire virgin-birth theological tradition.*

### Exodus 3:14 — The divine name as ontological formula
*Hebrew MT: "I am what I am" (*Ehyeh asher ehyeh*). LXX: "I am the One who is" (*Egō eimi ho ōn*). NETS.*
> God said to Moses: "*Egō eimi ho ōn*" [I am the Being / I am he who is].
> And he said: "Thus you shall say to the sons of Israel: 'The Being (*ho ōn*) has sent me to you.'"

*The LXX translators converted the Hebrew self-referential formula ("I am what I am" — ehyeh asher ehyeh, grammatically ambiguous / possibly "I will be what I will be") into a Greek ontological statement: God is *ho ōn*, "the Being", "the One who is." This reading drives Philo's identification of YHWH with Plato's self-subsisting Being, Aquinas's *ipsum esse subsistens*, and the entire tradition of Western philosophical theology's engagement with divine being.*

*Citations: NETS (Pietersma & Wright 2007), Oxford UP; Rahlfs-Hanhart LXX (2006), Deutsche Bibelgesellschaft.*

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Ptolemaic Egypt: a Greek-speaking ruling class governs a multilingual realm. Alexandria's Jewish quarter was substantial; many of its members no longer read Hebrew well, creating both liturgical and educational pressure for a Greek scripture.
- **Religious milieu.** Hellenistic Judaism — a Judaism navigating Greek philosophical and literary culture, producing distinctive forms (Wisdom literature, Philonic allegory, intertestamental apocalyptic) that the Hebrew-Aramaic Judaism of the homeland did not generate. The LXX is the *medium* in which Hellenistic Judaism formulates itself.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous Alexandrian Jewish scholars across multiple generations. The Aristeas-legend's seventy-two translators is theologized retrojection (the number echoes the seventy elders of Numbers 11), but the Pentateuch-translation core is plausibly ~-280 under Ptolemy II.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Adopted as scripture by every Greek-speaking Christian community in the 1st century. The NT writers (Paul, Mark, Luke, Hebrews) quote the LXX in their scripture citations — about 90% of NT OT quotations align with LXX wording. Rabbinic Judaism (post-70 CE) increasingly distanced itself from the LXX (with three new Jewish Greek translations by Aquila, Symmachus, Theodotion ~+130/+200, more literal to Hebrew). Origen's *Hexapla* (~+240) collated six versions in parallel columns. The great Christian codices preserve LXX as the OT of the Eastern Church.

## Themes and motifs
- [[translation-as-theology]] — translation choices encode theological positions. Famous examples: Isa 7:14 *almah* "young woman" → LXX *parthenos* "virgin" (drives Matthew 1:23); Exod 3:14 "I am who I am" → LXX *Egō eimi ho ōn* "I am the Being" (drives Philonic and patristic ontology); Genesis 1:2's "wind" *rûaḥ* → LXX *pneuma* (drives spirit-Christology).
- [[diaspora-judaism]] — the LXX is the textual substrate of the worldwide diaspora's religious life.
- [[messianism]] — LXX's rendering of certain prophetic and psalm passages strengthens messianic readability (Ps 22, Isa 53, Daniel 7).
- [[creation-by-word]] — LXX Gen 1's *kai eipen ho Theos* / *kai egeneto* ("and God said / and it came to be") is the textual seedbed for the Johannine Logos and patristic creation-theology.

## Disputes
- **Date and scope of the original Pentateuch translation.** The *Letter of Aristeas* says seventy-two translators in seventy-two days under Ptolemy II; modern scholarship accepts the date-zone (~-280) but treats Aristeas as a Hellenistic Jewish *propaganda piece* defending the LXX's scriptural status.
- **The Hebrew Vorlage.** The Qumran finds (4QSamᵃ, 4QJerᵇ, etc.) showed the LXX often translates a *different* Hebrew text than the Masoretic — meaning the LXX is sometimes an independent witness to an earlier Hebrew form, not merely a translation choice.
- **Status in rabbinic Judaism.** Once the founding translation of Hellenistic Jewish life, increasingly disowned after the Christian appropriation, leading to the Aquila/Symmachus/Theodotion re-translations.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges

- **The *parthenos* translation — most consequential single-word translation decision in Western religious history**: The LXX translator's choice to render Hebrew *almah* ("young woman") as Greek *parthenos* ("virgin") in Isa 7:14 generated the entire virgin-birth theological tradition when Matthew 1:23 read it as Messianic prophecy. A single translation decision created a doctrine that separated Christianity from Judaism and shaped 2000 years of Mariology (Stendahl, K. 1954. *The School of St. Matthew*. Gleerup. pp.98–104; Brown, R.E. 1977. *The Birth of the Messiah*. Doubleday. pp.143–153; Gundry, R.H. 1967. *The Use of the Old Testament in St. Matthew's Gospel*. Brill. pp.226–228).

- **LXX as the New Testament's Bible — the most consequential fact of canonical formation**: All NT authors quote the LXX, not the Hebrew — approximately 90% of OT quotations in the NT align with LXX wording rather than the Masoretic Text. The LXX is not a translation of the Bible that produced Christianity; it *is* the Bible that produced Christianity (Law, T.M. 2013. *When God Spoke Greek*. Oxford UP. pp.56–79; Hengel, M. 2002. *The Septuagint as Christian Scripture*. T&T Clark. pp.19–39; Jobes, K.H. & Silva, M. 2015. pp.183–210).

- **Exod 3:14 LXX ontologization — the birth of Western philosophical theology**: The LXX's rendering of *'ehyeh asher 'ehyeh* as *Egō eimi ho ōn* ("I am the Being") converted a Hebrew verbal self-identification into a Greek ontological claim: God = Being itself. This drives Philo's *De Vita Mosis* identification of YHWH with Plato's self-subsisting Good, Aquinas's *ipsum esse subsistens* in the *Summa Theologiae* I.3.4, and the entire tradition of *theologia naturalis* (Gilson, É. 1952. *Being and Some Philosophers*. PIMS. pp.40–53; Hadot, P. 1968. *Porphyre et Victorinus*. Études Augustiniennes. pp.221–234; Tobin, T.H. 1983. *The Creation of Man: Philo and the History of Interpretation*. CBQ Monograph 14. pp.14–19).

- **Qumran LXX fragments as independent Hebrew witnesses — most dramatic pre-critical discovery in text history**: The Dead Sea Scrolls' Greek LXX manuscripts (4QLXXLev^a, 7Q1, Papyrus Fouad 266) plus Hebrew manuscripts matching the LXX's *Vorlage* (4QJer^b shorter Jeremiah, 4QSam^a) proved that the LXX sometimes preserves an *earlier* Hebrew text than the Masoretic tradition — meaning the LXX is an independent textual witness to Scripture's own compositional history, not merely a translation (Tov, E. 2012. *Textual Criticism of the Hebrew Bible*, 3rd ed. Fortress. pp.127–143; Cross, F.M. 1998. *From Epic to Canon*. Johns Hopkins UP. pp.205–212; Ulrich, E. 1999. *The Dead Sea Scrolls and the Origins of the Bible*. Eerdmans. pp.109–121).
