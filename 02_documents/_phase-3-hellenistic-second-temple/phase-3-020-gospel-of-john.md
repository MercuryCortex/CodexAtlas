# Gospel of John

## What it is
The fourth canonical Gospel, structurally and theologically distinct from the Synoptics (Mark, Matthew, Luke). Twenty-one chapters: a soaring **Logos prologue** (1:1–18); a "Book of Signs" (chs. 2–12) organized around seven major miracles (water-to-wine, healing the official's son, paralytic, multiplication, walking on water, blind man, Lazarus) each interpreted by long discourses; a "Book of Glory" (chs. 13–20) covering the Last Supper, the Farewell Discourse (14–17), the Passion and Resurrection; and an epilogue (ch. 21, almost universally judged a later appendix). Distinctive features versus the Synoptics:
- Jesus speaks in extended philosophical discourses rather than parables and one-liners
- Seven "I AM" sayings with predicates (bread, light, gate, shepherd, resurrection, way, vine) plus absolute "I AM" (egō eimi) sayings (8:24, 8:58, 13:19)
- A multi-year ministry (multiple Passovers) versus the Synoptics' one-year frame
- Jerusalem-focused ministry; no birth narrative; no transfiguration; no exorcisms; no parables in the Synoptic sense
- Realized eschatology: eternal life is *now* (3:36; 5:24; 11:25–26)
- High Christology from the prologue onward

## Primary text — selected excerpts

Excerpts use the King James Version (1611), public domain. The KJV's English here is unusually close to the underlying Greek in its rhythm. For scholarly use consult the NRSV or ESV (translations of NA28/UBS5), and the Greek text directly at SBLGNT or Bible Gateway.

### John 1:1–14 — the Logos prologue

The hinge of the Hellenistic-Jewish Logos tradition, the Stoic-Philonic cosmic-Reason discourse, and a Christology that became normative for all later Trinitarian thought. The opening *en archē* (in the beginning) deliberately echoes Genesis 1:1 LXX (also *en archē*) — the Logos is identified with the agent of cosmic creation, then with the light shining in the darkness, then with the incarnate one.

> In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The same was in the beginning with God. All things were made by him; and without him was not any thing made that was made. In him was life; and the life was the light of men. And the light shineth in darkness; and the darkness comprehended it not.
>
> There was a man sent from God, whose name was John. The same came for a witness, to bear witness of the Light, that all men through him might believe. He was not that Light, but was sent to bear witness of that Light. That was the true Light, which lighteth every man that cometh into the world.
>
> He was in the world, and the world was made by him, and the world knew him not. He came unto his own, and his own received him not. But as many as received him, to them gave he power to become the sons of God, even to them that believe on his name: which were born, not of blood, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God.
>
> And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, (and we beheld his glory, the glory as of the only begotten of the Father,) full of grace and truth.

Citation: John 1:1–5, 6–14, KJV.

### John 3:1–8 — Nicodemus and being born from above

The *gennēthē anōthen* (born from above / again) discourse — the source-text of Christian theologies of regeneration and (in another register) of Gnostic Christianity's distinction between psychic and pneumatic generation.

> There was a man of the Pharisees, named Nicodemus, a ruler of the Jews: the same came to Jesus by night, and said unto him, Rabbi, we know that thou art a teacher come from God: for no man can do these miracles that thou doest, except God be with him.
>
> Jesus answered and said unto him, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born again, he cannot see the kingdom of God. Nicodemus saith unto him, How can a man be born when he is old? can he enter the second time into his mother's womb, and be born?
>
> Jesus answered, Verily, verily, I say unto thee, Except a man be born of water and of the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God. That which is born of the flesh is flesh; and that which is born of the Spirit is spirit. Marvel not that I said unto thee, Ye must be born again. The wind bloweth where it listeth, and thou hearest the sound thereof, but canst not tell whence it cometh, and whither it goeth: so is every one that is born of the Spirit.

Citation: John 3:1–8, KJV.

### John 8:56–59 — the absolute "I AM"

The most charged of the Johannine *egō eimi* sayings: without predicate, deliberately echoing Exodus 3:14's *egō eimi ho ōn* (LXX). The crowd recognizes the claim as blasphemy and takes up stones.

> Your father Abraham rejoiced to see my day: and he saw it, and was glad. Then said the Jews unto him, Thou art not yet fifty years old, and hast thou seen Abraham? Jesus said unto them, Verily, verily, I say unto you, Before Abraham was, I am. Then took they up stones to cast at him: but Jesus hid himself, and went out of the temple, going through the midst of them, and so passed by.

Citation: John 8:56–59, KJV.

### John 14:1–6, 15–17 — the Farewell Discourse and the Paraclete

The Farewell Discourse contains the seed-passages for later Trinitarian and Pneumatological theology: the Father's house with many mansions, the way/truth/life self-identification, and the promise of another *paraklētos* (advocate, comforter, helper) who will come after Jesus's departure.

> Let not your heart be troubled: ye believe in God, believe also in me. In my Father's house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you. I go to prepare a place for you. And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also. And whither I go ye know, and the way ye know.
>
> Thomas saith unto him, Lord, we know not whither thou goest; and how can we know the way? Jesus saith unto him, I am the way, the truth, and the life: no man cometh unto the Father, but by me. ...
>
> If ye love me, keep my commandments. And I will pray the Father, and he shall give you another Comforter, that he may abide with you for ever; even the Spirit of truth; whom the world cannot receive, because it seeth him not, neither knoweth him: but ye know him; for he dwelleth with you, and shall be in you.

Citation: John 14:1–6, 15–17, KJV.

### John 17:1–5 — Jesus's high-priestly prayer

The opening of John 17, the most theologically dense of Jesus's prayers in the canon: pre-existence ("the glory which I had with thee before the world was"), eternal life as a present knowing of God, the consummation of the Son's work.

> These words spake Jesus, and lifted up his eyes to heaven, and said, Father, the hour is come; glorify thy Son, that thy Son also may glorify thee: as thou hast given him power over all flesh, that he should give eternal life to as many as thou hast given him. And this is life eternal, that they might know thee the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom thou hast sent. I have glorified thee on the earth: I have finished the work which thou gavest me to do. And now, O Father, glorify thou me with thine own self with the glory which I had with thee before the world was.

Citation: John 17:1–5, KJV.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Composed in the late 1st century, in the wake of the Temple's destruction (+70) and the increasing separation of "the Jews" (a Johannine term used in tension-laden ways throughout) from the early Christian movement. The references to expulsion from the synagogue (*aposynagōgos*, 9:22; 12:42; 16:2) point to a community recently experiencing or anticipating such expulsion, perhaps related to the *birkat ha-minim* (blessing/cursing of "heretics") that emerged at Yavneh in the late first century.
- **Religious milieu.** A community shaped by Hellenistic-Jewish Logos/Sophia theology, Qumran-like cosmic dualism (light/darkness, truth/falsehood), and a developing high Christology. The community is internally Christian and externally in tension with formative Judaism. Some scholars (Käsemann, Bultmann, Pagels) detect proto-Gnostic affinities; others (Hengel, Brown) emphasize Johannine Christianity as a distinct stream within mainstream Christianity that *attracted* Gnostic readings rather than being itself Gnostic.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous. Irenaeus (~+180) is the first to attribute the Gospel to **John the apostle, son of Zebedee**, residing at Ephesus into old age. The Gospel itself refers to a "Beloved Disciple" (13:23; 19:26; 20:2; 21:7, 20–24) who is its tradition-bearer; ch. 21 explicitly says "this is the disciple who is bearing witness to these things and who has written these things" (21:24). Modern scholarship typically posits a *Johannine school* — a Beloved-Disciple-founded community in which the tradition was preserved, expanded, and finally redacted.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuously canonical. The early date of P52 (~+125) confirms wide circulation within a generation of composition. Heracleon (~+170, a Valentinian Gnostic) wrote what is the *earliest extant biblical commentary* — and it is on John, indicating Gnostic appropriation. Orthodox response (Irenaeus, *Adv. Haer.* 3.11) defended Johannine authorship partly to reclaim the Gospel from Gnostic exegetes.

## Themes and motifs
- [[logos-cosmic-reason]] — **John 1:1**: *En archē ēn ho logos, kai ho logos ēn pros ton theon, kai theos ēn ho logos.* "In the beginning was the Logos, and the Logos was with God, and the Logos was God." The Logos as *agent of creation* (1:3), as *life and light* (1:4), as *incarnated* in Jesus (1:14). **This is the final step of the three-step Stoic → Philo → John transmission of cosmic Logos.** All subsequent Christian Christology and Trinitarian theology presuppose this prologue.
- [[i-am-sayings]] — "Egō eimi" with predicates (6:35 bread; 8:12 light; 10:7 gate; 10:11 shepherd; 11:25 resurrection; 14:6 way/truth/life; 15:1 vine) and the absolute, theologically charged "Egō eimi" (8:58 "before Abraham was, *I AM*"), echoing LXX Exod 3:14's *Egō eimi ho ōn*.
- [[realized-eschatology]] — eternal life is a present possession (5:24); judgment is happening *now* in the encounter with Christ; the Spirit/Paraclete is the *continued presence* of Christ post-resurrection.
- [[light-and-darkness-dualism]] — 1:5; 3:19–21; 8:12; 12:35–46; structurally analogous to Qumran's two-spirits doctrine, though without the deterministic predestination. **Charlesworth, Brown, Frey: Qumran-Johannine parallels are striking and may reflect shared sectarian-apocalyptic milieu.**
- [[paraclete-spirit]] — the *paraklētos* (advocate/comforter, John 14–16) as the post-resurrection mode of Christ's presence; uniquely Johannine; later central to Pneumatology and (via reinterpretation) to Montanism, Manichaeism, and Islam's identification of Muhammad as the *Paraclete*.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]] — *parallel-motif* / *direct-influence* (debated mechanism): the Johannine Logos has its closest conceptual neighbor in Philo's Logos. Whether John knew Philo's writings or both drew on Hellenistic Jewish synagogue Logos-Sophia discourse is contested. Hengel, Borgen: substantial influence; Boyarin: shared Jewish theological vocabulary; Dodd: indirect.
- → [[phase-3-005-stoic-foundational-texts]] — *direct-influence* (mediated via Philo): the cosmic Logos is Stoic in origin.
- → [[phase-3-012-wisdom-of-solomon]] — *parallel-motif*: pre-existent Sophia (Wisdom 7) is the conceptual neighbor of the pre-existent Logos.
- → [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — *parallel-motif*: Johannine and Markan passion narratives share core structure; whether John used Mark or developed parallel tradition is debated.
- → [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — *shared-milieu*: cosmic dualism of light/darkness, "sons of light", truth/falsehood; not literary dependence but cultural-linguistic neighborhood.
- → [[phase-3-021-hermetic-corpus-earliest]] — *parallel-motif*: both deploy Logos / Nous / Light cosmologies in early Roman Imperial period; Hermetica's *Poimandres* is the closest pagan-philosophical analogue to John's prologue.
- → Gnostic Christianity (Phase 4) — *direct-influence*: Heracleon's commentary; Valentinian appropriation; later Mandaean traditions of John the Baptist.
- → Nicene Trinitarian theology — *direct-influence*: John 1 is *the* prooftext of Nicene Christology.

## Disputes
- **Authorship.** John son of Zebedee (Irenaeus, conservative tradition); a separate "John the Elder" (Eusebius noted this possibility from Papias); an anonymous Beloved Disciple distinct from the apostle (modern critical mainstream).
- **Date.** ~+90/+110 is mainstream; conservatives sometimes push earlier (~+70, before Temple destruction is no longer clearly in view); Bultmann pushed later.
- **Knowledge of Synoptics.** Independent tradition (Dodd, Brown) vs. knowing-and-correcting the Synoptics (Barrett, Smith, Schnelle).
- **Proto-Gnostic affinities.** Käsemann, Bultmann saw John as "naively docetic" or proto-Gnostic; the mainstream now sees John as Hellenistic-Jewish, with Gnostics later *appropriating* John rather than John being Gnostic.
- **Composition stages.** Multiple redactions detectable (the awkward 14:31 "rise, let us go from here" followed by three more chapters of discourse; ch. 21 obviously appended; "signs source" hypothesis).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The three-step Logos transmission chain (Stoic → Philo → John) — the vault's best-documented Christology source**: Stoic cosmology posited a *logos* (rational principle) permeating the cosmos (~300 BCE, Zeno; [[phase-3-005-stoic-foundational-texts]]). Philo of Alexandria (~20 BCE–50 CE) applied this to the Hebrew Bible: God's *logos* is his "eldest son," the cosmic architect, a "second God" who is both God's instrument of creation and the mediating principle between the absolutely transcendent Father and the created world ([[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]]). John's prologue (~90–110 CE) takes the final step: this Logos was with God from the beginning, *was* God, and *became flesh* in Jesus (1:14). The doctrinal chain is traceable in three documented steps across 400 years. Without the Stoic logos → Philo → John transmission, Nicene Christology and all Trinitarian theology cannot be understood. This is the vault's tightest Christological-origin evidence.
- **Heracleon's commentary — the Gnostics got there first (the Fourth Gospel was the Gnostic's Bible before it was the Orthodox's Bible)**: The earliest surviving biblical commentary on any book of the New Testament is Heracleon's commentary on John (~170 CE) — written by a Valentinian Gnostic. The Gnostics appropriated John so aggressively that Irenaeus, writing *Against Heresies* (~180 CE), felt compelled to defend Johannine authorship *specifically to reclaim the Gospel from the Gnostics*. The Gospel of Thomas, Apocryphon of John, and the Valentinian tradition all draw on Johannine vocabulary. What became the "most spiritual Gospel" of Catholic Christianity was the Gnostic's founding text first. The vault holds both sides of this contest simultaneously: [[phase-4-001-gospel-of-thomas]], [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]], and this Gospel.
- **The Paraclete → Muhammad (Islamic identification of the Johannine Comforter)**: The Farewell Discourse promises "another Paraclete" (*paraklētos*, 14:16) who will come after Jesus, teach all things, and guide into all truth (16:13). Islamic tradition — following a qur'anic verse (61:6) in which Jesus announces "a messenger after me whose name will be Ahmad" — identified the *Paraclete* as Muhammad, arguing that the Greek text preserves a corruption of *periklutos* ("the praised one") for *paraklētos* ("the comforter") — and that *periklutos* translates the Arabic *aḥmad* / *muḥammad* (the praised). The identification is central to Islamic Christology and is the Fourth Gospel's longest single-text reach into an entirely separate religious tradition.
- **"Before Abraham was, I AM" — the divine name as Christological claim (Exodus 3:14 → John 8:58)**: The LXX renders Exodus 3:14 (*YHWH says to Moses: "I AM WHO I AM"*) as *egō eimi ho ōn* — "I am the one who is." John 8:58 — "Before Abraham was, *egō eimi*" (without predicate, absolute) — maps Jesus onto this divine self-declaration with precision precise enough that the crowd immediately reaches for stones (8:59: *elphamōsen* = "they picked up stones to throw at him"). The blasphemy charge is immediate because the *egō eimi* formula is recognized. This is the moment John's "high Christology" is most compressed. The Exodus → John theological trajectory is the foundational move of all Christian identity-of-Jesus-with-YHWH theology.
