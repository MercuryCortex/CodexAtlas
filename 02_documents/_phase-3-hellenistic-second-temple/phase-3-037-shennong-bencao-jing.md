# Shennong Bencao Jing

## Identity

The **Shennong Bencao Jing** (神農本草經, "Divine Farmer's Classic of Herbal Medicine") is the first comprehensive Chinese pharmacopeia and one of the oldest systematic pharmacopeias in the world, composed c. 100–200 CE during the Eastern Han dynasty. Attributed by tradition to the mythological Divine Farmer ([[shennong]]) who tasted hundreds of plants personally to classify them, the text establishes the canonical three-tier classification of 365 medicinal substances that organized all subsequent Chinese pharmacopeia development.

## Three-Tier Classification: The World's First Pharmacological Safety Framework

The Shennong Bencao Jing organized 365 substances (matching the days of the year) into three tiers based on toxicity and therapeutic scope — an organizational logic that anticipates modern pharmacological risk classification:

| Tier | Count | Principles | Examples |
|------|-------|-----------|---------|
| Upper (superior, 上品) | 120 | Non-toxic; suitable for long-term use; promotes longevity and vitality | Ginseng, astragalus, liquorice, wolfberry, reishi mushroom |
| Middle (standard, 中品) | 120 | Low or moderate toxicity; therapeutic for specific conditions; short-to-medium use | Ephedra (ma huang), coptis, peony |
| Lower (inferior, 下品) | 125 | High toxicity; powerful symptom-specific action; short-term only | Aconite (fu zi), rhubarb (da huang), realgar |

This tiering is rational pharmacology: it distinguishes tonic (adaptogenic, long-safe) from therapeutic (condition-specific, medium duration) from potent-toxic (powerful, use-minimally) — the same three categories that underlie modern pharmaceutical scheduling.

## MASSIVE WIN: Six Independent Pharmacopeia Traditions

The world's earliest systematic pharmacopeias:
1. **Sumerian clay tablets** (c. 2500 BCE, Nippur) — 15 prescriptions; earliest pharmaceutical record
2. **Ebers Papyrus** (c. 1550 BCE, Egypt) — 877 prescriptions; largest ancient Egyptian medical text
3. **Atharva Veda** pharmacological hymns (c. 1200 BCE, India) — plant-medicine embedded in religious text
4. **Hippocratic Corpus** (c. 400 BCE, Greece) — ~400 substances catalogued
5. **Charaka Samhita** (c. 100 BCE–100 CE, India) — systematic Ayurvedic pharmacopeia
6. **Shennong Bencao Jing** (c. 200 CE, China) — 365 substances, three-tier classification

Six independent civilizations, zero contact for most of the sequence, converging on the same epistemic project: systematically cataloguing which plants/minerals/animals cure what.
