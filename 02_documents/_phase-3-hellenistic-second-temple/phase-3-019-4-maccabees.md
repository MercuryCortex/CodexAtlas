# 4 Maccabees

## What it is
A philosophical-rhetorical treatise framed as a synagogue homily, arguing that "pious reason is sovereign over the passions" (4 Macc 1:1; 1:7). The author illustrates the thesis with the **Maccabean martyrs** — the priest Eleazar (chs. 5–7), the seven brothers (chs. 8–12), and their mother (chs. 14–17) — who were tortured to death by Antiochus IV Epiphanes for refusing to eat pork. Their endurance proves that *logismos* (reasoned piety) can master the passions of pleasure, pain, and grief. The work fuses **Stoic and Platonic ethics** (passions, virtues, soul-immortality) with **Jewish covenantal piety**, treating Torah-observance as the perfection of philosophy.

Critically, 4 Maccabees develops the martyrs' deaths as **vicarious atonement** for Israel:
- 4 Macc 6:29 — Eleazar's prayer: "Be merciful to your people, and let our punishment suffice for them. Make my blood their purification, and take my life as a substitute (*antipsychon*) for theirs."
- 4 Macc 17:21–22 — "through the blood of those pious ones and their death as an atoning sacrifice (*hilastērion*), divine providence preserved Israel."

This is the most explicit pre-Christian Jewish formulation of substitutionary atonement and is a load-bearing text for understanding NT atonement language.

## Primary text — selected passages

### 4 Maccabees 1:1–7 — The philosophical thesis: pious reason is sovereign over the passions
*Trans. Anderson, Hugh (1985). 4 Maccabees in Charlesworth (ed.), Old Testament Pseudepigrapha vol. 2. Doubleday.*
> The subject that I am about to discuss is most philosophical, that is, whether devout reason is sovereign over the emotions. So it is right for me to advise you to pay earnest attention to philosophy... Is it not the fact that reason rules over those emotions that hinder one from justice, such as malice; and also over those emotions that hinder one from manliness, such as anger; and over those emotions that hinder one from temperance, such as love of pleasure; and over those that hinder one from prudence, such as arrogance?

*4 Maccabees' opening is a formal philosophical thesis statement — the text announces itself as a philosophical treatise and deploys Stoic vocabulary (*pathē*, cardinal virtues, *logismos*) in service of a Jewish-theological claim: that Torah-observant piety, not Stoic reason divorced from revelation, is the highest form of philosophical virtue. The appropriation of Stoic argumentation for Jewish purposes is the defining gesture of the entire work.*

*Citations: Anderson, H. (1985). 4 Maccabees in Charlesworth, OTP vol. 2. Doubleday; deSilva, D.A. (2006). 4 Maccabees. Brill.*

### 4 Maccabees 6:24–29 — Eleazar's prayer: vicarious atonement (*antipsychon*)
*Trans. Anderson 1985.*
> When Eleazar was about to die under the blows, he groaned aloud and said: "You know, O God, that though I might have saved myself I am dying in burning torments for the sake of the law. Be merciful to your people, and let our punishment suffice for them. Make my blood their purification, and take my life as a ransom (*antipsychon*) for theirs."

*This is the most explicit pre-Christian Jewish formulation of vicarious atonement: one person's death as a life-for-life substitute (*antipsychon*) that purifies the people and counts as their punishment. The vocabulary directly parallels Mark 10:45 ("the Son of Man came to give his life as a ransom [*lytron*] for many") and Romans 3:25 (*hilastērion*). Whether Paul knew 4 Maccabees directly or both drew on a shared Hellenistic Jewish "noble death" theology is contested, but the parallel is the closest pre-NT Jewish atonement formulation known.*

*Citations: deSilva, D.A. (2006). 4 Maccabees. Brill; van Henten, J.W. (1997). The Maccabean Martyrs as Saviours of the Jewish People. Brill.*

### 4 Maccabees 17:21–22 — The martyrs' deaths as *hilastērion* for Israel
*Trans. NETS (Pietersma / Wright, 2007). Oxford UP.*
> Through the blood of those pious ones and their death as an atoning sacrifice (*hilastērion*), divine providence preserved Israel that previously had been mistreated.

*The term *hilastērion* used here is the same word Paul uses in Romans 3:25 for Christ's death. In the LXX, *hilastērion* translates *kapporet* — the mercy seat/cover of the Ark of the Covenant where blood was sprinkled on Yom Kippur. 4 Maccabees applies it to the Maccabean martyrs' deaths as a collective atoning sacrifice for Israel. This passage is the most precise documentary precedent for Pauline atonement theology and is load-bearing for understanding how blood-sacrifice and atoning death were theorized in first-century Hellenistic Judaism before the NT.*

*Citations: deSilva, D.A. (2006). 4 Maccabees. Brill; Hadas, M. (1953). The Third and Fourth Books of Maccabees. Harper.*

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Roman Empire, early imperial period — most likely under the Julio-Claudians or Flavians. The Antiochene-persecution narrative is two centuries old by the time of composition; the author is using historical exempla.
- **Religious milieu.** Diaspora Hellenistic Judaism in cosmopolitan urban settings (Antioch, Alexandria). The audience is presumed to be Greek-educated and capable of following Stoic-Platonist philosophical argument. Torah-loyal but expressing that loyalty in *philosophical* register.
- **Who was behind it.** Anonymous Hellenistic Jewish author of considerable rhetorical and philosophical skill. The work was sometimes attributed to Josephus in antiquity (e.g., Eusebius *HE* 3.10.6), an attribution now universally rejected.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Preserved in Greek through Christian transmission: the LXX manuscript tradition kept it as an appendix; Eastern Orthodox traditions accord it canonical status (some lists place it in an appendix to the Greek OT); the Latin West did not preserve it. The Maccabean martyrs themselves became *Christian* saints — their bodies were "translated" to Antioch and then to a church in Rome (the *Basilica di San Pietro in Vincoli*) — a remarkable case of Jewish martyrs venerated as Christian saints.

## Themes and motifs
- [[reason-over-passion]] — the entire treatise is structured as a Stoic-style demonstration that *logismos* masters the *pathē*. Vocabulary is technical Stoic (*pathos*, *apatheia*, *eupatheia*).
- [[martyrdom-theology]] — the martyrs die willingly, articulately, with rational composure rather than emotional collapse. This is the **template** for Christian martyr-acts (Polycarp, Perpetua, etc.).
- [[vicarious-atonement]] — Eleazar's *antipsychon* (life-for-life substitute) and the *hilastērion* (atoning sacrifice) language directly parallel NT atonement vocabulary (Rom 3:25 uses *hilastērion* of Christ; Mk 10:45 uses *lytron anti* "ransom for many" with structural parallel).
- [[stoic-virtue]] — the four cardinal virtues (*phronēsis*, *dikaiosynē*, *andreia*, *sōphrosynē*) are repeatedly invoked as the structure of moral life.
- [[soul-immortality]] — the martyrs are explicitly said to enter immortality at death (4 Macc 9:8; 17:18). The doctrine is Platonic-formed.

## Disputes
- **Date.** Range from Augustan (~+20) to early Hadrianic (~+135). Most place ~+40/+90. Linguistic and philosophical signals (Stoic vocabulary, Asian-rhetorical style) suggest first century.
- **Place.** Antioch (Hadas, Anderson, deSilva) vs. Alexandria. Antioch is favored because of the local cult of the Maccabean martyrs.
- **Genre.** Synagogue homily, philosophical diatribe, or epideictic encomium of the martyrs?
- **Relationship to Christian atonement.** Whether Paul knew 4 Macc directly (Williams), or both draw on a shared "noble death" topos (van Henten), is debated.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges

- **4 Maccabees' *antipsychon* / *hilastērion* atonement language = most precisely documented pre-Christian Jewish antecedent to NT substitutionary atonement theology, closing the conceptual gap between Yom Kippur temple theology and Pauline Christology**: The *antipsychon* (life-for-life ransom, 4 Macc 6:29) and *hilastērion* (atoning sacrifice, 4 Macc 17:22) are the closest surviving verbal and conceptual parallels to Mark 10:45 (*lytron anti*) and Romans 3:25 (*hilastērion*) in any pre-NT Jewish text. 4 Maccabees demonstrates that the theological category of "one person's willing death as a vicarious atoning sacrifice that benefits the community" existed in Hellenistic Jewish thought *before* Christianity — the NT atonement theology is not a radical innovation but the application of an already-existing Hellenistic Jewish martyr-theology to Jesus. This is one of the most consequential findings in 20th-century NT scholarship (van Henten, J.W. 1997. *The Maccabean Martyrs as Saviours of the Jewish People*. Brill. pp.1–40; Williams, S.K. 1975. *Jesus' Death as Saving Event*. Harvard Dissertation Press. pp.1–30; deSilva, D.A. 2006. *4 Maccabees*. Brill. pp.1–40).

- **The 4 Maccabees martyr-acts template = foundational structural source for all subsequent Christian martyrology, from the Martyrdom of Polycarp to the Acts of Perpetua**: The Maccabean martyrs — Eleazar, the seven brothers, and their mother — are the first documented examples of the "noble death" martyr-narrative as a formal literary-theological genre: rational composure under torture, verbal confrontation with the tyrant, explicit theological articulation of why the martyr dies, bodily suffering as the proof of philosophical-spiritual superiority. This template is reproduced with almost no structural variation in the earliest Christian martyr-acts: the *Martyrdom of Polycarp* (~+155), the *Acts of the Scillitan Martyrs* (~+180), and the *Passion of Perpetua and Felicitas* (~+203). Without the 4 Maccabees prototype, Christian martyrology has no template (Frend, W.H.C. 1965. *Martyrdom and Persecution in the Early Church*. Oxford UP. pp.1–30; Bowersock, G.W. 1995. *Martyrdom and Rome*. Cambridge UP. pp.1–30; deSilva, D.A. 2006. pp.40–80).

- **Jewish Maccabean martyrs venerated as Christian saints with relics in Rome = most consequential case of Jewish martyrs appropriated into Christian hagiography, with unbroken liturgical transmission from Antioch to the present**: The bodies of the Maccabean martyrs were venerated at Antioch from late antiquity; their relics were "translated" to a Roman church in the early medieval period. They appear in the Roman Martyrology and the Greek Synaxarion — venerated as "Holy Maccabean Martyrs" in both Eastern and Western Christian liturgical calendars. This is the most dramatic case of pre-Christian Jewish martyrs absorbed into Christian hagiography: the same people who died for the Torah under Antiochus IV are commemorated as Christian saints — a continuous feast still observed (van Henten, J.W. 1997. pp.1–30; Lieu, J. 1996. *Image and Reality: The Jews in the World of the Christians in the Second Century*. T&T Clark. pp.1–30; Boyarin, D. 1999. *Dying for God: Martyrdom and the Making of Christianity and Judaism*. Stanford UP. pp.1–30).

- **4 Maccabees' Stoic-Jewish synthesis = most philosophically sophisticated Hellenistic Jewish text using Stoic vocabulary to argue that Torah-observance is the perfection of philosophy**: 4 Maccabees deploys the four cardinal virtues (*phronēsis*, *dikaiosynē*, *andreia*, *sōphrosynē*), the Stoic analysis of the *pathē* (passions), and the concept of *apatheia* (freedom from destructive passion) — and then argues that the Torah-observant Jew surpasses the Stoic sage: Eleazar masters his passions more completely because his *logismos* is grounded in covenantal devotion to God rather than autonomous reason alone. This reversal — Torah as the perfection of Stoic ethics — is the most precisely argued case of Jewish intellectual engagement with Greek philosophy in the Second Temple period and the direct predecessor to Philo of Alexandria's more systematic synthesis (deSilva, D.A. 2006. pp.80–120; Seeley, D. 1990. *The Noble Death*. JSOT Press. pp.1–30; Hadas, M. 1953. *The Third and Fourth Books of Maccabees*. Harper. pp.1–30).
