# Hermetic Corpus — Earliest Stratum

## What it is
A scattered literary corpus attributed to **Hermes Trismegistus** ("thrice-greatest Hermes"), the Hellenistic syncretic figure who fuses the Egyptian scribe-god Thoth and the Greek messenger-god Hermes. The textual corpus has three main components:
1. **Corpus Hermeticum** (18 Greek treatises preserved as a Byzantine collection — Treatise I, the *Poimandres*, is the most famous and probably the earliest layer)
2. **Asclepius** (a longer dialogue preserved complete in Latin and partially in Coptic at Nag Hammadi)
3. **Stobaean Hermetica** (excerpts preserved by John of Stobi, ~+500)
4. **Coptic Hermetica from Nag Hammadi VI** (~+350 Coptic translations of 1st–3rd-century Greek originals)

The **Poimandres** (CH I) is the foundational mythic-philosophical narrative: Hermes is visited by Poimandres ("Shepherd of Men", a divine Nous-figure), who reveals the cosmogony — the divine Mind generates a second Mind / Logos, which crafts the cosmos; the primal Anthropos (Man) descends into matter through love of his own reflection and becomes mortal; salvation is the soul's *anabasis* (re-ascent) through the seven planetary spheres back to the source.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Roman Egypt across the 1st–4th centuries. The Hermetic milieu is *post-conquest*: Egyptian priestly culture continues but operates in Greek, articulates itself in Greek philosophical categories, and addresses a mixed Egyptian-Greek readership.
- **Religious milieu.** A genuine Greco-Egyptian *fusion*: Egyptian temple theology (Thoth's wisdom-and-creator role, the cosmic Nous-traditions of the Hermopolitan and Heliopolitan priesthoods) merged with Middle Platonist philosophy (the One, Nous, World-Soul hierarchy) and Stoic-Philonic Logos discourse. Hermetism is **not Christian and not Jewish** but stands as a parallel current with overlapping conceptual material.
- **Who was behind it.** Egyptian priestly intellectuals writing in Greek, addressing initiates. The texts often take the form of dialogues between Hermes and his disciples Tat, Asclepius, and Ammon — a teacher-disciple genre with roots in Egyptian instructional literature.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous circulation in Greek and Coptic through Late Antiquity; lost to Latin Europe by the medieval period except for the *Asclepius*; Greek *Corpus Hermeticum* manuscripts recovered by Italian humanists in the 15th century; Ficino's 1463 Latin translation triggered the *Renaissance Hermeticism* that influenced Pico della Mirandola, Bruno, and the European esoteric tradition. The Nag Hammadi discovery (1945) added Coptic Hermetic texts.

**Phase-boundary dispute.** Mainstream scholarship (Festugière, Copenhaver, Fowden) places the earliest layers of the Corpus Hermeticum ~+100/+200 — straddling the Phase 3 / Phase 4 boundary. We seed this node **here** to make Phase 3 connections visible (especially to Hellenistic-Jewish Logos and Stoic-Platonic philosophy) and **flag** that fuller treatment continues in Phase 4 (where Iamblichus, late Hermetica, and Coptic-Egyptian receptions belong).

## Themes and motifs
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — *gnōsis* (knowing) of one's true divine origin and ascent-path is salvation. CH I.21: "He who has known himself has entered into the Good." This vocabulary is shared with Christian Gnosticism without being identical to it.
- [[ascent-of-the-soul]] — the soul's return through the seven planetary spheres, shedding the *heimarmenē* (fated qualities) of each planet, until reuniting with the divine Nous (CH I.24–26).
- [[demiurge]] — a second divine Nous / Logos crafts the cosmos. Like Platonism, the demiurge is good and the cosmos beautiful — unlike Gnostic demiurges, who are ignorant or malevolent.
- [[anthropos-myth]] — the primal Man (Anthropos) descends from heaven through love of his own reflection in Nature, who embraces him; the result is dual-natured humanity (CH I.12–15). This myth structurally parallels Gnostic Anthropos-myths and (more loosely) Jewish-Christian Adam-Christology.
- [[logos-cosmic-reason]] — Hermetic Logos is the demiurgic, cosmos-ordering principle, conceptually parallel to Stoic-Philonic Logos.
- [[syncretism-egyptian-greek]] — Thoth-Hermes identification is fundamental; Egyptian gods are renamed Greek (Asclepius is Imhotep, Tat is Thoth himself); Egyptian temple theology is dressed in Hellenistic philosophical clothing.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — *direct-influence*: Hermetic cosmology is Middle Platonist in structure (the *Timaeus* demiurge, the world of Forms, the soul-ascent).
- → [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]] — *parallel-motif*: shared Alexandrian Middle Platonist + Logos milieu; Philo and the earliest Hermetica share extensive vocabulary while developing it for different religious commitments.
- → [[phase-3-005-stoic-foundational-texts]] — *direct-influence*: Stoic Logos and cosmology are absorbed and redirected.
- → [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — *parallel-motif*: Logos cosmologies in early Roman Imperial Egypt and Asia Minor share substrate; not literary dependence but shared conceptual neighborhood (Dodd's classic *The Interpretation of the Fourth Gospel* explored this at length).
- → Egyptian temple theology (Thoth, Heliopolitan and Hermopolitan cosmologies) — *direct-influence*: the Hermetic tradition is in real continuity with Egyptian priestly thought (Fowden 1986 is definitive).
- → Christian Gnosticism (Phase 4) — *parallel-motif*: Sethian and Valentinian systems share Anthropos-myth, gnosis-soteriology, soul-ascent through the spheres, while differing on the valuation of the demiurge.
- → Iamblichus and theurgic Neoplatonism (Phase 4) — *direct-influence*: Iamblichus's *De Mysteriis* treats Egyptian Hermetism as a legitimate (indeed superior) wisdom tradition.
- → Renaissance Hermeticism — *direct-influence*: Ficino's 1463 Latin Pimander launches the Western esoteric appropriation.

## Disputes
- **Date of the Corpus.** Casaubon (1614) demonstrated the *Corpus Hermeticum* could not be the ancient pre-Mosaic wisdom Renaissance humanists believed it was, but its actual dating ranges across two centuries. Mainstream now: ~+100/+300, with Poimandres at the early end (Fowden, Copenhaver). Some place earliest Hermetic literature back into the 1st c. BCE.
- **Egyptian vs. Greek origin.** 19th-century: purely Greek. 20th-century (Reitzenstein): Iranian-Babylonian. Modern consensus (Fowden, Mahé): genuinely Egyptian priestly culture in Greek expression.
- **Relationship to Gnosticism.** Parallel current sharing vocabulary (Festugière) vs. variant of Gnosticism (older scholarship). Mainstream now: parallel, not subset.
- **Christian or pagan-philosophical?** No evidence of Christian dependence; Hermetism is properly pagan-philosophical-religious.
