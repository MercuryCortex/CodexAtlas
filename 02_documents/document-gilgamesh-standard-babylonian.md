# The Epic of Gilgamesh — Standard Babylonian Version

## What it is
The canonical, twelve-tablet form of the world's oldest great epic, redacted around 1200 BCE by the scholar Sîn-lēqi-unninni from older Babylonian and Sumerian material. It tells of Gilgamesh, king of Uruk, and his wild companion Enkidu: their friendship, their slaying of Humbaba in the Cedar Forest and the Bull of Heaven sent by the spurned goddess Ishtar, Enkidu's death, and Gilgamesh's desperate, failed quest for immortality — which brings him to Utnapishtim, survivor of the Flood, and to the hard wisdom that death is the lot of humankind. Its Tablet XI preserves the flood story that startled the Victorian world with its kinship to Noah.

## Primary text

### Tablet XI (the Flood) — Utnapishtim speaks
*Trans. George (1999)*
> "I sent forth a dove and released it. The dove went off, but came back to me; no perch was visible so it circled back to me. I sent forth a swallow... I sent forth a raven... it saw the waters receding... it did not circle back to me."

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Mesopotamian polytheism; the gods send the Flood and regret it, and grant Utnapishtim alone eternal life.
- **Recovery.** George Smith's 1872 decipherment of the flood tablet — and his reported excitement at finding the dove and the ark — made Gilgamesh a sensation and reframed the study of Genesis.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The fullest text comes from Ashurbanipal's 7th-century library at Nineveh.

## Themes and motifs
- The quest for immortality — and its refusal; the human condition defined by death.
- [[underworld-descent]] — Tablet XII (an appended Sumerian translation) and Enkidu's vision of the netherworld.
- Friendship and grief — Gilgamesh's mourning for Enkidu as the engine of the plot.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-1-006-atrahasis]] / [[phase-1-016-eridu-genesis-flood]] — *shared-source*: the flood of Tablet XI is the Atrahasis/Ziusudra flood, imported into the epic.

- → [[document-genesis]] — *parallel-motif*: the closest extra-biblical analogue to Noah's flood (the ark, the birds, the sacrifice God smells, the covenant-like aftermath).
- → [[phase-2-036-ecclesiastes]] — *parallel-motif*: Siduri's "eat, drink, and be merry" counsel anticipates Qoheleth's carpe diem.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Tablet XI is the closest thing to a source for Noah's flood.** Utnapishtim's account — warned by Ea to build a great boat, loading it with his family, kin, and animals, riding out a deluge that destroys humanity, grounding on a mountain, sending out a dove, a swallow, and a raven, then offering a sacrifice whose sweet savor the gods "smelled" and gathered around "like flies" — matches Genesis 6–9 detail for detail (the boat, the mountain landfall, the bird-reconnaissance, the smelled sacrifice). The kinship, announced by George Smith in 1872, forced biblical scholarship to read Genesis within its Mesopotamian context and remains the single most famous Near-East-to-Bible parallel. Behind Genesis stands Gilgamesh, and behind Gilgamesh stands Atrahasis (George 2003, vol. 1 pp.508–526; Foster 2005 pp.85–90).
