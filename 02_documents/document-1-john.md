# First Epistle of John

## What it is
A homiletic tract from the Johannine community, written in the wake of a painful schism over the person of Christ. In the spare, dualistic idiom of the Fourth Gospel, it tests the spirits: those who deny that Jesus has "come in the flesh" are of the antichrist; those who walk in the light, love one another, and confess the Son are of God. From it come two of Christianity's most enduring formulas — "God is love" — and one of its most fateful terms — "antichrist."

## Primary text

### 1 John 4:8–9
*Trans. NRSV*
> Whoever does not love does not know God, for God is love. God's love was revealed among us in this way: God sent his only Son into the world so that we might live through him.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** A community torn by Christological schism (2:19); the secessionists' denial of Jesus's true humanity is an early form of docetism.
- **Authorship.** From the Johannine school, close in language and theology to the Gospel of John.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Among the undisputed Catholic Epistles.

## Themes and motifs
- "God is love" — the climactic definition (4:8, 16).
- Antichrist — those denying the incarnate Son; the term the epistle coins (2:18).
- Tests of fellowship — love, right confession, and obedience as the marks of abiding in God.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The birth of "the Antichrist."** The word *antichristos* appears in the entire Bible only in the Johannine epistles (1 John 2:18, 22; 4:3; 2 John 7), where it names not a single end-times tyrant but anyone who denies that Jesus is the incarnate Christ. Later tradition fused this term with 2 Thessalonians' "man of lawlessness," Daniel's blasphemous king, and Revelation's beast to build the towering medieval and modern figure of "the Antichrist" — a composite whose very name comes from a small community letter about a Christological schism (Brown 1982, AYB 30 pp.7–13, 330–335).
