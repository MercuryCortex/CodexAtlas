# Book of Micah

## What it is
A short prophetic book of the Twelve, alternating judgment and hope, from a rural Judahite prophet contemporary with Isaiah. It contains two of the most-quoted verses in the Hebrew Bible: the distilled summary of covenant ethics (6:8) and the prophecy of a ruler to come from Bethlehem (5:2), read by Matthew as foretelling Jesus's birthplace.

## Primary text

### Micah 6:8
*Trans. NRSV*
> He has told you, O mortal, what is good; and what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?

### Micah 5:2
> But you, O Bethlehem of Ephrathah, who are one of the little clans of Judah, from you shall come forth for me one who is to rule in Israel, whose origin is from of old, from ancient days.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Judah under Assyrian pressure; Micah champions the exploited countryside against the capital's elites and corrupt prophets.
- **Religious milieu.** Shares the justice ethic of Amos and the vision-of-peace tradition of Isaiah (the "swords into plowshares" oracle appears in both).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous canonical transmission within the Twelve.

## Themes and motifs
- Covenant ethics distilled — 6:8, the single most concise statement of what YHWH requires.
- [[messianism]] — the Bethlehem ruler-to-come (5:2), foundational to Christian nativity theology.
- Justice for the poor — the indictment of land-grabbing elites (2:1–2).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Micah 5:2 puts the Messiah in Bethlehem — the prophecy that fixed the Christmas story.** Matthew 2:6 and the chief priests' answer to Herod (and the assumption behind John 7:42) all rest on Micah's naming of Bethlehem, a "little clan," as the origin of Israel's ruler. An 8th-century prophet's word about a Davidic restoration became the scriptural anchor of the nativity in Bethlehem (Andersen & Freedman 2000, AYB 24E pp.466–477).
