# First Book of Maccabees

## What it is
A sober, pro-Hasmonean history of the Jewish revolt against the Seleucid king Antiochus IV Epiphanes (167–134 BCE). When Antiochus outlaws the Torah, defiles the Temple, and forces pagan sacrifice, the priest Mattathias and his five sons — above all Judas "Maccabeus" ("the Hammer") — lead a guerrilla war that recaptures Jerusalem, rededicates the Temple (the origin of Hanukkah), and establishes the independent Hasmonean dynasty. Written in deliberately biblical Hebrew style, it reads the revolt as the continuation of Israel's sacred history.

## Primary text

### 1 Maccabees 4:59
*Trans. NRSV (Apocrypha)*
> Then Judas and his brothers and all the assembly of Israel determined that every year at that season the days of the dedication of the altar should be observed with joy and gladness.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** The Seleucid empire's forced Hellenization and the Jewish armed response.
- **Religious milieu.** Zeal for the Torah and the Temple; martyrdom and holy war as faithfulness.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Greek text preserved by the church; the Hebrew is lost. Deuterocanonical, not in the Jewish canon — yet it records the events behind Hanukkah.

## Themes and motifs
- Righteous resistance — armed struggle for the Law, modeled on Phinehas's "zeal."
- Temple rededication — the founding of Hanukkah (4:36–59).
- [[divine-kingship]] — the Hasmoneans as God's instrument of deliverance.

## Connections (provisional)
