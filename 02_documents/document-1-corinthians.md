# First Epistle to the Corinthians

## What it is
Paul's longest and most wide-ranging pastoral letter, answering a tangle of crises in the boisterous church he founded at Corinth: factions, a scandalous sexual case, lawsuits, marriage and celibacy, food offered to idols, the conduct of worship and the Lord's Supper, spiritual gifts, and — climactically — the resurrection of the dead. Along the way it preserves the earliest written eucharistic words, the earliest list of resurrection appearances, and the incomparable "hymn to love."

## Primary text

### 1 Corinthians 15:3–4
*Trans. NRSV*
> For I handed on to you as of first importance what I in turn had received: that Christ died for our sins in accordance with the scriptures, and that he was buried, and that he was raised on the third day in accordance with the scriptures.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** A wealthy, gifted, divided Greek congregation; Paul addresses both elite "knowledge" and charismatic excess.
- **Authorship.** Undisputed Pauline; among the most securely-dated NT texts (~53–55 CE).
- **Discovery / transmission.** Within the Pauline corpus.

## Themes and motifs
- [[resurrection-of-the-dead]] — ch. 15, the earliest and fullest Christian argument for bodily resurrection.
- The body of Christ — the church as one interdependent body of many members (ch. 12).
- Love — the "more excellent way" of ch. 13.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **1 Corinthians 15:3–8 is the oldest datable Christian creed.** Paul says he is passing on what he "received" — a pre-formed confession (Christ died, was buried, was raised, appeared) that scholars date to within a few years of the crucifixion, making it the earliest surviving statement of the resurrection faith, older than any Gospel. Chapter 15 then mounts the foundational argument that without bodily resurrection "your faith is futile" — the text on which Christian eschatology stands (Fitzmyer 2008, AYB 32 pp.539–549).
