# Book of Tobit

## What it is
A charming diaspora novella set among the exiled northern tribes in Assyria. The pious, blind Tobit sends his son Tobias on a journey accompanied — unknowingly — by the angel Raphael in disguise; Tobias wins the much-widowed Sarah (whose seven previous bridegrooms were slain on their wedding night by the demon Asmodeus), drives off the demon, heals his father's blindness, and Raphael at last reveals his angelic identity. Beneath the folktale runs an earnest teaching on almsgiving, burial of the dead, and the hidden providence of God.

## Primary text

### Tobit 12:15
*Trans. NRSV (Apocrypha)*
> I am Raphael, one of the seven angels who stand ready and enter before the glory of the Lord.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Second Temple diaspora piety, with a developed angelology (named archangels) and demonology (Asmodeus) characteristic of the period.
- **Composition.** A Semitic-language original (confirmed at Qumran), later most widely read in Greek.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Deuterocanonical for Catholics, Orthodox, and Ethiopians; the Qumran Aramaic/Hebrew fragments were a major 20th-century find.

## Themes and motifs
- Angelic providence — Raphael ("God heals") as guide and healer.
- Almsgiving and burial — the supreme acts of diaspora piety.
- Demonology — Asmodeus, the slayer-demon driven off by Raphael.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Tobit names the seven archangels and the demon Asmodeus.** Raphael's self-revelation as "one of the seven angels who stand before the glory of the Lord" (12:15) is a foundational text for the seven-archangel scheme of later Jewish, Christian, and Islamic angelology, and Tobit's [[asmodeus]] (from the Avestan demon Aēšma-daēva) becomes a stock figure of medieval demonology and the Testament of Solomon — a small reminder that the angel-and-demon hierarchies of the Abrahamic faiths crystallized in the Second Temple period, partly under Iranian influence (Moore 1996, AYB 40A pp.272–280).
