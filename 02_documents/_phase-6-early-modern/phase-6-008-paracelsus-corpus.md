# Paracelsus' Theological-Medical-Alchemical Corpus

## What it is
A vast, polemical, vernacular, and chaotically transmitted corpus of medical, alchemical, astrological, and theological-mystical writings by the Swiss physician and Renaissance magus Paracelsus (Theophrastus von Hohenheim, 1493–1541). The corpus integrates: (1) a medical-philosophical reformation, replacing Galenic four-humors theory with a three-principles theory (sulphur, mercury, salt) and chemical-alchemical pharmacology (iatrochemistry); (2) a magical natural philosophy of *signatures*, *archei*, and elementals; (3) a microcosm-macrocosm anthropology in which the human is a chemical compendium of the cosmos; (4) a heterodox-but-Christ-centered theology including substantial mystical and pneumatological writings (largely unprinted in his lifetime); and (5) a polemical rejection of academic medicine and scholastic theology in favor of vernacular German and direct observation. Major works include the *Volumen Paramirum*, *Opus Paramirum*, *Astronomia Magna* (also titled *Philosophia Sagax*), *De Natura Rerum*, *Liber de Nymphis*, the *Archidoxis*, and the *Liber de Imaginibus*.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Holy Roman Empire and Swiss Confederation during the early Reformation. Paracelsus's peripatetic life carried him through reformed and Catholic territories; he was personally sympathetic to reform but to none of the established factions, and lived increasingly as a wandering empirico-mystical physician.
- **Religious milieu.** Reformation-era confessional fluidity. Paracelsus' Christology is Christ-centered but anti-confessional; his theology is closer to the radical reformation and to the German mystical tradition than to Wittenberg or Geneva.
- **Who was behind it.** Paracelsus himself; the textual transmission depended on disciples and editors, especially Johannes Huser, who produced the foundational Basel print edition (1589–1591) a half-century after Paracelsus' death.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Manuscript circulation 1520–1541; selective printing in Paracelsus' lifetime; major editorial recovery 1560s–1591 (the Huser edition); influential through the 17th century in iatrochemistry (Robert Fludd, Johannes Baptista van Helmont) and the Rosicrucian movement. Modern critical editing began with Karl Sudhoff in 1922.

## Themes and motifs
- [[microcosm-macrocosm]] — the human as a chemical-astrological summary of the cosmos; the foundational anthropology, drawn from Hermetic-Stoic tradition and operationalized in medicine.
- **Three principles (tria prima)** — sulphur (combustible / soul), mercury (volatile / spirit), salt (fixed / body). Replaces Aristotelian four elements as the explanatory base of pharmacology and pathology.
- **Signatures** — the visible form of created things reveals their inner virtue (the doctrine of *signaturae rerum*); plants resembling organs treat those organs.
- **Archei** — internal directive-spirits of organs and substances.
- [[prisca-theologia]] — Paracelsus participates in the Renaissance retrieval of Hermes, Pythagoras, and ancient wisdom; less philosophically articulated than in Ficino, more practical-magical.
- [[creation-by-word]] — Christ as Logos in the natural-magical register.
- [[soul-as-spark]] — Paracelsian *gabal* / *limbus*, the soul-spark internal to the body's chemistry.

- → [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] — *direct-influence*: Hermetic medicine and the macro-microcosm doctrine reach Paracelsus via the post-Ficino Renaissance current.
- → [[phase-6-009-agrippa-de-occulta-philosophia]] — *parallel*: contemporary Renaissance magic; Paracelsus and Agrippa are the two great Northern European magical syntheses of the 1530s, with different emphases (medical-chemical vs. ceremonial-Kabbalistic).
- → [[phase-6-017-boehme-aurora-mysterium-magnum]] — *direct-influence*: Boehme's three-principles theosophy (Sulphur-Mercury-Salt) is taken from Paracelsus and reworked theologically.
- → [[phase-6-022-fludd-utriusque-cosmi-historia]] — *direct-influence*: Fludd is a Paracelsian.
- → Carl Jung (Phase 7) — *direct-influence*: Jung's *Paracelsica* and his alchemical psychology project draw heavily on Paracelsus.
- → 17th-century iatrochemistry / van Helmont — *direct-influence*: Paracelsus founds the chemical-medical alternative to Galenism that becomes one root of modern biochemistry.

## Disputes
- **Authentic vs. pseudo-Paracelsica.** The textual transmission via disciples produced a substantial body of *Pseudo-Paracelsica*; authentication is ongoing scholarly work.
- **Place in the history of science.** Older positivist historiography (Singer, Sarton) treated Paracelsus as a transitional figure between magic and chemistry; Pagel and Webster have shown the integration of "magical" and "scientific" elements is internal to Paracelsus' own framework, not a historical transition.
