# Heptaplus + Conclusiones Cabalisticae

## What it is
Two complementary expressions of Pico's Christian Kabbalist program. The *Heptaplus* (1489) is a sevenfold allegorical exposition of the first chapter of Genesis, in which Pico reads the six days of creation simultaneously through (1) elemental physics, (2) celestial physics, (3) supercelestial / angelic world, (4) human microcosm, (5) the Hebrew Kabbalistic Sefirot, (6) the relations between these worlds, and (7) the Sabbath as the goal of Christ. The *Conclusiones Cabalisticae* are the Kabbalistic theses embedded in the 1486 *900 Conclusions* (Farmer's numbering, conclusions 11>1 through 11>72, plus 28>1 through 28>47 "according to one's own opinion"), in which Pico (a) identifies the Tetragrammaton with the name of Jesus by Kabbalistic letter-permutation, (b) maps the Sefirot onto the Christian Trinity and the soul's ascent, and (c) claims that Kabbalah converges on Christianity when properly read.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Florence under Lorenzo, the period of Pico's reconciliation with the papacy via Lorenzo's intercession; *Heptaplus* is dedicated to Lorenzo.
- **Religious milieu.** Post-1487 — Pico is operating in the shadow of the partial papal condemnation of the 900. The *Heptaplus* avoids the most provocative claims and presents itself as a Genesis commentary, but its Kabbalistic register is unmistakable.
- **Who was behind it.** Pico and Flavius Mithridates. The textual reconstruction by Wirszubski (1989) and Giulio Busi has identified the specific Kabbalistic sources: Recanati's commentary, the Zohar (in Mithridates' Latin), Sefer Yetzirah, Bahir, and Joseph Gikatilla's *Sha'arei Orah*.
- **Discovery / transmission.** *Heptaplus* was reprinted multiple times in the 16th century; the Cabalistic Conclusions remained controversial but circulated through Reuchlin's transmission.

## Themes and motifs
- [[sefirot]] — the ten emanations of Ein Sof, mapped onto Plotinian hypostases and Christian Trinitarian theology.
- [[creation-by-word]] — Pico reads Genesis 1 through letter-mysticism (Sefer Yetzirah's 22 letters as creative principles).
- [[prisca-theologia]] — Kabbalah added to the chain: Moses → oral tradition → the Talmudic-Kabbalistic transmission → Christ as the fulfillment.
- [[hidden-god]] — Ein Sof as the apophatic Godhead beyond the Sefirot.
- **Microcosm/macrocosm** — the human body maps onto the Sefirotic structure of the cosmos.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Zohar (Phase 5, to be seeded) — *manuscript-transmission*: Pico's primary Kabbalistic source via Flavius Mithridates' 1486 Latin translation.
- → Sefer Yetzirah (Phase 5, to be seeded) — *direct-quote / commentary*: the letter-mysticism is Yetziratic.
- → [[phase-6-003-pico-oration-900-conclusions]] — *companion-piece*: the *Heptaplus* develops the Kabbalistic theses of the 900.
- → [[phase-6-006-reuchlin-de-arte-cabalistica]] — *direct-influence*: Reuchlin systematizes Pico's Kabbalistic-Christological program.
- → [[phase-6-011-cordovero-pardes-rimmonim]] — *parallel*: Cordovero's Safed-school systematization of Kabbalah is a Jewish parallel to what Pico had attempted from outside the tradition; the two streams (Jewish Cordoveran and Christian Pichian) develop in parallel and occasionally cross.
- → Genesis 1 ([[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]]) — *commentary-on*: *Heptaplus* is a Genesis 1 commentary in form.

## Disputes
- **Did Pico convert Jewish Kabbalah into a Christian hermeneutic, or distort it?** Jewish scholarly assessments range from Wirszubski's relatively sympathetic reconstruction to Idel's more critical assessment of the Christianization. The methodological question — whether Christian Kabbalah is *Kabbalah* — remains live.
