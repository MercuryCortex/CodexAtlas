# Al-Asfar al-Arba'a (The Four Journeys)

## What it is
Mulla Sadra's nine-volume Arabic philosophical summa, *al-Hikma al-muta'aliya fi al-asfar al-aqliyya al-arba'a* ("The Transcendent Philosophy of the Four Intellectual Journeys"), the central work of his *Hikmat al-Muta'aliya* (Transcendent Philosophy) system. Organized around the four spiritual-philosophical journeys of the wayfarer in Sufi mysticism (from creature to Real, in the Real, from Real to creature with the Real, and in creature with the Real), the *Asfar* integrates the Aristotelian-Avicennan rational tradition, the Illuminationist (*Ishraqi*) philosophy of Suhrawardi, the Sufi metaphysics of Ibn 'Arabi, the Shi'i theology of the Imamate, and Qur'anic-hadith exegesis into a single synthesis. Sadra's central philosophical doctrines — *aṣālat al-wujūd* (the primacy of existence over essence), *al-ḥaraka al-jawhariyya* (substantial motion: things change in their very substance, not just accidents), *waḥdat al-wujūd al-tashkīkī* (graded unity of existence), and *al-ḥikma al-muta'āliya* (transcendent wisdom integrating discursive and intuitive knowledge) — are the doctrinal core of post-Avicennan Iranian Islamic philosophy.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Safavid Iran under Shah Abbas I (r. 1588–1629) and his successors. The Safavids had established Twelver Shi'ism as the state religion and were sponsoring a major intellectual revival including the Isfahan school of philosophy.
- **Religious milieu.** The Safavid Shi'i seminary establishment was sometimes hostile to philosophical theology (*hikma*) and to Sufism; Mulla Sadra in his youth was reportedly forced from Isfahan to seven years of seclusion at the village of Kahak near Qum, where he developed the core of his system. He returned to teach at Shiraz under the patronage of Allahverdi Khan and his son Imam Quli Khan.
- **Who was behind it.** Sadra personally; his teachers included Mir Damad (the founder of the Isfahan school) and Shaykh Baha al-Din al-'Amili. His students included Mulla Muhsin Fayd al-Kashani and 'Abd al-Razzaq Lahiji, who carried the school into the late 17th c.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Iranian seminary transmission; the modern publication and study of Mulla Sadra began in the late 19th century with the Tehran lithographs and has expanded dramatically since the 1950s (Sayyid Jalal al-Din Ashtiyani's editorial work; the Western reception via Henry Corbin and Seyyed Hossein Nasr).

## Themes and motifs
- ***Aṣālat al-wujūd*** — the primacy of existence: existence (*wujūd*) is the fundamental reality, with essences (*māhiyyāt*) being mental abstractions; reverses the dominant post-Avicennan position (Suhrawardi had argued the primacy of essence).
- ***Al-ḥaraka al-jawhariyya*** — substantial motion: change is not merely accidental but penetrates substance itself; the natural world is in continuous self-transcendence.
- ***Waḥdat al-wujūd al-tashkīkī*** — graded unity of existence: existence is one but admits of degrees of intensity; the position is a mediation between Ibn 'Arabi's strict *waḥdat al-wujūd* and the orthodox theological multiplicity.
- [[ascent-of-the-soul]] — the Four Journeys structure is itself a Sufi spiritual itinerary made philosophical.
- ***Al-ma'ād al-jismānī*** — bodily resurrection demonstrated philosophically (a Qur'anic doctrine that Avicenna had only allowed on revealed authority).
- ***Ittiḥād al-'āqil wa al-ma'qūl*** — the union of intellecter and intellected; Sadra's epistemology of intellection.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Ibn Sina / Avicenna (Phase 5) — *direct-engagement*: Sadra's framework is post-Avicennan; he agrees with the rational-demonstrative project but inverts the essence/existence priority.
- → Suhrawardi's Ishraqi philosophy (Phase 5) — *direct-engagement*: Sadra integrates Illuminationist categories (the world of *mithāl* / imaginal forms) while reversing the essence/existence priority.
- → Ibn 'Arabi (Phase 5) — *direct-influence*: Sadra integrates *waḥdat al-wujūd* in modified form.
- → [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] (via the Arabic *Theology of Aristotle*) — *direct-influence*: the emanationist-Plotinian framework underlies Sadra's metaphysics.
- → Allamah Tabataba'i / Ayatollah Khomeini (Phase 7) — *direct-descendants*: the 20th-c. Iranian philosophical-theological tradition is a continuous Mulla Sadra reception.
- → Henry Corbin's comparative work (Phase 7) — *direct-influence*: Corbin reads Mulla Sadra alongside Heidegger and the Western mystical tradition.

## Disputes
- **Originality.** Critics (e.g., Hairi Yazdi) have argued that much of Sadra's "originality" recombines existing materials; defenders (Nasr, Rizvi) emphasize the genuinely innovative synthesis.
- **Sadra and Sufism / Shi'ism.** How firmly Sadra's system belongs in each of his three confessional-philosophical sources (Peripatetic, Ishraqi, Sufi) and in Twelver Shi'ism specifically is a continuing scholarly question. He is unambiguously Shi'i but his Sufi and philosophical commitments are inseparable from his theology.
