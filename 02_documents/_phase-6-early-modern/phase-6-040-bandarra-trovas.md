# Trovas do Bandarra

## What it is

The ***Trovas do Bandarra*** are a corpus of **vernacular Portuguese prophetic verse** composed by **Gonçalo Annes Bandarra** (c. 1500–1556), a shoemaker from Trancoso in the Beira region of north-central Portugal, during approximately 1530–1556. Composed in *redondilha* (octosyllabic) metre with structured *Sonho* (Dream) units, the *Trovas* present prophetic-visionary content organized around the recurrent figure of **O Encoberto** ("The Hidden / Shrouded One") — a coming king who will restore Portugal's greatness, expel its enemies (specifically Castilian and Moorish), and inaugurate a universal-Christian-messianic kingdom under Portuguese imperial sovereignty.

The *Trovas* are foundational to the [[tradition-sebastianism|Sebastianist]] tradition. They are the **direct doctrinal-poetic source** of the *Quinto Império* / [[fifth-empire-quinto-imperio|Fifth Empire]] doctrine in vernacular Portuguese form, drawing on Daniel 2's four-kingdoms schema (extended to a fifth) and Joachim of Fiore's tripartite-Status schema (Father / Son / Spirit), refracted through Iberian Jewish-converso messianic vocabulary and Iberian-Catholic apocalyptic-Reconquista mentality.

## Context

- **Political milieu.** Pre-1578 Portugal under the late House of Aviz (Manuel I 1495–1521, João III 1521–1557, the regency, Sebastian I 1557–1578). Bandarra wrote during the João III period — a moment of consolidation of Portuguese imperial expansion (Brazil settled, India trade fully established) but also of internal religious tension (the Portuguese Inquisition established 1536; the *Cristãos-Novos* / converso community under intensifying persecution; the Index of Prohibited Books emerging).
- **Religious milieu.** The Portuguese-Jewish-converso community was the principal audience for the *Trovas*' messianic-typological vocabulary. Iberian Jewish messianism — under the long shadow of the 1492 Spanish expulsion and the 1497 Portuguese forced-conversion — was generating substantial vernacular religious-literary production in Iberian-Romance languages.
- **Who was behind it.** Bandarra personally. The 1541 Inquisition trial record (Arquivo Nacional da Torre do Tombo) is the principal contemporary attestation of his identity, profession, and the *Trovas* circulation. He was sentenced to abjuration *de levi* (the lighter form) and public penance — *not* to imprisonment or burning — and continued his cobbler's trade in Trancoso until his death c. 1556.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The *Trovas* circulated in manuscript form throughout Portugal and the Portuguese-Atlantic-imperial space from the 1540s onward. Added to the Index of Prohibited Books in 1581 (post-Iberian Union); continued clandestine manuscript circulation. **First printed edition**: Paris 1603, prepared by **Dom João de Castro** (the émigré Portuguese pretender-claimant), titled *Paráfrase e Concordância de algumas Profecias de Bandarra*. This Paris 1603 edition founded the modern textual tradition; subsequent 17th–18th-c. printings followed Castro's text with variations.

## Themes and motifs

- [[fifth-empire-quinto-imperio]] — Daniel 2's Babylon-Persia-Greece-Rome four-kingdoms schema extended to a Portuguese fifth-and-final kingdom of universal Christianization. Bandarra is the principal vernacular-prophetic articulation; António Vieira's *História do Futuro* later provides the systematic-theological systematization.
- [[messianism]] — *O Encoberto*, the Hidden King who returns to inaugurate the messianic kingdom. The figure is consciously ambiguous; post-1578 it crystallizes onto [[sebastian-i-portugal|Sebastian I]] but the typology itself is broader.
- [[hidden-god]] — the *deus absconditus* / hidden-king topos in Portuguese vernacular form.
- **Joachimist tripartite age** (Status of Father / Son / Spirit) — the inherited Calabrian-Cistercian apocalyptic-theological schema.
- **Iberian Jewish-converso messianism** — the *Mashiach ben David* expectation in vernacular Portuguese typological form, refracted through Iberian-Catholic Christological vocabulary.

## Connections (provisional)

- → [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — *influenced-by*: the four-kingdoms schema in Daniel 2 is one of Bandarra's two principal structural sources.
- → [[phase-7-037-pessoa-mensagem]] — *direct-influence*: Pessoa's *Mensagem* (1934) Section III, *O Encoberto*, includes a specific poem titled "Bandarra" and explicitly continues the *Trovas*' tradition.
- → António Vieira's *História do Futuro* and *Clavis Prophetarum* — *commentary-on* / *systematization-of*: Vieira's 17th-c. Fifth-Empire project takes Bandarra as one of his three principal predictive sources (alongside Daniel and Isaiah). Vieira's works not yet stubbed in vault.
- → [[event-battle-of-alcacer-quibir-1578]] — *retrospective-interpretive-imposition*: the *Trovas* are read after 1578 as predicting Sebastian's reign and return, despite Bandarra having written before Sebastian's birth.

## Disputes

- **Was Bandarra a Cristão-Novo (converso)?** See [[goncalo-annes-bandarra]] §Disputes. Open question; the *Trovas*' vocabulary is heavily Iberian-Jewish-influenced whether or not Bandarra was himself of converso descent.
- **Did the *Trovas* "predict" Sebastian?** No, in any predictive-historical sense. The match is retrospective interpretive imposition after 1578. But the tradition does not depend on predictive correspondence; it depends on the symbolic-typological structure being available in vernacular Portuguese form when the trauma occurred.
- **What is the relationship of Bandarra's tripartite-Status schema to direct Joachimist sources?** Van den Besselaar 1976 argues for substantial Joachimist influence (whether direct manuscript-access or via Iberian-monastic intermediaries); Gandra 1999 develops this thesis at length; Valdez 2010 is more cautious about specific source-attribution. Mainstream consensus: Joachimist structural schema is present whether or not direct Joachim manuscript access can be demonstrated.
