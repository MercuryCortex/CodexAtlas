# Vinaya Patrika

## What it is
A collection of 279 devotional petitions / hymns (*padas*) by [[tulsidas]], composed in his later career at Banaras (~1580s–early 1600s — Tulsidas died 1623). Unlike the *Ramcharitmanas* (1574–1576) which is a narrative re-telling of the Rāmāyaṇa story, the *Vinaya Patrika* is a personal-devotional petition cycle: the poet's own prayer-letters to Rāma seeking grace, forgiveness, intercession, and refuge. The structure is loosely organized: opening invocations to Gaṇeśa, the river Gaṅgā, the city of Banaras (Kāśī), Śiva, Sūrya, and Hanumān; then the great body of *padas* addressed to Rāma; concluding with the famous "petition" frame in which Tulsidas asks Hanumān to deliver his prayer-letter to Rāma. The work is one of the most personally-confessional pre-modern Indian devotional texts; the poetic voice is intimate, raw, often miserable in its sense of unworthiness — a mode unusual in Tulsidas's broader corpus.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Late-Akbar (Akbar d. 1605) and Jahangir-period Mughal North India. Banaras was a major Mughal-period Brahmanical-Sanskrit center; Tulsidas lived there as a public religious figure of considerable reputation, interacting (per tradition, with varying historicity) with Mughal officials.
- **Religious milieu.** The Akbar-period *sulh-i kull* atmosphere of religious-cultural plurality; the simultaneous flourishing of Vaishnava, Shaiva, Shakta, Sant, and Sufi devotional movements; the consolidation of Hindi as a literary language across Awadhi, Brajbhāṣā, and Khari Boli registers. Tulsidas's late career operates in this Akbar-period environment.
- **Who was behind it.** Tulsidas himself, in his late years at Banaras.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Ramanandi-sampradaya manuscript and oral transmission. The Gita Press Gorakhpur 20th-c. editions have made the text universally accessible to the Hindi-speaking Hindu reading public.

## Themes and motifs
- [[bhakti-devotion]] — intense personal devotion to Rāma; the petitioning voice is the *anya-bhāva* of the helpless devotee throwing himself on the Lord's grace.
- [[avatar-doctrine]] — Rāma is the supreme avatara of Viṣṇu; many of the *padas* meditate on Rāma's *līlā* (cosmic-play) and his accessible-personal forms.
- [[divine-name]] — *Rāma-nāma* (the name of Rāma) is repeatedly invoked as the sufficient devotional vehicle; the *Vinaya Patrika* is one of the principal sources of the *nāma-japa* (name-repetition) devotional emphasis in Hindi-Vaishnavism.
- [[lamentation-genre]] — many of the *padas* are confessional laments: the poet's failures, his attachment to the world, his unworthiness, his fear of death and the next life. The *lamentation-petition* mode is structurally adjacent to the Hebrew Psalms of individual lament (Psalm 6, 22, 38, 51, 102, 130, 143) and to the Christian *De Profundis* tradition.
- [[forgiveness]] — Rāma's forgiveness is the cardinal devotional hope; the poet is unworthy but Rāma is gracious.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-6-019-tulsidas-ramcharitmanas]] — *parallel-motif* / *commentary-on*: same poet, same Rāma-devotion theology, very different genre (narrative epic vs. personal petition).
- → [[phase-6-013-mirabai-bhajans]] — *parallel-motif* / *shared-milieu*: contemporary 16th-c. North Indian devotional poetry, different deity (Krishna vs. Rāma), same broad bhakti-emotional register.
- → Hebrew Psalms of individual lament — *parallel-motif*: the genre of personal devotional petition addressed to a personal God, mixing self-accusation, plea, and trust, is structurally cross-traditional. Allchin 1966 notes the comparative resonance; Mahmood (in Schimmel 1975) and Vaudeville have treated North-Indian Vaishnava devotional petition genre as structurally parallel to Sufi *munājāt* (intimate-prayer) and to Christian Psalm-derived devotional petition. The cross-tradition pattern is real; direct influence is not claimed.
- → Sufi *munājāt* tradition — *parallel-motif*: 11th–17th-c. Indo-Persian Sufi tradition of intimate-prayer literature (Anṣārī's 11th-c. *Munājāt*, Rumi's prayer-fragments, Hafiz's mystical petition mode) is structurally adjacent to Tulsidas's *Vinaya Patrika*. In the Akbar-period North Indian devotional milieu, Vaishnava-Sufi cross-pollination is documented; whether the *Vinaya Patrika* shows specific Sufi-vocabulary influence is contested but the structural-affective parallel is striking.
- → [[ramana-maharshi]] — *direct-influence*: Ramana frequently quoted Tulsidas (especially the *Hanuman Chalisa* and parts of the *Vinaya Patrika*) in his Tamil instructional speech.

## Disputes
- **Date of composition**: late Tulsidas career (1580s–early 1600s) is the standard placement; specific dating of individual *padas* is generally not possible.
- **Original ordering of the *padas***: the manuscript tradition shows variation in the ordering; the modern Gita Press edition is a standardized ordering.
