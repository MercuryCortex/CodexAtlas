# Theologia Platonica de immortalitate animorum

## What it is
Ficino's eighteen-book magnum opus, composed 1469–1474 and printed at Florence in 1482, demonstrating from Platonic and Neoplatonic principles the immortality of the human soul. The work is structured as a hierarchical ascent of being — body, quality, soul, angelic mind, God — modeled on Plotinus' five hypostases and Proclus' triadic analyses, but reorganized around the soul as the ontological hinge between time and eternity. Ficino draws on Plato, Plotinus, Iamblichus, Proclus, Augustine, Aquinas, and Hermes Trismegistus, presenting them as participants in a single perennial theology converging on Christian doctrine.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Florence under Lorenzo de' Medici (1469–1492), the high noon of Florentine humanism. Ficino had been ordained priest in 1473 during the composition.
- **Religious milieu.** Catholic orthodoxy permitted Christianizing Platonism but viewed Hermetic-magical practice with suspicion; Ficino's *De vita libri tres* (1489) would later be cautiously investigated by the Inquisition. The *Theologia* itself is doctrinally orthodox, aimed at fortifying Christian belief against Averroist denial of personal immortality.
- **Who was behind it.** Ficino working under Medici patronage, drawing on the complete corpus of Plato (which he was simultaneously translating, completed 1484) and the Plotinus translation he had begun.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous early-modern transmission; the work was widely read in Catholic Europe through the 16th–17th centuries and is foundational for the Cambridge Platonists (Henry More, Ralph Cudworth) in the 17th century.

## Themes and motifs
- [[soul-immortality]] — the central thesis: the soul is naturally immortal, demonstrable from its self-motion, its reception of universal Forms, and its convertibility upon the One.
- [[prisca-theologia]] — the underlying historiographic frame: Hermes, Orpheus, Pythagoras, Plato, and Christ teach a single ancient wisdom.
- [[hidden-god]] — apophatic-cataphatic theology inherited from Pseudo-Dionysius and Proclus.
- [[forms-and-archetypes]] — the Platonic Forms as God's thoughts, located in the divine Mind.

## Disputes
- **Originality vs. compilation.** Older scholarship (Kristeller) emphasized Ficino's originality; more recent work (Allen, Hankins) attends to his careful synthesis of patristic, medieval, and Neoplatonic sources.
