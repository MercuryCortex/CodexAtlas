# Etz Chayyim (Tree of Life) — Lurianic Kabbalah

## What it is
The Lurianic corpus — the body of Kabbalistic teaching given orally by Isaac Luria (ha-Ari, "the Lion," 1534–1572) during his brief twenty-month residence at Safed in 1570–1572, recorded almost entirely by his student Chaim Vital and edited posthumously over decades by Vital and his son Shmuel Vital. The principal extant text-systems are: *Etz Chayyim* (the philosophical-theological exposition organized in fifty *she'arim*, gates), the *Shemonah She'arim* (Eight Gates, an alternative more practical-meditational organization), and the *Pri Etz Chayyim* (on the Lurianic kavvanot — meditative intentions for prayer and the commandments). The Lurianic system reworks the entire Kabbalistic inheritance around three new central doctrines: *tzimtzum* (the contraction or self-withdrawal of Ein Sof to create a void in which finite reality could exist); *shevirat ha-kelim* (the breaking of the primal vessels meant to contain the divine light, scattering sparks of divinity into the realm of the shells / *kelippot*); and *tikkun* (the cosmic-and-historical work of restoration, in which Jewish prayer, commandment-observance, and intentional meditation gather the scattered sparks back to their source). The Lurianic system is the single most influential modern Kabbalistic theology.

## Primary text — selected excerpts

There is no full public-domain English translation of Vital's *Etz Chayyim* or the *Shemonah She'arim*. Two partial scholarly translations exist for selected sections — Donald Wilder Menzi and Zwe Padeh's *The Tree of Life: Chayyim Vital's Introduction to the Kabbalah of Isaac Luria* (Jason Aronson, 1999) covering *Sha'ar ha-Hakdamot* and the opening *heichalot*; and Yaakov Travis's selected translations. Aryeh Kaplan's *Inner Space* (Moznaim, 1990) summarizes Lurianic doctrine in English with primary-text quotations. The excerpts below are short fair-use renderings paraphrased with reference to Menzi-Padeh 1999 and to the Hebrew of *Etz Chayyim, Heichal Adam Kadmon, Sha'ar 1, anaf 2* (the "tzimtzum" passage). For any serious scholarly use consult Menzi-Padeh and Fine 2003.

### *Etz Chayyim*, Heichal Adam Kadmon, Sha'ar 1, anaf 2 — the doctrine of *tzimtzum*

The central Lurianic innovation. Before creation, Ein Sof (the Infinite) filled all reality. Creation required that the Infinite withdraw itself into itself, leaving a hollow space (*makom panui*) into which a thin line (*kav*) of divine light could enter and through which finite reality could emerge. Note the daring inversion: God's *first* creative act is self-withdrawal, not self-expression.

> Know that before the emanations were emanated and the creations were created, the supernal simple light filled all of existence. There was no empty place, no aspect of empty space or void; rather, all was filled with that simple, infinite light. There was no aspect of beginning and no aspect of end; rather, all was one simple, evenly-distributed light, called the *Or Ein Sof* (Light of the Infinite).
>
> When it arose in His simple will to create the worlds and to emanate the emanations, in order to bring to actuality the perfection of His deeds and His Names — for this was the cause of the creation of the worlds — behold, He contracted Himself, in the middle point of His light, in the very center, and the light contracted and withdrew to the sides surrounding this middle point. And there remained a vacant place, an empty space and void, around the precise middle point.
>
> This contraction (*tzimtzum*) was equal on all sides around that empty middle point, so that the empty space was perfectly round on every side, equally so... After this contraction took place, when there was a space and empty void in the precise middle of the light of the Ein Sof, a place was made in which the emanations, creations, formations, and actions could come into being. He then drew down from the *Or Ein Sof* a single, straight line, from His encompassing light into the empty space, and through this line the worlds were brought into being.

Citation: Chaim Vital, *Etz Chayyim*, *Heichal Adam Kadmon*, Gate 1 (*Sha'ar ha-Tzimtzum*), branch 2; paraphrased with reference to Menzi-Padeh 1999, pp. 1–22, and Fine 2003, pp. 130–149.

### *Etz Chayyim*, Heichal Nikudim, Sha'ar 8 — the breaking of the vessels

The second great Lurianic cosmogonic event. The first emanated vessels — the seven lower Sefirot in the original "World of Points" (*Olam ha-Nikudim*) — could not contain the intensity of the divine light. They shattered. Their shards fell into the lower realms, carrying sparks of divine light that became trapped among the demonic shells (*kelippot*).

> When the light came forth into the vessels of the Sefirot in their original arrangement, the vessels of the seven lower Sefirot from Chesed downward — Chesed, Gevurah, Tiferet, Netzach, Hod, Yesod, and Malkhut — were unable to contain the abundant divine light that streamed into them. They broke and shattered, and the sparks of light fell down with the broken shards into the world beneath.
>
> Some of those sparks remained suspended in the supernal realm. Others fell with the shells to the place of the *kelippot*, and from there to the lower worlds of formation, action, and finally the physical world... Wherever a spark fell, there an evil shell formed around it, like a husk around a seed. The work of the human soul, through the commandments and through the proper intentions of prayer, is to separate the sparks from the husks and elevate them, gathering them back to their root in the upper world. This is the work of *tikkun*.

Citation: Chaim Vital, *Etz Chayyim*, *Heichal Nikudim*, Gate of the Breaking of the Vessels, branches 1–2; paraphrased with reference to Menzi-Padeh 1999 supplemental material, Fine 2003, pp. 134–149, and the standard Hebrew edition Korec 1782 / Warsaw 1891.

### *Etz Chayyim*, *Heichal Adam Kadmon*, on the supernal Adam

Adam Kadmon ("Primordial Man") is the first emanation into the empty space created by tzimtzum — a vast cosmic figure containing within itself all the worlds. The structure is recognizably Sethian and Hermetic in shape (the *Anthropos* myth) and was noticed as such by Christian Kabbalists including Knorr von Rosenroth.

> After the tzimtzum and the entry of the straight line of light into the empty space, the first being to emerge there was Adam Kadmon — the Primordial Adam. From his ears, his nose, his mouth, his eyes, and his forehead came forth lights which became the worlds. From his ears came the world of Atzilut (Emanation) at its highest level; from his nose and his mouth, lower aspects of Atzilut; from his eyes, the Sefirot of the world of Nikudim, which was to break.
>
> Adam Kadmon is the prototype and root of all that is below. Every human being below is patterned after Adam Kadmon above; every soul has its root in some specific part of his cosmic body, and through that root the soul is connected to the work of cosmic restoration in that part.

Citation: Chaim Vital, *Etz Chayyim*, *Heichal Adam Kadmon*, on the emergence of Adam Kadmon and his lights; paraphrased with reference to Menzi-Padeh 1999 and Fine 2003. The Sha'ar ha-Gilgulim treats individual souls' roots in Adam Kadmon's anatomy in elaborate detail.

### *Sha'ar ha-Gilgulim* opening — the doctrine of reincarnation

The most popular Lurianic teaching, summarized at the opening of the *Gate of Reincarnations*. Souls return in successive lives to complete the *mitzvot* they failed to complete; through reincarnation the human soul participates in cosmic restoration.

> Know that all souls are rooted in Adam Kadmon and were included in the soul of the first Adam below. When Adam sinned, all the souls fell with him, mixed with the kelippot. Henceforth every soul must undergo the work of restoration, completing what it lacks, until it returns purified to its root above. A soul that has not completed in one lifetime what it was sent to do must return — this is the secret of *gilgul* (reincarnation).
>
> One soul may be reincarnated many times until it completes its task. Sometimes several sparks of soul are joined in a single body; sometimes a soul that has nearly completed its work is joined to one beginning its work, to assist it — this is the secret of *ibbur* (impregnation).

Citation: Chaim Vital, *Sha'ar ha-Gilgulim*, hakdamah 1; paraphrased with reference to the Yitzchok Bar-Lev 1996 partial English translation and the standard Hebrew edition. Scholem 1991 in *On the Mystical Shape of the Godhead* gives the definitive scholarly analysis of this doctrine.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Ottoman Galilee under Selim II and Murad III; Safed at its 16th-century apex as the Sephardic mystical capital.
- **Religious milieu.** The Safed Kabbalistic circle including Joseph Karo, Solomon Alkabetz, Moses Cordovero (d. 1570), Moses Alshich, and — for two years before his sudden death in a 1572 epidemic — Luria. Cordovero had just died; Luria emerged as the new theological center.
- **Who was behind it.** Luria himself lived in Egypt (Cairo) for fifteen years (~1555–1570) immersed in Kabbalistic study before arriving at Safed; his teaching emerged in pneumatic flashes, in interpretation of the Zohar, and through his students' detailed recording. Chaim Vital was the canonical recorder; Israel Sarug developed a separate "Sarugian" Lurianic system that became dominant in European Christian-Kabbalistic reception.
- **Discovery / transmission.** The manuscript transmission was deliberately restricted during the lifetime of Luria's students (Vital tried to control access); leakage and unauthorized copying began in the 17th century, and the corpus was widely manuscript-circulated by the time of the Sabbatean crisis of 1666. Print publication of the central texts begins in 1648 (Sarug-Bacharach) and reaches the Vital recension in 1782 (Korec edition).

## Themes and motifs
- [[tzimtzum]] — the central cosmogonic innovation: the infinite divine *withdraws* to make room for the finite. The doctrine generates a new theological grammar in which divine absence is the condition of creation.
- [[shevirat-hakelim]] — the breaking of the primal vessels: a catastrophe internal to the divine emanation, in which the lower seven Sefirot's containers shatter under the force of divine light, scattering sparks into the material world and into the shells of evil.
- [[tikkun-olam]] — the restoration: every commandment, every prayer-intention (*kavvanah*), every Sabbath-observance gathers a spark. The historical experience of exile becomes the structure of cosmic theology.
- **Partsufim** — the divine countenances (Arikh Anpin / Long Face = Keter; Abba / Father = Chokhmah; Imma / Mother = Binah; Zeir Anpin / Small Face = the six middle Sefirot; Nukva / Female = Malkhut), through which the Lurianic system reorganizes the Sefirotic structure into a more dynamic, gendered, and narrative cosmology.
- **Reincarnation (*gilgul*)** — Luria's *Sha'ar ha-Gilgulim* (Gate of Reincarnations) systematizes Jewish reincarnation doctrine; souls of historical figures are traced through successive lives.

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-6-011-cordovero-pardes-rimmonim]] — *direct-displacement / dialogue*: Luria knew the Cordoveran system; the Lurianic cosmogony reworks it with the mythic-narrative elements (tzimtzum, breaking, restoration) Cordovero did not develop.
- → [[event-jewish-expulsion-spain-1492]] — *historical-context*: the standard scholarly reading (Scholem) makes Lurianic Kabbalah the Sephardic theological digestion of the Expulsion experience; the cosmos itself is in exile.
- → Zohar (Phase 5) — *commentary-on*: Luria's teaching is presented as the true reading of the Zohar; Vital's writings include extensive Zoharic exegesis.
- → [[phase-6-025-nathan-of-gaza-treatise-on-dragons]] — *direct-influence / heretical-transformation*: Nathan of Gaza reads Shabbatai Tzvi's biography through Lurianic categories; the Sabbatean theology of the messiah-among-the-kelippot is Lurianic theology pushed to a heterodox conclusion.
- → Hasidism (Phase 6 late) — *direct-influence*: the Baal Shem Tov's circle integrates Lurianic categories into a populist devotional theology.
- → Christian Kabbalah / Knorr von Rosenroth's *Kabbala Denudata* (1677–1684) — *transmission-into-Christian-discourse*: extensive Lurianic translations and analyses make Lurianic theology accessible to 17th–18th-c. European Christian scholars including Henry More and Leibniz.

## Disputes
- **The Scholem thesis: 1492 → Lurianic Kabbalah.** Scholem argued that Lurianic cosmogony (tzimtzum-breaking-tikkun) is theologically driven by the experience of the Spanish expulsion. Idel and others have contested the directness of the link, noting that the central doctrines have proto-Lurianic precedents and that the time-lag (~80 years) is substantial.
- **Vital vs. Sarug vs. Tabul.** The three Lurianic recensions diverge on important points; reconstruction of "the historical Luria" is genuinely difficult. Yosef Avivi's three-volume Hebrew study is the modern critical effort.
- **Was Luria literate?** Vital reports that Luria himself wrote little; the famous "I cannot, because the source is too abundant for the pen" is taken seriously by scholars as a real cognitive description, not just hagiography.
