# Pardes Rimmonim (Orchard of Pomegranates)

## What it is
Moses Cordovero's monumental systematic exposition of Kabbalah, composed at Safed in 1548 when its author was only twenty-six. Organized into thirty-two *she'arim* (gates), the work systematizes the Zoharic and earlier Kabbalistic tradition: the doctrine of Ein Sof (the unknowable Infinite); the emergence of the ten Sefirot as divine emanations; the relations of the Sefirot among themselves (the *partsufim* / divine countenances were further developed in Cordovero's later *Or Yakar*, his thirteen-volume Zohar commentary); the doctrine of divine names; the place of evil within the divine system; the structure of the lower worlds and the human ascent. With the *Or Yakar* commentary on the Zohar (sixteen vols. in modern publication), the *Pardes* is the systematic monument of pre-Lurianic Kabbalah.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Ottoman Palestine; Safed in the Galilee under Sultan Süleyman I had become, in the wake of the 1492 expulsion from Spain and 1497 expulsion from Portugal, the principal Sephardic refugee and rabbinic center. The economic base of Safed in the mid-16th century was the wool trade.
- **Religious milieu.** The Safed Kabbalistic renaissance — Joseph Karo (*Shulchan Aruch* 1565), Solomon Alkabetz (*Lekha Dodi*), Cordovero, Moses Alshich, and (after Cordovero's death) Isaac Luria — making Safed for two generations the most intense site of Jewish mystical creativity since the Zohar circle.
- **Who was behind it.** Cordovero personally, a student of Joseph Karo (in halakhah) and Solomon Alkabetz (in Kabbalah); his own students included Isaac Luria (briefly, 1569–1570) and Chaim Vital.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Manuscript circulation during Cordovero's lifetime; print at Cracow 1591–1592. The systematic Cordoveran exposition was partly displaced by the Lurianic system after 1570, but Cordovero remained authoritative — the Hasidic movement of the 18th century returned to Cordoveran categories alongside Lurianic ones, and modern scholarly Kabbalah (Scholem, Tishby, Idel, Sack) treats the *Pardes* as the indispensable systematic text.

## Themes and motifs
- [[sefirot]] — the ten emanations articulated with unprecedented systematicity; Cordovero's most original move is the doctrine of *behinot* (aspects), in which each Sefirah contains structured aspects of all the others, generating an internal differentiation in the Sefirotic system.
- [[hidden-god]] — Ein Sof, beyond the Sefirot, beyond name, beyond predication.
- **Emanation (*atzilut*)** — the descent from Ein Sof through Keter into the differentiated Sefirot; Cordovero's emanation theology is continuous with medieval Neoplatonist emanation but operates on a Kabbalistic letter-and-name structure.
- [[creation-by-word]] — the letters of the divine name as creative principles, inherited from Sefer Yetzirah.
- **Evil within God?** — Cordovero's nuanced treatment of *Gevurah* (Strength / Severity / Judgment) as the root of evil within the Sefirotic structure itself — a doctrine that Luria will radicalize into the *Shevirat ha-Kelim*.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Zohar (Phase 5) — *commentary-on / systematization-of*: Cordovero's lifetime project was the Zohar; the *Or Yakar* is the longest and most authoritative pre-modern Zohar commentary.
- → [[phase-6-015-luria-vital-etz-chayyim]] — *direct-influence / displacement*: Luria knew the Cordoveran system, studied with Cordovero briefly, and developed his alternative (tzimtzum, shevirat ha-kelim, partsufim, tikkun) partly in dialogue with it. The two systems coexist in subsequent Kabbalistic transmission.
- → [[phase-6-004-pico-heptaplus-conclusiones-cabalisticae]] — *parallel*: Pico's Christian Kabbalah (1486–1489) and Cordovero's Jewish systematization (1548) are contemporary attempts to organize the Zoharic inheritance, from inside and outside the tradition respectively.
- → [[event-jewish-expulsion-spain-1492]] — *historical-context*: Safed Kabbalah is the Sephardic post-expulsion mystical response.
- → Hasidism (Phase 6 later) — *direct-influence*: Hasidic theology integrates Cordoveran and Lurianic categories.

## Disputes
- **Cordovero vs. Luria.** Scholem framed the relationship as Cordoveran *systematization* superseded by Lurianic *mythological-cosmogonic radicalization*. Idel and Sack have complicated this picture by showing the continuities and the long Cordoveran afterlife.
- **Christian Kabbalist reception.** Whether the Christian Kabbalists of the 16th–17th c. (Reuchlin, Postel, Knorr) actually read Cordovero or only the earlier Zoharic-Gikatillan stratum is debated; Knorr's *Kabbala Denudata* (1677–1684) does engage Cordoveran material.
