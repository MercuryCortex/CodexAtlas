# Mutus Liber (*The Mute Book*)

## What it is
A **wordless** illustrated alchemical treatise — 15 engraved plates with virtually no text — first published at La Rochelle in 1677 by the pseudonymous author "Altus" (probably the Protestant alchemist-physician **Isaac Baulot** of La Rochelle). The book's complete title declares the project: ***Mutus Liber, in quo tamen tota philosophia hermetica figuris hieroglyphicis depingitur*** — *The Mute Book, in which nevertheless the entire Hermetic philosophy is depicted in hieroglyphic figures*. The work shows two alchemists (a man and a woman — often read as the *adept* and *soror mystica*) progressing through the stages of the alchemical *opus*, with iconographic conventions inherited from the prior 16th-17th-c. illustrated-alchemy tradition.

**One of the canonical late-17th-c. Hermetic emblem works** held in the Bibliotheca Philosophica Hermetica, Yale Beinecke Mellon, and other major Hermetic collections.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Late-17th-c. Protestant French alchemy in La Rochelle (a Huguenot Atlantic-port city, eight years before the 1685 Revocation of the Edict of Nantes ended legal French Protestantism). The wordless format is part-strategic: a non-verbal book carries less Inquisitorial risk than a written one.
- **Iconography.** Each of the 15 plates contains numerous discrete iconographic elements (alchemical apparatus, sun-moon-stars, angels, the working couple, biblical scenes). The 14th plate is signed *F. Hali, etc.* — variously interpreted.

## Themes and motifs
- [[alchemy-as-spiritual-discipline]] — paradigmatic instance; the work is *wordless* precisely because alchemy in this register is held to communicate beyond discursive language.
- [[alchemical-marriage]] / [[coniunctio]] — the working couple is one of the strongest *adept-and-soror-mystica* iconographic instances.
