# Zwölf Schlüssel / Twelve Keys

## Overview

"**Basil Valentine**" — almost certainly a pseudonym for an unknown early 17th-century German alchemist — wrote under the name of a supposed 15th-century Benedictine prior of Erfurt, a persona that gave the texts an air of antiquity and monastic authority. The *Twelve Keys* (*Zwölf Schlüssel*) is a sequence of twelve illustrated emblems, each describing a stage of the alchemical Great Work accompanied by explanatory verse and prose. It is among the most reproduced alchemical emblem sequences of the 17th century.

## Antimony and the Chariot

Basil Valentine is also known for the *Triumphant Chariot of Antimony* — the foundational Renaissance text on antimony as a medical-alchemical agent. The *Chariot* introduced antimony (stibium) as a powerful purifying agent in Paracelsian medicine, generating the great 17th-century antimony controversy: orthodox Galenic physicians condemned it, Paracelsian-alchemical physicians championed it. Newton's alchemical notebooks show detailed engagement with the antimony chemistry Basil Valentine describes.

## Twelve Keys Structure

Each key corresponds to a stage of the Work, illustrated with a complex emblem:
- Keys 1–3: Preparation of matter (the king, the queen, the marriage of sun and moon)
- Keys 4–6: Putrefaction, fermentation, the black crow
- Keys 7–9: Whitening (*albedo*) — the resurrection
- Keys 10–12: Reddening (*rubedo*) and final Projection

The imagery of Keys 10–12 influenced Maier's *Atalanta Fugiens* (1618) and the broader emblematic tradition.
