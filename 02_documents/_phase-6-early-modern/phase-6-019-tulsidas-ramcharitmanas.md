# Ramcharitmanas

## What it is
Tulsidas's monumental Awadhi-Hindi retelling of the Rāma story, composed 1574–1576, organized like Valmiki's Sanskrit *Ramayana* into seven *kāṇḍa* (books) but in vernacular meters (*chaupai* and *doha*) and from the standpoint of *bhakti* devotion. Where Valmiki's Rama is a Kshatriya hero and (only ambiguously) divine, Tulsi's Rama is unambiguously God incarnate — Vishnu's *avatara* — and the entire epic is structured as a devotional aid for the worshipper. The work integrates the Sanskrit *Ramayana* tradition (Valmiki), the *Adhyatma Ramayana* (a 15th-century Sanskrit *bhakti*-philosophical Ramayana), Saiva inflections (Tulsi narrates the story partly through Shiva-and-Parvati frame conversations), and the broader bhakti devotional grammar. The Manas became and remains the central religious text of the North Indian Hindi-speaking Vaishnava world.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Mughal India under Akbar (r. 1556–1605); a period of relative religious tolerance and active Sanskrit-Persian-vernacular literary exchange.
- **Religious milieu.** North Indian bhakti renaissance ~1500–1700. Tulsidas works in the Ramanandi tradition (descended from the 15th-century reformer Ramananda), which combined Sanskritic Vaishnava theology with the popular sant-bhakti devotional grammar. Tulsi's contemporaries Surdas (Krishna-bhakti, Braj) and Mirabai (Krishna-bhakti, Rajasthani) work in the parallel Krishna register.
- **Who was behind it.** Tulsidas personally; the traditional biography (Priyadas's *Bhaktirasabodhini*, ~1712) and Tulsi's own remarks place him in long residences at Ayodhya and Varanasi.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous oral and manuscript transmission from composition; the Manas became foundational for *Ramlila* (the cyclic Rama-play festival, especially the famous Ramnagar Ramlila), for daily devotional recitation, and for the broader Hindi literary canon. The Gita Press Gorakhpur Hindi edition (1923) is the most-printed Hindu religious book of the 20th century.

## Themes and motifs
- [[bhakti-devotion]] — Tulsi's Rama is the personal Lord; devotion is the explicit soteriological frame.
- [[avatar-doctrine]] — Rama as Vishnu's full *avatara*; the *Bālakāṇḍa* opens with the cosmic frame in which Vishnu descends in response to the cosmic-dharmic crisis.
- [[divine-name]] — *Ram-nam* (the name "Ram") as itself salvific; Tulsi frames the chanting of the name as the easiest and most powerful spiritual practice for the *Kali Yuga*.
- **Sita** — Tulsi's Sita is the model of *bhakti*-devoted wife and the divine *shakti* aspect; the *Sundarakāṇḍa* sequence with Hanuman is among the most beloved devotional passages.
- **Hanuman as model devotee** — Hanuman is the model *bhakta*; Tulsi's separate composition *Hanuman Chalisa* is among the most widely recited Hindu devotional texts.

- → Valmiki's *Ramayana* (Phase 2, [[phase-2-017-mahabharata-ramayana-oral-layers]]) — *redaction-of / vernacular-retelling*: structurally a translation-into-Awadhi of the Sanskrit, but theologically transformed by bhakti reframing.
- → Adhyatma Ramayana (Phase 5, to be seeded) — *direct-influence*: this 15th-century Sanskrit *bhakti*-philosophical Ramayana is Tulsi's most direct proximate source.
- → Bhagavata Purana (Phase 5) — *parallel-bhakti*: theological framework.
- → [[phase-6-013-mirabai-bhajans]] — *contemporary-parallel*: Mirabai's Krishna-bhakti in Rajasthani.
- → [[phase-6-020-chaitanya-charitamrita]] — *contemporary-parallel*: Chaitanya's Bengali Krishna-bhakti.
- → modern Indian religious-political imagination — *foundational*: Gandhi reads the Manas daily; the *Ram-rajya* ideal mediates between the Manas and modern Indian political theology.

## Disputes
- **Date of composition.** Tulsi states he began on Ram Navami 1574; some scholars place the completion as late as 1577. Internal dating consistent with the standard range.
- **Tulsi's relation to Akbar.** Tradition reports a meeting; historicity unverified.
- **Tulsi's sectarian alignment.** Whether Tulsi is strictly Ramanandi or operates more independently is a long-running debate.
