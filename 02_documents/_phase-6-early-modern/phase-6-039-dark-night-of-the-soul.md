# Dark Night of the Soul (*Noche Oscura del Alma*)

## What it is
A prose-theological commentary by [[john-of-the-cross|John of the Cross]] on his own poem *En una noche oscura* (~1578), composed in stages 1578–1585, never finished (the commentary stops mid-second-book). The work is **paired with the *Ascent of Mount Carmel*** (*Subida del Monte Carmelo*, the earlier paired commentary) — together they constitute John's systematic apophatic-mystical account of the soul's purification on its journey toward union with God. The *Ascent* describes the *active* night (what the soul does to purify itself); the *Dark Night* describes the *passive* night (what God does to the soul that the soul cannot do for itself).

## Context
- **Biographical anchor.** John composed the early stanzas of the *Cántico* during his Toledo imprisonment (1577–1578); the poem *En una noche oscura* was probably written shortly after his escape. The prose *Dark Night* dates from ~1578–1585, during his Andalusian period.
- **Religious milieu.** Post-Tridentine Spanish Catholic mysticism; the Discalced-Calced Carmelite conflict; the Spanish Inquisition's monitoring of mystical writings (the *Alumbrados* / "Illuminist" condemnations of 1525, 1574, etc., made vernacular mystical theology institutionally suspect).
- **Theological-pastoral horizon.** Practical-pastoral for Discalced Carmelite spiritual direction. John works systematically through stages — including diagnostic criteria for distinguishing the *dark night* from melancholy, from spiritual indifference, from temptation.
