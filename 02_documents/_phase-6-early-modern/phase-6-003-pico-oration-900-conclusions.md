# Oration on the Dignity of Man + 900 Conclusions

## What it is
Two paired works by the 23-year-old Giovanni Pico della Mirandola, composed in 1486 for a planned public disputation at Rome: (1) the *Conclusiones nongentae* — nine hundred theses drawn from Latin scholastic, Arabic, Greek philosophical, Hermetic, Chaldean, Pythagorean, and Kabbalistic sources, organized into thematic blocks and presented for defense against any comer; and (2) the *Oratio* (later titled *De hominis dignitate*) — the preface speech to that disputation, which argues from a creation-narrative reworking that the human is the one being whose nature is *unfixed*: God set humans at the center of the cosmos and gave them the power to descend toward brutes or ascend toward angels by free choice. The Oration is one of the most celebrated short texts of the Renaissance; the 900 Conclusions are the operational program that the Oration was meant to launch.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Italy under late-Quattrocento humanist patronage; Florence (Lorenzo de' Medici), Rome (Innocent VIII), and Pico's own city of Mirandola.
- **Religious milieu.** Catholic orthodoxy was prepared to tolerate Christianizing Platonism (as in Ficino's *Theologia*) but reacted sharply to Pico's inclusion of Kabbalistic and Hermetic theses with strong-sounding Christological claims (e.g., that no science demonstrates the divinity of Christ better than magic and Kabbalah — Conclusion 9.9). Innocent VIII appointed a commission of inquiry in March 1487 which condemned thirteen of the 900 conclusions; Pico's *Apologia* (1487) defending them was treated as defiance, and the entire 900 was condemned by papal bull *Et si iniuncto nobis* in August 1487. Pico fled to France, was briefly imprisoned at Vincennes, and was eventually rehabilitated by Alexander VI in 1493.
- **Who was behind it.** Pico personally — but with substantial support from his Jewish teacher Flavius Mithridates (Guglielmo Raimondo Moncada), a converso who translated Kabbalistic texts (Sefer ha-Zohar excerpts, Sefer Yetzirah, Recanati's commentary on the Pentateuch) into Latin for Pico in 1486; Yohanan Alemanno also taught him Hebrew Kabbalah at Florence. Wirszubski (1989) traced the textual transmission.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous early-modern transmission of the printed 900 Conclusions and the 1496 Oration; the works founded the Christian Kabbalist tradition that runs through Reuchlin, Egidio of Viterbo, Knorr von Rosenroth, and into the broader esoteric current.

## Themes and motifs
- [[prisca-theologia]] — Pico extends Ficino's chain by adding the Chaldean Oracles (attributed to Zoroaster), the Orphic hymns, the Pythagorean *Carmina Aurea*, and — crucially — Kabbalah, claimed as Mosaic oral tradition preserved by the Jews.
- [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — explicit Hermetic-Kabbalistic claim that magic and Kabbalah demonstrate Christ better than Aristotle does (the most provocative thesis).
- [[soul-immortality]] / [[ascent-of-the-soul]] — the Oration's central claim that the human can ascend to the angelic by intellectual-mystical practice.
- [[hidden-god]] — the apophatic via Pseudo-Dionysius and Maimonides.
- **Free will / indeterminacy** — the Oration's most quoted move: God places Adam at the center and gives him no fixed nature, so that he may shape himself.

## Disputes
- **Were the condemned theses genuinely heterodox?** Catholic apologists then and since have read them as misunderstood orthodox claims; Farmer (1998) argues they were a deliberately maximalist syncretist provocation.
- **The Kabbalistic sources.** Wirszubski's reconstruction of Flavius Mithridates' Latin Kabbalistic translations (Pico's source) has been extended by Giulio Busi and others; the chain of textual transmission is now largely documented.
- **Originality.** How much of Pico's Kabbalah is Pico vs. Mithridates is debated.
