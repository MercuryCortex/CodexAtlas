# Theatrum Chemicum Britannicum

## Overview

**Elias Ashmole** (1617–1692) — astrologer, antiquarian, Herald, Freemason, and collector — compiled the *Theatrum Chemicum Britannicum* (1652) as the first systematic anthology of **English alchemical poetry**, deliberately framing it as the canonical expression of a distinctively English Hermetic tradition. The anthology contains works by George Ripley (the centrepiece), Thomas Norton's *Ordinal of Alchemy* (1477), John Dastin, William Blomfild, Chaucer's *Canon's Yeoman's Tale* (as evidence that Chaucer was an initiate), and over twenty other English alchemical authors.

## Ashmole's Significance in the Transmission Chain

Ashmole sits precisely at the junction between three traditions:
1. **English medieval alchemy** — he preserved and canonized Ripley, Norton, and the 15th-century English alchemical tradition
2. **Rosicrucian Hermeticism** — the *Theatrum* was conceived in the milieu of English Rosicrucianism; Ashmole corresponded with English Rosicrucian networks and translated the Rosicrucian manifestos
3. **Speculative Freemasonry** — Ashmole's diary records the earliest known account of a speculative (non-operative) Masonic initiation in England: he was made a Mason at Warrington Lodge on 16 October 1646 — six years before publishing the *Theatrum*

The *Theatrum* is therefore the text that most directly links the alchemical and Masonic transmission chains in England. Anderson's *Constitutions of Free-Masons* (1723) draws on the Hermetic-alchemical tradition that Ashmole assembled, and the Masonic myth of the *ars geometrica* as the royal art echoes Ashmole's myth of alchemy as the English royal tradition.

## Contents (selected)

- George Ripley: *The Compound of Alchemy* (the 12 Gates), *Verses upon the Elixir*, *Medulla Alchimiae*
- Thomas Norton: *The Ordinal of Alchimy* (1477) — the other canonical English alchemical poem
- John Dastin: *The Rosary of the Philosophers*
- William Blomfild: *Blomfild's Blossoms*
- Geoffrey Chaucer: *The Canon's Yeoman's Tale* (presented as evidence Chaucer was an alchemist)
- Anonymous *Hunting of the Greene Lyon*
- 12+ other texts
