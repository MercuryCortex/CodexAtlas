# De Arte Cabalistica

## What it is
Reuchlin's three-book dialogue on Kabbalah, written 1517 and dedicated to Pope Leo X, in which a Jew (Simon), a Muslim (Marranus), and a Pythagorean (Philolaus) meet at Frankfurt to discuss Kabbalah as the science of "the Messiah." The dialogue presents Kabbalah systematically — Sefirot, divine names, gematria, notarikon, temurah — and culminates in Reuchlin's claim that the Tetragrammaton YHWH becomes pronounceable by inserting the letter shin to yield YHShVH (Yeshua / Jesus), making Christ the realization of Kabbalistic mysticism. With his earlier *De Verbo Mirifico* (1494), this is the founding systematic statement of Christian Kabbalah; where Pico had been programmatic and aphoristic, Reuchlin is methodical and pedagogical.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** German Empire under Maximilian I; the year is 1517 — the same year as Luther's 95 Theses. Reuchlin is an established humanist and jurist in his sixties.
- **Religious milieu.** The Reuchlin Affair (1510–1520) had been raging: the converso Johann Pfefferkorn, backed by the Dominicans of Cologne, had demanded the confiscation of all Jewish books in the Empire; Reuchlin had been the lone Christian authority to defend the Talmud and Kabbalah from suppression. His defense (*Augenspiegel*, 1511) was condemned by Cologne; the case went to Rome where Leo X eventually ruled (1520) against Reuchlin but the controversy galvanized German humanists (the satirical *Letters of Obscure Men* of 1515–1517 emerged in his defense). *De Arte Cabalistica*, dedicated to Leo, is in part a final apologetic.
- **Who was behind it.** Reuchlin himself, drawing on his lifetime collection of Hebrew Kabbalistic manuscripts, his Hebrew study with Jacob ben Jehiel Loans (physician to Frederick III) and Obadiah Sforno, and his absorption of Pico's program.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous early-modern Christian-Kabbalistic transmission via Pistorius's 1587 anthology and Knorr von Rosenroth's *Kabbala Denudata* (1677–1684); read by Agrippa, Postel, Boehme indirectly, Henry More, and Athanasius Kircher.

## Themes and motifs
- [[sefirot]] — systematic exposition with Christological mapping.
- [[prisca-theologia]] — Pythagoras is brought into Kabbalah (a Reuchlin-specific move): Pythagorean arithmology and Kabbalistic gematria are claimed as cognate sciences.
- **Tetragrammaton mysticism** — the central Christianizing move: Pentagrammaton YHShVH.
- [[creation-by-word]] — Kabbalistic letter-cosmogony (Sefer Yetzirah).
- [[hidden-god]] — Ein Sof above the Sefirot.

## Disputes
- **Tetragrammaton-Pentagrammaton.** The YHShVH derivation is a Christian construction with no support in Jewish Kabbalistic tradition; Jewish Kabbalists then and now regard it as a confessional imposition.
- **How much Hebrew did Reuchlin actually know?** His Hebrew grammar (*De Rudimentis Hebraicis*, 1506) was the first by a non-Jewish author; competent but not native. His Kabbalistic reading depended substantially on his Jewish teachers and on the Pichian sources.
