# The Words of the Lord (Jacob Frank)

## What it is
**The Words of the Lord** (*Słowa Pańskie*) is the collection of sayings, dreams, and teachings of **Jacob Frank** (1726–1791), the radical Sabbatian who proclaimed himself the messiah and the reincarnation of Sabbatai Zevi, recorded in Polish by his disciples during his years at Brno and Offenbach. The teachings push Sabbatian **antinomianism** to its extreme: that in the era of redemption the old Torah is annulled, that salvation comes through descent into the abyss and the transgression of every law ("the way to life passes through nothingness"), and that Frank himself leads his followers through apostasy — culminating in the mass Frankist **conversion to Catholicism** (1759–60) while secretly retaining their messianic faith. The *Words* are gnomic, deliberately shocking, and saturated with a mythology of a hidden Maiden (the *Shekhinah*/Virgin) and the "Big Brother" power Frank claimed to serve.

## Significance
The Words of the Lord is the central document of the Frankist movement, the most extreme and best-documented outgrowth of the Sabbatian messianism that the prophet [[phase-6-025-nathan-of-gaza-treatise-on-dragons]] had theologized a century earlier. It is a key primary source for the study of antinomian, "redemption-through-sin" messianism — a phenomenon Gershom Scholem placed at the hidden hinge between traditional Judaism and Jewish modernity. The standard modern study is Maciejko's.
