# Interior Castle (*Castillo Interior* / *Las Moradas*)

## What it is
[[teresa-of-avila|Teresa of Ávila's]] mystical-theological masterwork, composed in two months (~June–November 1577) under direct command from her confessor Gracián. **The most systematic spatial-architectural account of mystical-spiritual development in the Western Catholic tradition.** The treatise describes the soul as an **interior castle** built of diamond / crystal — the soul itself is the castle, and the *King* (God / the Trinity) dwells at the innermost chamber. The soul's progress is described as movement through **seven sets of mansions** (*moradas*), each more interior than the last, culminating in the **seventh-mansion** experience of *unitive marriage* with the Trinity.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Post-Tridentine Catholic Reformation Spain under Philip II. Teresa was navigating the politics of the Calced-Discalced Carmelite conflict (which would culminate in [[john-of-the-cross|John of the Cross's]] imprisonment in Toledo 1577–1578 — *while* Teresa was composing the *Castillo*).
- **Theological-pastoral horizon.** Written for her Discalced nuns as a *practical-pastoral* spiritual itinerary. Teresa explicitly says she would have preferred to teach orally; the book exists because Gracián commanded it.
- **Inquisitorial context.** Teresa's *Vida* had been in Inquisitorial custody since 1574; she was writing *Castillo* under shadow. The cautious-prudent-orthodox tone is partly defensive.

## Themes and motifs
- [[bridal-mysticism]] — the soul's seventh-mansion *desposorio espiritual* (spiritual marriage) is the culminating bridal-imagery.
- [[soul-as-bride]] — explicit throughout.
- [[divine-immanence]] — Teresa's central architectural metaphor is *divine immanence* — God *within* the soul rather than separated from it.
- [[apophatic-mysticism]] — though Teresa is more kataphatic-imaginative than John of the Cross, the *Castillo*'s sixth and seventh mansions describe genuinely apophatic experiences.

## Disputes
- **Original vs. derivative.** McGinn 2022 strongly defends Teresa's theological originality; older readings (esp. early-20th-c. comparative-mysticism literature) tended to subordinate her to broader trends.
- **Converso-Jewish influences.** Some modern readings see traces of Jewish-mystical / converso intellectual heritage in the *Castillo*'s architectural-spiritual metaphor; the strong-Kabbalah-connection thesis is contested.
