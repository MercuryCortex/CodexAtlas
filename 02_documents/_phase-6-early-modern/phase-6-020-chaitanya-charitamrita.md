# Chaitanya Charitamrita + Shikshashtakam

## What it is
Two complementary core texts of Gaudiya Vaishnavism. The *Shikshashtakam* ("Eight Verses of Instruction") is the only composition traditionally attributed to Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (1486–1534) himself — eight Sanskrit shlokas summarizing the spiritual practice of *nama-sankirtana* (congregational chanting of the divine names), the progressive humility and surrender of the devotee, and the ecstatic-union goal in which the devotee desires nothing for self but only to serve Krishna. The *Chaitanya Charitamrita* is Krishnadasa Kaviraja's three-part Bengali biography-and-theology of Chaitanya, composed at Vrindavan c. 1612–1615 — the most authoritative of the Bengali Chaitanya texts, combining hagiography, doctrinal exposition (drawing on the systematic Gaudiya theology of Rupa and Jiva Goswami), philosophical analysis (especially *achintya-bheda-abheda*, "inconceivable simultaneous oneness and difference"), and detailed commentary on the *Shikshashtakam*. The *Charitamrita* is the doctrinal canon of Gaudiya Vaishnavism, the tradition that becomes globally visible in the 20th century through ISKCON.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Mughal Bengal and Odisha during the 16th century; Chaitanya's lifetime spans the rule of Hussain Shah of Bengal (r. 1493–1519, religiously tolerant) and the Gajapati rulers of Odisha. Vrindavan, the deserted holy site of Krishna's childhood, was rediscovered and developed as a pilgrimage center by Chaitanya's Six Goswami disciples under Mughal grants in the 16th century.
- **Religious milieu.** The Bengal/Odisha Vaishnava revival ~1500–1700, parallel to the North Indian bhakti renaissance further west. Theologically Gaudiya Vaishnavism distinguishes itself from Shankara's Advaita (rejected as inadequate to bhakti) and from Ramanuja's and Madhva's Vedanta (revered but argued to be incomplete without the *bhakti-rasa* theology of Rupa Goswami's *Bhakti-rasāmṛta-sindhu*).
- **Who was behind it.** Chaitanya himself as the ecstatic-mystical center; the Six Goswamis of Vrindavan (especially Rupa, Sanatana, and Jiva) as the theological systematizers; Krishnadasa Kaviraja as the canonical hagiographer.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous Bengali Vaishnava transmission; the Gaudiya Math movement (Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati, late 19th–early 20th c.) reorganized the tradition; ISKCON (founded 1966 by Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada) globalized it.

## Themes and motifs
- [[bhakti-devotion]] — the *bhakti-rasa* system of Rupa Goswami, in which devotion is the supreme *rasa* (aesthetic-religious mood) with five primary forms (*shanta*, *dasya*, *sakhya*, *vatsalya*, *madhurya* — peace, servant-relation, friendship, parental, lover-relation), of which the *madhurya* of Radha is supreme.
- [[divine-feminine]] — Radha as the supreme devotee and as the *hladini-shakti* (bliss-energy) of Krishna; the Radha-Krishna polarity is the central theological-erotic image.
- [[divine-name]] — *Nama-sankirtana*, congregational chanting of "Hare Krishna," is the Gaudiya core practice; the *Shikshashtakam*'s first verse celebrates the chanting as the supreme practice of the age.
- [[avatar-doctrine]] — Chaitanya is identified by his disciples as Krishna himself adopting the role of devotee — the "combined" Radha-Krishna avatar (*yugala-avatara*); the most theologically distinctive Gaudiya claim.
- ***Achintya-bheda-abheda*** — Jiva Goswami's signature doctrine: the inconceivable simultaneous identity and difference of devotee and Krishna; a theological mediation between Advaita oneness and Madhva dualism.

## Connections (provisional)
- → Bhagavata Purana Book X (Phase 5) — *direct-source*: Chaitanya's Krishna is the Vrindavan-cowherd Krishna of the Bhagavata, with the Radha addition (which the Bhagavata itself does not name).
- → Jayadeva's *Gita Govinda* (Phase 5) — *direct-influence*: Radha-Krishna devotional poetics.
- → [[phase-6-013-mirabai-bhajans]] — *contemporary-parallel*: Rajasthani Krishna-bhakti. Tradition narrates a meeting between Mira and Chaitanya in Vrindavan; historicity unverified.
- → [[phase-6-019-tulsidas-ramcharitmanas]] — *contemporary-parallel*: the Hindi Ram-bhakti counterpart in the same broad religious renaissance.
- → modern ISKCON (Phase 7) — *direct-descendant*: the Gaudiya Math → ISKCON line is the principal modern channel of this tradition.

## Disputes
- **Dating of the *Charitamrita*.** Krishnadasa's own dating points to c. 1615; some scholars place it slightly earlier or later.
- **Authenticity of the *Shikshashtakam*.** Whether Chaitanya himself composed all eight verses or whether some are disciples' compositions is occasionally raised; tradition treats them as Chaitanya's.
- **Theological status of Radha.** Gaudiya Vaishnavism elevates Radha to a status not explicit in the Bhagavata; the Goswami theological systematization is doing substantive doctrinal work.
