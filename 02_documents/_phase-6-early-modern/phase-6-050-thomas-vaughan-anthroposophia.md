# Anthroposophia Theomagica / Lumen de Lumine / Aula Lucis

## Overview

**Thomas Vaughan** (1621–1666) — Welsh Anglican clergyman, alchemist, and Hermetic philosopher — wrote under the pen-name **Eugenius Philalethes** ("Happy Lover of Truth") to produce the most philosophically sophisticated body of English Rosicrucian literature. His twin brother was the poet Henry Vaughan, whose mystical verse (*Silex Scintillans*) was demonstrably shaped by Thomas's Hermetic publications.

## The Rosicrucian Translator

Vaughan published the first English translations of the two foundational Rosicrucian manifestos — the *Fama* and *Confessio* — as *Fame and Confession of the Fraternity of R.C.* (1652). This made the Rosicrucian program available to the English-speaking world for the first time, directly shaping the later 17th- and 18th-century English Hermetic tradition that fed into Freemasonry.

## The More-Vaughan Polemic

The *Anthroposophia Theomagica* (1650) immediately attracted a polemical response from Henry More, the Cambridge Platonist: *Observations upon Anthroposophia Theomagica and Anima Magica Abscondita* (1650). Vaughan replied with *The Man-Mouse Taken in a Trap* (1650); More counter-replied; Vaughan replied again. The exchange ran through 1651.

The debate is philosophically significant: More objected to Vaughan's assertion that the soul is material (a fine spiritual body), that divine light is physically present in matter, and that the alchemical work is a literal engagement with cosmic spiritual forces. More wanted a rational Platonism cleaned of Hermetic materialism; Vaughan wanted an embodied Hermeticism where Spirit is *in* matter, not *above* it. The Vaughan position is structurally identical to Paracelsian vitalism; the More position is the origin of Cartesian-influenced idealist Platonism in England.

## Light Mysticism

*Lumen de Lumine* (1651) and *Aula Lucis* (1652) develop Vaughan's distinctive **light mysticism**: the divine light is not merely metaphorical but a literal subtle substance (*lux*) that emanates through the cosmic hierarchy — Neoplatonic light-emanation translated into alchemical-physical terms. This connects to the broader Hermetic light tradition: Ficino's *sol* as divine principle, Boehme's light-dark dialectic, Khunrath's oratory-laboratory as a space of divine light.
