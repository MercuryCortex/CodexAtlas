# Utriusque Cosmi Historia

## What it is
Robert Fludd's massive Latin encyclopedia of Hermetic-Kabbalist-Paracelsian cosmology, published in two principal parts at Oppenheim and Frankfurt 1617–1621 by Johann Theodore de Bry: the *Macrocosmi Historia* (the great world — cosmology, astronomy, astrology, geomancy, music of the spheres, weather, mineralogy, ascending hierarchies of being from rock to angel) and the *Microcosmi Historia* (the little world — human anatomy and physiology read through the macro-microcosm correspondence, music in the body, dreams and prophecies, the soul's faculties). The work integrates Ficino's Hermeticism, Reuchlin's Kabbalah, Agrippa's three-worlds schema, and Paracelsian alchemical medicine into a single grand visual-textual synthesis. The de Bry engravings — the chain of being descending from the Tetragrammaton to brute matter, the angelic-planetary-elemental hierarchy, the celestial monochord, the cosmic-bellows of inhalation and exhalation between God and creature, the Triple World — are the iconic image-canon of late-Renaissance esoteric cosmology.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Jacobean England (Fludd practiced medicine in London) and the Palatinate (de Bry published at Oppenheim under the patronage of Frederick V, the Calvinist Elector whose Bohemian adventure of 1619 collapsed in 1620). The Palatinate printing program collapsed when Spanish forces took the Palatinate in 1622–1623; de Bry's press moved to Frankfurt.
- **Religious milieu.** Fludd was Anglican Christian but his theological-philosophical commitment was thoroughgoing Hermetic-Kabbalist-Paracelsianism; he produced *Apologia Compendiaria Fraternitatem de Rosea Cruce* (1616) defending the Rosicrucian manifestos against Andreas Libavius and entered the celebrated polemic with Marin Mersenne and Pierre Gassendi (1623–1630) over Paracelsian-Hermetic science vs. early mechanical philosophy.
- **Who was behind it.** Fludd personally, financing the de Bry editions partly out of his own substantial medical practice income; Frederick V's Palatine entourage as the political-cultural environment.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Latin-language continental reception in the 17th century; Fludd is read by Kircher (who criticizes him), by Newton (who owned and annotated Fludd volumes), and by 18th-c. esotericism. The visual program — the iconic Fludd engravings — has had a vast afterlife in modern occult publishing.

## Themes and motifs
- [[microcosm-macrocosm]] — the central organizing principle: Fludd's two volumes are organized around the correspondence.
- [[creation-by-word]] — Hebrew Tetragrammaton mysticism is structurally central; one famous plate shows the Tetragrammaton as the source of the cosmic chain.
- [[prisca-theologia]] — Hermes, Moses, Plato, the prophets as witnesses to a single ancient wisdom.
- **Music of the spheres** — the celestial monochord plate gives the most famous Renaissance image of Pythagorean cosmic harmony.
- **Chain of being** — the *scala naturae* as a single continuum from God to brute matter, with the human at the meeting-point of the spiritual and material chains.

## Disputes
- **Fludd as scientist.** Older positivist historiography (Frances Yates excepted) dismissed Fludd as a relic; current scholarship (Huffman, Debus, Godwin) reads him as a serious natural philosopher whose Hermetic-Paracelsian commitments were rationally articulated even though they lost the early-modern scientific argument.
- **The Mersenne-Fludd controversy.** Mersenne's accusation that Fludd's project was magical and atheist (a strange combination) and Fludd's reply that mechanical philosophy was the truly impious position document the boundary-formation between Renaissance science and the new mechanical-mathematical natural philosophy.
