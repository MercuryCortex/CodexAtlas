# Splendor Solis (*Splendour of the Sun*)

## What it is
A **richly-illuminated alchemical-emblem treatise** in 22 chapters with 22 (in most mss) full-page miniatures, attributed to the pseudonymous **Salomon Trismosin**, composed in stages 1530s–1580s in Bavaria. **The British Library's Harley MS 3469 (1582)** is the most iconically-reproduced illuminated copy and is the witness Ritman-Library-style esoteric publishing has most often reproduced. **One of the visual-flagship works of Western alchemical-emblem literature**, treated by 20th-c. Jungian and contemporary esoteric reception as the central iconographic case of Western illuminated alchemy.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Bavarian Catholic alchemy-and-medicine, late-Reformation period; the text is non-confessional in register but draws on integrated Christian-Hermetic-Paracelsian milieu.
- **Authorship.** *Salomon Trismosin* is pseudonymous; the historical identity is disputed. A traditional claim (16th-17th c.) that Trismosin was Paracelsus's *teacher* (rather than student) is chronologically possible (~1490s-1500s) but unconfirmable.
- **Transmission.** Multiple richly-illuminated mss across Bavarian, Austrian, and English collections. The Harley MS 3469 (1582) is the principal modern-reproduced witness.

## Themes and motifs
- [[alchemy-as-spiritual-discipline]] — explicit integration of material *opus* with spiritual transformation.
- [[alchemical-marriage]] / [[coniunctio]] — central iconographic motif (especially plate 10 — the King and Queen in the alchemical bath, paralleling the *Rosarium*'s sequence).
- [[hermetic-correspondences]] — planetary-metallic correspondences explicit throughout.
- [[as-above-so-below]] — operative principle.
