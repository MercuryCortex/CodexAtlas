# Arcana Coelestia + Heaven and Hell

## What it is
The two best-known works of Emanuel Swedenborg (1688–1772), Swedish polymath natural philosopher who in 1745 turned from a distinguished scientific career (mining engineer to the Swedish Crown, anatomist, cosmologist) to a theological-visionary career he sustained for the remaining 27 years of his life. *Arcana Coelestia* (1749–1756) is a twelve-volume verse-by-verse Latin exposition of Genesis and Exodus organized around Swedenborg's doctrine of *correspondences* — every term in scripture has a "natural sense" (the literal narrative) and a "spiritual sense" (its meaning in the spiritual world) and a "celestial sense" (its meaning in the highest heaven of divine love); the internal sense, recoverable by correspondences, describes the spiritual and angelic states the natural narrative externally figures. *Heaven and Hell* (1758) is the topical synthesis of Swedenborg's accounts of the afterlife: heaven is organized as a *Maximus Homo* (Greatest Human, the cosmic-anthropic shape of the angelic communities); spirits after death gradually clarify into their dominant love (good or evil); heaven and hell are not divine punishments but the self-chosen homes of souls whose dominant loves harmonize or conflict with divine love. Swedenborg's system is at once a Bible-exegetical project, a visionary cosmography, and a Christian theology distinctive enough to have founded its own ecclesiastical movement (the New Church / Church of the New Jerusalem).

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Enlightenment Sweden under the Age of Liberty (1720–1772); Swedenborg was a member of the House of Nobles and a respected figure of the Swedish establishment. The Netherlands and England (Anglican-Latitudinarian Protestant, with a substantial book trade) were his preferred publishing venues; Sweden's stricter Lutheran orthodoxy made theological publication there problematic.
- **Religious milieu.** Lutheran orthodoxy hardening against Pietism and against Wolfian rationalism. Swedenborg's theology — particularly his rejection of the Athanasian Trinity (he held a unitary God in the person of Jesus Christ, identifying the Father with the soul, the Son with the body, the Holy Spirit with the proceeding influence — a non-Nicene Christology), his rejection of substitutionary atonement, and his exegetical method — was clearly heterodox by orthodox Lutheran standards, but he published anonymously and avoided direct confrontation with Swedish authority until 1769, when his Latin defense of his teaching produced a heresy investigation that was decided in his favor.
- **Who was behind it.** Swedenborg personally; the 1745 visionary experience in a London tavern (Swedenborg reports having been visited by Christ and given a commission to elucidate the spiritual sense of scripture) initiated his theological writing. He claimed lifelong direct experience of the spiritual world thereafter and produced ~20 substantial Latin theological works.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Anonymous publication during Swedenborg's lifetime; growing recognition in the late 18th c.; the Church of the New Jerusalem founded in London 1787 by Robert Hindmarsh; American expansion through the 19th century (a New Church congregation in Baltimore from 1792); influential reception via Blake, Coleridge, Carlyle, Emerson, Henry James Sr., D.T. Suzuki (who wrote in Japanese on Swedenborg in 1913), and Borges.

## Themes and motifs
- **Doctrine of correspondences** — every natural object has a fixed spiritual signification; scripture, dreams, and the natural world are read through the same hermeneutic. The doctrine has substantial affinity with the older microcosm-macrocosm tradition but is given a particular Bible-exegetical-visionary formulation.
- **Maximus Homo** — the cosmic Human form: heaven as a whole has the form of a Greatest Human, and individual angelic societies occupy organs and members within that form. The doctrine integrates Cabbalistic Adam Kadmon, Hermetic macroanthropos, and Swedenborg's own visionary experience.
- [[afterlife-judgment]] — Swedenborg's afterlife is not juridical but ecological: spirits gravitate to the community their dominant love makes them fit for. Hell is not God's punishment but the soul's chosen home.
- [[ascent-of-the-soul]] — gradual purification or hardening after death; the soul becomes what it deeply was.
- **Non-Nicene Trinity** — Trinitarian terminology preserved but reconfigured: the one God in the one person of Christ.
- **Marriage / conjugial love** — Swedenborg's distinctive doctrine that true marriage between one man and one woman continues in heaven and is itself a form of divine union (developed especially in *Conjugial Love*, 1768).

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[phase-6-017-boehme-aurora-mysterium-magnum]] — *parallel-not-direct*: Swedenborg's system has substantial structural parallels with Boehmian theosophy (visionary cosmography, divine self-articulation, the Maximus Homo / Adam Kadmon, the internal sense of scripture). Swedenborg denied direct knowledge of Böhme; modern scholarship treats the resemblance as parallel emergence from broadly similar Lutheran-spiritualist milieu (Bergquist 2005). Flag for Phase 7: Hegel's reception of both Böhme and (less directly) Swedenborg.
- → Bible (Phases 2–3) — *direct-commentary*: *Arcana* is at form a Genesis-Exodus commentary; the internal-sense doctrine creates a hermeneutic radically different from both pre-critical and historical-critical exegesis.
- → William Blake (Phase 7) — *direct-engagement*: Blake's *Marriage of Heaven and Hell* (1790) engages Swedenborg both with affinity (the visionary mode, the symbolic reading of scripture) and with rebellion (Blake satirizes Swedenborg's heavenly Order in favor of contrary-energy).
- → American Transcendentalism (Phase 7) — *direct-influence*: Emerson's *Representative Men* chapter on Swedenborg is canonical; the Transcendentalist absorption is substantial.
- → modern Spiritualism (Phase 7) — *precursor*: Swedenborg's reports of communications with the dead, his cosmography of the spiritual world, and the Church of the New Jerusalem's normalization of post-mortem spiritual experience prepared the cultural ground for the Fox sisters' 1848 spirit-rappings and the broader Spiritualist movement.
- → C.G. Jung (Phase 7) — *acknowledged-influence*: Jung as a teenager read Swedenborg.

## Disputes
- **Sanity assessment.** Swedenborg's visionary career has been variously diagnosed (temporal-lobe epilepsy, mystical experience, schizophrenia, mediumistic gift); the diagnostic question is less useful than the recognition that he sustained a coherent, productive, and influential 27-year visionary-theological project in a register that does not fit modern psychiatric or theological categories cleanly.
- **Relation to Böhme.** Lars Bergquist's exhaustive 2005 biography concludes that Swedenborg did not directly read Böhme but operated in a related Lutheran-spiritualist milieu that produced parallel forms.
- **Doctrinal classification.** Whether Swedenborgianism is a Christian denomination, a heterodox Christian movement, an esoteric tradition, or a new religious movement is variously argued; Swedenborg himself claimed simply to be restoring the true spiritual sense of Christianity.
