# Sūrat al-Tawba (Quran 9 — Repentance)

## What it is
A late-Medinan sura, by tradition among the last revealed, and the only one of the 114 that does not begin with the basmala ("In the name of God…"). It is a public legal and political document from the period just after the conquest of Mecca: it announces the dissolution of treaties with polytheist parties who had broken them, sets a four-month period of safe-conduct, regulates the Tabūk campaign and rebukes those who hung back, defines the lawful recipients of the alms-tax (9:60), and lays down the terms on which non-Muslim "People of the Book" live under Muslim rule (9:29) — the seed of the later dhimma system. It also records that any idolater who seeks protection in order to hear the Qur'an must be granted it and escorted to safety (9:6).

## Primary text

### Quran 9:6
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> If any one of the idolaters should seek your protection, grant it to him so that he may hear the word of God, then take him to a place safe for him, for they are people with no knowledge.

### Quran 9:60
*Trans. Abdel Haleem (2004)*
> Alms are meant only for the poor, the needy, those who administer them, those whose hearts need winning over, to free slaves and help those in debt, for God's cause, and for travellers in need.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Medinan, c. 631 CE; the community is now a state, and the sura legislates its dealings with treaty-partners, hypocrites, and subject peoples.
- **Tradition.** Classical exegesis explains the missing basmala variously — as a continuation of the preceding Sūrat al-Anfāl, or as fitting a sura that opens with a declaration of severance rather than mercy.
- **Discovery / transmission.** 9:29 became the textual anchor of the *dhimma* — the protected, tax-paying status of Jews, Christians, and other "People of the Book" across the Islamic world.

## Themes and motifs
- Treaty and its dissolution — fidelity to the keeper, severance from the breaker.
- The protected peoples — coexistence under terms (jizya, dhimma).
- Repentance — the sura's title; even the three who stayed from Tabūk are forgiven (9:118).

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The charter of the "protected peoples."** Sūrat al-Tawba is the most consequential legal sura for how Islam organized life with other faiths. Verse 9:29 — that the People of the Book pay the *jizya* and so live secured under Muslim rule — became, in the hands of the jurists, the **dhimma**: a contract of protection under which Jews, Christians, Zoroastrians, and others kept their religion, courts, and communal autonomy in exchange for a poll-tax and defined limits. For all its inequalities, this institutionalized a durable legal pluralism centuries before the European wars of religion, and it is the direct ancestor of the Ottoman *millet* system that governed the empire's Christians and Jews into the twentieth century; it parallels, on a different footing, the Roman *foederati* and the late-antique treaties of protection. The sura is also the storm-centre of Quranic interpretation: its "verses of the sword" (9:5, 9:29) are read by some as time-bound responses to specific treaty-breakers — note that 9:6 *immediately* commands asylum and safe escort for any idolater who wishes merely to hear the revelation — and by others, in the medieval doctrine of *naskh* (abrogation), as general rulings; modern scholarship stresses the concrete occasion of the post-conquest treaties and the asylum clause that qualifies them (Friedmann 2003 pp.54–86; McAuliffe 2006 pp.181–198; Nasr 2015 pp.503–545). The vanished basmala — the one sura that opens without "In the name of God, the Lord of Mercy" — has itself been debated since the first generation.
