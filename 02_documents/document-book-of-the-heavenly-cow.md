# The Book of the Heavenly Cow (The Destruction of Mankind)

## What it is
A New Kingdom myth — its oldest copy on the outermost golden shrine of Tutankhamun — telling how the creator-sun Ra, grown old, discovers that humanity is plotting against him and resolves to destroy them. He sends out his Eye as the lioness-goddess Sekhmet, who massacres humans with such fury that the land runs with blood; horrified, Ra relents and has the fields flooded with thousands of jars of beer dyed red to look like blood. Sekhmet drinks it, grows too drunk to kill, and humankind is spared. The weary Ra then withdraws from the earth, lifted into the sky on the back of the heavenly cow.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** New Kingdom solar theology; the dangerous, pacifiable Eye-goddess and the withdrawal of the creator.
- **Function.** Etiology of human mortality, of the goddess's festivals of drunkenness, and of the sky's separation from the earth.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Royal tomb walls and Tutankhamun's shrine.

## Themes and motifs
- The gods' decision to destroy humanity — and the relenting that saves it.
- The Eye of Ra — Hathor/Sekhmet, the goddess as both nurturer and exterminator.
- The deception that spares — red beer mistaken for blood.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Egypt's near-destruction of humanity — the Nile's answer to the Flood.** Like the Mesopotamian flood traditions (Atrahasis, Gilgamesh XI, the Eridu Genesis) and Genesis 6, the Book of the Heavenly Cow turns on a creator-god who decides to annihilate a humanity that has become intolerable — and then pulls back from total destruction. Egypt's version substitutes a slaughtering lioness and a trick of red beer for a deluge, but the structural theology is the same: humanity survives only because the deity relents. It is the Egyptian member of the widespread ancient Near Eastern "gods destroy humankind, then spare a remnant" family of myths (Lichtheim 1976 pp.197–199; Hornung 1982).
