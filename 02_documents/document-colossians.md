# Epistle to the Colossians

## What it is
A letter in Paul's name to a church he had not founded, written to counter a seductive "philosophy" of ascetic and angelic piety by exalting the all-sufficiency of Christ. Its centerpiece is one of the earliest and grandest Christ-hymns (1:15–20), proclaiming Christ as the image of the invisible God, the agent and goal of all creation, in whom "all the fullness of God was pleased to dwell."

## Primary text

### Colossians 1:15–17
*Trans. NRSV*
> He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation; for in him all things in heaven and on earth were created, things visible and invisible... all things have been created through him and for him. He himself is before all things, and in him all things hold together.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** A syncretistic local teaching combining Jewish calendar/food observance, asceticism, and the veneration of cosmic powers — answered by an all-encompassing Christology.
- **Authorship.** Stylistically transitional; a key witness in the development from the undisputed Paul to Ephesians.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Transmitted within the Pauline corpus.

## Themes and motifs
- Cosmic Christ — the Christ-hymn's vision of Christ as creator and sustainer of all things.
- [[messianism]] — the supremacy and "fullness" of the Messiah over every power.
- Fullness (plērōma) — "in him the whole fullness of deity dwells bodily" (2:9).

## Connections (provisional)
- → [[document-ephesians]] — *direct-influence*: Ephesians reworks Colossians; the two are the closest literary pair in the corpus.
- → [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — *parallel-motif*: the Christ-hymn's pre-existent agent-of-creation anticipates John's Logos prologue.
- → [[document-romans]] — *shared-milieu*: Pauline Christology and the cosmic scope of redemption.
