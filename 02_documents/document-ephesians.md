# Epistle to the Ephesians

## What it is
A serene, liturgical circular letter in the name of Paul, with little of the situational urgency of the undisputed letters. It unfolds a grand vision of God's eternal plan to unite all things in Christ, of the Church as Christ's cosmic body reconciling Jew and gentile into "one new humanity," and of the believer's calling to walk worthily — closing with the famous "whole armor of God." Its close literary dependence on Colossians and its developed, un-Pauline style make it the centerpiece case for deutero-Pauline authorship.

## Primary text

### Ephesians 2:14–16
*Trans. NRSV*
> For he is our peace; in his flesh he has made both groups into one and has broken down the dividing wall, that is, the hostility between us... that he might create in himself one new humanity in place of the two, thus making peace.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** A now largely-gentile Pauline church, after Paul, articulating a universal ecclesiology and a cosmic Christology.
- **Authorship.** The leading disputed letter: vocabulary, sentence-structure, and a realized eschatology diverge from the undisputed Paul.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Transmitted within the Pauline corpus from the earliest collections.

## Themes and motifs
- The cosmic Church — the body and "fullness" of Christ, uniting heaven and earth.
- Jew–gentile reconciliation — the "dividing wall" abolished (2:14).
- [[messianism]] — all things gathered up "in Christ" as the summing-up of history (1:10).
