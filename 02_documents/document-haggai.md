# Book of Haggai

## What it is
A brief, precisely-dated book of four oracles delivered over a few months in 520 BCE, urging the discouraged returnees from Babylonian exile to stop dwelling in their own paneled houses and rebuild the ruined Temple. Haggai's blunt exhortations — paired with Zechariah's visions — succeeded: the Second Temple was finished and dedicated in 515 BCE.

## Primary text

### Haggai 2:6–7
*Trans. NRSV*
> For thus says the LORD of hosts: Once again, in a little while, I will shake the heavens and the earth and the sea and the dry land; and I will shake all the nations, so that the treasure of all nations shall come, and I will fill this house with splendor.

## Context
- **Political milieu.** Persian-period Yehud, a small temple-province under Darius I; the Davidic heir Zerubbabel serves as governor.
- **Religious milieu.** The reconstitution of Judaism around the rebuilt Temple after the exile.
- **Discovery / transmission.** Continuous canonical transmission within the Twelve.

## Themes and motifs
- Temple-rebuilding — the practical and theological heart of the book.
- [[messianism]] — Zerubbabel as YHWH's chosen "signet ring" (2:23), a flicker of Davidic restoration hope.
- [[divine-kingship]] — the shaking of all nations and the glory of the LORD's house.
