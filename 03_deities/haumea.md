# Haumea

Haumea is the great Hawaiian goddess of **fertility, childbirth, and the generative power of life** — one of the most ancient and important figures of Hawaiian (and broader Polynesian) mythology. She is above all a **mother**: the genealogies make her the parent of a host of gods and chiefs, most famously the volcano-goddess **[[pele|Pele]]** and her sister Hiʻiaka, and she presides over **human reproduction and safe birth**, credited in myth with introducing **natural childbirth** (delivery through the body) in place of an earlier, fatal practice. Her defining quality is **inexhaustible renewal**: Haumea can make herself young again and again, marrying down the generations and **giving birth endlessly**, sometimes from unusual parts of her body — an image of the unceasing regeneration of life itself. She is associated with the **wild plant foods** and the trees that feed people, and with magical implements like the *makalei* branch that attracts fish. As a primal life-bearing female power she parallels the Polynesian earth-mother **[[papatuanuku|Papatūānuku]]**. Haumea embodies the Hawaiian reverence for the female generative force at the root of all living increase.
