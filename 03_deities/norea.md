# Norea

## Identity
A Sethian-Gnostic feminine figure of revelation — variously the daughter, wife, or sister of Seth — who resists the rapacious archons (the rulers of the lower cosmos) in the *Hypostasis of the Archons* (NHC II,4) and *On the Origin of the World* (NHC II,5). When the archons attempt to violate her, fire pours from her mouth and she is rescued by the angel Eleleth. Norea is closely associated with — sometimes identified with — the *spiritual Eve* of these texts; she carries the gnostic illumination forward through history.

## Earliest attestation
- [[phase-4-006-hypostasis-of-the-archons]] — the major Norea narrative.
- [[phase-4-007-on-the-origin-of-the-world]] — parallel material.
- The *Thought of Norea* (NHC IX,2) — a short hymnic-revelatory tractate.
- The lost *Book of Norea* mentioned by Epiphanius (*Panarion* 26.1.3) and attributed to "Gnostics."

## Mythological role
Norea functions as the *unviolated bearer of the gnostic seed* — the lineage from her continues the Sethian-pneumatic descent through history. Her resistance to the archons is the soteriological inversion of Eve's traditional submission. In rabbinic tradition, *Naamah* (Gen 4:22) is a candidate antecedent; some traditions identify Norea as the wife of Noah (Norea = Noah's wife pattern). The figure has gathered scholarly attention as a striking Gnostic female-revelatory figure.

## Iconography
No surviving Gnostic iconography is known.

## Disputes
- The relationship of Norea to other female figures in the Sethian corpus (Eve, Sophia, Eleleth, Barbelo).
- The relation to Jewish-extra-canonical traditions (Naamah, Noah's wife, etc.).
