# Ala

## Identity
Earth goddess of Igbo traditional religion — mother of fertility, owner of the land, keeper of the law and the dead. Ala is the most universally-worshipped Igbo *alusi* (intermediate deity); every Igbo community has its Ala shrine, and Ala violations (*nso ala* — "abomination against Ala") are the most serious offenses in Igbo customary law (Metuh 1981). She is the structural complement of [[chukwu-igbo]]: where Chukwu is sky and creator, Ala is earth and continued moral order.

## Earliest attestation
Pre-colonial Igbo oral tradition; Nri-Awka archaeology (c. 9th-c. CE Igbo-Ukwu finds include religious objects) suggests Ala-cult continuity. Modern documentation in Cole & Aniakor (1984) and Metuh (1981).

## Mythological role
Ala received the dead (burial is *return to Ala*), governed the agricultural calendar (yam cultivation belongs to Ala), and enforced moral law. The category *nso ala* — abomination against the earth — covers offenses like murder, incest, certain taboo violations; the moral order of Igbo society is structurally Ala's. Land-disputes are Ala disputes; oath-taking invokes Ala. Achebe's *Things Fall Apart* (1958) Chapter 8 documents the *nso ala* concept in the context of Umuofia's response to Okonkwo's accidental killing.

## The Mbari tradition
Ala-cult is associated with the *mbari* temple-art tradition (especially in Mbaise and Owerri Igbo): elaborate sculpted-clay temples built in Ala's honor, then deliberately allowed to return to earth. Mbari are some of the most-elaborate African religious art forms, with sculptural programs depicting Ala (often seated with her children at the temple-center), other deities, cosmological narratives, and contemporary social commentary. Cole (*Mbari* 1982) is the standard study.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[asase-yaa]]** (Akan) — closest West-African parallel; both are earth-mother + moral-law goddesses with sacred-day prohibitions (Akan Thursday-no-farming; Igbo Ala's various market-day prohibitions).
- **[[gaia]]** (Greek) — universal earth-mother archetype.
- **Pacha Mama** (Andean), **Geb** (Egyptian) — broader earth-deity comparison.

## Cross-tradition parallels — the law-bearing earth-mother
Ala is one of the most-developed examples of the earth-mother + moral-law deity combination. Where Greek Gaia is primarily cosmological-genealogical, Ala is primarily moral-legal: she sustains the agricultural and social order through her enforcement of the *nso ala* category. This makes her structurally closer to Akan [[asase-yaa]] than to Greek Gaia. The earth-as-moral-law pattern in West Africa is a distinct sub-pattern of the broader earth-mother archetype (Mbiti 1969).

## Iconography
Mbari temples; the python (sacred animal, killing one was a serious *nso ala*); yam altars; Ala statuary often shows her seated with children, white-painted body.
