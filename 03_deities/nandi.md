# Nandi

## Identity
Nandi (Sanskrit *Nandī*, "the rejoicer") is the bull-mount (*vāhana*) of Shiva, gatekeeper of Mt. Kailasa, leader of Shiva's host of *gaṇas* (attendants), and — in the Nandīśvara theology of the Shaiva Purāṇas — an aspect of Shiva himself. Every Shaiva temple has a stone Nandi seated in the courtyard or *maṇḍapa* facing the inner sanctum, so that the worshipper's line of sight passes from Nandi to the *liṅga*; the bull's gaze toward the god is the model of devotion. He is the primary Shaiva devotee. His name *Nandī* (from *nand-* "to rejoice") expresses the devotional bliss of Shiva's worship (Doniger 1975; Kramrisch 1981).

## Earliest attestation
- **Iconographic.** Nandi-as-mount imagery appears in Kushan-period (1st–3rd c. CE) coinage and sculpture; the Pallava period (6th–9th c.) crystallizes the standard temple-Nandi seated-bull form.
- **Textual.** Puranic literature — especially the *Liṅga Purāṇa*, *Vāyu Purāṇa*, and *Skanda Purāṇa* — gives the developed Nandīśvara theology (~+400 to +900 in the conventional redaction-windows).
- **Vedic substrate.** Rig Veda 2.33 and the *Śatarudrīya* (Yajurveda) already associate Rudra (= early Shiva) with cattle and the bull; Nandi is the crystallization of this older substrate (Doniger 1981).

## Mythological role
- **Mount and gatekeeper.** Nandi conveys Shiva across the worlds and guards the entrance to Kailasa. In numerous Puranic narratives Nandi performs the gatekeeping function — most famously refusing entry to Ravana, who attempts to lift Kailasa.
- **Devotee-prototype.** In the **Periya Purāṇam** (Tamil Shaiva canon, ~+1135) and earlier Shaiva theology, Nandi is the model devotee, the *bhakta* whose unceasing attention to Shiva models the Shaiva spiritual ideal.
- **Aspect of Shiva.** In Nandīśvara theology, Nandi is not separate from Shiva but a partial-incarnation — the bull-form of the god. This is the deepest theological reading of the Shaiva bull-cult.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[shiva]]** — *ancient-identification*: in Nandīśvara theology, Nandi is an aspect of Shiva himself.
- **[[rudra-shiva-early]]** — *ancient-identification*: the bull-association traces back to Vedic Rudra-Shiva (RV 2.33; *Śatarudrīya*).
- **[[apis-bull]]** — *scholarly-parallel* (structural, not genetic): Egyptian sacred-bull cult is an independent instance of the wider sacred-bull-cult typology; no direct relation, but typologically parallel.

## Iconography
The seated bull facing the linga is the defining Shaiva temple-iconographic element. Major Nandi sculptures include the colossal Nandi at the Brihadishvara Temple, Thanjavur (Chola period, 11th c.); the Lepakshi Nandi (Vijayanagara, 16th c., one of the largest monolithic stone Nandis); the Chamundi Hill Nandi at Mysuru. The Nandīśvara anthropomorphic form (bull-headed humanoid with three eyes) is found in Tantric Shaiva contexts.

## Disputes
- **Vedic continuity vs. post-Vedic crystallization.** Whether Nandi is a direct Vedic continuation (the bull of Rudra) or a post-Vedic iconographic invention later retro-justified by Vedic citations is debated; Kramrisch 1981 argues for genuine continuity, others for a more discontinuous emergence.
- **Relation to Indus Valley "Proto-Shiva."** Marshall's 1931 identification of the Indus Valley "Pashupati seal" (Mohenjo-daro DK 5475) as a "Proto-Shiva" surrounded by cattle is contested; if accepted, it would push the sacred-bull-Shiva association back to ~-2300. Most current scholarship treats the Pashupati identification as unproven.
