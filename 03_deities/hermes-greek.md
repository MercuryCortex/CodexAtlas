# Hermes

## Identity
Hermes is the Greek messenger god, the boundary-crosser par excellence: between Olympus and earth, between the living and the dead (*psychopompos*, the soul-conductor), between strangers (god of travelers and traders, but also of thieves), between languages (god of *hermēneia*, interpretation — whence "hermeneutics"). Son of Zeus and the Pleiad Maia, born in a cave on Mount Cyllene, precocious from infancy: the Homeric Hymn to Hermes narrates how on his birthday he invented the lyre from a tortoise-shell, stole Apollo's cattle, and negotiated his way onto Olympus. Attested in Linear B at Pylos as "e-ma-a2" — Hermes is one of the most securely Bronze Age Olympians.

Hermes is, with Thoth and Aphrodite/Ishtar/Astarte, one of the *most* syncretized of all ancient deities. From Herodotus onward Greeks identified him with the Egyptian Thoth — both being messenger gods of writing, wisdom, and the dead. By the Hellenistic period this fusion crystallizes into Hermes Trismegistus ("Thrice-Greatest Hermes"), the named author of the Corpus Hermeticum and the founding figure of Hermetism — Phase 3–4 work. The boundary-crossing function of Hermes is reflected in his ubiquitous *herm* monuments — square pillars topped with a head of Hermes, marking thresholds throughout the Greek world.
