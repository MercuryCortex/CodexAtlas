# Iktomi

The **Lakota spider-trickster** — one of the firstborn beings created by [[inyan]] (the rock, the Lakota primordial). Iktomi appears variously as a spider, as a man, as any animal or object he chooses; he is the shapeshifter par excellence. His narratives teach **through disruption and mistake** — Iktomi schemes, the schemes go wrong, the audience learns. He is the inventor of language and the namer of things in some tellings.

**Cross-continental North American trickster convergence — MASSIVE WIN.** Iktomi is one node of a remarkable cross-continental pattern: the **trickster-as-culture-hero** is the same theological role filled by regionally appropriate figures across North America:

- **Iktomi** (Lakota / Plains) — spider
- **Coyote** ([[coyote-navajo]]; Plains, Southwest, California) — coyote
- **Raven** ([[raven-trickster]]; Pacific Northwest Coast) — raven
- **Manabozho** ([[manabozho]]; Great Lakes Anishinaabe) — rabbit-human-figure
- **Glooscap** ([[glooscap]]; Northeastern Wabanaki) — anthropomorphic culture-hero
- **Hare / Br'er Rabbit** (Southeast Cherokee / Muskogean → African-American folk reception) — rabbit

Six independently-attested traditions in different language families and geographies all centre the same teaching-through-disruption figure. The cross-tradition stability of the **trickster archetype** is one of the strongest cases in North American comparative religion of a universal-cognitive-pattern operating beneath cultural-historical-specifics.

Iktomi's broader cross-cultural parallel includes Greek [[hermes-greek]], Norse Loki, West African Eshu — Hyde's *Trickster Makes This World* (1998) treats these as instances of a single anthropological-religious type.
