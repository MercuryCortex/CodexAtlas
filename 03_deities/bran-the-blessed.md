# Bran the Blessed (Bendigeidfran)

Bran the Blessed (*Bendigeidfran*, 'Blessed Raven') is the semi-divine king of Britain in Welsh mythology, protagonist of the Second Branch of the *Mabinogion*, *Branwen ferch Llŷr*. He is a figure of enormous stature — literally too large to fit inside any building — and is the owner and giver of the **Pair Dadeni** (the Cauldron of Rebirth), the central Welsh antecedent to the Holy Grail.

The narrative: Bran gives the Pair Dadeni to the Irish king Matholwch as a bride-gift with his sister Branwen. The Irish abuse Branwen; war follows. The Irish use the cauldron to resurrect their fallen warriors. Bran's half-brother Evnissyen destroys the cauldron by self-sacrifice from within. Bran himself is fatally wounded by a poisoned spear; he instructs his seven surviving companions to cut off his head and carry it with them — it will continue to speak and prophesy for 87 years (the Feast of the Noble Head, one of the Three Happy Hospitalies of the Island of Britain).

Bran's severed-head motif connects him to the wider Celtic cult of the sacred head (see [[sacred-head]]): the head as the seat of the soul, continuing to speak after death, presiding over a feast outside time.

In Arthurian tradition, Roger Sherman Loomis (*Celtic Myth and Arthurian Romance*, Columbia UP, 1927) identifies Bran as the Celtic origin of *Bron*, the Maimed Fisher King who guards the Grail: the cauldron of rebirth becomes the Grail; Bran's wound (the king who cannot die but cannot be healed) becomes the Fisher King's wound; the question that heals the Fisher King replaces the self-sacrifice that destroys the cauldron. The transformation is not exact but structurally coherent.
