# Matsya

## Identity

Matsya ("the Fish") is the first **avatar** (descent, incarnation) of [[vishnu]] — the form the preserver-god takes to save life through the cosmic flood. In the standard list of Vishnu's ten avatars (the *Dashavatara*), Matsya comes first, fittingly: the fish belongs to the primordial waters of the deluge from which the renewed world emerges. In art Matsya is shown either as a great horned fish or as a hybrid with a human upper body and a fish's tail.

## Mythological role

Matsya's myth is the Indian **flood** story, attached to the figure of [[manu-vedic]]. A tiny fish comes into Manu's hands and asks protection; in gratitude it warns him of the world-destroying flood to come, tells him to build a boat, and instructs him to tie the boat to its horn when the waters rise. The fish grows to immense size, and as the deluge covers the earth it tows Manu's boat to safety on a northern mountain, preserving the seed of humanity and the sages. In a parallel strand (the *Matsya Purana*), Matsya also **recovers the Vedas**, which a demon had stolen and hidden in the cosmic waters — so the fish-avatar saves not only living creatures but the sacred knowledge itself, the basis of dharma, through the chaos of the flood.

## Cross-tradition significance

Matsya occupies the **divine-savior** role in the cross-tradition flood complex: where the Mesopotamian flood has the wise water-god Ea/Enki secretly warn the flood-hero to build his boat, the Indian flood has the fish Matsya perform the same office for Manu. As a benevolent aquatic power who preserves life and sacred order against the destroying waters, Matsya pairs with [[manu-vedic]] (the surviving ancestor) to complete the Indian end of the flood-hero wire that runs across the Mesopotamian, biblical, Greek, and Indian worlds. The motif of saving the **sacred texts** through the flood is a distinctively Indian elaboration of the universal deluge theme.
