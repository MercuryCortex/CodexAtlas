# Yaldabaoth

The specifically Sethian name for the ignorant demiurge — etymology debated: Aramaic *yalda bahut* ("child of chaos") or *yalda bohuth* ("child of shame"), or a play on names of the OT God. Born without consort to Sophia (irregular emanation), Yaldabaoth steals divine power from his mother, fashions the seven archons, creates the material cosmos as a flawed imitation of the Pleroma, and declares "I am God and there is no other" — a citation of Isaiah 45:5 / Exodus 20:3, recoded as proof of his ignorance, since higher beings (the Monad, Barbelo, the Pleroma) exist that he is unaware of.

Among the most consequential polemical figures in the history of religions: the Sethian identification of the Hebrew Bible's creator-god with Yaldabaoth is the most explicit ancient inversion of canonical Jewish-Christian theology preserved in extant texts. Pearson 1990 traces the polemic's likely roots in Jewish-heretical inversions; Williams 1996 cautions against reading the polemic as straightforwardly anti-Jewish (it may be anti-imperial-orthodox); King 2006 reads ApocJohn as Christian theology of Genesis through Platonist categories.
