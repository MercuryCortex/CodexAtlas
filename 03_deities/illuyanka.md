# Illuyanka

## Identity
Illuyanka (Hittite *illuyanka-* "serpent / dragon") is the chaos-monster of the Hittite *Illuyanka Myth* (CTH 321), defeated by the Storm-God of Hatti (Tarḫunna / in imperial cult, Teshub) in two surviving recensions. The myth was recited at the *Purulli* spring festival — the new-year / agricultural-renewal festival celebrating the storm-god's victory.

Illuyanka is the **Anatolian instance of the chaoskampf serpent**, with cross-tradition cognates in Mesopotamian Tiamat, Ugaritic Yam / Lotan, Hebrew Leviathan, Greek Typhon and Python, and Vedic Vritra. Calvert Watkins (1995) demonstrated that the Hittite formulation *MUŠilluyankan kuenta* ("he slew the serpent") preserves an Indo-European poetic formula (PIE *gʷhen- ogʷhim "to slay the serpent") that also generates the Vedic *áhann áhim* ("he slew the dragon," of Indra/Vritra) — the same formula, the same myth, in two Indo-European branches.

## Earliest attestation
- Boğazköy archive Old Hittite version (~-1600 to -1500) and Late Hittite version (~-1300 to -1200).
- Ritual context: the *Purulli* festival of spring, recited annually to ensure the storm-god's renewed cosmic kingship.

## Mythological role
Two versions are preserved:

**Old version.** Illuyanka defeats the Storm-God in initial combat. The Storm-God's daughter Inara, with the help of a mortal named Hupasiya, prepares a banquet; Illuyanka and his offspring eat and drink themselves into a stupor; Hupasiya binds Illuyanka; the Storm-God comes and slays him. Hupasiya is granted a house and a goddess-mistress (Inara), with the condition that he not look out the window at his mortal wife and children; he breaks the taboo; Inara kills him. The myth thus contains both the chaoskampf and a folk-tale of the broken taboo.

**Late version.** Illuyanka steals the Storm-God's heart and eyes after defeating him. The Storm-God begets a son by a mortal woman; the son marries Illuyanka's daughter; on his wife's request, he asks his father-in-law Illuyanka for the heart and eyes (as a bridal gift); restored, the Storm-God comes and slays Illuyanka — but also slays his own son, who has loyally insisted on dying with his Illuyanka in-laws as the price of his honor.

## Cross-tradition identifications
All scholarly parallels (not ancient identifications):
- **[[tiamat]]** — Mesopotamian chaoskampf serpent defeated by Marduk.
- **[[yam]]** — Ugaritic Sea defeated by Baal.
- **[[typhon]]** — Greek serpent defeated by Zeus (Hesiod, Theog. 820–880); West 1997 argues direct transmission via Anatolian intermediaries.
- **Leviathan / Rahab** — Hebrew Bible chaoskampf residues.
- **Vritra** — Vedic Indra's antagonist, the Indo-European cognate via Watkins's serpent-slaying formula.
- **Python** — Greek Apollo's serpent at Delphi (later instance of the type within Greek religion itself).
- **Lotan** — Ugaritic seven-headed serpent (KTU 1.5 I 1–3), the closest verbal parallel to biblical Leviathan and structurally cognate to Illuyanka.

## Iconography
Limited; Anatolian glyptic art preserves storm-god-vs-serpent compositions (Hittite-period seals showing the storm-god striking a serpentine creature).

## Disputes
- **Direct Anatolian transmission to Greek Typhon.** West (1997) argues for direct transmission from the Illuyanka tradition to Hesiod's Typhonomachy; some scholars argue independent Indo-European inheritance (since Watkins shows the formula is PIE-deep). Both are likely: an inherited PIE serpent-slaying formula reinforced by Bronze Age cultural contact.
