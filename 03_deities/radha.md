# Radha

Krishna's beloved among the cowherd women of Vrindavan, and in Gaudiya Vaishnava theology his supreme consort — the personification of *bhakti* itself, the soul yearning for the divine. Radha is theologically *not* Krishna's wife (she is married to another cowherd, Ayan); the *parakiya* — extra-marital — character of her love is read by Gaudiya theologians as expressing the absolute, non-instrumental nature of true devotion.

Radha emerges relatively late in the textual record: she does not appear in the major Puranic Krishna texts (Harivamsha, Vishnu Purana, even the Bhagavata Purana mostly omits her). The first dedicated literary celebration is Jayadeva's Sanskrit *Gīta Govinda* (~+1170, Bengal), which becomes the load-bearing devotional source. The Gaudiya tradition founded by Chaitanya (~+1500) makes Radha-Krishna the supreme divine couple and the soteriological goal: the devotee aspires to be a *sakhi* (female friend) of Radha, participating in her *mahabhava* — her supreme devotional ecstasy.

Comparative resonance with [[sacred-marriage]] and Sufi/bhakti viraha-bhakti (love-in-separation). See [[divine-feminine]].
