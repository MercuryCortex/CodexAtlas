# Holy Spirit

## Identity
The third person of the Christian Trinity — eternally proceeding from the Father (and from the Son, *Filioque*, in Western trinitarian tradition; from the Father alone in Eastern Orthodox doctrine — the central late-antique-medieval theological-political dispute that contributed to the East-West [[event-great-schism-1054]]). The Holy Spirit's distinctive roles in Christian theology: inspiration of the Hebrew Bible prophets and of the New Testament writers; descent on Jesus at his baptism; outpoured at Pentecost as the Spirit of the Church; indwelling believers, distributing charisms, sanctifying.

## Earliest attestation
- Hebrew Bible: *ruaḥ YHWH* (Spirit of YHWH) — the dynamic agency of divine action (Gen 1:2; Judges-prophetic empowerment).
- Second Temple: developing personification (Wisdom-Spirit; Qumran *spirit of truth*).
- New Testament: [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] (the most developed pneumatology), [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] (the Paraclete tradition), [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] (the Spirit-Christology-anthropology fusion).

## Mythological role
- Inspirer of Scripture (2 Tim 3:16; 2 Pet 1:21).
- Agent of incarnation (Lk 1:35).
- The Pentecost-outpouring (Acts 2).
- Indweller of believers (Rom 8).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- Islamic *Rūḥ al-Quds* (Holy Spirit) is identified in Islamic tradition with the angel Gabriel (Q 2:97); not equivalent to the Trinitarian Holy Spirit in Christian theology.
- Jewish *Ruaḥ ha-Qodesh* — divine inspiration, especially prophetic, but never personified as a separate divine person.

## Disputes
- The Filioque clause (added at Toledo 589; defended in the West, rejected in the East).
- Pneumatomachian controversy (4th c., resolved at Constantinople 381).
- Pentecostal-charismatic theological emphases vs. cessationist positions.
