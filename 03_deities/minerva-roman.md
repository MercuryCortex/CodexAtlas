# Minerva

## Identity
Roman goddess of **wisdom, crafts (especially weaving), strategic war, and medicine** — adopted from Etruscan *Menerva* (which itself was probably an indigenous Italic deity rather than a direct Greek import), then assimilated to [[athena]] through *interpretatio romana*. One of the **Capitoline Triad** (Jupiter–Juno–Minerva), the supreme civic-religious triad of the Roman state. Her major Aventine temple was the guild-hall of Roman artisans; her **Quinquatria festival** (March 19–23) was both the artisans' holiday and (because crafts include the military arts) a martial-purification rite.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[athena]]** — *ancient-identification*: Roman cognate.
- **[[neith]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Plato *Timaeus* 21e–22b identifies Egyptian Neith with Athena/Minerva — the deepest ancient cross-tradition identification of the wisdom-warrior-craftswoman figure.
- **[[saraswati]]** — *parallel-motif*: goddess of wisdom and arts.
