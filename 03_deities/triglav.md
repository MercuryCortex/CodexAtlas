# Triglav

## Identity
Triglav ("Three-Headed") is a high god of the **West Slavs**, worshipped above all at **Szczecin** and **Wolin** on the Baltic coast. His three heads were understood to signify his rule over **three realms** — the heavens, the earth, and the underworld — and his eyes and lips were bound with a **golden veil** so that he would not see the sins of mortals, nor be compelled to speak of them.

## Earliest attestation
Described by the 12th-century biographers of **Otto of Bamberg** (Ebbo and Herbord), who recount the Christianization of Pomerania and the destruction of Triglav's temples (c. 1124–1128) — among the fullest eyewitness accounts of any Slavic cult.

## Mythological role — the three realms and the oracle-horse
Triglav's chief temple at Szczecin housed a **sacred black horse** used for divination: it was led across rows of spears laid on the ground, and the god's will was read from whether it touched them. His triple form made him a god of total sovereignty — sky, land, and the dead all under one divinity — and his cult, with its gold-veiled idol, was wealthy enough that the missionaries recorded its plunder in detail.

## Cross-tradition
Triglav is the Slavic instance of the **polycephalic / three-realm god** — the divinity whose multiple heads or faces command distinct domains, a form found in the Greek triple Hekate and in three-function sovereignty schemes across Indo-European religion.

## Iconography
A three-headed idol, eyes and mouth covered with gold; the sacred black horse; the triple-peaked temple.
