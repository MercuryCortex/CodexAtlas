# Bumba

Bumba (also **Mbombo**) is the **supreme creator god** of the **Kuba (Bushongo)** people of the Democratic Republic of Congo, and the central figure of one of Africa's most vivid cosmogonies. In the beginning, the myth tells, there was only **darkness and primeval water**, and within it the gigantic, pale being Bumba, alone. Seized with a violent pain — a stomach-ache — Bumba **vomited up the creation**: first the **sun**, whose heat dried the waters to reveal land; then the **moon and the stars**; and then, in further bouts of vomiting, the **animals** — the leopard, the eagle, the crocodile, the fish, the tortoise, and others — and finally **human beings**. The creatures in turn brought forth their own kinds, populating the ordered world. Having made and arranged all things, Bumba surveyed his creation, pronounced it good, and withdrew, leaving humankind in possession of the earth. As a high-god creator from whom the whole cosmos issues, Bumba belongs with the great African supreme deities — the Akan **[[nyame|Nyame]]**, the Bantu **[[mulungu|Mulungu]]** and Leza — here expressing creation through the strikingly bodily image of the world brought up out of the creator himself. Bumba embodies the Kuba vision of an ordered, good world drawn from a single original being.
