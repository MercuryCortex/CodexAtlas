# Belobog

Belobog ("the White God") is the supposed Slavic god of **light, day, and good fortune** — and a figure whose reality is genuinely uncertain. He is known almost entirely as the **bright counterpart to [[chernobog|Chernobog]]** ("the Black God"), in a proposed cosmic dualism of white against black, luck against misfortune, day against night. The crucial problem is the evidence: only **Chernobog** is attested in an early source — the 12th-century *Chronica Slavorum* of **Helmold of Bosau**, who reports that the Wendish Slavs toasted both a good god and an evil "Zcerneboch" — whereas **Belobog himself appears in no early text**, surfacing only in later sources, in **place-names** (hills called *Bělbog*), and in the inferences of Romantic-era and modern scholars who reconstructed him by symmetry from Chernobog's name. Much of the neat "white-god/black-god" dualism, so often repeated, may therefore be an **over-systematization** rather than authentic Slavic theology. Belobog is included here as a real object of scholarship — but flagged as **largely reconstructed**, a cautionary case of how a tidy opposition can be built from a single attested half.
