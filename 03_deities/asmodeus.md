# Asmodeus

## Identity
Demon-king of lust in Second Temple Jewish demonology — name derived from **Avestan *Aēšma daēva*** ("Demon of Wrath"), one of the clearest documented imports of Zoroastrian demonology into Jewish religious thought during the Achaemenid Persian period (c. -550 to -330). In the **Book of Tobit** (~3rd c. BCE) Asmodeus has killed Sarah's seven successive husbands on their wedding nights out of demonic love for her; the archangel Raphael instructs Tobias to burn fish-liver-and-heart smoke, driving Asmodeus to "the upper regions of Egypt" where Raphael binds him. **Babylonian Talmud Gittin 68a–b** narrates that Asmedai briefly tricks King Solomon out of his magic ring and rules Israel in his place — a foundational Jewish legend of demonic-kingship-impersonation.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Aēšma daēva** — *ancient-identification*: Persian etymological source.
- **[[satan-christian]] / [[lucifer]] / [[beelzebub]]** — *parallel-motif*: medieval Christian demonology often catalogues Asmodeus as one of the seven princes of Hell (e.g. Peter Binsfeld 1589: Asmodeus = prince of lust).
