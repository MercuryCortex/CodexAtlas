# Sethlans

## Identity
Sethlans is the **Etruscan divine smith** — the god of fire and the forge, master of the metalworker's craft. He is the Etruscan member of the single Mediterranean smith-god mediated between the Greek [[hephaestus]] and the Roman [[vulcan-roman]].

## Earliest attestation
Known chiefly from **engraved bronze mirrors** and votive contexts of the Etruscan cities (7th–2nd c. BCE), where he appears at his characteristic mythological moments.

## Mythological role — the smith who opens the god's head
Sethlans' signature scene on Etruscan mirrors is the **birth of Menrva**: it is Sethlans who strikes open the head of the sky-king [[tinia]] with his axe so that the armed goddess springs forth — the Etruscan rendering of the Greek myth in which Hephaestus splits Zeus's head at Athena's birth. He is the craftsman whose fire and tools serve the other gods.

## Cross-tradition — the divine-smith type
Sethlans belongs to the **divine-smith** neighborhood: the craftsman-god who forges the gods' wonders, alongside [[hephaestus]] (Greece), [[vulcan-roman]] (Rome), Ptah (Egypt), Kothar (Canaan), and Wayland (Germania). Through Etruria this figure passes from Greek into Roman religion.

## Iconography
A bearded craftsman in a conical workman's cap, with hammer, tongs, and axe; the forge-fire.
