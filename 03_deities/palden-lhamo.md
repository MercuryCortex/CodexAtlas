# Palden Lhamo

Palden Lhamo (*dPal ldan lha mo*, "Glorious Goddess"; Sanskrit **Śrī Devī**) is the supreme **female Dharma-protector (Dharmapāla)** of Tibetan Buddhism — the **only female among the principal wrathful protectors** and one of the most important guardian deities of the whole tradition. She is the special protectress of **Lhasa**, of the **Gelug** school, and of the **Dalai Lamas**, whose lineage she watches over; the sacred oracle-lake **Lhamo La-tso**, in whose waters visions are sought to locate the reincarnations of high lamas, is hers. Her iconography is among the most fearsome in Buddhist art: a wrathful, dark figure riding a **mule across a sea of blood**, the mule's hindquarters bearing an **eye** grown over a wound, carrying a skull-cup and a club, crowned with skulls. This terrifying form, derived from the fierce Indian goddesses (**[[kali|Kālī]]** / Śrī Devī / Durgā) and Buddhicized, is the energy of a protector who annihilates the enemies of the Dharma. Among the wrathful guardians she is the female counterpart to **[[mahakala|Mahākāla]]**. Palden Lhamo embodies the ferocious compassion of the protector — wrath turned wholly to the guarding of the teaching and its people.
