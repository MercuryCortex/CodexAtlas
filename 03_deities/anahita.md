# Anāhitā

## Identity
Anāhitā ("the Immaculate" or "the Spotless") is the great Zoroastrian goddess of waters, fertility, purity, wisdom, and martial prowess. Her full Avestan name is *Arədvī Sūrā Anāhitā* — "Strong Damp Spotless One" — the celestial source of all waters, flowing down from the cosmic mountain Hara Berezaiti to the great cosmic sea Vourukaša. Her hymn, the Ābān Yašt (Yašt 5), is one of the longest and most vivid in the Avestan corpus. Like Mithra she is absent from the Gathas and central in the Younger Avesta; the two figures are the most prominent restored deities of the Indo-Iranian inheritance.

Artaxerxes II (404–358 BCE) explicitly invokes Anāhitā alongside Ahura Mazda and Mithra in royal inscriptions, and according to Berossus (via Clement of Alexandria) established cultic statues of her — the first known monumental cult-image worship in Iranian religion, a likely Mesopotamian-influenced innovation. Under Achaemenid and especially Seleucid / Parthian rule Anāhitā was widely identified with Mesopotamian Ishtar, Greek Aphrodite, Artemis, and Athena — the syncretism extends from Asia Minor through the Iranian plateau. Her cult continued through Sasanian times (the temple at Iṣṭakhr was a great Anāhitā center, where the early Sasanian kings served as hereditary priests).
