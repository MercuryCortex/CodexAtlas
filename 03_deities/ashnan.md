# Ashnan

## Identity

Ashnan (also **Ezina**) is the Sumerian goddess of grain — the personified power of the cereal crop, of the cultivated field and its yield of barley and wheat. She is the agricultural counterpart of [[lahar]], the god of the flocks: where Lahar stands for herding (wool, milk, meat), Ashnan stands for farming (grain, bread, beer). Together they personify the two halves of the Mesopotamian food economy.

## Mythological role

Ashnan is known chiefly from the disputation *Lahar and Ashnan* (*Ewe and Grain*). The poem's prologue tells how, in the holy mound the **Duku**, the gods had as yet no proper bread or clothing; so Ashnan (Grain) and Lahar (the Ewe) were created there to feed and clothe the Anunnaki, and were then sent down to humankind. Among humans the two goddesses dispute, each extolling her own gifts — grain boasting of bread and beer and the sustenance of the field, the ewe boasting of wool and milk and the wealth of the flock — until the gods settle the quarrel. The myth dramatizes the divine origin of agriculture and the cultural rivalry of farming and herding.

## Cross-tradition significance

As a grain-deity, Ashnan belongs to the widespread ancient category of personified cereal-divinity — the sacred grain whose growth, death (harvest), and renewal mythologize the agricultural cycle, a complex that elsewhere produces figures from the Greek Demeter to the dying-and-rising vegetation gods. Within Mesopotamia she pairs with [[nisaba]] as a grain-goddess, and her disputation with Lahar embodies the deep farmer-versus-herder tension that recurs across Near Eastern literature, including the Genesis story of the farmer Cain and the herdsman Abel.
