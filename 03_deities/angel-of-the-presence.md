# Angel of the Presence (Angel of the Face)

## Identity
The **Angel of the Presence** (Hebrew *malʾak panim*, "angel of the face/presence"; Ge'ez *malʾaka qǝddase*) is the chief angelic revealer in the **Book of Jubilees** — the high-ranking archangel who *dictates Jubilees itself* to Moses on Sinai. The framing device of the entire book is that Moses receives both Torah and Jubilees (as a complementary revelation) on Sinai, with God dictating directly some parts and the Angel of the Presence dictating others. This makes the Angel of the Presence *the angelic narrator of the Book of Jubilees as a whole* (in the framing of the text itself), and one of the most theologically elevated angelic figures in Second-Temple-Jewish literature.

The figure is canonical scripture in [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]] (where Jubilees is the *Mashafa Kufale*, a full canonical book of the Old Testament), making this one of the angelic figures with continuous canonical-scriptural status in Ethiopian Christianity that is essentially unknown to mainstream Western Christianity except through the post-19th-c. recovery of pseudepigraphic Second-Temple-Jewish literature.

## Earliest attestation
- **Isaiah 63:9** — *"In all their distress he too was distressed, and the angel of his presence saved them"* — the foundational Hebrew Bible phrase, ambiguous in reference but later read as proto-archangelic.
- **Jubilees Prologue + 1:27–29 + 2:1** — the framing-dictation event on Sinai: God commands "the angel of the presence" to write the history for Moses, "saying: Write the whole account of creation..." The Angel of the Presence speaks in first-person voice for most of the book.
- **Jubilees 6:22; 12:25–26; 16:1, 15; 17:11; 18:9–12; etc.** — recurring presence across Jubilees.
- **Qumran 4Q400–407 (Songs of the Sabbath Sacrifice)** — sectarian liturgical texts featuring angels of the presence in heavenly-worship contexts.
- **Ge'ez Mashafa Kufale** — only complete textual witness.

## Mythological role
The Angel of the Presence's three distinct functions in Jubilees:

1. **Dictation of Torah and Jubilees to Moses on Sinai.** This is the foundational framing: Sinai revelation is not direct-divine-speech alone but mediated through this archangel. The theological move *interpolates a high-angelic-mediator* between God and Moses for the Sinai event — a move with significant downstream theological consequences. (The Pauline argument in Galatians 3:19 — "the Torah was ordained through angels by the hand of a mediator" — is engaging this same Second-Temple framework, though probably indirectly.)

2. **Intercession before the divine presence.** The Angel intercedes for Israel and stands "before the face" of God (the etymology of *malʾak panim* — the angel who is in the divine presence).

3. **Chief of the holy angels.** Distinct from Mastema (the prince of *evil* spirits), the Angel of the Presence is the chief of the *holy* angels — the leader of the angelic-administrative ranks on the righteous side of cosmic affairs.

The figure stands theologically between:
- the *malʾak YHWH* (the angel-of-the-LORD) of the Pentateuch — sometimes spoken of as if identical with YHWH himself,
- the Enochic Son of Man (the Elect One pre-existently named before the Head of Days),
- the later rabbinic *Metatron* — the great chief angel of Hekhalot mysticism, sometimes called "the lesser YHWH."

Orlov 2005 demonstrates the Angel of the Presence is one of the load-bearing pre-Hekhalot precursors of Metatron, with the Jubilees-figure feeding into the later Enoch-Metatron tradition (where Enoch is exalted to become Metatron — itself a development of 1 Enoch 71:14's Enoch-as-Son-of-Man identification).

## Cross-tradition identifications

- **[[metatron]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Angel of the Presence is one of the foundational pre-Hekhalot streams feeding into the rabbinic Metatron tradition (Orlov 2005). The "Lesser YHWH" / "Prince of the Presence" titles in 3 Enoch (Hekhalot) develop directly from this Second-Temple substrate.
- **[[michael-archangel]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: some Second-Temple Jewish traditions identify the Angel of the Presence with Michael as chief of holy angels; the identification is not universal.
- **[[melchizedek]]** (11QMelchizedek) — *scholarly-parallel*: the heavenly-priestly Melchizedek of Qumran 11Q13 shares structural features (Kobelski 1981).
- **Christ as mediator** (Hebrews 1, Pauline Galatians 3:19) — *parallel-form*: early Christian mediation-theology engages the same Second-Temple framework that the Angel of the Presence inhabits.

## Iconography
Not iconographically distinct in Ethiopian Orthodox art from general angelic-presence imagery.

## Disputes
- **Identity with Michael.** Whether the Jubilees Angel of the Presence *is* Michael under different title. Most scholarship treats them as distinct-but-related figures (the Angel of the Presence is more individuated as the *dictator* of Jubilees specifically).
- **Relationship to Metatron.** Whether direct development or one of multiple streams feeding the later Metatron tradition. Orlov 2005 demonstrates substantial direct continuity.
- **The "lesser YHWH" question.** Whether the Angel of the Presence's near-divine status (speaking and acting on God's behalf, mediating Sinai revelation) crosses the boundary into "two powers in heaven" territory. Segal 1977 and Boyarin 2004 see this as part of the broader bi-throne / two-powers pattern in Second-Temple Judaism.
