# Poseidon

## Identity
Poseidon is the Greek god of the sea, of earthquakes (*Enosichthon*, "Earthshaker"), and of horses. One of the three sons of Kronos who divided the cosmos by lot — Zeus took the sky, Hades the underworld, Poseidon the sea (Iliad 15.187–193). He wields the trident, drives a chariot drawn by hippocamps, and is, in the Odyssey, the principal divine antagonist of Odysseus after the blinding of his son Polyphemus.

The Linear B tablets (Pylos especially) attest Poseidon (po-se-da-o) prominently — possibly more prominently than Zeus in the Mycenaean cult of Pylos. This has led to the hypothesis that Poseidon was the preeminent god of Mycenaean Pylos, demoted in the post-Mycenaean rationalization to the role of sea-god / second sibling. His domains of horses and earthquakes might be retained traces of an older "underground / chthonic / generative" role. His name's etymology is uncertain; "husband of Da" (Earth?) and "drink-giver" have both been proposed.
