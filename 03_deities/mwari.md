# Mwari

## Identity
Supreme being of Shona traditional religion (Zimbabwe, parts of Mozambique and South Africa) — creator, sender of rain, owner of the land. Mwari is distinctive among sub-Saharan-African high-gods in possessing an active oracular cult: the voice of Mwari speaks from specific caves in the Matopos hills near Bulawayo, mediated by hereditary medium-priests of the Mwari cult. This makes Mwari more *deus revelatus* (the revealing god, accessible through specific cult-mechanisms) than *deus otiosus* (the high god remote from human affairs). Daneel (1970) is the standard ethnography.

## Earliest attestation
The Mwari cult at Matopos is associated archaeologically with the Great Zimbabwe culture (c. 1100–1450), though oral tradition makes the cult considerably older. The Rozvi confederacy (16th–17th c.) was closely tied to the Mwari oracle. Colonial-era European reports document the cult from the 19th c.

## Mythological role
Mwari is the creator and the rain-sender. The Mwari oracle at Matopos historically integrated political, agricultural, and military life: chiefs sought legitimation through the oracle; farmers sought rain-prayers; warriors sought sanction. The oracle's voice is mediated by hereditary medium-priests (the priesthood is itself heritable in specific Rozvi lineages). The cult survived colonial repression and continues today in modified form.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Christian God** — 19th-c missionaries used "Mwari" as Shona-Bible translation for *theos*, with the same consequences and ambiguities as the Yoruba Olodumare-God translation. Shona Independent Churches subsequently developed a Mwari-Christianity synthesis (Daneel 1971) preserving Mwari-cult elements within Pentecostal/Apostolic Christian forms.

## Cross-tradition parallels
- **[[olodumare]]** (Yoruba), **[[nyame]]** (Akan), **[[unkulunkulu-zulu]]** (Zulu) — sub-Saharan-African supreme-being archetype. The mode of accessibility varies (Mwari oracular; Olodumare via orisha; Nyame remote; Unkulunkulu remote).
- The supreme-being-with-active-cult pattern distinguishes Mwari from the "deus otiosus" generalization that some early 20th-c comparative religion (Eliade 1958) applied too broadly to African high-gods. Recent African-Religions scholarship (Mbiti 1969; Daneel 1970; p'Bitek 1971) has dismantled the deus-otiosus stereotype.

## Iconography
No anthropomorphic image; Mwari is approached through the voice from the cave. The eagle is the cult-animal. Rain itself is sometimes referenced as Mwari's manifestation.
