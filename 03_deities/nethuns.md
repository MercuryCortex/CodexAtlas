# Nethuns

## Identity
Nethuns is the Etruscan god of water — springs, lakes, rivers, and eventually the sea — and the probable etymological source of the Roman god **[[neptune-roman]]** (Latin *Neptunus*). He appears in the **Piacenza Liver** (the bronze liver used for Etruscan divination, c. 100 BCE) in a section associated with the water domain, placing him securely within the Etruscan divine hierarchy. Unlike Roman Neptune's predominantly maritime identity, the earliest Etruscan evidence associates Nethuns with **freshwater sources** — springs, rivers, and sacred lakes — reflecting the centrality of thermal and sacred springs in Etruscan sacred geography. The great thermal springs of Etruria (Saturnia, Vicarello, Viterbo) were nodes of Etruscan sacred landscape; Nethuns's domain encompassed their divine power.

## Earliest attestation
- The **Piacenza Liver** (c. 100 BCE) names Nethuns in a section of the divination organ associated with water, establishing his position in the Etruscan divine council.
- Etruscan inscriptions and votive deposits at spring sanctuaries across Etruria, beginning c. 6th century BCE.
- The phonological proximity of *Nethuns* to *Neptunus* (Latin) suggests early transmission; the Roman festival *Neptunalia* (July 23) may preserve an Etruscan freshwater origin — it was celebrated near waterways, not the sea.

## Mythological role
Nethuns presided over the Etruscan sacred waters: the thermal springs (*fontes calidi*) that dotted the volcanic landscape of Etruria, the crater lakes of the Alban Hills and Lake Bolsena, and the river systems of the Tiber and Arno watersheds. Springs were theologically significant in Etruscan religion as liminal points where the divine world surfaced into the human — water rising from underground was a form of divine communication, and spring sanctuaries were sites of healing, oracle, and votive deposit.

As Etruscan-Roman contact deepened and Roman sea-power expanded, Nethuns's domain was assimilated to the Greek **[[poseidon]]** sea-god model through *interpretatio Graeca*. The later Roman Neptune inherits both the Etruscan freshwater-spring tradition and the Greek sea-god tradition, creating a composite deity. The freshwater-spring origin of the *Neptunalia* festival preserves a trace of Nethuns's pre-maritime character.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[neptune-roman]]** — *ancient-identification*: The name *Neptunus* almost certainly derives from Etruscan *Nethuns*; the water-deity role transferred with the name. This is one of the most phonologically direct Etruscan-to-Latin divine name transmissions documented.
- **[[poseidon]]** (Greek) — *ancient-identification* (via Roman Neptune): Neptune was identified with Poseidon through *interpretatio Graeca*; Nethuns's domain thus maps onto Poseidon's maritime sovereignty through this chain.
- **[[enki-ea]]** (Sumerian/Akkadian) — *scholarly-parallel*: Enki/Ea governs the *abzu* (the sacred underground sweet-water ocean); Nethuns's freshwater-spring domain parallels Enki's rule over the subterranean waters that feed springs and rivers. Both are water-deities associated with wisdom, civilization's foundations, and sacred underground sources.
- **[[varuna]]** (Vedic) — *scholarly-parallel*: Varuna began as the cosmic sovereign who oversees cosmic order (*rita*) and shifted toward water-lordship as his primary domain in later Vedic texts — the same trajectory Nethuns underwent (freshwater spring deity → sea deity through Roman contact). Both represent domain-expansion along the water axis.
- **[[ran]]** (Norse) — *scholarly-parallel*: Ran is the Norse goddess of the sea who collects the drowned; she represents the sea's power over human life as Nethuns's later sea-domain also encompasses.
- **[[njord]]** (Norse) — *scholarly-parallel*: Njord presides over coastal waters, seafaring, and fishing — the maritime water-deity role that Nethuns's later form (via Neptune) occupies.
- **[[aegir-norse]]** (Norse) — *scholarly-parallel*: Aegir is the Norse personification of the primal ocean; his domain maps onto Nethuns's expanded sea-deity character.

## Iconography
Nethuns in his later (Hellenized) form adopts the Poseidon-Neptune type: a mature bearded male with trident, accompanied by marine creatures (dolphins, sea-horses, fish). In his earlier, more distinctly Etruscan form, iconography is associated with spring-water contexts — water-vessels, fish of freshwater rivers, and the springs of the Etruscan thermal landscape.

## Disputes
- **Etymology**: The connection of *Nethuns* to *Neptunus* is widely accepted but not phonologically transparent; alternative derivations from a PIE root *\*nebh-* ("moist," "cloud") have been proposed for Neptune independently. Whether Nethuns is the source of the Latin name or both derive independently from an earlier common root remains debated.
- **Freshwater vs. maritime priority**: Older scholarship treated Neptune as a sea-god from the beginning; more recent work (Dumézil, Radke) argues for a freshwater origin (springs, rivers) consistent with the *Neptunalia* festival context and with Nethuns's Etruscan evidence. The maritime identity is likely secondary, developed through contact with Greek Poseidon.
