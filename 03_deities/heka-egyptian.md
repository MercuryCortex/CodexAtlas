# Heka

## Identity

Heka (Egyptian *ḥkȝ*) is both the Egyptian word for **magic** and the god who personifies it. But "magic" understates him: Heka is the fundamental **creative-efficacious energy** of the cosmos — the power that makes creation happen, that gives the gods' words and the priest's rituals their effect, that animates and protects. He is "the one before whom the gods tremble," a force older than the differentiated world and instrumental in its making.

## Mythological role

Heka is said to have existed **before duality** — "before two things had developed in this land" — that is, before the split of the primordial unity into the manifold world. He is the power by which the creator brought the cosmos into being and by which that creation is daily renewed. In the solar theology, Heka travels in the **barque of [[ra]]** as one of the great powers attending the sun-god on his journey, together with **[[hu-sia|Hu and Sia]]** — utterance and perception. At Esna he was worshipped as a child-god, son of the ram-god Khnum and the lioness Menhit. Because magic was understood as the force that made *any* effective action possible — including healing and protection from danger — Heka was also closely tied to medicine and to the apotropaic arts, frequently shown grasping serpents to signify mastery over the most dangerous powers.

## Cross-tradition significance

Heka embodies the Egyptian conviction that **the cosmos is sustained by an efficacious creative power** wielded through perception, speech, and ritual. As the energizing force behind the creator's word, he is part of the distinctive Egyptian theology of **creation by intelligence and utterance** (most fully developed in the Memphite doctrine; see [[hu-sia]] and [[ptah]]), in which the world is made by the thought of the heart and the command of the tongue — a conception that offers one of the closest ancient parallels to the later notion of creation by the divine word (the *logos* of John 1, the "and God said" of Genesis 1).
