# Mahuika

## Identity
Mahuika is the Māori **goddess of fire** — the keeper of the sacred flame, who holds fire in her **fingernails and toenails**, and from whom humankind first won the gift of fire.

## Earliest attestation
Pan-Polynesian oral tradition (Māori, with cognates such as Tahitian/Hawaiian *Mafuike*), recorded by 19th- and 20th-century ethnographers; central to the great cycle of the trickster-hero [[maui-polynesian]].

## Mythological role — the winning of fire
The defining myth: [[maui-polynesian]], wanting fire for humankind, visits his fire-goddess kinswoman Mahuika and asks for fire; she gives him one of her burning **fingernails**. Mischievously he extinguishes it and returns again and again, until she has given up nearly all her fiery nails. Enraged at last, Mahuika hurls her remaining fire across the world; Māui flees the flames, and Mahuika casts the last spark into the **kaikōmako tree** — and so fire comes to live in wood, to be drawn out ever after by friction. It is the Polynesian account of how humankind acquired fire.

## Cross-tradition
Mahuika anchors the Polynesian form of the **fire-theft / fire-gift** motif — the winning of fire from its divine guardian for the benefit of mortals — structurally parallel to the Greek [[prometheus]], and a Pacific instance of the universal myth of the origin of fire.

## Iconography
A fierce ancestress with fire in her nails; the burning tree; the friction fire-drill.
