# Nanaya

## Identity

Nanaya (Nanāya) is a Mesopotamian goddess of erotic love and sensuality who emerged at **Uruk** in close association with [[inanna-sumerian]]. Where Inanna/Ishtar was a vast and many-sided goddess — of love and war, of the morning and evening star — Nanaya came to specialize and personify the **amorous, sensual** aspect: feminine allure, the joy of love, music and song. She became, over time, one of the most widely and enduringly worshipped of all Mesopotamian deities.

## Mythological role and cult

Nanaya is celebrated above all in love-poetry and hymns that dwell on her beauty and erotic power. Originally a near-double of Inanna at Uruk, she gradually acquired an independent profile and a remarkably durable cult. In the Babylonian period she was paired with [[nabu]], the scribal god of Borsippa, as his consort. Her worship spread far beyond Mesopotamia: into Syria, into Elam and Iran (where she was associated with Anahita), and into the Hellenistic and Parthian world, where Greeks identified her with Artemis. This unusually wide and long-lived diffusion makes Nanaya one of the great "exportable" goddesses of the ancient Near East.

## Cross-tradition significance

Nanaya is a key witness to the **dispersion of the Mesopotamian love-goddess**. As a specialized form of Inanna/[[ishtar-akkadian]] who took on a life of her own and traveled across the entire Near East — fusing with Anahita in Iran and Artemis in the Greek world — she illustrates how a single divine type could be carried, renamed, and re-rooted across cultures. For an investigation of how the great goddess of love and her attributes moved between traditions, Nanaya is one of the most far-reaching threads.
