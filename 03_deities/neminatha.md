# Neminātha

Neminātha (also **Ariṣṭanemi**, "Nemi") is the **twenty-second Tīrthaṅkara** ("ford-maker") of Jainism — one of the twenty-four enlightened teachers who, in each cosmic age, reveal the path across the ocean of rebirth. His defining story is among the most beloved in Jain literature and a perfect emblem of **ahiṃsā** (non-violence): a Yādava prince of **Dvārakā** and, in Jain tradition, a **cousin of [[krishna|Kṛṣṇa]]**, Nemi was riding to his own wedding to the princess **Rājīmatī** when he heard the terrified cries of the animals **penned to be slaughtered for the wedding feast**. Overcome with compassion, he turned his chariot around, renounced the world rather than be the cause of such killing, and went to **Mount Girnar**, where he attained omniscience (*kevala-jñāna*) and final liberation; Rājīmatī, in devotion, renounced after him. His emblem is the **conch**. By weaving Neminātha into the family of Kṛṣṇa, Jain tradition stakes its own claim on the great Yādava saga while making him the supreme exemplar of the Jain refusal to harm any living being.
