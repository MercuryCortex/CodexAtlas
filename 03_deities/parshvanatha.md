# Parshvanatha

Parshvanatha (Sanskrit *Pārśvanātha*) is the **23rd Tīrthaṅkara** ("ford-maker") of Jainism and the most historically substantiated Jina before Mahavira — most scholars accept him as a real ascetic teacher of roughly the 8th–9th century BCE. He completes the vault's coverage of the Jain ford-makers, which already holds the first (**[[rishabha-jain]]**) and the twenty-fourth (**[[mahavira-jain]]**). His iconographic signature is the **seven-hooded cobra** arching protectively over his head — the serpent-king Dharanendra and his queen Padmavati shielding the meditating Parshva from a storm raised by a demon, a scene that makes his images instantly recognizable. Historically he is pivotal: Mahavira's own parents are said to have belonged to Parshva's order, and Mahavira's reform consisted in extending Parshva's **four restraints** (the *cāturyāma*) into the **five great vows** of classical Jainism — so Parshvanatha is, in effect, the founder of the tradition that Mahavira systematized.
