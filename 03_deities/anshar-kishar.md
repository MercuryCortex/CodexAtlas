# Anshar and Kishar

## Identity
Anshar and Kishar (Akkadian *Anšar* and *Kišar*; Sumerian *AN.ŠÁR* "totality of heaven / heaven-horizon" and *KI.ŠÁR* "totality of earth / earth-horizon") are the **second primordial pair** in the canonical Babylonian cosmogony of the *Enūma Eliš* — the parents of **Anu (An)** and grandparents of Ea (Enki). They are born from the first primordial pair [[lahmu-lahamu]], who in turn are born from the mingling of the primordial saltwater [[tiamat]] and the primordial freshwater [[apsu]]. The pair-name-etymology — heaven-totality / earth-totality — encodes their cosmogonic function: they are the **first articulation of the heaven-and-earth duality** out of the undifferentiated primordial waters, prior to the personalization of the sky-god Anu and the earth/underworld principles. (Lambert 2013; Dalley 1989.)

In late Assyrian theology (Sennacherib and the Sargonid kings, ~-700 onward), **Anshar is identified with [[ashur]]** — the national god of Assyria — and the *Enūma Eliš* is rewritten with Ashur in Marduk's role. This is a politically-motivated theological centralization consonant with Assyrian imperial ideology (Lambert 2013).

## Earliest attestation
- **Sumerian god-list traditions** (~-2500 onward) — Anshar and Kishar appear in pre-Babylonian Sumerian-Akkadian god-list traditions as a primordial pair predating their canonical role in the *Enūma Eliš*. The pair are inherited Mesopotamian cosmogonic vocabulary, not a Babylonian innovation.
- **Enūma Eliš Tablet I** (canonical Babylonian form ~-1100 written; older textual layers) — the principal extended attestation; Anshar and Kishar are named as parents of Anu in the cosmogonic genealogy (Tablet I lines 11-16). ([[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]])
- **Neo-Assyrian Anshar = Ashur recensions** (~-700 onward) — Sennacherib's and later Sargonid theology identifies Anshar with Ashur; surviving Neo-Assyrian *Enūma Eliš* recensions document this.

## Mythological role
Anshar and Kishar's mythological role is **purely genealogical-cosmogonic** — they have no narrative myth of their own. They function as the second articulation in the Babylonian cosmogonic genealogy:

1. **Mingling of waters** → [[apsu]] (freshwater) + [[tiamat]] (saltwater).
2. **First primordial pair** → [[lahmu-lahamu]] (Lahmu and Lahamu, "the hairy one" and his consort — perhaps a primordial cosmic-waters serpent-pair).
3. **Second primordial pair** → **Anshar and Kishar** (heaven-totality and earth-totality — first articulation of the cosmic duality).
4. **Anu / An** → sky-god, son of Anshar-Kishar.
5. **Ea / Enki** → grandson, the cunning god who slays Apsu and inaugurates the conflict that leads to Marduk's cosmogonic battle with Tiamat.

The Anshar-Kishar generation is therefore the **transition from undifferentiated primordial waters to differentiated cosmic space** — the conceptual moment at which heaven and earth become distinct principles capable of generating personal deities. They are abstract cosmogonic principles personified into a pair, much as Greek Ouranos-and-Gaia in Hesiod's *Theogony* play the equivalent structural role.

In the Assyrian recension Anshar-as-Ashur plays a more active narrative role — Ashur takes Marduk's place as the cosmic warrior who slays Tiamat, with corresponding political theology of Ashur's universal kingship.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ashur]]** — *ancient-identification* (Assyrian-political): Anshar = Ashur in late Assyrian theology; politically-motivated.
- Greek Ouranos-and-Gaia in Hesiod's *Theogony* play the same structural role (heaven-totality and earth-totality as primordial pair generating the gods); this is *parallel-form* / structural cognacy at the level of the cosmogonic-genealogy pattern, NOT documented transmission. The structural parallel is noted by comparative-religion scholarship (West 1997 *The East Face of Helicon*) without claiming direct Mesopotamia-to-Greek transmission at this specific point.

## Iconography
None canonical. Anshar and Kishar are primordial-abstract figures without iconographic attestation in the Mesopotamian visual record; they exist primarily in the god-lists and the cosmogonic-genealogy of the *Enūma Eliš*. The late Assyrian Anshar-as-Ashur identification inherits Ashur's iconography (the winged-disk above a warrior figure with bow) but this is Ashur's iconography, not original to Anshar.

## Disputes
- **Etymological reading of *an-šár* / *ki-šár*.** "Totality of heaven / earth" or "horizon of heaven / earth" — both readings are defensible philologically; the cosmogonic-totality reading is mainstream.
- **Anshar = Ashur identification: political or genuinely-theological.** Lambert 2013 reads it as politically-motivated Assyrian imperial-religious centralization; some scholars argue for a more genuinely-theological substrate (an older Anshar-Ashur cognacy independent of Sennacherib's program). The mainstream view follows Lambert.
- **Sumerian vs. Akkadian / Babylonian originality.** Anshar-Kishar are clearly inherited Mesopotamian vocabulary attested in older Sumerian god-lists; their *narrative* canonical role in the *Enūma Eliš* is Babylonian (Lambert 2013).
