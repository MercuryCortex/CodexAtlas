# Hebe

Hebe (Greek *Hḗbē*, "Youth," the bloom of early adulthood) is the daughter of **[[zeus]]** and **[[hera]]** and the goddess of youthful vigor. On Olympus she is the **cupbearer** who pours the nectar and ambrosia for the gods (a role she later yields to the Trojan boy Ganymede), and she performs domestic services for her mother Hera. Her defining myth is her marriage to **[[heracles]]** upon his apotheosis: when the hero is received among the immortals, Hebe becomes his divine bride, sealing his reconciliation with Hera — a union celebrated by Hesiod and Pindar as the reward of toil with eternal youth. She had genuine cult (notably at Phlius and Sicyon, where she was worshipped as a goddess of pardon, *Dia*), and the Romans identified her with **Iuventas**.
