# Kotharat

## Identity

The Kotharat (Ugaritic **Kôṯarātu**, "the skillful ones") are a Canaanite **heptad** — a group of seven goddesses who preside over conception, childbirth, and marriage. Their name comes from the root *k-ṯ-r*, "to be skilled," the same root as the craftsman-god [[kothar-wa-khasis]]: where he is the divine artisan of metal and weaponry, the Kotharat are the divine artisans of new life. They are associated with swallows, perhaps reflecting the swallow's return as a sign of seasonal renewal and fertility.

## Mythological role

The Kotharat are most prominent in two Ugaritic poems. In *The Marriage of Yarikh and Nikkal* (KTU 1.24) they attend the wedding of the moon-god [[yarikh]] and his bride Nikkal, presiding over the union and the conception to follow — the text invokes them as the "skillful goddesses, daughters of the new moon." In the *Epic of Aqhat*, the hero Danel entertains the Kotharat for seven days when his long-awaited son is to be conceived, securing their blessing on the birth. In both, they are the divine guarantors of fertile marriage and successful childbirth.

## Cross-tradition significance

As a **heptad of birth-and-marriage goddesses**, the Kotharat belong to the widespread ancient pattern of plural birth- or fate-goddesses who attend conception and delivery — comparable to the Mesopotamian birth-goddesses, the Egyptian Meskhenet/Heqet, and the later Greek Eileithyia and the Moirai who appear at a child's birth to fix its destiny. They are a useful node for the cross-cultural complex of divine midwives and the sacralization of childbirth.
