# Typhon

## Identity
Typhon (Greek *Typhōn*, also *Typhōeus*, *Typhaōn*) is the chaos-serpent antagonist of Zeus in Hesiod's *Theogony* (lines 820–880) — the last and greatest of the monstrous offspring of Gaia and Tartarus, a hundred-headed dragon with fire-breathing mouths and a serpentine lower body who challenges Zeus for the sovereignty of the cosmos. Zeus defeats him with the thunderbolt and imprisons him beneath the earth (in Sicily under Mt. Etna in the Pindaric / Aeschylean version). Typhon is the **Greek instance of the wider Indo-European and Near-Eastern chaoskampf serpent-monster type** — cognate with Hittite Illuyanka, Ugaritic Lotan, biblical Leviathan, Vedic Vritra, Mesopotamian Tiamat, and (parallel-form, distinct branch) Egyptian Apophis (West 1997, 2007; Watkins 1995; Day 1985).

## Earliest attestation
- **Hesiod, *Theogony* 820–880** (~-700) — the principal early Greek narrative.
- Earlier oral attestation is inferred from the multiple variant traditions (Hesiod, Homeric *Hymn to Apollo* 305ff., Apollodorus 1.6.3) — the myth was already standard by Hesiod's time.
- Iconographic attestations on Chalcidian and Athenian black-figure vases (6th c. BCE).

## Mythological role
**The Typhonomachy** (combat of Typhon and Zeus). Typhon, born of Gaia (Earth) and Tartarus as her response to Zeus's defeat of the Titans and Giants, attacks Olympus. Zeus battles him with thunderbolts; in some versions (Apollodorus) Typhon initially defeats Zeus, cuts his sinews, and hides them in the Corycian cave in Cilicia — until Hermes and Pan retrieve them. Zeus ultimately throws Mt. Etna over Typhon, whose breathings cause volcanic eruptions (Pindar *Pythian* 1; Aeschylus *Prometheus Bound* 351ff.).

The **Cilician localization** of the combat (Mt. Casius / the Corycian cave) is the principal textual fingerprint of West's argument for Anatolian-Hittite transmission of the myth via the Illuyanka tradition.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[illuyanka]]** — *parallel-form / Anatolian substrate*: West 1997 argues for direct Anatolian-Hittite transmission; Watkins 1995 traces the inherited PIE formula.
- **[[tiamat]]** — *parallel-form*: Mesopotamian chaoskampf serpent.
- **[[lotan]]** — *parallel-form*: Ugaritic seven-headed serpent — the closest verbal-iconographic parallel.
- **[[vritra]]** — *parallel-form*: Vedic instance of the inherited Indo-European serpent-slaying formula.
- **[[apophis]]** — *parallel-form*: Egyptian chaos-serpent (independent Egyptian branch); identified with Typhon in Plutarch's *De Iside et Osiride*.
- **[[set]]** — *folk-syncretism / ancient-identification*: Plutarch's *Greek interpretatio* identifies Egyptian Set with Typhon (because both are antagonists of the ordering god — Osiris/Zeus).
- **[[satan-christian]]** — *scholarly-parallel / candidate ancestor-of*: the chaoskampf serpent is the mythological substrate of Christian Satan-as-dragon (Revelation 12); Forsyth 1987 makes the case in detail.
- **Python** — the *parallel-form* within Greek religion itself (Apollo's serpent at Delphi).

## Iconography
On Chalcidian and Athenian black-figure vases (6th c. BCE), Typhon appears as a winged man-figure with serpent legs and multiple serpent-heads; the *Caeretan hydria* (Munich; ca. -540) is among the most famous instances. The iconographic creature passes into later Hellenistic and Roman period imagery, where it influences the developing figural vocabulary of monstrous antagonists.

## Disputes
- **Direct Anatolian transmission vs. independent Indo-European inheritance.** West (1997) argues for direct Anatolian-Hittite borrowing into the Greek tradition; Watkins (1995) shows the underlying PIE formula is deep enough for some of the formal parallels (Vedic, Hittite, Greek) to be inherited rather than borrowed. The current consensus is *both*: inherited PIE serpent-slaying formula reinforced by Late Bronze Age Anatolian cultural contact (West 2007).
- **Identification with Set vs. with Apophis.** Plutarch identifies Typhon with Set (the disorderly god, brother of Osiris); the Egyptian Apophis (chaos-serpent enemy of Ra) is structurally closer to Typhon. The Plutarchan *folk-syncretism* groups them.
