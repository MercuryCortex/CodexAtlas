# Amenominakanushi-no-Kami

## Identity
The first kami named in the *Kojiki* (712 CE) and the most abstract deity in the entire Shinto canon. His name means "Lord of the August Center of Heaven" — he is the pivot-point of the cosmos, the celestial axis from which all else unfolds. One of the three **Zoka Sanshin** ("Separate Heavenly Deities" who came into being spontaneously and without parents), alongside [[takamimusubi]] and Kamimusubi. In the *Kojiki*'s opening sequence he appears, then immediately withdraws — *musubi* (generative force) passes to the two flanking deities. He has no myth, no offspring, no consort, no cult, no iconography. He is the placeholder for pure absolute origin.

## Earliest attestation
- *Kojiki* Book 1, opening section — the first named deity in the first sentence of Japanese recorded mythology (712 CE).
- *Nihon Shoki* (720 CE) — variant accounts; Amenominakanushi appears in some recensions but with less primacy than in the *Kojiki*.
- No pre-*Kojiki* inscriptions or shrine attestations; the deity's current centrality derives from Kokugaku elevation.

## Mythological role
Amenominakanushi plays virtually no active role in Shinto myth. He is invoked precisely because of his absence from myth: a deity so primordial and absolute that no story can contain him. The *Kojiki*'s cosmogony begins with him and then immediately moves past him to the generative deities who actually create. This structural position — supreme first, then silent — is the template for how archaic cosmogonies handle the untellable origin.

The **Kokugaku** scholars of the 18th century, especially Motoori Norinaga and later Hirata Atsutane, elevated Amenominakanushi to the supreme kami of the entire Shinto pantheon, identifying him with a universal divine center analogous to the Abrahamic God. This elevation was a *modern* theological move superimposed on an ancient minimal presence.

The **Zoka Sanshin triad** — Amenominakanushi (center) + [[takamimusubi]] (high creative force) + Kamimusubi (root creative force) — functions as the generative substrate before the [[izanagi]]-[[izanami]] creator-pair appears. The triad precedes even the primordial couple.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ein-sof]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Kabbalistic "Infinite" that precedes the Sefirot, never depicted, never mythologized; the hidden absolute from which emanation flows. The structural identity is striking: both are the cosmogonic first that immediately gives way to the manifest divine hierarchy below.
- **[[the-one-plotinus]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Plotinus's The One — beyond being, beyond thought, beyond predication; the absolute source from which Nous, Soul, and Matter emanate without the One "doing" anything. Amenominakanushi's instantaneous withdrawal maps onto this perfectly.
- **[[ometeotl]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Aztec "Two-Lord" dual absolute — no temple, no dedicated cult, no images — the unrepresented ground of all Aztec theogony. Five independent traditions (Shinto, Kabbalistic, Neoplatonic, Aztec, Chinese) develop this same "hidden, iconless, formless first-principle above all manifest gods" structure.
- **[[tian-heaven]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Heaven (*Tian*) in Chinese cosmology — the impersonal supreme principle that delegates to the manifest Jade Emperor and the lower divine hierarchy while remaining itself beyond form.
- **[[pangu]]** — *parallel-motif*: the Chinese cosmic giant who pre-exists all gods; Amenominakanushi pre-exists all manifest kami.
- **[[takamimusubi]]** — *ancient-identification*: fellow Zoka Sanshin triad member; the pair represents the cosmic polarity (high generative force / root generative force) from which creation flows.

## Iconography
None. Amenominakanushi has no traditional iconographic representation — this absence is the point. Modern Shinto shrines that focus on him (notably Hokkaido Shrine) do so via the Meiji-era Kokugaku revival and present no anthropomorphic image.
