# Saturn

## Identity
Saturn (Latin *Saturnus*, archaic *Saeturnus*) is the primordial Roman agricultural god, ruler of the lost Golden Age, and (after the Hellenizing *interpretatio Graeca* of the late Republic) the Roman equivalent of Greek [[kronos]] — father of [[jupiter]] and the senior generation of the Olympian pantheon. His name is probably from Latin *serere* / *satus* "to sow," marking him as the patron of sowing. His Roman temple, the Temple of Saturn (*aedes Saturni*) at the foot of the Capitoline Hill in the Forum Romanum, was one of the oldest and most important in the city — traditionally dedicated in -497, rebuilt under Munatius Plancus in -42, and housing the Roman state treasury (the *aerarium Saturni*). The temple statue is reported to have been bound with woolen fetters except during the **Saturnalia** (December 17–23 in the Imperial period), the great Roman festival of social inversion: slaves dined with masters or were waited on by them, the *pilleus* (freedman's cap) was worn by all, gambling normally illegal was permitted, gifts (*sigillaria*) were exchanged, and a *Saturnalicius princeps* (Lord of Misrule) was elected from among the company. Saturn was popularly remembered as the king of the lost Golden Age of peace, plenty, and natural justice — a memory that became politically and culturally productive across Roman literature (Vergil's "Saturnia regna" in *Eclogue* 4) and into the Christian and modern reception of antiquity.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[kronos]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Hellenizing late Republic gave Saturn all of Kronos's mythology (castration of Uranus, swallowing of children, defeat by Jupiter-Zeus, exile to Italy where he founded the Golden Age). By Ovid this identification is complete.

## Disputes
- **Pre-Greek origin.** Whether Saturn was originally a purely Italic agricultural deity (Wissowa) onto whom Kronos-mythology was later mapped, or whether the identification reflects deeper Italic-Greek shared inheritance, is debated. Modern scholarship (Dumézil, Beard-North-Price) treats Saturn's earliest Italic core as agricultural-sowing, with the cosmogonic-Titan content imported by *interpretatio*.
- **Saturnalia → Christmas.** The popular claim that December 25 Christian Christmas directly continues Saturnalia (December 17–23) is partially correct as cultural-atmospheric inheritance (gift-giving, feasting, social inversion vestiges in Christmas pranks), but the actual date of Christmas is fixed by other considerations (probably the alignment with the conception-on-March-25 Annunciation calculation; or the Sol Invictus December 25 birthday). The continuity is real but more diffuse than "same festival, new name."
