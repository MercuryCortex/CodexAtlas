# Cerridwen

Cerridwen is the Welsh witch-goddess — keeper of the cauldron of *awen* (divine inspiration), shape-shifter, Dark Mother, and the figure through whose body the greatest of Welsh bards, Taliesin, is reborn. She presides over the most important initiation narrative in Welsh mythology: the accidental reception of divine wisdom, the initiatory pursuit, and the death-and-rebirth of the candidate through the divine feminine vessel.

Her defining myth is the cauldron narrative preserved in the *Book of Taliesin* (*Llyfr Taliesin*, c. 1275 CE): Cerridwen brews *awen* for one year and a day for her ugly son Morfran; the servant boy Gwion Bach accidentally receives the divine gift; Cerridwen pursues him through successive shape-shifts; she swallows him; she gives birth to Taliesin. See [[sacred-cauldron]] for the full theological analysis.

Cerridwen has been identified in modern Celtic scholarship and modern Wicca as a Welsh form of the Dark Goddess — the feminine principle associated with the dark moon, winter, death, and transformation. Ronald Hutton (*The Triumph of the Moon*, Oxford UP, 1999) documents how Cerridwen was incorporated into modern Wiccan theology in the 20th century as one of the aspects of the Triple Goddess.
