# Sansin

Sansin (*Sanshin* 산신, 山神, "Mountain God") is the Korean **tutelary deity of the mountains** — and, in a country that is overwhelmingly mountainous, one of the most pervasive and beloved figures of Korean folk religion and shamanism (**Muism**). He is classically depicted as a serene **old bearded sage seated beneath a pine tree**, accompanied by a **tiger** (which is at once his servant, his messenger, and sometimes his very form — the tiger itself being a mountain-spirit in Korean lore). To him villagers pray for protection, good harvests, longevity, and the granting of sons. So deep is his hold that even **Buddhist temples** across Korea maintain a special **Sanshin-gak** ("mountain-god hall"), where the indigenous mountain deity is honoured alongside the Buddha — a striking accommodation of native belief within the imported religion. His roots reach to the very origin of the nation: the foundational *Samguk Yusa* relates that **[[tangun|Dangun]]**, the founder of the first Korean kingdom, at the close of his long reign withdrew into the mountains and **became a Sansin**. He embodies the Korean sense of the mountains as living, protective, sacred powers.
