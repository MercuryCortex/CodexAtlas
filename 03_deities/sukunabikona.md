# Sukunabikona

Sukunabikona is the diminutive Shinto deity of **medicine, healing, brewing, and craft** — a tiny god who, in the Izumo cycle of the *Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki*, arrives mysteriously **across the sea in a minuscule boat**, clad in feathers or moth-skin, so small that no one at first knows who he is. He becomes the close companion and partner of the great land-god **[[okuninushi|Ōkuninushi]]** in the ***kuni-zukuri***, the **"making and shaping of the land"**: together the towering earth-lord and the tiny knowledge-deity travel the country **establishing the arts of civilization** — medicine and the healing of people and beasts, the brewing of sake, agriculture, and protective magic against calamity and pestilence — before Sukunabikona abruptly departs, crossing to the "eternal land" (Tokoyo) over the sea. As the divine patron of **medicine and sake**, he is enshrined at healing-springs and pharmacological and brewing centers to this day. Sukunabikona embodies the Japanese theme of the small, uncanny outsider whose hidden wisdom helps civilize the world.
