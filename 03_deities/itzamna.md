# Itzamna

## Identity
The supreme creator-god of the Maya pantheon, particularly in Yucatec sources. Itzamna is patron of writing and learning, of the calendar and divination, of the priesthood (the *ah k'in* and *ah dzib*). He is regularly depicted as an elderly toothless man with scribal implements; his name appears with the *ahau* (lord) sign in his headdress. In Schellhas's classic letter-classification of Maya codex deities, Itzamna is God D.

## Earliest attestation
Late Classic Maya art (~600–900 CE) onward; principal Yucatec sources (Landa, Chilam Balam) attest him as supreme god of pre-conquest Yucatec religion.

## Mythological role
In late Postclassic Yucatec sources, Itzamna is creator and culture-bringer — he gave humans writing, calendar, agriculture, ritual. He governs the daytime sky (his nighttime counterpart in some readings is the Old Jaguar God). He is associated with the *itzam* — supernatural-iguana / caiman / cosmic-monster — that frames the cosmos.

## Iconography
Old man with square eye; toothless; large nose; the *ahau* sign in his headdress; sometimes shown with a celestial caiman / two-headed serpent (the *itzam* cosmic frame); scribal implements.
