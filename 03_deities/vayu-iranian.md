# Vayu (Iranian)

Vayu (Avestan *Vāyu*; also **Vāta** in his pure wind-aspect) is the Iranian god of the **wind and the middle air** — the moving atmosphere that fills the great space between earth and heaven — and one of the most theologically interesting figures of Zoroastrianism because of his striking **ambivalence**. In the dualist Zoroastrian cosmos, divided between the realm of light above (**[[ahura-mazda|Ahura Mazda]]**) and the realm of darkness below (Angra Mainyu), Vayu occupies the **in-between**, the intervening air — and so he is conceived as **two-natured**: there is a **"good Vayu"** of the upper atmosphere, a mighty warrior-yazata in golden armour, swift and irresistible, honoured in his own hymn the **Ram Yasht (Yasht 15)** and invoked for victory and the safe passage of the soul; and a darker **"bad Vayu"** of the lower air, associated with death and the hostile powers. He is a deity inherited from before Zoroaster: the Iranian Vāyu and the **Vedic [[vayu|Vāyu]]** are the **same Indo-Iranian wind-god**, cognate in name and function, split off from a shared ancestral religion — the Indian Vāyu staying a straightforward wind-and-breath god while the Iranian Vayu was drawn into the moral dualism of the Zoroastrian worldview. Vayu embodies the air as the contested middle ground of a cosmos divided between good and evil.
