# Hu and Sia

## Identity

Hu and Sia are paired Egyptian personifications of two divine faculties essential to creation: **Hu** is *authoritative utterance* — the creative, commanding word — and **Sia** is *perception, insight, understanding* — the divine mind that conceives. Together they are the means by which the creator-god both **thinks** the world and **speaks** it into being. They are closely associated with the sun-god [[ra]], in whose barque they ride, and with the creative power Heka.

## Mythological role

In one Heliopolitan account, Hu and Sia come into existence from drops of blood that fall from the sun-god Ra, and thereafter accompany him perpetually in the **solar barque**, flanking the god along with [[heka-egyptian]] (magic). There they function as the instruments of Ra's rule: Sia perceives and knows, Hu pronounces and commands, and Heka makes the pronouncement effective. The pair receive their fullest theological development in the **Memphite Theology** (the "Shabaka Stone"), the great Egyptian doctrine of creation by intelligence and word. There the creator [[ptah]] makes the world in two stages: he **conceives** each thing in his *heart* — the Egyptian seat of thought and perception, the domain of Sia — and then brings it into being by the **command of his tongue** — the domain of Hu. "Every divine word came into existence through what the heart devised and the tongue commanded."

## Cross-tradition significance

Hu and Sia stand at the heart of the most philosophically striking of all ancient creation doctrines: the Egyptian conception of **creation by perception and the spoken word**. The Memphite theology — the world conceived by the divine heart-mind (Sia) and uttered into being by the divine tongue (Hu) — is one of the closest and most-discussed ancient parallels to the later biblical and Greek ideas of creation by the divine word: the repeated "and God said … and it was so" of **Genesis 1**, and the **Logos** ("In the beginning was the Word") of John 1. As the personified creative Word and creative Mind of Egyptian theology, Hu and Sia are a flagship node for the cross-tradition theme of **creation by the word**.
