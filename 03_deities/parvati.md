# Pārvatī

## Identity
Consort of [[shiva]], daughter of the Himalayan king Himavat (her name means **"of the Mountain"**); reincarnation of Sati (Shiva's first wife, who self-immolated at Daksha's sacrifice). The principal benign **mother-goddess** form of the great goddess in Shaiva and Shakta theology — and the form from which **all other Hindu goddess-forms** can be theologized as emerging (Durga, Kali, Bhairavi, Chamunda as her wrathful aspects; Annapurna, Lalita as her nurturing aspects). Mother of [[ganesha]] (whom she created from her own bath-paste) and [[skanda-karthikeya]].

## Mythological role
**The wedding of Shiva and Parvati** is among the most-celebrated marriages in Hindu narrative — the Shiva Purana's central narrative, with the gods scheming to bring the ascetic-misanthropic Shiva into productive cosmic householding so that the demon Taraka — who can only be slain by a son of Shiva — can be defeated by Skanda. **Ardhanārīśvara**: the iconographic form of half-Shiva-half-Parvati split down the body's vertical axis — the most explicit visual statement of male-female non-duality in any religious iconography.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[durga]] / [[kali]]** — *ancient-identification*: forms of the same Mahādevī.
- **[[shiva]]** — inseparable cosmic-consort; Ardhanārīśvara.
- **[[gaia]] / [[rhea]] / [[cybele]]** — *parallel-motif*: mountain-mother / great-mother.
