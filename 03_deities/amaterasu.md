# Amaterasu Ōmikami

## Identity
The sun-goddess and chief deity of Shinto, born from the left eye of [[izanagi]] during his post-Yomi purification (*misogi*). Divine ancestor of the imperial line through her grandson Ninigi-no-Mikoto, whose great-grandson Jimmu became the first emperor of Japan — a genealogy formalized in the *Kojiki* (712) and *Nihon Shoki* (720) under Tenmu / Genmei court direction and used to legitimate Yamato imperial sovereignty for over 1,300 years.

## Earliest attestation
- *Kojiki* book 1 chs. 12–17 — birth-from-Izanagi narrative, the rock-cave (*Ame-no-Iwato*) hiding myth, and the descent of Ninigi.
- *Nihon Shoki* book 1 — parallel mythography with court-redacted variants.
- **Ise Grand Shrine** (*Naikū*) — the sacred bronze mirror (*Yata no Kagami*) as her physical *shintai* (god-body); shrine first attested c. 4th c. CE.

## Mythological role
The **rock-cave myth** (*Ame-no-Iwato*): after Susanoo's depredations, Amaterasu withdraws into a cave, the world goes dark; the gods stage a divine ritual (Uzume's bawdy dance, the bronze mirror, the *jewel-rope*) to lure her out — a structural template for ritual return-of-light. The **Heavenly Descent** (*tenson kōrin*) of her grandson Ninigi establishes the three imperial regalia: mirror, sword (Kusanagi from Susanoo), magatama jewel.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ra]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: solar-sovereign-divine-ancestor (Pharaoh as son of Ra ↔ Tennō as descendant of Amaterasu).
- **[[sun-goddess-of-arinna]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the rare feminine-solar type (Naumann 1996).
- **[[helios]]** / **[[sol-invictus]]** — *parallel-motif*: solar high-deity.
- **Mahāvairocana / [[vairocana]]** — *folk-syncretism*: medieval *honji-suijaku* doctrine equated Amaterasu with Vairocana, the Cosmic Buddha — a Buddhist-Shinto theological fusion that lasted ~800 years until the Meiji *shinbutsu bunri* (1868) separation.

## Iconography
The rising sun, bronze mirror, rooster (sun-herald). Officially aniconic in Shinto practice — no anthropomorphic icons in the Ise inner shrine; the mirror itself *is* her presence.
