# Juno

## Identity
Juno (Latin *Iuno*) is the Roman queen of the gods, consort of [[jupiter]], protectress of women across their life-stages (especially childbirth as **Iuno Lucina**), and the second seat of the Capitoline Triad with Jupiter and Minerva. Her name is etymologically related to *iuvenis* "young one" — Juno presides over the vital force of femininity. She had multiple distinct cult-titles representing different functional aspects: **Iuno Regina** ("the Queen," the Capitoline Triad form), **Iuno Lucina** ("of Light/Childbirth"), **Iuno Sospita** (an archaic Lanuvian warrior-Juno in goatskin), **Iuno Moneta** ("the Warner / Counselor") — whose temple on the Capitoline housed the Roman mint, from which we get the word *money*. Juno also presided over the monthly Kalends and over each Roman woman's personal *iuno* (a feminine equivalent of the male *genius* — a personal-tutelary spiritual principle). After the late-Republican *interpretatio Graeca* Juno inherited all of [[hera]]'s mythology in Latin literature (Vergil's *Aeneid* opens with Juno's vindictive opposition to Aeneas, a Hera-archetype role).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[hera]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Hellenizing late Republic gave Juno all of Hera's mythology. The two are functionally identical in Latin imperial-era literature, though Juno's specifically *Roman* cultic functions (Moneta, Lucina, the women's personal *iuno*) have no direct Hera counterpart.

## Disputes
- **Italic origins vs. Hellenization.** Palmer (1974) argues for substantial pre-Hellenizing Italic Juno-cult with multiple regional functional forms (Sospita at Lanuvium, Curitis at Falerii, the Capitoline state-form at Rome). The current scholarly consensus is that Italic Juno was a complex of related goddesses partially unified through Roman state cult, then further mapped onto Hera.
- **Etruscan Uni.** The Etruscan goddess Uni at Veii is generally identified as a parallel or source for Juno; the famous *evocatio* of Juno from Veii to Rome in -396 (Livy 5.21–22) ritualized this assimilation.
