# Daēnā

Daēnā (Pahlavi **Dēn**) is one of the most profound concepts-made-divine in Zoroastrianism — at once **"the Religion"** (the revealed faith, right vision, insight) and the **conscience or inner self** of each person, the spiritual essence built up out of one's own thoughts, words, and deeds. Her most vivid role is **eschatological**. In Zoroastrian teaching, on the **fourth day after death** the soul comes to the **Chinvat Bridge**, the "Bridge of the Separator," where its life is judged (by **[[rashnu-zoroastrian|Rashnu]]** the just, with Mithra and Sraosha). There the soul meets its own **Daēnā coming to greet it** — and she takes the exact form of the life it lived: to the **righteous** she appears as a **beautiful radiant maiden** of fifteen, fragrant and lovely, who declares "I am thine own Daēnā, thy good thoughts and words and deeds," and leads the soul across to paradise; to the **wicked** she comes as a **hideous, stinking hag**, the embodied ugliness of an evil life, and the bridge narrows to a knife-edge over which the soul falls into torment. Daēnā thus makes the afterlife a meeting with **oneself**: each person is judged by, and reunited with, the very soul they made. Closely linked to **[[ashi|Ashi]]** (reward), she embodies the Zoroastrian conviction that conscience is destiny.
