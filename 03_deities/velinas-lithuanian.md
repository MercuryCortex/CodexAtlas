# Velinas / Velnias

Pre-Christian Baltic (Lithuanian + Latvian) chthonic god of the dead and magic. Etymologically the closest cognate of Slavic [[veles]], descending from a shared Proto-Balto-Slavic root *Vels- / *Wel- meaning "the dead" (cf. Old Norse *valr* "the slain", Latin *Valhalla* etymology debates).

## Pagan profile

In Greimas's structural reconstruction (1992), Velinas occupies the Lithuanian pagan religion's chthonic-magical pole opposite Perkūnas's celestial-thunder pole — the same opposition Ivanov-Toporov reconstruct for Slavic Veles vs Perun. The pair is structurally identical across the Balto-Slavic split, which strongly suggests Proto-Balto-Slavic inheritance rather than parallel independent development.

## Christianization-as-demonization

The most striking fact about Velinas is **what his name became**: modern Lithuanian *velnias* simply means "devil." The pre-Christian deity-name was demoted, lexically, to the post-Christian word for the Christian Adversary. Lithuania was the last European pagan state to Christianize (1387 conversion under Jogaila), making Velinas's demonization both later and more linguistically traceable than most parallel cases.

This is the textbook European-pagan parallel of the Zoroastrian *asura → daēva* inversion (see [[asura-deva-inversion]]): an absorbing tradition condemns the predecessor's deity-class wholesale, transforming gods into demons. The same fate befell Slavic Veles (condemned in 12th-c. Russian sermons), but the Slavic etymological trace is fainter — Russian retains *Veles* as a proper-name from the Primary Chronicle but doesn't use it for the Christian devil the way Lithuanian uses *velnias*.

## Why this matters for the wheel

The Veles-Velinas pair is the cleanest case of:
- Balto-Slavic deity-pair preserved across the language split
- Convergent Christianization fate (same demonization mechanism applied to both)
- Lexicalization of demonization (Velinas → *velnias*-as-devil)

It pairs with the Perun-Perkūnas storm-god cognate as the two anchor pairs of Balto-Slavic religious reconstruction.
