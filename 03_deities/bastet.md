# Bastet

## Identity
Bastet (Egyptian *Bsṯt*; Greek *Boubastis*) is the **feline goddess of Bubastis** (Tell Basta, eastern Nile Delta), originally a fierce lioness-goddess in the Old Kingdom and gradually domesticated — beginning in the Middle Kingdom and confirmed by the Late Period — into the **gentle domestic-cat-headed goddess** of household protection, joy, music, and feminine fertility. Her annual festival at Bubastis was described by [[herodotus]] (*Histories* 2.59–60, 2.137–138) as the largest and most exuberant religious gathering in Egypt, drawing 700,000 pilgrims with ritual drinking, music, and ribald celebration. She is identified by Herodotus with the Greek [[artemis]] — one of the most stable Greek-Egyptian theological identifications.

## Earliest attestation
- Old Kingdom: invoked in the [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] as a lioness-goddess; Bubastis temple is one of the oldest continuously occupied cult sites in Egypt.
- Middle to New Kingdom: gradual transition from lioness to domestic cat iconography.
- **Late Period (especially 22nd-26th Dynasties, c. -900 to -525)** — massive expansion of the Bubastis cult, with 22nd-Dynasty Libyan pharaohs (Shoshenq I onward) making Bubastis their capital and centering the state cult there.
- **Ptolemaic-Roman**: vast cat-mummy production at Bubastis — millions of mummified votive cats deposited as offerings. The 19th-century European Egyptian cat-mummy "fertilizer" trade attests the scale of this votive industry.

## Mythological role
Bastet has comparatively little narrative mythology — she is principally a cult-figure. The major theological move is her **pairing with [[sekhmet]]** as the two aspects of feline divine power: Bastet is the gentle pacified form, Sekhmet the wrathful raging form. In the "Destruction of Humanity" myth ([[ra]] sends the Eye of Ra as Sekhmet to slaughter humanity, then tricks her into drinking beer-mixed-with-pomegranate-juice which she mistakes for blood; the gentled goddess returns as Bastet/Hathor), Bastet is the **resolved gentle aftermath** of divine wrath.

The annual Bubastis festival described by Herodotus is the principal ritual practice associated with her — boat processions on the Nile, ritual exposure, drinking, music, and pilgrimage exceeding any other Egyptian religious gathering of his time.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[artemis]]** — *ancient-identification*: [[herodotus]] *Histories* 2.137 and 2.156 explicitly equate Bastet/Bubastis with Artemis. The identification is structurally reasonable (both are feminine, both are associated with feral / wild creatures, both have animal-associated cult), and it is one of the most stably transmitted Greek-Egyptian theological equations.
- **[[sekhmet]]** — *ancient-identification*: theologically twin manifestations of feline divine power.
- **[[isis-egyptian]]** — *parallel-motif* + late *syncretic-identification*: in Greco-Roman Egypt, Isis absorbs many of Bastet's protective functions.

## Iconography
- Early form: **lioness-headed** woman (essentially indistinguishable from Sekhmet in pre-Middle-Kingdom contexts)
- Classical form: **cat-headed** woman, often holding a **sistrum** (sacred rattle), an **aegis** (decorated chest-amulet bearing her face), and a **basket** on the arm
- Often surrounded by kittens or accompanied by a basket of kittens
- Full cat form: seated domestic cat, especially in votive bronze statuettes (the famous "Gayer-Anderson Cat" in the British Museum is the iconic example)

## Sekhmet–Bastet theological doublet

The pairing of Bastet and [[sekhmet]] is one of Egyptian theology's most explicit **dual-aspect divine structures**: two goddesses understood as twin expressions of a single divine force. [[sekhmet]] is the lioness in wrath — the Eye of [[ra]] unleashed, the solar fire that destroys — while Bastet is the tamed and pacified form, the gentle cat, the same solar Eye at rest. The two are not separate independent deities who happen to be similar; they are **a theological formula**: Sekhmet sends plague and destruction, Bastet heals and protects. The same divine energy is differently directed.

This structure is confirmed iconographically: in earlier Old and Middle Kingdom periods, Bastet and Sekhmet share the lioness form and are nearly indistinguishable without textual labels. The domestication of Bastet's iconography (from lioness to domestic cat) is a later development that sharpens the contrast but does not alter the underlying theological unity.

The "Destruction of Humanity" myth enacts this doublet dynamically: [[ra]] sends the Eye as [[sekhmet]] to annihilate humanity, then must trick her into drinking beer colored to resemble blood — upon drinking, the wrathful goddess is pacified and returns as the gentle Bastet (or [[hathor]], in some versions). The theological message is explicit: divine wrath and divine protection are the same energy, re-directed.

This **wrath-mercy doublet** is a cross-tradition archetype: compare the Hebrew God's simultaneous attributes of wrath (din) and mercy (rachamim) in Kabbalistic theology, or the Shaivite tantric pairing of Kali (destroyer) with Uma (gentle wife). The Sekhmet-Bastet doublet is the Egyptian version — with the added precision that both are **solar Eye** manifestations, making the doublet also a solar theology (Wilkinson 2003).

## MASSIVE WIN: Bastet → Artemis → Diana transmission

**Herodotus 2.137 and 2.156** provide one of the clearest **ancient divine-equation attestations** in comparative religion: *"The Egyptians call her Bubastis; the Greeks call her Artemis."* This is not a later scholarly interpretation — it is an ancient Greek historian making an explicit identification c. 440 BCE, well within the period when the Egyptian-Hellenic cultural interface was actively producing theological syncretism.

The transmission chain is among the most recoverable in ancient Mediterranean religion:

1. **Bastet (Egyptian, cat-goddess of Bubastis, Old Kingdom onward)** — feline, protective, feminine, solar-Eye pacified form
2. **Artemis (Greek, huntress-goddess)** — the identification is structurally coherent: Artemis is the goddess of wild animals (her *potnia theron* / "mistress of animals" aspect), associated with the moon, with wilderness, with untamed feminine force. The **cat became Artemis's sacred animal** in the Hellenistic period precisely because of this Bastet-identification — a documented case of animal-sacredness crossing tradition via theological equation.
3. **Diana (Roman)** — the Roman equivalent of Artemis inherits the identification. Diana Lucifera (light-bearing) carries forward the solar-Eye lineage of Bastet's solar associations.

The Bastet-Artemis identification was confirmed and elaborated in the Hellenistic-Roman period as Greek colonists in Egypt (especially Alexandria) systematically mapped Egyptian deities onto Greek equivalents (*interpretatio graeca*). Herodotus's equation predates the Ptolemaic systematization but flows into it. Wilkinson (2003) notes that the identification is "one of the most stable and consistent in the entire Egyptian-Greek theological interface."

This is an **ancestor-of edge** (Bastet → Artemis) with a Tier-1 ancient-source attestation — the rarest and most load-bearing edge type in this vault. Wire: [[artemis]], [[tradition-mystery-cults]].

## Bastet and the sacred status of Egyptian cats

Bastet is the principal theological explanation for Egypt's extraordinary treatment of domestic cats as sacred animals. Cats were protected in Egypt partly because of their role in hunting cobras — the serpent that threatens the solar order (Ra is eternally threatened by Apophis the serpent). A cat defending the household is, in Egyptian theology, defending the solar order at a domestic scale.

The religious stakes were extreme: Herodotus (2.66) reports that Egyptians would rush into burning buildings to save cats before extinguishing the fire, and that killing a cat — even accidentally — was a capital offense. He also notes that Egyptians shaved their eyebrows when a household cat died as a sign of mourning (2.66). When cats died naturally, they were mummified and buried at Bubastis — producing the enormous cat-mummy industry attested archaeologically by millions of mummified cats recovered from the Bubastis site.

This sacredness is Bastet's most historically consequential legacy: the Egyptian elevation of the domestic cat to sacred status is the origin of the deep Western cultural association of cats with mystery, protection, and feminine power — transmitted through Greek and Roman contact with Egypt, through the Hellenistic-Roman fusion tradition, and ultimately into the medieval European symbolic vocabulary (including the later negative inversion, where cats became associated with witchcraft — a polemic-inversion of the original sacred feline) (Ikram 2005).

## Disputes
- **Date of the lioness-to-cat transition.** Conventionally dated to the Middle Kingdom (~-2000) with full domestication by the Late Period. Exactly when domestic cats arrive in Egypt (the cat was domesticated in Egypt, with Predynastic evidence) and how that maps onto Bastet's iconographic change is an active research area.
- **Pet cats vs. cult cats.** Whether the millions of votive cat-mummies at Bubastis represent (a) deliberately bred and killed sacred animals (the dominant view, supported by CT-scanning evidence of fractured necks in many mummies) or (b) pets buried with reverence (the older more sentimental view) is now settled in favor of (a).
