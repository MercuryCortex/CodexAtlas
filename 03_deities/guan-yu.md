# Guan Yu (關羽)

Historical Han general (~160–220 CE) who died in battle serving Liu Bei in the Three Kingdoms period. Posthumously deified through a millennium-long process of popular promotion, receiving imperial titles culminating in *Guāndì* (Emperor Guan) under the Wanli Emperor (1594) and *Sānjiao Fúmó Dàdì* (Great Emperor Who Suppresses Demons of the Three Teachings) — a title that captures his cross-tradition status.

He is the primary Chinese example of historical-figure deification — one of the most completely documented cases of a historical person becoming a god through grassroots veneration and imperial recognition. The process took ~1,400 years from death to imperial divine title.

**The Sanjiao synthesis embodied:** Guan Yu is simultaneously a Confucian exemplar of *zhong* (loyalty) and *yì* (righteousness); a Daoist deity of war and commerce; and a Buddhist Sangharama (monastery guardian bodhisattva). He is physically present in police stations, Triad initiation oaths, restaurants, martial arts schools, and Buddhist monasteries — the single figure who most completely embodies the [[tradition-sanjiao]] synthesis.
