# Catequil

The pre-Inca **lightning-god of the Conchucos** (central-northern Peru) — cult centre at **Huamachuco**, one of the most prestigious provincial oracle-shrines (*wak'a*) of the Inca empire. Catequil's lightning-divination oracle was consulted at the highest imperial level; Inca state administrators travelled long distances to consult Huamachuco-Catequil on military and political matters.

**The Atahualpa destruction (1532).** When the Inca civil war between Atahualpa and Huascar was decided in Atahualpa's favour, Atahualpa visited Huamachuco-Catequil to consult the oracle on the legitimacy of his rule. The oracle gave an unfavourable response. Atahualpa, enraged, ordered the shrine destroyed, the priests killed, and the Catequil idol thrown down. Within months, Atahualpa was captured by Pizarro at Cajamarca and executed (July 1533) — a coincidence the Andean tradition retrospectively read as **Catequil's revenge**. The colonial-period Andean memory of Catequil-and-Atahualpa is one of the most-narratively-elaborated retributive-divinity stories in any New World religion.

The Augustinian missionaries who attempted to extirpate the Catequil cult in the 1560s produced the *Relación de Huamachuco* — one of the principal early colonial sources on any Andean deity, written from inside the suppression operation but preserving substantial mythological and ritual detail.

Catequil functionally converges with [[illapa]] (the Inca state-pantheon thunder god) but remained nominally distinct; the Inca imperial religious administration accommodated both.
