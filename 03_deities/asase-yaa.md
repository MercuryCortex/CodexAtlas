# Asase Yaa

## Identity

*Asase Yaa* ("Thursday Earth" — *Yaa* is a Thursday-born female name in the Akan day-naming system) is the earth goddess of the Akan peoples of Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire — the great feminine counterpart to the sky-god [[nyame]]. She is the living earth: the mother of all who walk upon her surface, the receiver of the dead (who return to her body), the sustainer of agriculture, and (in some traditions) the embodiment of truth. She has no priests, no temples, and no sculptural cult-image — her presence is total and unmediated; every footstep is contact with Asase Yaa.

## Mythological role

Asase Yaa's primary mythology concerns her role as the boundary-keeper between living and dead: the dead are buried *in* Asase Yaa, who holds them until rebirth. Thursday (*Yawoada*) is her sacred day — a day of prohibition against farming, out of respect for the earth's own life. In some Akan traditions she is paired with [[nyame]] as the primordial divine couple: the sky rains upon the earth, the earth produces life — the classic sky-and-earth cosmological pair found across traditions.

## Cross-tradition identifications

- **[[geb]]** (Egyptian earth) — *parallel-form*: both are the earth personified as a divine being; the Egyptian earth is masculine (unusual cross-culturally), while Asase Yaa is feminine.
- **[[pachamama]]** (Andean earth goddess) — *parallel-form*: both are earth goddesses who receive the dead and sustain agriculture; independent convergence on the same divine role.
- **[[ki-sumerian]]** (Sumerian earth) — *parallel-form*: same function, independent origin.
