# Nārāyaṇa

## Identity
A name and aspect of Vishnu in his supreme-cosmic form — the deity reclining on the serpent Ananta-Śeṣa in the cosmic ocean of milk (*Kṣīra-sāgara*), from whose navel grows a lotus on which Brahmā the creator-demiurge is seated. The Nārāyaṇa form is Vishnu *at rest between cosmic cycles*, dreaming the cosmos into manifestation. The name *Nārāyaṇa* is glossed as "moving on the waters" (from *nāra* = waters + *ayana* = abode/movement) or "abode of humans" (*nara* = human + *ayana* = abode). In Vaishnava theology, Nārāyaṇa is the supreme being itself, and Vishnu's avataric descents emanate from him.

## Earliest attestation
- [[phase-2-006-brahmanas-aranyakas]] — *Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa* contains the early Nārāyaṇa-Puruṣa material.
- The *Nārāyaṇīya* section of the *Mahābhārata* (Śānti Parva 321–339) is a major Nārāyaṇa-theological text.
- *Bhagavad Gītā* and *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* develop the Vaishnava Nārāyaṇa-Vishnu identification.

## Mythological role
The supreme-cosmic resting deity from whom creation flows; in many Vaishnava narratives the cosmic-cycle pattern is *pralaya* (dissolution) → Nārāyaṇa reclining → Brahmā arising from his navel-lotus → creation.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[vishnu]]** — *ancient-identification*: in classical Vaishnavism Nārāyaṇa is Vishnu in supreme/transcendent form.
