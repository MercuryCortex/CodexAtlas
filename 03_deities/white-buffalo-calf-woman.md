# White Buffalo Calf Woman

## Identity
*Ptesáŋwiŋ* (White Buffalo Calf Woman) is the central sacred figure of Lakota religion and the mythological founder of the Seven Sacred Rites. She appeared to two hunters, killed the one who looked at her with lust, and spent four days with the Lakota people, teaching them the Seven Sacred Rites and presenting them with the *čaŋnúŋpa* (sacred pipe). As she departed, she transformed through a sequence of buffalo colors — black, red, yellow, and finally white — before disappearing. She is identified with Wóhpé (the Falling Star), one of the Associate Wakan Beings in Walker's account of the Sixteen Wakan (1980).

## Mythological role
White Buffalo Calf Woman is the covenant-bringer of Lakota religion: through her, humans received the sacred pipe (the primary mediating technology between humans and Wakan Tanka) and the Seven Sacred Rites (the ceremonial system). Her arrival is the founding event of Lakota religious life. She is not worshiped as a personal deity but revered as the sacred figure who established the terms of the relationship between the Lakota and the sacred.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Wóhpé** — *ancient-identification*: Walker's Oglala holy men identify White Buffalo Calf Woman with the Falling Star, the fourth Associate Wakan Being in the Sixteen Wakan hierarchy (Walker 1980 p. 70–71). This identification is internal to Lakota theology, not a colonial or scholarly imposition.
