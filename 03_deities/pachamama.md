# Pachamama

## Identity
Earth-Mother of pan-Andean religion — Quechua, Aymara, and many local Andean traditions venerate Pachamama as the earth itself, source of agricultural fertility, soil, and pasture. *Pacha* in Quechua means "earth/time/world"; *mama* is "mother." Pachamama is not a "goddess" in the Mediterranean sense of an anthropomorphic deity, but the earth substantively — offerings (*despachos* / *pago a la tierra*) are buried in the ground for her. Her veneration is the most continuous element of Andean religion from pre-Inca times to the present; even devoutly Catholic Andean peasants make *pagos* to Pachamama at planting and harvest.

## Earliest attestation
Pre-Inca Andean religion (continuity across cultural-political shifts); attested in early colonial sources; ethnographically continuous to the present.

## Mythological role
Less narratively developed than other Andean deities; Pachamama is more *operationally* than *mythologically* central. She receives offerings, gives or withholds agricultural fertility, and is the matrix of Andean *huaca*-religion (each local *huaca* is in some sense a particularized aspect or place of Pachamama). In contemporary Bolivia and Peru, *pago a la tierra* ceremonies at Carnival (February-March) and August 1 (*El Día de la Pachamama*) remain widespread.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Virgin Mary, especially Virgen del Cerro** — *folk-syncretism*: the famous 18th-c. *Virgen del Cerro* painting (Potosí) depicts the Virgin's body as the mountain of Potosí — a direct Pachamama-Mary fusion. Andean Catholic Marian devotion remains structurally Pachamama-inflected. Ref: MacCormack 1991; Sallnow 1987.

## Iconography
Largely aniconic. Symbolic: agricultural produce, coca leaves, the mountain itself.
