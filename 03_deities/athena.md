# Athena

## Identity
Athena is the Greek goddess of wisdom, skilled craft (*technē*), strategic and disciplined warfare (as opposed to Ares's reckless violence), and the *polis* — especially her own city Athens, whose Parthenon temple housed the colossal chryselephantine cult statue of Athena Parthenos (Phidias, 5th c. BCE). She is the virgin warrior-goddess, born — fully armed and adult — from the head of Zeus after Zeus had swallowed her pregnant mother Metis. Her epithets include *glaukōpis* ("gleaming-eyed" or "owl-eyed"), *Polias* ("of the city"), *Promachos* ("front-fighter"), *Parthenos* ("Virgin"), *Ergane* ("Worker").

The Linear B tablets attest "*a-ta-na po-ti-ni-ja*" — "Mistress Athena" — at Knossos in the Bronze Age, making her one of the most securely Mycenaean-attested Olympians. Her connection to the citadel-cult of palaces (Athena Polias) is structurally Mycenaean. Herodotus (2.59) and Plato (*Timaeus* 21e) identify her with the Egyptian Neith of Sais — an early example of *interpretatio graeca* of foreign goddesses, both Athena and Neith being virgin warrior craft-goddesses with city-protection functions. Her decisive role in the Iliad and Odyssey as patron of Achilles, Diomedes, and especially Odysseus marks her as the Olympian most active in human affairs as benefactor.
