# Ryūjin

## Identity
The dragon king of the sea and ruler of all marine life. Ryūjin (also called **Watatsumi**, "sea-body-spirit") dwells in the magnificent **Ryūgū-jō** ("Dragon Palace"), a glittering palace beneath the ocean whose chambers contain all the treasures of the sea. He controls the tides through two magical jewels: the **kanju** (tide-flowing gem) and the **manju** (tide-ebbing gem), which can flood or drain the sea at his will — instruments of total oceanic dominion. He is the grandfather of **Jimmu**, the legendary first emperor of Japan, through his daughter **Toyotama-Hime** — making him part of the divine genealogy that legitimizes the imperial line.

## Earliest attestation
- *Kojiki* Book 1–2: the **Hoori / Urashima** narrative cycle; Hoori-no-Mikoto (Fire-Fade) descends to Ryūgū-jō to recover his brother's lost fishhook; Ryūjin receives him as a guest, gives him his daughter Toyotama-Hime in marriage, and eventually presents the tide jewels as weapons against his brother.
- *Nihon Shoki* (720 CE) — parallel accounts with minor variants; the dragon-palace narrative firmly established.
- **Urashima Tarō** legend cycle (oral tradition; textually attested from *Man'yōshū* 759 CE): a fisherman is invited to Ryūgū-jō, spends what feels like days, returns to find centuries have passed — Ryūjin's palace as an otherworldly domain where time runs differently.
- **Buddhist Nāga mythology**: Buddhist iconographic and narrative influence from Nāga (serpent kings) of Indian tradition strongly shapes Ryūjin's later depiction.

## Mythological role
The **Dragon Palace sequence** is the central Ryūjin narrative: Hoori-no-Mikoto loses his brother's fishhook in the sea, descends to Ryūgū-jō, meets Ryūjin, marries his daughter Toyotama-Hime, recovers the hook, and is armed with the tide jewels for his conflict. The jewels as tide-weapons establish Ryūjin as a deity whose power over water is absolute and transferable — he can gift domination over the sea itself.

The **time-dilation of the underwater palace**: the Urashima Tarō motif (mortal visits divine underwater realm, returns to find centuries elapsed) is the Japanese instance of a pan-cultural "otherworld time" pattern. The Celtic **Tír na nÓg** (Land of Eternal Youth beneath the sea/beyond the western horizon) and **Avalon** run the same narrative logic: time in the divine realm is incommensurable with human time.

**Imperial genealogy**: Ryūjin's daughter Toyotama-Hime marries into the proto-imperial line, and her son Ugayafukiaezu fathers Jimmu. The first emperor's grandmother is thus a dragon-sea-deity — the ocean itself is woven into Japanese imperial descent, just as Roman emperors trace to Venus and Egyptian pharaohs to Ra.

**Buddhist Nāga transmission**: Buddhist iconographic influence from the Nāga kings (serpent-palace rulers beneath the sea in Indian cosmology) profoundly shaped how medieval Japanese depicted Ryūjin. The palace iconography, the dragon form, and the treasure-keeper role are partly a direct transmission from Buddhist Nāga mythology, which itself draws on even older Vedic serpent-deity traditions.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[poseidon]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: sea-sovereign who controls all oceanic forces with a divine instrument (trident / tide-jewels); ruler of the underwater realm and its creatures; ancestor of heroes through divine-mortal unions.
- **[[njord]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Norse sea deity who governs winds and fishing; sea-sovereignty as agricultural and maritime prosperity.
- **[[tangaroa]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Polynesian great sea god and creator of all marine life; sovereign of the ocean realm; probable deep genealogical connection through Austronesian prehistory.
- **[[aegir-norse]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Norse undersea king who hosts lavish feasts in his palace beneath the waves; the *underwater-palace-of-the-sea-king* motif is structurally identical — the sea as a mirror-world of the divine feast hall.
- **[[olokun]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Yoruba deity of the deep ocean, ruler of undersea riches and the domain beneath the waters.
- **[[tiamat]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Babylonian primordial salt-water dragon; Ryūjin's dragon-nature and dominion over the sea as a primordial/divine force parallels the Babylonian sea-dragon concept.
- **[[varuna]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Vedic sovereign of cosmic waters and divine law; water-domination as instrument of cosmic order.

## Iconography
Dragon or dragon-king in human form wearing robes covered with fish and sea-creature motifs. Sometimes depicted as a large sea dragon, sometimes as an elderly regal figure holding the tide-jewels. His palace is depicted as a gleaming undersea structure surrounded by sea creatures who serve as his court.
