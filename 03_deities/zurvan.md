# Zurvan

Zurvan (Avestan/Middle Persian *Zruvan*, "Time") is the deified **Infinite Time** (*Zurvan Akarana*) and the supreme principle of **Zurvanism**, the major heterodoxy of Sasanian Zoroastrianism. Orthodox Mazdaism is strictly dualist: **[[ahura-mazda|Ohrmazd]]** (good) and **[[angra-mainyu-ahriman|Ahriman]]** (evil) are two uncreated, coeternal principles. Zurvanism resolves the awkwardness of two ultimates by positing **one** source above them — Time itself — from whom both spring. Its famous myth: Zurvan, androgynous and alone, **sacrifices for a thousand years** desiring a son to make the creation; at the end he **doubts** whether the sacrifice will avail — and from that instant conceives **twins**, Ohrmazd born of the sacrifice and Ahriman of the doubt. Having vowed kingship to whichever is born first, Zurvan is tricked when Ahriman tears his way out prematurely, and so grants **Ahriman 9,000 years' dominion** before Ohrmazd's final victory. Condemned as heresy and eclipsed after the Islamic conquest, Zurvan nonetheless deeply shaped late-antique religious thought (and is widely identified with the lion-headed, serpent-entwined **Aion/Zurvan** figure of the Mithraic mysteries).
