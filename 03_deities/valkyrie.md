# The Valkyries

## Identity

The Valkyries (Old Norse **valkyrja**, "chooser of the slain") are female battle-spirits in the service of [[odin]]. Their role is twofold and grim: they decide who will fall in battle, and they convey the chosen dead — the *einherjar* — to **Valhalla**, Odin's hall, where the slain warriors feast and fight in preparation for Ragnarök, and where the valkyries bear them mead.

## Mythological role

Snorri's *Gylfaginning* lists the valkyries among the goddesses and explains their office: Odin "sends them to every battle; they choose who is to be slain and determine victory." The bravest of those who die in combat they carry to Valhalla; the rest of the battle-dead are shared with [[freyja]], who receives half of the slain in her hall Fólkvangr. In the heroic poems of the *Poetic Edda*, valkyries take on more individual and human form: figures like **Brynhild** and **Sigrún** are valkyries who love mortal heroes, defy Odin, and are punished or bound to human fate — blurring the line between divine battle-spirit, swan-maiden, and shield-maiden. The valkyries thus range from anonymous, fearsome choosers of death (as in the chilling *Darraðarljóð*, where they weave the web of war on a loom of entrails) to named, tragic heroines.

## Cross-tradition significance

The valkyries embody the Norse vision of **death-in-battle as a divine election** — to fall in war is to be chosen, taken up, and rewarded, a theology that underwrote the warrior ethos of the Viking Age. As female powers who determine who dies, they overlap with the [[norns]] (the fate-goddesses) and belong to the broad cross-cultural category of psychopomp and death-choosing figures — the beings who select and conduct the dead — while their specifically martial, mead-bearing, hall-of-the-slain character is a distinctive Germanic development.
