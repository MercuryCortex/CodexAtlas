# Budha

## Identity

Budha is the Hindu deity of the planet **Mercury** — one of the nine **Navagraha** (planetary gods) of Hindu astrology — and a god of intelligence, eloquence, commerce, and learning. (He is distinct from *Buddha*, the founder of Buddhism, despite the similar name.) His name is connected with *buddhi*, "intellect," and he presides over Wednesday (*Budhavāra*).

## Mythological role

Budha's birth is a celebrated Puranic episode: he is the son of **[[chandra]]** (the Moon-god) by **Tara**, the wife of the god Brihaspati (Jupiter). Chandra abducts Tara, and from their union Budha is born — bright and able, but of a contested parentage that nearly sparks a war among the gods until Brahma intervenes. As a graha, Budha takes his place in the **Navagraha** — the ennead of [[surya]] (Sun), [[chandra]] (Moon), Mangala (Mars), Budha (Mercury), Brihaspati (Jupiter), Shukra (Venus), [[shani]] (Saturn), Rahu, and Ketu — whose positions are read in *jyotisha* (Hindu astrology) to determine the fortunes of human life. Budha's influence is generally benign, governing the intellect, speech, trade, and adaptability.

## Cross-tradition significance

Budha is the Hindu member of the cross-cultural identification of **Mercury** with intelligence, communication, and commerce — the same association the planet carries in the Greco-Roman [[hermes-greek|Hermes]]/Mercury and in the Mesopotamian Nabu (the scribal god linked to Mercury). The Navagraha system itself reflects the great ancient exchange of astral science between Mesopotamia, the Hellenistic world, and India, making Budha a node in the shared history of planetary astrology across civilizations.
