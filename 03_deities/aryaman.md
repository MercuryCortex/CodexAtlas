# Aryaman

## Identity
Aryaman is a Vedic **Āditya** — one of the sovereign sons of [[aditi]] — the god of **custom, hospitality, and the marriage-bond**, the divine guarantor of the social ties that hold the Aryan community together.

## Earliest attestation
Invoked throughout the *Rigveda*, usually beside [[mitra-vedic]] and Varuna in the circle of the Ādityas who uphold *ṛta* (cosmic-social order).

## Mythological role — the keeper of custom and marriage
Aryaman presides over the obligations of **guest-friendship** and the rites that bind kin — above all **marriage**: the bride is led "on Aryaman's path," and he is invoked to grant her a husband and secure household. The Milky Way is called *aryamṇaḥ panthā*, "Aryaman's path." As an Āditya he shares in the sovereign function of guarding contracts and the moral order, the social counterpart of Mitra's bonds and Varuna's law.

## Cross-tradition
Aryaman is a node of the Indo-Iranian sovereign gods: his **Iranian cognate is Airyaman**, the yazata of healing and community, and his very name preserves the Indo-Iranian *arya* ("the people"). He belongs to the Mitra-circle that Dumézil placed at the heart of the first (sovereign-legal) function.

## Iconography
Rarely figured; present in the marriage-rite and in the band of the Milky Way.
