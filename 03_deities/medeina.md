# Medeina

Medeina (also **Meidein**) is the Lithuanian goddess of the **forest, the wild animals, and the hunt** — the divine mistress of the great Baltic woodlands. She is named among the gods in the 13th-century sources (the Hypatian Chronicle's account of the apostate king Mindaugas lists her, alongside the divine smith and a hare-god, among the deities he secretly honoured), making her one of the few Baltic deities with genuinely early attestation. Tradition imagines her as a **beautiful huntress** accompanied by — or taking the form of — a **she-wolf**, single and unwilling to marry, embodying the wilderness that resists domestication: she is said to discourage hunters rather than aid them, the protector of the wild against human encroachment as much as a patron of the chase. In her domain and character she is the Baltic counterpart of the Roman **[[diana-roman|Diana]]** and the Greek Artemis — the virgin lady of the beasts and the woods. Medeina embodies the Lithuanian reverence for the vast, sacred, untamed forest.
