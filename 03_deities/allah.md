# Allah

The supreme and exclusive deity of Islamic monotheism. *Al-Ilāh*, "the God," is the Arabic definite form of the common Semitic noun for deity, cognate with Hebrew *El* / *Elohim*, Aramaic *Elah* / *Elaha*, Ugaritic *El*, and Phoenician *El* — testifying to deep linguistic and (variously degreed) theological continuity across West Semitic religions. The Quran insists on Allah's unity (*tawhīd*), incomparability ("there is no thing like Him," 42:11), and ninety-nine names that articulate his attributes (al-Rahmān, al-Rahīm, al-Mālik, etc.).

The pre-Islamic Hijazi religious context attests a high god *Allāh* alongside polytheistic worship of goddesses [[allat|al-Lāt]], [[al-uzza|al-ʿUzzā]], [[manat|Manāt]] (the three "daughters of Allāh" named at Quran 53:19-22 and contested in the [[satanic-verses-incident|*gharānīq* / Satanic Verses tradition]]) and other deities including [[hubal]] (the principal cult-image of the Kaaba) and the Quran-71:23 antediluvian pantheon ([[wadd]], Suwāʿ, Yaghūth, Yaʿūq, [[nasr-pre-islamic|Nasr]]). The fuller pre-Islamic Arabian religious milieu is documented at the [[tradition-pre-islamic-arabian]] anchor. Whether pre-Islamic *Allāh* had already approached monotheistic status (Welch's *henotheism* hypothesis; the [[hanif-monotheism|*ḥanīf*-tradition]]) or was a generic high god raised to exclusive monotheism by the Quranic preaching (the more common view) is debated. The vault preserves this deity as a separate node from El (Canaanite), El/Elohim (Hebrew), and Yahweh — connected by *syncretic-edges* representing the linguistic and theological continuities — in line with the methodology's anti-collapse rule.
