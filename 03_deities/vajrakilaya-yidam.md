# Vajrakīlaya — Wrathful Tantric Buddha-Form

Vajrakīlaya (Sanskrit *Vajrakīla*, "diamond dagger / spike"; Tibetan *Dorje Phurba*) is a major **wrathful meditation-deity (yidam)** of Vajrayāna Buddhism, the Buddha-form that embodies the **phurba** — the three-edged ritual dagger that "nails down" and transmutes the forces obstructing enlightenment. He is iconographically a fearsome three-faced, six-armed figure wreathed in flame, his lower body itself a great three-bladed phurba, trampling the embodiments of ego and obstruction. His textual basis is the Indian **Vajrakīla-tantra** (c. 8th century), and he is one of the **Eight Herukas** (Kagyé) of the **Nyingma** school — practices that **[[padmasambhava]]** is said to have mastered and carried to Tibet, where Vajrakīlaya became one of the most widely practised of all yidams (also centrally transmitted in the Sakya school). His rite is the classic Vajrayāna paradox of **"liberation through compassionate destruction"** — the wrathful annihilation not of beings but of the obscurations that bind them.
