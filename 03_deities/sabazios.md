# Sabazios

## Identity
Sabazios is a Phrygian-Thracian mystery-cult deity of disputed precise function (sky-sovereignty god? ecstatic Dionysus-analogue?), variously syncretized in the Hellenistic and Roman periods with [[dionysus-mystery]], [[zeus]] (as Zeus-Sabazios), and in some Roman-period contexts with the Hebrew Yahweh-Sabaoth (a folk-etymological misreading of "Sabaoth" as "Sabazios"). His cult diffused widely across the Mediterranean from at least the 5th c. BCE — Aristophanes mocks "Sabazian rites" as a fashionably foreign ecstatic-religious novelty at Athens. The cult's distinctive material remains are the **bronze "Sabazian hands"** — votive right hands cast in bronze, with pine-cone fingers, snakes coiling around them, and a varying suite of cult-symbols affixed (ram, lizard, scales, jug, frog) — found across the Roman Empire from Britain to Anatolia in the 2nd–3rd centuries CE. Initiates were called *Sabaziastai*; the rites involved snake-handling ("pulling the snake through the bosom" — Demosthenes *On the Crown* 259 attacks his rival Aeschines for participating). The cult's appearance in Rome led to a senatorial expulsion of "Jews and Sabazian-worshippers" in -139 (Valerius Maximus 1.3.3), one of our earliest sources on either group's Roman presence — though the conflation suggests significant ancient confusion between the two cults.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[dionysus-mystery]]** — *ancient-identification*: from the 5th c. BCE Greek sources treat Sabazios as a Phrygian-Thracian Dionysus; the ecstatic rites, snake symbolism, and salvific-mystery structure align the two.
- **[[zeus]]** — *ancient-identification* (in Anatolian Phrygian contexts): Zeus-Sabazios dedications are pervasive in Roman-period Asia Minor.
- **Yahweh-Sabaoth** — *folk-syncretism* (mistaken): the Roman expulsion of -139 conflates the Jewish *Yhwh Ṣəḇāʾôt* ("LORD of Hosts") title with Sabazios on a phonetic-orthographic surface similarity; this is not a genuine theological identification but a Roman administrative-religious confusion.

## Disputes
- **Origin: Phrygian or Thracian?** Strabo (10.3.18) treats Sabazios as Phrygian; other ancient sources favor Thracian origin. Modern scholarship (Turcan 1996) suggests a Bronze-Age Anatolian sky-god substrate diffused into both Phrygian and Thracian zones.
- **Cult character: sky-sovereignty or ecstatic-mystery?** The Phrygian/Anatolian Zeus-Sabazios material suggests sovereign-sky theology; the Greek/Roman ecstatic-mystery diffusion suggests Dionysian-style ecstatic religion. The two faces probably reflect different recension-streams of the same complex.
- **Relation to Mithras-cult.** Some 19th-century scholarship (Cumont) linked Sabazios and Mithras-cult genealogically; modern scholarship (Beck, Lane) treats them as parallel but independent Roman-period Anatolian-mystery diffusions.
