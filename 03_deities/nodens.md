# Nodens

Nodens is a **Romano-British god of healing, the sea, and hunting**, known above all from his great late-Roman **temple-complex at Lydney Park** on the tidal Severn in Gloucestershire — a sanctuary that included a **healing shrine (with rooms for incubation, sleeping in the temple for a curative dream)** and that yielded a remarkable cluster of **votive dogs**, the dog being a healing animal (its licking thought to cure) and Nodens's special companion. The temple mosaic and finds are rich in **marine imagery** — tritons, sea-monsters, fishermen — tying him to the sea and the great estuary. In inscriptions he is equated with **Mars** (Mars Nodens) and, in his woodland-hunting aspect, with **[[silvanus|Silvanus]]**. Most importantly for the wider Celtic world, *Nodens* is **the same divine name as the Irish [[nuada|Nuada]]** (Núadu Airgetlám, "of the Silver Hand") and the Welsh **Nudd/Lludd** — one theonym surfacing across Britain, Wales, and Ireland. (J.R.R. Tolkien, who worked on the Lydney inscriptions, took an interest in the name.) Nodens exemplifies the deep continuities of Celtic religion beneath its Romano-British surface.
