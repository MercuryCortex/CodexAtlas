# Hebat

## Identity
Hebat (Ḫepat) is the chief goddess of the Hurrian pantheon and the consort of Teshub in the imperial-era Hittite-Hurrian cult. Her primary cult centers were Aleppo (Halab) — where she was paired with Hadad / Teshub — and Kizzuwatna (Cilicia). Her elevation to *imperial* goddess of the Hittites is largely the work of the 13th-century BCE queen Puduhepa, herself a Kizzuwatnan priestess, who in her famous prayer (CTH 384) explicitly identifies Hebat with the older Hittite Sun Goddess of Arinna — a theological merger that brought her Hurrian goddess into the imperial pantheon as the chief female deity.

## Earliest attestation
- Aleppine cult of Ḫalabaḫe / Ḫepat attested from at least the Old Babylonian period (~-1800 to -1600 BCE).
- Hittite-period attestations from the Boğazköy archive (Hattusa, ~-1400 to -1200): ritual texts, treaty witness-lists, and the Yazılıkaya sanctuary's central rock relief (chamber A) where she meets Teshub.
- Prayer of Puduhepa (CTH 384, ~-1250) — the most theologically explicit text identifying her with the Sun Goddess of Arinna.

## Mythological role
Hebat appears in the Kumarbi cycle as Teshub's wife and the mother of Sharruma. In the *Song of Ullikummi*, when Teshub is temporarily deposed, Hebat fears for him from her chamber on the heavenly mountain. Her cultic role is larger than her mythological one — she is principally a *cult* goddess of imperial worship, sworn-by in treaties, addressed in royal prayer.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[sun-goddess-of-arinna]]** — *ancient-identification*: Puduhepa's prayer identifies them, but this is a 13th-century imperial-cult merger of two originally distinct goddesses (Hurrian queen of heaven vs. Hittite chief solar deity).
- **[[hera]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: queen-consort of the storm-warrior cosmic king; structurally analogous role within the Hesiodic / Greek system to Hebat in the Hurrian-Hittite system.

## Iconography
Enthroned mature woman, often shown standing on or beside a panther/leopard, wearing the polos crown. At Yazılıkaya she leads the female procession that meets Teshub's male procession at the central rock-cut panel.

## Disputes
- **Identity with Sun Goddess of Arinna.** The Puduhepa identification is genuine and ancient but is best understood as imperial theological harmonization rather than original identity; van Gessel (1998) and Beckman (1995) treat them as originally distinct goddesses merged by 13th-century theology.
