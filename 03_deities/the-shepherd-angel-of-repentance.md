# The Shepherd (Angel of Repentance)

## Identity
**The Shepherd** (Greek *Poimēn*) — explicitly identified as the **Angel of Repentance** (*Angelos tēs Metanoias*) — is the central personified-angelic figure of [[hermas-of-rome]]'s *Shepherd of Hermas* ([[phase-4-065-shepherd-of-hermas]]). He first appears to Hermas in Vision 5 dressed as a shepherd, identifies himself as the angel God has assigned to Hermas, and then narrates the work's central content: 12 Mandates (moral commandments) and 10 Similitudes (parables) plus subsequent visions. Where the earlier Visions 1–4 are mediated by the Lady-Ecclesia personification ([[the-lady-ecclesia-hermas]]), the Shepherd takes over from Vision 5 onward and dominates the rest of the work.

The Shepherd is one of the most-developed *personified-angelic* figures in pre-Nicene Christian literature — a celestial being who is *not* Christ, *not* the Holy Spirit, but a distinct angelic-rank teacher-mediator personifying the moral-spiritual category of *Repentance*. The figure is canonically retained in [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]] (since the *Shepherd* itself is canonical scripture there), making him one of the most distinctive personified-angels in the Ethiopian Christian canonical-theological imagination.

## Earliest attestation
- **Shepherd of Hermas, Vision 5.1** — *"As I was praying in the house, and sitting on my bed, there came in a glorious man, in the dress of a shepherd, with a white skin wrapped about him, with a wallet on his shoulders and a staff in his hand."*
- The Shepherd then announces: *"I am the Shepherd to whom you have been entrusted... I have been sent by the most venerable angel to dwell with you for the rest of the days of your life."*
- Continues throughout Mandates 1–12 and Similitudes 1–10.

## Mythological role
The Shepherd functions as a personified-allegorical angelic teacher — the *Angel of Repentance* embodying the theological possibility of post-baptismal forgiveness. The figure mediates between humanity (Hermas as the visionary representing the Christian community) and the higher angelic ranks / God. The most consequential allegorical sequence in the work — the building of the Tower (the Church) from twelve mountains (the types of Christians) — is dictated by the Shepherd as cosmic-moral instruction.

In the broader history of personified-angelology, the Shepherd is theologically distinctive in being *named after a moral-virtue category* (Repentance) rather than after a name-with-El suffix (Michael, Gabriel, Uriel, etc.). This makes him structurally close to the Greco-Roman *Pistis*, *Eleos*, *Sophia* personifications and to the Jewish-Wisdom personification (Hokhmah / Sophia of Proverbs 8, Sirach 24).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[jesus-of-nazareth]]** (the Johannine Good Shepherd) — *scholarly-parallel*: shared iconography but distinct figure. Hermas's Shepherd is *not* Christ; he is the angel God has assigned to Hermas and to whom believers can repent. The Christological Good-Shepherd identification appears in early-Christian *art* (catacomb-paintings) but is distinct from Hermas's personified-angel Shepherd in *text*.
- **The Greek personified-Virtues** (Pistis, Eleos, Sophia, etc.) — *parallel-form*.
- **Sophia / Hokhmah** (Wisdom personifications in Jewish Wisdom literature) — *parallel-form*: similar personification-of-abstraction structure.

## Iconography
- Shepherd's garments (long white tunic, sheepskin wrap).
- Shepherd's staff.
- Wallet on the shoulder.
- Sometimes shown with sheep (the Christians) and the Tower-of-the-Church.
- Early Christian catacomb-art's "Good Shepherd" iconography sometimes draws on Hermas's Shepherd in addition to Johannine Christology.

## Disputes
- **Identification with Christ.** Hermas's Shepherd is textually distinct from Christ (in Similitude 9, Hermas sees both the Shepherd *and* the Son of God as separate figures). Some allegorical-Christological readings (especially post-Nicene) have tried to identify them; the textual evidence is against this.
- **Angelological status.** Whether the Shepherd is a "high-angelic" rank (Michael-equivalent) or a "personal-guardian-angel" rank (lesser angel assigned to individual). The text is ambiguous; Osiek 1999 surveys both readings.
