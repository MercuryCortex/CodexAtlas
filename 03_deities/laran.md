# Laran

## Identity
Laran is the **Etruscan god of war** — a youthful warrior-deity of battle and of the defence of boundaries, the Etruscan counterpart of the Greek [[ares]] and the Roman [[mars-roman]].

## Earliest attestation
Known from engraved bronze mirrors and votive bronzes (5th–3rd c. BCE), where he appears as a helmeted (often nude) youth bearing spear and shield, and in inscriptions among the ordered Etruscan pantheon.

## Mythological role
As the war-god, Laran presides over battle and martial valour; he also guards boundaries, a concern central to Etruscan religion (whose *disciplina* regulated the marking of sacred and civic limits). On mirrors he is sometimes shown with [[turan]], the love-goddess — the Etruscan form of the Ares-and-Aphrodite pairing of war and love.

## Cross-tradition
Laran is the Etruscan member of the **war-god** neighborhood — Ares, Mars, Sekhmet, the Morrígan, Inanna, Huitzilopochtli, Vahagn — the deity of battle and slaughter across the traditions.

## Iconography
A helmeted warrior-youth with spear and shield, often nude in the heroic manner.
