# Sky Woman (Ataensic)

## Identity

Sky Woman — *Aataentsic* in Mohawk, *Iotentsati* in Onondaga — is the central figure in Haudenosaunee creation mythology. She is the first being to inhabit the earth; her fall from the Sky World is the cosmogonic act that makes the world possible. She is not merely a goddess in the Olympian sense but the literal **origin of the terrestrial order**: without her fall, there is no earth, no Turtle Island, no humanity. Her primary names mean "she is ancient" (Aataentsic) or "Mature Flower" — suggesting both primordial depth and creative fecundity. She is the cosmological ancestor of all Haudenosaunee people.

## The Creation Narrative

The Haudenosaunee creation narrative begins in the **Sky World** (*Karionake*), a luminous realm above the primordial waters, populated by sky people not unlike human beings. A large tree — sometimes identified as the White Pine of Peace — stands at the center of the Sky World. Sky Woman's husband (or, in some accounts, a chief jealous of her apparent pregnancy) uproots this tree, leaving a hole in the floor of the Sky World.

Sky Woman peers through the hole and either falls, jumps, or is pushed:

1. **The fall**: She plunges downward through infinite space toward the primordial ocean below. As she falls, she grabs seeds and plants — in some accounts she carries corn, beans, squash (the Three Sisters), and medicinal herbs clutched in her hands.

2. **The catch**: Water birds — swans and geese — see her falling and form a living raft beneath her, breaking her fall and lowering her gently toward the ocean.

3. **The earth-diver**: The water animals convene. Someone must dive to the bottom of the ocean to bring up earth for her to stand on. Many animals try — otter, beaver — and fail. Finally, **Muskrat** dives. He descends so deep he nearly dies; when his body surfaces, he is dead or dying, but his paw holds a small clump of mud from the ocean floor.

4. **Turtle Island**: The animals place the earth on the back of the Great Turtle, who offers herself as the foundation. The earth expands and grows, spreading to become North America — **Turtle Island**. Sky Woman walks on this new earth; wherever she walks, plants grow.

5. **The twins**: Sky Woman gives birth. In most versions she gives birth to a daughter; the daughter (fathered by West Wind in some accounts) gives birth to twins:
   - **Sapling** (*Hahgwehdiyu*, the Right-handed Twin / Good Mind) — who creates animals, gentle rivers, fruit trees, the sun, and all life-sustaining things
   - **Flint** (*Hahgwehdaetgah*, the Left-handed Twin / Evil Mind) — who, in his haste or malice, creates thorns, sharp rocks, rapids, monsters, and all dangerous things

In some variants Sky Woman herself is the grandmother of the twins; in others she is the direct mother. The twins' contention throughout creation — and their final combat — explains why the world contains both beauty and difficulty. Flint is defeated but not destroyed; the dualism is **permanent**, not eschatological.

Sky Woman dies during or after childbirth. From her body grow the Three Sisters — corn from her head, beans from her heart, squash from her belly — the agricultural foundation of Haudenosaunee life. She becomes associated with the moon; the Good Mind places her there to watch over humanity.

## Turtle Island Cosmology

The cosmological image of Turtle Island — North America as a great island on the back of a turtle floating in cosmic waters — is structurally identical to the **axis mundi** concept: the Turtle is the stable center/support of the world; Sky Woman stands on it as the cosmos's first inhabitant. The [[axis-mundi]] (World Axis / Cosmic Pillar) connects Sky World and Earth through the hole in the celestial floor; Sky Woman's path is a descent along the vertical cosmic axis.

The image of the Turtle as cosmic foundation is also paralleled in Vedic cosmology (the tortoise *Kurma* as the support of the world — [[kurma-avatar]]) and in Chinese cosmology (four great turtles supporting the four corners of the earth — see *Liezi*). These are **parallel-form** convergences (independent structural solutions to the same cosmological question: what holds the earth up?) — no transmission documented.

## The Earth-Diver Motif — Global MASSIVE WIN

Sky Woman's creation narrative belongs to the **earth-diver** cosmogonic type — one of the most widely distributed creation patterns in world religion, and one of Mircea Eliade's strongest cases for a globally distributed motif (Eliade 1958, ch. 7; Dundes 1962).

The earth-diver structure:
1. A divine or semi-divine being (or several beings) exist in or above primordial waters
2. A smaller animal (muskrat, duck, turtle, frog) dives to the bottom of the water
3. Earth or mud is brought up from the deep
4. The earth grows, expands, or is placed on a larger animal's back to become the world

This pattern appears **independently** in traditions with no documented transmission pathway:

| Tradition | Diver | Foundation | Notes |
|-----------|-------|-----------|-------|
| Haudenosaunee | Muskrat | Turtle's back | Sky Woman falls first; Muskrat brings mud |
| Ojibwe / Anishinaabe | Muskrat (after Otter and Beaver fail) | Turtle's back | [[tradition-ojibwe]] variant: Nanabozho floats on a raft |
| Lakota | Muskrat (some versions) | "The Land" | [[tradition-lakota]] variant of the same structure |
| Finnish (*Kalevala*) | Diving duck laying egg on Ilmatar's knee | Egg-fragments become earth/sky | [[tradition-finno-karelian]] — the cosmic-egg variant of the earth-diver pattern; Ilmatar = the Water-Mother parallel |
| Vedic (*Shatapatha Brahmana* 14.1.2.11) | Prajapati as a boar (*varaha*) diving into cosmic waters | Earth lifted on boar's tusks | Later elaborated as Vishnu's [[varaha-avatar]] — the divine boar incarnation that rescues Earth-goddess *Bhudevi* from the cosmic ocean |
| Siberian shamanic traditions | A duck or other waterfowl | Earth brought from underwater | Multiple Siberian Turkic, Altai, Buryat variants; no transmission to North America documented |
| Cherokee | Waterbeetle (*Dayunisi*) | Mud brought up from water; earth stretched by buzzard's wings | Close structural parallel to Haudenosaunee; both Cherokee and Haudenosaunee are northeastern/southeastern woodland traditions |

**Investigative finding**: The earth-diver is one of the best-documented cases of **parallel independent invention** in world cosmogony — the same structural narrative logic (primordial water → animal diver → earth from the deep → growing island/world) appearing in circumpolar North America, Inner Asia, and Vedic tradition without any established transmission pathway connecting them. Whether this reflects a shared human cognitive pattern (the "cosmic mountain from the deep" archetype) or an extremely ancient migration-era narrative (carried out of Siberia via Beringia into the Americas) is the central scholarly debate. Dundes (1962) argues for a primal psychological significance; Eliade (1958) treats it as a hierophany of the primordial waters. What is not in dispute is the structural identity of the pattern.

## Significance Within Haudenosaunee Religion

Sky Woman is not merely cosmogonically important; she is the theological foundation for several defining features of Haudenosaunee society:

1. **Women's political authority**: The clan system descends matrilineally from Sky Woman. Clan mothers (*Gantowisas*) appoint and remove chiefs precisely because all clans trace their cosmic origin to this primordial female ancestor. Mann (2000) documents this as a theological-political principle: Sky Woman's sovereignty is institutionalized in the clan-mother system.

2. **The Three Sisters agriculture**: The corn, beans, and squash that emerge from Sky Woman's body are the agricultural calendar around which Haudenosaunee ceremonial life is organized. The Thanksgiving Address (*Ohen:ton Karihwatehkwen* — "the words that come before all else") offered before every council meeting gives thanks to the Three Sisters by name.

3. **Good Mind as political virtue**: The twin-birth narrative makes the **Good Mind** (*Hahgwehdiyu*) — cooperative, generous, life-sustaining intention — the central political virtue. The Great Law of Peace (*Gayanashagowa*) is the institutionalization of Sky Woman's cosmological gift of the Good Mind.
