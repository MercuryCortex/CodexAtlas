# Nanna

## Identity

Nanna (Old Norse **Nanna Nepsdóttir**) is a Norse goddess, the wife of the shining god [[baldr]] and the mother of [[forseti]], the god of justice. She is remembered above all for the manner of her death: she dies of grief at Baldr's killing and accompanies him in death, making her the Norse type of the devoted wife who will not be parted from her husband even by death. (She is unrelated to the Mesopotamian moon-god Nanna; the names coincide.)

## Mythological role

Nanna's story is part of the great myth of **Baldr's death** in Snorri's *Gylfaginning*. When Baldr — invulnerable to all things save mistletoe — is slain by the blind god Höd at Loki's instigation, the gods prepare his funeral on his ship Hringhorni. As Baldr's body is carried to the pyre, **Nanna's heart bursts with grief and she dies**; she is laid on the pyre to be burned beside him. She then dwells with Baldr in the realm of **[[hel]]**. When the god Hermód rides the long dark road to Hel to beg for Baldr's release, Nanna receives him and, when he departs, sends gifts back to the living: a linen robe for Baldr's mother [[frigg]] and a gold ring for the goddess Fulla — tokens of love and memory sent up out of the land of the dead.

## Cross-tradition significance

Nanna embodies the motif of the **wife who dies of grief and follows her husband into death** — the spousal bond that not even death severs, and the gifts sent back from the underworld as a sign of enduring love. The death-and-descent of the beloved, and the journey of a messenger to the land of the dead to seek the return of the lost one (Hermód's ride for Baldr), place Nanna's story within the wider ancient complex of underworld-descent and attempted-retrieval narratives (Inanna/Dumuzi, Orpheus/Eurydice, Demeter/Persephone).
