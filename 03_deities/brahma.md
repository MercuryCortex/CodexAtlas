# Brahmā

## Identity
Brahmā (Sanskrit ब्रह्मा) is the Hindu creator-deity and third member of the Puranic **Trimurti** alongside [[vishnu]] (the preserver) and [[shiva]] (the destroyer / transformer). His canonical iconography is **four-faced** (one face toward each cardinal direction; one original fifth face was severed by Shiva in a celebrated myth) with **four arms** holding the Vedas, a water-pot (*kamandalu*), a rosary (*mala*), and a sacrificial implement or lotus. His consort is [[saraswati]] (goddess of speech, knowledge, and the arts). His Puranic cosmogonic position is canonical: at the dawn of each cosmic cycle Brahmā is born from a **lotus rising from Vishnu's navel** as Vishnu reclines on the cosmic serpent Shesha on the primal ocean; Brahmā then generates the cosmos through the *manasaputra* (mind-born sons, the seven primordial sages / Prajapatis) and the differentiation of beings (Bailey 1983).

Brahmā is the Hindu creator-deity with **strikingly limited surviving active cult**: while Vishnu and Shiva command vast living devotional communities (Vaishnavism and Shaivism), Brahmā's active worship is largely confined to a single major temple at **Pushkar in Rajasthan** and a handful of minor shrines. This is a *fact* about reception-history, not a judgment about importance — Brahmā is theologically central as creator and structurally indispensable to the Trimurti, but the cosmogony-having-been-completed leaves him with limited ongoing ritual function relative to the preserver-and-destroyer pair. Bailey 1983 and Doniger 1976 both address this asymmetry.

## Earliest attestation
- **Brahmanas / Aranyakas** (~-800 to -500): the proto-Brahmā creator-figure is **Prajapati** (Shatapatha Brahmana cosmogonies; Aitareya Aranyaka). The figure-name Brahmā as a personified creator is post-Vedic. ([[phase-2-006-brahmanas-aranyakas]])
- **Upanishadic period** (~-600 onward): Brahmā as personified creator emerges out of *Brahman* (the impersonal absolute) and Prajapati; the distinction between *Brahman* (neuter, absolute) and *Brahmā* (masculine, personified creator) becomes canonical.
- **Epic / Puranic period** (~-200 to 500 CE): Brahmā is fully crystallized as the four-faced creator in the Trimurti structure. *Mahabharata* and *Ramayana* attestations are extensive. ([[phase-2-017-mahabharata-ramayana-oral-layers]])
- **Bhagavata Purana** (c. 9th-10th c. CE) and other major Puranas: canonical Brahmā cosmogonies. ([[phase-5-050-bhagavata-purana]])
- **Pushkar temple** (Rajasthan): the principal surviving Brahmā temple; the canonical center of his (limited) active cult. Continuous attestation from at least the medieval period.

## Mythological role
Brahmā's role is cosmogonic-functional within the Trimurti structure:

1. **Creator at the dawn of each cycle (*kalpa*).** At the beginning of each cosmic cycle Brahmā is born from the lotus rising from Vishnu's navel and generates the cosmos. The cycle is completed by Shiva's dissolution and resumes with Vishnu's next cosmic sleep and Brahmā's next lotus-birth.
2. **Father of the *manasaputra*.** The seven mind-born sons (Marichi, Atri, Angiras, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Vasishtha) and various Prajapatis are Brahmā's offspring; they in turn generate the lineages of gods, sages, and beings.
3. **Boon-granter to ascetics (frequently catastrophic).** A recurring Puranic narrative pattern: an asura performs extreme tapas; Brahmā is compelled by the cosmic order to grant a boon; the asura uses the boon for cosmic damage; Vishnu or Shiva must intervene to restore order. Brahmā's boon-granting is theologically *required* (he cannot refuse extreme tapas) but narratively *destabilizing* — a structural feature of Puranic mythography (Doniger 1976).
4. **The severed fifth head.** A celebrated myth: Brahmā originally had five heads; Shiva severs the fifth in punishment for various offenses (versions differ — for lying, for lust toward his daughter Saraswati, for arrogance). The Brahmā-shaped skull becomes attached to Shiva's hand as the *Kapali* (skull-bearer) iconographic motif. (Doniger 1976.)

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[prajapati]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Vedic-Brahmanic Prajapati is the proto-Brahmā creator-figure absorbed into Brahmā as the Puranic tradition crystallizes (Bailey 1983).
- **Hiranyagarbha** (the "Golden Womb" of RV 10.121): partially identified — the cosmogonic-egg from which the creator emerges is sometimes Brahmā-as-Hiranyagarbha in Puranic readings, though the original Rig Vedic Hiranyagarbha is more abstract.

## Iconography
See YAML for full attributes. The canonical Brahmā is:
- Four-faced (originally five), one face toward each cardinal direction.
- Four-armed, holding the Vedas, *kamandalu*, *mala*, and a sacrificial implement or lotus.
- White beard / elder appearance — "Grandfather" (*Pitamaha*).
- Seated on a **lotus-throne** (the cosmogonic lotus from Vishnu's navel) or on his *vahana* the **hamsa** (swan / goose).
- Red or golden complexion.

The **lotus-throne** is iconographically load-bearing — Brahmā IS the lotus-born creator. See [[lotus]] symbol node; Brahmā is canonical instance.

## Disputes
- **Why Brahmā has so little active cult.** The standard scholarly answer (Doniger 1976) is structural: the creator-deity's work is *done* at the start of the cycle; the preserver and destroyer have ongoing cosmic-maintenance functions that translate into ongoing ritual functions. Various Puranic legends explain the cult-absence as a curse (e.g., Saraswati's or Shiva's curse following the fifth-head myth), but these are after-the-fact explanations of an empirical fact.
- **Prajapati ↔ Brahmā continuity.** Whether Brahmā is best read as Prajapati renamed-and-iconographically-elaborated or as a fresh post-Vedic deity onto whom Prajapati's functions are mapped. Bailey 1983 argues for substantial continuity; mainstream scholarship follows.
- **Five heads vs. four.** The five-headed Brahmā with severed fifth head is canonical in Puranic mythography; some readings treat the fifth head as an iconographic-textual archaism.
