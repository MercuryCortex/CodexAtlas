# Heimdall

## Identity
The **watchman of the Æsir** — Heimdall stands at the eastern end of the rainbow-bridge **Bifröst** with his horn Gjallarhorn, guarding the approach to Ásgarðr. He needs less sleep than a bird, sees a hundred leagues by night or day, and can hear grass growing on the earth and wool growing on a sheep. At **Ragnarök** he sounds Gjallarhorn to summon the gods to their final battle, then kills and is killed by Loki — the mutual-destruction motif structurally common to several Ragnarök duels.

## Mythological role
**Rígsþula**: Heimdall, under the name *Rígr*, walks among humans and stays the night with three couples in three households — slaves' shack, freeholders' farm, jarl's hall — engendering by each wife the founding ancestors of the three social classes (Þræll → thralls, Karl → freemen, Jarl → nobles). Heimdall is thus **the divine origin of Norse social stratification**, with the *Völuspá*'s famous epithet for humanity — **"mighty descendants of Heimdall"** — applying to all three classes alike.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[gabriel-archangel]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: eschatological horn-blower.
- **[[purusha]] / [[ymir]]** — *parallel-motif*: divine origin of human social classes (Puruṣasūkta of Rig Veda 10.90 generates the four *varṇa*; Heimdall generates three Norse classes).
