# Ullikummi

## Identity

Ullikummi is the **stone monster of the Hurrian–Hittite Kumarbi cycle** — a blind, deaf giant of diorite or basalt that the deposed father-god [[kumarbi|Kumarbi]] engenders upon a great Rock for the express purpose of overthrowing his son, the storm-warrior king [[teshub|Teshub]]. It is not a worshipped deity with a cult, an iconography, or a genealogy of its own: it is a *chaos-being*, an instrument of a defeated god's revenge, made of stone precisely so that it can neither see, hear, nor be reasoned with. In the Atlas it is placed among the deities (following the same convention as the Hittite chaos-serpent [[illuyanka|Illuyanka]]) because it fills the **chaos-antagonist slot of the Anatolian** [[chaoskampf|Chaoskampf]] — the Hurrian occupant of the role held by [[tiamat|Tiamat]] in Babylon, [[yam|Yam]] at Ugarit, and [[typhon|Typhon]] in Hesiod. Its sole surviving source is the *Song of Ullikummi* (CTH 345), preserved in Hurrian and Hittite copies in the Boğazköy archive at Hattusa.

## In the Song of Ullikummi (CTH 345)

The *Song of Ullikummi* is the third movement of the Kumarbi cycle, following the *Song of Going Forth* (the succession proper, in which Kumarbi bites off and swallows the genitals of the sky-god Anu and is thereby made pregnant with Teshub) and the *Song of Hedammu* (Kumarbi's first monster-child, the sea-dragon). Having lost the kingship of heaven to Teshub, Kumarbi resolves to grow a champion that the storm-god cannot defeat:

- **Engendering.** Kumarbi unites with a great **Rock**, which bears him a son of stone. The child is named **Ullikummi** and is made of *kunkunuzzi*-stone (diorite/basalt). Because he is stone, he is **blind and deaf** — invulnerable to persuasion or trickery, unlike the seducible Hedammu.
- **Growth on the world-pillar.** To hide and nourish him, the infant is set on the right shoulder of **Ubelluri** — the giant who stands in the sea holding up heaven and earth, the **Hurrian Atlas-figure**. There Ullikummi grows at monstrous speed, an acre a day, until he rises out of the sea and reaches the sky.
- **The crisis in heaven.** Ullikummi's growth **blinds / displaces the Sun-God** and threatens to drive the gods from heaven; Teshub, warned by the Sun-God and by his sister Šauška, goes out to fight and is **initially defeated** — the storm-god cannot bring the stone giant down by force.
- **Ea's solution.** The gods turn to the wise god **Ea** (Hurrian Enki, the cunning craftsman-god). Ea descends to the storehouse of the primeval gods and fetches the **ancient copper cutting-tool with which heaven and earth were originally separated**, and with it **severs Ullikummi from the shoulder of Ubelluri**. Cut off from the world-pillar that fed his strength, the monster is fatally weakened.
- **Resolution.** With Ullikummi no longer rooted to Ubelluri, **Teshub defeats him** and reclaims cosmic kingship. The tablet is broken at the very end, but the narrative arc — the storm-king's sovereignty restored after the wise god neutralizes the chaos-monster — is secure.

The decisive narrative logic is distinctive within the chaoskampf family: the storm-god's raw combat *fails*, and victory comes only when a **cunning third god** removes the monster's structural support. This "wise-god resolver" beat is one of the features that links the episode to Hesiod's Typhonomachy (below).

## Cross-tradition significance (the Anatolian chaos-being)

Ullikummi is the **Hurrian–Hittite instance of the** [[chaoskampf|Chaoskampf]] **antagonist**: the chaos-being that the storm-warrior must defeat to secure or restore cosmic order. Its slayer [[teshub|Teshub]] and its begetter [[kumarbi|Kumarbi]] are already enrolled in the Atlas's chaoskampf hub; Ullikummi completes the Hurrian-cycle row of the hub's cross-tradition grid, set beside [[tiamat|Tiamat]] (defeated by Marduk), [[yam|Yam]] (defeated by Baal), and [[typhon|Typhon]] (defeated by Zeus).

Two registers of parallel must be kept apart:

- **Secure, within-tradition.** That Ullikummi is a chaos-monster of the storm-god combat, engendered by Kumarbi and defeated through Ea and Teshub, is simply what the text says (Hoffner 1998; Güterbock 1951–52). The kinship with the Hittite serpent [[illuyanka|Illuyanka]] — the other Anatolian chaos-antagonist of the storm-god, likewise beaten only with outside help — is a same-culture typological match, not a contested inference.
- **Cross-tradition / transmission-debated.** The **structural parallel to Hesiod's [[typhon|Typhon]]** (Theogony 820–880) — a monstrous challenger to the storm-king's sovereignty resolved by the intervention of a cunning god (Ea / Hermes) — belongs to the larger, well-known case that the **Kumarbi cycle is a documented source-stream for the Greek succession myth** (West 1997; Bachvarova 2016). Here the parallel is **structural / typological and a transmission claim**, not a linguistic cognate: scholarship treats the *fact* of Hurrian-Anatolian → Greek influence on this material as strong, while debating the **mechanism** (Late Bronze Age singer networks vs Iron Age "orientalizing" Phoenician intermediaries — see Disputes on [[kumarbi|Kumarbi]]).

The wider link to the Mesopotamian paradigm ([[tiamat|Tiamat]]) is **general chaoskampf typology** only — a shared "storm-warrior defeats primordial chaos-being to secure kingship" pattern, plausibly spread through Bronze Age cultural networks, not a claim of textual descent for Ullikummi specifically.

## Disputes

- **Reading and by-names.** The monster's stone material, *kunkunuzzi* (diorite/basalt), is sometimes treated by translators almost as an alternative designation; the precise mineralogical identification and whether *kunkunuzzi* functions as a by-name are points of philological detail (Güterbock 1951–52; Hoffner 1998).
- **Fragmentary ending.** The Song of Ullikummi survives in a **broken state**; the final defeat is reconstructed from the narrative logic and from the cycle's overall arc rather than read off a complete colophon. The restoration is mainstream but not textually total.
- **Mechanism of Greek transmission.** As with the cycle as a whole, the **fact** of the Hurrian-Anatolian → Greek parallel (Ullikummi ↔ Typhon, Kumarbi ↔ Kronos) is broadly accepted, while the **route** is debated — West (1997, 2007) favors direct Bronze Age transmission, Burkert (1992) Iron Age Phoenician mediation, Bachvarova (2016) a continuous singer tradition. This debate is carried in full on the [[kumarbi|Kumarbi]] node.
