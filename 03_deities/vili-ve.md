# Vili and Ve

## Identity

Vili and Vé are the two brothers of [[odin]] — the sons of [[bor-norse]] and the giantess Bestla — who, together with Odin, **create the world** and animate the first human beings. Their names are meaningful: *Vili* means "will" and *Vé* means "holy place" or "sanctuary," so that the creator-triad embodies, alongside Odin (often glossed as "spirit/inspiration"), the will and the sacred. (The *Völuspá* gives a variant creating triad of Odin, Hœnir, and Lóðurr — a parallel threefold form of the same creation.)

## Mythological role

The brothers perform the two foundational acts of Norse cosmogony. First, they **slay the primordial giant [[ymir]]** and build the world from his dismembered body: his flesh becomes the earth, his blood the seas and lakes, his bones the mountains, his skull the dome of the sky (held up at four corners by dwarves), and his brains the clouds. Second, walking along the seashore, they find two lifeless tree-trunks — Ask and Embla — and **give them the faculties of life**: Odin gives breath/spirit, Vili gives mind and movement (or wit and feeling), and Vé gives the senses, speech, and outward form. Thus humankind receives its powers from the three brothers together. In one striking episode (in *Lokasenna* and Ynglinga saga), when Odin is away in exile, Vili and Vé take over his wife Frigg — a hint of their near-equivalence to Odin.

## Cross-tradition significance

Vili and Vé anchor two great Norse mythic patterns. The **creation of the world from a slain primordial being** (Ymir) is the Norse member of a widespread Indo-European and Near Eastern motif — the cosmos formed from the body of a primal giant or chaos-monster (compare Tiamat, from whose body Marduk builds the world). And the **threefold gift of life to the first humans** is the Norse anthropogony, in which mind, sense, and breath are conferred by a divine triad — a counterpart to the various creation-of-humanity accounts in which the gods endow lifeless matter with the faculties of a living person.
