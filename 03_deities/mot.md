# Mot

Mot (Ugaritic *Môtu*; cognate with Hebrew *māwet*, "death") is the personified Death of Canaanite mythology and Baal's principal cosmic antagonist in the third section of the [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]] (KTU 1.5–1.6). Mot is depicted with an enormous insatiable mouth: "His lip to earth, his lip to the heavens; his tongue to the stars. Baal must enter his innards, must go down into his mouth like an olive-cake, the produce of the earth and the fruit of the trees." He summons Baal to the underworld; Baal cannot refuse; Baal descends and dies; the cosmos withers; Anat eventually kills Mot by tearing him apart, threshing, burning, grinding, and scattering — but Mot too, like Baal, revives, and the second confrontation (KTU 1.6 VI) ends with Shapash the sun-goddess mediating between Baal and Mot: Mot's submission and Baal's restoration to kingship.

## Mythological role
Mot encodes the Levantine seasonal cycle (dry season as Mot's victory, rainy season as Baal's return) at the cosmic mythological level. Theologically he is the necessary negative principle whose periodic defeat by Baal constitutes the cosmic order. His persistence as a *defeated but not eliminated* power matches the cyclic reality of agricultural Levantine experience. The Hebrew Bible preserves residual death-personification language clearly derived from this Canaanite background: Hosea 13:14 ("O Death, where are your plagues? O Sheol, where is your destruction?") and Isaiah 25:8 ("He will swallow up death forever") use the same vocabulary and imagery in personified-Death poetry. Paul's quotation "O death, where is your victory?" (1 Cor 15:55) preserves the line into the New Testament — a distant theological descendant of the Ugaritic Mot.
