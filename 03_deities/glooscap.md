# Glooscap

The **Wabanaki culture-hero** — known across the Mi'kmaq, Maliseet, Passamaquoddy, Penobscot, and Abenaki nations of the Atlantic Northeast (Maritimes, New England, Quebec, Maine). Glooscap is a **transformer-creator**: he shapes the existing world's features (the cliffs of Cape Blomidon in Nova Scotia are his work; the great Saint John River is his canoe-track; the moose population was reduced from giant-original-size to current-size by his hand), gives names to animals and plants, teaches the people how to live, and departs across the eastern water in a stone canoe with a promise of return.

The Glooscap narratives are paired with his brother **Malsumis the Wolf** — the malevolent twin-counterpart whose attempts to undo Glooscap's creation are repeatedly thwarted. The twin-creator-and-malevolent-other pattern is structurally identical to the Haudenosaunee Sky-Woman-twins ([[sapling-deity]] and [[flint-deity]]) — a probable common northeastern-North-American substrate.

Glooscap is one of the **Algonquian-family culture-hero pair**: Glooscap covers the northeast woodlands; [[manabozho]] covers the Great Lakes Anishinaabe; the Cree Wisakedjak covers the boreal northwest. All three share narrative DNA — a shared Algonquian-substrate culture-hero who shifts identity-and-name across the family's vast linguistic territory.
