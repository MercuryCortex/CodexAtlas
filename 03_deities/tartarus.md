# Tartarus

## Identity

Tartarus (Greek **Tartaros**) is, in Greek cosmogony, both a **primordial deity** and the **place** he personifies — the deep abyss at the very bottom of the cosmos, far below the ordinary underworld of [[hades]]. Hesiod names him among the first beings to exist, alongside [[chaos-primordial]] and [[gaia]], making Tartarus part of the irreducible original structure of the world: the pit beneath everything.

## Mythological role

Hesiod measures the depth of Tartarus famously: a bronze anvil would fall nine days from heaven to earth, and nine more from earth to Tartarus — it is as far below the underworld as the sky is above the earth. It is the **prison of the Titans**, into which Zeus casts Kronos and his allies after the Titanomachy, sealed with bronze gates made by Poseidon. Uniting with Gaia (Earth), Tartarus fathers **[[typhon]]**, the hundred-headed storm-serpent whose battle with Zeus is the Greek form of the chaoskampf. In later tradition Tartarus becomes the place of punishment for the great sinners (Tantalus, Sisyphus, Ixion) — the Greek "hell" beneath Hades.

## Cross-tradition significance

As a **primordial abyss** present from the first moment of creation, Tartarus belongs to the family of cosmogonic "deeps" and voids — conceptually adjacent to the primordial-waters/void complex of [[chaos-primordial]] (the Greek yawning gap), the Norse Ginnungagap, and the Mesopotamian Apsu. As the father (with Gaia) of Typhon, he also stands at the origin of the Greek chaoskampf, linking the primordial-abyss motif to the storm-god-versus-monster combat that recurs across the ancient Near East.
