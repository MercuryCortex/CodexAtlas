# The Norns

## Identity

The Norns (Old Norse **Nornir**) are the Norse goddesses of fate — most famously a **triad** named **Urðr** (Urd, "that which became," the past), **Verðandi** ("becoming," the present), and **Skuld** ("that which shall be," the future). They shape the *ørlög* — the "primal layings-down," the destiny — of gods and human beings alike, and they tend the world-tree **Yggdrasil** at the Well of Urd, where the gods hold their council.

## Mythological role

In the *Völuspá* and Snorri's *Gylfaginning*, the three great Norns come from the Well of Urd beneath one of Yggdrasil's roots; they carve fate onto wood (or, in later imagery, weave it), and each day they draw water and white clay from the well to sprinkle the tree so that its limbs do not rot — sustaining the cosmos itself even as they apportion the fates within it. Beyond the three great Norns there are said to be many lesser norns — some of the Aesir, some of the elves, some of the dwarves — who attend each birth and "shape the life" of the newborn, some kindly and some cruel, which is why people's fortunes differ. The Norns' decrees are **binding even on the gods**: the doom of the Aesir at Ragnarök is something the Norns have already laid down.

## Cross-tradition significance

The Norns are the Norse member of the **Indo-European fate-triad** — three female powers who measure out destiny — and stand in close parallel to the Greek **[[the-moirai]]** (Clotho, Lachesis, Atropos) and behind them [[ananke]] (Necessity). The shared image of three goddesses who spin, weave, or carve the fixed thread of every life, and to whose decree even the highest gods must submit, is one of the clearest cases of a common Indo-European mythic inheritance. The Norns thus complete the cross-tradition **fate** neighborhood: Norns ↔ Moirai ↔ Necessity, paralleled further by the Mesopotamian "decreeing of destinies."
