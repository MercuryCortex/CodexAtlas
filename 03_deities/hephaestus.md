# Hephaestus

## Identity
Hephaestus is the Greek Olympian smith-god: lord of fire (in its forge-and-volcano aspect, distinct from Hestia's hearth-fire and Helios's solar fire), master of metallurgical craft, maker of the gods' weapons and palaces, the lame artisan ridiculed by the other Olympians yet indispensable. Born to Hera (in Hesiod, parthenogenetically as her retaliation for Zeus's parthenogenetic Athena; in Homer, of Zeus and Hera together), he is cast from heaven (twice, in different versions) and lands lame on Lemnos. His marriage to Aphrodite — and her affair with Ares — is one of the famous comic-tragic vignettes of the Odyssey.

Hephaestus's volcanic association ties his workshop to Mount Etna in Sicily, Mount Mosychlos on Lemnos, and Mount Olympus's hidden forges. Herodotus identifies him with the Egyptian Ptah, the great Memphite craft-god / creator. Lemnos was a major cult center, with associations to the Indo-Mediterranean pre-Greek substrate; some scholars have argued Hephaestus is a pre-Greek deity inherited by the Greek-speakers. The Roman Vulcan inherits the smith-god role.
