# Faunus (Roman)

## Identity

Faunus is one of Rome's oldest indigenous deities — a woodland and pastoral god predating the major Greek imports. He is god of forests (*silvae*), fields, flocks (*greges*), and wilderness, but also a prophetic deity whose oracles were delivered through dreams and voices heard in the forest. As **Lupercus** he presides over the Lupercalia festival. As **Inuus** he protects livestock fertility. As **Fatuus** ("the speaker") he delivers prophecy.

The *fauni* (plural) are also a class of woodland spirits — half-man, half-goat beings — analogous to Greek satyrs and sileni. The individual Faunus is their king and archetype.

## Lupercalia

**Lupercalia** (February 15) is one of Rome's oldest and most resilient festivals — attested archaeologically to before the foundation of the Republic and surviving until at least 494 CE (when Pope Gelasius I condemned it). It is the festival most directly tied to Faunus in his Lupercus aspect.

The ritual:
1. Two groups of priests (*Luperci* — the *Quinctiales* and *Fabiani* representing Rome's two founding families) sacrificed goats and a dog at the **Lupercal cave** (the cave on the Palatine Hill where the she-wolf nursed Romulus and Remus, according to myth).
2. The priests smeared blood on two young men's foreheads with a bloody knife, then wiped it off with wool dipped in milk; the men were required to laugh.
3. The *Luperci* then ran naked or nearly naked through the streets of Rome carrying *februa* — strips of goatskin (the *thong of Faunus*). Women who were struck by these thongs were believed to gain fertility or easy childbirth.

This ritual has several distinct elements: purification (*februare* = to purify, giving February its name), fertility promotion, and possibly an archaic apotropaic rite against wolves (*lupus* = wolf; the Lupercal = the Wolf Cave). In 44 BCE, **Julius Caesar** famously declined the crown offered to him by Mark Antony at the Lupercalia — one of the most politically significant moments in Roman history framed within this ancient pastoral festival.

The Lupercalia's survival into late antiquity and its eventual Christianization (Pope Gelasius I argued in 494 CE that it had been Christianized as the Feast of the Purification of Mary, February 2 — though this is debated) makes it one of the clearest case studies in Roman religion's transformation under Christianity.

## Prophecy

Faunus's prophetic function is distinctive: he delivers oracles not through formal temple consultation but through wild, nocturnal means — voices heard in forests, dreams sent to sleepers lying on sacred fleeces (*incubatio*). This "feral prophecy" contrasts with Apollo's orderly Delphic oracles and reflects Faunus's archaic, pre-Hellenized character. He is a god of the *liminal* — the edges of fields, the night, the forest margin.

Virgil (*Aeneid* 7.81–106) presents Faunus as an oracle-god consulted by King Latinus through incubation at his sacred grove, foreshadowing the coming of Aeneas and the Trojan-Roman destiny. This Virgilian scene explicitly ties Faunus to the mythological pre-history of Rome.

## Relation to Pan

The identification with **[[pan-greek]]** was ancient and literary from at least Virgil onward. Both are:
- Goat-legged / goat-horned woodland gods
- Patron of shepherds and flocks
- Connected to music (pan pipes)
- Sources of sudden irrational fear (*panic* = Pan's terror)

But Faunus retains distinctively Roman characteristics: the prophetic function, the Lupercalia ritual, and the archaic Latin genealogy (Faunus as son of Picus, grandson of Saturn — placing him in the pre-Roman Italic mythological lineage). Roman Faunus is not merely Pan under a Latin name.

## Cross-tradition connections

- **[[pan-greek]]** — *ancient-identification*: explicit in Virgil and Ovid; both are horned woodland fertility deities; the identification is literary but deep.
- **[[cernunnos]]** — *parallel-motif*: Celtic antlered lord-of-animals; Gallo-Roman syncretism zones produced hybrid iconography; no documented Roman ancient-identification, but the structural parallel is one of the strongest cross-Celtic-Italic examples.
- **[[saturn-roman]]** — *family-connection* (variant genealogy): Faunus sometimes appears as Saturn's son or grandson, placing him in the archaic pre-Olympian Roman divine lineage.
- **[[diana-roman]]** — *shared-domain*: both are wilderness deities with lunar (Diana) and forest (Faunus) aspects; Diana Fauns pairing appears in forest-shrine contexts.

## Disputes

- **Lupercalia's meaning**: the precise purpose of the Lupercalia has been debated since antiquity. Is it primarily a fertility rite? Purification rite? Wolf-apotropaic? The mixture of goat sacrifice, blood-smearing, naked running, and thong-striking suggests a genuinely archaic ritual with multiple accumulated layers.
- **Faunus vs. Lupercus**: some ancient sources treat these as separate deities who were later merged; others treat Lupercus as simply Faunus's title in the Lupercalia context. Modern scholarship leans toward treating them as aspects of a single deity-complex.
