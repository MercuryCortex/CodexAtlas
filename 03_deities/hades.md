# Hades

## Identity
Hades is the Greek lord of the underworld and ruler of the dead. The third of the three Cronid brothers (with Zeus and Poseidon), he received the underworld as his domain when they divided the cosmos by lot. His name was avoided in speech — speaking the name was thought to risk attracting his attention — and was often replaced with euphemisms: *Plouton* ("Wealthy One", whence Latin Pluto, referencing his subterranean wealth in grain and metals) or simply "the Other One". His realm in Homer is a shadowy, joyless half-existence; the Odyssey's Nekyia (book 11) is the locus classicus for early Greek conceptions of the dead.

Hades is the abductor of Persephone, daughter of Demeter — the myth (most fully in the Homeric Hymn to Demeter) that grounds the Eleusinian mysteries and establishes the seasonal cycle of Persephone's half-year in the underworld and half-year above. In post-Homeric and especially Hellenistic religion, Hades / Plouton acquires positive associations: the Eleusinian mysteries promise initiates a happier post-mortem condition, and in the Hellenistic period the Egyptian-Greek syncretic god Serapis (Osiris + Hades/Apis) becomes a major imperial cult.
