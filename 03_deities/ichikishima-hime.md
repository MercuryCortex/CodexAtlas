# Ichikishima-hime

Ichikishima-hime is the youngest of the three **Munakata goddesses** (*Munakata Sanjojin*) of Shinto — sea-deities born, according to the *Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki*, when **[[susanoo]]** and his sister **[[amaterasu]]** made their *ukehi* pledge: Amaterasu took Susanoo's sword, broke it in three, chewed and blew it out as mist, and from that mist came the three sisters. With her sisters **[[tagori-hime]]** and **[[tagitsu-hime]]** she guards the perilous sea-lanes between Kyushu and the Korean peninsula — the goddesses of **Munakata Taisha**, whose island shrine of **Okinoshima** preserved one of the greatest hoards of ancient maritime votive offerings (a UNESCO World Heritage site). Ichikishima-hime is the most widely worshipped of the three, enshrined at **Itsukushima** (Miyajima), famous for the great vermilion *torii* standing in the sea. Her chief historical importance is a transmission-wire: under **honji-suijaku** she was identified with **[[benzaiten]]** — the Japanese form of the Indian goddess **Sarasvatī** — so that the Shinto sea-maiden and the Buddhist goddess of music, eloquence, and wealth merged into one venerated figure across Japan (Itsukushima, Enoshima, Chikubushima). She is thus a node where the indigenous kami tradition and the India-derived Buddhist pantheon fuse.
