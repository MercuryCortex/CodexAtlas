# Urcuchillay

Urcuchillay (Quechua *Urquchillay*) is the Inca deity who **protects and multiplies the herds** — above all the **llamas and alpacas** on which Andean pastoral life depended. The chronicler Bernabé Cobo records that the herders worshipped him as a brilliantly multicolored llama, beseeching him to keep their animals safe and fertile. His most striking feature is celestial. The chroniclers associate the herd-protector llama with a constellation (Cobo/Polo link it to stars in **Lyra**), while the Inca also read a great **"Llama" in the dark of the Milky Way** — and the two are often merged under his name. This celestial Llama is, uniquely for Andean astronomy, a **dark-cloud constellation** (*Yacana*): a llama-shaped silhouette read not in the stars but in the black interstellar dust-lanes of the Milky Way, its two bright "eyes" the stars α and β Centauri. In the cosmology recorded by Urton, the dark Llama descends at midnight to **drink water** from the earthly sea, regulating the flow of springs and rivers — so the humble herd-protector is woven into the Andean water cycle and the turning of the heavens. He exemplifies the distinctively Andean reading of the sky by its dark shapes as much as its lights.
