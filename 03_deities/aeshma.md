# Aeshma

## Identity
Aeshma (Avestan *Aēšma*) is **the principal daeva ("demon") of wrath in Zoroastrian theology** — the personified destructive-violent rage that corrupts humans at the moment of anger, the cosmic opposite of [[sraosha]] (the yazata of obedience and hearkening to the divine word). Aeshma is **one of the four arch-daevas** of Zoroastrian demonology alongside Aka Manah (Evil Mind, opposite of [[the-amesha-spentas|Vohu Manah]]), [[druj]] (the Lie), and Azi (Greed). Aeshma's signature attribute is the **bloodied bludgeon** (*xrūidru-*) — the weapon of murderous violence. For the Codex Atlas mission Aeshma is **massively load-bearing** as the source-figure for **Asmodeus** in Hebrew Apocryphal and rabbinic and Christian demonology — **the single most-direct etymological-and-genealogical chain from Zoroastrian demonology into the Western Abrahamic demonological tradition**.

## Earliest attestation
- **Gathas** ([[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]]): Aeshma appears in Yasna 29.1, 30.6, 48.7, 49.4 — the personified violent-wrath that corrupts humanity, in particular the rage that turns nomadic-cattle-raiding into a destructive cosmic principle.
- **Younger Avesta** ([[phase-2-004-yasna-younger-avesta]]): Yasna 10.8, Yasht 11 (the entire Yasht dedicated to Sraosha and his opposition to Aeshma), Vendidad 19. Aeshma becomes the canonically-paired adversary of [[sraosha]].
- **Pahlavi tradition** (~+300 onward): Aeshma persists as principal daeva of wrath; the Bundahishn and Denkard treat his theological-cosmic role.

### The Hebrew-Apocryphal appropriation
- **Book of Tobit** (~-200 to -100, Hellenistic-Jewish Apocrypha): the demon **Asmodeus** (Greek *Asmodaios*, Aramaic-Hebrew *Ashmedai* / *Asmedai*) is the principal antagonist of the narrative. He has killed Sarah's seven previous bridegrooms on their wedding nights; he is bound by the angel **Raphael** "in the deserts of Upper Egypt" (Tobit 8:3) after Tobias drives him away with the smoke of fish-liver-and-heart. **The book's demonology is explicitly Persian-influenced** — the seven-archangel scheme + Asmodeus + the bound-in-the-desert exorcism-narrative all reflect intensive Iranian religious-cultural impact on Hellenistic Diaspora Judaism.
- **Rabbinic Aramaic-Hebrew**: extensive Ashmedai narratives in **Babylonian Talmud Gittin 68a-b** (the demon-king is tricked by Solomon using a magic ring; Asmodeus is forced to build the Temple but eventually overthrows Solomon and reigns disguised as the king for years before being defeated). Targumic literature elaborates.
- **Medieval Christian demonology**: Asmodeus appears in the *Testament of Solomon* (3rd-c. CE pseudepigraphon) as one of the named demons Solomon binds; in medieval Christian demonological catalogs (Wierus's *Pseudomonarchia Daemonum* 1577; *Lemegeton / Lesser Key of Solomon* 17th c.) Asmodeus is one of the **seven princes of Hell**, associated with lust.

## Mythological role

### In Zoroastrian theology
Aeshma is the **cosmic principle of wrath-and-violence**, paired oppositionally with [[sraosha]]. The Sraosha-Aeshma combat is a structural pattern of Zoroastrian theology: every act of obedient-hearkening to the divine word weakens Aeshma; every act of violent-rage strengthens him. Aeshma is also the demon who at death seeks to drag the souls of the wicked down from the Cinvat Bridge.

### In Hebrew-Christian demonology
Asmodeus's role transforms in transmission:
- **Tobit**: lust-demon who kills bridegrooms before consummation (Sarah's seven husbands); exorcised by smoke of fish + by the binding of Raphael.
- **Rabbinic Gittin**: trickster-demon-king with quasi-juridical relations to Solomon; the demon-as-cosmic-counterpart-to-the-righteous-king.
- **Medieval Christian**: one of the seven princes of Hell; associated with lust. Often depicted with three heads (man, ram, bull).
- **Modern occult / popular**: the demon-name persists in 19th–20th-c. Gothic-horror, Goetic-magical, and pop-cultural contexts.

## Cross-tradition identifications

### THE MASSIVE-WIN: Aeshma → Asmodeus etymological-genealogical chain

The transmission **Aēšma (Avestan daeva) → Asmedai (Hebrew Apocrypha + rabbinic) → Asmodeus (Greek-Christian) → Christian demonology** is **the single cleanest documented Zoroastrian-into-Abrahamic demonological transmission** in the entire religious-historical record. Hutter 1988 and the Encyclopaedia Iranica entry trace the phonetic transmission step by step:

- Avestan ***Aēšma***
- Pahlavi ***Khashm*** (with the *daeva* prefix sometimes preserved)
- Hebrew-Aramaic ***Ashmedai / Asmedai*** (Tobit, rabbinic)
- Greek ***Asmodaios*** (LXX Tobit)
- Latin ***Asmodeus*** (Vulgate)

**This is more than parallel-pattern — this is direct linguistic-transmission of the demon-name across 1500+ years and four languages.** Mainstream Christian demonology textbooks rarely cite the Zoroastrian origin; Zoroastrian studies rarely follow the figure into Christian demonology. The cross-field-boundary makes it gold-zone for the Codex Atlas.

### Other cross-tradition adjacencies
- **Daeva-vs-yazata structure → Christian demon-vs-angel structure**. The Zoroastrian distinction between daevas (demons) and yazatas (worship-worthy beings) provides the **structural template** for Christian demon-vs-angel theology — both are subordinate spiritual beings divided into good and evil camps, with cosmic warfare ongoing between them. Christian angelology (Pseudo-Dionysius's *Celestial Hierarchy*) and demonology (the seven princes of Hell) reflect the Zoroastrian template mediated through Hellenistic-Jewish Second-Temple developments.
- **Aeshma → Mara (Buddhism)** — *scholarly-parallel*. The Buddhist demon Mara (who tempts the Buddha at the moment of enlightenment) shows possible Indo-Iranian shared demon-of-corruption-at-decisive-moment patterning; direct transmission is unprovable.

## Iconography
- **No standard ancient Zoroastrian iconography** — Aeshma as daeva is not depicted in surviving ancient Iranian art (Zoroastrian iconographic tradition is sparse compared to neighboring religions).
- **Pahlavi-period and modern Parsi**: Aeshma occasionally depicted as warrior-figure with bloodied club.
- **Christian demonology**: Asmodeus depicted with three heads (man + ram + bull) in medieval-Christian demonological manuscripts (*Lemegeton*, *Wierus*). The iconography is medieval-Christian, not derived from Zoroastrian.

## Disputes
- **Phonetic chain.** The *Aēšma → Asmedai* transmission is the standard view (Hutter 1988); a minority view holds the Hebrew name has independent Semitic etymology. The standard view is dominant.
- **Tobit's Persian-influence depth.** Whether the Book of Tobit is *thoroughly* Persian-influenced (seven-archangels + Asmodeus + the desert-binding are all Iranian-shaped) or only superficially so is debated. Recent scholarship leans thoroughly.
- **Direction of transmission for the "demon-king" narratives.** Whether the rabbinic Gittin 68 Solomon-Asmodeus narrative descends from Persian folklore or independently develops from biblical-Solomon traditions is debated. Most current scholarship treats it as substantially Persian-mediated.
