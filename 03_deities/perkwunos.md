# *Perkʷunos — the Proto-Indo-European Storm-God

**\*Perkʷunos** is the reconstructed weather- and thunder-god of Proto-Indo-European religion —
not an attested deity but a comparative-linguistic *headwater*, the common ancestor from which
a whole family of historical storm-gods descends. The name is built on the root *\*per-* "to
strike," and clusters with the oak (*\*perkʷus*), the mountain-top, and the god's striking
weapon.

## Two lines of descent — keep them distinct

The scholarship separates two things that are easy to blur:

1. **Etymological reflexes** (the *name* survives): Slavic **[[perun]]** and Baltic
   **[[perkunas]]** are the transparent anchors; the Vedic name-reflex is the rain-god
   *Parjanya*, and the Norse survives in Fjǫrgyn, mother of **[[thor]]**.
2. **The chaoskampf mytheme** (the *story* survives): the storm-god who slays a serpent to
   release the waters — **[[indra]]** vs Vṛtra, **[[thor]]** vs Jǫrmungandr, **[[perun]]** vs
   Veles, **[[zeus]]** vs Typhon. This functional inheritance (see **[[chaoskampf]]**) runs
   wider than the name, reaching gods like Indra and Zeus whose *names* reflex other PIE deities.

This node is the hub that unites four of the Atlas's top-transmission roots — Indra, Thor,
Perun, and (functionally) Zeus — into a single reconstructed family, and shows *how* they are
related: some by inherited name, some by inherited myth. It is the deep-time source of the
storm-god branch; its modern re-synthesis runs through the comparative mythologists
([[james-frazer]], and especially Dumézil and Watkins) who reconstructed it.
