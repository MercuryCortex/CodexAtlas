# Ilmatar (Water-Mother)

Stub — to be expanded. In the Finnish *Kalevala* (compiled by Elias Lönnrot from oral tradition, first published 1835; expanded 1849), Ilmatar — the "Daughter of Nature," an Air-Maiden — descends into the primordial ocean and floats there for 700 years. A teal (diving duck) flies over the water and, finding no land, lands on Ilmatar's knee, which rises from the water. The duck lays seven eggs (six of gold, one of iron) on Ilmatar's knee. She shifts and the eggs fall; from their fragments the world is made: the lower shell becomes the earth, the upper shell the sky, the yolk the sun, the white the moon, the smaller fragments the stars and clouds.

Ilmatar then gives birth (after 30 years of labor) to Väinämöinen — the great shaman-hero of the Kalevala — making her the mother of the heroic age.

**Cross-tradition significance**: See [[earth-diver-creation]] for the full cross-tradition table. Ilmatar is the Finnish cognate of the Haudenosaunee [[sky-woman-haudenosaunee]] — both are primordial feminine figures floating on cosmic waters through whom the world is created. The Kalevala version uniquely bridges the **earth-diver** motif (the diving duck as agent of creation) and the **cosmic egg** motif ([[cosmic-egg]]) — the duck's egg is both the diving act (bringing creation from above to the floating Water-Mother) and the egg-as-world-in-potential. This is the clearest bridge between the two major North Eurasian cosmogonic motif-types.
