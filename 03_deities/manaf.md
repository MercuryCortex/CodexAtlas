# Manāf

Manāf was a **deity of the Quraysh**, the ruling tribe of pre-Islamic **Mecca**, and according to Ibn al-Kalbī's *Book of Idols* one of the **greatest of their idols** — an image that worshippers would touch or stroke as an act of devotion. His specific character and function are, however, poorly preserved: the early Islamic antiquarians who recorded the pagan past were more interested in condemning the idols than in describing their theology, and Manāf survives mostly as a name. That name, though, was clearly weighty: the clan **ʿAbd Manāf** ("servant of Manāf") was one of the leading lineages of the Quraysh and an ancestral line of the Prophet Muhammad himself, a sign of how prominent the god had once been. He stood among the Meccan idols — with **[[hubal|Hubal]]** and the goddesses al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, and Manāt — that the rise of Islam would sweep away. Manāf exemplifies how thoroughly the pre-Islamic Meccan pantheon was effaced, leaving major gods reduced to little more than a remembered name.
