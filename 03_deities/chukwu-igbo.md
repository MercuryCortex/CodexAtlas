# Chukwu

## Identity
Supreme being of Igbo traditional religion (southeastern Nigeria). The name *Chukwu* combines *Chi* (personal-spirit, destiny, divine particle) with *ukwu* (great) — "the Great Chi." Alternative title *Chineke* unpacks as "Chi who creates" (*Chi-na-eke*). Chukwu is creator, source of every individual's *chi* (the spark of divine destiny each person carries), and the high-god to whom all intermediate Igbo deities (*alusi* or *agbara*) ultimately refer. Metuh (1981) is the standard theological treatment.

## Earliest attestation
Pre-colonial Igbo oral tradition; first European documentation 17th–18th c. trade reports; systematic ethnography Basden (1921), then Igbo-authored theology Metuh (1981) and Achebe's literary documentation (*Things Fall Apart* 1958, *Arrow of God* 1964).

## Mythological role
Chukwu created all things and gave each person their *chi* — the individual destiny-spirit, the divine particle that mediates between Chukwu and the person. The Igbo proverb *Onye kwe, chi ya ekwe* — "if a person agrees, their *chi* agrees" — frames the relationship between human will and divine destiny as collaborative. Chukwu is mostly approached through the *alusi* (intermediate deities — [[ala-igbo]] the earth, Amadioha the thunder, Agwu the divinatory spirit, etc.) rather than directly.

## The Arochukwu oracle
Historically the most significant cult-center of Chukwu was the Long Juju at Arochukwu — an oracle of Chukwu maintained by the Aro confederacy. The oracle adjudicated disputes across Igboland and dominated trans-Igbo politics from the 17th c. through its destruction by British forces in 1902. The Arochukwu oracle's network of trade and slave-supply made Aro influence extensive even though Aro themselves were a small confederacy. Isichei (1973) documents the cult-political integration.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Christian God** — 19th-c missionaries used Chineke as the Igbo-Bible translation for *theos*, and the identification persists. Achebe's *Things Fall Apart* (1958) is the canonical literary documentation of the missionary-Igbo encounter and the dual-translation consequences.

## Cross-tradition parallels
- **[[olodumare]]** (Yoruba), **[[nyame]]** (Akan), **[[mwari]]** (Shona), **[[unkulunkulu-zulu]]** (Zulu) — sub-Saharan-African supreme-being archetype.
- The *chi*-as-personal-divine-particle concept has structural parallels to the Greek *daimon* (a personal divine accompanying spirit), to the Christian *guardian angel*, and to Hindu/Buddhist *karma* as personal-destiny-pattern. Metuh (1981) cautiously notes the Greek *daimon* parallel but resists telescope-scale identification.

## Iconography
No anthropomorphic image; Chukwu is approached through the *alusi* and through personal *chi*. The sun and sky are sometimes referenced; the Aro shrine historically (destroyed 1902) housed a sacred grove and cave-oracle.
