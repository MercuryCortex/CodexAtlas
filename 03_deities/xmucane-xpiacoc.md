# Xmucane and Xpiacoc

## Identity

Xmucane and Xpiacoc are the **divine grandparents** of the *Popol Vuh*, the K'iche' Maya book of creation — the oldest of the gods, the aged diviner-pair who are called "the Bearer and the Begetter," "Grandmother of Day, Grandmother of Light." Xmucane is the grandmother; Xpiacoc the grandfather. As the wise elders of the divine council, they are consulted in the great works of creation, and Xmucane plays the decisive role in the making of human beings.

## Mythological role

In the *Popol Vuh*, after several failed attempts to make creatures who can speak and worship the gods — beings of mud that dissolve, and of wood that have no minds — the creators turn to the right material. **Xmucane grinds the white and yellow maize** into meal, and from this maize-dough the flesh of the first true humans is formed; their substance is corn, the staff of Mesoamerican life. The grandparents are also diviners: they cast lots with maize kernels and coral seeds to read fate. And Xmucane is the bereaved mother and grandmother at the heart of the epic — mother of **[[hun-hunahpu]]** and his brother, who are killed by the lords of the underworld Xibalba, and grandmother of the **Hero Twins** Hunahpu and Xbalanque, whom she raises and who avenge their father.

## Cross-tradition significance

Xmucane's grinding of maize to create humankind is the Maya form of the widespread **creation-of-humanity-from-primal-substance** motif — here the substance is corn, the sacred staple, just as elsewhere it is clay (Mesopotamia, Genesis) or other primal matter. The pairing of an aged grandmother-and-grandfather creator-couple, and the structure of repeated, failed attempts before humanity is rightly made, are distinctive Maya contributions to the cross-tradition anthropogony. As diviners, they also embody the deep Mesoamerican bond between creation, maize, and the calendar.
