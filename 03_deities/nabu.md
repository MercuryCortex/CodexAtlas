# Nabu

## Identity
Nabu is the Babylonian and Assyrian god of writing, wisdom, and destiny — scribe of the gods, son and herald of [[marduk]], keeper of the Tablet of Destinies (*ṭup šīmāti*) on which the fates of all things are inscribed. His chief cult center was the Ezida temple at Borsippa, ~17 km south of Babylon, paired liturgically with Marduk's Esagila in Babylon: at the Babylonian New Year (Akitu) festival the statue of Nabu was processed from Borsippa to Babylon to participate in the renewal of cosmic order. His name *Nabû* derives from the Semitic root *nb'* "to name/call/proclaim" — Nabu is the divine herald-proclaimer. By the Neo-Assyrian and Neo-Babylonian periods Nabu had absorbed the older functions of the Sumerian scribe-goddess Nisaba; the literate professional classes throughout the empire took Nabu as patron, and Nabu-theophoric personal names (Nebuchadnezzar = "Nabu, protect my offspring"; Nabopolassar; Nebuzaradan; biblical Nebo) are pervasive in the period.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[thoth]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: modern comparative scholarship links Nabu and Thoth as the two great Bronze-Age scribal-wisdom-destiny gods; structurally close though without ancient cultic identification.
- **[[hermes-trismegistus]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the late-antique Hellenistic syncretism that produced Hermes Trismegistus (Thoth-Hermes) drew also on the Mesopotamian scribal-mediator type Nabu represents; the [[hermes-trismegistus]] node lists Nabu among its equivalents.
- The biblical "Nebo" is a direct loan; Isaiah 46:1 mocks "Bel bows down, Nebo stoops" — the Hebrew prophets knew Marduk and Nabu as the chief gods of Babylon.

## Disputes
- **Date of cult-elevation.** Nabu rises from a relatively minor deity in the Old Babylonian period to one of the two chief gods of the empire by the Neo-Babylonian period. The precise mechanism (Aramaean migration influence? Babylonian dynastic preference?) is debated; Pomponio (1978) tracks the elevation via the Neo-Assyrian state-cult evidence.
- **Relation to Nisaba.** Sumerian Nisaba was the original scribe-goddess; Nabu absorbs much of her function but not all (Nisaba retains a residual cult). Whether this is gendered replacement or parallel cultic developments is debated.

## Alphabet Layer

Nabu is the patron deity of cuneiform — the world's first writing system, invented c. 3200 BCE in Uruk temples. The full sacred-writing context of cuneiform (temple origin, scribal school system, cylinder seals, connection to Gilgamesh and Enuma Elish) is documented at [[alphabet-cuneiform-sacred]]. Nabu is the first node in the scribal-deity-triad convergence: Nabu (Mesopotamian) / Thoth (Egyptian) / Hermes (Greek) — three independent civilizations independently generated the same boundary-crossing divine scribe figure.
