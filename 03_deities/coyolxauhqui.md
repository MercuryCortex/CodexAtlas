# Coyolxauhqui

The **moon goddess** whose dismemberment by her brother [[huitzilopochtli]] is the founding theological act of the Mexica state. The myth (recorded in Sahagún's *Florentine Codex*, Book 3): [[coatlicue]] (the earth mother) is sweeping at Coatepec ("Snake Mountain"), is mysteriously impregnated by a feather; her existing daughter Coyolxauhqui and 400 sons (the *Centzon Huitznahua*) plot to kill her in shame at the disgraceful pregnancy; Huitzilopochtli is born fully-armed from her womb and beheads Coyolxauhqui, throws her dismembered body down the mountain.

**The Templo Mayor as theological architecture.** The Templo Mayor pyramid at Tenochtitlán IS Coatepec. The great **Coyolxauhqui Stone** (a 3.25-metre carved disk depicting the dismembered goddess) was placed at the base of the pyramid's south stair-case — the position her body landed in the myth. Every human sacrifice performed at the Templo Mayor's summit ended with the corpse being thrown down the steps to land on Coyolxauhqui's image: each sacrifice ritually re-enacted the cosmic dismemberment.

The stone was rediscovered in 1978 during electrical works behind Mexico City's Metropolitan Cathedral — a discovery so important it triggered the Templo Mayor excavation that has since recovered most of what is known about Mexica state religion.
