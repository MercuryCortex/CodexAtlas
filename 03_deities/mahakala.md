# Mahakala

## Identity
Mahākāla — "the Great Black One" / "the Great Time" — is a fierce protective deity shared (with distinct meanings) by Tantric Hinduism and Tantric Buddhism. *In Hinduism*: a fierce manifestation of Śiva associated with time, death, and final dissolution; the *Mahākālī* of Ujjain (the *Mahākāleśvara* temple, one of the twelve Jyotirliṅgas) is a central pilgrimage site. *In Tibetan Buddhism*: the principal *dharmapāla* (dharma-protector) of the Kagyu, Sakya, and Gelug schools, representing the fierce-compassion aspect of awakened mind acting to protect dharma and practitioners. Iconography: dark blue or black skin, multi-armed (typically two, four, or six), wrathful expression with bared fangs and three eyes, garland of severed heads, surrounded by flames; tramples on a corpse. The Buddhist Mahākāla is a tantric *appropriation* and transformation of the Śaiva original — Sanderson 2009 documents the iconographic and ritual continuities — but the doctrinal content is reread through Buddhist categories: Mahākāla's fierceness is the *compassionate* fury that protects beings from their own karmically-produced sufferings.

## Earliest attestation
Hindu: Śaiva Tantric corpus (~5th c. onward); Mahākāla temple at Ujjain attested in classical literature. Buddhist: Tantric Mahākāla cycles in late Indian Buddhism (~8th–11th c.); Tibetan adoption from ~1000.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[rudra-shiva-early]]** — *direct-influence*: Buddhist Mahākāla is an appropriation of Śaiva Mahākāla / Bhairava-Śiva.
- **[[kali]]** — feminine counterpart in Hindu Tantric Shaktism (Mahākāla / Mahākālī).
