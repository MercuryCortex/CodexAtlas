# Tara

## Identity
Tārā — "She Who Crosses Over / Saves" — is the principal feminine Buddha / bodhisattva of Tibetan Buddhism, the swift liberator from suffering. The traditional account: Avalokiteśvara, weeping with compassion at the suffering of beings, sheds a tear; Tārā arises from the tear. She is widely worshipped in twenty-one forms (the *21 Praises of Tārā* is a foundational liturgy); the two most-recognized are **Green Tārā** (associated with active compassion and swift action, the principal everyday-worship Tārā) and **White Tārā** (associated with long life and healing). Iconography: green or white skin (per form), seated in lalitāsana (one leg extended in readiness to step forth), two-armed, holding lotuses. Her *mantra* — *Oṃ Tāre Tuttāre Ture Svāhā* — is one of the most-recited Tibetan Buddhist mantras after Avalokiteśvara's *Oṃ Maṇi Padme Hūṃ*. Atīśa (~1040) brought systematic Tārā tantric practice to Tibet; the Kadampa school he founded made Tārā central, and the practice spread through all subsequent Tibetan schools.

## Earliest attestation
Indian Tantric Buddhist Tārā texts from ~7th c. onward. Tibetan adoption: Atīśa's mission ~1040. The 21-forms iconography is fully developed by ~1100.
