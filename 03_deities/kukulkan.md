# Kukulkán

## Identity
The Maya Feathered Serpent — Yucatec *Kukulkán*, K'iche' *Q'uq'umatz*. Both names mean "Feathered Serpent" in their respective Maya languages. Kukulkán is the Maya manifestation of the pan-Mesoamerican Feathered Serpent complex; the El Castillo pyramid at Chichén Itzá (built ~800–1000 CE) is dedicated to Kukulkán, and at the spring and autumn equinoxes, the play of sunlight on its terraced edges creates a serpent-of-light descending the northern staircase — a deliberate architectural-astronomical-religious effect.

## Earliest attestation
Yucatec Maya Postclassic; Q'uq'umatz attested in the Popol Vuh and K'iche' tradition; Chichén Itzá iconography ~9th–10th c.

## Mythological role
In the Popol Vuh, Q'uq'umatz pairs with Tepeu in the cosmogony — together they speak the world into being. In Yucatec tradition, Kukulkán is associated with the historical-mythic priest-king of Chichén Itzá, a Mexican-influenced figure (the Toltec wave that brought Quetzalcoatl-cult south into Yucatán, ~10th c.).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[quetzalcoatl]]** — *ancient-identification*: Yucatec Kukulkán and central-Mexican Quetzalcoatl are the same Feathered Serpent under different linguistic forms; the identification is internal-Mesoamerican (cf. the Chichén Itzá / Tula architectural-iconographic synthesis ~9th–10th c.).
- **Q'uq'umatz (K'iche' highland Maya)** — *manuscript-transmission*: K'iche'-language form of the same figure.

## Iconography
Feathered serpent; serpent-of-light architectural effect at Chichén Itzá El Castillo; serpent columns at Maya-Toltec buildings.
