# Bor

## Identity

Bor (**Borr** or **Burr**) is a god of the second primordial generation in Norse cosmogony — the son of [[buri]] (the first god) and the father, by the giantess Bestla, of [[odin]] and his brothers Vili and Ve. He is essentially a **genealogical link**: the bridge between the spontaneously-generated first god and the brothers who create the world.

## Mythological role

Snorri's *Gylfaginning* states it briefly: Buri had a son named Bor, who took as his wife **Bestla**, the daughter of the frost-giant Bölthorn; and Bor and Bestla had three sons — **[[odin]], Vili, and Ve** ([[vili-ve]]). This marriage is significant: it **joins the divine line of Buri to the giant line**, so that Odin and his brothers are descended on their mother's side from the giants (the *jötnar*) whom the gods will so often oppose. Bor himself performs no deeds in the surviving myths; his importance is entirely in his place in the chain Buri → Bor → Odin, and in the giant-marriage that makes the chief gods part-giant.

## Cross-tradition significance

Bor exemplifies the Norse cosmogony's interest in **genealogy as cosmic structure** — the careful tracing of the gods' descent from the primordial ice (through Buri and Bor) and their intermarriage with the giants who embody the older, chaotic powers of the world. The motif of the creator-gods being themselves descended from, and married into, the primordial giant-race is a distinctive feature of Norse myth, dramatizing the deep kinship — and enmity — between the ordering gods and the elemental forces they must subdue.
