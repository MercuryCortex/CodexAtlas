# Telipinu

## Identity
Telipinu is the Hittite vanishing-vegetation god of the disappearing-and-returning type. The *Telipinu Myth* (CTH 324) is the most fully-preserved Hittite mythological narrative of this pattern: enraged for unspecified reasons, Telipinu storms off into the steppe, and his disappearance brings catastrophe — grain withers, ewes refuse their lambs, cows refuse their calves, men and gods alike starve. The Storm-God his father, the Sun-God, and a fly-bee succession of envoys search for him; the bee finds him asleep in a meadow and stings him awake. He returns in worse anger; only the rituals of the goddess Kamrušepa (with cedar resin, fig, and olive) appease him. He returns to his temple, and fertility returns to the land.

## Earliest attestation
- Boğazköy archive Old Hittite / Middle Hittite texts (~-1600 to -1400); the myth is preserved in multiple recensions.
- Cult attested across the Hittite imperial period in royal ritual.

## Mythological role
- **The disappearing-god myth-pattern.** Telipinu is the textbook Anatolian instance of the type. His withdrawal causes cosmic infertility; his return restores it. The myth is recited in *purulli* / ritual contexts to ensure agricultural fertility.
- The Telipinu myth is one of *several* Hittite "vanishing-god" texts (similar texts exist for the Sun-God, the Storm-God of Nerik, and others), but Telipinu's is the most complete and most clearly tied to agricultural ritual.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[dumuzi-tammuz]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the descent-and-return vegetation-god pattern. Dumuzi descends to the underworld in Inanna's place, and his return is mourned/celebrated annually in the Tammuz cult (still attested in Ezekiel 8:14, where Jerusalem women weep for Tammuz). Telipinu's disappearance into the steppe is structurally cognate (Mazoyer 2003).
- **[[baal-hadad]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Ugaritic Baal descends into Mot's gullet and is later restored; same myth-type.
- **[[adonis-hellenistic]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Hellenistic Levantine descendant of the type.
- **Persephone / Demeter-Persephone cycle** — *scholarly-parallel*: a Greek seasonal-agricultural variant of the same myth-type, with the gender reversed.

## Iconography
Limited; Telipinu does not have a strong iconographic tradition; he is primarily known from text.

## Disputes
- **Whether Telipinu "dies" or merely "vanishes."** The Hittite text describes withdrawal-and-return, not death-and-resurrection in the strict sense. Calling him a "dying-and-rising god" is overstatement (Mettinger 2001 cautioned generally against the type-category, though the disappearing-god motif is real and distinct).
