# Kalki

## Identity
Kalki is the tenth and final avatar (*avatāra*, "descent") of [[vishnu]] — the divine warrior who has not yet come but is prophesied to appear at the nadir of the Kali Yuga (the current age of spiritual degeneration, traditionally said to have begun in 3102 BCE with the death of Krishna) to destroy the wicked, end the current cosmic cycle, and restore *dharma* before the world dissolves in *mahāpralaya* (great dissolution) and a new Satya Yuga (golden age) begins.

Unlike the nine previous avatars — Matsya (fish), Kūrma (tortoise), Varāha (boar), Narasiṃha (man-lion), Vāmana (dwarf), Paraśurāma, Rāma, [[krishna]], and the Buddha (included in most lists) — Kalki has **not yet incarnated**. He is the avatar-to-come, whose arrival marks the absolute end of the present world-order.

## Mythological description
The *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* (12.2) and *Vishnu Purāṇa* (4.24) describe Kalki:
- He will be born in the village of Sambhala to a Brahmin named Viṣṇuyaśas
- He will ride a **white horse named Devadatta** (a name also associated with one of the Buddha's horses and with a conch)
- He will wield a **blazing sword** (or comet-like weapon)
- He will travel across the earth with the speed of wind, **destroying the wicked** — specifically the barbarians (*mlecchas*) and false kings who have made the world ungovernable
- After the destruction, a new Satya Yuga will begin

The *Kalki Purāṇa* (later, ~10th–12th c. CE) elaborates this into a full narrative with Kalki's birth, education, marriage to Padmā, military campaigns, and eschatological battles.

## Position in the Yuga cycle
The Yuga cycle (*mahāyuga*) runs: Satya Yuga (1,728,000 years) → Tretā Yuga (1,296,000 years) → Dvāpara Yuga (864,000 years) → Kali Yuga (432,000 years). We are in the Kali Yuga. Kalki arrives at its end to close the cycle. This cosmological framework distinguishes the Hindu eschatological imagination from the linear Zoroastrian/Jewish-Christian model: the end is not **final** but **cyclical** — after destruction comes a new golden age, which in turn degenerates through the same pattern.

## Buddha as ninth avatar — the polemic inversion
Most lists include the Buddha as the ninth avatar of Vishnu (before Kalki). The theological function of this inclusion is contested:
1. **Absorptive syncretism**: incorporating Buddhism into the Vaishnava framework as a heterodox but divinely sanctioned path — "the Buddha teaches non-injury to animals, which is what Vishnu intended for this age."
2. **Polemic inversion**: the Buddha deliberately taught false doctrine to lead demons and the wicked astray — a *deliberate deception* by Vishnu to undermine the power of enemies of dharma.
The ambiguity between these two readings is characteristic of how dominant traditions process the presence of successful rivals.

## Messianic-future-savior pattern
Kalki is the Hindu member of the **[[messianic-future-savior]]** cross-tradition cluster: alongside [[maitreya]] (Buddhist), [[saoshyant]] (Zoroastrian), the Christian Second Coming of [[jesus-christ-deity]], and [[muhammad-al-mahdi]] (Islamic), Kalki represents the Hindu version of the universal eschatological expectation of a cosmic savior arriving at the world's worst moment.

The distinctive Hindu contribution to this pattern is the **cyclical** framing: Kalki does not end history permanently but closes the current mahāyuga so the cosmic cycle can begin again from the Satya Yuga. This is fundamentally different from the linear eschatologies of Zoroastrianism, Christianity, and Islam, where the end is unrepeatable.
