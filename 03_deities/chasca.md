# Chasca

Chasca (Quechua *Ch'aska*, "shaggy / radiant," as of a star's streaming rays) is the Inca deity of the planet **Venus** — the brilliant star of **dawn and dusk** (*Ch'aska Quyllur*). In the celestial hierarchy reported by the Spanish chroniclers (Cobo, Garcilaso), Chasca is a **page or attendant** of **[[inti]]** the Sun and **[[mama-quilla]]** the Moon, going before them at daybreak and nightfall as their herald — the bright "star of dawn" that announces the sun's rising. She was associated with the freshness of dawn, with **dew**, with flowers, and with the protection of **maidens and the young**. As one of the principal *quyllur* (stars) in the Inca sky-religion — which mapped a whole pantheon onto the constellations and the dark cloud-shapes of the Milky Way — Chasca is the Andean counterpart of the Venus-deities found across the Americas and the wider world, and her absence left a conspicuous hole in the vault's Inca sky.
