# Olokun

## Identity
Olokun ("Owner of the Sea/Ocean Floor") is the Yoruba orisha of the deep ocean — specifically the unreachable depths, not the surface. Where [[yemoja]] governs rivers and the surface of the sea (accessible, life-giving, maternal), Olokun governs what lies at the bottom: unfathomable wealth, the dead who drowned, and the mystery that cannot be directly known.

Olokun's most distinctive theological attribute is **inaccessibility**: the ocean floor cannot be seen or reached. This makes Olokun the Yoruba deity who most directly embodies the *divine apophatic* — the aspect of the sacred that exceeds human comprehension. In some theological accounts, Olokun is considered to *be* the unfathomable nature of Olodumare (the supreme deity) made manifest in the natural world.

## Gender ambiguity
Olokun's gender is one of the most variable in the Yoruba pantheon:
- In **Benin (Edo)** tradition: male; the Oba of Benin's power derives partly from Olokun
- In **some Yoruba** traditions: female
- In **diaspora (Lucumí/Cuba, Candomblé/Brazil)**: often androgynous or of ambiguous gender, sometimes described as a chained deity whose full power would destroy the world if released

This variation reflects how widely Olokun's cult spread and how different communities adapted the deity to their own theological frameworks. The gender ambiguity itself may encode Olokun's theological function: that which is beyond ordinary categories.

## The chained deity
One of the most striking elements of Olokun mythology, particularly in diaspora traditions: Olokun is described as **chained** at the bottom of the sea, because their full power unconstrained would overwhelm the world. [[olodumare]] bound them there. This mythology — a deity of immense power held under constraint — parallels cross-cultural patterns of the dangerous divine (cf. [[fenrir]] bound by the gods, the Titans in Tartarus).
