# Ba Xian (Eight Immortals)

The **Eight Immortals** — the most popular Daoist deity-collective in Chinese folk religion. Eight semi-historical figures (mostly Tang and Song dynasty) who attained Daoist immortality (*xian*) through different paths and now traverse the cosmos together, often depicted on a boat "crossing the sea" (*Ba Xian Guo Hai* — "The Eight Immortals Crossing the Sea," the foundational narrative).

**Each Immortal represents a different social condition** — a deliberate sociological-theological architecture:
- **Lü Dongbin** (吕洞宾) — the scholar; carries a magical sword
- **Zhongli Quan** (钟离权) — the general/military-man; carries a fan that revives the dead
- **Tieguai Li** ("Iron-Crutch Li", 铁拐李) — the beggar/disabled; iron crutch and medicine-gourd
- **He Xiangu** (何仙姑) — the woman (the only unambiguous female); a lotus-flower
- **Lan Caihe** (蓝采和) — gender-ambiguous; carries a basket of flowers
- **Han Xiangzi** (韩湘子) — the youth/musician; plays a magical flute
- **Cao Guojiu** (曹国舅) — the noble (brother to an Empress); castanets
- **Zhang Guolao** (张果老) — the elderly/sage; rides a paper donkey

The architecture is unique in world religion: an **immortal-collective whose composition deliberately maps the social spectrum** (scholar/general/disabled/woman/youth/noble/elder/gender-fluid), modelling that Daoist immortality is achievable from any social position. The Ba Xian crystallized as a fixed grouping in the **Yuan dynasty** (13th-14th century) from earlier independent immortal-cults; their continuing pan-Chinese popularity makes them the most-frequently-depicted deity group in Chinese folk-religious art.
