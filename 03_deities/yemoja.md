# Yemọja

## Identity
Yoruba orisha of the Ogun River (in West Africa) and of motherhood, fertility, and protection of children. In the diaspora — especially Cuban Lucumí and Brazilian Candomblé — Yemọja is reconfigured as the orisha of the *sea* rather than rivers, the change reflecting the Middle Passage experience and the centrality of ocean-coastal life in Cuba and Brazil. Yemayá in Cuba and Iemanjá in Brazil are among the most popular and publicly visible orishas; Iemanjá's New Year's Eve festivities at Rio de Janeiro's beaches draw millions.

## Earliest attestation
Cult on the Ogun River well-documented in 19th- and 20th-c. ethnography; transmission to Cuba and Brazil documented from 18th c. onward.

## Mythological role
In one Ifá narrative, Yemọja is the mother of fourteen orishas, including Ṣàngó, Ọya, and Ọ̀ṣun; she gave birth to them and to the cosmos's waters. In other lineages she is one orisha among siblings. Her motherly aspect is constant.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Our Lady of Regla (Virgen de Regla)** — *folk-syncretism* (Cuban Lucumí): the dark-skinned Marian patroness of Regla (the harbor town across the bay from Havana) pairs with Yemayá; both are protectors of sailors and of the harbor.
- **Mami Wata** — *scholarly-parallel*: broader West-African and African-diasporic water-spirit complex; Mami Wata is a related but distinct figure (often associated with European-influenced mermaid iconography), and the relationship is one of family-resemblance, not identity. Ref: Drewal 2008.

## Iconography
Blue-and-white cloth (Cuban / Brazilian); fish; anchor; mirror-fan; her sacred number is seven.
