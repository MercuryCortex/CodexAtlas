# Mahāsthāmaprāpta

Mahāsthāmaprāpta ("He who has Attained Great Strength"; Chinese **Dàshìzhì**, Japanese **Seishi**) is a great **bodhisattva of Mahāyāna Buddhism**, embodying the **power of wisdom** by which beings are drawn out of suffering. He is best known as one of the **two principal attendants of the Buddha [[amitabha|Amitābha]]** in the Western Pure Land of **Sukhāvatī**: together they form the **"Amitābha triad,"** with Mahāsthāmaprāpta standing on one side of the Buddha (representing **wisdom/power**) and **[[avalokitesvara|Avalokiteśvara]]** on the other (representing **compassion**). In the *Sūraṅgama Sūtra* he gives a celebrated teaching on attaining awakening through the unbroken **recollection of the Buddha** (the practice that, in East Asian Pure Land, becomes the *nianfo/nembutsu*), which made him a patron of that method. His identifying emblem is the small **water-jar** in his crown. Though less independently popular than his compassionate counterpart, Mahāsthāmaprāpta is a constant presence in Pure Land art and devotion as the wisdom-power at Amitābha's side.
