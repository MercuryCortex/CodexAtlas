# Yaghūth

The pre-Islamic deity of the **Madhḥij tribal confederation** (northern Yemen and adjacent inner Arabia) — one of the five idols named in **Quran 71:23** as the deities of Noah's people that the pre-Islamic Arabs inherited and worshipped. The name *Yaghūth* in Arabic means *"He Who Helps"* — a theophoric form preserved in the personal name *ʿAbd Yaghūth* ("servant of Yaghūth"), attested in pre-Islamic poetry and in early Islamic genealogical records.

Ibn al-Kalbi's *Kitāb al-Aṣnām* describes the idol as **lion-formed** — connecting Yaghūth iconographically with the South Arabian lion-cult attested in Sabaean and Ḥimyaritic art (the protective lion at temple gates, parallel to the [[lamassu]] and [[wadjet]]-uraeus apotropaic-threshold complex).

See [[suwa]] for the broader Quran 71:23 antediluvian-pantheon framing; this set of five deities is the Quranic-anchored category of pre-Islamic Arabian idolatry.
