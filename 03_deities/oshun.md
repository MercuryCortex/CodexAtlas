# Ọ̀ṣun

## Identity
Yoruba orisha of the Oshun River (which flows through the sacred grove at Osogbo, Nigeria), fresh water, love, fertility, wealth, beauty, and sweetness. Òṣun is one of the most popular and most-petitioned orishas in both African and diasporic Yoruba religion. She is associated with sweetness in all dimensions — honey, sweet water, sweet speech, sweet wealth — but her displeasure brings drought and barrenness.

## Earliest attestation
Cult at Osogbo, Nigeria, well-attested in 19th-c. European sources; archaeological-grove material at Oshogbo Sacred Grove (UNESCO World Heritage Site 2005); Ifá corpus references throughout.

## Mythological role
In the founding narrative of Osogbo, Òṣun made a covenant with the people of Osogbo: she would protect their city from war and bring fertility if they honored her annually with the Òṣun-Osogbo festival, which continues. In another narrative, when the orishas first descended to earth and the male orishas excluded her from cosmic-administration councils, the world fell into infertility and chaos until they restored her authority — a Yoruba theology of feminine indispensability. She is variously consort of Shàngó and rival of his other wives (Oyá, Obá).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Our Lady of Charity of El Cobre** — *folk-syncretism*: La Virgen de la Caridad del Cobre is the patroness of Cuba; her shrine at El Cobre is national. The yellow-robed Marian image matches Òṣun's gold-yellow iconography; the historical Caridad legend (three sailors saved at sea) was assimilated into Òṣun-Yemayá water-protection schema in Cuban Lucumí.
- **Oxum (Brazilian Candomblé)** — *manuscript-transmission*: the same orisha in Bahian liturgy.

## Iconography
Yellow cloth and gold/brass adornment; river-water; honey; mirrors (vanity attribute); peacock fan; five cowries or five-anything (her sacred number).
