# Mummu

## Identity

Mummu is a primordial being of Babylonian cosmogony, known chiefly from the opening of the *Enuma Elish*, where he is the vizier (*sukkal*) and counselor of [[apsu]], the personified primordial fresh water. He is the third member of the pre-creation trio that exists before all other gods: [[apsu]] (the fresh waters), [[tiamat]] (the salt sea), and Mummu between them. His name carries a famously contested set of meanings — *mummu* can denote "mold" or "form," the active "creative life-force," or "mist / tumult / noise" — and this ambiguity has made Mummu a focus of scholarly attention out of proportion to his small narrative role.

## Mythological role

In the *Enuma Elish*, the younger gods born within Apsu and Tiamat are noisy and disturb Apsu's rest. Apsu resolves to destroy them; Tiamat objects, but Mummu encourages Apsu's plan, and the two go together to confront her. The wise god Ea ([[enki-ea]]) learns of the plot, casts a sleep-spell on Apsu, kills him, and establishes his own dwelling upon him — and in the same act seizes Mummu, binds him with a nose-rope, and takes away his *mummu* (often rendered as his radiance, crown, or vital force). Mummu is thus subdued together with his master at the very beginning of the cosmic struggle that culminates in Marduk's defeat of Tiamat and the creation of the world.

## Significance

Mummu's interest lies less in his deeds than in his name and his structural position. As the counselor who personifies "form" or "the creative force" attending the primordial waters, he has been read by some interpreters as a personification of the organizing principle latent in the pre-creation chaos — a faint Mesopotamian analogue to later notions of a creative *logos* or formative power within the abyss. Within the Atlas he completes the primordial trio that stands at the head of the *Enuma Elish*, the text behind the Babylonian end of the "primordial waters / the deep" cross-tradition cluster ([[tiamat]] · [[nun]] · the *tehom* of Genesis 1:2).

## Disputes

The meaning of *mummu* is genuinely unsettled: Akkadian lexicography supports several readings, and translators differ on whether to treat it as a proper name, an epithet, or a common noun in the relevant lines. Whether Mummu is a fully distinct deity or a near-personification of an abstract quality of Apsu is correspondingly debated.
