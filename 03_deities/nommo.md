# Nommo

## Identity
The Nommo (singular and plural) are the eight primordial paired ancestors created by [[amma-dogon]] through his successful union with Earth. They are four male-female pairs (eight beings total), amphibious fish-like beings with green skin, forked tongues, and a capacity for speech. The Nommo inhabit both the primordial sky-waters and the terrestrial waters (rivers, ponds); they are the weavers of cosmic order, the givers of speech to humanity, and the prototypes of all living things. They descend to Earth in an ark (the "granary of the master of pure earth"), bringing the seeds of civilization. In Dogon cosmology, the Nommo are the successful completion of Amma's creation — the contrast to the chaos generated by the Pale Fox (Ogo). The primary sources are Griaule's *Dieu d'eau* (1948) and the fuller elaboration in *Le Renard Pâle* (1965).

## Earliest attestation
Like all Dogon theological cosmology, the Nommo are attested through 20th-century ethnographic fieldwork by Marcel Griaule and Germaine Dieterlen (1931 onward). There is no pre-colonial textual documentation. The cosmological elaboration of the Nommo as eight paired primordials is especially prominent in *Le Renard Pâle* (1965). Van Beek's 1991 re-study found that ordinary Dogon informants had much less detailed knowledge of the Nommo cosmology than Griaule's sources; the full elaborated system is best understood as initiatory/esoteric knowledge among specific lineages.

## Mythological role
The Nommo's key roles:
1. **Products of the successful creation**: After the chaos of Ogo/Pale Fox, Amma's corrected union with Earth produces the Nommo — eight perfect paired beings representing completeness (4 male + 4 female = 8 total).
2. **Cosmic weavers**: The Nommo weave the fabric of the universe using speech — each word/utterance creates a thread; language itself is weaving. The weaving loom is a Nommo-gift.
3. **Givers of speech**: The Nommo descend from the sky in an ark carrying the seeds of agriculture and civilization; they breathe speech into the first ancestors, activating human language.
4. **Water dwellers**: The Nommo inhabit rivers and sky-waters; they are propitiated at water sources. Dogon blacksmiths, weavers, and griots each have a patron Nommo.
5. **Cosmic sacrifice**: One Nommo is sacrificed and dismembered to purify the Earth after Ogo's contamination; the body parts become the altars and sacred geography of Dogon territory.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[apkallu]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: The Mesopotamian Apkallu are seven fish-sages (part-human, part-fish, or fish-shaped) sent by Enki/Ea from the primordial waters (*Abzu*) before the flood to teach humanity the arts of civilization. The structural parallel to the Nommo — primordial aquatic beings who bring civilization, speech, and order — is among the most striking cross-cultural typological matches in comparative mythology. Numerological difference: Apkallu = 7; Nommo = 8.
- **[[matsya-avatar]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Vishnu's fish avatar (Matsya) saves Manu/humanity from the primordial flood and is the archetype of the fish-deity-as-civilizational-savior. The Nommo's role as fish-beings who bring civilization parallels this pattern.
- **[[nun]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: The Egyptian primordial waters (Nun) and the Nommo's home in the primordial ocean share the theme of cosmic water as the matrix of creation and the dwelling of primordial beings.
- **[[amaunet]]** and the Hermopolitan Ogdoad — *scholarly-parallel*: The Egyptian Ogdoad is eight primordial forces in four male-female pairs (same structure as the Nommo: 4 pairs = 8). The Ogdoad includes [[kek-egyptian]] (darkness), [[nun]] (water), Amaunet (hiddenness), Huh (eternity). The structural identity — eight paired primordials constituting the complete matrix from which ordered creation emerges — is remarkable.
- **[[enki-ea]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Enki dwells in the Abzu (primordial underground freshwater ocean) and is the source of wisdom/civilization through watery wisdom, as the Nommo dwell in primordial waters and bring civilizational gifts.
- **[[apsu]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Apsu as the primordial freshwater ocean in Mesopotamian cosmogony shares the typological position of primordial-waters-as-creative-matrix with the Nommo's watery origin.

## The Sirius Controversy (Tier 3-4)
Robert Temple's *The Sirius Mystery* (1976) claimed that the Nommo cosmology encodes precise astronomical knowledge of Sirius B (a white dwarf star, companion to Sirius A, invisible to the naked eye without telescopes), including its orbital period (~50 years) and mass density. Temple argued this knowledge was transmitted to the Dogon from ancient Egypt. **Assessment**: This claim is classified Tier 3-4 (speculative/unsupported). Astronomers (Carl Sagan, Ian Ridpath) and anthropologists (van Beek) have noted: (1) Griaule's fieldwork preceded Temple's book by decades and the specific Sirius claims are not independently verified; (2) van Beek's re-study found no widespread Dogon knowledge of Sirius B's properties; (3) statistical contamination from European contact cannot be excluded. The hypothesis is historically interesting as an example of ancient-astronaut pseudoscience but has no evidential support meeting Tier 1-2 standards.

## Iconography
The Nommo are depicted in Dogon wooden sculpture as elongated, upright fish-like humanoid figures, often androgynous or with doubled features (male/female on the same figure). The Kanaga mask (used in Dama mortuary rites) encodes the Nommo's descent and ascent. The weaving shuttle is a Nommo attribute. Granary doors and shutters are carved with paired Nommo figures flanking the central axis.
