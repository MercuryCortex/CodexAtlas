# Tlaltecuhtli

Tlaltecuhtli (Nahuatl *Tlāltēcuhtli*, "Earth Lord") is the Aztec **earth deity** — and, in the creation account, the primordial **earth-monster** from whose torn body the world is made. In the myth, a vast toothed monster floats in the primeval waters, hungry for flesh; **[[quetzalcoatl]]** and Tezcatlipoca, transformed into serpents, **seize her and rip her in two**, one half forming the **earth** and the other lifted up to make the **sky**. To console the dismembered goddess, the other gods decree that everything humans need shall come from her body: her hair becomes trees and grasses, her skin the meadows, her eyes the springs and caves, her mouth the rivers, her shoulders the mountains. But she remains hungry: she has gnashing mouths at her elbows, knees, and joints, and she **demands human hearts and blood** and **swallows the dead** (and the sun each night), giving Aztec sacrifice its cosmological logic — the earth must be fed to keep yielding life. She is most spectacularly known from the colossal circular **Tlaltecuhtli monolith** unearthed at the Templo Mayor in 2006, one of the masterpieces of Aztec sculpture.
