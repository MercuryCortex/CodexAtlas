# Avalokiteśvara

## Identity
Avalokiteśvara — "the Lord who looks down (with compassion)" — is the great Bodhisattva of *karuṇā* (compassion) in Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna Buddhism. Across Asia he is the most widely invoked bodhisattva and the closest analog in any Asian tradition to a *universal* saviour-figure. His central role is universal compassionate intervention: in Mahāyāna sūtra rhetoric (Lotus Sūtra Ch. 25), the suffering being who merely calls his name with single-pointed devotion is saved from fire, water, demons, prison, and the dangers of childbirth — a soteriology of *grace* not unlike Pure Land *tariki* or Hindu *prapatti* (cf. [[bhakti-devotion]]). Iconographically: lotus-bearing (*Padmapāṇi*), often multi-armed in tantric forms (the famous *Sahasrabhuja* / thousand-armed form), with the small image of [[amitabha]] in his crown identifying him.

The deity travels: Indian Mahāyāna Avalokiteśvara → Tibetan *Chenrezig* (with the Dalai Lama as his living incarnation) → Chinese *Guanyin* / Kannon (progressively feminized from ~10th c. CE, decisively female by Ming period — see [[guanyin]] and Yü 2001) → Japanese Kannon → Vietnamese Quan Âm. The gender transformation in East Asia is one of the most studied cases of cross-cultural deity-modification in religious history.

## Earliest attestation
First appearances in early Mahāyāna sūtra literature (~-100 to +200 CE): the *Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā* (where he plays a minor role), then the *Saddharmapuṇḍarīka* (Lotus Sūtra, ~+100, Ch. 25 *Samantamukha* "Universal Gateway" — the *locus classicus*). The *Kāraṇḍavyūha Sūtra* (~+400) establishes his fully cosmic-saviour identity and gives the famous mantra *Oṁ Maṇi Padme Hūṁ*. Earliest iconography: Gandhāran (~+200 to +500).

## Manifestations in vault documents
- **Heart Sūtra:** [[phase-5-002-heart-sutra]] — Avalokiteśvara is the speaker, teaching Śāriputra that "form is emptiness, emptiness is form." The most-recited Mahāyāna text, with Avalokiteśvara as its prajñāpāramitā mouthpiece.
- **Tibetan Book of the Dead:** [[phase-5-029-bardo-thodol]] — Avalokiteśvara appears among the peaceful deities in the *bardo* visions.
- **Pure Land:** Amitābha's cosmic emanation; in Pure Land mortuary practice, the dying devotee is greeted by Amitābha attended by Avalokiteśvara and Mahāsthāmaprāpta.
