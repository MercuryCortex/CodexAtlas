# Lilith

## Identity
Lilith is the feminine demon of Jewish tradition, with deep Mesopotamian roots, marginal canonical Hebrew Bible presence (a single mention in Isaiah 34:14 as a desert demon), and explosive medieval development. The 8th–10th-c. *Alphabet of Ben Sira* — a satirical-folkloric Hebrew text — gave Lilith her famous biography: Lilith was Adam's first wife, created (like Adam) from earth, and she refused to lie beneath him in sexual relations, claiming equality; pronouncing the ineffable Name, she flew off to the Red Sea; God sent three angels (Senoy, Sansenoy, Semangelof) to retrieve her; she refused to return; she became a demoness of the night, killer of infants (especially in the eight days before circumcision), and a seductress causing nocturnal emissions in men. In Kabbalah, particularly the [[phase-5-027-sefer-ha-zohar]], Lilith becomes the demonic counterpart of Shekhinah — the dark feminine within the *sitra achra* (the Other Side / left-emanation evil counterpart to the sefirotic tree); during the Temple's destruction, Shekhinah goes into exile, and the bed of God (Tiferet) is taken over by Lilith. Modern Jewish feminism (Judith Plaskow 1972 onward) has substantially recuperated Lilith as a symbol of feminine independence.

## Earliest attestation
Akkadian *lilītu* / *lilû* — night demons — attested ~2000 BCE in cuneiform incantations. Hebrew *lilit* mentioned once in Isaiah 34:14 (~6th c. BCE) among the wild creatures inhabiting Edom's ruins. Hellenistic and rabbinic period: brief mentions in the Dead Sea Scrolls (4Q510, 4Q511) and the Babylonian Talmud. Medieval folkloric biography: *Alphabet of Ben Sira* (~8th–10th c.). Kabbalistic systematization: Zohar (~1280).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Akkadian Lilītu / Lamashtu** — *ancient-identification*: linguistic cognate; functional continuity as night demon and threat to infants.
- **Mary Magdalene** in some popular esoteric / Theosophical contexts — *folk-syncretism*: a 20th-c. New Age conflation without historical basis.
- **Hekate** in Greek tradition — *parallel-motif*: night-feminine-demonic role overlap; no causal link.

## Disputes
- **Adam-first-wife legend.** Origins of the *Alphabet of Ben Sira* narrative — likely satirical / folkloric rather than serious theology in its original context, but received as semi-canonical in medieval Jewish folklore and especially in Kabbalah.
- **Feminist reception.** Plaskow 1972's recuperation of Lilith as feminist symbol is contested by some traditionalist Jewish writers as anachronistic.
