# Nanshe

## Identity
Nanshe is the daughter of [[enki-ea]] and the goddess of social justice, dream-interpretation, divination, fishery, and weights-and-measures in the Sumerian pantheon. Her cult is centered in the Lagash state — primarily at Nigin (Tell Surghul) and at Sirara — and she is one of the three principal deities of Lagashite religion alongside [[nin-girsu]] (her brother in some traditions) and [[bau-sumerian]]. The Nanshe Hymn, edited by Heimpel (1981), portrays her as the divine patron of the widow, the orphan, the debtor, and the fair scale — a remarkably explicit articulation of social-justice theology for the early third millennium. She is also the principal Sumerian dream-interpreter; in [[phase-1-018-gudea-cylinders]] it is to Nanshe that Gudea travels to receive interpretation of his ambiguous temple-construction dream.

## Earliest attestation
- Early Dynastic god lists from Fara/Shuruppak and Abu Salabikh (~-2600).
- The Ur-Nanshe inscriptions from the First Dynasty of Lagash (~-2500) — Ur-Nanshe is named for her.
- The Reforms of Urukagina (~-2350) invoke Nanshe as patron of the just king's care for the poor.
- The Kesh Temple Hymn ([[phase-1-001-kesh-temple-hymn]]) names her among the great gods.
- The Nanshe Hymn — composed Ur III or Old Babylonian, surviving in OB copies — is the most extensive single source.

## Mythological role
- *The Nanshe Hymn*: extended description of her annual festival at Nigin, where the people gather, the granaries are inspected, weights and measures are checked, and Nanshe judges the conduct of officials and ordinary people through the year. The hymn enumerates wrongs — false weights, taking advantage of the widow, mistreatment of the orphan — that Nanshe punishes.
- *Nanshe and the Birds*: a catalogue-style composition naming the waterfowl of the southern marshes and assigning each to Nanshe's service.
- In *Gudea Cylinder A* ([[phase-1-018-gudea-cylinders]]): Gudea travels to her at Nigin to have his temple-building dream interpreted. Her dream-interpretation function is fundamental to the legitimacy of the entire Eninnu temple project at Girsu.
- In some Lagashite traditions she is sister of Ningirsu and a sometime substitute-mother figure in the local theology.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- Nanshe is one of the more locally-specific Sumerian deities; her cult does not transfer cleanly to the Akkadian or later traditions. She appears in Akkadian-period god lists but with diminished autonomous profile.
- Some scholarly comparisons have been made with later Mesopotamian dream-interpretation traditions and with Egyptian Ma'at (justice), but neither rises above *scholarly-parallel*.

## Iconography
Seated goddess in horned crown; her distinguishing features in seal images are fish, waterfowl (the pelican is especially associated with her), and the measuring reed. The connection to fishery is consistent across the iconography.

## Disputes
- **Parentage**. Daughter of Enki is the dominant tradition; in some local Lagashite variants she is daughter of Enlil. The Enki affiliation is presumed earlier.
- **Genealogical relation to Ningirsu**. Sister in some Lagashite texts, daughter or niece in others; the variability reflects the layered Lagashite local theology.
