# Mastema (Prince of Evil Spirits)

## Identity
**Mastema** (Hebrew/Aramaic *Maśṭēmâ*, "animosity," "accusation," "hostility"; functioning as both *common noun* and *proper name* across Jubilees) is the **chief of evil spirits** in the **Book of Jubilees** (composed Hebrew, ~150 BCE; canonical scripture only in [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]] — see [[phase-3-009-jubilees]]). He functions as a *bureaucratic-administrative* cosmic adversary: an angelic-rank figure who *requests permission* from God to retain a tenth of the post-Flood evil spirits to "exercise the authority of [his] will" (Jubilees 10:8–9). God grants this and the Mastema-led tenth becomes the operative force of testing-and-temptation in human history.

This is theologically distinctive: Mastema is *not* a metaphysically-dualist co-eternal evil principle (as in Zoroastrian Ahriman or Manichaean dualism). He is a subordinated angelic-rank antagonist who operates *within* the divine cosmic-administrative order, with God's permission, for purposes of testing the righteous. The pattern is structurally close to "the satan" of Job 1–2 and Zechariah 3 (the *accuser* member of the divine council) but more elaborated, more personalized, and more sustained as a narrative agent across Jubilees's retelling of biblical history.

In the Ethiopian Orthodox canonical reception, Mastema is a known and named figure of Old Testament theology in a way he is not in any other Christian tradition.

## Earliest attestation
- **Jubilees 10:7–14** — God commands Mastema and the post-Flood evil spirits to be bound; Mastema requests permission to retain 10% for testing-purposes; God grants this and the rest are bound.
- **Jubilees 11:5, 11** — Mastema-spirits cause idolatry and warfare in the post-Flood era.
- **Jubilees 17:15–16; 18:1–12** — **the Aqedah / Binding-of-Isaac is reframed**: it is *Mastema* who suggests to God that Abraham be tested with the sacrifice of Isaac (functionally identical to Job 1–2's "the satan" suggesting Job be tested). This is one of the most theologically consequential reframings in Jubilees.
- **Jubilees 19:28** — Mastema is named as the spirit Jacob asks God to keep away from him.
- **Jubilees 48:1–19** — **the entire Egyptian-plagues-and-Exodus narrative is reframed as Mastema-vs-Moses**: Mastema tries to kill Moses on the way to Egypt (Exodus 4:24 reframed); Mastema empowers the Egyptian magicians to imitate Moses's signs; Mastema is restrained while Israel passes through the Red Sea.
- **Qumran 4Q225 (Pseudo-Jubilees)** — Aramaic recension of Jubilees material, including Aqedah-with-Mastema.
- **Damascus Document (CD-A 16:5)** — sectarian Qumran reference to "the Prince of Mastema."
- **Ge'ez Mashafa Kufale** (canonical Jubilees in [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]]) — the only complete textual witness to the full corpus.

## Mythological role
Mastema's theological function is the **cosmic-administrative locus of moral testing and supernatural evil-agency**. The figure does three things across Jubilees:

1. **He proposes and instigates the testing of the righteous** (most consequentially the Aqedah of Abraham/Isaac). The theological move here is to *shift the agency of testing* off God himself onto a subordinate antagonist — a move that parallels Job 1–2 and that is theologically central for theodicy: God does not personally test his people; an administrative-evil-agent does, with permission.

2. **He empowers competing supernatural agents** — most strikingly the Egyptian magicians at Pharaoh's court (Jubilees 48), who imitate Moses's signs because Mastema empowers them. This makes Mastema the origin-of-magic in the Egyptian-magical-tradition, paralleling the Watcher-origins of magic in 1 Enoch 8.

3. **He governs the post-Flood demonic remnant** — the surviving 10% of the antediluvian evil spirits, descended from the Watchers via the Nephilim, retained for testing-of-humanity purposes.

The Mastema figure is theologically pivotal because it stabilizes a *cosmic-administrative* model of evil that occupies the middle ground between:
- the diffuse "the satan / accuser" of Job 1–2 and Zechariah 3 (a generic adversary-role within the divine council),
- the developed *individuated* Satan of late-Second-Temple and Christian theology (Revelation 12; later patristic),
- and the metaphysically-dualist Ahriman/Angra Mainyu of Zoroastrianism (a co-eternal evil principle).

Mastema is one of the most-individuated pre-Christian Jewish satanic-adversary figures; his role in the Aqedah, in the Exodus, and as the cosmic governor of demons is more sustained than Job's "the satan" and more constrained than Christian Satan.

## Cross-tradition identifications

- **[[satan-christian]]** — *scholarly-parallel*, *partial-equivalence*. Mastema is one of the load-bearing pre-Christian Jewish developments of the satanic adversary-figure. The Christian-mainstream Satan absorbs Mastema's functions (instigator of testing; governor of demons; cosmic adversary) under a different name.
- **[[azazel]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Mastema and Azazel are parallel cosmic-evil-leader figures in distinct Second-Temple Jewish texts (Jubilees vs. 1 Enoch). They are not identified in either text but are functionally cognate.
- **Belial / Beliar** (Qumran sectarian) — *scholarly-parallel*: the Qumran *Community Rule* (1QS), *War Scroll* (1QM), and other sectarian texts develop Belial as cosmic adversary; structurally close to Mastema (Stuckenbruck 2014).
- **Iblis** ([[tradition-islam]]) — *parallel-form*: the Quranic Iblis is structurally cognate (a subordinated angelic-rank adversary).
- **Ahriman** (Zoroastrian) — *parallel-form*, *distinct-metaphysics*: Mastema is administratively-subordinated; Ahriman is cosmologically-dualist co-eternal. The structural parallel is recognized but the metaphysics differ.

## Iconography
Not iconographically standardized in Ethiopian Orthodox art (where demonic figures generally are not iconographically elaborated).

## Disputes

- **Mastema as proper name vs. common noun.** Across Jubilees the term *maśṭēmâ* sometimes appears as a generic noun ("animosity") and sometimes as a proper name ("the Prince of Mastema" or simply "Mastema"). The boundary is fuzzy; VanderKam 2001 treats it case-by-case.
- **Relationship to Satan in later Jewish-Christian tradition.** Whether the developed Christian Satan is *direct development* from Mastema or *parallel development* from a common Job-and-Zechariah substrate. Most scholarship treats Mastema as one of multiple lineages feeding into the consolidated Christian Satan-figure.
- **Independent of the Watchers.** Mastema operates separately from the Watchers (who are bound before he requests his 10% retention). The relationship is that Mastema *inherits* the post-Watchers evil-spirit-population, not that he *is* one of them.
