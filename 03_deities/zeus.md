# Zeus

## Identity
Zeus is the king of the Greek Olympian pantheon: sky-god, storm-god, lord of thunder, dispenser of justice, sovereign whose nod (the Homeric *kataneuō*) shakes Olympus. Homer's standard epithet for him is *patēr andrōn te theōn te* ("father of gods and men"). He is depicted as enthroned on Mount Olympus surrounded by the gods, wielding the thunderbolt forged by the Cyclopes, attended by his eagle. The Hesiodic succession myth narrates his rise: youngest son of Kronos and Rhea, hidden in Crete, returning to overthrow his father, defeating the Titans and Typhoeus to establish cosmic kingship.

Zeus is one of the most securely reconstructable Proto-Indo-European deities: PIE *Dyēus-pətēr ("Sky Father") yields Greek Zeus (vocative Zeu pater), Vedic Dyauṣ Pitṛ, Latin Iūpiter / Diespiter, Illyrian Deipaturos, possibly Germanic Tīwaz. The Mycenaean Linear B tablets (~-1400 BCE) record *Diwo* (di-we) and *Diwija* (di-wi-ja, feminine), giving Zeus and an early consort attested centuries before Homer. The Roman *interpretatio* identified him with Jupiter from earliest contact. Cross-tradition parallels with Vedic Indra (Indo-European dragon-slayer formula, Watkins 1995) and structural parallels with Hittite Teshub and Mesopotamian Marduk (storm-warrior cosmic king) place Zeus within a broad Eastern Mediterranean / Indo-European sovereign-storm-god tradition.
