# Ixtab

## Identity

Ixtab ("Rope Woman") is the Maya goddess of **suicide** — specifically of death by **hanging** — and the divine guide who conducts such dead to a place of rest. She is among the most vivid figures of the Yucatec Maya death-pantheon, known especially from Bishop Diego de Landa's sixteenth-century account and from the Maya codices.

## Mythological role

The Maya, Landa reports, held that those who **hanged themselves** went not to the dreaded underworld but to a paradise — a shady, restful place beneath the world-tree, where they were received by Ixtab. Suicide by hanging was thus, for the Maya, an honorable and even blessed death in certain circumstances (as were death in childbirth, in battle, or as a sacrifice). Ixtab is depicted in the codices **hanging from the sky by a rope** looped around her neck, her eyes closed and a black circle of decay on her cheek marking her as a corpse — yet she is a benevolent psychopomp, not a figure of horror. She represents the Mesoamerican conviction that the *manner* of one's death determined one's fate in the afterlife far more than the conduct of one's life.

## Cross-tradition significance

Ixtab reflects the distinctive Mesoamerican **theology of death by category** — the belief that *how* a person dies (in battle, in childbirth, by sacrifice, by hanging) routes them to a particular afterlife, rather than a moral judgment on their deeds. She belongs to the rich Maya death-and-underworld complex (with [[ah-puch]] and the lords of Xibalba), and as a death-goddess parallels the Aztec [[mictecacihuatl]]. Her benevolent reception of suicides is a striking counterpoint to the condemnation of suicide in many other traditions.
