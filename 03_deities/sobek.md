# Sobek

## Identity
Sobek (Egyptian *Sbk*; Greek *Souchos*) is the **crocodile-god of Egypt**, embodying both the lethal threat and the fertility-bringing power of the Nile crocodile. His major cult-centers are the **Fayum oasis** (where Crocodilopolis / Shedet keeps tame crocodiles in temple pools — described in detail by [[herodotus]] *Histories* 2.68–70 and [[diodorus-siculus]] 1.89) and **Kom Ombo** in Upper Egypt (where the surviving Ptolemaic-Roman temple is uniquely double-dedicated to Sobek and Horus the Elder, with parallel mirror-image sanctuaries). He represents one of the most distinctive Egyptian deity-types — a god of **terrifying primal animal power** harnessed into the protective service of Pharaonic order.

## Earliest attestation
- Mentioned in the [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] (Old Kingdom) — Sobek is invoked to assist the dead king in his afterlife journey.
- Major prominence in the **Middle Kingdom** (especially 12th Dynasty, c. -1991 to -1786) — several pharaohs named Sobekneferu, Sobekhotep; Fayum cult-temple at Shedet greatly expanded.
- New Kingdom and Late Period: continues, especially in the Fayum.
- **Ptolemaic-Roman period**: massive cult expansion under the Ptolemies who patronized the Fayum cult lavishly (tame temple-crocodile worship described in extensive papyrological documentation, including the Tebtunis temple archives).
- The double Sobek-Horus temple at **Kom Ombo** (most surviving construction Ptolemaic and Roman) is one of the best-preserved Ptolemaic-Roman temples in Egypt.

## Mythological role
Sobek is principally a **cult-figure** rather than a narrative-figure: he has comparatively little story-mythology, and is invoked primarily for protection, fertility, and warding off Nile crocodiles' threat. In some Middle Kingdom traditions he is a **primordial creator** rising from the waters of [[nun]] — fitting his crocodile-on-Nile iconography. In the Pyramid Texts he is the king's powerful ally in the afterlife journey; in the Coffin Texts he is invoked against demons.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ra]]** — *ancient-identification* / *syncretic-fusion*: Middle Kingdom Sobek-Ra at the Fayum. The solar-crocodile composite represents the sun rising from the primordial waters.
- **[[horus]]** — *ancient-identification* (Kom Ombo): paired temple-dedication; theological identification with the kingly-warrior aspect.
- **No major Greek equivalent.** Herodotus and Diodorus describe the cult ethnographically but do not give Sobek a Greek divine equivalent — Greek religion lacks a parallel crocodile-god. Sobek's *Greek name* Souchos appears in Greek-language papyri from Ptolemaic-Roman Egypt as a transliteration, not a translation.

## Iconography
- **Crocodile head** on a human body, or **full crocodile body** (uniquely literal animal-iconography compared to most Egyptian gods)
- **Atef-crown** (twin plumes), **solar disc** on the head (especially in Sobek-Ra form), or simply a uraeus
- **Ankh and *was*-scepter**
- The Fayum temple-pools kept living crocodiles as cult-images, adorned with gold and gem-encrusted jewelry — described in detail by Herodotus and corroborated archaeologically by mummified gold-adorned crocodiles from the Fayum

## Disputes
- **Sobek's theological status.** Whether Sobek is a primordial creator or "just" a regional patron of crocodiles is contested: some Middle Kingdom hymns elevate him to creator-god, but most cult-practice treats him as a specialized patron deity rather than a high-god.
- **Greek-Egyptian reception.** Greek and Roman ethnographers (Herodotus, Strabo, Plutarch, Aelian) treat the Fayum crocodile-cult as the exemplar of "bizarre Egyptian animal worship" — the most extreme example of Egyptian theriomorphism. Whether this Greek polemical framing tells us anything reliable about Egyptian theology (as opposed to Greek ethnographic prejudice) is questioned by modern scholarship (Frankfurter 1998).
