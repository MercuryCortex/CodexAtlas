# Jade Emperor (Yuhuang)

## Identity
The supreme sovereign of the **celestial bureaucracy** of Daoism and Chinese folk religion — Heaven imagined as a vast civil administration mirroring the imperial state, with the Jade Emperor as the heavenly counterpart of the human emperor. Although Heaven (*Tian*) and the High Lord (*Shangdi*) are attested from Shang oracle bones (c. -1200), the personal anthropomorphic Jade Emperor crystallized under the **Song dynasty (960–1279)**, with formal imperial canonization by Emperor Zhenzong in 1012 as **"Highest Ruler of the August Heaven, Lord of the Golden Palace, Jade Emperor."**

## Mythological role
**Administrator of fate**: presides over the heavenly registry of births, deaths, and karmic accounts. **Lunar New Year audience**: each year the Kitchen God (Zao Jun) reports to the Jade Emperor on each household's conduct — the etiological frame for sending Zao Jun off with sweetened lips (sticky candy) to soften his report. **City Gods and Earth Gods** report up through the bureaucracy to him.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[shangdi]] / [[tian-heaven]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Jade Emperor is the personalized form of the earlier abstract High God / Heaven.
- **[[zeus]] / [[indra]] / [[odin]]** — *parallel-motif*: chief of a celestial assembly.
- **[[three-pure-ones]]** — Daoist supreme triad; the Jade Emperor's relationship to them varies by school.
