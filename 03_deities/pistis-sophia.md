# Pistis Sophia

## Identity
Pistis Sophia ("Faith-Wisdom") is the **fallen-and-redeemed wisdom-aeon** who gives her name to the 3rd–4th-century Coptic Gnostic treatise [[phase-4-009-pistis-sophia|*Pistis Sophia*]]. In the work's mythic drama she is an aeon of the divine fullness who, deceived by a counterfeit light, descends from her station, is robbed of her light-power by the archon-figures, and from the depths of Chaos utters thirteen Repentances. The risen Jesus, narrating to his disciples, recounts how he ascends and descends through the aeons to rescue her and restore her to her place. She is thus the principal narrative subject of one of the most elaborate surviving Gnostic cosmological texts.

## Earliest attestation
Pistis Sophia is named throughout the Askew Codex text *Pistis Sophia* (extant in a Coptic manuscript, the underlying Greek ~3rd c. CE) and appears in the cosmogony [[phase-4-009-pistis-sophia|On the Origin of the World]]'s circle of Gnostic figures, listed there alongside [[yaldabaoth]], [[sophia-gnostic|Sophia]], and [[the-pleroma|the Pleroma]] — which indicates the texts could distinguish "Pistis Sophia" as a named figure within the broader Sophia mythology.

## Context
The fall of Sophia is the engine of the Gnostic cosmogony: her error introduces deficiency and (in many systems) the birth of the demiurge [[yaldabaoth]]. In *Pistis Sophia* the emphasis shifts from cosmogony to **soteriological drama** — her repentance and rescue become the model for the human soul's own fall, repentance, and return to the light. The text's thirteen Repentances are densely interwoven with Psalms and Odes of Solomon citations.

## Cross-tradition findings
- **Sophia and the Hebrew wisdom tradition.** The Gnostic Sophia/Pistis-Sophia figure stands in a contested relationship to the personified Wisdom (*Ḥokmah* / Sophia) of Proverbs 8, Ben Sira, and the Wisdom of Solomon, and to the [[the-monad-gnostic|Monad]]/Barbelo-derived feminine aeons. Scholarship treats this as a **development out of, and polemical reworking of**, Jewish wisdom and Platonic World-Soul speculation rather than a simple borrowing (Layton 1987; Rasimus 2009). The relationship to [[sophia-gnostic|the broader Gnostic Sophia]] is `continuous-development`.
