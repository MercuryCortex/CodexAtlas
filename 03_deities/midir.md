# Midir

## Identity
Midir is a lord of the **Tuatha Dé Danann** and king of the *sídhe* (otherworld mound) of **Brí Léith** — a god of the hidden Otherworld, its wealth, and its perilous beauty. After the Tuatha Dé withdraw into the fairy-mounds, Midir is among the great otherworld kings who rule the realm beneath and beside the mortal world.

## Earliest attestation
Central to the medieval Irish **Mythological Cycle**, above all the *Tochmarc Étaíne* ("The Wooing of Étaín"), preserved in the Book of the Dun Cow and later manuscripts but reflecting older tradition.

## Mythological role — the reclaiming of Étaín
Midir's great tale is his pursuit of [[etain]]. His first wife's jealousy transforms Étaín and casts her out; reborn after a thousand years and married to the High King **Eochaid Airem**, she is sought again by Midir, who wins a game of *fidchell* against Eochaid and claims a kiss — then carries Étaín off, the two rising through the roof as **swans** linked by a golden chain. The story is the classic Irish account of the otherworld lover who reclaims his bride across death and rebirth.

## Cross-tradition
Midir is an instance of the **otherworld-lord** who draws a mortal-bound woman into his hidden realm — structurally akin to the Greek [[hades]] and his claiming of Persephone, and to fairy-abduction motifs across Indo-European myth.

## Iconography
A richly-dressed otherworld king; the swan-pair on the golden chain; the *sídhe* mound of Brí Léith.
