# Coyote

## Identity

Coyote is the great **trickster–creator** of many Native North American peoples — especially across the Great Basin, the Plains, California, the Plateau, and the Southwest. He is one of the most complex figures in world mythology: simultaneously a **culture-hero** who helps shape the world and bring its institutions, and a **buffoon** whose greed, lust, and vanity make him the perpetual dupe of his own schemes. He is a shape-shifter, a survivor, and a paradox — wise and foolish, creative and destructive, sacred and obscene.

## Mythological role

Coyote's roles vary widely among the hundreds of nations that tell of him, but recurring threads stand out. As **co-creator**, he often shapes the present world and its features, arranges the order of things, and — significantly — is frequently responsible for the **origin of death**: in many tellings it is Coyote who decrees (or, by his own carelessness, ensures) that death shall be permanent, so that human beings are mortal because of him. As **trickster**, he steals fire or other goods for humankind, outwits the powerful, and is in turn humiliated by his own appetites in a vast cycle of comic and cautionary tales. He is thus both the bringer of culture and the embodiment of disorder and folly — the figure through whom the world became the imperfect, mortal, but livable place it is.

## Cross-tradition significance

Coyote is a paradigmatic instance of the **trickster** — the cross-cultural figure, studied classically by Paul Radin, who crosses boundaries, breaks rules, and in doing so generates change, exchange, and the conditions of human life. He stands beside [[raven-trickster]] (the Northwest Coast creator-trickster), the Norse [[loki]], the Greek [[hermes-greek]] and [[prometheus]], the West African [[eshu]] and Anansi — all ambivalent boundary-crossers in whom cleverness and folly, creation and disruption, are inseparable. His specific association with the **origin of death** also links him to the worldwide complex of myths explaining how mortality entered the world.
