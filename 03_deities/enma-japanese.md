# Enma

East Asian Buddhist king of hell and chief judge of the dead — the Japanese form of Chinese 閻羅王 *Yánluó-wáng*, which is in turn the Chinese phonetic-and-semantic transcription of Sanskrit *Yama*. Enma is the East-Asian re-instantiation of Vedic [[yama-vedic]] (the first mortal, who became king of the dead in the Rigveda), filtered through ~1000 years of Chinese Buddhist iconographic and doctrinal development.

## The transmission chain

Sanskrit *Yama* (RV-period, ~-1500 BCE — the first mortal, sovereign of the dead)
→ Buddhist *Yama* (canonical retention as the underworld judge in Pali + Sanskrit Buddhist scripture)
→ Middle Chinese 閻摩 *Yán-mó* (phonetic transcription, Han-period Buddhist translations)
→ Chinese 閻羅王 *Yánluó-wáng* (Tang-period elaboration with Chinese imperial-court iconography)
→ Japanese 閻魔王 *Enma-Ō* (Heian period onward via Chinese Buddhist transmission)
→ Korean 염라대왕 *Yeomna-daewang* (parallel Chinese-Buddhist transmission)

## The Ten Kings of Hell

The Chinese *Sutra of the Ten Kings* (*Shíwáng-jīng* 十王經, ~9th–10th c. CE — apocryphal, written in China not translated from India) elaborated the underworld court into a tribunal of ten judging kings. The newly-dead pass through each king's court at fixed intervals (7 days, 14 days, 21 days... up to 3 years), with each king examining a specific aspect of the deceased's deeds. Yama / Enma is the **5th king** — the chief judge whose name became the metonym for the whole tribunal. This Ten Kings cycle is the structural source of East Asian Buddhist funeral practice (the 7-day memorial rites, 49-day mourning period, etc.).

## Doctrinal nuance — judge, not punisher

A common Western misreading flattens Enma into a Buddhist Satan. The Buddhist framework is karmic: Enma is the **judge** who reveals the soul's deeds and assigns the appropriate hell (or rebirth) — he is not the source of evil, nor does he punish out of malice. In some Japanese Buddhist traditions (esp. those linked to Jizō Bosatsu / Kṣitigarbha — node not yet in the corpus) Enma himself is a bodhisattva in disguise, who took on the harsh judging-role out of compassion.
