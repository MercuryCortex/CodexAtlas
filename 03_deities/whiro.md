# Whiro

Whiro (often **Whiro-te-tipua**, "Whiro the demon/uncanny one") is the Māori personification of **darkness, evil, and death** — the great adversary at the heart of the Māori myth of the cosmic struggle between the powers of light and the powers of the dark. A son of the primordial parents **[[ranginui|Ranginui]]** and **[[papatuanuku|Papatūānuku]]**, Whiro became the implacable enemy of his brother **[[tane|Tāne]]**, the god of forests and of light who brought knowledge and life to the world. Their enmity crystallized when Tāne **ascended through the heavens to obtain the three baskets of sacred knowledge** (*ngā kete o te wānanga*) for humankind: Whiro sent hordes of his creatures — **insects, vermin, reptiles, and birds** — to attack and stop him, and failing, was defeated and **driven down into the underworld** (Rarohenga). From there Whiro wages unending war against the world of light, and it is he who looses **disease, decay, and death** upon humankind, his agents the biting and crawling things and the maladies that gnaw at the body. He embodies the Māori understanding of evil and mortality as an active, malevolent power — the dark brother who chose descent, forever opposed to Tāne and the living world above.
