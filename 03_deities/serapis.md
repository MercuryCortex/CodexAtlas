# Serapis

## Identity
Serapis (Greek *Sarapis*) is the syncretic high-god of Ptolemaic Egypt, deliberately engineered as a cult-fusion under **Ptolemy I Soter** (~-300) and his successors to provide a deity worshippable by both Greek settlers and native Egyptians. The name derives from the Memphite cult of *Osor-Hapi* — the deceased Apis bull, ritually identified with Osiris in the netherworld. Onto this Egyptian core Greek priests (Timotheus of Eleusis and the Egyptian priest Manetho, by tradition) layered iconography and attributes from Greek Zeus, Hades, Dionysus, and Asclepius, producing a bearded, throned, fully Hellenized cosmic king with a grain-measure (*modius*) crown and Cerberus at his feet.

## Earliest attestation
- Founding under Ptolemy I Soter, ~-300 (Plutarch *De Iside et Osiride* 28; Tacitus *Histories* 4.83–84 — Tacitus claims a dream revelation directed Ptolemy to import the cult statue from Sinope on the Black Sea).
- Earliest iconographic attestations: silver coinage of Ptolemy I; statues from the Serapeum of Alexandria (the great cult-temple, destroyed by Christian mob in +391 under Theophilus, Patriarch of Alexandria, as part of Theodosian-era anti-pagan campaigns).

## Mythological role
Serapis presides over (a) the underworld and afterlife (inheriting Osiris's role); (b) fertility of grain and the Nile flood; (c) healing through dream-incubation (his temples often hosted *enkoimēsis* — therapeutic dream sleep, like Asclepieia); (d) cosmic kingship (Plutarch and the aretalogies treat him as effectively the universal god). The cult statue at the Alexandrian Serapeum, attributed to the sculptor Bryaxis, was enormous and famous throughout the empire.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[osiris]]** — *ancient-identification*: Serapis IS the Hellenized Osiris-Apis; the etymology is explicit (Plutarch *DIO* 28–29).
- **[[apis-bull]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Memphite Apis bull, ritually deceased and merged with Osiris, is the Egyptian half of the name.
- **[[zeus]]** — *ancient-identification*: aretalogies and dedications call him "Zeus Sarapis"; Apuleius *Met.* 11 has the goddess Isis name Serapis as supreme.
- **[[hades]]** — *ancient-identification*: Tacitus *Histories* 4.83–84 treats Serapis as Hades / Pluto, with the Cerberus iconography.
- **[[dionysus-mystery]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: mystery-cult elements (initiation, death-and-renewal) link Serapis to Dionysian mystery currents.

## Iconography
- Bearded mature man in Greek style (typologically descended from Asclepius and Zeus)
- *Modius* (grain measure / agricultural container) headdress
- Throne with sceptre
- Cerberus, the three-headed dog, at his feet (the underworld dimension)
- Often paired in temples with Isis (consort) and Harpocrates (their son)

## Disputes
- **Origin of the cult.** Was Serapis (a) deliberately engineered by Ptolemy I from native Memphite Osor-Hapi (mainstream — Stambaugh, Fraser); (b) imported as a statue from Sinope on the Black Sea (Tacitus's tradition); or (c) a more organic Egyptian-Greek synthesis preceding Ptolemy?
- **Theological status.** Some 1st–4th-c. CE aretalogies treat Serapis (and Isis) as effectively universal deities encompassing all others — a kind of *henotheism within polytheism* analogous to philosophical monotheism. Whether this counts as monotheism is contested.
