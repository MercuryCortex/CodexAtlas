# Ra

Ra is the Egyptian sun god, the senior member of the Heliopolitan ennead, and across most of Egyptian history the implicit chief deity behind all the local cults that absorb him by composition (Amun-Ra at Thebes, Ra-Horakhty in funerary contexts, Khnum-Ra at Elephantine, etc.). Ra's daily passage across the sky in his solar bark, his nightly journey through the twelve hours of the underworld (the *Amduat*, the *Book of Gates*, the *Book of Caverns*), and his daily rebirth at dawn form the master cosmological rhythm of Egyptian religion. In the Heliopolitan creation account ([[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] and elaborations) Ra emerges as Atum-Ra from the primordial waters of [[nun]] on the first mound; he generates [[shu]] (air) and [[tefnut]] (moisture) by self-conception, who beget [[geb]] (earth) and [[nut]] (sky), who beget [[osiris]], [[isis]], [[set]], [[nephthys]].

## Mythological role
Beyond the cosmogony, Ra is central to: the *Destruction of Mankind* myth (Ra grows old and humanity rebels; he sends Sekhmet/Hathor to slaughter them, then relents by tricking her with beer dyed red); the daily and nightly cycles structured in the New Kingdom underworld books; and the [[divine-kingship]] theology that identifies the pharaoh as "son of Ra" (*sa-Ra*), a title pharaohs bore from the 4th Dynasty onward. The Atenist reform ([[phase-1-011-great-hymn-to-aten]]) radicalized one strand of Ra's complex by isolating the solar disc as sole worshipped form; after Akhenaten the Theban composite Amun-Ra resumed dominance.
