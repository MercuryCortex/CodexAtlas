# Chicomecoatl

The **mature female maize-goddess** of the Aztec — "Seven Serpent," the goddess of the harvested dried-corn that sustained the Mexica through the dry season. Chicomecoatl is depicted carrying corn in both hands, wearing a headdress of dried maize-cobs and red body-paint, holding the *chicahuaztli* rattle-staff used in agricultural ritual.

**Ochpaniztli festival (September).** The major Aztec festival of the maize harvest — a young woman impersonating Chicomecoatl was sacrificed; her skin was worn by a priest in subsequent ritual dancing as the deity-embodied-in-the-victim. The deity-in-the-skin-of-the-sacrificed-impersonator is one of the most distinctive Aztec ritual-theological forms; Xipe Totec's "flayed lord" cult uses the same logic. The Ochpaniztli rite extended the body-of-the-deity-is-the-food logic into ritual practice: the corn that the community ate had passed through the body of the goddess who was its substance.

Chicomecoatl pairs with [[centeotl]] (the young male maize-god) and with the young goddess Xilonen in the gendered Aztec maize-deity cluster. See [[mayahuel]] for the agave/pulque counterpart and [[corn-mother]] for the broader Mesoamerican-and-North-American body-of-the-deity-IS-the-cultivar pattern.
