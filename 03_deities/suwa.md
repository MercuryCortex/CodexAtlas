# Suwāʿ

One of the five pre-Islamic Arabian deities named in **Quran 71:23** (Surah Nuh) as the idols of Noah's people that the Arabs of the Hejaz later inherited and worshipped:

> *"And they said: 'Forsake not your gods, and forsake not Wadd, nor Suwāʿ, nor Yaghūth, nor Yaʿūq, nor Nasr.'"* (Q 71:23, Pickthall trans.)

The Quranic listing is the load-bearing textual evidence for treating these five as a coherent theological category — the **antediluvian-pantheon** that the Quran condemns as the original idolatry from which all subsequent polytheism descends.

Ibn al-Kalbi's *Kitāb al-Aṣnām* (c. 819 CE) assigns Suwāʿ specifically to the **Hudhayl tribe** at Yanbū (on the Red Sea coast north-west of Medina) and describes the idol as female-figured — though the grammatical gender of *Suwāʿ* is masculine in Arabic. The cult was suppressed during the Islamic conquest of the Hejaz in the late 620s; the idol was destroyed by ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ on the Prophet's instruction.
