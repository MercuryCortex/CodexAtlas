# Triton

Triton (Greek *Trítōn*) is the son of **[[poseidon]]** and **[[amphitrite]]** and the herald of the sea — a fish-tailed merman (the figure who gives the whole class of later "tritons" its name) who calms or rouses the waves by blowing his twisted **conch-shell trumpet**. Hesiod places him with his parents in their golden palace beneath the sea (*Theogony* 930–933). He guides the Argonauts out of Lake Tritonis in Libya (Apollonius, *Argonautica* 4), and Ovid has him sound the retreat of the waters after Deucalion's flood (*Metamorphoses* 1). From the Hellenistic period the singular Triton multiplies into a decorative retinue of sea-tritons accompanying marine processions in art.
