# Hevajra

Hevajra is one of the principal **meditational deities (yidams)** of late Indian and Tibetan **Vajrayāna Buddhism**, and the central deity of the **Hevajra Tantra**, a foundational text of the **Yoginī (Mother) class of Anuttarayoga Tantra**. He appears as a **dark-blue wrathful Heruka** — multiple heads, arms, and legs, garlanded with skulls, holding skull-cups that bear deities and animals, dancing upon corpses — locked in **yab-yum** union with his consort **Nairātmyā**, whose name ("No-Self") declares the wisdom of emptiness that the embrace embodies. Despite this terrifying form, Hevajra is not a demon but a vehicle of awakening: his practice is a method for transmuting the raw energies of passion and aggression into **the union of bliss and emptiness**, the goal of the highest tantra. He belongs to the Buddha-family of **[[akshobhya|Akshobhya]]**, and stands beside **[[chakrasamvara|Chakrasamvara]]** as one of the two great heruka cycles of the period. In Tibet he is especially central to the **Sakya** school, whose core *Lamdre* ("Path and Fruit") system is built upon the Hevajra Tantra.
