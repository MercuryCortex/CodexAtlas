# Coeus

## Identity

Coeus (Greek **Koios**) is one of the twelve Titans, a son of [[uranus-greek]] (Heaven) and [[gaia]] (Earth). His name was connected by the Greeks with questioning and the inquiring intellect, and by the alternative form *Polos* with the **axis or pole of heaven** — making Coeus a Titan of the northern celestial axis and of the rational, questioning mind. With his sister-consort [[phoebe-titan]] he stands at the head of an important Titan line.

## Mythological role

Coeus has little narrative of his own beyond his genealogical importance. United with Phoebe, he fathers **[[leto]]** and **Asteria** — and so becomes the grandfather of the Olympians **Apollo** and **Artemis** (through Leto) and, through Asteria, an ancestor of **Hecate**. As one of the Titans born of Uranus and Gaia, he belongs to the generation that, under Kronos, ruled before the Olympians; in the standard account the Titans are defeated by Zeus in the Titanomachy and confined to Tartarus. Coeus thus stands at the junction between the primordial sky-and-earth generation and the bright Olympian gods who descend from his daughter.

## Cross-tradition significance

Coeus is part of the Greek system of **Titans as the structural framework of the cosmos** — several of them (Coeus/the axis, Hyperion/the sun's height, Oceanus/the world-river, Iapetus/the underworld pillar) function less as personalities than as the great fixed coordinates of the world. His pairing with Phoebe to produce the line of prophetic and solar light (Leto → Apollo) makes him a quiet ancestral node in the genealogy linking the primordial Titans to the Olympian order.
