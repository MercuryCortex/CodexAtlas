# Inti

## Identity
Sun god of the Inca empire (Tawantinsuyu); patron deity of the imperial dynasty and supreme object of Inca state cult. The Inca emperor was *Sapa Inca* — son of the Sun — and the imperial lineage traced descent from the mythic Manco Cápac, who emerged from Lake Titicaca by Inti's command. The Coricancha (Temple of the Sun) in Cusco was the imperial cultic center, its principal image the *Punchao* — a great golden disk representing Inti, melted down by the conquistadors. Inti's cult was the state-imposed religion; local Andean populations continued their own local *huaca* cults (cf. the Huarochirí Manuscript) under Inca-administrative imposition.

## Earliest attestation
Inca state religion ~1200–1533 CE; documented in early colonial sources (Cieza de León 1553; Polo de Ondegardo 1571; Garcilaso de la Vega 1609; Bernabé Cobo 1653). Pre-Inca Andean solar imagery exists but the specifically Inti-imperial cult is Inca.

## Mythological role
In the *Comentarios reales* (Garcilaso 1609), Inti sends his children Manco Cápac and Mama Ocllo to emerge from Lake Titicaca and found Cusco; they teach the Andean people civilization. The annual *Inti Raymi* festival (June solstice) was the principal imperial-religious celebration.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Christ / Catholic monstrance solar imagery** — *folk-syncretism*: Andean colonial-Christian art repeatedly transfers Inti solar imagery to Eucharistic monstrance and Christ-the-Sun iconography. The Corpus Christi feast in colonial Cusco (held near the date of the pre-conquest Inti Raymi) functioned as syncretic Inti-Christ celebration.

## Cross-tradition MASSIVE WIN: the solar-imperial-patronage cluster

Inti is the most politically formalized instantiation of a universal pattern: the solar deity as the patron and legitimating foundation of a state religion, with the ruler claiming divine solar descent.

| Figure | Tradition | Royal claim | Primary temple | Solar feast |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Inti** | Inca (Tawantinsuyu) | Sapa Inca = Son of the Sun | Coricancha, Cusco | Inti Raymi (June solstice) |
| **[[ra]]** | Egyptian | Pharaoh = Son of Ra (Ba of Ra incarnate) | Heliopolis / Karnak | Sunrise rituals daily |
| **[[amaterasu]]** | Japanese (Shinto) | Emperor = descendant of Amaterasu | Ise Shrine | New Year / accession rites |
| **[[surya]]** | Vedic/Hindu | Kṣatriya solar lineage (*sūryavaṃśa*) | Konark Sun Temple | Ratha Saptami, Chhath Puja |
| **Sol Invictus** | Roman (3rd c. CE) | Emperor Aurelian's divine patron | Temple of Sol Invictus, Rome | December 25 natalis solis |

The structural pattern is invariant: the solar deity is maximally universal (everyone sees the sun), maximally powerful (nothing lives without it), and maximally imperial (rules over all territory the sun touches). The political theology follows necessarily.

**MASSIVE WIN — Inti Raymi ↔ Christmas**: Sol Invictus' December 25 natalis solis (birthday of the unconquered sun) was selected by the 4th-c. Church for the Nativity of Christ partly because it was an existing solar feast. When Spanish missionaries moved Peru's Corpus Christi celebration to June (near the June-solstice Inti Raymi), they replicated in the colonial Andes the same solar-feast substitution that had occurred in Rome 1,200 years earlier.

## Iconography
Golden disk with rays and a human face — the *Punchao*. Solar imagery throughout Inca art.
