# Khonsu

## Identity
Khonsu (Egyptian *Ḫnsw*, "the wanderer" — referring to the moon's path across the sky) is the **moon-god of Thebes** and the **divine child** of the [[amun]] / [[mut]] / Khonsu **Theban triad** that defines New Kingdom Egyptian state religion. He is a god of healing, exorcism, childbirth, and the measurement of time, with a major cult temple at Karnak (the Khonsu temple, completed under Ramesses III) and a famous oracular function attested in the Bentresh Stela.

## Earliest attestation
- Listed among the gods in the [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] and [[phase-1-009-coffin-texts]] (Old to Middle Kingdom) — though in early attestations he has a more aggressive, even cannibalistic character (the "moon devourer of hearts" in some Coffin Texts).
- Rises to prominence with the Theban Amun cult under the 18th Dynasty.
- Khonsu temple at Karnak completed under Ramesses III (~-1180); Khonsu cult flourishes through the Late Period.
- The **Bentresh Stela** (Louvre C 284, Ptolemaic-era inscription claiming much older date) narrates Khonsu's healing mission to a foreign princess — a major source for his oracular and healing role.

## Mythological role
Khonsu in his developed New Kingdom form is the **gentle divine child** of the Theban triad, providing the third member alongside father Amun and mother Mut. As a moon-god he marks time and seasons; as a healing god he is invoked in medical incantations; as an oracular god he is consulted by procession-oracle (the cult-image is carried out and questioned, answering through movement of the priests carrying the barque). The **Bentresh Stela** is the principal narrative — Ramesses II's Hittite wife is reported to have a sister possessed by an evil spirit in distant Bactria; Khonsu's cult-image is sent on a multi-year journey to exorcise her; the foreign prince attempts to keep the god but releases him after a divine dream.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[thoth]]** — *ancient-identification* (partial): both are Egyptian moon-gods, sometimes interchangeable in lunar contexts. Their cult-identities remain distinct (Thoth = wisdom and writing; Khonsu = healing and time).
- **Heracles (Greek)** — *ancient-identification*: [[herodotus]] *Histories* 2.43–44 identifies an Egyptian "Heracles" dated millennia before the Greek hero. Most modern scholars identify this Egyptian Heracles as Khonsu of Thebes (Lloyd 1976), making Khonsu the principal Egyptian source for the **Heracles-as-Egyptian-import** theory in [[herodotus]].

## Iconography
- Mummiform body (like Osiris and Ptah)
- **Lunar disc resting in a crescent** on the head — the principal Khonsu-attribute
- **Side-lock of youth** — single braid falling from the side of the head, marking him as a child / young man
- Crook and flail (royal regalia)
- Sometimes hawk-headed (assimilation to Horus); sometimes baboon (assimilation to the Thoth-baboon)

## Disputes
- **Original character.** Whether early Khonsu was a dark / chthonic / cannibalistic deity who was domesticated into the gentle Theban-triad child (some Coffin-Text readings, Posener 1981) or whether the "moon devourer" passages are unrelated, is contested.
- **Herodotean Heracles.** The identification of Herodotus's Egyptian Heracles with Khonsu is the dominant scholarly view but not unanimous — some prefer Heryshef or a generalized "Heracles-figure" composite.
