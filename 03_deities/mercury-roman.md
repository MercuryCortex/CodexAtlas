# Mercury (Roman)

## Identity
Mercury is the Roman god of commerce, travelers, messengers, and (in his psychopompic role) the guide of souls to the underworld. His name is etymologically connected to *merx* / *mercari* ("merchandise / to trade"), indicating that he was originally and especially a *commerce-god* in Roman cult — a flavor not as pronounced in his Greek counterpart Hermes. The cult was formally established at Rome with a temple on the Aventine in -495, paired with a guild of merchants (*collegium mercatorum*). The temple, the merchant guild, and the festival on May 15th (Mercuralia) all centered on commercial blessing.

## Earliest attestation
- Aventine temple dedicated -495 (Livy 2.21, 2.27).
- The Mercuralia festival: May 15th; merchants sprinkled themselves and their goods with water from the Porta Capena sacred well.
- The cult and iconography are largely imported from Greek Hermes, but the commercial specialization is Roman.

## Mythological role
Like other major Roman deities, Mercury's mythology is essentially Greek Hermes's mythology under Latin name. Roman-specific function: presider over commerce, *mercatura*. As psychopomp he conducts the dead to Hades — a role that connected him to the *Lares* and household death-cult. In Late Antiquity, Mercury was equated with **Hermes Trismegistus**, transferring the Greco-Egyptian wisdom-revelator role onto the Roman god: Latin Hermetica use *Mercurius Ter Maximus*.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[hermes-greek]]** — *ancient-identification*: complete and foundational.
- **[[hermes-trismegistus]]** — *ancient-identification*: Latin Hermetica.
- **[[thoth]]** — *ancient-identification* (mediated via Hermes): the Egypt-Greek-Roman triple equation.
- **[[lugus]]** (Celtic) — *ancient-identification*: Julius Caesar (*De Bello Gallico* 6.17) reports the Gauls' chief god is "Mercury"; modern scholarship identifies this Roman *interpretatio* with the Celtic Lugus (whose name survives in Lyon, Léon, Carlisle). Mercury-Lugus shrines proliferate in Roman Gaul.

## Iconography
- Young, beardless male (often nude or with chlamys)
- *Caduceus*: winged staff with twin serpents
- *Petasos*: winged traveler's hat
- *Talaria*: winged sandals
- *Marsupium*: purse (uniquely Roman emphasis — the merchant's purse)
- Sometimes accompanied by a ram or cockerel

## Disputes
- **Native Roman content.** Was there a native Roman *mercator*-god (commerce protector) that Mercury overwrote, or is the entire deity an early Greek import? Older scholarship (Wissowa) treats him as essentially Greek-imported with Roman commercial flavor.
- **Mercury-Lugus identification.** Caesar's *interpretatio* and the proliferation of Gallo-Roman Mercury shrines are well-attested; the *specifically Lugus* identification depends on linguistic and iconographic argument (Birkhan, Maier).
