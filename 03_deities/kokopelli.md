# Kokopelli

Kokopelli is the **humpbacked flute-playing** fertility deity of the Puebloan Southwest — one of the most widely depicted figures in the **rock art** of the Ancestral Puebloan world, where his hunched, flute-piping silhouette appears on canyon walls from at least the early first millennium CE. He is a god (and Hopi **katsina**, *Kookopölö*) of **fertility** in every register: of crops and rain, of game animals, and of human reproduction — older petroglyphs and kiva murals often show him **ithyphallic**, and Hopi tradition makes him a randy seducer whose flute lures and impregnates. His characteristic **hump** is variously read as a **bundle of seeds**, a **trader's pack** (linking him to the long-distance traders who carried his image and perhaps his cult northward from Mesoamerica, where a humpbacked flute-player and the macaw-feather trade connect him to Aztec merchant-gods), or a sack of songs. Domesticated by modern Southwest tourism into a benign decorative emblem, the original Kokopelli was a far earthier and more potent **spirit of generation, music, and travel** — the piper whose tune makes the seeds, the rain, and the people increase.
