# Rūaumoko

Rūaumoko is the Māori god of **earthquakes, volcanoes, and geothermal activity** — and his nature is bound up with one of the most moving images in Māori cosmology. He is the **youngest child of the primordial parents**, the sky-father **[[ranginui|Ranginui]]** and the earth-mother **[[papatuanuku|Papatūānuku]]**, who lay locked in a tight embrace in the beginning, their many children crushed in the darkness between them. When the other children forced their parents apart to let in the light, the infant Rūaumoko was **still unborn (or still at the breast) within his mother**, and so he remained inside her, in the body of the earth — the one child who never left Papatūānuku. His **movements and turnings deep within his mother** are felt by the living as **earthquakes**, and the **volcanic fire and geothermal heat** that the gods gave him for warmth break out as eruptions and hot springs; his shifting is also linked to the **turning of the seasons**, warmth and cold passing as he moves. Rūaumoko gives a tender and powerful explanation for a land as seismically and volcanically alive as Aotearoa — the restlessness of the last, unborn child held forever in the earth's body.
