# Aššur (Ashur)

## Identity
Aššur is the national high god of Assyria — eponymously named with and named for the cult-city Aššur on the Tigris, which is itself eponymously named with and named for the country (the rare case where city, god, and nation share one name). From the Old Assyrian period (~-2000) onward Aššur is the city-god of Aššur; from the Middle Assyrian period he is the imperial god of the Assyrian state; in the Neo-Assyrian empire he is promoted to a universal-creator role, displacing Marduk programmatically and absorbing the cosmic-king functions of Enlil. He is the divine sponsor of Assyrian kingship: the king is called *šangû* (priest) of Aššur, and every campaign and every conquest is reported back to Aššur in the *Letters to the God* genre (Lambert 1983; Maul 2017).

## Earliest attestation
- The cult-city Aššur (modern Qal'at Sherqat) has temple-foundations going back to the early Early Dynastic / Akkadian period (~-2400); the god Aššur is identifiable in the texts from the Old Assyrian *kārum* documents (~-1900, Kanesh archive) onward.
- The earliest unambiguous theological elaborations come from the Middle Assyrian period royal inscriptions (~-1350 onward).
- The Neo-Assyrian programmatic promotion of Aššur to supreme creator-god is visible in Sennacherib's *Aššur-version of Enuma Elish* (~-700) — see Lambert 1983.

## Mythological role
Aššur has limited independent mythology of his own in pre-Neo-Assyrian sources — he functions as the royal-patronage deity, and his "mythology" is the political-theological narrative of Assyrian campaigns and conquests as the will of Aššur. In Neo-Assyrian theology, the political-theological move of Sennacherib and his successors gives Aššur a cosmogonic role by identifying him with **An-šár** — the elder generation of *Enuma Elish*, father of Anu and grandfather of Marduk — thereby making Aššur older and higher than Marduk. The Aššur-version of *Enuma Elish* substitutes Aššur's name for Marduk's in the combat with Tiamat (Lambert 1983).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[anshar-kishar|Anshar]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Neo-Assyrian programmatic identification of Aššur with the primordial creator-principle An-šár of *Enuma Elish* (Lambert 1983).
- **[[enlil]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: functional absorption of Enlil's role as supreme decree-giver and king-of-the-gods (Lambert 1983; Maul 2017).
- **[[marduk]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: programmatic Assyrian substitution for Marduk in cosmogonic-warrior role.

## Iconography
Most distinctively, the **winged sun-disc with the archer-within** — the so-called *Aššur emblem* — appears above Assyrian kings on reliefs at Nimrud, Nineveh, and Khorsabad as the visible presence of the god in battle. The Aššur temple (Ekur) at Aššur is the architectural focus of the cult.

## Disputes
- **Origin of the name and original character.** Whether Aššur was originally the deified city / locale (Lambert) or a distinct mountain/storm god whose name was later coterminous with the city is unsettled. The "deified locality" view is the current default (Maul 2017).
- **Extent of Anshar-identification before Sennacherib.** Lambert 1983 argues the Anshar=Aššur identification is a Sennacherib-era political move; some scholars see precedents earlier in the Middle Assyrian period.
