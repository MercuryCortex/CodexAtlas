# El

El (Ugaritic *ʾIlu*; Hebrew *ʾēl*) is the high god and patriarch of the Canaanite pantheon. His epithets in the Ugaritic texts emphasize his age, wisdom, and patriarchal authority: "Father of Years" (*Ab Šanima*), "Creator of Creatures" (*Bāniyu binwāti*), "Bull El, the Compassionate" (*Tōr ʾIl Ḏū-pidi*). He sits enthroned at the "source of the rivers, at the springs of the two oceans" — a cosmological location at the navel of the world. He presides over the divine assembly and dispenses decrees, but he is not the active warrior of the pantheon (that is [[baal-hadad]]); his agency is more like that of a wise patriarchal grandfather than that of a vigorous king. His consort is [[asherah]] / Athirat, "Lady Athirat of the Sea." In the [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]] El authorizes both Yam's initial elevation and Baal's eventual palace and kingship — his sanction is required, but he is petitioned and persuaded rather than commanding directly.

## Mythological role
The Ugaritic Baal Cycle places El at the center of the divine council's deliberations: Anat petitions him for Baal's palace; he initially resists, then yields under pressure from Asherah; he weeps for the dead Baal; he authorizes Mot's removal. The mythological El is dignified, sometimes comic (the [[phase-1-014-ugaritic-ritual-texts]] include the famous KTU 1.114, "El's drinking party," in which El gets drunk and is humorously degraded). Theologically the most important fact about Canaanite El is his relationship to Israelite religion: the Hebrew Bible itself often calls Israel's god *ʾēl* (El), Israel's name means "El strives" or "El rules," and many of the El-epithets from Ugarit reappear in the Hebrew Bible attached to Yahweh (e.g., "El Elyon," "El Shaddai," "El of Bethel"). Smith (2001, 2002) and Cross (1973) argue persuasively that the Canaanite El is the figure who becomes the chief god of pre-monolatrous Israel and is then merged with Yahweh during the long process of Israelite monotheistic consolidation. Per the vault's splitting rule, the Canaanite El and the Hebrew El/Elohim are separate nodes joined by `syncretic-edges` (ancient-identification).

## Vault Connections

**HYPER WIN — El=El: the substrate of biblical monotheism.** The Canaanite El and the God of the Hebrew patriarchs share *the same name, the same epithets, the same throne-location, the same divine council, the same consort.* El Elyon ("God Most High") in Genesis 14:18–20 = Ugaritic El Elyon. El Shaddai ("God of the Mountains/Almighty") in Genesis 17:1 = Ugaritic *El of the mountain*. El Olam ("Everlasting God") in Genesis 21:33 = Ugaritic El "Father of Years." Israel (*Yisra-el*) = "El strives" or "El rules" — the nation's name encodes El as its founding deity, not Yahweh. This is not transmission through textual influence: it is continuous Canaanite religious culture that the Hebrew patriarchal narratives preserve without disguise. The Canaanite El IS the God of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob — the identification with Yahweh comes later.

**MASSIVE WIN — Psalm 82: the divine council scene verbatim from Ugarit.** Psalm 82:1 — "God (*Elohim*) takes his stand in the divine assembly; among the gods (*elohim*, plural) he renders judgment" — is a verbatim structural reproduction of the Ugaritic divine council scene in which El presides over the *phr bn ilm* ("congregation of sons of El"). The Psalm continues: "I declared, 'You are gods (*elohim*), sons of the Most High (*bĕnê ʿelyôn*) all of you'" — the divine council members are explicitly called *elohim* and *sons of the Most High*, exactly the Ugaritic *bn ilm* (sons of El). The Psalm ends with these gods condemned to die "like humans" because they have judged unjustly — a polemic that presupposes the polytheistic framework it polemicizes against. Psalm 82 is the Hebrew Bible's most transparent window onto the Canaanite-Israelite divine council system. Jesus quotes it in John 10:34 ("Is it not written in your law, 'I said you are gods'?") to deflect the charge of blasphemy — three traditions (Ugaritic myth → Hebrew psalm → Gospel argument) in one verse.

**Deut 32:8–9 DSS restoration: the clearest evidence of El-above-YHWH.** The Masoretic Text of Deuteronomy 32:8 reads: "When the Most High divided the nations, he fixed the boundaries of peoples *according to the number of the sons of Israel*." This makes no sense: why would the nations' number match Israel's? The Septuagint reads "sons of God (*angelōn theou*)" and the Dead Sea Scrolls (4QDeut^j) read "sons of El (*bĕnê ʾēl*)" — confirming the original text had the divine council. The verse originally said: El Elyon (= Canaanite El) divided the nations and assigned them to his divine sons, with YHWH receiving Israel as *his portion*. YHWH is El's son here, not El himself — a pre-merger, henotheistic layer where El and YHWH are distinct. The MT scribal "correction" to "sons of Israel" erased this. The DSS restoration is one of the most important textual discoveries for Israelite religious history.
