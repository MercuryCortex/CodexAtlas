# El

El (Ugaritic *ʾIlu*; Hebrew *ʾēl*) is the high god and patriarch of the Canaanite pantheon. His epithets in the Ugaritic texts emphasize his age, wisdom, and patriarchal authority: "Father of Years" (*Ab Šanima*), "Creator of Creatures" (*Bāniyu binwāti*), "Bull El, the Compassionate" (*Tōr ʾIl Ḏū-pidi*). He sits enthroned at the "source of the rivers, at the springs of the two oceans" — a cosmological location at the navel of the world. He presides over the divine assembly and dispenses decrees, but he is not the active warrior of the pantheon (that is [[baal-hadad]]); his agency is more like that of a wise patriarchal grandfather than that of a vigorous king. His consort is [[asherah]] / Athirat, "Lady Athirat of the Sea." In the [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]] El authorizes both Yam's initial elevation and Baal's eventual palace and kingship — his sanction is required, but he is petitioned and persuaded rather than commanding directly.

## Mythological role
The Ugaritic Baal Cycle places El at the center of the divine council's deliberations: Anat petitions him for Baal's palace; he initially resists, then yields under pressure from Asherah; he weeps for the dead Baal; he authorizes Mot's removal. The mythological El is dignified, sometimes comic (the [[phase-1-014-ugaritic-ritual-texts]] include the famous KTU 1.114, "El's drinking party," in which El gets drunk and is humorously degraded). Theologically the most important fact about Canaanite El is his relationship to Israelite religion: the Hebrew Bible itself often calls Israel's god *ʾēl* (El), Israel's name means "El strives" or "El rules," and many of the El-epithets from Ugarit reappear in the Hebrew Bible attached to Yahweh (e.g., "El Elyon," "El Shaddai," "El of Bethel"). Smith (2001, 2002) and Cross (1973) argue persuasively that the Canaanite El is the figure who becomes the chief god of pre-monolatrous Israel and is then merged with Yahweh during the long process of Israelite monotheistic consolidation.
