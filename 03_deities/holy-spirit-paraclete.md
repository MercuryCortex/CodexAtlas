# Holy Spirit (Paraclete)

The third person of the Christian Trinity, sometimes called the Paraclete (from Greek *paraklētos*, "advocate / comforter") following Gospel of John 14–16. The Spirit's full divinity was a later doctrinal settlement than the Son's: largely fixed at [[event-council-of-nicaea-325|Nicaea, +325]] for the Son, and at the First Council of Constantinople (+381) for the Spirit (expanding the Nicene Creed).

The *filioque* controversy — whether the Spirit proceeds from the Father *and* the Son (Western) or from the Father *through* the Son (Eastern) — is one of the dogmatic axes of the [[event-great-schism-1054|East-West split]]. Theologically minor at one level, structurally load-bearing at another.

Distinctive features in this graph:
- **Inspiration of scripture**: 2 Tim 3:16 (*theopneustos*) — the Spirit as breath-of-God in scriptural authority; foundational for [[sola-scriptura|Reformation sola scriptura]] hermeneutics.
- **Pentecost**: [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] chapter 2 — the Spirit's descent on the disciples; foundational for charismatic and Pentecostal Christianity.
- **Indwelling believers**: Pauline pneumatology — the Spirit dwells in baptized believers, producing the *fruits* (Gal 5:22–23) and *gifts* (1 Cor 12).
- **Feminine reception**: early Syriac Christianity (Odes of Solomon, Aphrahat) used feminine pronouns and imagery, building on Hebrew *Ruach* (fem.) and Jewish Wisdom-theology ([[wisdom-personified]]).
