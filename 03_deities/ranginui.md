# Ranginui

## Identity
Sky-father of Māori cosmogony; consort of Papatūānuku (Earth Mother). Ranginui and Papatūānuku embraced in the primordial darkness so closely that no light could pass between them and no space existed for their children; their children, crushed and dark, eventually conspired to separate them. Tāne-mahuta succeeded in forcing them apart, creating the lit-world between sky and earth. Ranginui's tears for his beloved Papa are the rain.

## Earliest attestation
Māori oral tradition; documented in 19th-c. compilations (Grey 1854; White 1887; Best 1924).

## Mythological role
Primordial sky-father; principal action is the embrace with Papa and the subsequent separation. After separation, he is the sky above; his stars are the children of the night that he and Papa generated; his rain expresses his continuing love for Papa, who in turn sends mist upward to him.

## Iconography
The sky itself. Aniconic in any traditional sense.
