# Iŋyaŋ (Rock)

One of the four Superior Wakan Beings in the Lakota hierarchy of the Sixteen Wakan, documented by Walker's interviews with Oglala holy men (1980). Iŋyaŋ (Rock / Stone) is the primordial — the first of all beings, that which existed before everything else. Walker's informants describe Iŋyaŋ as the source: at the beginning, only Iŋyaŋ existed; he poured his blood outward to create the earth (Makȟá) and the sky (Skáŋ), and in doing so shrank and hardened into stone. Rock is therefore both the origin and the remainder — the substrate from which everything else came. Stone is treated as sacred (*wakȟáŋ*) throughout Lakota ceremonial life; the stones used in the sweat lodge ceremony (*Inípi*) are addressed as "Grandfathers" and heated to carry the prayers of participants.
