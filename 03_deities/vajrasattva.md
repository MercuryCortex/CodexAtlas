# Vajrasattva

## Identity
Vajrasattva — "the Diamond-Being" / "the Adamantine Being" — is the Buddha of purification in Vajrayāna Buddhism. He is the source of the 100-syllable mantra recited as the principal Vajrayāna purification practice, said to purify the obscurations of body, speech, and mind accumulated over many lifetimes. Iconographically: white-bodied, two-armed, seated in vajra-asana, holding a vajra at his heart (right hand) and a bell at his hip (left hand); often in yab-yum union with his consort Vajrasattvātmikā / Vajragarvī. In some Tantric systems he is the "Sixth Buddha" beyond the Five Dhyāni Buddha-families (Vairocana, Akṣobhya, Ratnasaṃbhava, Amitābha, Amoghasiddhi); in others he is the unifying principle of all five families. He is closely related to Vajradhara — the doctrinal distinction varies by tradition, with some schools effectively identifying them.

## Earliest attestation
Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha (~700–800, the foundational Yoga-Tantra). Tibetan reception via the early Sarma translators.
