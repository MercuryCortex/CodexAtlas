# The Amesha Spentas

## Identity
The *Aməša Spəṇta* ("Bounteous Immortals") are the six divine aspects through which Ahura Mazda creates and rules the world — or, with Ahura Mazda / Spenta Mainyu himself counted as the seventh, the *heptad* of cosmic principles that constitutes Zoroastrian theology's divine order. In the Gathas they appear primarily as abstract qualities; in the Younger Avesta they are personified divine beings, each presiding over one of the seven created elements:

1. **Vohu Manah** ("Good Mind") — presides over cattle / animals
2. **Aša Vahišta** ("Best Truth") — presides over fire
3. **Xšaθra Vairya** ("Desirable Dominion") — presides over metals
4. **Spənta Ārmaiti** ("Holy Devotion", feminine) — presides over earth
5. **Haurvatāt** ("Wholeness/Health", feminine) — presides over waters
6. **Amərətāt** ("Immortality", feminine) — presides over plants
7. **Spenta Mainyu / Ahura Mazda** himself, presiding over humanity

The seven-fold structure has been one of the most-discussed pieces of evidence in the long debate over Zoroastrian influence on Jewish and Christian angelology. The Second Temple Jewish texts (1 Enoch, Tobit, Qumran sectarian writings) develop a seven-archangel hierarchy (Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, Uriel, Raguel, Saraqael, Remiel in 1 Enoch 20) that has no clear Hebrew Bible precedent; the structural and numerical parallel to the Amesha Spentas, in the chronological window of Persian-period contact (539–332 BCE), is the strongest single piece of evidence for Zoroastrian influence on Jewish angelology cited by Boyce & Grenet (1991) and others. Direct verbal borrowing is not demonstrable; structural and conceptual influence is the standard scholarly hypothesis.
