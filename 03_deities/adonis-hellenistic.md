# Adonis (Hellenistic)

## Identity
Adonis is the beautiful youth-god loved by Aphrodite (Greek) or Astarte (Phoenician), killed in the prime of youth by a wild boar, mourned annually by women in seasonal women's-only festivals. The name **Adonis** derives from the West-Semitic title *ʾădōn* ("lord, master"), used as an epithet of the Phoenician version of the Mesopotamian vegetation god **Dumuzi / Tammuz** — making Adonis genealogically a Greek-Hellenistic reception of the Mesopotamian-Levantine dying-vegetation tradition.

The cult flourished particularly in **Byblos** (where the Adonis river ran red each spring, traditionally with his blood) and **Aphaca** (in Mount Lebanon). It spread to **Alexandria** under the Ptolemies (Theocritus *Idyll* 15 describes an Adoniazousai festival), to **Athens** (the *Adōnia* women's festival in summer), and into the Roman world.

## Earliest attestation
- Phoenician Adonis cult: archaeological and epigraphic from Byblos and Aphaca, ~-700 onward.
- Greek literary attestation: Sappho fragment 140 ("Cytherea, delicate Adonis is dying, what are we to do?"); Aristophanes *Lysistrata* 387–398 mocks the Adonia.
- Theocritus *Idyll* 15 (~-275 Alexandria) — the most detailed surviving account of the cult festival.
- Ovid *Metamorphoses* 10.503–739 — Latin literary version.

## Mythological role
The canonical Greek myth (Ovid, Apollodorus):
- Myrrha falls in love with her own father and tricks him into incestuous union; pursued, she is transformed into a myrrh tree.
- Adonis is born from the tree.
- His beauty is so great that Aphrodite places him in a chest and entrusts him to Persephone — who refuses to return him.
- Zeus decrees Adonis shall spend a third of each year with Persephone (underworld), a third with Aphrodite, and a third where he chooses (he chooses Aphrodite).
- Adonis is gored to death by a wild boar (sometimes interpreted as a jealous Ares).
- His blood becomes the anemone flower.
- Aphrodite's lament becomes the model for women's annual ritual mourning.

The **Adonia** ritual involved planting fast-growing seeds in shallow pots ("gardens of Adonis"), tending them for eight days, watching them sprout and then quickly wither — a vivid liturgical performance of premature death and unfulfilled life. Women wailed lamentations and processed with images of the dead youth.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[dumuzi-tammuz]]** — *ancient-identification* (via Levantine transmission): the etymological and structural connection is direct. Lucian *De Dea Syria* 6–8 identifies the Byblian Adonis with the Phoenician form of the Mesopotamian god.
- **[[attis]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: dying-youth-of-mother-goddess pattern.
- **[[osiris]]** — *scholarly-parallel* / *ancient-identification* (Plutarch DIO 15).
- The Hebrew Bible's Tammuz reference: **Ezekiel 8:14** describes women weeping for Tammuz at the north gate of the Temple — the same cult under its Mesopotamian-Levantine name attested in Iron Age Jerusalem.

## Iconography
- Beautiful young male, often nude or partially draped
- Wild boar (sometimes shown attacking him)
- Anemone (sacred plant born from his blood)
- Myrrh tree (his birth-tree)
- Frequently shown with Aphrodite mourning over his body

## Disputes
- **"Dying and rising god"?** Frazer (1906–15) treated Adonis as the paradigm of the type; Smith (1987), Mettinger (2001) re-examined. **Adonis-as-resurrected** is firmer than Attis: Lucian *De Dea Syria* 6, Origen *Contra Celsum* 6.34, and the Adonia rituals (women rejoiced as well as wept) point to a genuine death-and-renewal cycle. The category survives the modern critique.
- **Channel of transmission Mesopotamia → Levant → Greece.** Etymologically clear (*ʾădōn*); historically the Phoenician sea-trade and Bronze Age Levantine contact are the routes. Direct Greek borrowing from Mesopotamia is not required; the Phoenician staging is sufficient.
- **Christian parallels.** Frazer and others noted Adonis-Easter calendar overlap (Easter / Adonia both spring); the connection is *parallel-motif* not direct dependence per current scholarship.
