# Nidhogg

## Identity

Nidhogg (**Níðhöggr**, roughly "Malice-Striker" or "the one who strikes with hatred") is the dragon-serpent of Norse myth that lies coiled at the lowest root of the world-tree **Yggdrasil**, gnawing it from below, and that tears at the corpses of the dead in the underworld. It is a personified force of **decay and corruption** — the entropic power that works ceaselessly against the cosmic order embodied in the tree.

## Mythological role

Nidhogg appears in the *Poetic Edda* and in Snorri's *Gylfaginning*. Beneath Yggdrasil lie three roots; under one, in the well of the cold realm, Nidhogg gnaws unceasingly, so that the world-tree suffers more than people know — browsed by deer above, rotting at the side, and chewed by Nidhogg below. A squirrel, Ratatoskr, runs up and down the trunk carrying insults between the eagle at the top and Nidhogg at the root. In the underworld Nidhogg dwells at **Náströnd**, the "Corpse-Shore," where it sucks the blood and tears the bodies of the dead — especially oath-breakers and murderers. At **Ragnarök**, in the climactic vision of *Völuspá*, Nidhogg comes flying up from below, a dark dragon bearing corpses on its wings — one of the last images of the poem, ambiguous between an ending and a grim survival into the renewed world.

## Cross-tradition significance

Nidhogg is the Norse embodiment of the **serpent-of-decay at the root of the cosmos** — the chaos-power that gnaws perpetually at the world-order. It pairs with [[jormungandr]], the world-encircling sea-serpent, as the two great draconic threats to the Norse cosmos, and belongs to the wide ancient family of **chaos-serpents** (Tiamat, Apophis, Leviathan, Vritra) that the order of the world is forever set against. The image of the serpent coiled at the root of the world-tree, undermining it from below, is among the most evocative cosmological pictures in Germanic myth.
