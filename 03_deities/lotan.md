# Lotan

## Identity
Lotan (Ugaritic *ltn*, vocalized as *Lōtan* or *Litan*) is the **seven-headed chaos-serpent** of the Ugaritic Baal Cycle, defeated by Baal (and/or his consort Anat) as part of the cosmogonic combat that establishes Baal's kingship over Yam (Sea) and the cosmos. The key passage — KTU 1.5.i.1–3, in Mot's speech to Baal — names Lotan with the unforgettable epithet pair: *bṯn brḥ* ("fleeing serpent") and *bṯn ʿqltn* ("writhing serpent"), described as *šlyṭ d-šbʿt rʾašm* ("the mighty one with seven heads"). These epithets are the **direct verbal source** of the Hebrew Bible's description of Leviathan at Isaiah 27:1 — *nāḥāš bāriaḥ* and *nāḥāš ʿăqallātôn* — establishing the cognate identity of Ugaritic Lotan and Hebrew Leviathan beyond reasonable doubt (Day 1985; Smith 1994).

## Earliest attestation
- KTU 1.5.i.1–3 — the principal Ugaritic textual attestation, in Mot's speech to Baal within the Baal Cycle (~-1400 to -1200, tablet from Ras Shamra).
- The Ras Shamra tablets were excavated by the French mission under Claude Schaeffer beginning 1928; the Baal Cycle tablets were among the earliest finds.

## Mythological role
Lotan is a memory or trophy within the larger Baal Cycle's structure: Mot, the death-god, taunts Baal by recalling Baal's defeat of Lotan — implying that just as Lotan was crushed, Baal too can be defeated. The combat itself is not narrated *in extenso* within the surviving Baal Cycle tablets, but it is alluded to as established mythological fact; the chaoskampf is the prior cosmogonic act that justifies Baal's kingship. The Baal-Lotan combat is therefore an established part of Ugaritic cosmogony alongside the better-attested Baal-Yam combat.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[leviathan]]** — *ancient-identification* (linguistically): Ugaritic *ltn* = Hebrew *lwytn* (same Northwest Semitic root *lwy*, "to twist, coil"). The same myth survives in Israelite tradition with Yahweh in Baal's role: Isaiah 27:1 (Yahweh slays Leviathan, the fleeing/writhing serpent); Psalm 74:13–14 (Yahweh crushes the heads of the sea-monsters); Job 26:12–13; Psalm 89:9–10. Day 1985 reconstructs the cycle.
- **[[yam]]** — *ancient-identification*: Lotan operates in Yam's domain (the Sea); some scholars (Cassuto, Day) read Lotan as a serpentine manifestation of Yam himself.
- **[[tiamat]]** — *parallel-form*: Mesopotamian sea-chaos.
- **[[illuyanka]]** — *parallel-form*: Anatolian instance.
- **[[typhon]]** — *parallel-form*: Greek instance, multi-headed serpent type.
- **[[vritra]]** — *parallel-form*: Indo-European-Vedic instance.

## Iconography
Limited; some Ugaritic and broader Late Bronze Levantine seals show a Baal-storm-god figure striking a serpent, which scholars associate with the Lotan / Yam combat (Smith 1994 plate selection).

## Disputes
- **Distinct from Yam or identical with Yam.** Whether Lotan is a distinct chaos-monster operating in Yam's domain or a serpentine manifestation of Yam himself is the central interpretive question. Smith 1994 treats Lotan as distinct (a separate chaos-creature, like *Tannin* and the *Šaliyat* of seven heads, all subsidiary to Yam); Cassuto and Day allow more identification.
- **Direct ancestor-of-Leviathan vs. parallel-form.** Day 1985 argues the strongest case for *direct* literary-mythological ancestry — Hebrew Leviathan is the Yahwistic continuation of Ugaritic Lotan with a change of antagonist (Baal → Yahweh). Some critics treat the relation as *parallel-form* (shared West-Semitic cosmogonic vocabulary, not direct borrowing). The verbal calque at Isaiah 27:1 — *nāḥāš bāriaḥ* / *nāḥāš ʿăqallātôn* mirroring *bṯn brḥ* / *bṯn ʿqltn* — is the strongest piece of evidence for *ancestor-of*.
