# uNkulunkulu

## Identity
Supreme being of Zulu traditional religion — name literally "the great-great-one" or "the ancient ancestor." Unkulunkulu is the creator, the first ancestor of humanity, and the source from whom the reed-bed (*uHlanga*) of humanity emerged. Distinctive feature: Unkulunkulu sits ambiguously between a HIGH-GOD category and a FIRST-ANCESTOR category — different Zulu informants and lineages emphasize one or the other (Berglund 1976). The Zulu religious system relies more heavily on direct ancestor-veneration (*amadlozi*) than on cultic relation to the high-god, who tends to deus-otiosus distance.

## Earliest attestation
Pre-colonial Zulu oral tradition; first systematic European documentation Callaway (1870) — an extensive collection of Zulu religious testimony preserved in original Zulu with translation, foundational to 19th-c comparative religion. Modern ethnography Berglund (1976).

## Mythological role
Unkulunkulu broke off the first humans from the reed-bed (*uHlanga*) and sent them into the world. He sent the chameleon to tell humans they would live forever; the chameleon dawdled; he then sent the lizard to tell humans they would die; the lizard arrived first, and so death entered the world (the *uHlanga* origin-myth and the chameleon-lizard death-arrival myth are the two best-documented Unkulunkulu narratives — Callaway 1870).

## Chidester (1996) — supreme-being or missionary construction?
A significant scholarly debate addressed by Chidester *Savage Systems* (1996): some critics argue that "Unkulunkulu as supreme-being" is partly a 19th-c. missionary construction, with the original Zulu category being more ancestral/lineage-founder than transcendent-creator. Berglund (1976) and Mbiti (1969) treat Unkulunkulu as a genuine Zulu high-god concept; Chidester documents the missionary entanglement carefully. Current consensus accepts that Unkulunkulu IS a Zulu category but with more ancestral-dimensional content than missionary writers initially recognized — the supreme-being framing absorbed pre-existing ancestral material into a high-god-shaped translation slot.

## Cross-tradition parallels
- **[[mwari]]** (Shona), **[[olodumare]]** (Yoruba), **[[nyame]]** (Akan) — sub-Saharan African supreme-being archetype.
- The deus-otiosus pattern (high-god distant from daily cult, with practical religion focused on intermediaries) appears more strongly in Zulu/Unkulunkulu than in Shona/Mwari (active oracle) or Yoruba/Olodumare (mediated via orisha). The variation across Bantu high-gods is a useful corrective to Eliade-style telescope-scale identification.

## Iconography
No anthropomorphic image. The reed (*uHlanga*) is the origin-symbol. The sky is sometimes referenced as Unkulunkulu's domain.
