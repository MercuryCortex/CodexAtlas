# Baraqel

## Identity
**Baraqel** (Hebrew *baraq* = "lightning" + *El* = "God"; "Lightning of God" or "God's Lightning") is one of the named **Watchers** of 1 Enoch, appearing in three places:

1. **1 Enoch 6:7** — among the twenty chiefs-of-tens who swore the oath at Mount Hermon under [[semyaza]].
2. **1 Enoch 8:3** — as the Watcher who **taught humanity the signs of the lightning-flashes** (lightning-divination, an established Mesopotamian-priestly art).
3. **1 Enoch 69:11** — listed in the Parables' Watcher-teachings catalogue, again with the lightning-signs role.

1 Enoch 8:3 (Nickelsburg-VanderKam 2004): *"...Baraqel taught the signs of the lightning flashes; Kokabel taught the signs of the stars..."*

## Earliest attestation
- **1 Enoch 6:7** (chiefs list), **8:3** (lightning-signs teaching), **69:11** (Parables recapitulation).
- **Qumran 4Q201, 4Q202** — Aramaic fragments attest the broader Watchers tradition.
- **Ge'ez Mashafa Henok** — only complete textual witness.

## Mythological role
Baraqel teaches **brontoscopy / keraunoscopy** — divination by lightning and thunder. This was a real and prestigious Mesopotamian priestly divinatory practice (the cuneiform omen series *Enūma Anu Enlil* contains extensive lightning-omens; see [[phase-1-019-enuma-anu-enlil]]); the Enochic polemic against Baraqel's teaching of it places lightning-divination in the same Watcher-illicit-art register as Kokabiel's astrology, Penemue's writing, and Azazel's metallurgy and cosmetics. The polemic targets Babylonian and Etruscan-Roman augural-divinatory traditions in which lightning-omens were standard priestly knowledge.

In Etruscan religion, the *Libri Fulgurales* (Books of Lightning) constituted a complete priestly discipline (the *fulguratores*); in Greek and Roman religion, augury from lightning (*ex caelo*) was one of the highest forms of divination. The Enochic-Jewish tradition theologically frames *all* of this as Watcher-corruption.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- The Mesopotamian *Enūma Anu Enlil* lightning-omen tradition is the implied polemical target.
- Etruscan-Roman *fulguratura* and Greek augury are parallel-form structural cognates.

## Iconography
Not standardized.

## Disputes
- **Same figure across 6:7 and 8:3?** Most scholars (Nickelsburg 2001) treat the three attestations as a single Watcher; minor variants in name-form across recensions complicate this slightly.
