# Ninazu

## Identity
Ninazu ("Lord-Physician" in Sumerian) is a chthonic deity with a double profile: at **Enegi** (near Ur) he is a son of Ereshkigal and an underworld god of the dead; at **Eshnunna** (in the Diyala) he is a warrior city-god whose cult is taken over in the Akkadian period by the Akkadian god **Tishpak**, who inherits Ninazu's temple and his emblematic snake-dragon (*mušḫuššu*). The two profiles — chthonic-healing and warrior — represent two regional traditions of the same god, brought into the Mesopotamian pantheon as a junior member of the underworld-related cluster (Wiggermann 2000; Black & Green 1992). His etymological connection to *Lord-Physician* preserves a folk-theological link between the underworld and the healing arts: in Mesopotamian cosmology, both belong to the realm of *šūtu*-knowledge and *namburbi*-ritual.

## Earliest attestation
- Early Dynastic god lists from Fara and Abu Salabikh (~-2600 to -2500) name Ninazu among the senior pantheon.
- Temple Hymn 14 of the [[phase-1-003-enheduanna-hymns]] is dedicated to the Eshnunna temple of Ninazu — making this composition our earliest extended literary attestation.
- The Enegi cult (his chthonic profile) is attested in administrative texts from Ur III (~-2100 to -2000); the Eshnunna succession by Tishpak is attested in royal inscriptions of the Akkadian and early Old Babylonian period (Wiggermann 1992).

## Mythological role
Ninazu has limited mythological narrative; he is a regularly-named member of the underworld pantheon (alongside Ereshkigal, Namtar, and others) and a frequent recipient of cult-attention in healing and apotropaic contexts. His son **Ningishzida** has a more developed mythology — including the descent-to-the-underworld text *Ningishzida's Journey to the Underworld* — that probably reflects the family-business of chthonic-healing inherited from Ninazu.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **tishpak** — *ancient-identification*: cult-successor at Eshnunna. Wiggermann 1992 traces the *mušḫuššu* snake-dragon: Ninazu → Tishpak → Marduk (after Hammurabi's incorporation of Eshnunna ~-1762, the Marduk cult of Babylon absorbs the Tishpak emblem). The Babylonian *mušḫuššu* — the snake-dragon at the Ishtar Gate and on Marduk's standard — is therefore traceable back to Ninazu at Eshnunna.
- **[[ningishzida]]** — *ancient-identification*: son and functional successor in chthonic-healing.
- **[[ereshkigal]]** — *ancient-identification*: in the Enegi tradition, his mother — the Mesopotamian queen of the underworld.

## Iconography
The **mušḫuššu** (snake-dragon) is Ninazu's defining emblem — a serpent body with lion paws and bird talons. This iconographic creature passes from Ninazu to Tishpak to Marduk and ends as the most famous Babylonian heraldic creature (Ishtar Gate, ca. -575).

## Disputes
- **Single god or two regional gods.** Whether the Enegi (chthonic) Ninazu and the Eshnunna (warrior) Ninazu are originally a single deity later differentiated regionally, or two originally distinct deities later identified, is debated. Wiggermann *RlA* treats them as one god with regionally-emphasized profiles.
- **Origin of the *mušḫuššu*.** Whether the *mušḫuššu* originated with Ninazu and was transferred forward, or whether it is older and was attached to Ninazu, is unsettled. Wiggermann 1992 reconstructs the forward transmission from Ninazu onward.
