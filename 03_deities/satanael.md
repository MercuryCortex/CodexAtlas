# Satanael (Bogomil Satan-Demiurge)

## Identity
Satanael is the central cosmogonic figure of Bogomil — and of mitigated-dualist Cathar — theology: the *fallen first-born son of God* who is given permission to create the visible cosmos (or arrogates this role through rebellion) and who is responsible for the material world and human bodies. The name preserves the *-el* suffix (= God / divinity in Hebrew, retained in Greek and Slavonic angelological naming) characteristic of angelic names; in some versions, Satanael loses the *-el* suffix upon his final eschatological defeat, becoming merely "Satan." The narrative parallels the Sethian Gnostic Yaldabaoth almost exactly: a heavenly being demiurgically responsible for material creation, identified (in the Bogomil-Cathar reading) with the creator-God of the Hebrew Bible. In the absolute-dualist Cathar systems represented by the [[phase-5-023-liber-de-duobus-principiis]], this figure is replaced by a *coeternal* evil principle (true two-principles dualism); the Bogomil mitigated position retains a created-and-fallen Satanael, preserving formal monotheism while accomplishing the same dualistic moral binary at the cosmic level.

## Earliest attestation
Bogomil cosmological teaching ~10th c. (the priest Bogomil; the early Bulgarian sources are lost but reconstructable from Byzantine heresiological responses). Earliest textual witness: Euthymius Zigabenus's *Panoplia Dogmatica* (~1110) preserves Bogomil cosmology in detailed refutation. The *Interrogatio Iohannis* (~1190, Bogomil-Cathar) is the principal surviving direct Bogomil-Cathar text.

## Mythological role
Creates the visible cosmos and human bodies; tempts Adam and Eve; engineers the human race's enslavement in matter. The OT creator is identified with Satanael; OT history is the devil's regime. Christ (a docetic angelic figure, not a fleshly human in Bogomil-Cathar theology) is sent by the Father to break Satanael's hold and liberate the spiritual elect.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[yaldabaoth]]** — *scholarly-parallel* / *direct-influence?*: the structural parallel is so close that it is *the* foundational comparative-religion question in medieval dualism studies. Stoyanov 2000 and Hamilton argue for genealogical transmission (Manichaean → Paulician → Bogomil); Moore 2012, Pegg argue independent reinvention from heretical NT readings.
- **[[prince-of-darkness-manichaean]]** — *direct-influence?*: same transmission debate.
- **[[set]]** (Egyptian) — *parallel-motif* (no causal link): structural cosmic-antagonist role.

## Iconography
Cathar literature is almost entirely textual rather than iconographic — the Cathars rejected church imagery. No surviving authentic Cathar imagery of Satanael.

## Disputes
- **Manichaean genealogy.** The signature dispute. Stoyanov, Hamilton, Obolensky: genuine transmission. Moore, Pegg: independent emergence.
- **Mitigated vs. absolute dualism within the movement.** Bogomil mitigated dualism (Satanael fallen) vs. Cathar Albanense absolute dualism (two coeternal principles, no Satanael narrative needed). The Concorezzan Cathars kept the Bogomil position; the Albanenses moved to absolute dualism.
