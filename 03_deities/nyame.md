# Nyame

## Identity

*Onyame* (or *Nyame*; full honorific: *Onyankopon* — "the great one who alone is great") is the supreme being of the Akan peoples of Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire. Nyame is the ultimate creator and cosmic sovereign: the sky, rain, and sunlight are all direct expressions of Nyame's presence. Nyame is approached only obliquely — through the *abosom* (nature-spirit intermediaries) and through the *Okra* (divine spark) deposited in each person. The most widespread Adinkra symbol — *Gye Nyame* ("except God") — expresses Nyame's unparalleled supremacy: "I fear nothing except Nyame."

## Earliest attestation

Oral attestation across multiple Akan ethnic groups; documented in 19th-c. European missionary and colonial records (Basel Mission accounts from the 1830s onward); systematic scholarly analysis begins with Danquah (1944).

## Mythological role

Nyame created the cosmos and is its sustaining ground. The first humans are understood to have originally lived close to Nyame (some myths place humans in the sky with Nyame, and the earth as the place of exile — a myth structurally parallel to the Edenic expulsion). Nyame gives each person their *Okra* (divine spark / portion of Nyame) at birth and receives it back at death. Nyame is paired with [[asase-yaa]] (the Earth) as the fundamental divine couple: sky and earth, heaven and ground, the two poles of Akan cosmological existence.

## Cross-tradition identifications

- **[[olodumare]]** (Yoruba supreme being) — *scholarly-parallel*: both are supreme high-gods standing above pantheons of lesser intermediary deities, approached indirectly; both give a divine spark to each person at birth. Mbiti (1969) analyzes this pattern across over 270 African traditions. **Edge: `parallel-form`**.
- **Christian God / Allah** — *folk-syncretism*: Basel Mission and British colonial missionaries translated *Theos/Deus/God* as *Nyame*; Islamic *Allah* was similarly mapped. The translation was linguistically and theologically plausible because Nyame already satisfied the roles of creator, sovereign, and ultimate ground. See [[tradition-akan]].

## Iconography

Nyame is not represented in sculptural or pictorial form — aniconic, like [[olodumare]]. The *Nyame dua* (a forked branch with a clay pot filled with water, placed in the courtyard of a house) functions as a minimal shrine; the water collects the morning dew as a sign of Nyame's blessing.

## Disputes

- **Monotheism question:** Danquah (1944) argued Akan religion is monotheistic, with Nyame as the sole God and *abosom* merely ministers; critics (Wiredu 1980; Gyekye 1987) argue this is Christian-inflected and that the Akan system is better described as a hierarchical cosmology with a supreme being presiding over many divine agents. The current scholarly consensus acknowledges the monotheism framing as a legitimate *emic* position while maintaining the analytical distinction.
