# Ameretat

## Identity
**Ameretat** (Avestan *Amərətāt*, "Immortality / Deathlessness / Long-Life"; Pahlavi *Amurdād*; Modern Persian *Mordad*) is **the sixth of the six Amesha Spentas** and the **third of the three feminine members** of the heptad. Ameretat personifies **deathlessness** — the eschatological gift of unending life that the righteous receive at the cosmic renovation. Among the seven created elements assigned to the heptad, Ameretat presides over **plants**.

In nearly every liturgical context, Ameretat is **paired with [[haurvatat]] (Wholeness)** as the twin eschatological-reward-principles. The pair is so tight that scholars treat them as a single theological-eschatological unit.

## Earliest attestation
- **Gathas** ([[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]]): named throughout, almost always paired with Haurvatat.
- **Younger Avesta** ([[phase-2-004-yasna-younger-avesta]]): personified Amesha Spenta; the Haoma Yašt explicitly associates Ameretat-Haurvatat with the qualities the Haoma sacred-drink confers.
- **Pahlavi tradition**: Amurdād-Hordād remain paired through the Bundahishn + Denkard tradition.
- **Modern Iranian calendar**: *Mordad* (July–August) preserves the Amesha Spenta name in continuous Iranian identity.

## MASSIVE-WIN: Ameretat ↔ Vedic Amṛta ↔ Greek ambrosia
The Avestan **Amərətāt**, Vedic **Amṛta**, and Greek **ambrosia** are all direct cognates of **Proto-Indo-European *\*n̥-mr̥-tó-*** ("not-dying / un-death") — the negation of the root *\*mer-* "to die." This is **one of the cleanest single PIE etymological transmissions in the comparative-religion corpus**:

- **Vedic Amṛta** = the nectar-of-immortality the gods drink to remain deathless (the Indian counterpart to Soma).
- **Greek ambrosia** = the food/drink of the gods that confers immortality (Iliad, Odyssey, Homeric Hymns).
- **Avestan Amərətāt** = the personified-divine-principle of deathlessness in the Zoroastrian heptad.

All three derive from the same PIE root and preserve the same semantic core: **the substance / condition that defeats death**. The personification-into-Amesha-Spenta is distinctively Zoroastrian — the Vedic + Greek branches keep the term as substance-name rather than divine-person.

(Mayrhofer 1986-2001 *EWAia*; West 2007 *Indo-European Poetry and Myth* ch. 3.)

## Persian-period transmission: Ameretat → resurrection-of-the-dead
The Zoroastrian eschatological-immortality template — **deathlessness conferred at the cosmic-end** — is one of the four channels of Persian-period transmission Cohn (1993, *Cosmos, Chaos and the World to Come*) documents from Zoroastrianism into Second-Temple Judaism. The Jewish-Christian emergence of **bodily-resurrection doctrine** (Daniel 12:2, 2 Maccabees 7, 1 Corinthians 15) takes its structural shape from the Avestan Ameretat-at-Frashokereti template. The Avestan formulation predates the Jewish formulation by centuries; the Achaemenid-period contact window (-539 to -332) makes transmission plausible.

## The eucharistic substrate
The Avestan Haoma sacred-drink rite confers **Haurvatat + Ameretat** on the worshipper — making the rite a sacramental-anticipation of the eschatological-reward-pair. The pattern (sacred-drink-conferring-immortality) propagates through the Mithraic mysteries cultic adaptation into the Hellenistic-Mediterranean sacred-meal-template, contributing to the broader substrate from which the Christian eucharist eventually emerges.

## Disputes
- **Gathic personhood.** Whether Ameretat-Haurvatat are abstract-principles or personified-divine-beings in the Gathic stratum is debated; Boyce 1975 reads them as principle-with-implicit-personification.
- **Persian → Jewish bodily-resurrection scope.** How much of the Second-Temple shift toward bodily-resurrection eschatology is Persian-influenced vs. internal Jewish development is contested. Hultgård 1995 + Cohn 1993 favor substantial Persian contribution.
