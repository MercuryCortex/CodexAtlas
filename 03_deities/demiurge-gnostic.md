# The Demiurge (Gnostic)

In Gnostic literature — especially Sethian texts — the demiurge is the ignorant or malevolent craftsman who fashions the material cosmos in imitation of higher realities he does not understand. In the Apocryphon of John he is *Yaldabaoth*, the misshapen offspring of Sophia produced without a consort; he steals Sophia's power, fabricates the world and the seven archons, and declares "I am God and there is no other" (a polemic re-coding of Isaiah 45:5 / Exodus 20:3) — proof of his ignorance, since higher beings exist that he is unaware of. Valentinian Gnosticism softens the picture: the demiurge is psychic rather than evil, an unwitting agent of Sophia's redemption.

The figure is a *deliberate inversion* of two earlier figures: (a) Plato's demiurge of the Timaeus, the good craftsman who imitates the eternal Forms; (b) the Hebrew Bible's creator-god. The Sethian identification of the OT creator with an ignorant lower power is among the most consequential polemical moves of late antiquity — it is the most direct route by which a non-mainstream Christianity reads scripture against its plain sense.
