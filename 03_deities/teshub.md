# Teshub

## Identity
Teshub (Hurrian Teššub; Hittite cognate Tarḫunna; Luwian Tarhunza) is the storm-weather-warrior god and cosmic sovereign of the Hurrian and Hittite pantheons. He wields the thunderbolt, rides a chariot drawn by the divine bulls Šerišu ("Day") and Ḫurri ("Night"), and is enthroned as king of the gods after overthrowing his father Kumarbi in the Hurrian succession myth. His name and cult migrated outward from a Hurrian heartland (Upper Mesopotamia, the Khabur triangle, North Syria) into the Hittite empire, where he was syncretized with the older Anatolian Indo-European storm-god Tarḫunna and installed as the male partner of the Sun Goddess of Arinna in imperial cult.

Teshub is the *direct mythological source* for the Greek Zeus in the Hesiodic succession-myth chain. The Hurrian–Hittite Anu → Kumarbi → Teshub generational displacement is the structural template (and very likely the literary one) for Hesiod's Ouranos → Kronos → Zeus (Bachvarova 2016; West 1997). His Anatolian-Syrian continuum with Ugaritic Baʿlu / Hadad is documented in Late Bronze Age diplomatic and cultic texts (Schwemer 2001).

## Earliest attestation
- Hurrian theonyms attested in Old Assyrian colony texts (Kültepe / Kanesh, ~-1900 to -1750 BCE).
- Hittite Old Kingdom cult of the Storm-God: attested under Hattusili I (~-1650).
- The Kumarbi cycle compositions, preserved on tablets from the Boğazköy archive (Late Bronze Age Hattusa, ~-1400 to -1200), give the systematic theogony.
- Iconographic: Yazılıkaya rock-cut sanctuary outside Hattusa (~-1250), where Teshub is the central figure of the chamber-A procession, meeting Hebat on her panther.

## Mythological role
- **Storm-warrior.** Teshub kills the great dragon Illuyanka (Hittite Illuyanka Myth) — chaoskampf in its Anatolian form, recited at the Purulli spring festival.
- **Succession-myth victor.** In the *Song of Kumarbi* (Song of Going Forth), Teshub is born after his grandfather Anu's severed genitals (which Kumarbi has bitten off and swallowed) impregnate Kumarbi; Teshub eventually overthrows his father Kumarbi and assumes cosmic kingship.
- **Defender against chaos-monsters.** In the *Song of Hedammu* and *Song of Ullikummi* (Kumarbi cycle), Kumarbi engenders monstrous children (the sea-dragon Hedammu; the stone giant Ullikummi) to retake the throne; Teshub defeats them, retaining kingship.
- **Cultic counterpart of the Hittite king.** The Hittite Great King's titulature places him under the Storm-God of Hatti and the Sun Goddess of Arinna; treaties are sworn in their names.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[zeus]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: storm-warrior cosmic king attaining sovereignty by overthrowing father; the succession-myth chain Anu–Kumarbi–Teshub is the source-form of Ouranos–Kronos–Zeus (Bachvarova 2016; West 1997). Hesiod's Typhonomachy (Theog. 820–880) also derives from Teshub's combats with Hedammu / Ullikummi.
- **[[baal-hadad]]** — *ancient-identification*: continuous storm-god cult across the Anatolian–North Syrian zone; Hurrian theonyms appear in Ugaritic ritual texts, and the Aleppine Hadad / Ugaritic Baʿlu / Hittite Tarḫunna / Hurrian Teššub were treated as the same god in Late Bronze Age diplomatic and cultic practice (Schwemer 2001, 2008).
- **[[marduk]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: structural parallel as storm-warrior who attains cosmic kingship by defeating a chaos-monster.
- **Tarḫunta / Tarhunza (Luwian)** — *ancient-identification* within the Anatolian zone: same god, Luwian Indo-European name.

## Iconography
Striding warrior god, beard, conical horned cap, holding triple-pronged lightning-bundle and mace; often atop or accompanied by a bull, or driving a chariot drawn by his two bulls Šerišu and Ḫurri. The Yazılıkaya central panel is the canonical representation.

## Disputes
- **Indo-European vs. Hurrian origin.** Hittite Tarḫunna is Indo-European (PIE *terh₂- "to overpower"; cognate to Norse Thor / Þórr, Vedic Tarutar epithet). Hurrian Teššub is non-Indo-European. The Late Bronze Age imperial cult fused them. Scholars disagree on which name dominates which cult context (Schwemer 2001).
- **Date of the Kumarbi cycle.** Composition probably ~-1600 to -1400 in Hurrian; standard Hittite recensions ~-1400 to -1200. Whether the cycle is a Hurrian original translated into Hittite, or co-developed, is debated (Hoffner 1998; Bachvarova 2016).
