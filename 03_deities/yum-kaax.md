# Yum Kaax

The Yucatec Maya **god of the forest and of maize** — "Lord of the Forest" (*Yum K'aax*). Yum Kaax is the post-Classic Yucatec continuation of the Classic Maya Maize God tradition (see [[hun-hunahpu]] for the K'iche' / Classic Maya version of the same figure). The continuity is documented in iconography (the maize-foliated young male) and in Landa's 16th-century *Relación de las Cosas de Yucatán*, the principal early colonial source on Yucatec Maya religion.

Yum Kaax is the patron of the **milpa** — the traditional Maya slash-and-burn maize-cultivation field — and of the hunter's forest. The double association of forest-and-maize maps the Maya cultural relationship to landscape: the milpa is cut from the forest, the forest reclaims the milpa, the maize cycles through both states. Yum Kaax is therefore not just an agricultural deity but a *landscape-deity* whose presence the Maya farmer-hunter navigates between cleared and uncleared zones.

See [[centeotl]] for the Aztec male maize-god, [[corn-mother]] for the broader cross-Mesoamerican-and-North-American body-of-the-deity-is-the-cultivar pattern.
