# Vishvakarma

Vishvakarma (*Viśvakarman*, "the All-Maker," "All-Accomplishing") is the **divine architect and craftsman** of Hindu mythology — the artificer who fashions the wonders of the gods. He forges their weapons (above all the **vajra**, Indra's thunderbolt, a role he shares with the closely related smith-god **Tvaṣṭṛ**), builds their flying palaces and chariots, and raises the great mythic cities — golden **Lanka**, Krishna's **Dvaraka**, the Pandavas' **Indraprastha**. His roots are deep and lofty: in the late **Rigveda** (hymns 10.81–82) *Viśvakarman* is invoked as a **creator-demiurge** who shapes heaven and earth, "the one who, sacrificing, gave up all these worlds" — one of the earliest Indian cosmogonic visions, in which creation itself is divine craftsmanship. In living Hinduism he is the patron god of **artisans, engineers, smiths, and builders**, honoured at the annual **Vishwakarma Puja** when craftsmen worship their tools. Vishvakarma embodies the idea of the cosmos as a made thing — and of skilled making as a sacred act.
