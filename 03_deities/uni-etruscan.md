# Uni

## Identity
Uni is the supreme goddess of the Etruscan pantheon: queen of heaven, divine consort of **[[tinia]]**, and third member of the Etruscan Capitoline Triad alongside **[[menvra-etruscan]]**. She is the Etruscan counterpart of Greek **[[hera]]** and the direct progenitor of Roman **[[juno]]** — the name *Iuno* (Juno) almost certainly derives from *Uni* through Etruscan-to-Latin phonological transmission. Uni presided over marriage, sovereignty, the protection of cities, and the lives of women. She was worshipped across Etruria as a civic protector goddess, and her most famous shrine at Pyrgi (the port of Caere, modern Cerveteri) is the site of one of the most extraordinary documents in ancient Mediterranean religion.

## Earliest attestation
- Etruscan votive inscriptions naming Uni appear from the 6th century BCE onward.
- The **Pyrgi Tablets** (~-500 BCE): three gold tablets found in 1964 at the site of ancient Pyrgi, the sanctuary-port of Caere. Two tablets are in Etruscan, one in Phoenician. The texts record the dedication of a sanctuary by Thefarie Velianas, king of Caere, and the Phoenician tablet explicitly equates the goddess of the sanctuary with **Astarte**. The Etruscan tablets name the sanctuary as belonging to Uni. This bilingual equation — Uni = Astarte — is among the most significant surviving documents of Etruscan religion and Phoenician-Etruscan contact.
- Etruscan tomb paintings and terracotta votive figurines from c. 550–300 BCE depict Uni in the Hera-type: crowned, veiled, enthroned.

## Mythological role
Uni's mythological role converges with that of Hera in the Hellenized Etruscan tradition: divine queen, consort of the sky-father, protector of legitimate marriage and royal/civic legitimacy. She could extend divine protection (*fulgur conditum* — buried lightning) over cities and sanction the rule of kings, as documented in the Pyrgi dedication. In Etruscan tomb art, Uni sometimes appears with Hercle (Heracles), nursing him in a scene of divine adoption — a scene paralleling the Greek myth of Heracles suckling from Hera, possibly via direct Etruscan adoption of Greek iconographic programs.

## Cross-tradition identifications
The Pyrgi Tablets create a documented transmission chain:

- **[[astarte-canaanite]]** — *ancient-identification* (~-500 BCE, Pyrgi Tablets): the only contemporary bilingual document equating an Etruscan goddess with a Semitic deity. Uni **is** Astarte in the Pyrgi sanctuary context.
- **[[juno]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Roman goddess Juno is the direct descendant of Uni; the name, function, and Capitoline Triad position transferred wholesale when Rome absorbed Etruscan religious structures.
- **[[hera]]** — *ancient-identification*: Etruscan *interpretatio Graeca* identified Uni with Hera; Uni's iconography in Etruscan art is the Hera-type (crown, scepter, veil).
- **[[ishtar-akkadian]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: via the Astarte identification: Astarte is the Levantine form of Ishtar (Babylonian) = **[[inanna-sumerian]]** (Sumerian). The Pyrgi Tablets place Uni at the western terminus of the Inanna → Ishtar → Astarte → Uni → Juno transmission chain.
- **[[aphrodite-greek]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Astarte was also identified with Aphrodite in Greek contexts; the Uni-Astarte equation thus touches the Aphrodite cluster as well.

## Iconography
Uni is depicted as a queenly mature woman: crowned (often with a polos or diadem), veiled, seated on a throne or standing with a scepter. The lion appears as her symbol in some contexts. The pomegranate, associated with fertility and marriage, accompanies her in votive figurines.

## Disputes
- **Etymology of "Uni"**: disputed; possible connections to Latin *iuno* (a woman's vital spirit or genius), to an Etruscan root, or even to PIE *yeu-* ("vital force"). No consensus.
- **Pyrgi scope**: the Pyrgi equation applies to a specific sanctuary context (~-500 BCE) run by a king with Phoenician trading contacts. Whether Uni was universally identified with Astarte across Etruria or whether this was local diplomatic-religious syncretism is debated.
