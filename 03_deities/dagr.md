# Dagr

## Identity

Dagr (Old Norse **Dagr**, "Day") is the Norse personification of the daylight — the son of [[nott-norse]] (Night) and the Aesir god **Delling** ("the shining one," associated with dawn). Described as "bright and fair after his father's kin," Dagr is the daytime that follows his mother's night around the wheel of the sky.

## Mythological role

Dagr's role is set out in Snorri's *Gylfaginning* (drawing on the Eddic poem *Vafþrúðnismál*). After Night (Nott) bears Dagr to Delling, the All-Father places both mother and son in the heavens, each with a horse and chariot, to ride around the earth every twenty-four hours in succession. Dagr drives the horse **Skinfaxi** ("shining-mane"), and "from his mane is shed light over all the earth and sky" — the brightness of the horse's mane being the light of day itself. Day rides after Night, so that the alternation of the two is simply the orderly procession of these two sky-riders. Dagr is thus less a personality than a cosmological mechanism — the divinization of daylight as a rider in the sky.

## Cross-tradition significance

Dagr completes the Norse **day–night pair** with his mother [[nott-norse]], paralleling the Greek pairing in which [[erebus]] (Darkness) and [[nyx-primordial]] (Night) beget Aether and Hemera (Day). The conception of daylight as a luminous horse-drawn rider crossing the heavens — distinct from the sun-disk (Sól) itself — reflects the characteristic Norse and broader Indo-European habit of dividing the bright sky into multiple personified powers (Day, Sun, Dawn) and explaining their regularity through chariot-journeys across the firmament.
