# Kamadhenu

Kamadhenu (*Kāmadhenu*, "the wish-fulfilling cow"; also **Surabhi**, "the fragrant") is the **divine cow** of Hindu mythology — the miraculous provider who yields not only endless milk and ghee but **any desire** asked of her, and who is conceived as containing **all the gods within her body** (each part of the cow a deity), making her the theological foundation of the Hindu sanctity of the cow. She emerges, in the great cosmogonic churning of the ocean (**Samudra Manthan**), among the treasures drawn up from the sea, and is entrusted to the sages. As the wish-cow **Nandini** in the hermitage of the sage Jamadagni, her attempted theft by the tyrant-king Kartavirya is the spark that ignites **[[parashurama]]**'s war on the warrior caste; as **Vasishtha**'s cow she likewise defends her master against royal greed. The mother of all cattle, Kamadhenu is the emblem of *daana* (sacred giving), abundance, and the cow's protected status that runs so deep in Hindu life.
