# Selket

## Identity
**Selket** (Egyptian *Srqt*, often vocalised *Serqet*; Greek *Selkis*) is the Egyptian **scorpion-goddess**, a protective and funerary deity of the very first rank attested continuously from the Predynastic / Proto-Dynastic period (~-3100) through the end of the Ptolemaic era. Her full title *Srqt-ḥtyt* — "she who causes the throat to breathe" — encodes her dual function as **bringer of venom** and **withholder of venom**: the scorpion-sting that closes the throat is hers, and so therefore is its antidote. She is one of the **four canopic-shrine sisters** (with [[isis-egyptian]], [[nephthys]], and [[neith]]) who stand outstretched-armed around the gilded shrine containing the embalmed viscera of the dead, and one of the principal protectors of the deceased pharaoh in the Pyramid Texts. In magical-medical practice she is **nurse of [[horus]] in infancy** and patron of the *cippus*-priests who treat venomous bites and stings. For the Codex Atlas's astrology mission Selket is the canonical **Egyptian scorpion-deity** sitting in the **Mars-ruled Scorpio decans** (decan 22 in the Senenmut decan-list, confirmed in Dendera; see [`_assets/data/astrology-decans.json`](../_assets/data/astrology-decans.json)), and the natural anchor for any Mars-wedge that needs a scorpion-archetype divine figure.

## Earliest attestation
- **Predynastic / Early Dynastic (~-3100 to -2700)** — the scorpion-glyph appears on royal name-cartouches ("King Scorpion" of Hierakonpolis on the *Scorpion Macehead*, c. -3200, though the relationship to the goddess proper is iconographic-onomastic rather than fully theological). Wilkinson 2003 pp. 233–234.
- **Old Kingdom — [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] (~-2400 to -2150)** — Selket appears repeatedly as protectress of the deceased pharaoh, alongside Isis and Nephthys; *PT* Utterance 690 invokes her with Neith as the goddesses "of the south and west" of the canopic shrine (the orientation varies across tombs). Faulkner 1969 / Allen 2005 (*Ancient Egyptian Pyramid Texts*) translations.
- **Middle Kingdom — [[phase-1-009-coffin-texts]] (~-2050 to -1750)** — Serqet continues as canopic protector and as a venom-magician; *CT* spells in the *Magical Papyrus Harris* genre invoke her against scorpion-stings.
- **New Kingdom — [[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]] (~-1550 to -50)** — depicted on funerary papyri among the four canopic goddesses; spell BD 151 (the "Mummification Chamber" spell) names her.
- **Tutankhamun's tomb (~-1323)** — the **golden canopic shrine** of [[tutankhamun]] is flanked on its four sides by free-standing gilded statues of Isis, Nephthys, Neith, and Serqet, each c. 90 cm tall with outstretched protective arms. Selket occupies the west side; she wears the scorpion atop her head and faces inward toward the canopic chest containing the king's viscera. This is the **single most-photographed cult-image of Selket** and the centrepiece of her global iconographic recognition (Carter 1923; Eaton-Krauss & Graefe 1985 *Tutankhamun's Tomb Series* vol. 2; see [[event-tutankhamun-tomb-discovery-1922]]).
- **Late Period (~-664 to -332)** — major Saite + 30th-Dynasty revival of Serqet's magical-medical cult; the *Metternich Stela* (Nectanebo II, ~-360 to -343) is the densest single Late-Period attestation of Serqet-as-venom-curer.
- **Ptolemaic-Roman (~-332 to -30 and beyond)** — Greek inscriptions render her *Selkis*; the cult continues through the closure of Egyptian temple religion.

## Mythological role
- **The four canopic protectresses.** Selket guards the canopic jar of *Qebehsenuef* (the falcon-headed Son of Horus who protects the **intestines**). The quartet — Isis / Duamutef-stomach, Nephthys / Hapi-lungs, Neith / Imsety-liver, Selket / Qebehsenuef-intestines — is one of the oldest stable iconographic-ritual ensembles in Egyptian religion. The assignments shift slightly across tombs and periods (the Tutankhamun shrine for example has Selket on the west protecting the south-facing Qebehsenuef jar), but the **four-fold quartet** is invariant.
- **Nurse of Horus.** In the *Metternich Stela* and related *cippi-of-Horus* magical texts, Serqet is one of the seven scorpions who accompany Isis as she hides the infant Horus in the Delta marshes from Set's assassins. The Horus-cippus genre — small portable stelae showing the child Horus trampling scorpions and crocodiles — is essentially Serqet's amulet-magic stabilised in stone. Pinch 2002 p. 188.
- **Venom-master.** The fundamental theological logic: she who brings the sting also withholds it. Egyptian magical-medical practice (the *Brooklyn Papyrus*, the *Metternich Stela*, the *Magical Papyrus Harris*) consistently invokes Serqet for protection against venomous bites; the priests who treat such bites are called *kherep Serqet* — "controllers of Serqet."
- **Pyramid-Texts protector.** In PT Utterance 690 and parallels, Selket is named alongside the other three canopic sisters as guardian of the king's body in the afterlife. The *PT* establish the canonical canopic quartet by the end of the Old Kingdom.
- **Pharaonic protector in life.** Royal regalia and amulets bearing Selket's scorpion-glyph were worn by living pharaohs as protective magic; the scorpion atop the king's name-shrine is a continuous Egyptian iconographic device.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ishara]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Ishara is the major Mesopotamian / Hurrian / Hittite scorpion-goddess (Akkadian *Išḫara*), invoked in oath-curses and depicted with the scorpion symbol. The Selket / Ishara pairing is **structural, not transmitted**: scorpion-as-feminine-divine-emblem appears independently in the Nile Valley and Mesopotamia, both arising from the same ecological reality (deadly scorpions in arid south-eastern Mediterranean / Near East). Black & Green 1992 *Gods, Demons and Symbols of Ancient Mesopotamia* p. 110; Wilkinson 2003 p. 233. Default edge type: `parallel-form`. **No documented transmission**.
- **[[scorpion]] (the symbol-node) / Greek Skorpios** — *iconographic-parallel*: the Greek constellation **Scorpius** (the scorpion that slew Orion, per Hesiod *Astronomia* fr. 4 and Eratosthenes *Catasterismi* 7) sits opposite Taurus on the celestial sphere. The Egyptian decanic system maps Selket onto the Scorpio decans (see [`astrology-decans.json`](../_assets/data/astrology-decans.json) decan 22, Senenmut TT 353 ~-1473) — but the Greek and Egyptian scorpion-imagery are independent; the Hellenistic decanic synthesis brought them into the same frame via Liber Hermetis and Picatrix (Greer & Warnock 2011 *The Complete Picatrix*). See [[scorpion]] for the cross-tradition cells.
- **The four canopic-protectresses ensemble** — *iconographic-link* to [[isis-egyptian]], [[nephthys]], [[neith]]: a stable Egyptian iconographic quartet from Old Kingdom to Roman period; Selket's individuality as scorpion-goddess sits inside this ensemble.

## Iconography
- Tall feminine figure in long sheath dress, frequently with **outstretched arms** in the canopic-shrine pose (Tutankhamun shrine; many New Kingdom papyri).
- **Scorpion atop the head** is the canonical signifier; the scorpion is always shown **without its sting** in protective contexts (the sting is symbolically muted to indicate Serqet's withholding-aspect).
- Ankh and was-sceptre in her hands in many depictions; sometimes a was-sceptre alone.
- Tutankhamun shrine: gilded wood, ~90 cm tall, eyes inlaid, the scorpion crisply modelled atop the long tripartite wig.

## Astrological / decanic placement
- **Senenmut decan-list (TT 353, ~-1473)** decan 22 (= Scorpio decan 1, 0°–10° tropical, **Mars-ruled face** in the Chaldean order) gives Selket as the decan-deity association. The Dendera ceiling (~-50) and Sety I tomb (~-1290) preserve the same alignment with minor name-variants. (Neugebauer & Parker 1960–69 *Egyptian Astronomical Texts* III.)
- This places Selket squarely in the **Mars wedge** of the classical planetary-deity table — Mars rules Scorpio in classical astrology, and Selket sits in the Mars-decan-of-Mars face. For the Codex Atlas's Astrology→Pantheon Mars cluster, Selket is the natural Egyptian scorpion-deity anchor (alongside [[seth]] for Mars's storm-violence aspect).
- The Antares cross-tradition convergence (Al-Qalb / Xīn / Kardia — "the heart of the scorpion") sits in decan 18 (Virgo decan 3) per the Manazil / Xiu / Greek traditions; see [`astrology-decans.json`](../_assets/data/astrology-decans.json) decan 18 for the MASSIVE convergence note.

## Disputes
- **Canopic-orientation variability.** Different tombs assign the four sisters to different cardinal points around the canopic shrine; the Tutankhamun shrine places Selket on the west, but Saqqara private tombs of the same period vary. Eaton-Krauss & Graefe 1985 documents the variations.
- **Scorpion-king onomastic continuity.** Whether the Predynastic "Scorpion King" of Hierakonpolis was theologically connected to Selket-as-goddess, or whether the scorpion-glyph was purely a royal name-symbol that later acquired goddess-association, is debated. Wilkinson 2003 treats them as iconographically continuous but ontologically distinct.
- **Late-Period magical-medical synthesis.** The Late-Period intensification of Serqet-magic (Metternich Stela; Brooklyn Papyrus snake / scorpion magic) is in part a defensive reaction to Persian and then Greek pressure; Frankfurter 1998 *Religion in Roman Egypt* reads it as cult-revival rather than continuous tradition. The scholarly consensus is that the cult was continuous but its prominence varied.
