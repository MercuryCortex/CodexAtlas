# Watatsumi

## Identity
Watatsumi (Ōwatatsumi) is the great Shintō **sea-kami** — the sovereign of the ocean and its tides, who dwells in the **Ryūgū-jō**, the dragon-palace beneath the waves. He is widely identified with [[ryujin]], the dragon-king of the sea, the two names naming a single oceanic sovereignty.

## Earliest attestation
Born, in the *Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki* accounts, of [[izanagi]] in the generation of sea, river, and mountain kami. His cult is strong among fishing communities and at the Shiga and Watatsumi shrines.

## Mythological role — the dragon-palace and the tide-jewels
Watatsumi's great narrative is the tale of **Hoori (Yamasachi-hiko)**, who loses his brother's fishhook and descends to the dragon-palace, where he is received by Watatsumi, marries the sea-king's daughter **Toyotama-hime**, and is given the **tide-jewels** (kanju and manju) that raise and lower the sea — by which he masters his brother. Through this line Watatsumi becomes a divine ancestor of the imperial house. The undersea palace, the time that runs differently below the waves, and the sea-king's daughter are motifs that recur across maritime mythologies.

## Cross-tradition
Watatsumi is the Shintō member of the **sea-sovereign** type — the god who rules the deep, commands the tides, and holds court in a palace beneath the waters — structurally parallel to the Greek [[poseidon]] and the Roman Neptune. Within Shintō he merges with the dragon-king [[ryujin]].

## Iconography
A dragon or a robed sea-king enthroned in a coral palace; the tide-jewels; attended by fish, turtles, and the creatures of the sea.
