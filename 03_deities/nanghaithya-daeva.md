# Nāŋhaiθya (the Discontent-Daeva)

**Nāŋhaiθya** (Avestan; Pahlavi *Nāhayya*) is **one of the principal daevas** (demonized divine beings) in the Zoroastrian-reform pantheon — the cosmic-counter-principle to **[[spenta-armaiti]]** (Holy Devotion, the fourth Amesha Spenta) + structurally paired with the Vedic divine twin **Nāsatyas** (the Aśvins) as **one of the cleanest documented cases of the asura-deva inversion**.

## Earliest attestation

- **Gathas** ([[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]]): Yasna 30.6, where Nāŋhaiθya is listed among the daevas the Gathic-Zarathustra rejects.
- **Vendidad (Vidēvdad) 19.43, 10.9**: the daevic-pantheon lists.
- **Pahlavi Bundahishn 27**: the developed daevic-cosmology with Nāŋhaiθya/Nāhayya placed as a principal demon.

## MASSIVE-WIN: Nāŋhaiθya = Nāsatya — the cleanest asura-deva inversion case

The Avestan Nāŋhaiθya is the **demonized-Avestan counterpart of the Vedic Nāsatyas** (the Aśvins, the divine twin healer-deities of Vedic religion). The cognate-pair is documented:

- **Vedic Nāsatyas** (RV 1.180, 2.41, 8.5, 8.8 — hundreds of Rigvedic hymns address them): beloved horse-riding twin gods, healers + rescuers, hugely popular figures who exemplify divine-aid-in-distress.
- **Avestan Nāŋhaiθya**: a single demonized figure; one of the principal daevas opposing the Amesha Spentas.

Both derive from **Proto-Indo-Iranian *\*nasatya-*** ("true / saving"). The cognate-pair is one of the **cleanest documented examples of the asura-deva inversion** ([[asura-deva-inversion]]) in action — at the level of **named-individual-cognate-deity**, not just at the class-level (asura/ahura vs. deva/daeva inversion).

The case demonstrates that the Zoroastrian-reform was **not merely a re-categorization of divine-classes** but an active **demonization of specific named deities** that had been beloved Vedic figures. The Vedic divine-twin healer-deities (the Aśvins) — universally beloved on the Indian branch — are reduced to a single principal-daeva on the Iranian branch.

(West 2007 *Indo-European Poetry and Myth* ch. 7; Skjærvø 1993 EIr 'Daēva'; Mayrhofer 1986-2001 *EWAia*.)

## The principal-daevas pantheon

Nāŋhaiθya is part of the developed Pahlavi daevic-pantheon (Bundahishn 27) which mirrors the Amesha Spenta heptad — each principal daeva opposes one of the Amesha Spentas:

| Amesha Spenta (good) | Principal daeva (cosmic-counter) |
|---|---|
| [[asha-vahishta]] (Truth) | Indra (the Vedic king-of-gods, demonized) |
| [[vohu-manah]] (Good Mind) | Akōman (Bad Mind) |
| [[khshathra-vairya]] (Desirable Dominion) | Sauruva / Sarva (the Vedic Rudra/Śarva, demonized) |
| [[spenta-armaiti]] (Holy Devotion) | **Nāŋhaiθya** (Discontent) |
| [[haurvatat]] (Wholeness) | Tauruvi (Destroyer-of-Wholeness) |
| [[ameretat]] (Immortality) | Zairi (Destroyer-of-Immortality) |
| (Spenta Mainyu / Ahura Mazda) | [[angra-mainyu-ahriman]] (the cosmic-evil-spirit) |

The mirror-pantheon arrangement makes the Zoroastrian daevic-system a **complete inversion of the orthodox-Mazdean cosmic-order**.

## Disputes

- **Exact semantic.** Whether "Nāŋhaiθya" means "discontent" specifically (the standard interpretation) vs. some other negative-state is debated; the etymology is preserved from the Vedic Nāsatya but the Zoroastrian-reform context demands the negative-semantic.
- **Single figure vs. demonized twin-pair.** Whether Avestan Nāŋhaiθya is the demonized-version of the singular Vedic Nāsatya-name (covering both twins) or just one of the Aśvins is debated. Most scholarship favors the singular-name-covering-both interpretation.
