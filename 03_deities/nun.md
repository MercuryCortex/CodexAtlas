# Nun

Nun is the personified primordial waters of Egyptian cosmogony — the undifferentiated, dark, infinite ocean that exists *before* the first creation and that continues to surround the ordered cosmos. He is not so much an object of cult as a cosmological principle: no temple, no priesthood, no festival. From Nun emerges [[atum]] on the first mound (the *benben*), initiating the Heliopolitan creation sequence. Each night the solar boat of [[ra]] is taken through the realm of Nun in its passage through the underworld; each morning Nun lifts the boat back into the sky for the new day's voyage. The cosmos as the Egyptians imagine it is a bubble of ordered being held inside the infinite waters of Nun — a remarkably stark cosmological geometry.

## Mythological role
Nun functions as the cosmic origin and the cosmic surround. The Hermopolitan theology elaborated him into one of the four primordial pairs of the **Ogdoad** — Nun and Naunet (the waters), Heh and Hauhet (infinity), Kek and Kauket (darkness), Amun and Amaunet (hiddenness) — four male-female pairs of frog-headed and snake-headed deities that represent the eight aspects of pre-creation chaos from which the first sun rises at Hermopolis. In the **Destruction of Mankind** myth, when humanity rebels against Ra, the aged sun-god convenes the gods *in Nun* — the primordial waters serve as a place outside of ordinary cosmos where the most fundamental decisions are taken. Nun's structural parallel with Mesopotamian [[apsu]]/[[tiamat]] and with Hebrew *tehôm* (the "deep" of Gen 1:2) is one of the clearest cross-Near-Eastern cosmogonic continuities, though Egyptian and Mesopotamian cosmogonies differ sharply in *what happens* to the primordial waters (Egyptian: emergence and orderly differentiation; Mesopotamian: combat and dismemberment).
