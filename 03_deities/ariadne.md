# Ariadne

## Identity
Daughter of King Minos of Crete and Pasiphaë, who gave Theseus the *clew* (ball of thread) by which he navigated and exited the Cretan labyrinth after slaying the Minotaur. Theseus abandoned her on Naxos; there she was found by Dionysus, who married her and elevated her — her bridal crown becoming the constellation *Corona Borealis* (catasterism). Ariadne is poised between mortal heroine and goddess — the Cretan-Mediterranean background of the figure is layered: a likely Minoan-Mycenaean goddess in origin (the *Linear B* Knossos tablet records *a-da-ra-ka* possibly her name), absorbed into the Theseus narrative as heroine and into the Dionysian cult as divine consort.

## Earliest attestation
- Linear B Mycenaean material (debated identification with later Ariadne).
- Homeric *Iliad* and *Odyssey* — incidental mentions.
- Hesiod and the Cypria.
- Catullus 64 and Ovid *Metamorphoses* 8 give the most-read literary versions of her abandonment-and-divine-rescue.

## Mythological role
- Cretan princess who enables Theseus's escape from the labyrinth.
- Bride of Dionysus, immortalized in the heavens.
- Cultic figure on Naxos and Crete; the Ariadne-Dionysus wedding was the focus of regular ritual celebration.

## Iconography
The labyrinth thread; the constellation crown; the sleeping figure (commonly depicted) discovered by the arriving Dionysus.
