# Lucifer

## Identity
The name **Lucifer** ("Light-Bringer") is the Latin Vulgate (Jerome, ~380 CE) translation of Hebrew *Heylel ben-Shahar* in Isaiah 14:12 — originally a taunt against a Babylonian king or **Helel** the morning-star deity of Canaanite-Syrian astral religion, but read by Tertullian, Origen, and Augustine as referring to **the fall of Satan**. The reading is **interpretive**, not philological: the Hebrew Isaiah passage names no rebel angel. But Jerome's Latin rendering crystallized "Lucifer" as a proper name for the pre-fall Satan, and from there into the entire Western Christian imagination.

## Mythological role
**Patristic**: Tertullian *Adv. Marc.* 5.11.11, Origen *De Princ.* 1.5.5, Augustine *City of God* 11.15 — the canonical interpretation that Isaiah 14 describes Satan's fall from heaven through pride. **Dante**: *Inferno* 34 places Lucifer at the bottom of Hell, three-faced and frozen in ice, gnawing on Judas, Brutus, and Cassius. **Milton**: *Paradise Lost* (1667) gives Lucifer the most rhetorically magnificent speeches in English literature — "Better to reign in Hell than serve in Heaven" — beginning the **Romantic-era inversion** by which Lucifer becomes the heroic light-bringer, the rebel against tyrannical sovereignty. **Romantic reception**: Blake, Shelley, Byron, Anatole France — Lucifer as the figure of Enlightenment, rebellion against received authority, technical and intellectual fire-bringing (the explicit Prometheus equation). **Modern Satanism** (Anton LaVey 1966; Church of Satan; Temple of Set; Joy of Satan etc.) — adopts "Lucifer" *not* the diabolical "Satan" precisely to leverage this Romantic light-bringer / liberator framing.
