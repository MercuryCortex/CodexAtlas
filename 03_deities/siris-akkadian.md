# Siris

## Identity
Siris (also Siraš, Sirash) is the Akkadian goddess of beer and brewing, the functional counterpart of Sumerian [[ninkasi]] in the Akkadian-language stratum of Mesopotamian religion. She is invoked in tavern-songs, in beer-ration administrative formulae, and in the daily-offering lists of the Babylonian temples — beer being one of the two staple components (with bread) of the Mesopotamian sacrificial menu. Unlike Ninkasi, who has a major dedicated hymn (the *Hymn to Ninkasi*), Siris has no equivalent extended textual monument; her presence is principally onomastic and cultic. The relationship between Ninkasi and Siris is one of the better-attested examples of the Sumerian-Akkadian theonym doublet: in some Old Babylonian god lists they are treated as identifications of one goddess, in others as a pair (mother-daughter or older-and-younger doublet), and in still others as essentially interchangeable.

## Earliest attestation
- Old Babylonian god lists name Siris alongside (or as identification of) Ninkasi.
- The *Drinking Song* (ETCSL 5.7.3) names both Ninkasi and Siris in tavern context.
- Akkadian beer-incantations and tavern songs invoke her.
- Personal names compounded with Siris (e.g., *Siris-ummi*, "Siris is my mother") attest her cultic recognition in private piety from the OB period onward.
- Neo-Assyrian and Neo-Babylonian temple-offering lists include beer-offerings to Siris.

## Mythological role
Siris does not have her own narrative myth. Her role is **cultic and onomastic**:
- ***Beer-personification***: she is the divine personification of beer itself; brewers and tavern-keepers invoke her in the daily round.
- ***Offering recipient***: in temple-offering routines beer is offered with her as nominal divine recipient (alongside or as Ninkasi).
- ***Tavern-song presence***: she is named in the Sumerian *Drinking Song* and in later Akkadian tavern-genre texts.
- ***Onomastic theophoric***: personal names compounded with Siris indicate genuine devotional recognition of her, not merely symbolic personification.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ninkasi]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Sumerian counterpart. The Ninkasi/Siris pair is among the better-attested cases of Sumerian-Akkadian theonym doublet; the linguistic distinction may have outlasted any theological distinction, with both names usable in Akkadian-language texts.
- *Scholarly parallel*: Egyptian Tenenet/Tjenenet (a brewing-and-childbirth goddess) and the Egyptian Hathor in her brewing-intoxication aspect (the *Destruction of Mankind* myth) are sometimes compared cross-culturally; these are *scholarly-parallel*, not ancient identifications.

## Iconography
Limited dedicated iconography. The beer-vat (in cuneiform a recognizable pictograph and divine-determinative attribute) is her sign; she appears in seal scenes accompanying drinking and feasting iconography, indistinguishable from Ninkasi when distinction is even attempted.

## Disputes
- **Siris vs. Ninkasi — one goddess or two?** The Mesopotamian brewing-divinity complex has Siris/Siraš and Ninkasi as the two principal names. In some Old Babylonian god lists they are explicitly identified; in others they appear as a pair (sometimes mother and daughter, with Siris as the elder); in still others they are interchangeable. Civil 1964 leans toward identity at the cultic level with linguistic-doublet preservation; Stol 1994 treats the OB evidence as preserving an originally distinct pair that increasingly merged. The vault separates them per the splitting rule — Ninkasi as the Sumerian, Siris as the Akkadian — with the ancient-identification edge capturing the merger.
- **Gender of Siras/Siraš in some sources**. A few Akkadian sources appear to treat *dSiraš* as masculine — possibly reflecting a separate male beer-god (the "beer-mate" of Siris), or a textual error, or genuine gender-fluidity of the personification. The majority of attestations are feminine.
