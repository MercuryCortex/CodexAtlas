# Orisha Oko

Orisha Oko (*Òrìṣà Oko*, "the orisha of the farm") is the Yoruba **orisha of agriculture and the cultivated earth** — patron of farmers, of the fertility of the soil, and of the **harvest**, especially the all-important new **yam**. His cult, centered historically on the town of **Irawo**, made him one of the great agrarian deities of Yorubaland, to whom the prosperity of the fields was owed and whose festivals marked the agricultural year. Like many earth-and-farming deities he is also a guardian of **morality and social order**: Orisha Oko was invoked to **settle disputes and adjudicate accusations** (notably charges of witchcraft), the fertile earth standing as a witness to truth and a punisher of the wicked. His emblems join fertility to iron — a hoe, an iron staff — linking him to the wider Yoruba complex of cultivation and to **[[ogun|Ogun]]**, lord of iron. In domain he is the Yoruba counterpart of earth-and-agriculture powers across West Africa, such as the Igbo **[[ala-igbo|Ala]]**. Orisha Oko embodies the sacred dependence of Yoruba life on the farmed, generous, truth-keeping earth.
