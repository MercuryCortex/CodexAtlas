# Lamashtu

Lamashtu (*Lamaštu*; Sumerian **Dimme**) is the dreaded Mesopotamian **demoness who preys on pregnant women, mothers, and newborns** — the cause assigned to miscarriage, stillbirth, puerperal fever, and crib-death. Uniquely among the maleficent powers, she is a goddess in her own right, the **daughter of [[an-sumerian|Anu]]** cast out of heaven, who acts on her own malignant will rather than at the gods' command. She is depicted as a horror: a lion- or bird-headed female with a hairy body, donkey's teeth, and taloned hands, suckling a pig and a dog while clutching serpents and standing on a donkey in a boat bound for the underworld. Against her the Mesopotamians deployed an enormous **apotropaic apparatus** — incantations, amulets, and, most famously, the lesser demon **[[pazuzu]]**, whose own hideousness was turned to protective use: Pazuzu-amulets and plaques show him driving Lamashtu back down to the netherworld, guarding the household and the birthing-room. As the archetypal child-threatening night-demoness she stands behind the later **[[lilith]]**, the Greek Lamia and Gello, and a long folklore of infant-stealing spirits.
