# Tāne

## Identity
Māori god of the forest, of trees and birds, and (in most traditions) the deity who effected the separation of Ranginui and Papatūānuku. After the separation, Tāne ascended to fetch the three baskets of knowledge from Io (in traditions including Io) or from the highest heavens (in traditions without Io); he created the first woman, Hine-ahu-one, from earth, breathed life into her, and from their union descended humanity.

## Earliest attestation
Māori oral tradition; documented in 19th-c. compilations.

## Mythological role
The principal action of Tāne is the *separation* of Ranginui and Papatūānuku — he lay on his back and pushed with his legs against the sky, forcing his parents apart. He also created the first woman Hine-ahu-one; their daughter Hinetītama (the Dawn Maiden) became Tāne's consort, then fled in shame to the underworld when she discovered his identity, becoming Hine-nui-te-pō ("Great Hine of the Night"), goddess of death.

## Iconography
Forests and trees. The kauri (giant New Zealand tree) is sometimes called Tāne Mahuta directly — a specific named ancient kauri in Waipoua Forest, Northland, is known by that name.
