# Shakti

Shakti (Sanskrit *Śakti*, "power, energy, capacity") is the **divine feminine power** of Hinduism — the dynamic, creative energy that animates the cosmos and the gods, and, in the **Shakta** traditions, the supreme Goddess (*Mahadevi*, *Adi Parashakti*) who is herself the ultimate reality. As a theological principle, Shakti is the **active** counterpart to **[[shiva]]**'s static consciousness: a famous Tantric maxim holds that "without Śakti, Śiva is but a corpse" (*śiva* without *śakti* is *śava*), and the half-male, half-female **Ardhanarishvara** icon makes the two one. Every god has his Shakti — Vishnu's Lakshmi, Brahma's Saraswati — for it is the goddess-power that lets him act. In full **Shaktism**, crystallized in the **Devi Mahatmya** (c. 500–600 CE), this is taken to its conclusion: all the great goddesses — **[[durga]]**, **[[kali]]**, **[[parvati]]**, Lakshmi, Saraswati — are forms of the one Shakti, who is declared the source of the male gods themselves and the highest *brahman*. Her Vedic ancestor is **[[vac-goddess|Vāc]]** (Speech), who in the Rigveda's Devīsūkta already proclaims herself the sovereign power moving through all the gods. Shakti is also the inner energy of the body (*kundalini*), coiled at the base of the spine, whose awakening is the goal of Tantric yoga.
