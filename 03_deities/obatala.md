# Obàtálá

## Identity
The "King of the White Cloth" — orisha of creation (specifically the moulding of human bodies from clay), purity, wisdom, elder-authority, and the cool/calm. Obàtálá is sometimes named *Òrìṣàńlá* ("the great orisha"), reflecting his theological seniority among orishas. He is regularly called by Olódùmarè to act on his behalf in cosmic matters.

## Earliest attestation
Documented in Yoruba oral tradition; Ifá corpus references throughout; 19th-c. European sources record his cult at Ife (the sacred-mythic origin city, where his principal shrine remains).

## Mythological role
In a major Ifá creation narrative (preserved in the *odu Ogbe Yono* and parallel verses), Olódùmarè entrusts Obàtálá with descending from heaven on a chain to create earth from primordial water; Obàtálá becomes drunk on palm wine and falls asleep; Odùduwà takes the task and creates earth instead — the foundation of a major Yoruba political-religious tension. Obàtálá awakens, is given charge of moulding human bodies, and ever after refuses palm wine (his devotees abstain). The drunken-Obàtálá narrative also explains the existence of disabled humans (those Obàtálá moulded while intoxicated) — these are regarded as sacred to Obàtálá and protected.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Our Lady of Mercy (Las Mercedes)** — *folk-syncretism* (Cuban Santería): Obatalá is paired with Las Mercedes in Cuban Lucumí; the white-garbed Marian figure matches Obatalá's white-cloth iconography. Ref: Brandon 1993.
- **Oxalá (Brazilian Candomblé)** — *manuscript-transmission*: the same orisha, lightly modified in Yoruba-Bahian liturgy.

## Iconography
White cloth, white beads (*efun* — chalk-white), occasionally a horse-tail flywhisk (*irukere*); coolness, snail-pace, deliberation are conceptual attributes.
