# Ikenga

Ikenga (*Ìkéngà*, roughly "the strength/place of one's power") is among the most distinctive religious objects-and-deities of the Igbo — a **personal god of achievement, enterprise, and the strength of the right hand**. It takes the form of a carved wooden figure crowned with **two horns** (a ram's combative power), usually holding a **blade in its right hand** and a **trophy head or tusk in its left** — emblems of the industry, valour, and accomplishment by which a man makes his way in the world. Crucially, Ikenga is **individually owned**: a man (traditionally; some titled women too) commissions his own Ikenga at the start of adult life, **"feeds" and honours it**, and credits it with his successes — for it embodies his ***chi*** (personal destiny) and the **power of his right hand** to work, fight, farm, and prosper. It operates within the larger Igbo cosmos of the supreme **[[chukwu-igbo|Chukwu]]** and the doctrine that each person comes into the world with an assigned destiny that effort must fulfil. When its owner dies, the Ikenga is often split or buried with him. Ikenga embodies the strikingly **individualistic, achievement-oriented** ethic at the heart of Igbo religion — the sacralization of self-made success.
