# The Good God (Cathar)

## Identity
In Cathar dualist cosmology, the supreme Good God is the transcendent principle of all that is spiritual, good, and incorruptible — the Father whom Jesus reveals in the Gospels, the source of light and the destination of the saved. He is the creator of *spiritual* reality only — souls, angels, the invisible kingdom — and is *not* the creator of the material cosmos, which is the work of Satanael (mitigated dualist) or of the coeternal evil principle (absolute dualist). The Cathar Good God is *not* the creator-God of Genesis (whom Cathars identified with Satan / the evil principle); the Old Testament is, with qualifications, rejected as the testament of the evil power. The Cathar Good God is purely transcendent, purely loving, but radically separated from material creation — accessible to humans only through the spiritual / Catharist sacraments (especially the *consolamentum*, the laying-on of hands by which one became a *perfectus* / *bonus homo*) and through release from the body at death.

## Earliest attestation
The systematic Cathar two-Gods cosmology is attested in Byzantine sources on Bogomilism (Euthymius Zigabenus ~1110), in the *Interrogatio Iohannis* (~1190), in the *Liber de Duobus Principiis* (~1240), and in extensive anti-Cathar polemical literature (Moneta of Cremona ~1241, Rainerius Sacconi ~1250).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[the-monad-gnostic]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: structurally parallel transcendent-Good-beyond-material-demiurge. The defining feature of the Cathar Good God is *not* a positive doctrinal content but a structural separation from the material creator — exactly the Sethian Monad's role.
- **[[father-of-greatness]]** (Manichaean) — *scholarly-parallel* / *direct-influence?*: same structural role; the foundational debate on Manichaean → Cathar transmission applies here.
- The Father in canonical Christian theology — *polemic-against* / *parallel*: Catholic apologists insisted the Cathar Good God *was* the canonical Father; Cathars insisted he was *not* the OT creator. The argument is exegetical and metaphysical.

## Disputes
- **Manichaean genealogy.** Same debate as for Satanael; whether the Cathar Good God descends genealogically from Manichaean / Sethian transcendent-Father theology, or is independently re-derived from radical reading of the NT and Marcionite-style critique of the OT.
- **Reading of John 1:1–3.** "All things came into being through him" — Catholic reading: through the Father's Word (= Christ). Cathar reading: through the spiritual principle, with the material world *not* among "all things" (since it was made by the other principle).
