# Astghik

## Identity
Astghik (Armenian: Աստղիկ, meaning "little star" or "starlet") is the Armenian goddess of love, beauty, and water — the Venus of the Armenian pantheon. Consort of [[vahagn]], daughter of [[aramazd]]. Her name is explicitly astral: *astgh* (Armenian for "star") + the diminutive suffix *-ik*, making her "the little star" — pointing directly to the planet Venus as her celestial identity. Her temples were at **Ashtishat** (near Lake Van) and **Artashat** (the ancient Armenian capital). Her festival **Vardavar** (from *vard*, "rose," or possibly from an older stem) was one of the major annual Armenian festivals, celebrated with **water-throwing** and **rose-scattering** — a water-and-flower festival whose pre-Christian structure survived Christianization so completely that it is still celebrated today (officially the Feast of the Transfiguration of Christ, but with water-throwing that preserves the pre-Christian form intact).

## Earliest attestation
- **Agathangelos** (c. 400 CE) and **Movses Khorenatsi** (c. 470 CE): primary literary sources.
- The Vardavar festival's pre-Christian character is documented by the survival of water-throwing practices after Christianization — a strong marker of genuine pre-Christian antiquity.
- Temple at Ashtishat (also home to [[vahagn]]'s cult): destroyed after 301 CE Christianization.

## Mythological role
Astghik's mythological role centers on love, beauty, water, and the celestial identity of the morning star (Venus). As the love-goddess she is the Armenian equivalent of the Near Eastern great love-goddess tradition (Inanna → Ishtar → Astarte → Aphrodite → Venus → Astghik), with the planet Venus as her astral manifestation. Her water-goddess aspects tie her to fertility and to the Zoroastrian Anahita's water domain (though she is distinct from [[anahit-armenian]], who is Aramazd's daughter with different emphasis).

The **Vardavar festival** is Astghik's most important surviving cultural legacy: celebrated in midsummer, it involves throwing water on people and releasing doves — both elements echoing love-goddess festivals across the Mediterranean (Aphrodite's sea-birth, Ishtar's descent bringing the waters). The Christianization of Vardavar as the Transfiguration (Kreşer in Armenian Christianity) displaced the explicit divine identity but preserved the water-throwing entirely — a case study in festival continuity across religious change.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[aphrodite-greek]]** — *ancient-identification*: Hellenistic *interpretatio graeca* identified Astghik with Aphrodite (Agathangelos; Russell 1987). The rose and water elements of Vardavar map directly onto Aphrodite's rose-associations and sea-birth.
- **[[venus-roman]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the planet-Venus identification that underlies Roman Venus equally underlies Astghik; both names mean forms of "star" or "the bright one."
- **[[astarte-canaanite]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the name *astarte* is cognate with *astghik* through the Semitic root *ʿśtr* (star); both are love-goddesses as the planet Venus (Russell 1987).
- **[[inanna-sumerian]]** / **[[ishtar-akkadian]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the love-goddess-as-Venus-morning-star is a pattern originating with Inanna/Ishtar that Astghik participates in through the broader Near Eastern tradition.
- **[[hathor]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Egyptian goddess of love, beauty, and music associated with the planet Venus; another node in the ancient love-goddess-as-morning-star network.
- **[[oshun]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Yoruba love-goddess of rivers and beauty; a West African parallel to the water-and-love-goddess pattern Astghik instantiates.

## Iconography
Astghik's iconography is imperfectly preserved after Christianization. Likely associations: doves (standard love-goddess attribute across the Near Eastern tradition), roses, and water. Her festival imagery — water and roses — is her most vivid surviving iconographic register. The dove-release tradition in the Vardavar festival points toward a dove-and-water iconography comparable to Aphrodite and Ishtar.

## Disputes
- **Vardavar's etymology.** Whether *Vardavar* derives from *vard* (rose) or from a deeper pre-Armenian stem is debated; Russell 1987 argues for the rose etymology; others propose an older agricultural or water-festival origin independent of the rose connection.
- **Astghik vs. Anahit.** The relationship between Astghik (love, beauty, Venus) and [[anahit-armenian]] (fertility, wisdom, war, the golden mother) is not entirely clear from surviving sources — they appear to have separate cult identities but some functional overlap. Russell 1987 treats them as distinct deities.
- **Zoroastrian substrate.** Astghik's possible relation to Zoroastrian Anahita's water aspects (distinct from Anahit's martial-mother transformation) is discussed cautiously in the scholarship; Astghik may preserve a separate strand of Anahita's water domain.
