# Yamuna

## Identity

Yamuna is the goddess of the river **Yamuna** (Jamuna), the great tributary of the Ganges and one of the holiest rivers of Hinduism. As a deity she is the daughter of **[[surya]]** (the Sun) and the **twin sister of [[yama-vedic]]**, the god of death — the feminine **Yamī** to his Yama. With **[[ganga]]** she forms the celebrated pair of north-Indian river-goddesses.

## Mythological role

Yamuna's mythology runs along two channels. In the **Vedic** layer she is Yamī, the twin of Yama; the *Rig Veda* preserves a striking dialogue-hymn (10.10) in which Yamī urges union upon her brother Yama, who refuses — a primal exchange about incest, mortality, and the origin of the human line. As a **river-goddess** she is the dark-watered Kālindī, and above all she is the river of **[[krishna]]'s** Vrindavan: it is in and beside the Yamuna that the child and youth Krishna performs his play with the cowherd-girls (gopis) and subdues the many-headed serpent Kāliya who had poisoned her waters. Bathing in the Yamuna is held to confer purification and grace, and her confluence with the Ganga (and the mythical Sarasvati) at Prayag (Allahabad) is one of the supreme pilgrimage sites of India.

## Cross-tradition significance

Yamuna belongs to the Hindu **divinization of rivers** — the sacred waterway personified as a goddess and worshipped for the purification and life she bestows, a pattern paralleled by Nile-deification in Egypt (Hapi) and the river-cults of many traditions. As the sister of Yama (Death), she also threads into the cross-tradition complex of paired primordial twins and the origin of mortality (compare the Iranian Yima/Yamī). Paired with Ganga, she completes the great river-goddess dyad at the heart of Indian sacred geography.
