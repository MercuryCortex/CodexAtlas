# Anahita (Zoroastrian)

Anahita (Avestan *Arədvī Sūrā Anāhitā*, "the Mighty, Immaculate [Waters]"; Middle Persian *Anāhīd*) is the great Zoroastrian **yazata of the waters, fertility, and victory** — the personification of the cosmic world-river that pours down from the heights, source of all rivers and seas, of fruitfulness in women and herds, and of strength in battle. The **Ābān Yašt** (Yašt 5), one of the longest hymns of the Younger Avesta, is dedicated to her, picturing a beautiful, strong maiden in a beaver-skin cloak and a star-studded golden crown, driving a four-horse chariot of cloud, rain, wind, and sleet, before whom warriors and heroes from Ahura Mazda down beg for her favour. Her cult took on imperial dimensions when **Artaxerxes II** (early 4th c. BCE) promoted statue-shrines of her across the Achaemenid realm — and in doing so her worship **absorbed the image-cult of Mesopotamian Inanna-[[ishtar-akkadian|Ishtar]]**, the great Venus-goddess of love and war. Adopted in Armenia as the foremost goddess **[[anahit-armenian|Anahit]]**, compared by the Greeks to **[[aphrodite-greek|Aphrodite]]** and Artemis, and parallel as an Indo-Iranian river-goddess to the Vedic **[[saraswati|Sarasvatī]]**, Anahita is one of the most widely radiating deities of the Iranian world.
