# Cernunnos

## Identity
Antlered nature-god of the Celts — the cross-legged **horned figure** holding a torc and a ram-headed serpent who appears in the central panel of the **Gundestrup Cauldron** (c. -150 to -50 BCE, the most spectacular Celtic ritual vessel ever found, discovered in a Danish bog in 1891). His name appears only once epigraphically — on the **Pillar of the Boatmen** (Paris, ~14–37 CE), where the inscription *CERNVNNOS* glosses an antlered head — but the iconography is widespread across Gaul, Britain, and the Celtic-Thracian frontier.

## Mythological role
**Lord-of-the-animals** (*Potnios Theron*) — Cernunnos is depicted surrounded by stags, bulls, ram-headed serpents, hounds, and other wild creatures, an iconography running back to Minoan Master-of-Animals figures (Knossos, c. -1600) and the Indus Valley Pashupati seal (Mohenjo-Daro, c. -2500). His posture — **cross-legged, palms-on-knees** — is shared with both of those earlier figures; the visual cognacy is striking, but transmission is **not demonstrable** (per vault methodology, this is `parallel-form` not `ancestor-of`).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[pan-greek]]** — *parallel-motif*: horned wild-nature god.
- **Pashupati seal** — *parallel-form* (NOT *ancestor-of*; per vault methodology — symbol-research discipline): visual cognacy without documented transmission.

## Iconography
The single most distinctive Celtic divine iconography: stag-antlered, cross-legged, holding a torc (Celtic status-marker) and a horned/ram-headed serpent (uniquely Celtic motif).
