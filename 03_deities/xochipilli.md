# Xochipilli

Xochipilli (Nahuatl *Xōchipilli*, "Flower Prince") is the Aztec god of **flowers, the arts, and pleasure** — patron of song, dance, poetry, games, feasting, and "flowery" things generally, the divine sponsor of beauty and delight (and, in his more dangerous aspect, of excess and the diseases that punish it). As **Macuilxochitl** ("Five-Flower") he heads the *Ahuiateteo*, the five gods of pleasure-and-overindulgence keyed to the calendar. He is the masculine counterpart of the flower-goddess **[[xochiquetzal]]**. His most famous image is the celebrated **Aztec statue of Xochipilli** (now in the Museo Nacional de Antropología): a god seated in ecstatic trance, head tilted back, his body and the base carved all over with stylized **flowers — several of them identifiable as psychoactive plants** (morning-glory, sacred mushroom, tobacco, possibly *Datura*), which led ethnobotanists (Wasson, Schultes) to read him as the patron of **entheogenic, visionary ecstasy** — the "flowery dream" of the Aztec sacred imagination.
