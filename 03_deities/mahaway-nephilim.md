# Mahaway (Nephilim, the giant-emissary to Enoch)

## Identity
**Mahaway** is the most narratively central of the named Nephilim in the **Book of Giants** ([[phase-3-033-book-of-giants-qumran-manichaean]]). He is **son of the Watcher [[baraqel]]** (per Qumran 4Q530), making him a *second-generation* descendant of the Watcher-tradition — fathered by the Watcher who taught lightning-divination. His narrative function is to serve as **the emissary who flies to Enoch's celestial dwelling** to obtain dream-interpretations on behalf of his fellow giants.

The Book of Giants narrative: when [[ohyah-nephilim]] and [[hahyah-nephilim]] (sons of [[semyaza]]) receive ominous prophetic dreams of the impending Flood, the giant-council convenes to seek interpretation. The giants are unable to interpret among themselves, so they commission Mahaway — who possesses the *flight-capability* (the Aramaic fragments preserve language of his ascending into the heavens) — to fly to Enoch, the antediluvian sage now removed to heavenly seclusion (cf. Genesis 5:24 — *"Enoch walked with God, and he was not, for God took him"*).

Mahaway makes the flight, reaches Enoch, receives the dream-interpretation, and returns to the giants with the verdict — that the Flood is coming as judgment and the Nephilim will be destroyed. This is one of the most striking *intercessory-emissary* narrative structures in Second-Temple Jewish apocalyptic literature.

## Earliest attestation
- **Qumran 4Q530** — the principal Mahaway-narrative fragment.
- **Qumran 4Q531, 4Q532** — additional Mahaway-related material.
- **Manichaean Book of Giants** — Mahaway is preserved in multiple Manichaean witnesses (Middle Persian, Sogdian, Uyghur — Reeves 1992).

## Mythological role
Mahaway is theologically distinctive as the *only Nephilim who consults Enoch directly* and the *only Nephilim with flight-capability*. His role makes him structurally analogous to:

1. **The Mesopotamian dream-emissary figures** (sages who interpret royal dreams or relay messages between humans and gods).
2. **The Watchers themselves** in their pre-rebellion celestial-emissary capacity (the Watchers were originally messengers / observers).
3. **Later angelic-emissary figures** in Jewish-Christian apocalyptic (Raphael in Tobit; the seven angels in Revelation; the angelic-mediators of 1 Enoch).

The flight-capability is theologically suggestive: Mahaway inherits a *partial-angelic* nature from his Watcher-father Baraqel, retaining the flight that the human-descended giants lack. This creates an interesting gradation in the Watcher-Nephilim hierarchy — most Nephilim are earth-bound giants, but Mahaway is the giant-with-residual-angelic-flight.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- The dream-emissary structural pattern is widely distributed in ancient Near Eastern literature.
- Manichaean cosmology integrates Mahaway substantially (Reeves 1992).

## Iconography
Not standardized.

## Disputes
- **Etymology.** "Mahaway" / "Mahawai" — Aramaic / Sogdian / Coptic variants. The name does not have a clearly-attested etymology.
- **Father-identification.** Qumran 4Q530 names Baraqel as Mahaway's father; some Manichaean recensions differ. Stuckenbruck 1997 accepts the Baraqel-Mahaway filiation as primary.
