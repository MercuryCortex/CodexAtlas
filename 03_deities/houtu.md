# Houtu

## Identity
Houtu (后土, "Sovereign Earth") is the Chinese **deity of the earth** — the personified and deified Earth itself, the great counterpart to **Heaven** ([[tian-heaven]] / [[shangdi]]). In the imperial religion the Earth was worshipped at a **square altar** (earth being square as heaven is round), and Houtu received the state sacrifice as the power that bears and nourishes all things.

## Earliest attestation
Houtu appears from the classical period as the spirit of the soil and the recipient of the imperial earth-sacrifice; the cult was formalized in the Han and continued at the **Altar of Earth (Ditan)** in Beijing through the Qing. Over time Houtu, originally of fluid gender, came to be venerated as a **great goddess**, "Empress of the Earth."

## Mythological role — the deified Earth
Where [[shangdi]] / Heaven is the sovereign above, Houtu is the sovereign below: the soil's fertility, the stability of the land, and the receiving earth into which the dead return. The pairing of round Heaven and square Earth, of the Heaven-altar and the Earth-altar, makes Houtu one half of the fundamental Chinese cosmological dyad. As "Empress Earth" she became a popular goddess of the land and of the underworld's surface.

## Cross-tradition
Houtu is the Chinese member of the **divine Earth** — the earth personified and worshipped — alongside the Greek [[gaia]] and the Vedic Prithvi. The Heaven/Earth complementarity she anchors is a Chinese expression of the widespread Sky-Father / Earth-Mother polarity.

## Iconography
The square earth-altar; the colour yellow (the centre/earth in the five-phase scheme); a robed empress in later iconography.
