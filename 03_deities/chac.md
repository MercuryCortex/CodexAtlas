# Chaac

## Identity
Maya rain god — one of the most-attested deities in Maya iconography and codex art. Chaac has the distinctive long curved (or trumpet-like) nose, holds a lightning-axe, and presides over rain, thunder, lightning, and agricultural fertility. He is structurally identical to central-Mexican Tlaloc, with whom he shares a pan-Mesoamerican rain-god type. In late Postclassic and colonial Yucatec sources, Chaac is fourfold — *Chaak Xib Chaak* (red, east), *Sak Xib Chaak* (white, north), *Ek' Xib Chaak* (black, west), *Kan Xib Chaak* (yellow, south) — each Chaac governing one cardinal direction.

## Earliest attestation
Late Preclassic Maya art onward; extensive Classic and Postclassic attestation; prominent in the Dresden, Madrid, and Paris codices.

## Mythological role
Chaac brings rain by striking lightning-axe on clouds; agricultural success depends on him. Yucatec colonial *ch'a-cháak* ceremonies (still performed in some Maya communities) petition the Chaacs at the start of the planting season.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[tlaloc]] (central Mexican)** — *scholarly-parallel*: same pan-Mesoamerican rain-god type.

## Iconography
Long curved nose (distinctive); T-shaped tooth; axe / lightning-celt; serpent associates; spiral eye.
