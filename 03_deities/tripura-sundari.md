# Tripura Sundari

Tripura Sundari (*Tripurasundarī*, "the Beautiful Goddess of the Three Cities/Worlds"; also **Lalitā**, "the Playful," and **Rājarājeśvarī**, "Empress of Emperors") is the **supreme Goddess of the Śrī Vidyā tradition**, one of the most sophisticated and influential schools of Hindu Tantra. She is the highest **Shakti** — the absolute conceived as transcendent feminine beauty, power, and bliss — united with **[[shiva|Shiva]]** in his form as **Kāmeśvara** ("Lord of Desire"), their non-dual embrace expressing the ultimate reality. Her worship is famously aniconic and geometric: she is meditated upon as the **Śrī Chakra / Śrī Yantra**, the great diagram of nine interlocking triangles, and invoked by the secret fifteen- and sixteen-syllable **Śrī Vidyā mantra**. In iconic form she is a radiant red goddess bearing a **noose, goad, sugarcane bow, and five flower-arrows**, enthroned upon a seat whose very legs are the great gods. She is also the third of the ten **Mahāvidyās**, the beautiful counterpart to the fierce **[[kali|Kālī]]**. Tripura Sundari represents the height of Śākta theology — the cosmos as the play of a single, supremely beautiful divine consciousness.
