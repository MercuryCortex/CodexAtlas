# Ningal

## Identity
Ningal ("Great Lady") is the consort of [[nanna-sin]] (the Sumerian moon god) and mother of the two major astral deities — [[inanna-sumerian]] (Venus) and [[utu-shamash]] (the sun) — and in many traditions also of ishkur (storm). Her cult center is the **Egipar**, the *en*-priestess's residence within the Ekishnugal temple complex at Ur; she is the female counterpart to Nanna and the divine recipient of the *en*-priestess's service (the same office held by [[enheduanna]]). She is the daughter of Ningikuga (a reed god) and Ninhursag in some genealogies. From the Old Babylonian period she is increasingly known by her Akkadian/West-Semitic name Nikkal, and the cult migrates into Aramaic and Ugaritic spheres via Harran, where the moon-cult of Sin and Nikkal survives into Late Antiquity.

## Earliest attestation
- Early Dynastic god lists from Fara/Shuruppak and Abu Salabikh (~-2600 to -2500) name NIN.GAL among the senior pantheon.
- Temple Hymn 8 of the [[phase-1-003-enheduanna-hymns]] is dedicated to her sanctuary at Ur.
- The Kesh Temple Hymn ([[phase-1-001-kesh-temple-hymn]]) lists her among the great gods.
- Royal inscriptions of the kings of Ur (Ur-Nammu, Shulgi, Amar-Sin, Ibbi-Sin) repeatedly dedicate building works to her.
- The *Lamentation over the Destruction of Ur* gives her one of the most extended first-person divine laments in Sumerian literature.

## Mythological role
- Her primary role is consort and mother. She is not the protagonist of a major narrative myth in the way Inanna is.
- In the *Lamentation over the Destruction of Ur* (Kramer 1940), she stands before the divine council pleading for her city; her grief over Ur's destruction is the text's emotional center. This is among the earliest extended literary expressions of divine grief.
- In the *Marriage of Nikkal* (Ugaritic, KTU 1.24), she — as Nikkal — is married to the moon god Yarikh (Ugaritic moon), continuing the role westward.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[nikkal-akkadian]]** — *ancient-identification*: standard Akkadian rendering, identical deity. The Aramaic cult of Sin and Nikkal at Harran continues into the Roman and even early Islamic period; the Sabians of Harran venerated this pair.
- The Ugaritic Nikkal-wa-Ib is a West-Semitic continuation (with the moon god Yarikh) that adapts the Mesopotamian pair to a new astronomical theology.

## Iconography
Seated goddess in horned crown alongside Nanna; in seal scenes typically with a crescent over her shoulder. No distinct iconography independent of the moon-cult.

## Disputes
- **Genealogy of Inanna**. The maternity of Inanna is divided in the sources: she is daughter of Ningal-and-Nanna in the Ur tradition, but daughter of An or daughter of Enki in other traditions. The variation reflects local cultic emphases rather than a single contradiction.
- **Identification with Nikkal at Ebla and Mari**. The phonological identification Ningal → Nikkal is uncontested; the question of whether the Eblaite/early West Semitic Nikkal is a Mesopotamian import or an independently emerged cognate is debated.
