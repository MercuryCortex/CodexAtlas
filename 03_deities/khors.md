# Khors

Khors is an **East Slavic solar deity** — one of the gods whose idols, according to the *Russian Primary Chronicle*, **Prince Vladimir set up on the hill above Kiev in 980** (alongside Perun, Dazhbog, Stribog, Simargl, and Mokosh) as the official pantheon of pre-Christian Rus', just years before he converted the realm to Christianity. Khors is generally understood as a god of **the sun** or the solar disk (some scholars argue instead for a lunar or specifically "old/winter sun" sense, and he is often coupled with Dazhbog, another solar figure). What makes him especially intriguing is his **name**, which is widely judged to be of **Iranian origin** — compare Persian *xoršid* ("sun") and Avestan *hvarə* — suggesting the influence of the Iranian-speaking steppe peoples (Scythians, Sarmatians, Alans) on the East Slavs along their southern frontier. Beyond the chronicle's bare list and a few later mentions, little is securely known of his myth or cult, and much of what is said about him is reconstruction. Khors stands as a marker of the Iranian threads woven into early Slavic religion.
