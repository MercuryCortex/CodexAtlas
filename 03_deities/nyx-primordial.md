# Nyx

## Identity
Nyx (Greek *Νύξ*, "Night") is the **primordial goddess of Night** in Greek cosmogony — in [[hesiod]] a second-generation primordial emerging from [[chaos-primordial]], but in **Orphic theogonies the supreme primordial deity**, the source from which all subsequent gods including [[zeus]] derive. Her cosmic centrality in Orphic tradition makes her a load-bearing figure for understanding how Greek religion's *mystery-cult* side imagines the primordial — radically different from the Olympian-centered religion of city-state cult, and structurally closer to later Hellenistic and Christian theological darkness-imagery.

## Earliest attestation
- **[[hesiod]]** *Theogony* 123: Nyx born of Chaos; couples with Erebus to produce Aether and Hemera; then Nyx alone produces a long list of dark abstractions (Doom, Fate, Death, Sleep, Dreams, Blame, Distress, the Hesperides, the Moirai, Nemesis, Deceit, Old Age, Strife). The *Theogony*'s longest single genealogical list issues from Nyx, marking her cosmic productivity.
- **Orphic theogonies** (multiple recensions, recovered partly through the **Derveni Papyrus**, c. -340 — the earliest surviving Greek philosophical text — and through later quotations in Damascius, Proclus, and the Neoplatonists): in most Orphic recensions Nyx is the supreme primordial, the egg-laying mother from whom [[phanes-protogonos]] emerges, the wise counselor of Zeus.
- **Aristophanes** *Birds* 693–702 — parodies an Orphic-style cosmogony in which Nyx lays the cosmic egg from which Eros hatches; this is one of the earliest external attestations of Orphic-Nyx cosmogony, c. -414.
- **Roman reception**: Nox in Latin literature is a fading echo; the Orphic Nyx returns in late-antique Neoplatonism (Proclus, Damascius).

## Mythological role
Two distinct cosmogonic roles:

1. **Hesiodic Nyx**: second-generation primordial, paired with Erebus, mother of the dark abstractions of the world. Cosmically significant but not supreme.

2. **Orphic Nyx**: **the supreme primordial deity**, often (in the standard Orphic recension reconstructed by West 1983) the mother of [[phanes-protogonos]] who emerges from the cosmic egg she laid. Zeus consults Nyx as the wise primordial counselor before/during/after his cosmic kingship; in some recensions Zeus *swallows* Phanes to restart the cosmogony with himself as origin — and Nyx survives as the primordial Night who remains older than all the gods.

The Orphic Nyx-cosmogony is **structurally important for the reception-history of Christian darkness-mysticism**: pseudo-Dionysian "divine darkness" (the *theia gnophos*), the *via negativa* of [[gregory-of-nyssa]] and later Christian apophatic theology, and the *cloud of unknowing* tradition all stand in genealogical contact with the Greco-Orphic / Neoplatonist tradition of cosmic Night as the supreme principle. [[proclus]] and Damascius (last head of the Platonic Academy, 6th c.; no node yet) are the immediate channels.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Nox (Roman)** — *ancient-identification*: standard Roman *interpretatio*.
- **[[hekate]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: late-Hellenistic partial merger in theurgic and magical contexts.
- **Tehom (Hebrew "Deep")** — *scholarly-parallel*: the pre-creation darkness of Genesis 1:2 is functionally close to Greek Nyx, though there is no documented transmission. The cross-cultural family of "primordial dark feminine waters" (Tiamat, Nun, Nyx, Tehom) is a comparative-religion staple.
- **Christian apophatic "divine darkness"** — *appropriated-by* (indirectly): Neoplatonist Nyx-theology feeds into pseudo-Dionysius's *theia gnophos*, then into Gregory of Nyssa, Eckhart, the *Cloud of Unknowing*, and modern Christian mystical theology.

## Iconography
- **Dark-veiled woman** — long mantle, sometimes star-studded
- **Wings** in some Orphic traditions (Nyx as winged egg-laying mother)
- **Inverted or extinguished torch** (Hellenistic-Roman iconography)
- Often accompanied by Hypnos (Sleep) and Thanatos (Death) — her children
- The **Orphic cosmic egg** in mystery-cult iconography is laid by Nyx

## Disputes
- **Date and recension of Orphic theogony.** Multiple recensions: the *Eudemian Theogony* (mentioned by Eudemus, pupil of Aristotle), the *Rhapsodic Theogony* (the most influential, ~1st c. BCE or earlier), and the *Hieronymian / Hellanican Theogony*. Which Nyx-centered cosmogony is earliest is debated (West 1983 is the standard reconstruction; Bernabé's *Orphicorum Fragmenta* the standard collection).
- **The Derveni Papyrus's date.** Conventionally late 4th c. BCE (so the text itself is c. -340), but the Orphic theogony commented on in the Papyrus is older (probably 5th c. BCE or earlier). This is the **earliest surviving Greek philosophical text** and a major scholarly recovery (Kouremenos et al. 2006; Janko 2002).
