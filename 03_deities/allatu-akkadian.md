# Allatu

## Identity
Allatu (also Allatum) is the standard Akkadian rendering of Sumerian [[ereshkigal]] — queen of the netherworld (Kurnugi, "the Land of No Return"; also called Irkalla), keeper of the protocols of death, sister of Inanna/Ishtar. The name **Allatu** appears in Akkadian-language texts from the Old Babylonian period as a phonetic-Akkadian alternative to the Sumerian-writing Ereshkigal; in some text-types Allatu is preferred (especially in personal names, in ritual lists, and in certain West-Semitic-flavored OB contexts at Mari), in others Ereshkigal continues as the literary form. The *Descent of Ishtar*, the *Nergal and Ereshkigal* myth, and the Mesopotamian funerary corpus use the names interchangeably with no theological distinction — Allatu and Ereshkigal are the same goddess in two parallel-current naming traditions.

**Important distinction**: Allatu (Akkadian, queen of the underworld) is NOT to be confused with **Allat** (the pre-Islamic Arabian goddess, one of the daughters of Allah in Quranic context, attested in Nabataean and Palmyrene contexts as a major North-Arabian deity). The two names are phonologically similar and may share a deep Semitic root (*'l-* "god, goddess"), but they are functionally and historically distinct deities — the Arabian Allat is principally a high-goddess / warrior / Venus-figure, not a netherworld queen, and her cult emerges in a different geography and chronology. Scholarly literature has sometimes conflated them in older comparative-religion treatments; current Assyriology and Arabian-studies both reject the equation.

## Earliest attestation
- Old Babylonian sources use Allatu in personal names and ritual texts.
- The Mari archive (~-1800) contains West-Semitic-flavored Allatum attestations.
- The Akkadian **Descent of Ishtar** (Standard Babylonian period; Lapinkivi 2010) names Ereshkigal as netherworld queen — Allatu appearing alternately in textual traditions.
- The **Nergal and Ereshkigal** myth (Amarna ~-1400 and Sultantepe ~-700; Gurney 1960) uses Ereshkigal as the literary form; ritual and personal-name evidence shows Allatu in parallel use.
- Akkadian incantations and kispu (offering-to-the-dead) rituals invoke her by both names.

## Mythological role
Allatu inherits Ereshkigal's full mythological profile in Akkadian-language texts:
- ***Descent of Ishtar***: when Ishtar descends to Kurnugi, Allatu/Ereshkigal receives her, demands the seven gate-strippings, kills her, and is finally compelled by the gods (via Asushunamir's intervention) to release her with Tammuz as substitute. The text is the shorter, sharper Akkadian counterpart to the Sumerian *Inanna's Descent*.
- ***Nergal and Ereshkigal***: Nergal/Erra descends to the netherworld, insults Allatu's messenger Namtar, seizes Allatu by the hair to kill her; she pleads, offers herself in marriage; he remains as her consort. The myth etiologizes the dual rulership of the netherworld.
- ***Funerary cult***: Allatu receives kispu offerings as the ultimate destination of the dead; ritual texts address her in funerary contexts.
- ***Incantations***: she is named in apotropaic incantations seeking to prevent illness (which is conceptualized as a netherworld-incursion) and in necromantic-prohibitive contexts.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ereshkigal]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Sumerian counterpart; the two are the same goddess across the Sumerian-Akkadian linguistic boundary. Per the vault's splitting rule, the Sumerian Ereshkigal node and the Akkadian Allatu node are maintained as separate; the existing Ereshkigal node is itself tagged "Sumerian / Akkadian" reflecting that the figure crosses the boundary. The Allatu node disambiguates the specifically Akkadian-language naming tradition.
- *Scholarly parallel*: Greek Persephone, Norse Hel, and other queen-of-the-dead figures are sometimes compared; these are *scholarly-parallel*, not ancient identifications.
- **Distinguish from Arabian Allat**: see *Disputes* below — Allatu (Akkadian netherworld queen) and Allat (Arabian high-goddess) are NOT the same deity and should not be syncretized.

## Iconography
Allatu shares Ereshkigal's iconographic profile: enthroned netherworld queen, sometimes depicted with the seven gates of Kurnugi or with her divine attendants (Namtar, Belet-Seri). Limited dedicated iconography compared to the upper-world goddesses.

## Disputes
- **Allatu vs. Arabian Allat**. Older comparative-religion scholarship sometimes conflated the two (treating Allat as a survival of the Mesopotamian netherworld queen into the Arabian peninsula). Current scholarship rejects this: Allat is a high-goddess / warrior / Venus-figure in pre-Islamic Arabia, not a netherworld queen; her cult emerges from a different theological matrix; the phonological similarity reflects shared Semitic *'l-* roots, not theological identity. The Arabian Allat is treated in her own right in the pre-Islamic Arabian corpus (not in this node).
- **Allatu / Ereshkigal as one or two**. The Akkadian-language preference for Allatu in some text-types might reflect a real (and now invisible) theological nuance, or it might be purely a question of register (Akkadian phonetic spelling vs. Sumerian logogram). Current scholarship treats them as the same goddess in two parallel-current writing traditions.
- **Reading of the Mari Allatum theology**. The OB Mari attestations show an Allatum-cult with somewhat distinctive features (possibly West-Semitic-inflected); whether this is a regional variant of the Mesopotamian netherworld queen or a partially independent West-Semitic deity later assimilated is unresolved.
