# Nzambi Mpungu

## Identity

*Nzambi Mpungu* ("God above" or "God supreme" — *mpungu* meaning "supreme/above all") is the supreme being of the BaKongo and related Bantu-speaking Central African peoples. Like [[olodumare]] (Yoruba) and [[nyame]] (Akan), Nzambi Mpungu is the ultimate creator and cosmic ground, approached only indirectly through intermediaries — specifically through the *bakulu* (ancestors) and the spirits housed in *minkisi* (sacred power objects). Nzambi is the source of *moyo* (life-force) and *mpema* (breath): all living things participate in Nzambi's life.

The name *Nzambi* spread widely through Central and Southern Africa via Bantu migration and linguistic diffusion; many related Bantu peoples (including Kongo, Mbundu, and others) use cognate terms for the supreme being. In diasporic contexts, the name *Zambi* or *Zambia* appears in Brazilian Candomblé Angola and Cuban Palo Monte.

## Earliest attestation

Documented in Portuguese missionary records from the late 15th century (after first contact 1483); the Manikongo's letters to the Portuguese king reference Nzambi in a syncretic BaKongo-Christian context. Systematic scholarly analysis from MacGaffey (1986) and Thompson (1983).

## Mythological role

Nzambi Mpungu is the creator and ultimate sustainer. The cosmos is organized around the *dikenga dia Kongo* (Kongo cosmogram) — the four-moment cycle of the sun representing the cycle of life, death, ancestral existence, and rebirth. Nzambi presides over this cycle as its ground and source. The living and dead are separated by *kalunga* — the cosmic water-boundary (identified with the ocean, with rivers, and with the threshold of death). Nzambi governs both sides of the kalunga-threshold.

## Cross-tradition identifications

- **Christian God** — *folk-syncretism*: Portuguese missionaries identified *Nzambi* with the Christian God; the Manikongo Afonso I (r. 1509–1543) adopted Christian vocabulary while maintaining Kongo cosmological structures. The cross-symbol resonance (Kongo *dikenga* + Christian cross) facilitated this identification — see [[tradition-bantu-kongo]].
- **[[olodumare]]** (Yoruba) / **[[nyame]]** (Akan) — *scholarly-parallel*: all three are supreme beings of major West/Central African traditions; all are aniconic deus otiosus figures approached through intermediaries; all are the ultimate ground of life-force. Mbiti (1969) places them in the same typological category across his survey of 270+ African traditions. **Edge: `parallel-form`**.
- **[[bondye-vodou]]** (Haitian Vodou) — *scholarly-parallel*: Vodou's Bondye ("Good God") is the transcendent supreme being approached through the *lwa*; its structure reflects both Yoruba *Olodumare* and Kongo *Nzambi* influences given the substantial Kongo population in Saint-Domingue.

## Iconography

Aniconic. Nzambi is not represented in sculptural form. White (*mpemba*) is the sacred color of Nzambi and the ancestors — the color of the land of the dead and of transcendent divine presence. The *dikenga dia Kongo* (cross-within-circle) is the closest thing to a symbol of the cosmic order Nzambi embodies, though it represents the cosmological structure rather than Nzambi as a person.
