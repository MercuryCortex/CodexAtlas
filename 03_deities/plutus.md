# Plutus

Plutus (*Ploûtos*, "wealth") is the personification of **riches** — originally and specifically the **abundance of the grain-harvest**. Hesiod makes him the son of **[[demeter]]** and the hero Iasion, conceived "in a thrice-ploughed fallow field" (*Theogony* 969–974): wealth is the gift of the grain-goddess, the full storehouse. In the Eleusinian iconography the child Plutus, holding a cornucopia, is presented by Demeter and Persephone as the blessing of the Mysteries. Later Greek thought turned him into a moral allegory: in Aristophanes' comedy *Ploutos* he is **blind** — distributing wealth at random, without regard to who deserves it — until he is healed and learns to enrich only the just. His name underlies *plutocracy* and connects (via "the rich one," *Plouton*) to **[[hades]]**, lord of the underground source of grain and metal.
