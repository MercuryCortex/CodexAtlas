# Toyouke

Toyouke (Japanese *Toyouke-Ōmikami*) is the Shinto goddess of **food, grain, and sustenance**, and the deity of the **Outer Shrine (Gekū)** of **Ise Jingū** — Japan's most sacred shrine-complex. Her presence at Ise has a specific origin: according to tradition, around 478 CE the sun-goddess **[[amaterasu]]**, enshrined in the Inner Shrine (Naikū), revealed in a dream that she could not take her meals in peace alone, and asked that Toyouke be brought from **Tanba** province to attend her. Toyouke was duly installed in the Outer Shrine, and ever since her priests have performed the **higoto-asayū-no-ōmikedmae** — the offering of sacred food to Amaterasu twice daily, every day, for some fifteen centuries. She is thus the divine provider who sustains even the gods, a "great-food deity" (*ōgetsuhime* type) akin to **[[inari]]** and Ukemochi, and through the Ise rice-offerings the patron of agriculture, industry, and the everyday nourishment of life. The 20-year rebuilding of both shrines (*Shikinen Sengū*) keeps her cult, like Amaterasu's, perpetually renewed.
