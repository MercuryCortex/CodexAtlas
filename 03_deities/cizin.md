# Cizin

The Yucatec / Lacandon Maya **death-and-earthquake spirit** — "the Stinking One" — depicted as a skeletal or decomposing figure adorned with a collar of eyeballs and accompanied by an owl. The relationship between Cizin and [[ah-puch]] (the Yucatec death-god named by Diego de Landa in 1566) is one of the longest-debated identifications in Maya scholarship: Thompson (1970) treated them as one figure with multiple names; Taube (1992) and the Lacandon ethnographic record (Perera & Bruce 1982) suggest distinct underworld figures with overlapping function.

The **contemporary Lacandon Maya** (in the rain-forest of eastern Chiapas, one of the few Maya groups that resisted Spanish conversion into the late 20th century) continue an explicit **Kisin/Cizin cult**: Kisin causes earthquakes by stomping his foot in the underworld, punishes the wicked dead, and is held in active religious memory.

Structurally Cizin parallels [[mictlantecuhtli]] (Aztec death-god) and [[supay]] (Andean underworld figure) as the **decomposition-personified** death deity — a category distinct from underworld-rulership (Hades, Yama, Ereshkigal) and distinct from death-as-ferryman (Charon, Anubis-psychopomp). The Mesoamerican death-deity complex consistently emphasizes the *putrefaction* aspect, which Cizin's name ("the Stinking One") makes explicit.
