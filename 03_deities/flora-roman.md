# Flora

Flora is the Roman goddess of **flowers and of all that blossoms** — the budding of crops, vines, and orchards as much as ornamental blooms — and so a deity of springtime fertility and youthful abundance. Her great festival, the **Floralia** (28 April – 3 May), was a famously exuberant and licentious affair of games, riotous colour, and theatrical performance, reflecting her association with the generative energy of the flowering season; the very ripening of the grain was thought to depend on her favour, and a *flamen Floralis* served her cult. Ovid, in the *Fasti*, gives her a Hellenizing origin-myth equating her with the Greek nymph **Chloris**, ravished by **Zephyrus** the west wind and rewarded with dominion over flowers — a poetic rather than cultic identification. Flora embodies the Roman recognition of blossoming itself as a divine power on which the harvest, and the renewal of the year, depended.
