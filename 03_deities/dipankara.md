# Dīpaṃkara

Dīpaṃkara ("Lamp-bearer"; Chinese **Randeng**) is one of the **Buddhas of the distant past** — and the pivotal one in the story of how the present Buddha came to be. According to the **Nidānakathā** and many Jātaka-cycle texts, countless aeons ago a wise ascetic named **Sumedha** (or Megha) met the Buddha Dīpaṃkara and, in an act of utter devotion, **laid down his own body and hair in the mud** so the Buddha could cross without soiling his feet. Dīpaṃkara then gave Sumedha the **prediction (vyākaraṇa)** that he would, far in the future, become the Buddha **Śākyamuni** (Gautama). This moment is the **mythic origin of the bodhisattva path** — the original vow that launched the future Buddha across his many lives — and a favourite subject of Buddhist art, especially in **Gandhāra** and among the **Newars of Nepal**, where Dīpaṃkara processions remain important. He is commonly grouped with **[[maitreya|Maitreya]]**, the Buddha of the future, to frame Śākyamuni within the long succession of **Buddhas across the three times** — past, present, and future. Dīpaṃkara embodies the immense temporal depth of the Buddhist vision of awakening.
