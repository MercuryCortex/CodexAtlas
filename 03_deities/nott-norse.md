# Nott

## Identity

Nott (Old Norse **Nótt**, "Night") is the Norse personification of night — a giantess of the line of Nörvi (Narfi), and the mother, through successive marriages, of some of the great cosmic figures: **Aud**, **Jörd** (Earth, the mother of [[thor]]), and **[[dagr]]** (Day). She is one of the personifications of the great regular motions of the sky.

## Mythological role

Snorri's *Gylfaginning* explains the alternation of night and day genealogically and astronomically. Nott, dark like her giant kin, has three husbands; by the last, **Delling** (a god of the Aesir, associated with dawn), she bears a son who is "bright and fair after his father's people" — **Dagr**, Day. The All-Father then takes mother and son, gives each a horse and chariot, and **sets them in the sky to ride around the earth every twenty-four hours, one after the other**. Nott rides first on the horse **Hrímfaxi** ("frost-mane"); each morning the foam from his bit falls as the dew on the earth. Dagr follows on **Skinfaxi** ("shining-mane"), whose mane lights up the sky and the world.

## Cross-tradition significance

Nott is the Norse member of the Indo-European family of **personified Night**, closely paralleling the Greek [[nyx-primordial]] (Nyx): both are female Night, both stand at the head of a day-and-night genealogy (Nyx with Erebus mothers Day/Hemera; Nott mothers Dagr), and both are imagined moving across the heavens. The mythologizing of the day–night cycle as the orderly chariot-ride of Night and Day across the sky is a vivid example of the Norse turn for explaining cosmic regularities through divine genealogy and motion.
