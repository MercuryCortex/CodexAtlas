# Eir

## Identity

Eir (Old Norse **Eir**, perhaps "mercy" or "help") is the Norse goddess of healing and medicine — named by Snorri as "**the best of physicians**." She is a somewhat shadowy figure, appearing in lists rather than narratives, but consistently associated with the skilled restoration of health.

## Mythological role

Eir is attested in three contexts that scholars have tried to reconcile. Snorri's *Gylfaginning* lists her among the **ásynjur** (goddesses), calling her the best of healers — placing her in the circle of [[frigg]]'s handmaidens. The Eddic poem *Fjölsvinnsmál* names an Eir among the maidens who attend the goddess Menglöth on the healing-hill **Lyfjaberg** ("hill of healing/medicines"), where the sick and wounded are made well. And a list of valkyrie-names also includes an Eir, linking her to the battlefield (where healing and the choosing of the slain meet). Whether these are one goddess seen in three roles or several figures sharing a name is debated, but the through-line is clear: Eir personifies the **healing art** and the power to make the injured whole.

## Cross-tradition significance

Eir is the Norse representative of the widespread **divinization of healing and medicine** — the deity who personifies the physician's skill. She stands as a functional parallel to the Greek healing complex of [[asclepius-greek]] and his daughters Hygieia ("health") and Panacea ("all-healing"), and to the Mesopotamian healing-goddess [[gula-akkadian]], "the great physician." The recurrence of dedicated healing deities — often female, often associated with a sacred hill or spring — across these traditions reflects the universal ancient concern with sickness, medicine, and the sacred sources of cure.
