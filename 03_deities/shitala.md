# Shitala

Shitala (*Śītalā*, "the Cool One") is the North Indian and Bengali **goddess of smallpox** and related pox-diseases, sores, and fevers — the deity who, like her southern counterpart, both **inflicts and heals** these afflictions, so that an outbreak is read as her arrival and her grace at once. Her very name is apotropaic: to call the goddess "cool" is to plead for the *cooling* of the fever's heat, and her worship centers on cooling substances — water, neem leaves, and offerings of **basi** (cold, previous-day food). She is iconically shown **riding a donkey**, holding a **broom** (to sweep disease away — or in), a **winnowing-fan**, and a **pot of cool water**. Theologically she is a form of the great **Goddess** (Devi/Shakti, linked to **[[parvati|Parvati]]** and Durga) in her disease-bearing aspect, and is closely paralleled by the Tamil **[[mariamman|Mariamman]]**. Though vaccination has ended smallpox itself, Shitala's cult endures across North India and Nepal as a protector of children and a guardian against epidemic disease.
