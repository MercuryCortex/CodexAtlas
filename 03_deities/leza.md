# Leza

Leza (also **Lesa**) is the **supreme creator and high god** of the south-central Bantu peoples — the **Tonga, Ila, Bemba, and Kaonde** of present-day Zambia and neighbouring regions. He is a sky- and weather-god of the classic African high-god type: the **omnipotent maker who gives and takes life**, so closely identified with the heavens that in everyday speech **the rain, thunder, and storm are simply his action** — "Leza is falling" can mean "it is raining," and the thunder is his voice. He is the source of fertility and of death alike, the one "who does what no other can," appealed to in drought, sickness, and bereavement. A famous Ila tale — "the woman who wearied of Leza" — tells of an old woman who, having lost all her kin to death, sets out to climb to heaven to demand of Leza why he has afflicted her, only to learn that none escape his ordering of life and death: a poignant meditation on suffering and the inscrutable high God. Leza is the south-central counterpart of **[[mulungu|Mulungu]]** and, more broadly, of the Akan **[[nyame|Nyame]]** — regional faces of the one supreme God over the Bantu spirit-world.
