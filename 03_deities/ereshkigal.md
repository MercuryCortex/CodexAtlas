# Ereshkigal

## Identity
Ereshkigal ("Lady of the Great Earth Below") is the queen of the Sumerian/Akkadian netherworld (Kur / Irkalla) and the elder sister of [[inanna-sumerian]]. Her seat is the *Ganzir* palace within the netherworld, behind seven gates. Her first consort is Gugalanna ("Bull of Heaven"), killed by Inanna and Gilgamesh in the [[phase-1-004-gilgamesh-old-babylonian]] tradition — Inanna's descent to Ereshkigal in [[phase-1-017-descent-of-inanna]] is partly framed as a funerary visit for Gugalanna. Her second consort is [[nergal]], in the Akkadian *Nergal and Ereshkigal* myth (Amarna and Sultantepe recensions, ~-1400 to -700). She is the principal Mesopotamian netherworld figure; her domain is the gloomy, dust-filled afterlife shared by all the dead regardless of moral standing.

## Earliest attestation
- Early Dynastic god lists.
- *Inanna's Descent to the Netherworld* (preserved in Old Babylonian copies, presumed Ur III in origin).
- The Akkadian *Descent of Ishtar* (Standard Babylonian period).
- *Nergal and Ereshkigal* in two main recensions (Amarna ~-1400, Sultantepe ~-700).
- Ritual and incantation texts naming her as the netherworld destination.

## Mythological role
Central in three of the most important Mesopotamian narratives concerning death and the underworld: *Inanna's Descent*, *Descent of Ishtar*, and *Nergal and Ereshkigal*. In all three she is portrayed as terrible but bound by the protocols of the netherworld — Inanna can be killed but not denied a substitute; Nergal cannot leave once he has eaten her food. She rules through procedural and oath-bound authority, not arbitrary terror.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[allatu-akkadian]]** — *ancient-identification*: standard Akkadian rendering.
- *Scholarly parallel*: Persephone (Greek), Hel (Norse), and other queen-of-the-dead figures have been compared; these are *scholarly-parallel*.
