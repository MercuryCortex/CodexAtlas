# Ibeji

Ibeji (*Ìbejì*, "the born-two") is the Yoruba orisha of **twins** — a deity of special importance because **Yorubaland has among the highest rates of twin-births in the world**, so that twins are a familiar and powerfully charged presence. Twins are regarded as **sacred and extraordinary**: a single soul shared between two bodies, bringers of **great fortune, wealth, and blessing** to the family that honours them properly — but also bearers of heightened spiritual power and danger if neglected. The first twin born is named **Taiwo** ("the one who tastes the world first"), sent ahead to test it by the **Kehinde**, who though born second is considered the **elder**. Because twin-infant mortality was high, a remarkable practice grew up around their cult: when a twin dies, a small wooden figure, an ***ere ibeji***, is carved and thereafter **cared for as the living child** — fed, washed, clothed — so that the surviving twin's soul-partner is kept content and the family's fortune preserved. Twins are associated with the thunder-orisha **[[shango|Shango]]**. Ibeji embodies the Yoruba reverence for twin-birth as a visitation of doubled, sacred power.
