# Savitr

## Identity

Savitr (Sanskrit **Savitṛ**, "the Impeller" or "Stimulator," from the root *sū*, "to impel, to bring forth") is a Vedic solar deity — the personification of the sun's **quickening, life-rousing power**. He is closely connected with, yet distinguished from, [[surya]] (the visible solar disk): Savitr is the divine impulse that the sun embodies, especially at the two thresholds of dawn and dusk. He is described in golden terms — golden-handed, golden-tongued, riding a golden chariot — and is among the most luminous figures of the *Rig Veda*.

## Mythological role

Savitr's characteristic action is **impulsion**: each morning he rouses the world, setting all beings to their tasks and sending the sun on its course; each evening he brings rest and draws the day to its close. He "impels" not only physical motion but also right action and the gods themselves. His supreme importance in later Hinduism rests on a single verse: the **Gayatri mantra** (Rig Veda 3.62.10), the most sacred and most recited of all Vedic prayers, is addressed to Savitr — "May we attain the excellent glory of the god Savitr, that he may **stimulate (impel) our thoughts**." Because of this, the mantra itself, and its personified goddess, came to be called **[[gayatri]]** (or Sāvitrī, "belonging to Savitr").

## Cross-tradition significance

Savitr is a refined instance of **solar theology** — the distinction between the sun as visible body and the sun as divine impelling power is a subtle Vedic contribution to the wider ancient family of sun-gods. As the deity of the supremely sacred Gayatri, he stands at the devotional center of the daily Hindu practice of *sandhya* (the dawn/dusk worship), making him one of the most ritually consequential of the Vedic gods even as his mythological personality remained abstract.
