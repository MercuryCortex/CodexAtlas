# Simargl

Simargl (also **Semargl**) is one of the more enigmatic gods of pre-Christian Rus' — named in the *Russian Primary Chronicle* among the six deities whose idols **Prince Vladimir erected at Kiev in 980**, yet given no description there or anywhere else, so that almost everything about him is inference. The leading scholarly view connects both his **name and his form** to the Iranian **Simurgh / Senmurv**, the great winged dog-bird of Iranian myth that perches on the tree bearing all seeds and scatters them across the earth — which would make Simargl a **winged-hound guardian of plants, seeds, and new growth**, a protective creature mediating between earth and sky, and another trace (with **[[khors|Khors]]**) of **Iranian influence** on the steppe-bordering East Slavs. (A famous textual curiosity: some later manuscripts mis-divide the name into two gods, "Sim and Rgl.") Simargl belongs to **[[perun|Vladimir's pantheon]]**, the short-lived official cult of Kiev swept away by the conversion to Christianity. He stands as one of the clearest hints of how far Iranian religious imagery reached into the early Slavic world.
