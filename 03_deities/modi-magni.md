# Modi and Magni

## Identity

Modi (**Móði**, "wrath" or "courage") and Magni (**Magni**, "the mighty," "strength") are the two sons of [[thor]], the thunder-god. Their names personify the two components of their father's might — battle-courage and sheer physical strength — and their chief mythological significance is that they are among the gods destined to **survive Ragnarök** and to inherit Thor's hammer Mjölnir in the renewed world.

## Mythological role

Magni's birth and precocious strength are told in the myth of Thor's duel with the giant **Hrungnir**. When Thor kills Hrungnir, the giant falls with his leg pinning Thor to the ground, and none of the gods can free him — until the three-night-old Magni, son of Thor by the giantess **Járnsaxa**, lifts the giant's leg away as if it were nothing, declaring that he would have killed the giant with his bare fist. Thor rewards him with the horse Gullfaxi. Modi appears chiefly in the Ragnarök prophecies: the poem *Vafþrúðnismál* foretells that "Modi and Magni shall have Mjölnir" when the battle-strife ends — that is, after Thor has fallen slaying the world-serpent [[jormungandr]], his sons will take up his hammer in the world reborn.

## Cross-tradition significance

Modi and Magni belong to the distinctively Norse theme of **survival and renewal beyond the end of the world**. Norse cosmology is unusual in its vivid eschatology: Ragnarök destroys the gods and the cosmos in fire and flood, but a remnant survives — Vidar and Vali, the returning Baldr, and Thor's sons Modi and Magni, who carry their father's power (Mjölnir) into the new age. As the inheritors of the thunder-god's weapon, they embody the continuity of divine strength across cosmic death and rebirth, a counterpart to the renewal-after-catastrophe motifs found in other traditions' flood and end-time narratives.
