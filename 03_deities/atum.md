# Atum

## Identity

Atum is the self-emergent primordial creator of Egyptian Heliopolitan theology — the "Complete One" (the name *itm* derives from the verb *tm*, "to be complete, to be perfect, to finish"). He emerges spontaneously from the primordial waters of [[nun]], stands alone on the first mound (the *benben* — the primeval hill that first rose from the receding waters), and generates the first divine couple [[shu]] (air) and [[tefnut]] (moisture) by self-procreation. The act of creation is variously described: by masturbation (most explicitly in [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] PT 600, where his hand is personified as a goddess), by spitting them forth, or by sneezing. These variants coexist within the corpus and are not treated as contradictory — they are multiple facets of the same theological event: differentiation out of undifferentiated unity.

From Shu and Tefnut descend [[geb]] (earth) and [[nut]] (sky), and from them the second-generation gods [[osiris]], [[isis-egyptian]], [[set]], and Nephthys — the Heliopolitan **Ennead** (nine gods, with Atum as first). Atum is paired and fused with [[ra]] as Atum-Ra: where Ra is the visible daily sun in his noon power, Atum is the primordial creator-origin and the sun at evening — the aged, descending form. The Pyramid Texts already use "Atum-Ra" as a unified theological formula (PT 217).

## Mythological role

Atum's mythology is primarily cosmogonic, not narrative — he is the principle from which the world begins, not a hero who fights or journeys. He represents the Egyptian theological concept of *self-emergent existence*: the universe begins not by combat (contrast the Mesopotamian *Enuma Elish*, where Marduk slays Tiamat) but by a self-actualizing principle differentiating itself out of undifferentiated potency ([[nun]]). James P. Allen (1988) calls this the **Heliopolitan cosmogony** and distinguishes it carefully from the Memphite Theology of [[ptah]] (creation by divine word/*Logos*) and the Hermopolitan Ogdoad (primordial chaos-pairs preceding creation).

Atum's most theologically remarkable appearance is his **eschatological role** in Coffin Texts 76–80 and Book of the Dead Spell 175. When Osiris asks him what the end of time will be, Atum replies:

> "I am going to destroy all I have made. This land will return to the primordial flood (*Nun*) as in its first state. I am what will remain, together with Osiris, after I have assumed my forms as a snake that no man knows and no god sees."

This is one of the oldest documented eschatological "return to origin" motifs in human history — the complete cosmos (created from Nun by Atum's differentiation) returns to Nun, with Atum alone subsisting as a serpent in the primordial ocean.

## Self-Created God: cross-tradition parallel

| Tradition | Figure | Mechanism | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Egyptian (Heliopolitan)** | **Atum** | Emerges from primordial Nun by self-procreation (masturbation/spit/sneeze); creates the first pair | PT 600; Allen 1988 |
| **Vedic** | **Purusha** / **Prajapati** | Cosmic being who is both the creator and the material of creation; sacrifices himself to generate the cosmos (Rig Veda 10.90) | Rig Veda 10.90; O'Flaherty 1981 |
| **Egyptian (Hermopolitan)** | **Ogdoad** | Eight primordial powers in four male-female pairs (Nun/Naunet, Heh/Hauhet, etc.) precede and generate creation without themselves being created | Hermopolitan cosmogony; Allen 1988 |
| **Neoplatonic** | **The One** | Eternal, self-subsistent, generates Nous by a kind of self-emanation that doesn't deplete it; creation without causation from outside | Plotinus, *Enneads* I.7, V.1 (Tier 1) |
| **Gnostic** | **Monad / Bythos** | Ineffable, self-contained first principle from whom the divine pleroma emanates in Aeons; the Valentinian *Bythos* ("Depth") as first cause | Irenaeus quoting Valentinus, *Against Heresies* I.1 (Tier 1) |

**Edge type: parallel-motif** — independent convergence on the theological problem of self-caused first existence. No documented transmission from Atum to Neoplatonism; however, Egyptian Heliopolitan theology was known in broad outlines to Hellenistic authors, and the problem of the uncaused first cause is a pan-human philosophical problem (Allen 1988; Hornung 1982).

## Atum as the First and Last Man — Christian Last Man parallel

Atum's role as both the primordial being and the one who survives cosmic dissolution makes him the Egyptian anchor of the "return to origin" eschatology. This has a remarkable structural parallel in Paul's "second Adam":

> "For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all shall be made alive... The first man Adam became a living being; the last Adam, a life-giving spirit." (1 Corinthians 15:22, 45)

Paul's theology of the "first Adam" (beginning) and "last Adam" (Christ as eschatological completion) maps structurally onto Atum's dual role:

| Egyptian | Pauline-Christian |
|---|---|
| Atum = the "Complete One" who initiates creation | Adam = the "first man" from whom all human life derives |
| Atum's self-creation from Nun = origin of differentiated existence | Adam's formation from dust = origin of mortality |
| Atum at the end of time dissolves back into Nun as a serpent | The "last Adam" (Christ) brings creation to eschatological completion; "God may be all in all" (1 Cor 15:28) |
| The cosmos returns to where it began (Nun = no differentiation) | All things "subjected" — cosmic unity restored through Christ |

**This is the oldest documented eschatological "first-and-last" motif** (Coffin Texts and BD 175 predate Paul by at least 1,500 years). Paul was almost certainly not directly influenced by Egyptian Atum theology — but the theological *structure* of a first being who is also the agent of cosmic closure is documented in Egyptian theology long before it appears in Christianity.

**Edge type: parallel-motif** (no demonstrated direct influence). Wire: [[tradition-christianity-canonical]], [[logos-johannine]].

Note: The serpentine form Atum assumes at the end of time is also interesting in light of the Edenic serpent in Genesis — both mark the boundary between the primordial state and differentiated human existence, though the valences are opposite (Atum's serpent = sacred continuity; Eden's serpent = transgression). This is a contested parallel and is noted as structural resemblance only.

## Atum-Ra synthesis

The compound "Atum-Ra" or "Ra-Atum" appears already in the Pyramid Texts (the oldest surviving Egyptian religious corpus, c. 2350 BCE) and represents the theological fusion of the two greatest solar-creator figures in Egyptian religion. Ra is the visible, powerful sun at noon, sailing across the sky in his solar boat. Atum is the primordial originating force and the aging, setting sun descending into the western horizon toward the Duat (underworld). In the *Amduat* (the royal underworld book), the sun fuses with Osiris at midnight and is reborn as [[ra]] at dawn — but the *beginning* of the solar journey into night is under Atum's identity.

The synthesis formula means: the same divine creative power that initiated the cosmos (*Atum*) is also the power that perpetually re-creates itself through the daily solar cycle (*Ra*). This is not conceptually different from the distinction between *primordial creation* and *cyclical maintenance* — Atum handles the ontological origin, Ra handles the cosmological cycle.

## Disputes

- **The act of creation.** Masturbation (PT 600, Coffin Texts) vs. spit/sneeze variants — Allen (1988) treats all three as simultaneous facets of a single theological concept rather than as competing traditions. The hand that Atum uses in masturbation is personified as the goddess Iusaaset ("she who comes and is great"), turning the solo act into a primordial coupling.
- **Atum and the "first monotheism" question.** Hornung (1982) specifically argues that Egyptian theology — including the Heliopolitan system — is emphatically *polytheistic* in structure: the gods are differentiated aspects of a divine reality, not subordinated to a single supreme god. This is a standing debate in Egyptology.
- **The eschatological dissolution and its date.** The Atum-Osiris dialogue in BD 175 / CT parallel is one of the rare Egyptian eschatological texts. Its pre-Pauline date (minimum c. 2000 BCE for CT forms) is secure; the *theological* implications are debated.
