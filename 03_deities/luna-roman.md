# Luna

Luna is the Roman goddess of the **moon** — the night-light by which months were measured (the very word *month* tracking her phases) and the celestial counterpart to **Sol**, the sun. Her cult was old: tradition placed a temple to Luna on the **Aventine** and a shrine of *Luna Noctiluca* ("Luna who shines by night") on the **Palatine**, lit through the night. In art she drives a **two-horse chariot (biga)** — paired against Sol's four-horse *quadriga* — her crescent at her brow and a veil arching above her like the vault of the night sky. The Romans equated her straightforwardly with the Greek **[[selene|Selene]]**, and over time her lunar role was increasingly drawn into the composite moon-goddess identified with **[[diana-roman|Diana]]** and Hecate. As one half of *Sol et Luna*, Luna stood for the ordered alternation of day and night in the Roman cosmos.
