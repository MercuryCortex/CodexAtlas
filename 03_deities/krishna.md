# Krishna

The eighth and most theologically elaborated *avatāra* of Vishnu (see [[avatar-doctrine]]), and in the Gaudiya tradition (and certain Puranas) *svayam bhagavan* — the supreme deity in his own right, of whom Vishnu is itself a partial expression. Krishna's life spans three distinct narrative-theological registers: the cowherd-child of Vrindavan (playful, erotic, intimate with Radha and the *gopis*); the warrior-counselor of Kurukshetra (deliverer of the *Bhagavad Gita* to Arjuna); and the dying king of Dvaraka.

Earliest attestations: Vasudeva-Krishna cults at Mathura attested archaeologically and epigraphically from the 2nd century BCE (Heliodorus pillar, ~-110, records a Greek ambassador as a *Bhagavata*). The Mahabharata's Krishna is a primarily warrior figure; the Vrindavan-cowherd Krishna develops in the Harivamsha and Bhagavata Purana (~+800–1000). The bhakti-devotional Krishna of Tulsidas, Chaitanya, Mirabai, and Surdas is the late Phase 5 / Phase 6 form.

Modern reception: ISKCON (the Hare Krishna movement, A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, founded 1966) brought Gaudiya Vaishnavism to the global West.
