# Krishna

## Identity

Kṛṣṇa is the central deity of Vaiṣṇava Hinduism in most of its post-classical (~ post-1000 CE) forms — supreme Lord, cosmic instructor, cowherd, lover, child, warrior, and ultimate object of *bhakti*-devotion ([[bhakti-devotion]]). Iconographically: dark-blue or black-skinned, in cowherd-pastoral form he plays the *vēṇu* (flute) while dancing with the *gopīs* of Vraja-Vṛndāvana; in royal form he wears the peacock-feather crown, yellow garments, and bears the Sudarśana cakra (discus). Theologically, classical Pāñcarātra and Bhāgavata traditions treat Kṛṣṇa as the eighth *avatāra* of Viṣṇu (with [[rama]] as seventh); Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya, 16th c.) elevates him to *svayaṃ bhagavān* — Kṛṣṇa is the supreme Lord himself, of whom Viṣṇu is a partial expansion.

The principal narrative sources are the *Mahābhārata* (where Kṛṣṇa is the Yādava prince, ally and tactical advisor to the Pāṇḍavas, and the cosmic teacher of the *Bhagavad Gītā*, [[phase-2-027-bhagavad-gita]]); the *Harivaṃśa* and *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* (which give the Yādava-Vrishni warrior-prince narrative and early cowherd material); and especially the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* ([[phase-5-050-bhagavata-purana]], ~+900), Book X, which is the locus classicus of the *gopī-līlā*, the *rāsa-līlā* dance, the eroticized-mystical theology of *prema*-bhakti, and the foundation of medieval Krishna-devotion. The *Bhagavad Gītā* (within *Mahābhārata*, redacted c. 2nd c. BCE – 2nd c. CE) is the most influential single Hindu text, declaring *bhakti-yoga* as the supreme spiritual path and Kṛṣṇa as the *puruṣottama* (supreme Person) of whom all gods are aspects.

## Earliest attestation

The historical-religious dossier is complex (Hardy 1983, Bryant 2007): the cult of the *Vāsudeva* (Yādava-Vrishni hero) is attested epigraphically by ~2nd c. BCE (Heliodorus Garuḍa-pillar at Besnagar, ~110 BCE); the Vedic *Kṛṣṇa-Āṅgirasa* of the *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* 3.17 is a separate (older) figure of unclear connection; the *Gopāla* cowherd-deity emerges in the Tamil-medium *Āḻvār* poetry of South India (6th–9th c.) and is fused with Vāsudeva-Kṛṣṇa in the *Bhāgavata Purāṇa*'s northern Sanskritization of southern bhakti material — Hardy's central thesis. The classical synthesized Kṛṣṇa of post-1000-CE Vaiṣṇavism is the product of this long syncretic process.

## Manifestations in vault documents

- **Bhagavad Gītā / Mahābhārata:** [[phase-2-027-bhagavad-gita]] within [[phase-2-017-mahabharata-ramayana-oral-layers]] — the Pāṇḍava ally and cosmic teacher of Arjuna; *Bhagavad Gītā* the central Hindu theological text.
- **Bhāgavata Purāṇa Book X:** [[phase-5-050-bhagavata-purana]] — locus classicus of the gopī-līlā and rāsa-līlā.
- **Mirabai's Krishna-bhakti:** [[phase-6-013-mirabai-bhajans]] — Mirabai's *padas* (~1500–1545) of erotic-mystical longing for *Giridhar Gopāl* (Krishna-as-lifter-of-Govardhana).
- **Tulsidas:** [[phase-6-019-tulsidas-ramcharitmanas]] — Tulsidas centers his bhakti on Rāma but acknowledges Kṛṣṇa.
- **Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism:** [[phase-6-020-chaitanya-charitamrita]] — Caitanya Mahāprabhu (1486–1534) inaugurates Bengali Krishna-bhakti; the *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* (Krishnadāsa Kavirāja, c. 1615) is its theological capstone and the foundational text of modern *gauḍīya* (Bengali) Krishna-theology.
