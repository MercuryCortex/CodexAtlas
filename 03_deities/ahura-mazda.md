# Ahura Mazda

## Identity
Ahura Mazda ("Wise Lord", from Avestan *ahura* "lord" + *mazdā* "wise") is the supreme uncreated god of Zoroastrianism. In the Gathas of Zarathustra he is the source of Truth (*aša*) and the Good, opposed by the Lie (*druj*) embodied in Angra Mainyu. Whether the Gathic Ahura Mazda is the *parent* of Spenta Mainyu (the Bounteous Spirit) and Angra Mainyu (the Hostile Spirit) — making him a transcendent first principle above the dualism — or whether Ahura Mazda *is* Spenta Mainyu and is co-eternal with Angra Mainyu, is a foundational interpretive question dividing Zoroastrian theology (Zurvanism took the former view; mainstream Zoroastrianism in dualistic form took the latter).

Ahura Mazda is the chief named god in the Achaemenid royal inscriptions of Darius (Bisotun, ~-520 BCE) and Xerxes — "By the favor of Ahura Mazda I am king" — making him the only god of an early Iron Age world empire that we can name with certainty from contemporary state inscriptions. His worship continued through the Sasanian period (224–651 CE) and survives in the modern Parsi and Iranian Zoroastrian communities. Scholarly parallels to Vedic Varuṇa (Indo-Iranian Asura/Ahura sovereign) and to post-exilic Jewish YHWH (parallel transcendent monotheizing development under Persian conditions) are extensively debated.
