# Shams

Shams (*Šams*, "the Sun") is the **South Arabian sun-goddess** — and, unlike the masculine sun-gods of Mesopotamia and Egypt, a *feminine* solar deity, in keeping with a West-Semitic pattern in which the sun is conceived as a goddess. She was a major national and tribal deity, especially prominent in **Ḥimyar**, where she appears with cult-titles such as *Dhāt-Baʿdān* and *Dhāt-Ḥimyam* ("She of Baʿdān/Ḥimyam"), and she had temples and dedications across the South Arabian kingdoms. Her name shares the common Semitic sun-root (š-m-š) with the Mesopotamian **[[utu-shamash|Shamash]]**, but the relationship is one of shared linguistic inheritance with reversed gender rather than borrowing. As the great luminary of the South Arabian sky, Shams stood among the chief deities of the region's polytheism until the spread of monotheism (Judaism, then Christianity and Islam) displaced the old pantheon in the centuries before Muhammad.
