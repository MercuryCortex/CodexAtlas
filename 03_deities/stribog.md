# Stribog

Stribog is the Slavic god of wind and air, one of the six deities erected as idols by Prince Vladimir I of Kiev in 980 CE — the only documented *official* pre-Christian Slavic pantheon. The Primary Chronicle records Vladimir's installation of idols of Perun, Khors, Dazhbog, Stribog, Simargl, and Mokosh on a hill in Kiev.

His name likely derives from Proto-Slavic *\*stri-* (spread/scatter) + *\*bogъ* (deity/wealth) — "scatterer of wealth" or "distributor." The *Tale of Igor's Campaign* (12th c.) calls the winds "Stribog's grandsons," preserving a poetic epithet that suggests a mythology of winds as his descendants.

All six Vladimir-pantheon deities were superseded when Vladimir I adopted Byzantine Christianity and ordered the idols destroyed in 988 CE — one of the most precisely dated acts of religious transformation in medieval European history.

Stribog belongs to [[tradition-slavic]], the broader pre-Christian Slavic religious world, alongside pantheon-members [[perun]] (thunder), [[veles]] (underworld/cattle), and [[svarog]] (celestial fire). His aeolian structural parallel — deity of winds governing air and storms — appears across Indo-European traditions: Greek Aeolus, Vedic Vayu, Japanese Fujin; the shared pattern reflects an ancient functional categorization of weather-domain divine roles.
