# Yaśodā

## Identity
The foster-mother of the Vaishnava deity Krishna, wife of the cowherd-chieftain Nanda of Vraja (Vrindavan/Mathura region). Krishna's birth-mother is Devakī (in the Mathura jail); Vasudeva carries the newborn Krishna across the Yamuna to Yaśodā's village to escape Kamsa's slaughter of newborns. The Vraja-Yaśodā narratives are among the most theologically significant in the Krishna cycle: the foster-mother who binds the cosmic Lord with rope (the *Dāmodara* story — "Krishna of the bound belly"), who witnesses the *vātsalya-bhāva* (motherly love) as one of the highest devotional relationships to the divine in Gaudiya-Vaishnava theology. The *vātsalya-bhāva* is the relational stance one cultivates toward the divine *as one's child*.

## Earliest attestation
- The *Harivaṃśa* (appendix to the Mahābhārata, 4th–5th c. CE) preserves early Krishna-Yaśodā material.
- The *Bhāgavata Purāṇa* Book X (10th c. CE) is the canonical full development; Yaśodā is a central figure of Books 10.7–10.13.
- [[phase-6-020-chaitanya-charitamrita]] — Gaudiya-Vaishnava theological elaboration.

## Mythological role
- Foster-mother of Krishna; the relational figure through whom *vātsalya-bhāva* (motherly devotional posture) is theologically articulated.
- Witness to the child Krishna's miraculous play (*līlā*) in Vraja.
