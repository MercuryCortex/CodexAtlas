# Papa Legba

## Identity
The *lwa* of the crossroads in Haitian Vodou. Papa Legba is the gatekeeper between the human world and the world of the *lwa*; no Vodou ceremony begins without invoking him to open the gate. He is invariably depicted as old, infirm, walking with a cane, dressed in rags — paradoxically the most powerful of the *lwa* in his function. He speaks all human languages and is the translator between the spirit-world and human supplicants.

## Earliest attestation
Brought from West Africa (Fon Legba and Yoruba Èṣù) via the slave trade; documented in Saint-Domingue / Haitian sources from the 18th c. onward; Métraux 1959 is the standard mid-20th-c. attestation.

## Mythological role
Legba opens. In Vodou ceremony, the opening *priye Ginen* invokes Legba: *"Papa Legba, ouvri baryè-a pou mwen"* — "Papa Legba, open the gate for me." Until Legba opens, no other *lwa* can come; the gate stays shut. He is also, in the Petwo nasyon, *Met Kalfou* — the master of the crossroads at midnight, a more aggressive aspect.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[eshu]]** — *manuscript-transmission*: direct West-African origin. Papa Legba is Èṣù transformed by Saint-Domingue context, with the African young-virile aspect transmuting to the elderly-infirm Haitian iconography (a transformation that may itself be a Vodou theological move — the most powerful seeming to be the most vulnerable).
- **Saint Peter** — *folk-syncretism*: Peter holds the keys to heaven's gate; the operational pairing is direct.
- **Saint Lazarus** (regional variation in some Vodou lineages) — *folk-syncretism*: Lazarus's beggar-iconography pairs with Legba's ragged-cane appearance.

## Iconography
Old man with cane / crutches; ragged clothes; pipe; straw hat; crossroads-imagery; black-and-white or red-and-black coloration.
