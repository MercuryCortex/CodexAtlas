# Bolon Tzakab

The Classic Maya **"Nine Generations / Nine Lineages"** god — designated **God K** in the alphabetical typology of Classic Maya deities (the convention introduced by Paul Schellhas in 1904 and still in use). Bolon Tzakab is the deity of **dynastic legitimacy** and royal lineage transmission — the supernatural underwriting of Maya kingship.

**The serpent-foot iconography.** God K is diagnosable by his **one leg ending in a serpent** — the most distinctive Maya divine iconographic motif. The **manikin sceptre** carried by Maya kings on stelae and lintels depicts Bolon Tzakab in miniature, his serpent-foot extending toward the king's hand: the sceptre is the visible token of dynastic legitimacy, the king's claim to rule sourced in God K's continuing presence.

**God K and the Maize God paired.** Recent epigraphy (Stuart 2005; Freidel, Schele & Parker 1993) reads the Classic Maya royal-ideology as a coupling of two divine identities: the king is the **Maize God** ([[hun-hunahpu]]) at apotheosis-and-resurrection, and the king carries **God K** (Bolon Tzakab) at coronation-and-legitimation. The two are conceptually linked but iconographically distinct — Maize God = the king's life-cycle and resurrection-through-corn; God K = the king's lineage-position and serpentine-divine-sanction.

The **Chilam Balam** colonial-Yucatec prophetic books continue to name Bolon Dzacab, indicating the cult survived into the post-Conquest period in attenuated form before fading from active practice.
