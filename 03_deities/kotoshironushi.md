# Kotoshironushi

Kotoshironushi (Japanese *Koto-shiro-nushi*, "master of word-affairs / the spoken matter") is an oracular Shinto kami, the son of the great Izumo deity **[[okuninushi]]**. His decisive role comes in the **kuni-yuzuri** ("transfer of the land") episode of the *Kojiki* and *Nihon Shoki*: when the heavenly deities demand that Okuninushi cede the rule of the earthly realm, Okuninushi defers the answer to his son. Kotoshironushi — found fishing at Cape Miho — gives the **divine assent**, agreeing that the land should be surrendered to the heavenly grandchild, then claps his hands in a reverse (*ama no sakate*) and conceals his boat behind a green fence, withdrawing from the world. As the kami who *speaks the divine will*, he is associated with oracle and divination. In later popular religion he is widely fused with **Ebisu**, the cheerful fishing-and-fortune god of the Seven Lucky Gods — the fishing-rod-and-sea-bream image at Miho and Nishinomiya — carrying his cult into the everyday devotion for prosperity.
