# Cipactli

## Identity
Cipactli is the Aztec primordial earth-monster — a vast composite creature, part crocodilian, part shark, part multi-mouthed chaos-beast, floating on the primordial sea before creation. The name derives from Nahuatl roots meaning "crocodile" or "caiman." Cipactli is not a deity in the cult sense — no temples, no priests, no worship — but a cosmogonic precondition: the raw material of the world, a chaos-body that must be killed and dismembered so that ordered creation can be made from its substance. In Aztec cosmogony, Cipactli is the answer to the question: what was the world made *from*?

The key act: [[quetzalcoatl]] and [[tezcatlipoca]] used Tezcatlipoca's own foot as bait, dangling it in the primordial sea. Cipactli rose and bit off Tezcatlipoca's foot — this is why Tezcatlipoca is always depicted with a mirror or obsidian disk where his foot should be. Then the two gods seized Cipactli and tore the creature apart. The earth (*tlaltecuhtli*, "earth lord") was made from Cipactli's back; the sky from its body raised upward; the waters from its own fluids. The primordial monster became the cosmos. But Cipactli's spirit survived in the earth — the Aztecs conceived the earth as a living, hungry entity with mouths at every joint, always demanding blood and hearts to be fed. This is why sacrifice was cosmologically necessary: to feed the earth-body that is Cipactli, forever hungry.

## Earliest attestation
- Aztec codices and early colonial chronicles. The *Historia de los Mexicanos por sus Pinturas* (c. 1535) is among the earliest post-conquest texts recording the Cipactli cosmogony. Florentine Codex ([[phase-8-004-florentine-codex-sahagun]]) provides the systematic account of Nahua cosmology in which Cipactli figures. Codex Borgia ([[phase-8-002-codex-borgia]]) contains iconographic evidence of the earth-monster concept. The figure of the earth-crocodilian has pre-Aztec Mesoamerican antecedents going back to Olmec and Teotihuacan iconography.

## Mythological role
In the Aztec cosmogonic narrative: after Omeyocan (the place of duality) had generated the active gods, and after the Four Suns had been created and destroyed, the primordial sea was the only substrate. Cipactli floated in it — a monster with mouths at every joint, perpetually hungry. Quetzalcoatl and Tezcatlipoca drew it to the surface. The resulting battle was violent; Tezcatlipoca lost his foot to Cipactli's jaws. Then the two gods split the monster: its back became the earth (*tlaltepactli*), its body was raised to form the sky. Earth was thus a living wounded body.

The cosmological consequence: the earth is animate, suffering, and hungry. Every earthquake is Cipactli stirring. The mouths on the earth-monster's body are why the earth consumes the dead and demands offerings. Human sacrifice in Aztec theology was partly a feeding of the world-body — without blood, the earth-beast starves, and the earth collapses back into chaos.

The first day-sign in the Aztec ritual calendar (tonalpohualli) is **Cipactli** — the primordial crocodilian marks the beginning of all time-counting. The calendrical position underlines its cosmogonic primacy.

## Cross-tradition identifications — MASSIVE WIN: the Dismembered-Body Cosmogony

Cipactli is the Mesoamerican instance of one of the most globally widespread cosmogonic patterns: **the world is created from the dismembered body of a primordial being**.

- **[[tiamat]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: This is the closest structural match in the Old World. In the Babylonian *Enuma Elish* (c. 1200 BCE), [[marduk]] kills the primordial sea-dragon Tiamat, splits her body in two, and from the two halves makes the sky and the earth. Cipactli is a water-floating chaos-monster; Tiamat is the primordial sea itself personified. Both are killed by the active god of order; both have their bodies converted into cosmic structure. The primordial water-monster dismembered to create the sky and earth is one of the strongest Aztec-Babylonian structural parallels in the vault.

- **[[ymir]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: In Norse cosmogony (*Prose Edda*, Snorri; [[phase-8-015-prose-edda-snorri]]), Odin and his brothers kill the primordial giant Ymir and dismember him. His flesh becomes the earth, his blood the seas, his bones the mountains, his skull the sky, his brain the clouds. The structural identity with the Cipactli cosmogony is near-complete: primordial being floating in void/sea → killed by the active gods → body dismembered → each part converted into a cosmic domain. Both Cipactli and Ymir leave a living residue: Cipactli's earth hungers; Ymir's blood floods the proto-giants.

- **[[purusha]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: The Rigveda (10.90, c. 1200 BCE) describes the sacrifice and dismemberment of Purusha, the cosmic giant, by the gods. His mouth becomes the Brahmin caste; his arms the Kshatriya; his thighs the Vaishya; his feet the Shudra. His mind becomes the moon; his eye the sun; his breath the wind; his navel the atmosphere; his head the sky. The pattern is identical: the primordial being's body is the material of cosmic structure, distributed by sacrifice. Purusha's dismemberment also grounds the social order, extending the cosmogonic body-as-cosmos logic into society.

**The pattern across traditions**: a primordial body (chaos-being, sea-monster, giant, cosmic person) must be killed/sacrificed and its parts distributed into the ordered cosmos. This motif appears in Aztec (Cipactli), Babylonian (Tiamat), Norse (Ymir), Vedic (Purusha), and Chinese (*Pangu*) traditions. Michael Witzel (2012) argues this is among the oldest recoverable mythological patterns, with possible deep-time roots (Laurasian mythological family). Whether the pattern reflects parallel development from a common cosmological logic (the world as body) or genuine ancient transmission is the key open question.

## Iconography
Cipactli in the codices is depicted as a large crocodilian floating in water, with mouths (with flint-knife teeth) at every joint of its body — elbows, knees, shoulder joints. This "mouths everywhere" iconography emphasizes the earth's perpetual hunger. The Aztec date-sign for the first day of the ritual calendar (1-Cipactli) uses a stylized crocodilian head. Earth-monster imagery in Aztec sculpture (notably the famous *Tlaltecuhtli* monolith) shows the earth as a squatting, open-mouthed chaos-creature — the residue of Cipactli in Aztec art.

## Disputes
- Whether Cipactli is a deity, a monster, or a cosmogonic substance is a genuine scholarly question. It is never worshipped, has no cult, and has no agency beyond hunger — yet it precedes and enables creation. Most scholars treat it as a cosmogonic motif rather than a deity proper. It is included here as a deity node because it is a named sacred being with cosmological significance, fitting the vault's inclusive approach to sacred figures.
- The relationship between Cipactli and *Tlaltecuhtli* (the earth-lord/earth-lady) in Aztec texts varies across sources — sometimes they are distinct, sometimes the earth-monster is addressed under Tlaltecuhtli's name.
