# Gaia

## Identity
Gaia (or Gē, "Earth") is the primordial Greek Earth-Mother. In Hesiod's Theogony, she emerges third after Chaos and Tartarus (so not literally first, but functionally so as the foundation of the cosmos); she produces Uranus parthenogenetically as her equal partner; the cosmos is generated from their union. She instigates the castration of Uranus (helping Kronos to overthrow him), helps Zeus overthrow the Titans, and gives birth to Typhoeus to challenge Zeus — repeatedly cast in the role of the primordial mother whose generative power both founds and challenges the male sovereigns.

Gaia had limited but real cult — most notably as the *original* possessor of the Delphic oracle before Apollo took it from her (the Pythian Apollo cult preserves the memory of this prior holder). She is also invoked in solemn oaths (Hesiod's "By Earth and starry Heaven") as the most ancient witness. The Roman identification with Tellus / Terra is *interpretatio graeca*. Cross-cultural parallels in the Earth-Mother + Sky-Father primordial-couple pattern are widespread — Mesopotamian An-Ki, Vedic Dyauṣ Pitṛ-Pṛthivī Mātṛ, etc. — and may reflect a deep substrate or convergent religious imagination.
