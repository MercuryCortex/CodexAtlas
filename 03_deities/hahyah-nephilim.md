# Hahyah (Nephilim, son of Shemihazah)

## Identity
**Hahyah** is the brother of [[ohyah-nephilim]] and second of [[semyaza]]'s named giant-sons in the **Book of Giants** ([[phase-3-033-book-of-giants-qumran-manichaean]]). The two brothers function together throughout the narrative — both receive Flood-foreshadowing dreams, both consult fellow giants, both send [[mahaway-nephilim]] to Enoch for interpretation. The pairing of two giant-brothers as parallel-named dreamers (Ohyah / Hahyah; the Aramaic forms differ by single letters) is one of the literary signatures of the Book of Giants.

## Earliest attestation
- **Qumran 4Q530** — Hahyah and Ohyah receive paired prophetic dreams of the Flood.
- **Manichaean Book of Giants** — Hahyah preserved across multiple Manichaean witnesses (Reeves 1992).

## Mythological role
Hahyah's narrative role parallels Ohyah's: he receives a vision of the impending Flood (in his case, of a great garden being uprooted), joins Ohyah in consulting the giant-council, and accepts Enoch's interpretation. The two brothers face their doom together in the final battle.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- The paired-brothers structure is a recurring Mediterranean and Mesopotamian narrative pattern (Castor and Pollux; the Mesopotamian Apkallu sages in pairs). No direct cross-tradition identification is attested for Hahyah specifically.

## Iconography
Not standardized.

## Disputes
- **Identity-of-pairing.** Whether Ohyah / Hahyah are originally distinct figures or a single figure split into two named parallels (a common pre-Christian-Jewish-apocalyptic narrative device). The Aramaic forms differ enough to support distinctness; the literary function pairs them as a unit.
