# Gadreel

## Identity
**Gadreel** is one of the named **Watchers** of 1 Enoch 69, distinguished by having **two** load-bearing transgressions assigned to him in the text:

1. **He led astray Eve** — 1 Enoch 69:6 identifies Gadreel as the seducer of Eve in Eden, making this Enochic text an early-Jewish identification of a *named* angelic-figure (not merely "the serpent" in unmarked Genesis 3 terms) with the Edenic temptation. This is theologically consequential: it is one of the earliest pre-rabbinic and pre-Christian texts to *name* the Edenic-tempter.
2. **He taught humanity the instruments of death (weapons of war)** — sword, dagger, shield, breastplate of bronze.

1 Enoch 69:6 (Nickelsburg-VanderKam 2004): *"And the third was called Gadreel. This one showed the sons of men all the deadly blows, and he led astray Eve, and he showed the weapons of death to the sons of men, the shield and the coat of mail, and the sword for slaughter, and all the weapons of death to the sons of men."*

## Earliest attestation
- **1 Enoch 69:6** — Book of Parables (~1st c. BCE–1st c. CE).
- **Ge'ez Mashafa Henok** — the only complete textual witness.
- The figure does *not* appear in the earlier Book of the Watchers (1 Enoch 1–36) chiefs-of-tens list at 6:7 or the teaching-roles list at 8:3 — Gadreel is specific to the later Book of Parables.

## Mythological role
Gadreel's *two* transgressions — Eve-seduction *and* weapons-of-war — are coupled in the Enochic text in a theologically significant way: the weapons that cause human violent death and the deception that introduced death into Eden are presented as a unified moral catastrophe, both attributable to a single Watcher-figure. This is the Enochic intensification of the Genesis 3 / Genesis 6 fusion: in mainstream Genesis the serpent of Eden (Gen 3) and the *bĕnê hāʾĕlōhîm* (Gen 6:1–4) who beget the Nephilim are separate narratives; the Enochic Book of Parables fuses them via Gadreel — the Watcher who is both the Edenic tempter and the bringer of war-instruments.

The identification of the Edenic-serpent with a named angelic-figure (rather than just "the serpent" of Genesis) prefigures the much-later mainstream-Christian identification of Satan with the serpent (Revelation 12:9 makes this explicit; pre-Revelation Jewish tradition is variegated). Gadreel is one of the *earliest* such identifications.

In Ethiopian Orthodox canonical reception, Gadreel is named and recognizable; in mainstream Western Christianity, the figure is essentially unknown until post-Bruce-1773 recovery of the Ge'ez text.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[satan-christian]] / [[satanael]]** — *parallel-form* / *partial-identification*: Gadreel as Edenic-tempter is the Watcher-tradition's antecedent for the later mainstream-Christian Satan-as-Edenic-serpent identification. The two figures are not equivalent (Gadreel is one Watcher among many; Satan is a singular adversary-figure) but they share the Edenic-tempter role.
- **Sammael / Samael** (later rabbinic tradition) — *parallel-form*: rabbinic tradition (Pirkei DeRabbi Eliezer ch. 13; later Kabbalistic literature) identifies the Edenic-serpent with Sammael; this is a parallel post-Enochic Jewish tradition rather than a direct continuation.

## Iconography
Not iconographically standardized in Ethiopian Christian art.

## Disputes
- **Date of the Book of Parables.** 1st c. BCE vs. 1st c. CE composition affects the relationship between Gadreel's Eve-seduction role and parallel rabbinic / Christian Edenic-serpent identifications. Most current scholarship dates the Parables pre-Christian (Nickelsburg-VanderKam 2012); some hold for early-Christian-era.
- **Etymology.** "Gad" (fortune?) + "El" (God); etymology not securely settled.
