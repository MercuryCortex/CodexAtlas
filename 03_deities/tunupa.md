# Tunupa

Tunupa (Aymara *Thunupa*) is the great **thunder-, lightning-, and volcano-god** of the Aymara peoples of the Lake Titicaca basin and the Bolivian altiplano — and simultaneously a **wandering culture-hero** who travels the high plateau teaching, judging, and being persecuted. As a weather-deity he governs the storms, the rain, and the fire of the volcanoes (the great peak **Cerro Tunupa** overlooking the Salar de Uyuni bears his name, and one tradition makes the vast salt-flat the tears or milk of Tunupa). As a culture-hero his legend strikingly resembles that of the creator **[[viracocha]]**: an itinerant teacher-figure, bearded in some tellings, who moves through the Collao establishing order and is mocked, attacked, and set adrift on Lake Titicaca in a reed boat — which cuts the channel of the Río Desaguadero as it goes. This wandering-teacher pattern led colonial chroniclers and missionaries to conflate Tunupa with Viracocha and even to cast him as a pre-Columbian Christian "apostle," a syncretism that illustrates how Andean and Christian sacred-history were blended after the conquest.
