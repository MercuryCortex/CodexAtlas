# Ein Sof (The Infinite)

## Identity
*Ein Sof* — literally "without end" / "the Infinite" — is the Kabbalistic name for God-in-himself, beyond and prior to all manifestation, beyond and prior to the ten *sefirot*. The term emerges in late 12th- to 13th-century Provençal and Spanish Kabbalah, where it names the absolute apophatic transcendence: that which cannot be predicated, named, or thought, and that which is the unmanifest source from which the sefirot emanate.

*Ein Sof* is sometimes paired with even further-apophatic designations (*Ayin* — Nothingness; *Or Ein Sof* — Infinite Light), and the relation between *Ein Sof* and the highest sefirah *Keter* is variously construed across Kabbalistic schools — some treat *Keter* as almost identical with *Ein Sof*, others as the first emanation from it. The doctrine permits Kabbalah's bold inner-divine descriptive language (about sefirotic dynamics, divine sacred marriages) without compromising God's absolute transcendence: all such descriptions apply to the manifest emanations, never to *Ein Sof* itself.

---

## Earliest attestation
The term first appears in 12th-c. Provençal Kabbalist writings (Isaac the Blind's circle, ~1200), is implicit in [[phase-5-026-sefer-ha-bahir]], and becomes central in [[phase-5-027-sefer-ha-zohar]] (~1280). The fully developed doctrine — including *Ein Sof*'s self-contraction (*tzimtzum*) — is later, in Lurianic Kabbalah (Phase 6, ~1570, [[phase-6-015-luria-vital-etz-chayyim]]).

---

## The Tzimtzum: Ein Sof's self-contraction

The most philosophically audacious development in Lurianic Kabbalah: *Ein Sof*, the Infinite, cannot simply emanate the world — the world has no space to exist if the Infinite fills all. Therefore *Ein Sof* must first **contract** (*tzimtzum*) — withdraw from a "region" within itself, creating the *tehiru* (void) into which the finite cosmos can come into being. Then the divine light re-enters the void through the *reshimu* (residue) and the *kav* (the ray of light).

This is not literal spatial contraction (Ein Sof has no spatial location) but a metaphysical self-limitation: God makes room for what is not-God. The theological implications are immense:
- Creation is an act of divine **self-limitation**, not divine self-expression
- The cosmos exists in a space "evacuated" by God — it is inherently incomplete, separated from the divine fullness
- This sets up the [[shevirat-hakelim]] (Breaking of the Vessels): the light re-entering the void shatters the first containers, scattering divine sparks into the husks (*kelipot*) — and makes [[tikkun-olam]] (cosmic repair) both necessary and possible

## MASSIVE WIN: The apophatic summit across five traditions

Ein Sof occupies the same structural position — the absolute prior to all predication, the source from which emanation proceeds — across five independent mystical traditions spanning 2,500 years. The convergence is the most important cross-tradition structural finding in the history of mysticism:

| Tradition | Figure | Period | Key text | What makes it apophatic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Neoplatonism (Greek) | [[the-one-plotinus]] | 3rd c. CE | *Enneads* VI.9 | "The One" is beyond Being, beyond Nous, beyond all predication; emanates but is not diminished; only known by "removal" (*aphaeresis*) |
| Kabbalah (Jewish) | **Ein Sof** | 13th c. | *Zohar*; Luria's *Etz Chayyim* | "Without end" — beyond all names of God, prior to all sefirot; the Zohar says of Ein Sof: "No thought can grasp You at all" |
| Christian mysticism | [[the-godhead-eckhart]] | 14th c. | *Sermons*; *The Nobleman* | The *Gottheit* ("Godhead") beyond God-as-Father-Son-Spirit; "the still desert" where no name can apply; the "Nothing" of pure being |
| Sufi Islam | [[al-haqq]] (Ibn ʿArabī) | 13th c. | *Fusus al-Hikam*; *Futuhat* | *al-Haqq* ("the Real") is the Absolute prior to all divine Names and Attributes; the Names are how the Absolute self-discloses, not what it is |
| Christian theosophy | [[ungrund]] (Boehme) | 17th c. | *Aurora* (1612); *Mysterium Magnum* (1623) | The divine "groundless" (*Ungrund*) prior to Wrath/Love, prior to any divine self-distinction; God's absolute prior to God's will |

**The structural identity:** all five are:
1. Prior to all emanations, names, attributes, and divine predicates
2. Known only negatively (by saying what the absolute is *not*)
3. The source from which a structured divine self-disclosure proceeds (Sefirot / Hypostases / Trinity / Attributes / divine Nature-principles)
4. Not a person, not a being, not a relation — the *condition of possibility* for being, persons, and relations

**The lineage question:** Neoplatonism → Kabbalah is historically documented (Provence Kabbalah absorbed Arab-mediated Neoplatonism; Altmann 1944). Kabbalah → Boehme is plausible via Christian Kabbalah (Pico → Reuchlin → Khunrath → Boehme's milieu; Weeks 1991). Eckhart and Ibn ʿArabī are likely independent convergences — apophatic logic produces this structure wherever thinkers push monotheism to its logical limit.

**Sells 1994** (*Mystical Languages of Unsaying*) argues these convergences are not accidental: apophatic language has a grammatical logic that generates similar structures wherever it operates. The "unsaying" — the move that names the absolute only to immediately unsay the name — is a trans-traditional mystical practice, not mere coincidence.

## The Daoist parallel: Wuji

Outside the Western-monotheist axis, Chinese cosmology offers a striking parallel:

**Wuji** (無極, "without limit," "the Limitless") is the state before *Taiji* (太極, the "Supreme Ultimate" — primordial yin-yang polarity). In the cosmogony of Zhou Dunyi (1017–1073) and the Neo-Confucian synthesis:
- Wuji produces Taiji (the first distinction, yin-yang)
- Taiji generates the Five Phases, Heaven and Earth, the Ten Thousand Things

*Wuji* = "without limit" and *Ein Sof* = "without end" are **literal semantic equivalents** in Chinese and Hebrew. Both name the state prior to the first cosmic distinction. The convergence is structural and linguistic — though there is no historical transmission between Song-dynasty Neo-Confucianism and 13th-century Provençal Kabbalah.

## Disputes

**Relationship to Keter.** Different Kabbalistic schools place *Ein Sof* outside the sefirotic system (most Castilian Kabbalists) or partially within it (some Geronese views). The Zohar sometimes treats *Keter* as almost synonymous with *Ein Sof*, other times as the highest sefirot facing toward Ein Sof.

***Tzimtzum* doctrine — literal vs. metaphorical.** Whether *Ein Sof* literally self-contracts to make space for creation (Luria, taken literally by some Hasidic schools) or this is purely metaphorical language for divine self-limitation (Cordovero, Schneur Zalman of Liadi's *Tanya*) — a debate that runs through Hasidism and continues in modern Jewish theology.
