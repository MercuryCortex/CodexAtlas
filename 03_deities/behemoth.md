# Behemoth

## Identity

Behemoth (Hebrew **בְּהֵמוֹת**, *bĕhēmôt*) is the great land-beast of the Hebrew Bible — the terrestrial chaos-creature that God parades before Job in the second divine speech (Job 40:15-24) immediately before the marine monster [[leviathan|Leviathan]]. The name is the plural of the ordinary Hebrew noun *bĕhēmâ* ("beast," "cattle," "animal"), here used as an **intensive plural** — "the Beast" of beasts, the supreme animal — rather than a true numerical plural. Like [[tehom|Tehom]] and Leviathan, Behemoth has no cult, no genealogy, and no anthropomorphic iconography: it is a cosmological creature held within Yahweh's creative jurisdiction, "the first of the ways of God" (Job 40:19), whom only its Maker can approach with a sword. In the Atlas it is placed among the chaos-beings as the **land-counterpart to Leviathan** — the two together forming the standing Hebrew chaos-monster dyad of sea and dry land.

## In the Book of Job

Behemoth's sole appearance in the Hebrew Bible is the showpiece of God's second answer to Job: "Behold now Behemoth, which I made along with you; he eats grass like an ox" (Job 40:15). The passage (Job 40:15-24) catalogues the creature's overwhelming bodily power — strength in the loins, force in the muscles of the belly (40:16); a tail that "sways like a cedar" (40:17); bones like **tubes of bronze**, limbs like **bars of iron** (40:18). It grazes on the mountains where the wild beasts play (40:20) and lies beneath the lotus among the reeds of the marsh, unmoved when the river rages: "if a river is turbulent he is not frightened; he is confident, though the Jordan rushes against his mouth" (40:23). The rhetorical climax is that no human can capture it — "Can one take him by his eyes, or pierce his nose with a snare?" (40:24) — only God, "the one who made him" (40:19), can bring near his sword.

The function of Behemoth in Job is **theodicy by way of zoology**: God's reply to Job's complaint is not a moral argument but a display of creatures at the limit of human mastery, of which Behemoth (land) and Leviathan (sea) are the two greatest. Their untameability is the point: the world contains powers Job cannot govern and barely comprehend, yet they remain God's creatures. This is the same domesticating, Wisdom-tradition move that Job 41 performs on Leviathan — the chaos-beast retained as exhibit of divine power rather than as enemy in combat.

## Cross-tradition significance (the Hebrew land-chaos beast)

Behemoth is comparatively legible chiefly as **the terrestrial half of the Hebrew chaos-monster pair**, the dry-land counterpart to the sea-chaos [[leviathan|Leviathan]]. The pairing is already implicit in the back-to-back Job 40-41 speeches and becomes explicit in the Second Temple period. In **1 Enoch 60:7-10**, the angel explains that on the day of creation the two monsters were separated: Leviathan, a female, was made to dwell in the abysses of the ocean, and Behemoth, a male, took possession of the waste wilderness east of the garden of Eden. **4 Ezra 6:49-52** gives the same separation a rationale — God created the two great creatures on the fifth day but, because the watery part of creation could not hold both, assigned one to the sea and one to the dry land, keeping them "to be eaten by whom you wish, and when." **2 Baruch 29:4** completes the trajectory: in the days of the Messiah, Behemoth will come forth from the land and Leviathan from the sea, and the two will become the **banquet of the elect**. The rabbinic tradition (Babylonian Talmud, **b. Bava Batra 74b-75a**) develops the same eschatological feast in detail.

Within the broader **[[chaoskampf|Chaoskampf]]** field, Behemoth occupies the chaos-beast slot alongside [[tiamat|Tiamat]] (Mesopotamia) and [[apophis|Apophis]] (Egypt) — but the parallel is **structural and typological, not a linguistic cognate**, and Behemoth's chaoskampf credentials are weaker than Leviathan's in one decisive respect: the Hebrew Bible preserves **no surviving Yahweh-vs-Behemoth combat fragment**. Where Leviathan carries explicit combat residue (Yahweh "crushed the heads of Leviathan," Ps 74:13-14; the dragon slain "in that day," Isa 27:1), Behemoth is never said to have been fought. Its place in the combat-complex is therefore **derivative** — it is drawn into the chaoskampf orbit by (a) its fixed coupling with the combat-monster Leviathan and (b) the eschatological re-deployment in 1 Enoch, 4 Ezra, and 2 Baruch, where both beasts, bound at creation, reappear at the end of the age (see [[apocalyptic-thunderer-vs-serpent]]). The honest framing: Behemoth is securely a **primordial cosmic beast** and securely **Leviathan's land-pair**; its identity specifically as a *defeated chaos-combatant* is a comparative inference, supported by the pairing and the eschatology rather than by any combat text.

## Disputes

**Mythic chaos-beast vs naturalistic hippopotamus.** The central interpretive split concerns what Job 40:15-24 describes. On the **naturalistic reading**, Behemoth is a real animal — almost universally identified as the **hippopotamus** (eats grass, lies in the marsh and reeds, unbothered by the river), occasionally the elephant or the wild ox/buffalo; on this view the divine speeches simply showcase the most imposing creatures of the natural world, and the plural *bĕhēmôt* is the ordinary word for "beast" magnified. On the **mythological reading** (developed by Gunkel's heirs and synthesized by Whitney 2006 and Batto 1992), Behemoth is a primordial chaos-creature whose pairing with the manifestly mythic Leviathan, "first of the ways of God" status, and later eschatological role mark it as more than a zoo specimen — the naturalistic features being the same domesticating overlay that turns Leviathan (Job 41) into God's creature rather than God's enemy. The detail that Behemoth eats *grass* and lies quietly in the reeds is read by naturalists as decisive for the hippopotamus and by mythologists as exactly the point of the domestication. Most critical commentators (e.g. Habel 1985) treat the two readings as compatible layers: a **mythic chaos-beast described in the idiom of an actual large animal**.

**Was Behemoth ever a combat-monster?** A related dispute is whether Behemoth had an independent chaoskampf myth now lost, or whether it was only ever the *land-pendant* invented to balance the sea-monster Leviathan. The absence of any combat fragment in the Hebrew Bible, and the fact that the developed pairing first appears in Second Temple texts, lead some scholars to regard Behemoth's chaos-monster status as **secondary** — a back-formation from Leviathan and the chaos-beast schema — rather than an inherited combat-myth in its own right.
