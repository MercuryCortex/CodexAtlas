# Metatron

## Identity
Metatron is the supreme angelic figure of late-antique and medieval Jewish mystical tradition — the chief of the heavenly court, the "Prince of the Face" (*Sar ha-Panim*), the scribe of heaven, and the most controversial figure in classical rabbinic angelology. In *3 Enoch / Sefer Hekhalot* (a Hekhalot text, ~5th–8th c.), Metatron is identified as the transformed patriarch Enoch — raised bodily to heaven (Genesis 5:24, "and Enoch walked with God, and he was not, for God took him"), translated into the angelic Metatron, enthroned on a throne like the divine throne, granted seventy names, and called by God "the Lesser YHWH" (*YHWH ha-Katan*). The figure caused acute rabbinic theological anxiety — the famous Babylonian Talmud passage (b. Hagigah 15a) describes the apostasy of Elisha ben Avuyah ("Aher"), who saw Metatron seated in heaven and exclaimed "perhaps there are two powers in heaven!" (*shtei reshuyot*), then fell into heresy. Subsequent rabbinic literature works to subordinate Metatron's status; in Kabbalah, Metatron retains a central role as the highest of the angels associated with the lowest sefirah Malkhut / Shekhinah.

## Earliest attestation
The name *Metatron* (etymology debated — possibilities include Latin *metator* "guide/measurer," Greek *meta* + *thronos* "behind the throne," or some Aramaic root) appears first in tannaitic-amoraic texts (~200–500 CE) — the Talmud and 3 Enoch are the earliest substantive sources. Iconographic representation is later (medieval Kabbalistic manuscripts).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[michael-archangel]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: both are chief angels; some functional overlap (intercessor for Israel); not identical in source texts.
- The Two-Powers-in-Heaven figure that early rabbinic Judaism polemicized against (Boyarin 2004 argues this is a binitarian Judaism within Second-Temple-period Israel that the rabbis subsequently suppressed) — Metatron's hypostatic-near-divine status preserves traces of this earlier binitarian theology.
- **[[jesus-of-nazareth]]** as the "Son of Man" in Christian theology — *parallel-motif*: Boyarin 2004 argues Christian Christology and the binitarian-Metatron tradition share a common Second-Temple Jewish substrate.

## Disputes
- **Etymology.** No consensus on the source of the name *Metatron*.
- **Status.** "Lesser YHWH" — does this make Metatron divine? Rabbinic tradition struggles with the implication; the Aher narrative serves as a cautionary boundary.
- **Continuity with Second-Temple "two-powers" theology.** Boyarin's controversial thesis (2004) — that the rabbinic Metatron preserves traces of a pre-rabbinic Jewish binitarianism that early Christianity also expressed.

## The Transformation Narrative (3 Enoch)

*3 Enoch / Sefer Hekhalot* (Hebrew, ~5th–8th century, pseudepigraphically attributed to Rabbi Ishmael) narrates the Enoch-Metatron transformation in detail. Rabbi Ishmael ascends to heaven and meets Metatron, who explains his origin:

> "I am Enoch son of Jared. When the generation of the Flood sinned and said to God 'Go away from us', the Holy One, blessed be He, took me from among them to be a witness against them in the high heavens."

Metatron describes his transformation:
1. **Translation**: God took Enoch from earth and "raised him up in a fiery chariot with fiery horses, servants of glory, to the highest heaven"
2. **Transformation of the body**: "My flesh was turned to flame, my sinews to blazing fire, my bones to juniper coals... the light of the Shekinah in my eyes was like the great light, the appearance of my eyeballs like the sun"
3. **Enthronement**: "The Holy One, blessed be He, placed for me a throne like the throne of glory... and I sat on it"
4. **The name**: "He called me by seventy names... and the greatest of them all is YHWH ha-Katan — the Lesser YHWH"
5. **The crown**: "He put on my head a crown and called me 'the lesser YHWH' in the presence of his whole household on high"

This is an explicit divine-vicegerent enthronement — exactly the Daniel 7 pattern (Son of Man receives universal sovereignty from the Ancient of Days) applied to the specific patriarch Enoch.

## The Aher Apostasy (b. Hagigah 15a)

The Babylonian Talmud records the story of Elisha ben Avuyah, who became an apostate and was called *Aher* ("the Other"). His apostasy was triggered by *seeing Metatron seated in heaven*:

> "Aher mutilated the shoots [of the heavenly garden]. Of him the Scripture says, 'Let not your mouth bring your flesh into guilt' (Eccl. 5:5)... Aher beheld Metatron, to whom had been given permission to sit and write down the merits of Israel. Said Aher: 'I have learned that there are two divine powers.' Immediately they brought Metatron forth and gave him sixty lashes of fire."

The extraordinary thing about this text: it reports that a recognized Jewish teacher *drew the correct theological conclusion* from what he saw — there *is* a second divine power enthroned in heaven — and this conclusion was declared heresy. Metatron is then immediately flogged to demonstrate his subordination.

The flogging of Metatron is an act of theological damage-control: the rabbis could not eliminate Metatron from the tradition (he was too embedded), so they staged his subordination within the narrative itself. But the tradition they were suppressing — the "two powers" theology — is preserved in the very act of suppression.

## The Logos Parallel

Metatron's structural position in the Hekhalot tradition is identical to Philo's Logos ([[logos-philonic]]):

| Metatron (Hekhalot) | Philonic Logos |
|---|---|
| "The Lesser YHWH" | "The Second God" (*Deuteros Theos*) |
| Scribe who records all creation | Blueprint of creation |
| Intermediary between God and the heavenly court | Intermediary between transcendent God and matter |
| Enthroned on a throne like God's throne | Called "God's throne" (*thronos theou*) |
| Given "seventy names" — all God's names | Called by all divine names: Son, Archangel, High Priest |
| Enoch-as-man transformed into divine figure | Not man-derived, but the logic points toward incarnation |

The step Philo does not take — which John 1 takes — is *identifying this figure with a specific historical person*. The Johannine move is: the Logos = Metatron = Jesus of Nazareth.
