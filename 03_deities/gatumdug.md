# Gatumdug

## Identity
Gatumdug was the **mother-goddess and divine protectress of the Sumerian city-state of Lagash**. In the local Lagashite pantheon she held the maternal/tutelary role — the goddess who "gives birth to" the city and its ruler and who nurtures and shields the community. She was venerated especially at Lagash and its sacred centre Girsu during the late Early Dynastic and Neo-Sumerian periods.

## Earliest attestation
Gatumdug is named prominently in the [[phase-1-018-gudea-cylinders|Gudea Cylinders]] (~2125 BCE), the longest extant Sumerian literary inscription, where ruler Gudea of Lagash addresses her as his divine mother in the dream-and-temple-building narrative: he calls on her protection and identifies her as the deity who engendered him in his royal-divine legitimacy. Her name also appears in earlier Lagashite royal inscriptions of the Early Dynastic period.

## Context
The Gudea Cylinders are in part a theological compendium of Lagashite religion at its mature stage. The senior pantheon — An, [[enlil]], Enki, [[ninhursag]] — presides above the city gods, while Gatumdug and the warrior-god Ningirsu function as the specifically Lagashite tutelary deities. Gatumdug's maternal patronage of the ruler is a standard Mesopotamian device of royal legitimation: the king is "the one whom the goddess engendered."

## Cross-tradition findings
Gatumdug is best understood as a **local instantiation of the widespread Mesopotamian mother-goddess type**, structurally parallel to [[ninhursag]] (the great Sumerian birth/mother-goddess) without a documented merger. The city-tutelary mother-goddess is itself a recurrent Near Eastern pattern; Gatumdug's value to the Atlas is as a concrete, well-attested instance of how a Sumerian city bound its political legitimacy to a maternal deity.
