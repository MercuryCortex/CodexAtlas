# Tanit

Tanit (Punic *Tnt*) is the chief goddess of Carthage and the western Phoenician (Punic) world — the supreme female deity of the Carthaginian state from at least the 5th century BCE, paired with **Baal Hammon** as her consort (often invoked before him in dedications, an unusual precedence). Her emblem, the "**sign of Tanit**" — a trapezoid surmounted by a horizontal bar and a disc, a schematic female figure — is the single most ubiquitous religious symbol of the Punic world, stamped on stelae, mosaics, and pottery across North Africa and the western Mediterranean. She is the western, Carthaginian development of the eastern Phoenician **[[astarte-canaanite]]** (Astarte), bearing the epithet *Tanit pene Baal*, "Tanit, face of Baal."

## Cult and afterlife
Tanit is the principal deity of the Carthaginian **Tophet** — the open-air precinct where urns of cremated infants and animals bear *mlk* dedications to Tanit and Baal Hammon, the material counterpart to the biblical polemic against the *mlk* offering (see [[moloch]]; Day 1989). After Rome destroyed Carthage (146 BCE), her cult did not die but was Romanized as **Dea Caelestis** / *Iuno Caelestis* ("Heavenly Juno"), the great goddess of Roman Africa whose temple at Carthage stood for centuries — Augustine still polemicizes against "Caelestis the great" (*City of God* 2.4). Her home is Punic-Phoenician; the Caelestis cult is a major (tier-2) Roman appearance-wire.
