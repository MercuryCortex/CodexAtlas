# Rongo

## Identity
Rongo (in full, *Rongo-mā-Tāne*) is the Māori atua of cultivated food — above all, the kūmara (sweet potato, *Ipomoea batatas*), which was the primary prestige cultivated crop in Māori horticulture. He is also associated with peace, fertility, and the sphere of non-violent activity (as opposed to his brother Tū, the war-god). Rongo is one of the sons of Ranginui and Papatūānuku in the great siblings narrative, and his domain is the domesticated counterpart of Haumia's wild-food realm. His Polynesian cognates — Hawaiian *Lono* and Tahitian *Ro'o* — confirm the deep antiquity and pan-Polynesian distribution of this deity-complex.

## Earliest attestation
Reconstructed to ancestral Polynesian religion; recorded in Māori tradition by Elsdon Best and other ethnographers from the 1870s onward. The kūmara was introduced to Polynesia from South America (a confirmed Polynesian-Amerindian contact event ~1200 CE), and Rongo's role as kūmara-guardian may represent a religious adaptation to the adoption of this critical crop.

## Mythological role
**Sons-of-Rangi**: Rongo is one of the brothers who debated separating Ranginui and Papatūānuku; after the separation, each domain is assigned, and Rongo receives cultivated plants and the kūmara as his charge.

**Escape into the earth**: When Tāwhirimātea (god of storms) unleashed his wrath on the brothers who had separated the sky and earth, Rongo and Haumia hid themselves in the earth — in the body of Papatūānuku. This is an etiological myth: the kūmara and fernroot are found underground because their guardian deities concealed themselves there.

**Rāhui and ritual protection**: Rongo's authority underlies the institution of *rāhui* — the ritual restriction (tapu) placed on food resources to allow them to recover. A rāhui on a fishery or food-gathering area was declared under Rongo's authority, making violation a religious transgression as well as a social one.

**Peace**: The domain of Rongo and the domain of Tū (war) are structurally opposed in Māori cosmology. Times of peace are Rongo's domain; times of war are Tū's. A "house of Rongo" (*whare Rongo*) was a peaceful space.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[lono]]** — *structural-parallel*: Hawaiian Lono governs agriculture, rain, fertility, the Makahiki festival (a period of peace and sport), and the planting season. He is the most precise functional cognate of Māori Rongo across Polynesia. Linguistic reconstruction supports a shared Proto-Polynesian deity (Best 1924; Beckwith 1940).
- **[[haumia-tiketike]]** — *paired-domain*: Rongo (cultivated food) and Haumia (wild food) together encompass plant-based sustenance. They are conceptually twinned.

## Iconography
No elaborate iconographic tradition in pre-European Māori carving is specifically attached to Rongo. He is primarily a ritual-agricultural deity whose presence is invoked through karakia (incantations) during kūmara planting and harvest.
