# Tathāgata — The Thus-Come-One

Tathāgata is the principal **honorific the Buddha uses for himself** in the Pāli and Sanskrit canon — etymologically poised between *tathā-gata* ("thus-gone," to nirvāṇa) and *tathā-āgata* ("thus-come," to teach), marking the Awakened One as having gone and come **in accord with *tathatā*, "suchness," reality-as-it-is**. In early Buddhism it designates the historical Buddha and the arhats who follow his path; in the **Mahāyāna** it expands into a metaphysical category embracing **all Buddhas** — pre-eminently the **Five Tathāgatas** (the Wisdom Buddhas, headed by **[[vairocana]]**, and including **[[amitabha]]**), each a manifestation of the awakened state across the cosmos. The related doctrine of the **tathāgatagarbha** — the "womb / embryo of the Tathāgata," the buddha-nature latent in all sentient beings — is one of the most influential developments of the term, underpinning much of East Asian and Tibetan Buddhist thought. The title is standard across Theravāda, Mahāyāna, and Vajrayāna alike.
