# Hina

The **pan-Polynesian moon-goddess** — attested across virtually every Polynesian tradition under closely-cognate names: **Hina** (Hawaiian, Tahitian), **Hine** / **Hine-nui-te-pō** (Māori), **Sina** (Samoan), **Ina** (Cook Islands), **Mahina** (Tahitian — *mahina* also means moon as common noun in modern Tahitian). The pan-Polynesian consistency of the name AND the lunar association makes Hina one of the most-clearly-reconstructable **Proto-Polynesian** religious figures in the entire Pacific.

**Multiple regional Hina-mythologies, structurally consistent:**
- **Hawaiian Hina** is the mother of [[maui-polynesian]]; she lives in the moon, where the dark patches are her tapa-beating implements (the kapa-cloth she beats in the moon is the source of the *makali'i* — the small Pleiades pattern). The Kumulipo creation chant gives Hina a primary position.
- **Māori Hine-titama** (the Dawn-Maiden) is the wife of [[tane]] the forest god; on discovering that Tane is also her father, she descends to the underworld and becomes **Hine-nui-te-pō** (the Great Lady of the Night) — the goddess of death whose attempted defeat by Maui (squeezing through her body) ends in his being crushed and the introduction of mortality to humanity.
- **Samoan Sina** is the heroine of the **Sina-and-the-Eel** narrative — the original coconut-tree origin myth (the eel-lover's severed head becomes the first coconut).

**Why Hina has been missing from comparative-religion attention until recently.** The pan-Polynesian Hina was systematically obscured by the early colonial-missionary preference for documenting **masculine** Polynesian deities (Tangaroa, Tane, Tu, Rongo). The Hina cycle and the feminine-principle structure of Polynesian theology have been recovered through ethnographic work mostly post-1900 (Beckwith, Best, Stillman). Hina represents the **largest cohesive missing-feminine** in the documented Polynesian pantheon as the vault has it.
