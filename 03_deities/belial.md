# Belial

## Identity
In the Hebrew Bible, *beliya'al* is initially a *common noun* meaning "worthlessness" or "wickedness" — "sons of Belial" (Deut 13:13, 1 Sam 2:12, etc.) means "worthless men, troublemakers." By the Second Temple period, *Belial* has been *personified* into the supreme evil-spiritual figure: the leader of the Sons of Darkness in Qumran's War Scroll (1QM), the chief adversary in the Two Spirits discourse, the head of the dominion of darkness in the present age until eschatological judgment. In the Pauline corpus, 2 Cor 6:15 has *Beliar* as the foil for Christ ("what concord has Christ with Belial?"). The figure overlaps with but is not identical to *Satan* / *the devil* / *Azazel* / *Mastema* — Second Temple Jewish demonology produces a small cast of overlapping adversarial figures with shifting names.

## Earliest attestation
- [[phase-2-011-hebrew-bible-d-source]] — Deut 13:13 *bene-beliya'al*.
- [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — the *Community Rule* Two-Spirits discourse (1QS III–IV), the *War Scroll* (1QM), and the *Damascus Document* (CD) all make Belial the supreme present-age adversary.
- [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — 2 Cor 6:15 *Beliar* as the antithesis of Christ.

## Mythological role
- Leader of the spirits of darkness in the dualist Qumran framework.
- Eschatological adversary destined for defeat at the end-time war.

## Cross-tradition identifications
The Qumran-period overlap with [[satanael]], Mastema, and the later figure of Satan is significant but not identifying; Belial is one figure in a cast.
