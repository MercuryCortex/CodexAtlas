# Vertumnus

Vertumnus (older **Vortumnus**) is the Roman god of **change and transformation** — his name linked by the Romans to *vertere*, "to turn" — and so of the **turning of the seasons** and the **ripening of garden and orchard produce**. His signature power is shape-shifting: he can assume any form, a gift central to his myth. He is most likely of **Etruscan origin** (the antiquarian tradition connects him to the Etruscan federal god **Voltumna**, and Propertius treats the famous statue in the Vicus Tuscus as an Etruscan incomer), yet his cult was wholly naturalized at Rome, with a temple on the Aventine. In Ovid's *Metamorphoses* he woos the orchard-goddess **[[pomona|Pomona]]**, approaching her in many disguises — an old woman among them — before revealing his true youthful self and winning her. Vertumnus embodies the Roman sense of the year as constant fruitful turning, the seasons ripening one into the next.
