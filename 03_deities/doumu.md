# Doumu

Doumu (*Dǒumǔ* 斗母/斗姆, "Mother of the Dipper") is a major Daoist goddess — the divine **mother of the Big Dipper** and of the **Nine Emperor Stars** (the seven visible Dipper stars plus two hidden ones), and so a great power over **fate, longevity, and the cosmic registers of life and death** governed by the northern asterism. Honoured as a Queen of Heaven, she presides over the stellar bureaucracy to which Daoists petitioned for the lengthening of life. Her most striking feature is her **iconography**: she is depicted with **multiple arms and heads**, holding the sun, the moon, and ritual implements, seated upon a **lotus throne** — a form taken **directly from the Buddhist goddess [[marici|Mārīcī]]** (Chinese Molizhitian). In the religious ferment of Tang–Song China, as Daoism and Buddhism borrowed freely from one another, the Daoist Dipper-Mother absorbed Mārīcī's esoteric image almost wholesale, so that the two goddesses became iconographically near-identical while retaining distinct cosmological roles. Doumu is a vivid case-study in Chinese inter-religious borrowing — a Buddhist deity's body housing a Daoist stellar power.
