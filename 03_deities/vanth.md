# Vanth

## Identity
Vanth is the **Etruscan winged death-demon** — a female spirit who attends the moment of death, witnesses the deeds of the living, and **guides the soul** to the underworld. Unlike the menacing [[charun-etruscan]], Vanth is not punitive but inevitable: she is the presence of death itself, neither cruel nor kind.

## Earliest attestation
Pervasive in Etruscan funerary art of the 4th–2nd centuries BCE — tomb paintings (the François Tomb, Tomba dell'Orco), sarcophagi, and urns — where she appears great-winged, often with a torch to light the dark road, and sometimes a key or scroll.

## Mythological role — the guide and witness of the dead
Vanth stands at the threshold of death: she is shown at scenes of dying and slaughter (even at the death of Greek heroes adapted into Etruscan art), bearing a **torch** to lead the soul, snakes wound about her arms, her wings spread. She frequently accompanies **Charun**, the two together escorting the dead — Vanth the guide, Charun the grim ferryman/hammer-bearer. She embodies the Etruscans' intense concern with the journey after death.

## Cross-tradition
Vanth is the Etruscan member of the **psychopomp** — the divine guide of souls — alongside Hermes Psychopompos, Anubis, the Valkyries, Yama, and Xolotl.

## Iconography
A winged young woman with a lit torch, key, or scroll; snakes on her arms; often paired with Charun at the door of the tomb.
