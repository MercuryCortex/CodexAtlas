# Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli

Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli (Nahuatl *Tlāhuizcalpantēcuhtli*, "Lord of the House of Dawn") is the Aztec god of **Venus as the morning star** — and the **Venus-manifestation of [[quetzalcoatl]]**. In the Toltec-derived myth (the *Anales de Cuauhtitlan*), after the culture-hero Quetzalcoatl immolates himself on a pyre at the eastern shore of Tlapallan, his heart ascends and **becomes the Morning Star**, and he reappears as Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli. The Mesoamericans watched Venus with extraordinary care — the *Codex Dresden* and *Codex Borgia* contain precise Venus-tables — because its **heliacal rising** after invisibility was thought **dangerous**: the first rays of the dawn-star were imagined as **spears or darts** that the god hurls down to strike different classes of victims on different days of the Venus cycle (Aveni). He pairs with **[[xolotl]]**, the evening-star aspect, the two halves of Venus that lead and trail the sun. He is a prime example of the Mesoamerican fusion of precise naked-eye astronomy with myth and sacrifice.
