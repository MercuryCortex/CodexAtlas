# Vajradhara

## Identity
Vajradhara — "the Vajra-Holder" / "the Diamond-Holder" — is the primordial Buddha of the Sarma (New Translation) schools of Tibetan Buddhism: Sakya, Kagyu, and Gelug. He is the *dharmakāya* (ultimate truth-body of Buddhahood) personified, the source from which all Tantric Vajrayāna teachings emerge, and the apex of the master-disciple transmission lineages (*paramparā*) on which Vajrayāna depends. Iconographically: deep blue color (the color of the ultimate sky-space / dharmadhātu), seated in vajra-asana, holding vajra and bell crossed at the heart; often in non-dual union (*yab-yum*) with his consort Prajñāpāramitā. The doctrinal point: Vajradhara is not *another* Buddha alongside Śākyamuni; he is the Buddha-as-eternal-truth, the *teaching-mind* of all Buddhas, with Śākyamuni as a temporal *nirmāṇakāya* (transformation body) manifestation.

## Earliest attestation
Emergence in later Indian Tantric Buddhism (~700–1100); systematic treatment in the Hevajra Tantra (~9th c.) and the Cakrasaṃvara cycle; Tibetan reception via Marpa Lotsawa (~1050) and the subsequent Sarma transmissions.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[samantabhadra-buddha]]** — same role in Nyingma; the Sarma vs Nyingma difference is sectarian-iconographic.
- **[[vajrasattva]]** — closely related Vajra-family Buddha; sometimes conflated.
- **Ādibuddha** (general term — no node yet; concept-level) — Vajradhara is *the* Ādibuddha (primordial Buddha) in Sarma traditions.
