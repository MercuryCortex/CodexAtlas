# Ananke

## Identity

Ananke (Greek **Anankē**, "Necessity," "Compulsion") is the Greek personification of inevitability — the binding, inescapable order of the cosmos, the force to which even the gods must yield. She is a primordial principle rather than a goddess of cult, appearing chiefly in cosmological and philosophical contexts: the abstract power of "what must be."

## Mythological role

Ananke is most prominent in two settings. In **Orphic cosmogony**, she is paired with **Chronos** (Time) at the very beginning of things: the two are imagined as a serpent (or serpent-pair) coiled around the primordial **cosmic egg**, their encircling embrace constituting the first order from which the firstborn god [[phanes-protogonos]] is hatched. In **Plato's** *Republic* (the Myth of Er), Necessity sits at the center of the cosmos holding the great **spindle** on which the heavens turn, and the three [[the-moirai]] (Fates) — here her daughters — sit around her singing of past, present, and future as they turn the whorls. Ananke thus stands behind Fate itself: the Moirai apportion destiny, but they do so under Necessity.

## Cross-tradition significance

Ananke is the Greek philosophical-religious face of **cosmic inevitability** — the principle that the order of things is binding and cannot be evaded. As the power behind the [[the-moirai]] and as Chronos's partner in the Orphic ordering of the cosmos, she belongs to the wider ancient concern with fate, destiny, and the decreeing of what must be — a concern paralleled by the Norse Norns, the Mesopotamian decreeing of destinies by the Anunnaki, and the "Tablet of Destinies" that confers cosmic authority. She is a key node for the cross-tradition theme of **fate and necessity**.
