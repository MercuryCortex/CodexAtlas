# Geb

## Identity
Geb is the Egyptian god of the earth, member of the Heliopolitan Ennead, son of Shu (Air) and Tefnut (Moisture), brother and consort of Nut (Sky), and father of the second generation — Osiris, Isis, Set, Nephthys. He is one of the unusual ancient near-eastern earth deities who is masculine (contrast Greek Gaia, Sumerian Ki — both feminine).

## Earliest attestation
- [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] (Old Kingdom, 5th–6th dynasties, ~-2350 onward) — Geb is regularly named in the Heliopolitan cosmogony.
- [[phase-1-009-coffin-texts]] (Middle Kingdom) and [[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]] (New Kingdom onward) continue this.

## Mythological role
- **Earthly half of the Sky/Earth division.** In Egyptian cosmogony Geb is forcibly separated from Nut (his sister-consort) by their father Shu, who steps between them and lifts Nut into her arched-sky position. Standard iconography: Geb supine, Nut arched above, Shu standing between them, sometimes assisted by other gods raising Nut at her hands and feet.
- **King of the earth before Osiris.** In the divine succession Geb is the earth-king before passing rule to Osiris. The pharaoh sat on "the throne of Geb."
- **Father of Osiris.** This makes Geb the grandfather (via Osiris) of Horus, i.e., grandfather of all reigning pharaohs in their Horus-identification.

## Iconography
- Often reclining on his back, in green or with vegetation
- Goose hieroglyph (his name *gb* is written with the white-fronted goose)
- Sometimes phallic (the Geb-Nut separation)
- Occasionally wearing the Lower Egyptian red crown
