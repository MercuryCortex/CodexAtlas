# Nur Muhammadi (The Muhammadan Light)

## Identity
*Nūr Muḥammadī* — the Muhammadan Light — is the Sufi metaphysical principle of the primordial light through which God created the cosmos, identified with the inner / archetypal reality of the Prophet Muhammad. The doctrine builds on hadith reports (some authentic, some weak by Sunni canonical standards) in which God says "I created Muhammad's light before all things" or "I was a prophet while Adam was still between water and clay." Early Sufi exegetes (Tustarī ~860, then more systematically in Hallajian and post-Hallajian Sufism) developed this into a Logos-doctrine: Muhammad's *Light* is the first creation, the Pen with which God writes the Tablet, the medium through which all subsequent creation comes into being. In Ibn ʿArabī's *Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam*, this becomes the doctrine of the *Ḥaqīqa Muḥammadiyya* (Muhammadan Reality) — Muhammad as the perfect human (*al-insān al-kāmil*), the cosmic logos through which divine self-disclosure is structured. Every prophet is a partial manifestation; Muhammad is the complete manifestation, prior in metaphysical rank to his historical existence. The doctrine is the structural Sufi parallel to Johannine *Logos* Christology — and is sharply controversial within Sunni Islam, opposed especially by Wahhabi and Salafi traditions as innovation (*bidʿa*).

## Earliest attestation
Sahl al-Tustarī (~860, *Tafsīr*) — first explicit treatment. Hallajian circle (~900–922). Systematic in al-Ghazālī (~1100), Ibn ʿArabī (~1230), and the entire post-Akbarī Sufi tradition.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[logos-johannine]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: structurally parallel — primordial creative principle, identified with the divine messenger figure, "through whom all things were made." Schimmel 1975, 1985 develops the comparison.
- **[[adam-kadmon]]** in Kabbalah (primordial cosmic Man) — *scholarly-parallel*: parallel anthropomorphized cosmic-archetype theology.
- Christ-Logos in Christian theology — *scholarly-parallel*: same.

## Disputes
- **Orthodoxy.** Wahhabi / Salafi tradition rejects *Nūr Muḥammadī* as heretical innovation; mainstream Sunni (Ottoman, Maghrebi, South Asian) and Shi'a traditions accept it.
- **Sources.** The hadith reports underlying the doctrine are graded variously (some authentic, some weak, some fabricated by Sunni hadith-scholars' standards) — the doctrinal load borne by weak hadith is part of the controversy.
