# Iao

## Identity

Iao is the Greek transliteration of the Hebrew divine name YHWH — the most consequential act of religious translation in the ancient Mediterranean world. By rendering the unpronounceable four-consonant name (the Tetragrammaton: יהוה) as a pronounceable three-vowel Greek word (ΙΑΩ), the Jewish-Greek tradition created a divine name that could be *spoken*, *sung*, and *invoked* — and that immediately crossed into Gnostic, Hermetic, and theurgic contexts.

Iao does not belong to a single tradition. It is, uniquely, a *cross-tradition interface* — the one divine name that appears in:
- Jewish-Greek prayer and liturgy (LXX tradition)
- Greek Magical Papyri (PGM) as the supreme invocation
- Gnostic texts (Nag Hammadi) as both an archon-name and a supreme divine designator
- Origen's report of Ophite Gnostic hierarchies (*Contra Celsum* VI.31–32)
- Gem amulets alongside [[abrasax]]
- Christian magical practice

The name is a *phoneme* with cosmic power — and the tradition of treating it as such is itself one of the most revealing cross-tradition data points in the history of religion.

## Iao in the Greek Magical Papyri

The Greek Magical Papyri (PGM; papyri from Egypt, 2nd century BCE – 5th century CE; compiled from Greek, Demotic, and Coptic sources) are the single most important archive of late-antique religious practice. IAO appears in them with extraordinary frequency — as the single most powerful divine invocation available to the practitioner.

The supreme triple formula: **"IAO SABAOTH ADONAI"**

This three-name invocation combines:
- **IAO**: YHWH — the supreme divine name
- **SABAOTH**: "Lord of Hosts" (Hebrew *tseva'ot*) — the God of the celestial armies
- **ADONAI**: "My Lord" — the substitute name for YHWH in Jewish reading practice

In the PGM, these three Hebrew divine names are invoked together as the maximum divine address — the complete name of the supreme power. A practitioner invoking "IAO SABAOTH ADONAI" is addressing the God of Israel by his three most powerful names simultaneously.

**The cultural implication:** By the 2nd century BCE, the names of the God of Israel had become the most powerful divine invocations in the Mediterranean magical world — used by practitioners who were not necessarily Jewish, in contexts that mixed Egyptian, Greek, and Jewish sacred-name traditions. The God of the Hebrew Bible had, through his name alone, become the supreme divine power in Greco-Egyptian magical practice.

## Iao in the Gnostic Hierarchy (Origen, Contra Celsum)

Origen of Alexandria (*Contra Celsum* VI.31–32, c. 248 CE) reports the seven-archon hierarchy of the Ophite Gnostic school. The seven archons rule the seven planetary spheres, and each has a name. IAO is placed as one of these archons — not the supreme God but one of the intermediate planetary rulers.

This creates a remarkable theological ambiguity: the same name ("IAO") functions simultaneously as:
1. The supreme divine name in PGM magical practice
2. An archon-name in Ophite Gnostic cosmology

The Gnostic move is deliberate: by demoting YHWH/IAO to archon status, the Ophites perform the same inversion as the Sethians do with [[yaldabaoth]] — the god of the Hebrew Bible is not the supreme God but a planetary archon, powerful but cosmologically subordinate. The use of the actual sacred name (IAO) rather than a substitute (Yaldabaoth, Saklas) makes the Ophite version even more direct in its anti-mainstream theology.

## MASSIVE WIN: IAO = YHWH — The Transliteration That Crossed a World

The translation of YHWH → IAO is not a minor philological detail but the most consequential act of divine-name translation in the ancient world:

**The mechanism:** Hebrew YHWH has four consonants (Y-H-W-H) but is traditionally vocalized as its substitute "Adonai" (Lord) in Jewish reading. The Greek LXX (Septuagint) sometimes writes IAO as the transliteration of the divine name — using the three vowels that can be approximated from the Hebrew consonants. IAO is thus both transliteration and interpretation: a vowel-sequence that can be *pronounced*.

**What the translation enables:**
- A name that was theologically *unpronounceable* (the Tetragrammaton) becomes a phoneme that can be *sung*, *chanted*, and *invoked*
- The name crosses from Jewish liturgy (where it is scrupulously guarded) into Greek magical practice (where it becomes the supreme invocation)
- The name enters Gnostic texts as both a divine name and a figure in the cosmic hierarchy

This parallels other acts of sacred-name translation across traditions:

| Tradition | The sacred name | The constraint | The translation | The effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jewish / Gnostic | YHWH → IAO | Unpronounceable consonants | Greek vowel-transliteration | Becomes a magical invocation available to all |
| Kabbalistic | Tetragrammaton → Adonai → Ein Sof | The name cannot be said; even "Adonai" is only a substitute | The infinite name (*Ein Sof*) replaces the four-letter name | The divine is renamed as the infinite itself |
| Sufi Islamic | *Allah* → *Al-Haqq* → *Hu* | The full name is too vast; the divine breath is the essence | *Hu* (the divine exhalation) is the ultimate sacred sound | The divine is distilled to pure breath |
| Hindu | Brahman → AUM (Om) | Brahman cannot be named | AUM encodes the three states of consciousness and silence | The divine is encoded as the primordial sound-structure |

## MASSIVE WIN: "Iao Sabaoth Adonai" — The Triple Divine Name

The triple invocation "IAO SABAOTH ADONAI" belongs to a cross-tradition pattern: the triple divine name as complete cosmic address:

| Formula | Tradition | The three names | What the triad encodes |
|---|---|---|---|
| IAO — SABAOTH — ADONAI | Gnostic-Hermetic-Jewish (PGM) | Three Hebrew divine names | The complete address of the supreme God |
| Om — Tat — Sat | Hindu Vedantic | Brahman (sound) — That (the absolute) — Being | The triple affirmation of divine reality |
| Father — Son — Holy Spirit | Christian Trinity | Three persons of one divine nature | The complete divine economy of creation, redemption, sanctification |
| Ahura Mazda — Spenta Mainyu — Asha | Zoroastrian | The supreme God — the Holy Spirit — Truth | The triad of divine-cosmic-ethical principles |
| Hu — Haqq — Hayy | Sufi Islamic | Divine breath — the True — the Living | Three divine names encoding the essence of divine reality |

**The cross-tradition pattern:** Three divine names constitute a *complete* divine address — one name alone is insufficient, two is incomplete, three achieves a sense of totality. The theological logic (which may be connected to the power of three in human cognition, or to the structure of beginning-middle-end) appears to be transcultural. The PGM triple IAO-SABAOTH-ADONAI is the Jewish-Gnostic-Hermetic form of this universal triple-name theology.

## MASSIVE WIN: The Supreme Name as Sacred Phoneme

IAO as pure pronounceable sound participates in a cross-tradition theology of the sacred phoneme — the idea that a specific sound-sequence carries cosmic power:

| Sacred phoneme | Tradition | What the sound encodes | Cosmic claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| IAO | Gnostic-Hermetic (PGM) | YHWH in Greek vocalization | The supreme divine power concentrated in three syllables |
| AUM (Om) | Hindu / Buddhist | The three states (waking-dreaming-deep sleep) + the silence | The primordial sound of Brahman / the sound of creation |
| Hu | Sufi Islamic | The divine exhalation; the word "He" (هو) = divine pronoun | The breath of God; the sound from which all creation emerges |
| AMEN / AMEN | Jewish / Christian / Islamic | Affirmation; derived from Hebrew *emuna* (faithfulness) | The sound that seals divine speech; the phoneme of divine assent |
| *Namu Amida Butsu* | Pure Land Buddhist | The six-character name of Amitabha Buddha | The name-as-sound that transmits Buddha-nature to the chanter |

**The convergence:** The belief that a specific sound or name carries irreducible cosmic power — that the *phoneme itself* is the divine — appears in every major tradition examined in the Atlas. IAO is the most explicitly cross-traditional of these: it begins as Hebrew theology, enters Greek transliteration, becomes a Greek magical invocation, appears in Gnostic texts, is inscribed on gem amulets, and crosses into Coptic, Demotic, and later Arabic magical practice. No other divine name crosses as many tradition-boundaries while retaining recognizable identity.
