# Verethragna

## Identity
Verethragna (Avestan *Vərəθraγna*, "slayer of *vərəθra* / smasher of obstacles"; Pahlavi-Persian *Bahram*) is the **Zoroastrian yazata of victory** — the personification of victorious warrior-power, the destroyer of cosmic-and-mundane obstacles, the bestower of strength in battle. The principal Avestan source (**Yasht 14 / Bahram Yasht**) describes Verethragna's **ten *avatar-incarnations*** — the wind, the bull-with-golden-horns, the white horse, the burden-camel, the boar, the golden-youth-of-fifteen, the raven, the wild ram, the fighting he-goat, and the armed warrior. The **boar-form** is the most iconic. He is **directly cognate with Vedic [[indra]]** (*Vṛtrahán*, "slayer of Vṛtra") — making Verethragna-Indra **one of the clearest Indo-Iranian shared-religious-theonym cases** in comparative religion. He is identified with **Ares** in Greek-Hellenistic *interpretatio* and (more theologically) with **Mars** in Roman religion via the Parthian-Roman religious contact zone.

## Earliest attestation
- **Avestan**: Yasht 14 (Bahram Yasht) — the major liturgical hymn. Less prominent in the Gathas; Verethragna is younger-Avestan-period elaborated.
- **Vedic cognate Vṛtrahán**: Rigveda extensively names Indra as *Vṛtrahán* — the Vedic figure preserves more dragon-slayer narrative content; the Iranian figure shifts toward the yazata-warrior register.
- **Sasanian period (~+224 onward)**: Bahram is a major Sasanian royal-religious cult-figure. Multiple Sasanian kings are named **Bahram** (Bahram I, II, III, IV, V, VI, VII); the **Bahram fires** (the highest-grade Zoroastrian temple-fires) are dedicated to him.

## Mythological role

### The ten incarnations (Yasht 14)
Verethragna manifests to Zarathustra in ten successive forms, each conveying a different aspect of his cosmic-victorious power:
1. **Wind** — irresistible swift force.
2. **Bull with golden horns** — power-and-fertility.
3. **White horse with golden ears** — speed-and-nobility.
4. **Burden-camel** — endurance-and-strength.
5. **Boar** — the most iconic; sharp-tusked relentless killer-of-enemies. Sasanian art frequently depicts Verethragna as boar or boar-rider.
6. **Golden-youth-of-fifteen** — youthful prime.
7. **Raven** — swift messenger.
8. **Wild ram** — virile force.
9. **Fighting he-goat** — aggressive battle.
10. **Armed warrior** — the human-warrior form.

### Bahram fires
The highest grade of Zoroastrian temple-fires are **Ataš Bahrāms** ("Fires of Victory") — consecrated through extraordinary purification rituals combining 16 different fires from 16 different sources, ritually purified over a year. Only 9 currently operative Bahram fires exist worldwide (8 in India, 1 in Iran). They are the most sacred Zoroastrian institutions and named for Verethragna.

## Cross-tradition identifications

### Indo-Iranian shared theonym: Verethragna ↔ Indra-Vṛtrahán
**The cleanest single Indo-Iranian shared-religious-vocabulary case.** Both names derive from Proto-Indo-Iranian \*vr̥tra-ǵhan- "slayer of the obstacle/resistance":
- **Vedic Indra**: develops into a high-god, dragon-slayer (Vṛtra is a literal cosmic dragon blocking the waters), king of devas. The narrative content is extensively preserved.
- **Avestan Verethragna**: develops as a yazata (subordinate to Ahura Mazda), the narrative content is preserved less narratively and more as the ten-incarnation enumeration.

**Note on the daeva-deva inversion.** In one of the most-famous structural inversions in the history of religion, **Iranian** religion treats *daevas* (cognate with Vedic *devas*) as DEMONS while preserving *yazatas* and *ahuras* (cognate with Vedic *asuras*) as DIVINE; **Vedic** religion treats *devas* as GODS and *asuras* as DEMONS. The inversion suggests a major religious-reformist break — Zarathustra's reform deliberately demoting the warrior-pantheon of pre-Zoroastrian Iranian religion (which preserved the older Indo-European warrior-god structure). Verethragna survives the Zoroastrian reform as one of the few originally-warrior-cult figures retained in yazata status. (Boyce 1975 vol. 1 ch. 6; West 2007.)

### Other cross-tradition cognates
- **Greek Ares** — *interpretatio*: Hellenistic identification.
- **Roman Mars** — *interpretatio*: in the Parthian-Roman religious contact zone, Verethragna is identified with Mars. Mithras-Mars-Bahram identifications are visible in some 2nd-3rd c. CE syncretic religious-Roman material.
- **Armenian Vahagn** — *direct descent*: Armenian polytheism (pre-Christian) retained Verethragna as Vahagn, with extensive Iranian inheritance.

## Iconography
- **Sasanian period**: Verethragna as boar (or boar-rider) is the most iconic. Bahram-themed seal-impressions, royal-iconography, and rock-reliefs depict the boar-form.
- **Royal-religious iconography**: Sasanian kings adopt Verethragna's boar-emblem on royal symbolism.

## Disputes
- **Etymology of *vərəθra*.** "Obstacle / resistance" is the standard reading; some scholars argue for narrower "defensive barrier" or broader "anything-that-blocks-victory."
- **The ten-incarnation enumeration.** Whether the ten incarnations are originally-coherent or are a later-Avestan-period compilation of locally-associated theriomorphic figures is debated. The **avatar-doctrine parallel with Vishnu's dasavatara** (ten incarnations) is structurally striking but probably accidental convergence rather than transmission (Boyce 1975 is cautious).
- **Verethragna-Mithra-Mars Roman-Iranian syncretism scope.** The 2nd-3rd c. CE Roman-Iranian religious-syncretic identifications are real but their scope is debated.
