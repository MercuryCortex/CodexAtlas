# Hubal

## Identity
Hubal (Arabic *Hubal*, هبل) is the **principal cult-image inside the Kaaba at Mecca in the pre-Islamic period**, smashed by Muhammad at the conquest of Mecca in 630 CE (8 AH). He is the most prominent of the multiple idols housed in the Kaaba in the immediately pre-Islamic Hejaz, and is the deity invoked by the Quraysh in their public divinatory consultations: a set of **seven divinatory arrows (*qidāḥ*)** was kept before his cult-image for oracular consultation in matters of importance — paternity disputes, oaths, military expeditions. The canonical description (Islamic-tradition sources) makes him a humanoid cult-image of **red carnelian** with a gold-replacement right hand (the original right hand having been broken off at some earlier point). His scholarly origin is debated: most likely a **Nabataean / Levantine import** brought to Mecca via the caravan-trade routes, possibly by ʿAmr ibn Luḥayy (the legendary introducer of idolatry to Mecca in Islamic-tradition memory), with cultic substrate from the broader Northwest-Semitic / Aramaean world. (Hawting 1999; Hoyland 2001.)

**Source-critical note.** Almost everything specific we know about Hubal comes from the Islamic-tradition reconstructive sources (Ibn Ishaq, Ibn al-Kalbi's *Kitāb al-Aṣnām*, al-Tabari, al-Azraqi's *Akhbār Makka*), which are 8th–10th c. CE polemical-reconstructive texts written from within the established Islamic-monotheist framework. Hawting 1999 makes this source-critical caution explicit: the Islamic-period sources have strong polemical interests in constructing pre-Islamic Arabian idolatry as the foil for Islamic monotheism, and contemporary pre-Islamic Arabian epigraphic attestation of Hubal specifically is **thin** (a few Nabataean / Safaitic inscriptions, much disputed in reading). The detailed Kaaba-Hubal narrative is therefore best read as Islamic-tradition reception-history with a real but partially-recoverable pre-Islamic cultic substrate.

## Earliest attestation
- **Nabataean inscriptions** (~-100 onward, possibly earlier) — a few Nabataean inscriptions name *Hubal* or near-equivalent; the Nabataean attestation is the strongest non-Islamic-source attestation. Hawting 1999 and Hoyland 2001 discuss; the readings are partly contested.
- **Pre-Islamic Hejazi cultic substrate (immediately pre-Islamic)** — Hubal inside the Kaaba per the Islamic-tradition memory; the Kaaba's Hubal cult-image is the principal pre-Islamic Meccan religious focus, smashed in 630 CE ([[event-conquest-of-mecca-630]]).
- **Ibn Ishaq *Sirat Rasul Allah* (~750 CE, redacted by Ibn Hisham c. 833) — [[phase-5-043-ibn-ishaq-sirat-rasul-allah]]** — the earliest extended Islamic-tradition narrative of Muhammad's life including the Kaaba-idol-smashing.
- **Ibn al-Kalbi *Kitāb al-Aṣnām* (Book of Idols, c. 9th c. CE)** — the principal Islamic-tradition reconstructive catalog of pre-Islamic Arabian idols including Hubal. Use source-critically.
- **Quranic textual attestation** — Hubal is NOT named directly in the Quran (unlike al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, Manāt at Sura 53:19-22); the Quranic critique of Meccan idolatry is general rather than naming Hubal specifically. This is a notable absence: the principal Kaaba-idol per the Islamic-tradition memory is not Quranically named.

## Mythological role
No surviving mythological narrative for Hubal — neither pre-Islamic Arabian myths nor extended Islamic-tradition mythography of his prior history. His role in the pre-Islamic Meccan religious system was **cultic-divinatory** rather than mythological:

1. **Principal Kaaba-idol.** Of the multiple idols housed in the pre-Islamic Kaaba (Islamic-tradition memory counts 360, though this is likely a stylized number), Hubal was the central / largest cult-image.
2. **Divination via seven arrows (*qidāḥ*).** Important Quraysh decisions — paternity claims, oaths, expeditions — were resolved by arrow-casting before Hubal's image. The arrows were marked (e.g., *na'am* "yes" / *lā* "no"; or with specific outcome labels) and drawn by a keeper of the cult.
3. **Smashed at the conquest of Mecca, 630 CE.** Muhammad's conquest of Mecca in 8 AH includes the destruction of the Kaaba's idols including Hubal; subsequent Islamic tradition treats this as the foundational act of monotheistic cleansing of the Kaaba.

## Cross-tradition identifications
**Probably Nabataean / Levantine import.** Hawting 1999 discusses the Nabataean / Levantine cultic origin hypothesis: Hubal's iconographic and onomastic profile fits the broader Northwest-Semitic / Aramaean cultic world better than it fits an indigenous Hejazi tradition, suggesting import via the caravan trade. Specific cross-tradition identifications (with which Northwest-Semitic / Nabataean deity?) are not securely attested; some scholars propose Ba'al-Shamin, others a Nabataean astral deity, but no identification is mainstream-consensus.

No ancient identifications with Greek / Roman / Iranian deities are securely attested.

## Iconography
Per the Islamic-tradition sources (Ibn al-Kalbi, al-Azraqi):
- **Humanoid cult-image** — anthropomorphic, distinguishing Hubal from the more typical aniconic *baetyl* pre-Islamic Arabian cult-images (such as the Taif Allāt-stone).
- **Red carnelian** — the canonical material per the Islamic-tradition description.
- **Gold-replacement right hand** — the original right hand was broken off at some earlier point; replaced with gold.
- **Standing or seated inside the Kaaba**, with the seven divinatory arrows before him.

The anthropomorphic / carnelian description sits uneasily with the broader pre-Islamic Arabian preference for aniconic *baetyls* (sacred stones); this is one source-critical reason to read the detailed Islamic-tradition description with caution. The anthropomorphic Hubal may reflect a genuinely imported Nabataean / Levantine humanoid cult-image, or it may be later Islamic-tradition embellishment.

## Disputes
- **Origin: indigenous vs. import.** Mainstream scholarly view (Hawting 1999, Hoyland 2001): Hubal is probably a Nabataean / Levantine import to Mecca, possibly via the caravan-trade routes. This is consistent with Islamic-tradition memory (ʿAmr ibn Luḥayy as introducer-of-idolatry), but the specifics of the import-route are partly speculative.
- **Source-critical reliability of the Kaaba-Hubal narrative.** Hawting 1999 emphasizes: the detailed Kaaba-Hubal description and the smashing-by-Muhammad narrative come from later Islamic-tradition sources with strong polemical interests. The historical core (a pre-Islamic Meccan cult-image, destroyed at the conquest of Mecca) is secure; the iconographic and ritual details are reconstructive.
- **Why Hubal is not Quranically named.** Notable absence; the Quran names al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, Manāt but not Hubal. Various explanations: (a) Hubal's worship was already less central than the three-goddesses cluster by Muhammad's lifetime; (b) the Quran's idolatry-polemic is general rather than naming-specific; (c) Hubal as an imported / foreign cult image was less theologically central than the "daughters of Allāh." No consensus.
