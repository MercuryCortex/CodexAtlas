# Zagreus

The primordial Dionysus of Orphic theogony — son of Zeus and Persephone, dismembered by the Titans in bull-form, heart preserved by Athena, and reborn as the "second" Dionysus through Semele.

## The Orphic myth

In the Orphic theogony preserved through Olympiodorus's commentary on Plato (with substantial classical attestation across Nonnus, Diodorus Siculus, and the Orphic Hymns), Zeus and Persephone produce a child, Zagreus, intended as the next ruler of the cosmos. Hera, jealous, sends the Titans to kill him. The Titans lure the child with toys (including a mirror) and dismember him while he is in bull-form, eating his flesh. Zeus reduces the Titans to ash with a thunderbolt; from this ash humanity is created — inheriting both a Titanic (material, criminal) nature and a Dionysian (divine) spark from the consumed flesh of Zagreus. The heart of Zagreus is preserved by Athena and re-implanted into Semele, producing the "second" Dionysus.

## Theological role

The Zagreus dismemberment is the central Orphic mystery — the foundational myth from which the Orphic-Pythagorean doctrines of soul transmigration, ritual purification, and ultimate ascent through reincarnation derive. The duality humans carry (Titanic body + Dionysian soul) is the ontological basis for the entire Orphic ethical and ritual system.
