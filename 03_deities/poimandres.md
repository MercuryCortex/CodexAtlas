# Poimandres

The revealer-figure in Corpus Hermeticum I, who appears to the narrator and unfolds the cosmogony and anthropology of Hermetic revelation. The name has two competing etymologies: Greek *poimēn-andrōn* ("shepherd of men") or Egyptian *p-eime n-rē* ("knowledge of Re") — Fowden 1986 favors the Egyptian. Poimandres identifies himself as the Nous of the Sovereignty; in the text he reveals himself, then explains, then commissions the seer to preach. The figure is one of the clearest cases of late-antique revelation-literature genre conventions: a divine intelligence visits a human visionary and discloses cosmic truth.
