# Mictecacihuatl

Mictecacihuatl (Nahuatl *Mictēcacihuātl*, "Lady of the Dead") is the **queen of Mictlan**, the deepest of the nine layers of the Aztec underworld, and the consort of its lord **[[mictlantecuhtli]]**. The two are imaged as a skeletal or flayed-faced pair, jaws agape — hers said to swallow the stars during the day. Her chief office is to **guard the bones of the dead** of past creations: it is from Mictlan, past Mictecacihuatl and her husband, that Quetzalcoatl must steal the ancestral bones to remake humanity in the present age. She presided over Aztec **festivals of the dead** (notably during the month Tititl and a month-long observance that scholars connect to the modern **Día de los Muertos**, in which her imagery survives transformed into "La Catrina"). As the divine keeper of the dead and of the remains from which life is renewed, she embodies the Aztec conviction that death is the necessary reservoir of life.
