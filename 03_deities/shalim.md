# Shalim

## Identity

Shalim (Ugaritic **Šalim**, "Dusk" or "Completion") is the Canaanite god of dusk and the evening — the twin of [[shahar]] (Dawn) and, like him, a son of the high god [[el-canaanite]]. Where Shahar is the breaking of morning light and the morning star, Shalim is the falling of evening and the evening star (both being aspects of the planet Venus). His name shares the Semitic root **š-l-m**, "to be whole, complete, at peace" — the same root as Hebrew *šālôm*.

## Mythological role

Shalim appears alongside Shahar in *The Birth of the Gracious Gods* (KTU 1.23), the Ugaritic poem in which El fathers the twin astral deities who frame the day at its two thresholds. As a pair, Shahar and Shalim express the Canaanite habit of personifying the great regular motions of the sky — dawn and dusk, the morning and evening appearances of Venus — as divine children.

## Cross-tradition significance

Shalim's most enduring trace is toponymic and onomastic. The city of **Jerusalem** preserves his name: the form *Uru-Šalim* ("foundation/city of Shalim") attests to the dusk-god as the tutelary deity of the pre-Israelite settlement, and the element *šalim/šalem* survives in "(Jeru)salem" and in personal names (e.g. Absalom, *ʾAbšalôm*). The shared root with *šālôm* ("peace, completeness") links the dusk-god to the powerful biblical vocabulary of peace. Together with his twin [[shahar]] — the dawn-god behind the Isaiah-14 "Lucifer" — Shalim shows how deeply the Canaanite astral gods are woven into the names and language the biblical tradition inherited.
