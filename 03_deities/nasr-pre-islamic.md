# Nasr

## Identity
Nasr (Arabic *Nasr*, نسر — "vulture, eagle, raptor"; cognate with Northwest-Semitic *nešer* "eagle / vulture") is a **South Arabian (Himyaritic) theriomorphic deity** in vulture / eagle form, one of the five gods preserved in the Quranic antediluvian-pantheon list at **Quran 71:23 (Sūrat Nūḥ)**: *Wadd, Suwāʿ, Yaghūth, Yaʿūq, Nasr*. Per Ibn al-Kalbi's Islamic-tradition reconstruction, Nasr was worshipped at the **mountain shrine of Balkhaʿ in Himyar** by the **Dhū l-Kalāʿ** tribal cluster — a regional South Arabian cult that the Islamic-tradition narrative integrates into the broader Quranic Noah-typological frame. He is part of the broader South Arabian theriomorphic / astral pantheon and represents the **raptor-god** sub-type within the Semitic religious vocabulary. (Hoyland 2001; Hawting 1999; Reynolds 2018.)

## Earliest attestation
- **Himyaritic / South Arabian inscriptions** (c. -500 onward) — *Nsr* attested as theophoric element in personal names and in regional cultic contexts. The textual record is thinner than for Wadd or Almaqah; Nasr is a regional / tribal deity rather than a state-level high god.
- **Quran 71:23 (Sūrat Nūḥ)** (~610-632 CE) — the principal Islamic-period textual attestation, naming Nasr in the antediluvian pantheon. ([[phase-4-034-quran]])
- **Ibn al-Kalbi *Kitāb al-Aṣnām* (~9th c. CE)** — Islamic-tradition reconstructive description: Nasr at the Balkhaʿ mountain in Himyar under the Dhū l-Kalāʿ. Use source-critically per Hawting 1999.

## Mythological role
1. **South Arabian (Himyaritic) raptor-god.** Tribal / regional cult rather than state-level; the theriomorphic vulture / eagle form preserves a Semitic raptor-deity type.
2. **Antediluvian-pantheon-name in the Quran.** Quran 71:23 places Nasr alongside Wadd / Suwāʿ / Yaghūth / Yaʿūq in the mouths of Noah's polytheist contemporaries — a theological-typological move connecting pre-Islamic Arabian polytheism to the broader Quranic Noah-cycle (Sūrat Nūḥ, Sūrat al-Aʿrāf 7:59-64, Sūrat Hūd 11:25-49) of prophetic warning and unrighteous-generation destruction.
3. **The Dhū l-Kalāʿ cultic association.** Per Islamic-tradition memory, the immediate-pre-Islamic Nasr-cult was at Balkhaʿ under the Dhū l-Kalāʿ tribal cluster of Himyar.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[wadd|Wadd]] / Suwāʿ / Yaghūth / Yaʿūq** — the Quranic antediluvian-pantheon cluster.
- **Northwest-Semitic raptor-god type** — partial typological parallel to Akkadian Anzu / Zû, the lion-headed eagle-god of Mesopotamian mythology.
- **South Arabian Almaqah / Wadd / Sīn / ʿAmm** — fellow South Arabian regional deities, each with its own astral / theriomorphic profile.

## Iconography
- **Vulture / eagle / raptor form** — theriomorphic, per the Arabic *n-s-r* root.
- **Mountain-shrine cult** at Balkhaʿ in Himyar per Ibn al-Kalbi.
- **No surviving Nasr-image** confirmed from Himyaritic archaeology; iconographic reconstruction relies on the theriomorphic name and Islamic-tradition description.

## Disputes
- **Historicity of the specific Balkhaʿ / Dhū l-Kalāʿ cult description.** Per Hawting 1999, the detailed Islamic-tradition reconstructive description is later polemical-reconstructive material; the historical core (Nasr as a Himyaritic / South Arabian raptor-god, preserved in Quranic memory) is secure; the iconographic and tribal details should be read source-critically.
- **The antediluvian-pantheon framing.** The Quranic Noah-cycle's placement of Nasr in pre-Flood polytheist mouths is a theological-typological move (Reynolds 2018); the historical content of the list is late-antique Hijazi memory of these specific god-names.
- **Whether Nasr is a single deity or a class.** Some scholars have suggested *Nasr* could be a theophoric epithet ("the eagle-one") applied to multiple regional deities rather than a single named god; the evidence is thin in either direction. (Hoyland 2001.)
