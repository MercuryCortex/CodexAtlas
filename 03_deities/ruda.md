# Ruḍā

Ruḍā (*Rḍw*) was a prominent **astral deity of the North Arabian nomads** — the Thamudic and Safaitic tribes who left thousands of brief rock-graffiti across the deserts — associated with the **evening star, the planet Venus**, and invoked for protection, good fortune, and aid against enemies. Ruḍā is one of the deities most frequently named in Thamudic inscriptions, marking a major figure of the pre-Islamic nomadic pantheon. A striking feature is the **instability of the deity's gender**: Ruḍā appears in some contexts as a goddess and in others as a god, and overlaps in astral function with **[[al-uzza|al-ʿUzzā]]**, the Venus-goddess of the settled Arabs. The name is very old: the Assyrian annals of **Esarhaddon** (7th c. BC) record an Arabian deity *Ruldaiu* among idols carried off and returned, an early external attestation of the cult. Ruḍā exemplifies the astral orientation of North Arabian desert religion, in which the bright planets were the great protective powers.
