# Prajñāpāramitā — the Perfection of Wisdom

The *Prajñāpāramitā* sutras (composed ~-100 to +400) are the foundational Mahayana scriptural corpus; their central insight is [[emptiness-sunyata]]. The corpus ranges from the 100,000-verse *Śatasāhasrikā* down to the distilled *Heart Sutra* and *Diamond Sutra*. As doctrinal teaching, *prajñāpāramitā* is the transcendent wisdom that perceives the empty, dependently-arisen nature of all phenomena — the wisdom *by which* one becomes a Buddha.

In a striking iconographic move, this wisdom is also *personified* as a goddess — "the Mother of all Buddhas." She is the womb (*mātṛkā*) of awakening: each Buddha is born of her insight. Iconographically she sits on a lotus, four-armed, holding the *Prajñāpāramitā* text on a lotus stalk over her left shoulder.

This personification illustrates a structural Mahayana move: abstract doctrines acquire devotional/iconographic personalities without losing their philosophical content. The same logic produces Tārā, Avalokiteśvara, Mañjuśrī as personifications of specific Buddha-qualities. Compare [[wisdom-personified]] (Sophia / Chokhmah) — independent emergence of the same personification-of-wisdom-as-feminine pattern, with no transmission claimed by careful scholarship.
