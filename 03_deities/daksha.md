# Daksha

## Identity

Daksha (Sanskrit **Dakṣa**, "the skilled/able one") is a Hindu **Prajapati** — one of the progenitor-gods born of Brahma — and a great patriarch and master of ritual. He is the father of a vast progeny of daughters who become the wives of the gods: among them the twenty-seven *nakshatra* daughters married to the Moon-god Chandra (the lunar mansions), Aditi (mother of the gods) and Diti (mother of the demons), and — most fatefully — **Sati**, who becomes the wife of Shiva.

## Mythological role

Daksha's defining story is the **Daksha-yajna** — the sacrifice of Daksha. Disapproving of his ascetic, unconventional son-in-law [[shiva]], Daksha holds a great sacrifice and pointedly **excludes** Shiva and refuses him a portion. Sati, Shiva's wife and Daksha's daughter, attends despite Shiva's misgivings; humiliated by her father's insult to her husband, she **immolates herself** in the sacrificial fire (the origin of her name "Sati"). The enraged Shiva creates the fierce Virabhadra (and the goddess Bhadrakali), who storm the sacrifice, scatter the gods, and **behead Daksha**. Eventually, entreated by the gods, Shiva relents and restores Daksha to life — but with the **head of a goat**, since his own head has been destroyed. Sati is later reborn as [[parvati]] and wins Shiva anew.

## Cross-tradition significance

The Daksha-yajna dramatizes a deep tension in Hindu religion — between **orthodox brahminical sacrifice** (Daksha) and the **transgressive, ascetic, outsider divinity** (Shiva) — and its resolution in Shiva's forced inclusion in the sacrificial order. As a progenitor-patriarch whose many daughters populate the divine genealogies, Daksha also belongs to the cross-tradition type of the **primal ancestor/patriarch** whose offspring become the gods and the cosmic powers (compare the genealogical Titans and the Mesopotamian progenitor-gods). The motif of the slighted deity who violently demands his rightful share of the offering is itself widespread.
