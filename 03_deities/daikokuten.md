# Daikokuten

Daikokuten ("the Great Black Deva," 大黒天) is the Japanese god of **wealth, the household, and the harvest**, and one of the beloved **Seven Lucky Gods (Shichifukujin)** — recognizable everywhere by his **magic mallet** (a tap of which produces treasure), his great **sack of riches**, his perch atop **two bales of rice**, and his broad smile and flat black cap. His origins, however, are not Japanese at all: he is the **Japanese form of [[mahakala|Mahākāla]]**, the fierce dark Buddhist-and-Hindu protector, brought into Japan as a guardian and **kitchen-god of esoteric (Tendai and Shingon) temples**, where the "great black" deity watched over the monastery's food stores. Over the medieval centuries this terrifying protector was softened into a genial bringer of plenty — and, crucially, was identified by **wordplay with the Shinto land-god [[okuninushi|Ōkuninushi]]**, since the characters of Ōkuninushi's epithet *Ōkuni* ("great land," 大国) can also be read *Daikoku* (大黒). Through this homophonic fusion the imported Buddhist deva and the native earth-deity merged into a single, thoroughly Japanese god of fortune. Daikokuten is a textbook case of the *honji suijaku* combinatory religion of Japan — an Indian deity reborn as a smiling household god.
