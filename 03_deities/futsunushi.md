# Futsunushi

## Identity
Futsunushi is the Shintō **sword-kami** — the divine power of the cutting blade and of martial pacification, enshrined at **Katori** in eastern Japan and revered through the centuries by warriors and swordsmen.

## Earliest attestation
Recorded in the *Nihon Shoki* (720 CE) and *Kojiki* (712 CE); his name (*futsu*) evokes the swift, decisive stroke of the sword. Katori Shrine, his seat, is one of Japan's oldest and was a centre of the martial tradition.

## Mythological role — the pacification of the land
With [[takemikazuchi]] of Kashima, Futsunushi is sent down from the High Plain of Heaven to **subdue the Central Land of Reed Plains** (Izumo) before the descent of the heavenly grandson. The two sword-kami confront [[okuninushi]] and his sons, planting a blade upright in the waves and demanding the land's surrender — the mythic charter for the heavenly dynasty's rule. Futsunushi is thus the divine sword that pacifies, paired with Takemikazuchi as the two great warrior-kami of the east.

## Cross-tradition
Futsunushi belongs to the type of the **divine sword / martial pacifier** — the blade made god — and stands with Takemikazuchi in the Shintō account of conquest by sacred weapon.

## Iconography
The upright sword; the lightning-stroke; the warrior's blade enshrined at Katori.
