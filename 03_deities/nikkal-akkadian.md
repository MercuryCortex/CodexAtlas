# Nikkal

## Identity
Nikkal is the Akkadian/West-Semitic phonological evolution of Sumerian [[nin-gal-sumerian]] ("Great Lady") — the consort of the moon-god ([[nanna-sin]] in Mesopotamia, Yarikh in Ugarit), mother of the major astral deities (Inanna/Ishtar = Venus; Utu/Shamash = sun), and queen-of-the-moon-cult across an enormous geographical and chronological range. The Ningal → Nikkal phonological shift (loss of medial -g-, with Ningal → Nigal → Nikkal) is fully established by the Old Babylonian period; the name then radiates outward with the moon-cult of Sin into West Semitic and Aramaic contexts. **Harran** in northern Mesopotamia/Syria becomes the principal cult center of the Sin-Nikkal pair from the Middle Assyrian period onward, and the Harran cult survives — extraordinarily — into Late Antique and even early Islamic times among the **Sabians of Harran**, who venerated this pair as their principal divine couple as late as the 9th–10th centuries CE. At **Ugarit** (Late Bronze Age, ~-1300), Nikkal appears as **Nikkal-wa-Ib** in the tablet *KTU 1.24*, the **Marriage of Nikkal and Yarikh** — a poetic celebration of her marriage to the Ugaritic moon-god, with elaborate dowry-negotiation that is one of the most charming West Semitic ritual texts.

## Earliest attestation
- Old Babylonian sources begin using the Akkadian-form Nikkal alongside the Sumerian writing NIN.GAL.
- Middle Assyrian and Neo-Assyrian inscriptions document the Sin-Nikkal cult at Harran.
- The **Ugaritic *Marriage of Nikkal and Yarikh*** (KTU 1.24, ~-1300) is the principal West-Semitic Nikkal text.
- Aramaic inscriptions of the 1st millennium BCE document the cult across northern Syria and northern Mesopotamia.
- Late Antique sources (Bardaisan, the *Doctrine of Addai*, and Arabic-language descriptions of the Harran cult by writers such as Ibn al-Nadim and al-Mas'udi) attest the survival of the Sin-Nikkal pair at Harran into the early Islamic centuries.

## Mythological role
- ***Marriage of Nikkal and Yarikh (KTU 1.24)***: the principal West-Semitic narrative. The text opens with the moon-god Yarikh requesting Nikkal in marriage; a complicated dowry-negotiation between Yarikh and Nikkal's father (named *Hrhb*, a kingship-figure) is conducted; gifts of precious metals and goods are exchanged; the marriage is celebrated. The poem ends with a hymn to the *Kotharat* (the divine midwife-goddesses) ensuring fertile issue. The text is a marriage-blessing text — likely recited at human weddings as well as at the cult-marriage.
- ***Mesopotamian moon-cult consort***: in standard Mesopotamian theology Nikkal/Ningal is the consort of Sin and mother of Ishtar/Inanna and Shamash/Utu, inheriting Ningal's mythological profile entirely.
- ***Harran cult***: from the Middle Assyrian period the Sin-Nikkal pair at Harran becomes one of the most stable cults in the ANE, surviving political and religious upheaval — including the Roman conquest, the rise of Christianity, and the Islamic conquest — into the 10th century CE among the Sabians of Harran.
- ***Lamentation tradition***: the Sumerian *Lamentation over the Destruction of Ur* (in which Ningal mourns Ur) is the foundational lamentation text; the Akkadian-language inheritance of this tradition continues to associate Nikkal with mourning of destroyed cult-cities.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[nin-gal-sumerian]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Sumerian source; Nikkal is the phonological-and-linguistic evolution, not a syncretism between independently-emerged deities. The two are the same goddess across the Sumerian-Akkadian-West-Semitic linguistic continuum.
- **Yarikh** (Ugaritic moon-god) — *consort*: in the Ugaritic context Nikkal is paired with Yarikh rather than Sin, but the underlying theological structure (moon-god + queen-consort) is identical.
- *Scholarly parallel*: Greek Selene (as queen-of-the-moon) is sometimes compared; this is *scholarly-parallel*, not an ancient identification. The Greek moon-cult is structurally different (no male consort moon-god in the central Greek pantheon).

## Iconography
Seated goddess in horned crown alongside the moon-god; in seal scenes typically with a crescent over her shoulder or above the throne. At Harran her iconography fuses with the local Aramaic-style moon-cult imagery; Sabian-era descriptions emphasize lunar-astral attributes.

## Disputes
- **Nikkal-wa-Ib**. The Ugaritic *Nkl wa-Ib* (KTU 1.24) names "Nikkal-and-Ib." Whether *Ib* is a doublet-name, an epithet (perhaps "fruit"), a separate paired goddess, or a divine ancestor is debated. Pardee 2002 reviews the possibilities; the conservative reading is that Nikkal-wa-Ib is a doubled-name divine personality (like West-Semitic *Kothar-wa-Hasis*), not two beings.
- **Harran continuity**. The Sabians of Harran are described by Arabic writers as preserving the Sin-Nikkal cult into the 9th–10th centuries CE. Whether this is an unbroken continuity from the Bronze Age cult or a Late Antique reconstruction (perhaps under Neo-Platonist influence) is debated; Tamer Green's *The City of the Moon God* (1992) argues for genuine continuity with adaptation.
- **Ningal → Nikkal phonological reconstruction**. The shift is uncontroversial as Akkadian internal evolution; the question of whether the West-Semitic Nikkal is a Mesopotamian import (the dominant view) or an independently-emerged West-Semitic cognate that converges with the Mesopotamian form (a minority view) has been raised at Ebla and Mari. The current consensus favors import-and-adaptation.
