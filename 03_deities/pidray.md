# Pidray

## Identity

Pidray (Ugaritic **Pdry**, "girl of light" or "girl of mist") is a Canaanite goddess named as one of the three **daughters of [[baal]]**, the storm-god of Ugarit. With her sisters **Tallay** ("girl of dew/rain") and **Arsay** ("girl of the earth/underworld"), Pidray personifies an aspect of the moisture through which Baal's storms fertilize the land — she is the light-and-mist that accompanies the rain.

## Mythological role

In the **Baal Cycle**, Baal's three daughters — Pidray, Tallay, and Arsay — are repeatedly named together as members of the storm-god's household; Baal instructs the messenger-gods to bring word of his palace and power to them. Pidray, listed first, is the most prominent of the three and appears elsewhere in the Ugaritic ritual and pantheon texts as a recipient of offerings. The daughters together form a small triad embodying the beneficent waters — mist, dew, and the moisture of the earth — that make the difference between fertile and barren land in the dry Levantine climate.

## Cross-tradition significance

Pidray and her sisters belong to the Canaanite theology of **storm-and-fertility**, in which the rain-bringing storm-god's power is distributed among a household of female deities personifying mist, dew, and earth-moisture. This dramatizes the same concern — the divine provision of the water on which life depends — that animates the wider Near Eastern conflict between [[baal]] (rain) and [[mot]] (drought/death) and that lies behind the biblical theology of Yahweh as the giver of the early and latter rains. As a minor but securely attested member of Baal's circle, Pidray helps complete the Ugaritic pantheon around its central storm-god.
