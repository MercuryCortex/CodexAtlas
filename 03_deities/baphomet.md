# Baphomet

## The accusation (1307–1314)

When Philip IV of France arrested the Templars in October 1307, the interrogations extracted confessions of idol-worship under torture. The idol was variously called "Baphomet" (a name of uncertain etymology — possibly corrupted Arabic *Muhammad*, possibly a Provençal corruption of *Mahomet*, possibly unrelated entirely). The descriptions in the trial transcripts are wildly inconsistent:
- A bearded head
- A cat
- A figure with two faces (male and female)
- A carved skull
- A carved man on a horse
- A woman

**The scholarly verdict:** Barber (2006) and Frale (2007) — the two leading Templar historians — both conclude that the torture-extracted confessions cannot be taken as evidence of actual Templar practice. The Chinon Parchment (1308, discovered in the Vatican Secret Archive in 2001) records Pope Clement V's private absolution of the Templar leaders of heresy — while publicly condemning them under pressure from Philip IV. The idol-worship accusation was the political instrument of suppression, not the historical cause.

The historical Templars almost certainly did not worship "Baphomet." The name entered Western culture as an accusation.

## Éliphas Lévi's reconstruction (1854)

The accusation lay dormant until **Éliphas Lévi** (*Dogme et Rituel de la Haute Magie*, 1854) adopted the name and created the image that now defines "Baphomet" in world culture. Lévi's engraving shows:
- A goat-headed androgynous figure sitting in a pentagrammatic posture
- A torch between the horns (the "flame of intelligence")
- Arms pointing up and down, inscribed *SOLVE ET COAGULA* (dissolve and bind — the alchemical maxim)
- Female breasts (the divine androgyny — the coincidence of opposites in a single figure)
- A caduceus at the groin (Hermetic symbol of Hermes/Mercury)
- Bat wings

For Lévi, this figure **IS NOT A DEVIL**. It is the Hermetic Absolute — the symbol of the reconciliation of all opposites (male/female, light/dark, dissolve/bind). He explicitly states: "This is not the devil as imagined by the Middle Ages. It is Pan, it is the Universal Agent."

## Reception and adoption

The Lévi figure became the most widely appropriated occult image in Western culture:
- **Aleister Crowley** (already in vault): adopted the name as a magical persona ("To Mega Therion — the Great Beast") and identified with Baphomet; Thelema's Beast 666 is partly shaped by the Lévi figure
- **Anton LaVey / Church of Satan (1966):** adopted the goat-head in a pentagram (now called the "Sigil of Baphomet") as the Church of Satan's primary symbol — explicitly collapsingLévi's Hermetic symbol into the Christian-diabolical identification
- **The Satanic Temple (2012–present):** commissioned a bronze Baphomet statue (eight feet tall; unveiled 2019) — using the Lévi figure as a legal-political symbol of religious freedom and separation of church and state; the statue has been deployed in church-state separation court cases

The three uses are entirely different: Hermetic synthesis (Lévi), religious shock-value (LaVey), legal-political provocation (The Satanic Temple). The figure's meaning has multiplied across each adoption.

## What "Baphomet" actually shows

The Baphomet story is a perfect example of the vault's core methodology: a name that begins as a fabricated inquisitorial accusation → becomes a Hermetic symbol through 19th-century occult reconstruction → becomes a Satanic symbol through 20th-century religious appropriation → becomes a legal-political symbol through 21st-century deployment. Each stage is documented. The figure is real as a cultural phenomenon; the historical idol is not.
