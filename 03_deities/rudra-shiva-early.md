# Rudra / early Śiva

## Identity
Rudra is the Vedic outsider god — fewer Rig Vedic hymns than Indra or Agni, but a vivid presence: the howler, the lord of wild beasts, who bestows disease and healing both, who lives at the margins of the inhabited world, accompanied by his storm-band the Maruts. The famous Śatarudriya hymn (Yajurveda) catalogues his many forms. By the time of the Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad (a slightly later Upaniṣad, ~-400 BCE or thereabouts), Rudra has become *Śiva* — "auspicious" — and is being addressed as supreme cosmic deity, foreshadowing classical Śaivism.

The proposed continuity from the Indus Valley "Paśupati" seal (a horned cross-legged figure surrounded by animals, Mohenjo-Daro ~-2500) to Vedic Rudra to classical Śiva is a famous but contested hypothesis. Marshall (1931) proposed it; Doniger and others note the lack of a documented chain. The Indus Valley script remains undeciphered, so we cannot directly read this iconography. Whether or not the Paśupati seal is "proto-Śiva", Rudra is unambiguously the Vedic seed of what becomes one of Hinduism's two great theistic streams (Śaivism alongside Vaiṣṇavism).
