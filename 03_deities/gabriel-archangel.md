# Gabriel

Chief messenger archangel of the Abrahamic traditions. First named in Hebrew scripture in [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel|Daniel]] (8:16, 9:21) — Maccabean-era apocalyptic, post-Persian. The archangelic naming-of-named-angels is a Second Temple Jewish development with strong Persian-period influence ([[the-amesha-spentas|Zoroastrian Amesha Spentas]] are the structural precedent per Boyce, Shaked, Hultgård).

In each tradition:
- **Christianity** — Gabriel announces the birth of John the Baptist to Zechariah and the conception of Jesus to Mary ([[phase-3-018-luke-acts|Luke 1]]); the Annunciation becomes one of the most-painted scenes in Western art (Fra Angelico, Botticelli, Da Vinci, Caravaggio).
- **Judaism** — Gabriel as one of the four archangels (with Michael, Raphael, Uriel) in apocalyptic and Hekhalot literature.
- **Islam** — **Jibril** is the angel who reveals the Quran to Muhammad over 22 years. The Quran itself is Jibril's voice transmitting God's speech. Foundational for Islamic prophetology.

The shared Gabriel-figure across all three Abrahamic religions is one of the clearest cases of intra-tradition continuity at the angelological layer. Iconographically distinct in each.
