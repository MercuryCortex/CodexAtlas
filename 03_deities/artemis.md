# Artemis

## Identity
Artemis is the Greek Olympian of the wilderness, the hunt, virginity, and the transitions of young women (especially the rituals of girls' coming-of-age — *arkteia* at Brauron, where Athenian girls "played the bear"). Twin sister of Apollo, daughter of Zeus and Leto, virgin huntress, lady of wild animals (*potnia thērōn*). Her major sanctuaries: Ephesus (the great Anatolian Artemis), Brauron in Attica, Delos (her birthplace), Sparta (Artemis Orthia and the ephebic flogging ritual).

The Linear B tablets attest "a-ti-mi-te" at Pylos, securing her Bronze Age Mycenaean attestation. Her epithet *potnia thērōn* (Iliad 21.470) — "Mistress of Animals" — connects her to a much broader Eastern Mediterranean / Near Eastern type of the wild-animal-flanking goddess attested iconographically from the Bronze Age (Anatolian, Aegean, Levantine). The Artemis of Ephesus is iconographically very different from the maiden huntress of the Greek mainland — covered with multiple breast-like protrusions (some scholars: testicles of sacrificed bulls; some: dates; some: actual breasts), reflecting deep syncretism with Anatolian Mother-Goddess types adjacent to Cybele. Acts 19 records the riot at Ephesus over "Artemis of the Ephesians" — a measure of how distinct and important this cult was even in the early Christian period.
