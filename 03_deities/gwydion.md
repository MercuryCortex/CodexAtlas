# Gwydion

## Identity
Gwydion ap Dôn is the great enchanter of the Fourth Branch of the *Mabinogi* — the most magically powerful figure in the Welsh mythological tradition, and one of the most sophisticated divine-trickster figures in any European mythology. Son of Dôn (the Welsh divine mother, cognate of Irish Danu, probable reflex of the Proto-Celtic divine-mother *\*Dānu*), Gwydion is the nephew of the master-mathematician god **Math fab Mathonwy** and the guardian/creator of **[[lleu-llaw-gyffes]]**.

His name's etymology is uncertain; some scholars connect it to a root meaning "speech" or "poetry" (*gwydd* = "trees/knowledge/presence" + *-ion* masculine suffix), which would align him with the bardic-magical function his character embodies. In Welsh astronomical tradition, the **Milky Way** is called *Caer Gwydion* ("the fortress of Gwydion") — placing him in the celestial register alongside his role as the supreme mortal-world enchanter.

## Earliest attestation
The Fourth Branch of the Mabinogi (*Math uab Mathonwy*) survives in 14th-century manuscripts (*Llyfr Gwyn Rhydderch*, ~1350; *Llyfr Coch Hergest*, ~1400) drawing on oral tradition estimated to the pre-Norman period. The Welsh Triads mention Gwydion as one of the three great enchanters of Britain. There is no earlier textual attestation by name, though his divine type (trickster-enchanter son of the divine mother) is attested across Celtic branches.

## Mythological role
**The War Against Pryderi.** Gwydion covets the magical pigs that have come from Annwn (the otherworld) to Pryderi, lord of Dyfed. He travels to Pryderi's court disguised as a bard, tells stories all evening (Gwydion is also the supreme storyteller), and in the morning trades the pigs for an illusion of horses and dogs he has created with magic. When the illusion dissolves, Pryderi discovers the deception; war breaks out between Gwynedd and Dyfed. Gwydion defeats and kills Pryderi in single combat — a war caused entirely by his trickery.

**The Three Prohibitions on Lleu.** Gwydion's sister Arianrhod places three prohibitions (*tyngedau*) on her son Lleu: he will have no name unless she gives it; no arms unless she gives them; no human wife. Gwydion circumvents each prohibition through disguise and illusion — he tricks Arianrhod into naming Lleu and arming him, and he and Math create a wife of flowers for Lleu (**Blodeuwedd**). Gwydion's relationship to Lleu is essentially paternal — he creates him, protects him, and ultimately rescues him after Blodeuwedd's betrayal.

**The Animal Punishment.** For the crime of instigating the war against Pryderi (and the assault by his brother Gilfaethwy on Goewin, Math's foot-holder), Gwydion and Gilfaethwy are punished by Math: they are transformed into paired animals for three years — first a stag and a hind, then a boar and a sow, then a wolf and a she-wolf — each year forced to mate and produce offspring. The shapeshifting punishment makes Gwydion *experience* what his magic usually *imposes* on others.

**Lleu's Rescue.** After Lleu is spear-stabbed and transforms into an eagle, Gwydion tracks him through the sky, finds him perched in an oak tree, and restores him to human form through a series of sung *englynion* (verse-incantations) — three stanzas of decreasing distance that draw Lleu down from the branches into Gwydion's arms.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[loki]]** — *cross-tradition-parallel*: the divine trickster who instigates conflict through shapeshifting and deception, is punished for his trickery, and occupies the structurally necessary role of conflict-instigator in a mythological narrative.
- **[[odin]]** — *cross-tradition-parallel*: the wandering master of disguise and sorcery; Odin's *seiðr* magic and Gwydion's *hud* (enchantment) are both transgressive forms of divine magic associated with feminized/liminal practice.
- **[[hermes-greek]]** — *cross-tradition-parallel*: the divine trickster-craftsman who crosses boundaries and moves between worlds; the PIE trickster-divine type.
- **[[lugh]]** — *etymological cognate (debate)*: the Fourth Branch makes Gwydion the creator and protector of Lleu, the Welsh form of Lugh; whether this encodes a divine genealogy (the older magical tradition giving birth to the newer luminous-hero deity) or simply reflects narrative role is debated.
