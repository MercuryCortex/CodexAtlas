# Ares

## Identity
Ares is the Greek god of violent war, bloodshed, and martial frenzy — pointedly the *reckless* god of battle in contrast to Athena's strategic, disciplined warfare. The Homeric Olympians, including his own parents Zeus and Hera, treat him with a degree of disdain unique among the major gods (Iliad 5.890: "Most hateful to me of all the gods who hold Olympus art thou, for ever is strife dear to thee, and wars and slayings"). His most famous mythic role is his adulterous affair with Aphrodite, exposed in song in Odyssey 8 (the Demodocus episode).

Ares has cult — at Sparta, in Thrace (associated with him geographically), at the Athenian Areopagus — but considerably less than the other major Olympians. Linear B "a-re" at Knossos attests him in the Bronze Age. The Roman identification with Mars is *interpretatio graeca* but produces a striking asymmetry: Mars is the second-most-important Roman god (after Jupiter), father of Romulus, patron of the legions; the Greek Ares is a relative marginal figure. This asymmetry tracks the relative martial centrality of the two cultures.
