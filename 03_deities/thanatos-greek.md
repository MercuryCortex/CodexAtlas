# Thanatos

Thanatos (Greek *Thánatos*, "Death") is the personification of death in Greek myth — specifically of gentle, non-violent death (as distinct from the violent slaughter embodied by the Keres). Hesiod makes him a son of **[[nyx-primordial]]** (Night), born without a father, and the twin brother of **[[hypnos]]** (Sleep), with whom he is paired in art and poetry as a winged youth bearing an inverted torch (*Theogony* 211–212, 758–766). In *Iliad* 16 the twins Hypnos and Thanatos carry the body of Sarpedon home to Lycia. He is best known dramatically from Euripides' *Alcestis*, where Heracles physically wrestles Thanatos to win Alcestis back from death — one of the few myths in which Death is bested. The Romans render him **Mors**. Structurally he parallels the West-Semitic death-power **[[mot]]**, though without genetic connection.
