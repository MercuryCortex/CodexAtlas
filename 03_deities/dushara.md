# Dushara

Dushara (Arabic *Dhū al-Sharā*, "He of the Shara," after the mountain range behind Petra; Greek *Dousarēs/Dusares*) is the **supreme god of the Nabataeans** and the patron of their kings — "Dushara, the god of our lord [the king]" in Nabataean dedications. Like most North-Arabian deities he was worshipped **aniconically**, embodied in a *betyl* (Aramaic *mšbʾ*, "god-block"): a plain rectangular standing stone, the most famous being the great black block in Petra's holy-of-holies. As the Nabataean kingdom Hellenized, Dushara was identified above all with **Dionysus** (god of the vine and of seasonal death-and-renewal, with which his mountain-vegetation aspect resonated), and also with Zeus and Helios; coins of Bostra advertise the *actia Dusaria* games in his honor. His great goddess partner is **[[allat]]** (with al-Uzza). His cult reached from Petra across the Hauran and survived into the Roman provincial period, one of the best-documented Arabian religions.
