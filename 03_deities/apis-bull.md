# Apis

## Identity
Apis was the sacred Memphite bull — a single living animal at any given time, recognized at birth by ritual marks (a black coat with a white triangular blaze on the forehead, a scarab-shaped mark on the tongue, and a vulture-shaped patch on the back), worshipped at Memphis as the living *bꜢ* (manifestation) of [[ptah]] the Memphite creator-god. The reigning Apis bull was housed in a luxurious stall in the Memphite Ptah-temple precinct, fed, paraded in processions, and consulted oracularly (the bull's choice among presented food items, or its movements in its stall, were read as divine answers). At its death the Apis was mummified, processed in elaborate funerary ceremony, and entombed in the Serapeum at Saqqara — a gallery of monumental basalt sarcophagi excavated by Auguste Mariette in 1851. The dead Apis was henceforth identified with [[osiris]] under the compound name *Wsr-Ḥp* (Osor-Hapi / Osorapis); in the early Ptolemaic period (~-305 under Ptolemy I Soter) this Osor-Hapi was Hellenized into [[serapis]], the state-cult god of Ptolemaic Egypt, with iconography drawn from Greek Zeus/Hades/Asclepius but theological substance from the Memphite bull cult.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ptah]]** — *ancient-identification*: living Apis is Ptah's animal-incarnation.
- **[[osiris]]** — *ancient-identification* (at death): deceased Apis = Osor-Hapi.
- **[[serapis]]** — *Hellenistic-construct*: Ptolemy I's state-cult deity Serapis = Wsr-Ḥp Hellenized, fusing the dead Apis-cycle with Greek anthropomorphic theology.

## Disputes
- **Continuity of the cult.** The Apis cult ran ~3000 years with attested individual bull burials from at least the New Kingdom continuing into the Roman period; some scholars dispute whether the very early (Old Kingdom) attestations describe the same institutional cult or a structurally similar predecessor.
- **Etymology of "Serapis."** The Greek *Sarapis* < *Oserapis* < *Wsr-Ḥp* derivation is the modern consensus; older etymologies from "Sin-Apis" or other foreign-god derivations (Tacitus, *Hist.* 4.84) are now rejected.
