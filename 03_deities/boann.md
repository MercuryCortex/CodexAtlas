# Boann

## Identity
Boann (*Bóinn*, "White Cow" or "She of the White Cattle") is the divine source of the **River Boyne** in Irish mythology — she does not merely govern the river; she *is* the river. Her myth explains the Boyne's origin as a consequence of her transgression of a sacred well's prohibitions, and her body flows to the sea as the river itself.

## The creation of the Boyne
Boann's husband Nechtan possesses a sacred well (**Tobar Segais** or Connla's Well) that is forbidden to all but him and his three cup-bearers. Boann deliberately transgresses the prohibition, walking around the well counterclockwise (the sinister direction) to test its power. The well rises against her, stripping one arm, one leg, and one eye — and the waters chase her to the sea, creating the River Boyne as her flight-path.

This myth encodes a theological statement: sacred knowledge (the well = the source of poetry and wisdom) cannot be appropriated without consequence, but the consequence of transgression is the creation of a river that makes that same inspiration available to humanity. Boann's violation *gives* the world the Boyne.

## Mother of Aengus and Brú na Bóinne
Boann is the mother of [[aengus-og]] by the [[dagda]] — conceived during Nechtan's absence, with the sun held still so that the entire nine months passed as one day. This places both Aengus and Boann in intimate mythological connection with **Brú na Bóinne** (Newgrange), which sits directly in the Boyne valley. The river goddess and the love deity share the most sacred landscape of prehistoric Ireland.

## Saraswati parallel
The structural parallel between Boann (Irish river goddess of poetic inspiration) and Saraswati (Vedic river goddess of sacred speech and arts) may reflect a PIE sacred-river-of-inspiration type. Both are rivers that give creative/intellectual power; both are feminine. Olmsted (1994) pursues this comparison in detail.
