# Mārīcī

Mārīcī (Sanskrit "ray of light," "mirage"; Japanese **Marishiten**, Chinese **Molizhitian**) is a Buddhist deity of the **dawn-light and the rays of the sun** — the radiance that runs *ahead* of the visible sun, and so a power of **light, concealment, and invisibility**. Because she cannot be seen, seized, or harmed (any more than one can grasp a mirage or the dawn), she became a great **protective deity**, invoked for safety on journeys and, above all, for **invulnerability and victory in battle**: in Japan, **Marishiten** was a celebrated tutelary of the **samurai** and of the martial arts. Her iconography is vivid and ancient: she rides a **chariot drawn by boars** (or stands upon a boar), often with multiple arms and heads — one of them a boar's — carrying bow, needle and thread, and emblems of light. She represents a Buddhist absorption and transformation of pan-Indian **dawn** symbolism, inviting comparison with the Vedic dawn-goddess **[[ushas|Uṣas]]** and with solar deities such as **[[surya|Sūrya]]**, before whom she goes as the first light. Mārīcī embodies the protective, unseizable power of light at the edge of darkness.
