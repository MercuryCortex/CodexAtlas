# Kronos

## Identity
Kronos is the Greek pre-Olympian Titan king, son of Uranus and Gaia, who at his mother's prompting castrates his father with a sickle (the act that releases his siblings the other Titans and produces Aphrodite from the foam), then rules the cosmos until overthrown by his own son Zeus. His paranoid swallowing of each of his children at birth (to forestall the prophecy of being overthrown) — and Rhea's substitution of a stone for the infant Zeus — is the central scene of the Hesiodic succession myth.

Kronos's role is the *middle* generation of the three-generation succession (Uranus → Kronos → Zeus). Despite his violent reign in mythology, the Greek tradition also associates him with the *Golden Age* — a paradoxical pairing where his reign is remembered as a time of paradisal ease for mortals, before agriculture and toil. Folk etymology identifies *Kronos* with *Chronos* (Time), reinforcing his image as the devouring god of time (whence "Father Time" with his sickle in Western iconography); the philological identification is dubious, but the cultural pairing is ancient and influential.

The Hittite Kumarbi cycle's parallel — Kumarbi castrates the older sky-god Anu by biting off his genitals — is one of the cleanest cross-Mediterranean myth-transfer cases (West 1997). The Phoenician Sanchuniathon (preserved by Philo of Byblos / Eusebius) describes a parallel succession involving Elioun-El-Baal, suggesting Levantine intermediation as well. The Roman Saturn inherits Kronos's Golden Age association in the festival of the Saturnalia.
