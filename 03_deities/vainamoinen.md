# Väinämöinen

## Identity
The principal mythological hero of the Karelian-Finnish runo-poetry tradition and of Lönnrot's Kalevala. Väinämöinen is a primordial shaman-singer of extraordinary age, born of the air-maiden Ilmatar after centuries in her womb. He is the master *tietäjä* (knower / verbal-magician) of the cosmos's middle layer; his songs accomplish creative and destructive work; he is wise, melancholic, and ultimately displaced by the coming of a new (Christ-prefiguring) generation at the Kalevala's close. Väinämöinen's status as god, hero, ancestor, or cultural projection of the *tietäjä* role is variously interpreted across the tradition.

## Earliest attestation
Karelian-Finnish runo-poetry; documented in collection from the 17th c. (Petrus Magnus, Gabriel Calamnius) and especially from the 18th–19th c. (Henrik Gabriel Porthan, the *Suomen Kansan Vanhat Runot* corpus); central to Lönnrot's compilation of the Kalevala (1835/1849).

## Mythological role
In the Kalevala cosmogony, the egg cosmogony precedes Väinämöinen; he is born after Ilmatar's long pregnancy and emerges from the sea. His mythological-narrative activity is extensive: contests with rival singer Joukahainen (whom Väinämöinen sinks into a swamp by song); the wooing journeys (his attempt to win the Maiden of Pohjola fails); the forging of the Sampo by Ilmarinen at Väinämöinen's instigation; the Sampo-recovery raid; the construction of the first *kantele* from a giant pike's jawbone; his sailing away into the sea at the Kalevala's close after Marjatta's miraculous son supplants him.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Odin** — *scholarly-parallel*: both are wandering wise-old singer-shamans, masters of word-magic; the typological similarity is striking and the cultures had historical contact (Norse-Finnish), but no philological identification.
- **Christ-prefigured-supplanter narrative**: the Kalevala's closing canto (the Marjatta-and-child cycle) supplants Väinämöinen — strongly suggesting Christian-period reshaping of the tradition's closing material.

## Iconography
Old man with long white beard; *kantele*; sailing boat.
