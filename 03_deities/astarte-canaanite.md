# Astarte (Canaanite)

## Identity
Astarte (West Semitic *ʿAštart*) is the principal love-and-war goddess of the Canaanite and Phoenician pantheon. She appears in Ugaritic ritual texts (where she is rare in narrative but prominent in offerings); becomes central in the Phoenician city-states of Tyre, Sidon, and Byblos in the Iron Age; and travels with Phoenician colonization throughout the Mediterranean. She is the Levantine member of the great Semitic / Mediterranean love-and-war goddess complex — Inanna (Sumerian), Ishtar (Akkadian), Astarte (Canaanite/Phoenician), Aphrodite (Greek, decisively shaped by Phoenician influence on Cyprus).

## Earliest attestation
- Ugaritic ritual texts and offering lists, late Bronze Age (~-1400/-1200): see [[phase-1-014-ugaritic-ritual-texts]]; appearances in [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]] are subsidiary.
- Phoenician inscriptions from the early Iron Age onward.
- Egyptian New Kingdom syncretism: Astarte is incorporated into the Egyptian pantheon as a martial goddess (especially under the Ramessides); appears with Anat as a syncretic compound.
- Hebrew Bible: appears as *ʿAshtoreth* / pl. *ʿAshtarot*, vocalized polemically with the vowels of *bosheth* "shame" (1 Kings 11:5, 2 Kings 23:13, Judg 2:13; 1 Sam 7:3–4); represents foreign worship the Deuteronomistic Historian condemns.

## Mythological role
At Ugarit Astarte is less narratively elaborated than Anat (who plays the active war-goddess role in the Baal Cycle); her ritual prominence outstrips her narrative role. In Phoenicia she becomes the principal goddess of major city-states — *Astarte of Sidon*, *Astarte of Byblos*, *Astarte of Tyre* — each with distinct cultic traditions. She is queen, protectress of king and city, goddess of love and military victory.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ishtar-akkadian]]** — *scholarly-parallel* (genuine etymological cognate, shared functional core): both descend from a common Semitic *ʿAṯtar-* root; the deity is functionally the same across the East-West Semitic divide, though developed independently.
- **[[aphrodite-greek]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Phoenician colony at Paphos on Cyprus appears to be the main locus of Astarte→Aphrodite transmission; iconographic markers (nudity, dove, planetary Venus) follow. See [[divine-feminine]].
- **[[anat]]** — *folk-syncretism* in Egyptian Late Bronze Age; *distinct goddesses* at Ugarit.

## Iconography
- Often nude (especially in Cypriot-Phoenician statuary)
- Dove
- Lion (sometimes lion-mount)
- Star (representing Venus)
- Horned crown in some Bronze Age imagery
- Lotus (Egyptian-influenced contexts)

## Disputes
- **Astarte–Anat relation.** Whether they are originally one goddess split (Olyan 1988) or two distinct goddesses with later convergence (Day 2000, Smith 2002). Mainstream now: separate origin, increasing convergence.
- **Astarte → Aphrodite transmission.** The Cypriot-Phoenician hypothesis (Burkert 1992) is dominant; Budin (2003) argues for a more complex picture with significant independent Greek development.
