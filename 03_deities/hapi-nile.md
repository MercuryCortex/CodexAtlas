# Hapi

Hapi (Hapy) is the Egyptian god of the **annual Nile inundation** — not the river as such, but the *flood itself*, the surge of fertile water and black silt on which all Egyptian life depended. He is depicted with striking androgyny: a **blue or green-skinned man with pendulous breasts and a heavy paunch**, the very image of well-fed abundance, often shown in pairs tying together the papyrus and lotus of Upper and Lower Egypt (Hapi as the unifier of the **Two Lands**). The great **"Hymn to Hapi"** exalts him above the gods themselves — "if he is sluggish, the noses of the people are choked, and everyone is impoverished… the gods that are in heaven hunger" — for no temple offering could be made without his flood. (He is to be distinguished from **Hapy son of Horus**, the baboon-headed canopic guardian, a different deity.) Hapi embodies the Egyptian recognition that the entire civilization rested on one yearly natural event, divinized as a generous, ambiguous, indispensable power.
