# Yima

## Identity
Yima (Avestan *Yima Xšaēta*, "Yima the Radiant"; Pahlavi-Persian *Jamshid* — abbreviated from the Avestan name) is the **primordial king of the Zoroastrian-Iranian golden age** — the first ruler of humanity, the culture-hero who established kingship and civilization, the **builder of the *Var*** (the underground refuge constructed at Ahura Mazda's command to preserve human-and-animal life through the cosmic-winter catastrophe that ends the first cosmic epoch). He **originally possessed the *xvarənah*** (the divine royal-glory that legitimates kingship) but **lost it through pride** when he proclaimed himself rather than Ahura Mazda the source of cosmic order. He is **directly cognate with Vedic Yama** (Avestan *Yima* and Sanskrit *Yama* are the same Indo-Iranian name) and **structurally parallel to Vedic Manu** — both are first-king-and-first-mortal figures who survive cosmic catastrophe and from whom humanity descends. For the Codex Atlas mission Yima is load-bearing as **the Persianate world's central golden-age-and-cosmic-catastrophe mythological figure** — preserved continuously from Avestan tradition through Ferdowsi's **Shahnameh** (the great Persian national epic, ~+1010) into modern Persian literary-cultural identity.

## Earliest attestation
- **Gathas** ([[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]]): Yasna 32.8 — Yima mentioned (negatively, in connection with his pride/fall). The Gathic stratum already presupposes the broader Yima-narrative.
- **Younger Avesta**: extensively. **Vendidad Fargard 2** is the principal source — the dialogue between Ahura Mazda and Yima about the Var-construction during the cosmic-winter catastrophe. **Yasht 9** (Drvāspa Yasht) and **Yasht 19** (Zamyad Yasht) also treat Yima.
- **Pahlavi tradition**: the Bundahishn, the Mēnōg ī Khrad, the Dēnkard preserve and elaborate the Yima narrative.
- **Persian-Islamic period**: **Ferdowsi's *Shahnameh* (~+1010)** preserves the Jamshid-narrative as the central pre-Islamic Persian-epic golden-age-and-fall figure. Jamshid remains a central reference in Persian literary culture through Hafez, Saadi, modern Persian poetry.

## Mythological role

### The golden age
Yima rules for **1,000 years over a golden age** in which:
- No death, no disease, no old age.
- No cold, no heat, no hostility between animal and human.
- The earth becomes overcrowded with prosperous life — Yima three times expands the earth by one-third in response.

### The Var (underground refuge)
**Vendidad Fargard 2** preserves the central narrative: **Ahura Mazda warns Yima that a terrible cosmic-winter** (the *Mahrkušā winter*, "destroying winter") **will destroy the surface world**, killing all surface life. **Yima is instructed to build a Var** — a quadrangular underground refuge "as long as a horse-race, as broad as a horse-race, as high as a horse-race" — and to bring into it pairs of the best of every kind of animal, every kind of plant, every kind of human, plus stocks of food and water. The Var is closed; the cosmic winter rages above; the inhabitants survive within. When the winter passes, humanity re-emerges from the Var to repopulate the earth.

**The Yima-Var narrative is structurally parallel to but distinct from the Mesopotamian-biblical flood narrative.** The cosmic-cataclysm is **winter** (not flood), the refuge is **underground** (not on-water), the prescription is from Ahura Mazda (not the divine assembly). The cognate-pattern with Manu-and-the-boat and Noah-and-the-ark is structural-Indo-European-Near-Eastern shared cosmogonic vocabulary, not direct genealogical descent.

### The fall through pride
After the golden age and the Var-rescue, **Yima eventually falls through pride** (Yasht 19.34): he proclaims himself the source of cosmic order rather than Ahura Mazda. The *xvarənah* (divine royal-glory) departs from him in three stages (taken by Mithra, then by the warrior-hero Thraetaona, then by other figures). Yima is overthrown by the dragon-king Zahhāk and (in some versions) sawn in two.

## Cross-tradition identifications

### Yima ↔ Vedic Yama
**Direct etymological cognate** — Proto-Indo-Iranian \*Yamah, "twin." In Vedic tradition Yama becomes the **king of the dead** (the first mortal, who shows the way of death); in Avestan tradition Yima becomes the **first king and culture-hero** of the living. The divergent developments from the shared Indo-Iranian root are one of the most-striking comparative-religion cases of theological-divergence-from-shared-origin (West 2007).

### Yima ↔ Vedic Manu
**Structural parallel** — both are first-king-and-survivor-of-cosmic-catastrophe figures. Manu survives the **flood** in a boat advised by the Matsya-Vishnu; Yima survives the **cosmic-winter** in the Var advised by Ahura Mazda. Common Indo-European-Near-Eastern golden-age-and-cataclysm vocabulary.

### Yima and biblical-Adamic patterns
The Yima narrative shares **structural elements** with biblical-Adamic / Noahic patterns (golden-age before the fall; cataclysm-survival; humanity-descended-from-the-survivor) but **direct genealogical transmission is unprovable**. The patterns are shared Near Eastern cosmogonic-vocabulary. Some scholars (Boyce 1975) note possible Iranian influence on post-exilic Jewish elaboration of the Eden-and-Noah complex; conservative scholarship (Yamauchi 1990) resists. The structural parallel is uncontested.

### Persian-literary reception
**Ferdowsi's Jamshid (Shahnameh, ~+1010)** preserves the figure in extensive narrative-epic form for the Islamic-Persian world. Subsequent Persian literature continues — Hafez's "*Jām-e Jam*" (the magical all-seeing cup) becomes a major recurring Persian-poetic-metaphor for divine-revelatory consciousness. Modern Iranian cultural identity continues to engage Jamshid as foundational national figure.

## Iconography
- **Sasanian period**: Yima/Jamshid occasionally depicted as crowned-royal-figure, though Zoroastrian iconographic tradition is sparse.
- **Persian miniature painting (post-Islamic)**: extensive depictions of Jamshid in the *Shahnameh* manuscript-illustration tradition — Jamshid on his magnificent throne, building the cosmic-architecture, the Jam-e Jam cup, his eventual sawing-in-two at Zahhāk's command.
- **Modern Iranian cultural iconography**: Jamshid recurs in Persian visual and literary culture.

## Disputes
- **Yima-Yama divergence chronology.** When the Indo-Iranian \*Yamah-figure diverged into the Vedic Yama (king-of-the-dead) and Iranian Yima (first-king-of-the-living) is debated. The divergence is pre-Vedic / pre-Avestan; the precise period is unrecoverable.
- **Var-narrative dating.** Vendidad Fargard 2's Var-narrative is younger-Avestan-period composition; the underlying tradition is older but recoverable only through Vendidad.
- **Cosmic-winter vs. flood-cataclysm.** Why the Iranian tradition has cosmic-winter while Mesopotamian-biblical traditions have flood is partly geographic (Iranian cold-climate cosmogonic-imagination differs from Mesopotamian-Levantine wet-climate imagination) and partly culturally-traditional.
- **Yima's pride / fall.** The standard reading treats Yima's fall as moral (pride against Ahura Mazda). Some scholars argue the original Iranian Yima may have been a more straightforwardly-positive first-king with the fall-narrative being a Zarathustrian-reformist addition (downgrading the pre-Zoroastrian Iranian royal-religious tradition).
