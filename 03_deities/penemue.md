# Penemue

## Identity
**Penemue** is one of the named **Watchers** of 1 Enoch 69, specifically the Watcher who **taught humanity writing — using ink and papyrus**. In the Enochic theological frame, writing is one of the *forbidden arts* the Watchers illicitly transmitted to humans, alongside Azazel's weapons-and-cosmetics, Kasdeja's abortifacients, Kokabiel's astrology, Gadreel's weapons-and-Eve's-deception, and Baraqel's lightning-divination.

The text (1 Enoch 69:9–11, in the Nickelsburg-VanderKam 2012 translation): *"And the fourth was named Penemue. This one showed the sons of men what is bitter and what is sweet, and showed them all the secrets of their wisdom. He taught humans the art of writing with ink and papyrus, and through this many have gone astray from eternity to eternity and until this day. For humans were not created for this purpose — to confirm their good faith with pen and ink. For humans were created no different from the angels, so that they might remain righteous and pure, and death — which destroys everything — would not have laid its hand on them. But through this their knowledge, they are perishing, and through this power [death] is devouring them."*

## Earliest attestation
- **1 Enoch 69:8–11** — the Book of Parables (~1st c. BCE – 1st c. CE). The Watcher-teachings-list in 1 Enoch 69 is a later stratum than the original Book of the Watchers (1 Enoch 1–36) — the Parables (37–71) integrate and elaborate the earlier Watcher material.
- **Ge'ez Mashafa Henok** preserves the only complete textual witness; Penemue's role is canonically inscribed in Ethiopian Orthodox scripture.

## Mythological role
Penemue is the **Promethean-Thothian culture-bringer of writing** in the Enochic frame, but evaluated negatively: writing is, in the Enochic theological imagination, a corrupting transgression of the divinely-given boundary between angels (who possess writing legitimately, as the keepers of the *heavenly tablets* — see the closely-linked [[heavenly-tablets]] theme) and humans (who were not meant to have it). The theological move is striking: where most cultures celebrate the inventor-of-writing as a culture-hero (Thoth in Egypt, Nabu in Mesopotamia, Cadmus in Greece, the Confucian / Daoist Cangjie in China), the Enochic tradition treats writing as part of the broader Watcher-rebellion that *corrupts* humanity through forbidden knowledge.

This is theologically related to the Garden-of-Eden tree-of-knowledge narrative (Genesis 3) — where humanity acquires knowledge it was not meant to have — but the Enochic Watchers myth provides a distinct *cosmic-angelic* etiology where Genesis 3 provides an *anthropological* one. Both originate-of-evil traditions coexist in Second-Temple-Jewish thought (and in Ethiopian Orthodox canon, which preserves both as scripture).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[thoth]]** (Egyptian) — *scholarly-parallel*: structural inverse. Thoth is the divine *patron* of writing in Egyptian religion; Penemue is the *forbidden* angelic transmitter of writing in Enochic Judaism. The polarity is theologically meaningful — Egyptian religion is comfortable with divinely-given writing; Enochic Judaism is suspicious of it.
- **[[nabu]]** (Mesopotamian) — *scholarly-parallel*: Babylonian scribal god, patron of writing and divination; Penemue's role inverts this attitude.
- **Promethean fire-and-arts in Greek tradition** — *parallel-motif*: Prometheus gives humans fire and the technical arts against the will of Zeus; in Hesiod *Theogony* and *Works and Days* this is also ambivalent (Prometheus is punished; humans suffer). The Enochic Watchers tradition has structural similarities to Prometheus, though without genealogical connection.

## Iconography
Not iconographically standardized in Ethiopian Christian art. Modern Theosophical and Western-esoteric reception (post-Bruce 1773 / Charles 1912) sometimes visualizes the Watchers with quill-and-scroll iconography for Penemue; this is modern, not Ethiopian.

## Disputes
- **Etymology.** The name's meaning is uncertain; some propose Aramaic-derived "the inside one" or "one who is concealed." Not securely settled.
- **Date of the Book of Parables.** Whether 1st c. BCE or 1st c. CE — affects the relationship to NT material. Most current scholarship dates it pre-Christian (Nickelsburg-VanderKam 2012); some hold for early-Christian-era Jewish composition.
