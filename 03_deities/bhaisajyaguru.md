# Bhaisajyaguru

## Identity
Bhaisajyaguru ("Master of Remedies," "Medicine Buddha") is the Tathāgata ("Thus-Come One") of the Eastern Pure Land, *Vaiḍūryanirbhāsa* — the "Pure Lapis Lazuli" realm. He is the **only major Mahayana cosmic Buddha whose primary soteriological emphasis is physical healing and liberation from material suffering**, as opposed to the after-death rebirth focus of Amitabha. His body is the color of lapis lazuli (*vaiḍūrya*), radiating blue light. He holds a medicine bowl and a myrobalan plant (used in Ayurvedic medicine), and is attended by the bodhisattvas Sūryaprabha (sunlight) and Candraprabha (moonlight) and twelve yaksha generals who protect his devotees.

## The Twelve Vows
What distinguishes Bhaisajyaguru from other Mahayana cosmic Buddhas is the content of his vows (*praṇidhāna*) made in his bodhisattva career. The twelve vows explicitly address:
1. That his light-filled body illuminate all realms
2. That his body be lapis-lazuli radiance
3. That all beings lack nothing
4. That those who follow wrong paths be led to correct practice
5. That those who follow his teaching observe pure ethics
6. That those with sensory or physical disabilities be healed
7. That those sick, poor, and suffering find relief through his name
8. That women who wish to be reborn as men achieve this
9. That beings be freed from Māra's snares
10. That those condemned to torture or execution be freed
11. That the hungry be fed
12. That the naked be clothed

Vows 6, 7, 10, 11, and 12 are unique in Mahayana cosmology for their attention to **material deprivation, physical illness, and social injustice** as Buddhist soteriological concerns. The Bhaisajyaguru cult thus developed a specifically **this-worldly** Buddhism alongside the other-worldly emphasis of Amitabha Pure Land practice.

## Geographic spread and role
The Bhaisajyaguru cult spread across all Mahayana regions:
- **China**: introduced by Xuanzang's translation (650 CE); major cult at the Tang court; illness-healing rituals central
- **Japan** (*Yakushi Nyorai*): one of the earliest Buddhist images in Japan; Yakushi-ji temple (Nara, 718 CE) was the national healing temple; the Yakushi triad (Yakushi flanked by Nikkō and Gakkō bodhisattvas) one of the great masterworks of Nara sculpture
- **Tibet** (*Sangs-rgyas sman-bla*): central to Tibetan healing practices; the *Gyushi* (Four Medical Tantras) opens with Bhaisajyaguru's teachings; mantra recitation (*tadyathā oṃ bhaiṣajye bhaiṣajye mahābhaiṣajye rāja samudgate svāhā*) used in healing ritual to the present
- **Korea** (*Yaksa Yeorae*): major cult from the Silla period onward; temple Bulguksa (751 CE) houses a celebrated Yaksa Yeorae bronze

## Significance in the vault
Bhaisajyaguru is the **missing member** of the vault's Mahayana cosmic-Buddha cluster: Vairocana, Akshobhya, Ratnasambhava, Amoghasiddhi, and Amitabha were all added in the pantheon-eyeball batch, but Bhaisajyaguru — who functions differently from the Five Dhyani Buddhas (healing rather than cosmological) — was not included. His inclusion completes the Mahayana cosmic-Buddha landscape and opens the **healing deity cross-tradition spine**: Bhaisajyaguru ↔ [[asclepius-greek]] ↔ healing-serpent traditions ↔ Ayurvedic Dhanvantari.
