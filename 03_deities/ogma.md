# Ogma

## Identity
Ogma (Irish) / Ogmios (Gaulish) is the Celtic deity of eloquence and the credited inventor of the **Ogham alphabet** — the script used in the earliest Irish inscriptions (from the 4th century CE, though the tradition dates the script's divine origin to Ogma himself). He is simultaneously the champion-warrior of the Tuatha Dé Danann and the deity of learning and eloquence — a combination that mirrors the ancient Celtic ideal of the *filid* (poet-scholar) as equally a figure of power and wisdom.

## Lucian's description of Ogmios
The most striking ancient source is not Irish but **Greek**: Lucian of Samosata (~160 CE) describes a Gaulish image he saw, which his Gaulish interpreter explained as their "Hercules" — but depicted as an old man with chains running from his tongue to the ears of followers who come willingly. The Gaul explains: their god of eloquence is not gentle Hermes but *Hercules*, because eloquence is the most powerful force — stronger than physical strength.

This image — **golden chains of eloquent speech that lead men willingly** — is one of the most remarkable descriptions of a deity in ancient literature, and it captures something genuine about the Celtic valuation of eloquent speech as the highest form of power.

## Ogham and the divine script-inventor
Ogma's invention of Ogham connects him to a cross-tradition pattern of divine script-inventors: [[thoth]] (Egyptian hieroglyphs), [[hermes-greek]] (Greek letters in some traditions), Cangjie (Chinese characters), Brahma (Sanskrit Devanagari in Hindu tradition). The pattern — a deity of wisdom who gives humanity its writing system — appears so consistently across traditions that it suggests either universal mythological logic or transmission of the concept of "divine-gift-of-writing" across cultures.
