# Mut

## Identity
Mut (Egyptian *Mwt*, literally **"Mother"**) is the consort of [[amun]] / [[amun-ra]] and the mother of [[khonsu]] in the **Theban triad** that defines New Kingdom state religion. She rose with Amun's cult in the 18th Dynasty (~-1550) and is principally worshipped at the **Mut precinct** immediately south of the Karnak temple complex, connected to it by a processional avenue of sphinxes. Mut absorbs aspects of older mother-goddesses (Bastet, [[sekhmet]], [[wadjet]] — the lioness-vulture-cobra triad of feminine power) and functions as **the mother of all things** in Theban theology, parallel to the way Amun is the hidden father.

## Earliest attestation
- Becomes prominent only with the rise of the Theban Amun cult under the 18th Dynasty (c. -1550 onward).
- Earlier (Old Kingdom) attestations are rare and disputed; some take a Predynastic vulture-goddess as the origin.
- Massive Mut precinct construction at Karnak under Amenhotep III (c. -1390 to -1352) — the famous granite statues of lioness-headed Sekhmet-Mut that line the precinct (over 700 statues commissioned).
- Continues in Ptolemaic and Roman temple religion.

## Mythological role
Mut has comparatively little narrative mythology — like her consort Amun, she is principally a cult-figure rather than a story-figure. The principal mythic episode: Mut as the **"Eye of Ra"** dispatched to punish humanity, raging as a lioness, then pacified by [[thoth]] and restored to the form of the gentle vulture-mother. This integrates her with the *Eye-of-Ra* tradition shared with Sekhmet, Hathor, and Tefnut.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[sekhmet]]** — *ancient-identification*: Mut's wrathful lioness aspect is Sekhmet; the Amenhotep-III Sekhmet-Mut statues at Karnak make this iconographically explicit.
- **[[hera]]** — *ancient-identification*: in Hellenistic-Roman religious-ethnographic literature, the consort of Zeus-Ammon is identified with Hera.
- **[[isis]]** — *parallel-motif* + late-period *syncretic-identification*: in late-period and Greco-Roman Egypt, Isis absorbs many of Mut's functions as universal mother-goddess.

## Iconography
- Tall figure in long sheath dress with double-crown (the *pschent* — combined Upper and Lower Egypt crown) topping a **vulture headdress**
- The vulture's wings drape down around her face — the *nbtj* vulture symbolic of motherhood
- In wrathful aspect: lioness-headed (the Sekhmet form)
- Often carries the *ankh* and a papyrus / lily scepter

## Disputes
- **Pre-New-Kingdom history.** Whether Mut has Old Kingdom or Predynastic roots is contested. te Velde 1979 traces her to a generic Predynastic vulture-goddess; others treat her as a Theban Middle Kingdom novelty.
- **Relation to Sekhmet.** Whether Mut-Sekhmet identification is a New Kingdom theological synthesis or just a permanent overlap is debated (Hornung 1982).
