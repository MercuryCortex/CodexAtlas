# Dumuzi / Tammuz

The Sumerian / Akkadian dying-rising vegetation god, consort of [[inanna-sumerian]] / [[ishtar-akkadian]]. Dumuzi (Sumerian "righteous son") is the shepherd whose courtship and sacred marriage to Inanna is celebrated in some of the earliest erotic religious poetry in any language; he is also the god who is dragged to the underworld, mourned, and partially restored on a seasonal cycle that mirrors the Mesopotamian dry-wet agricultural year. The Akkadian form Tammuz spread widely: the month "Tammuz" (June/July) in the Babylonian calendar (and inherited into the Hebrew calendar) is named for the lamentation rite. The Hebrew Bible explicitly attests the Tammuz cult in Jerusalem in Ezekiel 8:14, where the prophet sees "women weeping for Tammuz" at the north gate of the Temple — proof that the cult had reached Judah by the early 6th century BCE.

## Mythological role
In **Inanna's Descent**, when Inanna returns from the underworld with demonic escorts demanding a substitute, she finds Dumuzi seated on his throne in fine clothes, not mourning her, and consigns him to take her place in the underworld. His sister Geshtinanna negotiates a half-year sharing: Dumuzi spends half the year below, half above. This is the foundational Mesopotamian [[dying-rising-god]] / vegetation-cycle myth. In the **Standard Babylonian Gilgamesh** Tablet VI, Gilgamesh rejects Ishtar's marriage proposal by listing her mistreated former lovers, beginning with "Tammuz, the husband of your youth, for whom you have decreed wailing year after year." The lamentation-of-Tammuz rite was performed by women in the Babylonian month named for him and persisted across millennia and traditions — Frazer's *Adonis Attis Osiris* (1906) controversially folded Tammuz into a pan-Mediterranean dying-rising-god complex; Mettinger (2001) has reasserted the category's validity for Dumuzi-Tammuz specifically while criticizing Frazer's over-generalizations.
