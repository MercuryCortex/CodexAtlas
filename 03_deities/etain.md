# Étaín

## Identity
Étaín is the radiant heroine of the *Tochmarc Étaíne* — a figure of **sovereignty, surpassing beauty, and serial rebirth**. Loved by the otherworld king [[midir]], she is the woman lost through transformation and recovered across a thousand years, and through her the kingship of Tara itself is contested.

## Earliest attestation
The *Tochmarc Étaíne* ("The Wooing of Étaín"), a tale of the Irish **Mythological Cycle**, preserved in the Book of the Dun Cow and the Yellow Book of Lecan.

## Mythological role — transformation and the soul reborn
Wedded to [[midir]], Étaín is the victim of his jealous first wife **Fúamnach**, who transforms her by sorcery into a pool of water, then a worm, then a **scarlet fly** of marvellous beauty. Buffeted on the winds for ages, she is at last **swallowed in a drink** by the wife of an Ulster chieftain and **reborn as a mortal Étaín** a full thousand years after her first life. Married then to the High King Eochaid Airem, she is sought once more by Midir, who reclaims her. Her serial metamorphosis is one of early Ireland's clearest images of the **soul passing through forms and ages**.

## Cross-tradition
Étaín joins the **sovereignty-goddess** who confers kingship and the **bride-between-two-worlds** type — paralleling the Greek [[persephone-greek]], the woman shared between the underworld and the living, and embodying a Celtic vision of rebirth and transmigration.

## Iconography
A woman of perfect beauty at her silver basin; the scarlet fly; the swan-pair rising on a golden chain with Midir.
