# Phanes-Protogonos

## Identity
Phanes (Greek *Φάνης*, "shining one"; also *Protogonos*, "first-born"; also *Erikepaios* in mystery-cult contexts; sometimes called *Metis*, "wisdom") is the **first-born god of Orphic mystery-cult cosmogony**. In the standard Orphic *Rhapsodic Theogony* (reconstructed by West 1983), Phanes emerges from the **cosmic egg** laid by Time (Chronos) or Night ([[nyx-primordial]]), as a **winged, hermaphroditic, multi-faced shining being** who contains the seeds of all subsequent gods. He is then **swallowed by [[zeus]]** in the Orphic narrative, allowing Zeus to absorb the primordial creative principle and become the supreme god of the visible cosmos. Phanes is the most theologically sophisticated primordial figure in any surviving Greek tradition and is structurally important for understanding how Greek **mystery-cult cosmogony** differs from Olympian-centered city-state religion.

## Earliest attestation
- **Orphic theogony** in multiple recensions: the Derveni Papyrus (~-340) commentary refers to the earlier Orphic theogony being commented on, which centered Phanes / Protogonos.
- **Aristophanes** *Birds* 693–702 (-414) — parodies an Orphic-style cosmogony in which a cosmic egg laid by Nyx hatches "golden-winged Eros" — Phanes-Eros.
- **Orphic Hymns** (compiled in the Hellenistic period, c. -200 to +200) — Hymn 6 is *To Protogonos*: "First-born, twofold, of the egg born... wandering through the world."
- Quoted extensively in the late-antique Neoplatonist commentators: **Proclus** *In Timaeum*, *Theol. Plat.*; **Damascius** *De Principiis*; **Hermias** *In Phaedrum*. These are the principal channels for reconstructing the Rhapsodic Theogony.
- **Mithraic** iconography: the lion-headed serpent-coiled time-god of Roman Mithraism (Aion / Zurvan / leontocephaline) is iconographically continuous with descriptions of Phanes (Beck 2006).
- Renaissance: Marsilio Ficino translates and discusses the Orphic Hymns; Phanes becomes a touchstone of Renaissance Hermetic and Orphic theology (Walker 1958; Hankins 1990).

## Mythological role
In the Orphic Rhapsodic Theogony:
1. **[[chaos-primordial]]** and Chronos (Time) exist; or in some recensions, Time alone in the cosmic void.
2. Chronos / Nyx produces the **cosmic egg**.
3. The egg splits; **Phanes emerges as the first-born**, winged, hermaphroditic, multi-faced, containing the seeds of all gods.
4. Phanes generates the next generation of gods — [[gaia]], [[uranus-greek]], and then the Titans.
5. After several generations, [[zeus]] becomes king and **swallows Phanes whole** (a divine cannibalism reconfiguring all creation inside Zeus's body), then begins the cosmos anew with himself as supreme origin.

This **Zeus-swallows-Phanes** motif is the Orphic theological signature: Zeus's kingship is not original (as in Hesiod) but is a **re-emergence of an older primordial via consumption and re-origination**. Zeus contains all things because he has swallowed the first-born god. This generates the Orphic *henotheist-monotheist* implication: there is really only one god — Zeus / Phanes / the All — articulated through multiple personae and cosmogonic episodes.

The hermaphroditic-androgynous character of Phanes is also theologically load-bearing: he/she is **prior to sexual differentiation**, the primordial unity from which both feminine and masculine emerge, structurally parallel to Egyptian creator-god self-generative theology (Atum self-generating Shu and Tefnut), Indian Purusha-Prakriti, and other primordial-androgynous cosmogonic patterns.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Eros** — *ancient-identification* (Orphic): Phanes is identified with the cosmic Eros — not the Aphroditean son but the **cosmic love-principle that draws all things into being**. The Phanes-Eros identification underwrites Plato *Symposium*'s speech of Aristophanes and Diotima.
- **Metis** — *ancient-identification* (Orphic): Phanes as primordial Wisdom; this identification connects the Orphic cosmogony to Hesiodic Metis-swallowed-by-Zeus (the better-known Olympian narrative is a doublet of the Orphic Phanes-swallowed-by-Zeus).
- **[[mithras-roman]] / Mithraic Aion / leontocephaline** — *iconographic-continuity*: the Roman Mithraic lion-headed time-god with serpent-coiling and zodiac-girdling is iconographically very close to descriptions of Phanes; Beck 2006 argues for genealogical continuity.
- **[[hermes-trismegistus]]** — *scholarly-parallel* + Renaissance *appropriation*: Renaissance Hermetic readings (Pico, Ficino) absorb Phanes into the Hermetic cosmic-Anthropos figure.
- **[[adam-kadmon]] (Kabbalah)** — *scholarly-parallel*: the primordial cosmic Anthropos / androgyne figure of Kabbalah and earlier Jewish mysticism is structurally close to Phanes; Scholem (1941) and Idel (1988) document the parallel without claiming direct transmission.
- **Christian logos and Christ-Anthropos** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Orphic Phanes-Protogonos as "first-born of all creation" through whom all things come into being is structurally close to Colossians 1:15–17 (Christ as *prōtotokos pasēs ktiseōs*, "first-born of all creation") and John 1:1–4. Direct genealogical transmission is unprovable; Christian appropriation of Orphic-philosophical first-born language is documented in [[clement-of-alexandria]] (*Protrepticus* and *Stromata* engage Orphism explicitly).

## Iconography
- **Winged hermaphroditic figure** — long hair, full bodily androgynous form
- **Serpent-coiled** — serpent winding around the body
- **Multi-headed composite** — ram, bull, lion, and serpent heads in the standard Rhapsodic description
- **Egg-shell halves** above and below — the cosmic egg from which Phanes emerged
- **Zodiac-girdled** in some Hellenistic-Roman representations
- The **Modena Relief** (~+150 CE, Galleria Estense, Modena): a famous marble relief of Phanes-Aion, the principal surviving classical visual representation, showing all these attributes integrated

## Disputes
- **Date of the Orphic Rhapsodic Theogony.** Reconstructed from late quotations; the consensus date for the core Rhapsodic recension is Hellenistic (c. 3rd–1st c. BCE), but the underlying material is older (5th–4th c. BCE Orphic poetry).
- **Mithraic-Orphic continuity.** Beck (2006) argues for substantial iconographic and theological continuity between Orphic Phanes and Mithraic leontocephaline; Ulansey (1989) and others stress Mithraic origin independence. The current view is some genuine continuity with substantial Mithraic theological innovation.
- **Renaissance reception accuracy.** Ficino, Pico, and the Renaissance Hermetic reading of Phanes substantially Christianize him as a logos / first-born / hermetic-Anthropos figure. Whether this is fidelity to the ancient Orphic material or Christian-Platonist projection is debated (Walker 1958 = mostly projection; Hankins 1990 more sympathetic).
