# Takeminakata

Takeminakata (Japanese *Take-minakata-no-kami*) is a Shinto warrior-god, a son of the Izumo lord **[[okuninushi]]** and the great deity of **Suwa** (the Suwa Taisha shrine-complex on Lake Suwa in Nagano, one of Japan's oldest cult-centres). His myth is the dramatic counterpoint to that of his brother **[[kotoshironushi]]** in the *kuni-yuzuri* ("transfer of the land"): where Kotoshironushi assents, Takeminakata **resists**, seizing the rock of the heavenly envoy **[[takemikazuchi]]** and challenging him to a trial of strength. They grip hands — and Takemikazuchi's arm turns first to ice and then to a sword-blade, crushing Takeminakata's grasp; defeated, Takeminakata flees across the land to **Lake Suwa**, where he finally submits and vows never to leave, becoming the god of Suwa. This **hand-grip duel is traditionally read as the mythic origin of sumo**. At Suwa his great festival, the **Onbashira**, in which huge fir pillars are felled and ridden down the mountainside every six years, preserves one of the most spectacular and dangerous of living Shinto rites. As a god of wind, water, war, and the hunt, Takeminakata was widely revered by the medieval warrior class.
