# Venkateswara

Venkateswara (Sanskrit *Veṅkaṭeśvara*, "Lord of the Venkata Hill"; popularly **Balaji** or **Srinivasa**) is the form of **[[vishnu]]** enshrined at **Tirumala–Tirupati** in Andhra Pradesh — the single most-visited and wealthiest religious shrine in the world, drawing tens of millions of pilgrims a year. In the temple's *sthala-purana*, Vishnu descends to the **Seven Hills** in the present degenerate age (*Kali yuga*) to be the accessible refuge of humankind, and there weds the local goddess **Padmavati**; as the husband of **[[lakshmi]]**/Sri he is "Srinivasa," the abode of Sri. The black standing *murti* — its eyes covered by the great white *namam* lest its gaze be too intense — receives the vows (and shorn hair, and gold) of pilgrims who come to discharge or beg his grace. Though theologically "merely" a form of Vishnu, Venkateswara is in practice one of the most powerful living deities of contemporary Hinduism, and his absence was a conspicuous gap.
