# Nike (Greek)

## Identity

Nike is the Greek personification and goddess of victory — divine confirmation that the gods favor the victor. She is daughter of the Titan Pallas and the Oceanid Styx (Hesiod, *Theogony* 383–385). She attends Zeus and is his instrument of victory in battle.

Nike's most famous cult statue is the **Winged Victory of Samothrace** (~190 BCE, now in the Louvre) — one of the greatest surviving works of Hellenistic sculpture. The **Temple of Nike Apteros** ("Wingless Victory") on the Athenian Acropolis (~427–424 BCE) gave her a wingless form in the Athenian tradition, so that victory could not fly away from Athens.

See **[[victoria-roman]]** for the full theological development, including the historically pivotal **Altar of Victory controversy** (382–394 CE) which made Victoria/Nike central to the conflict between Roman polytheism and Christianity.

Nike's winged iconography became the direct visual template for **Christian angel** imagery — one of the most archaeologically documented cases of pagan iconography absorbed into Christian visual theology.
