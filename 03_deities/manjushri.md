# Mañjuśrī

## Identity
The bodhisattva of transcendent wisdom (*prajñā*) — one of the principal Mahayana bodhisattvas alongside Avalokiteśvara (compassion) and Vajrapāṇi (power). Mañjuśrī's iconography is canonical: youthful princely figure, wielding a *flaming sword* in the right hand (cutting through ignorance) and a *book of the Prajñāpāramitā* in the left hand (or supported by a lotus over his left shoulder), riding a lion. He is the chief interlocutor of Prajñāpāramitā discourses and a major figure in the *Vimalakīrti*, *Lotus*, and *Avataṃsaka* sūtras. In Vajrayana, he is one of the principal yidam-deities; the famous mantra *oṃ a ra pa ca na dhīḥ* invokes him for the development of wisdom.

## Earliest attestation
Earliest attestations are in early Mahayana sutras (1st–3rd c. CE); Mañjuśrī appears as a major figure in the Prajñāpāramitā corpus.

## Mythological role
- Bodhisattva of wisdom; complements Avalokiteśvara (compassion); together they manifest the two wings of the Mahayana path.
- In some Tibetan traditions, identified with the *Khotanese* and *Mt. Wutai* (China) pilgrim-sites; Mt. Wutai is one of the four sacred Buddhist mountains of China precisely as Mañjuśrī's terrestrial abode.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- Wenshu / Monju / Jampelyang — the Chinese, Japanese, Tibetan names; same bodhisattva.
- Tibetan tradition includes the lineage-claim that Tsongkhapa, the founder of the Gelug school, was a direct emanation of Mañjuśrī.
