# Olódùmarè

## Identity
The supreme being of Yoruba religion. Olódùmarè is the source of *àṣẹ* (the cosmic creative force / authority), the ultimate creator and sustainer of the cosmos, and the assigner of *orí* (individual destiny). Crucially, Olódùmarè is *deus otiosus*: not approached directly in ordinary cult, but mediated through the *orishas* (lower deities). The two principal names — *Olódùmarè* ("Owner of the Source," etymology debated) and *Olorun* ("Owner of the Sky / Heavens") — emphasize ultimate sovereignty and transcendence.

## Earliest attestation
Yoruba oral-religious tradition is documented in European sources from the 17th c. onward (Dahomean and Yoruba kingdoms in contact with Portuguese, Dutch, English traders); systematic scholarly attestation begins with Samuel Ajayi Crowther's 19th-c. work and with William Bascom's mid-20th-c. ethnography. The Ifá corpus, textualized in the 20th c. (Abimbola 1968 onward), documents Olódùmarè's role across the divinatory verse-canon. No archaeological-iconographic attestation: Olódùmarè is not represented in image.

## Mythological role
Creates the cosmos and the orishas; sends Obatalá (in most traditions) to shape the earth from primordial waters and to mould human bodies; assigns destiny (*orí*) to each person before birth from a portion of the divine head; receives the dead (in some traditions) after life. The *orí* doctrine is one of the most distinctive features of Yoruba religion: each person's destiny is chosen before incarnation, in dialogue with Olódùmarè.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[bondye-vodou]]** (Haitian Vodou's *Bondye*, "Good God") — *scholarly-parallel* / *manuscript-transmission*: in Vodou, Bondye is the transcendent supreme being approached through the *lwa*; the structural identity with Olódùmarè-orisha relation is direct, mediated by Middle-Passage transmission. Ref: Brandon 1993; Desmangles 1992.
- **Cuban Lucumí *Olófin*** — *manuscript-transmission*: a Yoruba epithet of Olódùmarè preserved in Cuban liturgical vocabulary. Ref: Mason 1996.
- **Christian God** (in colonial Yoruba and diasporic syncretism) — *folk-syncretism*: Yoruba converts to Christianity translated *theos*/Deus with *Olódùmarè*; the identification is theologically intelligible from both sides and was used by Samuel Ajayi Crowther in his Yoruba Bible translation.

## Iconography
None directly. Olódùmarè is aniconic. The orishas have rich iconographic traditions; Olódùmarè does not.

## Disputes
- **Monotheism question.** Idowu (1962) argued Yoruba religion is monotheistic, with Olódùmarè the single supreme God and orishas merely "ministers." Critics (Westerlund, Pemberton) argue this is a Christian-influenced reading; Yoruba religion is structurally polytheistic, with Olódùmarè the high god of a layered system. Current consensus treats Yoruba religion as a "hierarchical pantheism" or "soft polytheism" with Olódùmarè the supreme but not exclusive divine.
