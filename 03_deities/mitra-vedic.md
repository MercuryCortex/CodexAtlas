# Mitra (Vedic)

## Identity
Mitra is the Vedic god of *the contract / covenant*. The Sanskrit common noun *mitra* means "friend, ally, contractual partner", and the deity personifies the binding social agreement. In the Rig Veda, Mitra and Varuṇa are almost always invoked as a pair — Mitra-Varuṇa — co-sovereigns of cosmic and moral order (*ṛta*). Dumézil's analysis (1948) treated them as complementary aspects of sovereignty: Mitra the bright, contractual, peaceable; Varuṇa the dark, magical, terrible. The Rig Veda has only one hymn (3.59) addressed to Mitra alone, in contrast to dozens to Varuṇa or Mitra-Varuṇa together.

In the Mitanni-Hittite treaty (~-1380 BCE) Mitra is invoked as divine witness alongside Varuṇa, Indra, and the Nāsatyas — testifying that the *Mitra-as-treaty-witness* function was already established centuries before the Rig Veda's redaction. This Vedic Mitra is to be distinguished sharply from (a) Avestan **Mithra**, the cognate Iranian yazata, whose worship the Younger Avesta restored after the Gathic reform (Yasht 10); and (b) the Roman **Mithras** of the mystery cult (Phase 3–4), which bears the name but is a substantially new Roman-era religious formation.
