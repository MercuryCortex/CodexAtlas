# Khandoba

Khandoba (*Khaṇḍobā*; also **Malhari**, **Mailara**, **Khanderao**) is one of the most important **folk deities of the Deccan** — worshipped across **Maharashtra and northern Karnataka**, with his great temple on the hill of **Jejuri** near Pune, where devotees shower one another with golden **turmeric powder (bhandara)**. He is understood as a **regional form of [[shiva|Shiva]]**: his central myth tells how Shiva descended as **Mārtāṇḍa Bhairava** — a name fusing the **sun** (Mārtāṇḍa) with the fierce Shaiva **[[bhairava|Bhairava]]** — to destroy the demon brothers **Mani and Malla**, whence his title *Mallari/Malhari*, "the enemy of Malla." He is typically depicted as a **mounted warrior** with a sword, often accompanied by a dog, riding down the demons. His worship famously crosses caste and even community lines (his two consorts, **Mhalsa** and **Banai**, are drawn from different castes, and he is patron to Brahmins, farmers, **Dhangar shepherds**, and warriors alike), making him a great integrative regional god. Some traditions further link him to the war-god **[[skanda-karthikeya|Skanda]]**. Khandoba exemplifies the living folk Shaivism of the Deccan — a high god made local, martial, and intimately tied to the land and its peoples.
