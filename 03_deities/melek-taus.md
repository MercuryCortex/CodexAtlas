# Melek Taus (Peacock Angel)

**Melek Taus** ("Angel Peacock" in Kurmanji) — the chief of the seven angels in Yazidi theology, who refused God's command to bow to Adam and was rewarded rather than punished for this act of supreme fidelity to God alone. The Peacock Angel is the primary mediator between God and humanity in Yazidi cosmology, presented in the *Kitêba Cilwe* as speaking in the first person to describe his own primordial nature.

Externally hostile Muslim authorities routinely identified Melek Taus with Iblis/Shaitan (Satan), using this identification to justify persecution. Yazidis firmly reject this equation: in their theology, Melek Taus's refusal was loyalty, not rebellion, and he wept for 7,000 years until his tears extinguished the fires of hell.

Cross-tradition: the "rehabilitated rebel angel" pattern — the divine or semi-divine figure who defies a command and is reread as faithful rather than fallen — appears across the vault in Gnostic traditions (the serpent in Eden as revealer of gnōsis), Lucifer/Morning Star mysticism, and Bodhisattva cosmology.
