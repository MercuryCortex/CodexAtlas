# Xuanwu

Xuanwu (玄武, the "Dark Warrior" or "Black Tortoise") is one of the great gods of Chinese religion — originally the astral emblem of the **north**, the **Black Tortoise** that is one of the four celestial palaces of the sky (with the Azure Dragon of the east, Vermilion Bird of the south, and White Tiger of the west), associated with **water, winter, and the northern quarter**. Over the Han and into the Tang and Song he developed from a constellation into a powerful **Daoist martial deity and exorcist**, depicted as a fierce barefoot warrior with loose black hair, wielding a demon-quelling sword, a **tortoise and snake** intertwined beneath his feet (his subdued animal-aspect). From the Song onward, after a naming taboo on the character *xuan*, he was worshipped as **Zhenwu** (真武, the "Perfected Warrior") — the same deity under a new devotional name. The **Ming** dynasty adopted Zhenwu as a dynastic protector and lavished imperial patronage on the mountain temple-complex of **Wudang**, which became (and remains) one of China's foremost pilgrimage centers and a cradle of Daoist martial arts. He is invoked across the Chinese world as a guardian against evil and a commander of exorcistic spirit-armies.
