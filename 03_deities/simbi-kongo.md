# Simbi

## Identity
A class of water-mediator spirits in Kongo religion (BaKongo of Lower Zaire, Angola, Cabinda), inhabiting fresh-water springs, wells, river-pools, and the rain itself. The Simbi (singular and plural; collective *bisimbi*) are not single named figures but a CATEGORY of *nkisi* (spiritual entities), each associated with a specific water-feature and a specific community. They serve as mediators between the living and the dead — water being the cosmological boundary between the two in Kongo theology (Thompson 1981). MacGaffey (1986) is the standard ethnographic reference.

## Earliest attestation
Pre-colonial Kongo oral and ritual tradition; documented in colonial-era reports from the 16th c. onward; first systematic ethnography MacGaffey (1986).

## Mythological role
In BaKongo cosmology, the cosmic-line *Kalunga* separates the living world (above) from the world of the dead (below); water surfaces — pools, rivers, the sea — are visible instances of this Kalunga boundary. The Simbi inhabit those boundary-surfaces and mediate communication and exchange between worlds. They are accessed via specific water-features: a particular spring becomes the residence of a specific Simbi, who is propitiated by the community that draws from that spring. The Simbi are simultaneously protectors of the water-source AND channels for ancestral communication.

## MASSIVE WIN — Atlantic transmission to Vodou Petwo and Gullah lowcountry
The Simbi cult is one of the documented contributors to the religious substrate of the Atlantic slave-trade diaspora alongside the Yoruba/Fon Òrìṣà-Vodun complex.

- **Haitian Vodou Petwo branch** — The "hot" Petwo branch of Vodou (vs the "cool" Rada branch) is largely Kongo-derived; the Simbi spirits enter as Simbi d'l'eau, Simbi Andezo, Simbi Makaya — water-spirits whose ritual protocols preserve Kongo elements (Thompson 1983 *Flash of the Spirit*).
- **Gullah lowcountry US** — The Gullah-Geechee communities of South Carolina and Georgia preserve Kongo religious elements that include Simbi-class water-spirits; the "bottle tree" tradition of catching evil spirits in glass bottles is one observed continuity (Thompson 1983, pp. 142–145).
- **Brazilian / Cuban Kongo-derived religions** — Palo Mayombe (Cuba) and Quimbanda (Brazil) preserve Kongo *nkisi* terminology and the water-spirit category.

## Iconography
Kaolin clay (*mpemba*) — the white clay associated with the dead — is rubbed on the body for Simbi contact. Water-pots placed at the spring; stones from river-beds become *minkisi* (charm-vessels) for specific Simbi. Blue is the most-associated color. The Kongo cosmogram (the four-moment cross marking the cosmic-line and cardinal points — Thompson & Cornet 1981) often appears alongside Simbi-related ritual paraphernalia.
