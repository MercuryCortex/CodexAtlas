# El (Hebrew)

## Identity
The high-god of the patriarchal-period Israelite religion, the figure under whose epithets — El Shaddai, El Elyon, El Olam, El Bethel, El Roi — the patriarchs of Genesis worship. Cross 1973 and Smith 2002 made the case that the early-Israelite "El" is fundamentally continuous with [[el-canaanite]] (Canaanite Ugaritic El) — the same high-god, worshiped by Hebrew populations within the Canaanite cultural milieu, before the Yahwistic revolution merged or identified the two. The Hebrew Bible's preservation of the divine name "Elohim" (plural form, singular reference), the epithets, and the patriarchal-period theology all reflect this earlier stratum.

## Earliest attestation
- The Genesis patriarchal narratives (J and E strata) — but as preserved within a redacted text dating to the early 1st millennium BCE.
- Iconographic continuity with West Semitic high-god imagery (the bull, the seated old man).

## Mythological role
In the early Hebrew tradition, El is the creator (Gen 14:19, "creator of heaven and earth"), the covenant-giver to Abraham (Gen 17, "I am El Shaddai"), the head of the divine council (residually preserved in Deut 32:8–9 in the LXX/4QDeut^j reading: "El Elyon assigned the nations their inheritance... allotting to YHWH his portion"). The redactional history of the Bible largely identifies El with YHWH (Ex 6:2–3: "I appeared to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob as El Shaddai, but by my name YHWH I was not known to them").

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[el-canaanite]]** — *scholarly-parallel* and substantial cultural-continuity claim per Cross 1973, Smith 2002.
- **[[yahweh]]** — *ancient-identification* (Hebrew-Bible's own theological move): YHWH absorbs / inherits / is identified with El.

## Disputes
- The extent of cultural continuity vs. religious differentiation between El and YHWH.
- The Deut 32:8 textual variant: "sons of Elohim/Israel" (MT) vs. "sons of El/God" (LXX, 4QDeut^j) — major theological consequences for the divine-council reading.
