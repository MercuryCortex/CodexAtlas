# Verethraghna

Zoroastrian *yazata* of victory and the smashing-of-resistance. One of the most popular divinities of pre-Islamic Iran — four Sasanian kings (Bahrām I, II, V, VI) named themselves after him, and Yasht 14 (the Bahram Yasht) is one of the most elaborate hymns in the Avestan corpus. He appears in ten forms (wind, bull, horse, camel, boar, youth, raven, ram, goat, armed warrior), with the boar as his most distinctive aspect.

**Cognate with Vedic Indra.** The name *Vərəθraγna* descends from Indo-Iranian *Vr̥tra-ghna, "dragon-slayer" — the standard Vedic epithet for Indra (Rigveda repeatedly calls Indra *vr̥tra-han*, "Vritra-slayer"). When Indra was demoted to a *daēva* in the Zarathustrian reform (see [[asura-deva-inversion]]), the dragon-slaying function did not disappear; it crystallized around the Indra-epithet itself, which became the name of an independent yazata. This is the textbook case of how a divine attribute can be reified into a separate deity when the original god is theologically rejected.
