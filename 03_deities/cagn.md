# ǀKaggen (Cagn)

## Identity
ǀKaggen (Anglicized as "Cagn" in 19th-century sources) is the supreme being and trickster-creator of the ǀXam San (Bushmen/Khoisan people of the Kalahari and Cape regions of Southern Africa). He is simultaneously the source of the world's fundamental order and its most disruptive element — a shapeshifter who takes the form of the praying mantis (his primary avatar), the eland, a louse, and various other animals. ǀKaggen creates carelessly and brilliantly: he created the moon from a feather (or from his son's shoe), the eland from shoe-leather belonging to his son, and he can reassemble himself after being torn apart. The primary source is W.H.I. Bleek and Lucy Lloyd's *Specimens of Bushman Folklore* (1911), which preserves narratives collected directly from ǀXam informants in the 1870s — one of the most rigorously documented primary archives of indigenous Southern African religion, held at the University of Cape Town (the "Bleek and Lloyd Archive").

The ǀXam San are largely extinct as a living culture (massacred and assimilated through the 18th–19th centuries); ǀKaggen survives through the Bleek-Lloyd archive, rock art (which David Lewis-Williams's neuropsychological model links directly to San shamanic trance practice), and in the traditions of related Khoisan groups (ǀGui, !Kung/Ju|'hoansi) who preserve cognate figures.

## Earliest attestation
San rock art in Southern Africa dates to at least 27,000–28,000 BP (Apollo 11 cave, Namibia), and David Lewis-Williams's work (*The Mind in the Cave*, 2002) argues convincingly that much of this art depicts shamanic trance experience in which figures like ǀKaggen are encountered. This would place ǀKaggen-type figures — or at least the religious complex in which such a figure operates — among the oldest documented religious traditions on Earth. The textual/verbal attestation begins with the Bleek-Lloyd recordings of the 1870s.

## Mythological role
Key ǀKaggen narratives from the Bleek-Lloyd archive:
1. **Creation of the eland**: ǀKaggen fashions the eland (the most sacred animal in San religion) from the shoe-leather of his son-in-law ǀKwammang-a. He tends it secretly, singing to it and caring for it; when it is killed and its blood spilled, ǀKaggen weeps and from the blood creates all the wild game animals of the world. The eland is ǀKaggen's "favourite creation."
2. **Creation of the moon**: ǀKaggen throws his shoe into the sky, commanding it to become the moon. In other versions it is a feather. The moon holds the dead.
3. **Shapeshifting**: ǀKaggen transforms at will — into the praying mantis (most common), into the eland, into a louse, and other forms. His transformations are often motivated by trickery, self-preservation, or creative play.
4. **Death and reassembly**: ǀKaggen can be killed (eaten by his adversaries) and then reassembled from a fragment — typically the gall-bladder, which reconstitutes the whole. He is thus associated with death-and-resurrection in a shamanic sense.
5. **The dance and trance**: ǀKaggen is associated with the healing dance (*ǀxam*), in which shamans enter trance and travel to the spirit world. Lewis-Williams's model identifies many rock art figures as ǀKaggen-encounters in trance.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[anansi]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Anansi (Akan spider-trickster) and ǀKaggen are the two most prominent African trickster-creators — both are small, clever, invertebrate-formed beings (spider / mantis) who create through cunning and narrative rather than brute power. The parallel is noted in comparative African religion literature (Pelton 1980).
- **[[eshu]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Eshu (Yoruba) as the ambiguous, crossroads-inhabiting trickster who enables all transactions and is neither good nor evil maps onto ǀKaggen's structural ambivalence: necessary for creation, source of disruption, neither purely beneficent nor malevolent.
- **[[loki]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Both Loki and ǀKaggen are shapeshifters whose creative-destructive energy cannot be contained within the social order; both are responsible for both gifts and catastrophes; both take animal forms.
- **[[coyote-navajo]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Coyote as the North American shapeshifting trickster-creator shares with ǀKaggen the trickster-as-world-maker typology — both are associated with error as constitutive of reality (Lewis Hyde's "Trickster Makes This World" thesis applies to both).
- **[[nuwa]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Nüwa creates humans from clay; ǀKaggen creates the eland from shoe-leather. Both instantiate the pattern of creation-from-transformed-humble-materials — the cosmic sacred made from mundane stuff.
- **[[thoth]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Thoth in animal form (ibis, baboon) as the creator-god of language/wisdom and ǀKaggen in mantis form as the creator-god of the Bushman world share the pattern of animal-embodied supreme intelligence.
- **[[rainbow-serpent]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Both are shapeshifting creator-beings of hunter-gatherer traditions in the Southern hemisphere, associated with rain, water, transformation, and the dangerous boundary between ordinary and sacred space.
- **[[nyame]]** — connecting tradition-context: Nyame (Akan) and ǀKaggen are both supreme being figures of Sub-Saharan Africa, attesting the diversity of African high-god concepts across ecological zones.
- **[[nzambi]]** — connecting tradition-context: The Kongo supreme being Nzambi (creator of all) and ǀKaggen represent two poles of African supreme-being theology — Nzambi the remote transcendent creator vs. ǀKaggen the immanent, playful, embodied trickster-creator.

## Iconography
ǀKaggen's primary form is the praying mantis (*Mantis religiosa* — a choice noted by scholars, given the mantis's habit of remaining absolutely still, then striking). In San rock art, ǀKaggen appears associated with the eland (the most frequently depicted animal in Southern African rock art) and with therianthropic (half-human, half-animal) figures — Lewis-Williams identifies these as shamans in trance who have become ǀKaggen. The moon is ǀKaggen's celestial attribute. Eland fat rubbed on the body is a sacred substance in San ritual, linked to ǀKaggen's eland-creation.

## Disputes
- **Universal vs. ǀXam-specific**: Some scholars (Katz, Marshall) note that the Ju|'hoansi (!Kung) of the northern Kalahari have a cognate figure (//Gao!na or "Big God") who is less tricksterish and more straightforwardly supreme. The trickster elaboration may be more pronounced in ǀXam tradition. Khoisan religious traditions are diverse; ǀKaggen is the ǀXam form.
- **Rock art interpretation**: Lewis-Williams's neuropsychological model (altered-state entoptic imagery) for San rock art is widely accepted but some scholars (Helvenston & Bahn) dispute the universality of the trance-state interpretation.
