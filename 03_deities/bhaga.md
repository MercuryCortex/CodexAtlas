# Bhaga

## Identity
Bhaga is a Vedic **Āditya** — son of [[aditi]] — the god of **fortune and the allotted share**, the divine dispenser who apportions wealth and good luck. His very name, *bhaga*, means "share, portion, fortune."

## Earliest attestation
Invoked in the *Rigveda* among the Ādityas, especially at dawn (the time of distribution) and in blessings for prosperity and a fortunate marriage.

## Mythological role — the apportioner of fortune
Bhaga is the god who **distributes** — who allots to each their portion of wealth, luck, and the goods of life; to be *bhagavant* is to be "possessed of fortune," and the great later title *Bhagavān* ("the Fortunate/Blessed Lord," used of Viṣṇu, Kṛṣṇa, and the Buddha) derives from his name. A blind god in some accounts (fortune being blind), he is invoked at dawn and in the marriage-rite alongside his fellow-Āditya [[aryaman]].

## Cross-tradition
Bhaga is one of the clearest **Indo-European** lexical-religious survivals: Vedic *Bhaga* is cognate with the Iranian *baga* ("lord, god") and the Slavic *bogъ* ("god") — the latter visible in [[dazhbog]] ("give-god"). The "apportioner of fortune" became, in Iranian and Slavic, the ordinary word for *god* itself.

## Iconography
Rarely figured; present in the dawn-distribution of wealth and in blessings of fortune.
