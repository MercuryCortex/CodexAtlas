# An / Anu

An (Sumerian *An*, "Sky"; Akkadian *Anu*) is the **sky-god and original supreme deity** of the Mesopotamian pantheon — the primeval Heaven itself, personified, and the ultimate source of all authority among gods and kings. So fundamental is he that his name, written with the star-sign 𒀭, doubles as the **determinative *dingir*** placed before the name of every deity: to be a god is, in the script, to be marked with An. He heads the divine council of the **Anunnaki** ("the offspring of An"), and supreme legitimate power is literally called ***anûtu***, "An-ship" — the office that can be conferred on another god, as it is in the *Enūma Eliš* when the assembled gods grant Marduk "the Anu-power."

## Mythological role
An stands at the top of the great Mesopotamian triad — **An (heaven), [[enlil]] (air/earth), [[enki-ea]] (the sweet waters)** — as the remote, sovereign father. With the earth **[[ki-sumerian|Ki]]** he engenders Enlil, whose forcible separation of heaven from earth makes the cosmos habitable. Yet An is a *roi fainéant*, a sovereign who reigns more than he rules: already in the third millennium his active cult and executive power pass to **Enlil** at Nippur, and later to **Marduk** at Babylon, while An retains nominal seniority as "king of the gods" and the final guarantor of decrees. His chief cult-centre is **Uruk**, where the **Eanna** ("House of Heaven") temple was shared with **[[inanna-sumerian|Inanna]]** — in one myth Inanna carries off the *me* (the divine offices of civilization) from An's domain to her city. His emblem is the **horned crown**, the very sign of divinity.

## Cross-tradition note
As the deified sky who fathers the gods and is eclipsed by a more active storm/air son, An belongs to the wide Indo-European-and-Near-Eastern pattern of the **otiose sky-father** (compare the Greek Ouranos displaced by Kronos and Zeus, and the Vedic Dyaus eclipsed by Indra) — though here the relationship is one of peaceful succession of authority rather than violent overthrow.
