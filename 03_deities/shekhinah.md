# Shekhinah (Divine Presence)

## Identity
*Shekhinah* — from the Hebrew root *š-k-n* (to dwell, encamp) — is the divine indwelling. In early rabbinic Judaism (Mishnah, Talmud, midrash) the term names God's *presence* with Israel, especially in the Temple, in the synagogue, and accompanying Israel in exile; it is a circumlocutory reference to God acting in immanent mode, not a separately personified entity. The momentous Kabbalistic development — first hinted at in [[phase-5-013-hekhalot-literature]], explicit in [[phase-5-026-sefer-ha-bahir]], systematized in [[phase-5-027-sefer-ha-zohar]] — *hypostatizes* the Shekhinah: she becomes a personified feminine divine entity, the tenth sefirah Malkhut, the bride / daughter of the upper sefirot, in tragic separation (during exile) from her divine masculine partner Tiferet. The inner-divine sacred marriage of Tiferet and Shekhinah — the *zivvug* (union) — is the metaphysical drama at the heart of the Zohar; the human's performance of *mitzvot*, especially the marital and sexual practices ritualized in Kabbalistic *Shabbat* observance, effects the *yichud* (unification) that restores Shekhinah to Tiferet. This is the most powerful divine-feminine theology in any major Western monotheism.

## Earliest attestation
The term appears in tannaitic literature (~200 CE) — in the Mishnah, the Targums (Onkelos, Pseudo-Jonathan), and the Talmuds — as a circumlocution for divine presence. The hypostatic-feminine reading begins in 12th-c. Provençal Kabbalah (Bahir) and culminates in the Zohar.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[sophia-gnostic]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Scholem 1941 famously argued the Kabbalistic Shekhinah's hypostatic-feminine role parallels Sophia in Sethian Gnostic theology. The parallel is structural (lower feminine in tragic separation, restored through cosmic drama) and is the single strongest comparative data point for the Scholem-Idel debate over Kabbalah's Gnostic-influenced origins.
- **[[wisdom-personified]]** (Hokhmah / Sophia of Wisdom of Solomon) — *direct-influence*: pre-rabbinic personified Wisdom (Proverbs 8; Sirach 24; Wisdom of Solomon 7–10) provides the literary precedent for personifying a divine feminine principle.

## Disputes
- **Hypostatic vs. linguistic.** Whether the Shekhinah of medieval Kabbalah is genuinely a divine person within the Godhead or a sophisticated linguistic-theological category is debated; the operative texts (Zohar) read clearly as the former.
- **Gnostic origin.** Scholem (yes); Idel (substantially no); Wolfson (complex, with attention to gender and to Christian-influenced symbolism).
- **Contemporary feminist readings.** The Shekhinah has been recovered in contemporary feminist Jewish thought (Plaskow, Adler, Frymer-Kensky) as a resource for non-androcentric Jewish theology.
