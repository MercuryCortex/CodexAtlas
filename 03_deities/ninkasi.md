# Ninkasi

## Identity
Ninkasi is the Sumerian goddess of beer brewing and the patron of the daily ration of bread and beer that constituted the staple of Sumerian sustenance. Her name is sometimes glossed "Lady who fills the mouth," referring to the beer-spout. In some genealogical traditions she is one of the seven (or eight) deities born from Enki's pain after his episode of consuming forbidden plants in *Enki and Ninhursag* — there Ninti delivers her with the etiology "let her be the one who fills the mouth." She is the protagonist of the **Hymn to Ninkasi**, an Edubba'a (scribal school) composition of approximately ~-1800 BCE that doubles as both a praise-hymn and a working brewing recipe: the verses describe in technical sequence the steps of beer production, and the hymn appears to have functioned as a mnemonic guide for brewers as well as a liturgical text.

## Earliest attestation
- Ninkasi appears in the Fara/Shuruppak god lists (~-2600) as a recognized deity.
- The *Hymn to Ninkasi* (Civil 1964) is the principal source — surviving in OB scribal-school copies, presumed Ur III or Isin-Larsa in original composition.
- Ur III administrative texts document beer rations under Ninkasi's nominal patronage; the brewing trade is institutionally connected to her cult.
- The *Drinking Song* (ETCSL 5.7.3) names her in tavern-context.

## Mythological role
- ***Hymn to Ninkasi***: the central source. The hymn's nine stanzas walk through her birth and her role as divine brewer; embedded in the praise is the technical sequence of brewing — soaking the bappir (twice-baked bread), milling the malt, mashing, fermentation in the vat, filtering the wort. The recipe-as-hymn structure is unique in the Sumerian corpus and is the basis for modern reconstructions of Sumerian beer.
- ***Enki and Ninhursag***: Ninkasi is born from Enki's mouth-affliction after he consumes the forbidden plants of Dilmun; Ninhursag heals each affliction by birthing a deity, and Ninkasi emerges from the mouth.
- She is invoked in tavern-songs, harvest-festival songs, and in workers'-ration administrative formulae.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[siris-akkadian]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Akkadian Siris (also Siraš) is a beer-goddess functionally identical to Ninkasi. Some Old Babylonian god lists treat them as two distinct beings, others as identical; this may reflect a Sumerian/Akkadian linguistic doublet.
- *Scholarly parallel*: comparison with Egyptian Hathor (as brewing/intoxication goddess in the Destruction of Mankind myth) is occasionally made; this is *scholarly-parallel*, not an ancient identification.

## Iconography
Limited dedicated iconography. The beer-vat (in cuneiform a recognizable pictograph) is her sign; she appears in seal scenes accompanying drinking and feasting iconography.

## Disputes
- **Ninkasi as historical brewmaster vs. divine personification**. Some popular treatments (and a strain of older scholarship) treat the Hymn as substantively a working brewer's manual with a thin theological wrapper; current Assyriology treats it as a genuine cultic hymn whose technical content reflects the Mesopotamian theological commitment that craft is divine knowledge.
- **Ninkasi and Siris**. Whether one goddess under two language-forms or a pair is unresolved.
