# Nabia

## Identity
Nabia (*Navia* in some inscriptions) is the principal water and river goddess of the Gallaecian-Lusitanian tradition — the northwestern Iberian counterpart of the Irish [[boann]] and the Vedic [[saraswati]]. Attested in ~30 Latin votive inscriptions across northern Portugal (Minho, Trás-os-Montes) and Galicia, she is associated with rivers, springs, and sacred water sources. The **River Navia** (Galicia) and possibly the **River Neiva** (Minho, Portugal) preserve her name, in the same way the River Boyne preserves Boann's.

## Inscriptions and epithets
The inscriptions reveal several aspects of her cult:
- **Nabia Corona** ("Nabia the crowned") — attested at least once; suggests a sovereignty dimension alongside the water function
- **Nabia with Vittoria** — compound dedications pairing her with the Roman goddess of victory; the nature of this pairing is unclear (Roman interpretatio assimilation? A specific compound deity-type?)
- Geographic epithets (*Nabia Elaesurraega*, etc.) — local forms of the goddess tied to specific valleys or water sources; the same epithet-formation pattern seen in Bandua and other Gallaecian deities, where the deity-type is locally instantiated across many specific places

## The PIE river-goddess spine
Nabia sits at the western terminus of the reconstructable PIE sacred-river-goddess tradition:

| Tradition | Figure | River | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vedic/Hindu | **[[saraswati]]** | Sarasvatī (disappeared in Thar Desert) | Sacred speech, knowledge, arts |
| Irish Celtic | **[[boann]]** | Boyne (Bóinn) | Poetic inspiration, sacred knowledge |
| Gallaecian/Lusitanian | **Nabia** | Navia (Galicia), Neiva (Minho) | Water, fertility, possibly sovereignty |
| Lithuanian | **[[laima]]** (fate) + river-associated Saulė | Various | Fate, destiny, sacred waters |

Three traditions — Indo-Iranian, Goedelic Celtic, and Iberian Celtic/para-Celtic — independently preserve a feminine sacred-river deity associated with divine power and fertility. Olmsted (1994) reconstructs this as a PIE goddess-type, which would make Nabia evidence for the oldest identifiable stratum of Portuguese religious history.

## What her cult reveals
Unlike [[endovelicus]], whose cult was centralized at a single major sanctuary (São Miguel da Mota), Nabia appears in dispersed, locally-specific forms across the rainy northwestern quadrant of Iberia — the most river-rich corner of the peninsula. This distribution pattern suggests she was not a single centralized deity but a river-goddess *type* instantiated across dozens of local watersheds, each with its own epitheted form. This is structurally identical to how Celtic river-goddess cults functioned across Gaul and Britain (the Sequana of the Seine source, the Verbeia of the Wharfe, the Sabrina of the Severn). Nabia in the northwest Iberian river system is the same phenomenon at the Atlantic terminus.
