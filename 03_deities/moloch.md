# Moloch

Moloch (Hebrew *Mōlek*, from West-Semitic *mlk*) names the cult of child sacrifice that the Hebrew Bible repeatedly and emphatically prohibits — "you shall not give any of your offspring to pass through the fire to Molech" (Lev 18:21; cf. 20:2–5). Whether *mlk* originally denoted a distinct **god** (a chthonic deity Molek) or a **type of sacrifice** (the *mlk*-offering, later vocalized with the vowels of *bōšet*, "shame," to yield *Mōlek*) is one of the classic disputes of West-Semitic religion: Eissfeldt (1935) argued from the Punic *mlk* inscriptions that it was a sacrificial term; Heider (1985) and Day (1989) re-argue for an underworld deity. The biblical authors treat it as a god whose worship is the archetypal abomination.

## Appearance in the Hebrew Bible and the Tophet
The cult is localized at the **Tophet** in the Valley of (Ben-)Hinnom (Hebrew *Gê-Hinnōm* → Greek *Gehenna*) south of Jerusalem, where children were "passed through fire" (2 Kgs 23:10, defiled by Josiah; Jer 7:31; 32:35). As **Milcom**, "the abomination of the Ammonites," the same or a closely allied god is the national deity for whom Solomon builds a high place (1 Kgs 11:5,7,33). The strongest material parallel is the Carthaginian **Tophet** sanctuaries, where urns of cremated infants bear *mlk* dedications to [[tanit]] and Baal Hammon — making the biblical polemic part of a wider West-Semitic phenomenon. Moloch's home is West-Semitic/Ammonite; his presence in Israelite scripture is a tier-2 appearance-wire of condemnation, not a membership.
