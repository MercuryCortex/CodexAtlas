# Enkimdu

## Identity

Enkimdu is the Sumerian god of farming and of irrigation works — "the lord of dike and ditch," the divine power of the cultivated, watered field. In the canal-dependent agriculture of southern Mesopotamia, where the dikes and ditches that distributed the river-water were the difference between harvest and famine, Enkimdu personifies the whole apparatus of cultivation.

## Mythological role

Enkimdu's famous appearance is in the courtship poem often titled *Inanna Prefers the Farmer*—or, more accurately, *Inanna Prefers the Shepherd* (the **Dumuzi–Enkimdu disputation**). The goddess [[inanna-sumerian]] is to be married, and two suitors press their claims: [[dumuzi-tammuz]], the shepherd-god, and Enkimdu, the farmer-god. Each extols his gifts — the shepherd his wool, milk, and cream; the farmer his grain, beans, and the produce of the irrigated field. Inanna (urged by her brother Utu) favors the shepherd Dumuzi. The poem then turns to reconciliation: rather than fight, Enkimdu graciously yields, offering Dumuzi the run of his fields and his friendship. The debate stages the same herder-versus-farmer tension as the [[lahar]]–[[ashnan]] disputation, here resolved peacefully.

## Cross-tradition significance

The Dumuzi–Enkimdu story is one of the oldest written versions of the **farmer-versus-herdsman** motif — the rivalry between the two foundational ways of life of the ancient Near East. Its peaceful resolution stands in pointed contrast to the violent outcome of the same opposition in the Hebrew Bible, where the herdsman Abel and the farmer Cain present competing offerings and the rivalry ends in fratricide. Enkimdu is therefore a key Mesopotamian comparandum for one of the most studied background-motifs of Genesis.
