# Varuṇa

## Identity
Varuṇa is the great Vedic sovereign of cosmic and moral order (*ṛta*). In the earliest Rig Vedic stratum (especially the family books, Maṇḍalas 2–7) Varuṇa is at least co-sovereign with Mitra and arguably the deeper authority — he sees all, he holds the cosmic order in place, he binds the wicked with his noose (*pāśa*), and his moral surveillance is intimate and inescapable. The Vedic adjective for him is "Asura" (in the early positive sense — "Ahura" cognate), grouping him with the high lords of Indo-Iranian inheritance.

In the Mitanni-Hittite treaty (~-1380 BCE) "Uruwana" (Varuṇa) is invoked alongside Mitra, Indra, and the Nāsatyas as Indo-Iranian divine witnesses — possibly the earliest cuneiform attestation of a Vedic deity. Through the Brāhmaṇa period Varuṇa is gradually demoted from cosmic sovereign to god of the waters / oceans, retaining his judicial cast but yielding cosmic kingship to other gods. The scholarly comparison with Avestan Ahura Mazda is one of the deepest Indo-Iranian connections: both are sovereign Asuras / Ahuras associated with cosmic order, oath-keeping, and moral surveillance.
