# Ọ̀ṣọ́ọ̀sì

## Identity
Ọ̀ṣọ́ọ̀sì is the Yorùbá òrìṣà of the hunt, the forest, and the wilderness — the archetypal hunter (*ọdẹ*) who provides game, tracks the unseen, and pursues the wrongdoer. His emblem is the bow strung with a **single arrow** (*ọfà*): one arrow, one target — the icon of single-minded focus and the perfect shot. He is patron of those who must find what is hidden, and is invoked for justice, since the hunter who tracks game also tracks the fugitive and the liar.

## Earliest attestation
Documented in Yorùbá oral tradition and the Ifá corpus (*odù*); strongly anchored in the western kingdom of **Ketu** (near the Dahomey frontier), of which he is the tutelary hunter-king. Nineteenth- and twentieth-century ethnography (Frobenius, Verger, Bascom) records his cult across Yorùbáland and, after the Atlantic trade, throughout Cuba and Brazil.

## Mythological role — one of the Warriors
Ọ̀ṣọ́ọ̀sì forms, with [[eshu]] and [[ogun]], the triad of **Warriors** (*Ẹgbẹ́ Ọ̀rìṣà Ìjà*) — the orishas received first and together at initiation in diaspora practice. The division of labour is exact: [[eshu]] opens the roads and removes obstacles; [[ogun]] clears the forest path with iron; Ọ̀ṣọ́ọ̀sì hunts, tracks, and brings home provision (and, by extension, justice). His bond with [[ogun]] is especially close — both are forest-dwellers, and Ogun's iron forges Osoosi's arrowhead, so the smith and the hunter are functionally inseparable. Through Ketu his cult passed to Bahia, where Candomblé's largest "nation" bears his kingdom's name.

## Cross-tradition — the divine hunter / master of animals
Ọ̀ṣọ́ọ̀sì instantiates the widespread **"lord of the hunt / master of animals"** type — a structural parallel (resemblance, not historical contact) with the Greek [[artemis]] and Roman [[diana-roman]] (the bow-bearing huntress of the wild), the Aztec hunt-god [[mixcoatl]] ("Cloud Serpent," armed with bow and darts), and the Celtic [[cernunnos]], lord of wild animals. Across these unconnected traditions the same figure recurs: the deity who governs the boundary between the cultivated village and the dangerous, provisioning wild.

## Diaspora syncretism
Carried to the Americas by enslaved Yorùbá, Ọ̀ṣọ́ọ̀sì surfaces under saints' masks (per [[theme-orisha-saint-syncretism]]):
- **Brazil ([[tradition-candomble]])** — identified with **São Sebastião** (St Sebastian), the arrow-pierced martyr; in some houses with São Jorge. Oxóssi is the great orixá of the **Ketu** nation.
- **Cuba ([[tradition-santeria-lucumi]])** — paired with **San Norberto** (St Norbert); kept among the Warriors.

## Iconography
A bow with a single arrow (*ọfà*); an iron crossbow in the diaspora; the colours green and amber/honey; the deer's antlers; a single feather. Devotees keep his tools in the Warriors' iron cauldron beside Ogun's.
