# Aphrodite

## Identity
Aphrodite is the Greek Olympian of love, desire, beauty, and sexuality. Her name's etymology has been contested for centuries; the Hesiodic folk-etymology ("foam-born", from *aphros* "foam") explains her birth from the foam that gathered around the genitals of the castrated Uranus thrown into the sea — a striking succession-myth detail in Theogony. Homer offers a different parentage (daughter of Zeus and Dione). Her oldest and most prestigious cult center was Paphos on Cyprus, a Phoenician colony from the Early Iron Age — the geographic fact that *grounds* the now-consensus scholarly view that Aphrodite was substantially shaped by the eastern Mediterranean love-and-war goddess complex (Inanna → Ishtar → Astarte) imported via Phoenician Cyprus.

The Aphrodite / Astarte / Ishtar / Inanna chain is one of the most thoroughly documented examples of trans-cultural goddess transmission in ancient religion. Per the splitting rule of this vault, each is a separate node: distinct cultic and literary evidence, with scholarly-parallel and syncretic-identification edges between them. The Roman identification with Venus is straightforward *interpretatio graeca*; Berossus reports Achaemenid Artaxerxes II identified Iranian Anāhitā with Aphrodite. Burkert (1992) and West (1997) detail the orientalizing transmission. Aphrodite's role in the Trojan War (the Judgment of Paris, her protection of Aeneas) and in lyric poetry (Sappho's invocations) makes her one of the most pervasive Olympian presences in Greek literature.
