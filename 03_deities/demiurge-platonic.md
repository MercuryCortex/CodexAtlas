# The Demiurge (Platonic)

In Plato's *Timaeus* the *dēmiourgos* — "public craftsman" — is the divine maker who, looking to the eternal Forms (*paradeigma*), shapes the disorderly motion of the receptacle (*chōra*) into the orderly cosmos. He is *good* ("he was good, and no envy ever arises in him concerning anything," *Tim.* 29e), *rational*, and *not omnipotent* — matter resists and he must persuade *anankē* (necessity); he is *subordinate* to the Good / the One but *supreme* as cosmic maker. This is a *craftsman creator* — distinct from a creator-by-fiat (Genesis) and from a primal generator (Vedic *prajāpati*). For deeper treatment of the inversion / variants see [[demiurge]].

Middle Platonists (Numenius, Atticus, Albinus) stratify the Demiurge — "first god" / "second god" — preparing the ground for Plotinus, who identifies the Demiurge with *Nous*, the second hypostasis after the One ([[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]]). Plotinus's *Ennead* II.9 ("Against the Gnostics") explicitly defends the Platonic good Demiurge against the Sethian inversion (Yaldabaoth / [[demiurge-gnostic]]) — the most consequential late-antique theological controversy about cosmic craftsmanship. Iamblichus and Proclus further systematize the Demiurge into a hierarchical theology ([[phase-4-020-iamblichus-on-the-mysteries]], [[phase-4-021-proclus-elements-of-theology]]); Pseudo-Dionysius and Aquinas mediate the picture into Latin Christianity.

Philo of Alexandria identifies the Mosaic Creator with the Platonic Demiurge ([[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]]), with the Logos as the *paradigm* (Forms) through which he creates — a synthesis foundational for later Christian-Platonist cosmology.
