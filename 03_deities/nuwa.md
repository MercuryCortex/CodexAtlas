# Nüwa

## Identity
The creator-goddess of Chinese mythology — fashioning humanity from yellow earth, in two batches: the first carefully shaped by hand (the aristocracy); the second mass-produced by dragging a cord through clay (the commoners). Her cosmogonic counterpart Fuxi sets up culture; she sets up *life*. The **Huainanzi 6.7** records her great repair of the cosmos when the pillars of heaven collapsed: she **smelted five-colored stones** to patch the broken sky, cut off the legs of the cosmic turtle Ao to prop the four corners, killed the black dragon, and stopped the great flood.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[fuxi]]** — brother-consort.
- **[[ninhursag]]** — *parallel-motif*: clay-anthropogony mother-goddess.
- **[[prometheus]]** — *parallel-motif*: clay-anthropogony culture-hero (Hesiod *Works and Days*; Apollodorus *Bibl.* 1.7.1).
- **[[gaia]]** — *parallel-motif*: primordial earth-mother whose name is etymologically linked to *kuwā*/*kuă*.
- **Cosmic-repair myth** — distinctive in Chinese mythography compared to most pantheons; the gods elsewhere create the cosmos once, Nüwa **maintains** it.
