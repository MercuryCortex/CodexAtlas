# Ōkuninushi-no-Mikoto

## Identity
The great earthly creator-deity who built the land of Japan (the "Visible World" / *utsushiyo*) before yielding it to the heavenly lineage of [[amaterasu]]. Ōkuninushi is a descendant of [[susanoo]] in some genealogies and Susanoo's son-in-law in others, positioning him as ruler of the earthly *kunitsukami* (land-deities) in contrast to the *amatsukami* (heavenly deities). His epithet "Master of the Great Land" encodes his role as sovereign of the terrestrial realm. He is also known as **Ōmononushi** (in his divine-spirit / *mitama* aspect enshrined at Ōmiwa Shrine) and syncretized with **Daikoku**, one of the Seven Lucky Gods.

## Earliest attestation
- *Kojiki* Book 1 — extensive mythological cycle: the White Rabbit of Inaba, the 80 brothers' attempts to kill him, descent to the underworld ruled by Susanoo, wooing Suseri-Bime, nation-building with Sukuna-Biko-Na, and the climactic "transfer of the land" (*kuni-yuzuri*) to the heavenly emissary [[takemikazuchi]].
- *Nihon Shoki* (720 CE) — parallel and variant accounts.
- *Izumo Fudoki* (733 CE) — detailed local mythological traditions of his earthly rule.
- **Izumo Grand Shrine** (*Izumo Taisha*) — one of Japan's oldest and most sacred shrine complexes; October in the old calendar is "Kannazuki" (Month Without Gods) everywhere except Izumo, where all the gods gather (*kamiari-zuki*) to negotiate marriages.

## Mythological role
The **White Rabbit of Inaba** myth: Ōkuninushi heals a rabbit stripped of its skin by his 80 cruel brothers, establishing him as the deity of compassion and medicine. The **80 killings**: his brothers kill him twice, once by crushing with a boulder, once by burning inside a tree — his mother appeals to divine beings and he is restored each time. This *repeated killing and restoration* is the vault's clearest Japanese instance of the dying-and-rising mytheme.

The **transfer of the land** (*kuni-yuzuri*): after [[takemikazuchi]] is sent by Amaterasu to negotiate, Ōkuninushi voluntarily yields the visible world to the heavenly dynasty in exchange for sovereignty over the hidden world (*kakuriyo*) — the spirit realm — and veneration at Izumo Grand Shrine. This act makes him the ruler of the dead, the fates, and the invisible domain, while the Yamato emperors rule the living world. It is a cosmological partition with exact structural parallels.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[osiris]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: killed repeatedly and restored; after yielding earthly kingship becomes ruler of the dead and the hidden realm. The sovereignty-killed-restored-then-lord-of-underworld pattern is nearly identical.
- **[[baldr]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: beloved, indestructible despite repeated killing attempts; restoration as pivot of cosmic renewal.
- **[[saturn-roman]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Golden Age king who yields sovereignty to the younger Jupiter dynasty but retains care of the earth; Ōkuninushi yields visible-world rule to Amaterasu's line but retains the spirit world.
- **[[enki-ea]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: creator-god who yields hierarchical precedence to younger divine generation while remaining master of the deep/hidden domain.
- **[[marduk]]** — *parallel-motif*: nation-/cosmos-builder deity whose construction of the terrestrial order establishes civilization.
- **[[inari]]** — *folk-syncretism*: overlapping domains of agriculture, prosperity, and rice; venerated together at Izumo-affiliated shrines.
- **[[nuwa]]** — *parallel-motif*: creative deity who shapes the terrestrial world and then withdraws from active governance.

## Iconography
In his Daikoku aspect: rotund, smiling, holding a mallet of abundance and a large sack of treasures, seated on bales of rice. In Shinto contexts he is aniconic or represented symbolically; the Izumo Grand Shrine's inner sanctuary contains no public image.
