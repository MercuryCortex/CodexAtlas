# Vesta

## Identity
Roman goddess of the hearth, both domestic and civic. The civic cult was central to Roman state religion: the *Aedes Vestae* (round temple) in the Roman Forum housed the *ignis perpetuus* (eternal flame), tended by the six Vestal Virgins — high-status priestesses (selected as girls, serving 30 years, sworn to chastity on pain of death) whose ritual maintenance of the flame symbolized the continuity of the Roman state. The Greek equivalent is Hestia, but the Roman Vestal institution has no exact Greek parallel and is a Roman religious-political peculiarity. The cult was abolished by Theodosius I in 391 CE in the Christianizing legislation; the temple sealed, the Virgin order disbanded.

## Earliest attestation
Traditional: the cult is attributed to Numa Pompilius (legendary 7th c. BCE); archaeologically the round temple in the Forum dates to at least the 6th c. BCE.

## Mythological role
Mythologically thin — Vesta's mythology in literary sources is minimal; her *cultic* role is what matters. Ovid's *Fasti* (1st c. BCE) preserves substantial Vestalia (June 7–15) ritual material.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Hestia** — *ancient-identification*: the Greek and Roman hearth-goddesses are systematically equated by ancient authors, though the *Vestal* institution is distinctively Roman.
