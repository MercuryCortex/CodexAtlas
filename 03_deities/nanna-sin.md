# Nanna / Sin

The Mesopotamian moon god, called Nanna in Sumerian and Sin (or Suen) in Akkadian. His principal cult center is the Sumerian city of Ur, where the Ekishnugal temple complex with its great ziggurat dominated the sacred quarter — the same Ur that is Abraham's place of origin in Genesis 11–12. Nanna/Sin is father of [[inanna-sumerian]] (Ishtar), the planet Venus, and of [[utu-shamash]], the sun god. In the Sumerian theological imagination the moon precedes and parents the day-stars: lunar months structure the calendar, and the moon's regularity makes him patron of mathematical-astronomical wisdom and of cattle (whose breeding cycles track the lunar calendar). The Akkadian princess-priestess [[enheduanna]] served as *en* (high priestess) of Nanna at Ur — the office through which her hymns ([[phase-1-003-enheduanna-hymns]]) entered the world.

## Mythological role
Sin's mythology is less narrative than Inanna's or Marduk's — he is more a sustaining cosmic presence than a hero. Late in Mesopotamian history Sin enjoyed a remarkable revival under the Neo-Babylonian king Nabonidus (~556–539 BCE), who promoted Sin of Harran to a near-supreme position and reportedly displeased Marduk's Babylonian priesthood — a religious-political conflict that may underlie the Babylonian acceptance of Cyrus the Persian's conquest (538 BCE) and indirectly Cyrus's permission for the Judean return from exile. Sin's later cult at Harran persisted into the Islamic period; the Sabians of Harran are the latest known organized practitioners.
