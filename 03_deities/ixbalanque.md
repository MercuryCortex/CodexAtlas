# Ixbalanque (Xbalanque)

## Identity
Ixbalanque ("Jaguar Sun" or "Little Jaguar Sun" — from K'iche' *ix* [feminine/jaguar prefix] + *balam* [jaguar] + *que* [possible diminutive]) is one of the **Hero Twins** of the *Popol Vuh* — the K'iche' Maya creation epic that is the foundational text of Mesoamerican mythology. Ixbalanque and his twin brother **[[hunahpu]]** are the central protagonists of the Popol Vuh's longest narrative section: their descent into **Xibalba** (the underworld), their defeat of the death lords through trickery and resurrection, and their final ascent to become **celestial bodies** (the sun and moon, or sun and Venus). Ixbalanque is characterized by **jaguar skin patches** on his body and is associated with the **moon** and **nighttime sun** (the sun's journey through the underworld). He is the trickster-pole of the twin pair — the more cunning, the more willing to use deception.

## Earliest attestation
- The Hero Twin mythology is attested in Classic Maya period art (300–900 CE) centuries before the Popol Vuh's written recording. Painted ceramic vessels show the Twins playing ball against the lords of the underworld; their story was known and depicted visually long before alphabetic transcription.
- **Popol Vuh**: the primary written source, recorded in K'iche' Maya using the Latin alphabet c. **1550s CE** (shortly after the Spanish conquest), preserved in a manuscript copied by Diego Reynoso; first transcribed for scholarly use by Francisco Ximénez c. 1701–1703.
- Scholarly editions: Tedlock (1985), Christenson (2003).
- The Hero Twin mythology may have roots in the Preclassic Maya period (c. 300 BCE–250 CE), based on iconographic evidence from San Bartolo murals (c. 100 BCE).

## Mythological role
**The Twins' origin**: Ixbalanque and Hunahpu are the sons of **Hun Hunahpu** (One Hunahpu, First Father) — their father was decapitated by the lords of Xibalba during an earlier ballgame. Hun Hunahpu's severed head impregnated the goddess Ixquic (Blood Moon) by spitting into her hand; she fled to the surface world and gave birth to the twins.

**The ball game**: the Twins' heroic career begins with their skill at the ballgame — so loud that it disturbs the lords of Xibalba, who summon them below.

**Descent into Xibalba**: the Twins descend voluntarily, passing through the underworld's traps — the dummy lords they correctly ignore, the successive Houses of trial (Dark House, Cold House, Bat House, Razor House, Fire House). They are killed: Hunahpu's head is taken by the bat demon Camazotz; Ixbalanque substitutes a squash for his brother's head until morning. Eventually, both Twins are **ground into powder** and their remains thrown into a river.

**Resurrection**: Five days later, the Twins reconstitute themselves — first as catfish, then as ragged performers, then in their full divine form. They perform miraculous feats (burning and restoring a house; sacrificing and resurrecting a dog; performing apparent self-sacrifice) that astonish the Xibalba lords.

**The defeat of Xibalba**: the Xibalba lords, fascinated, ask the Twins to sacrifice and resurrect *them* as well. The Twins sacrifice them and **do not resurrect them**. The lords of death are defeated not by force but by their own desire for the Twins' power — trickery as the supreme weapon against death.

**Celestial transformation**: after defeating Xibalba, the Twins **ascend to the sky** — Hunahpu becomes the **Sun**; Ixbalanque becomes the **Moon** (or, in some interpretations, **Venus**, the morning/evening star). This transformation is the final creative act: the Hero Twins' sacrifice and triumph generate the celestial order that governs time.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[hunahpu]]** — *tradition-internal*: the twin pair as indivisible mythological unit; Sun (Hunahpu) / Moon-Venus (Ixbalanque).
- **[[the-dioskouroi]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Greek Castor and Pollux — the divine twins who alternate between Olympus and the underworld; one mortal, one immortal; eventually transformed into the Gemini constellation. The divine-twin-alternation-death pattern may be reconstructable to Proto-Indo-European mythology (Calvert Watkins, *How to Kill a Dragon*; Bruce Lincoln).
- **[[osiris]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: dismembered/scattered body reconstituted through divine power; the resurrection-through-scattered-remains motif. Osiris's pieces reassembled by Isis / the Twins' ground powder reconstituting in the river.
- **[[inanna-sumerian]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the most structurally complete parallel to the Xibalba descent — voluntary descent into the underworld, death within the underworld, and return through creative trickery. Both narratives reconfigure the relationship between the living and dead worlds through the hero's transit.
- **[[persephone-greek]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: katabasis narrative ending in transformation of the underworld-upper world relationship. Persephone's capture and partial return / the Twins' victory and ascent.
- **[[izanagi]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Shinto descent into Yomi for the underworld encounter; horror, escape, and the cosmic consequence (the division of life and death).

## MASSIVE WIN: the divine twins

The **divine twin** archetype is one of the oldest reconstructable mythological patterns in human religion:

| Twins | Tradition | Mortal/Immortal | Celestial fate | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hunahpu + Ixbalanque** | K'iche' Maya | Both heroic, both die/resurrect | Sun + Moon/Venus | *Popol Vuh* |
| **Castor + Pollux** | Greek | One mortal, one divine | Gemini stars | Pindar; Homeric Hymns |
| **Romulus + Remus** | Roman | Both human; Romulus deified | Romulus = Quirinus (star) | Livy; Plutarch |
| **Yima + twin** | Iranian | Cosmogonic pair | — | Avesta |
| **Aśvins** | Vedic | Divine twins of dawn | Dawn horses | Rigveda |
| **Apollo + Artemis** | Greek | Both divine | Sun + Moon | Hesiod |
| **Amaterasu + Tsukuyomi** | Shinto | Both divine | Sun + Moon | *Nihon Shoki* |

The reconstruction of the Proto-Indo-European *Divine Twins* (*Dieusu Sunoue*, "Sons of the Sky Father") by Calvert Watkins, Donald Ward, and Bruce Lincoln suggests this may be the oldest mythological archetype recoverable through comparative method — predating 3000 BCE.

## MASSIVE WIN: the katabasis (descent to the underworld)

The Twins' Xibalba descent is the Mesoamerican instance of the most universally documented mythological journey:
- **Hero Twins** (Maya): descend to Xibalba, defeat death lords through trickery, ascend as sun and moon
- **[[inanna-sumerian]]** (Sumerian): descends to the Great Below, is killed, is resurrected through Enki's intervention, returns changed
- **Orpheus** (Greek): descends to Hades for Eurydice; wins her back through music; loses her by looking back
- **[[persephone-greek]]** (Greek): abducted to Hades; partial return governs the seasons
- **[[izanagi]]** (Shinto): descends to Yomi for Izanami; is repelled by horror; escape generates the division of life and death
- **Inanna/Ishtar** (Akkadian): parallel to Sumerian version
- **Aeneas** (Roman): descends to the Underworld with the Sibyl; receives prophetic vision; ascends transformed

The katabasis is the ur-narrative of transformation: you must go through death to transcend it. The Hero Twins' version is distinctive because they go *voluntarily*, die completely, and return through self-generated resurrection — the most radical form of the descent-and-return.

## Iconography
Ixbalanque is distinguished from Hunahpu by:
- **Jaguar patches on the skin** — dark spots of jaguar hide incorporated into his body, associating him with the nocturnal jaguar (the sun in the underworld, the "night sun")
- **Smaller or rounder face** compared to Hunahpu in Classic Maya ceramic depictions
- **Moon iconography**: crescent moon associations in some post-Classic depictions

In Classic Maya ballgame iconography, the Twins appear as ballplayers in full gear facing the skeletal lords of death across the ballcourt — a cosmic confrontation between life and death played out in the most sacred game.
