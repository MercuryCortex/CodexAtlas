# Sraosha

## Identity
Sraosha (Avestan *Sraoša*, "hearkening / heard-obedience" — etymologically the divine "Ear" or "Voice"; from the same root as English "listen") is **the principal yazata (worship-worthy divine being) of obedience-to-the-divine-word** in Zoroastrian theology. He is the personification of **hearkening to Ahura Mazda's instruction** — and by metonymy of **prayer itself**, since prayer is the human counter-response to the divine word heard-and-obeyed. He is **wakeful** (he never sleeps, guarding the world against [[angra-mainyu-ahriman]] during the third nightwatch when humans sleep); his sacred bird is the **rooster** (which announces the dawn — the daily defeat of darkness by light). He is one of the **three guardians of the Cinvat Bridge** (the bridge across which souls travel to the afterlife — see [[frashokereti-cosmic-renovation]]). His canonical adversary is [[aeshma]], the daeva of wrath; the Sraosha-Aeshma pairing is the structural template of Zoroastrian good-vs-evil cosmic combat.

## Earliest attestation
- **Gathas** ([[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]], ~-1200): Yasna 33.5, 43.12, 44.16, 45.5, 46.17 — Sraosha appears as personified obedience-to-Ahura Mazda's instruction.
- **Younger Avesta**: **Yasna 57 (the Sraosha Yasht)** — the principal liturgical hymn to Sraosha. Yasna 56–57 are the daily-prayer culmination of Zoroastrian worship.
- **Pahlavi tradition**: Sraosha (now *Srosh*) continues as central yazata of daily prayer + psychopomp. Pahlavi texts elaborate his role.

## Mythological role
- **Daily-prayer personification.** Sraosha is *the* Zoroastrian yazata of prayer — the practice of regular daily prayer at the five *gah* (watches of the day) is theologically grounded in Sraosha-veneration.
- **Wakeful guardian during the third nightwatch.** When humans sleep, Sraosha keeps watch against Angra Mainyu's demonic agents.
- **Psychopomp at death.** Sraosha is one of three yazatas (alongside Mithra and Rashnu) who attend the soul on the **Cinvat Bridge** — the bridge across which souls travel to either paradise (broad and well-lit for the righteous) or hell (narrow as a razor's edge for the wicked).
- **Opponent of Aeshma.** The Sraosha-Aeshma combat is constant: every act of obedient prayer weakens Aeshma; every act of violent rage strengthens him.

## Cross-tradition identifications

### Gabriel-and-Sraosha angelic-messenger parallel
The structural parallel between Sraosha and the archangel **[[gabriel-archangel]]** is real and instructive:
- Both are **principal angelic-messenger figures** in their respective traditions.
- Both **announce** — Sraosha through the rooster announcing the dawn; Gabriel through the Annunciation announcing the new age of salvation (Luke 1).
- Both are **wakeful guardians** of the cosmic order during periods of darkness/danger.
- Both have **psychopomp / soul-guide functions** in some traditions.

The Zoroastrian-influence-on-Second-Temple-Jewish-angelology argument (Boyce 1991, Hultgård 1998) places the development of named-archangelic hierarchy (the **seven archangels** of Tobit 12:15, 1 Enoch, etc.) in the Persian-period as Jewish absorption of Zoroastrian yazata-theology. The seven Amesha Spentas + Sraosha provide the **structural model** for seven-archangel theology that emerges in the Second Temple period.

### Logos / personified-Word parallel
Sraosha as the **personified divine Voice / Word-as-heard** has a structural parallel with later Hellenistic-philosophical-theological **logos** doctrines (Stoic *logos*; Philonic *logos*; Johannine *Logos* in John 1:1). The parallel is structural rather than directly genealogical, but the Sraosha-as-Voice-of-Ahura-Mazda figure may be one of the Near Eastern background traditions that contributed to the broader Hellenistic-Jewish religious-philosophical environment in which Johannine Logos theology developed.

## Iconography
- Sasanian-Pahlavi-period: warrior-figure with club (similar iconography to other warrior-yazatas).
- Sacred bird the **rooster** — the principal animal-attribute. Rooster-imagery in Sasanian art is often Sraosha-coded.
- Modern Parsi devotional art: human-figure with halo + club + rooster.

## Disputes
- **Etymology.** *Sraoša* from Indo-European root \*ḱlew- "to hear" (cf. English "listen," Latin *cluere*, Greek *kleos*) — uncontested.
- **Gabriel-parallel directness.** Whether the Sraosha → Gabriel structural parallel reflects direct genealogical transmission (via Persian-period Jewish absorption) or independent convergent development is debated. The dominant view (Boyce 1991) treats Persian influence as substantial.
- **Logos-parallel.** Whether Sraosha specifically contributed to the Hellenistic-Jewish *logos* development is more speculative than the angelology argument. Most current scholarship is cautious.
