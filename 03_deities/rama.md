# Rama

Seventh *avatāra* of Vishnu, exiled prince of Ayodhya, hero of the *Rāmāyaṇa*. Rama embodies *maryada* — the principled limit, the binding norm — in contrast to Krishna's *lila* (boundless play). Where Krishna can lie, steal butter, and seduce married women in service of cosmic teaching, Rama is constituted by his commitment to *dharma*: keeping his father's promise (going into exile), keeping his marriage vow (rescuing Sita), keeping his king's duty (banishing Sita on a citizen's rumor).

The Rama story has many forms — Valmiki's Sanskrit *Rāmāyaṇa*, Kamban's Tamil *Iramavataram*, Tulsidas' Awadhi *Ramcharitmanas*, the Thai *Ramakien*, the Indonesian *Kakawin Ramayana*, the Buddhist *Dasaratha Jataka*. Each retelling reconfigures Rama's character and the moral status of his decisions (especially regarding Sita and Shambuka). Richman 1991's "many Ramayanas" is the canonical critical framing.

Modern reception: Rama is the central icon of *Hindutva* political identification with the Ram Janmabhoomi at Ayodhya, the destruction of the Babri Masjid (1992), and the 2024 Ram Mandir consecration — Rama as deity *and* as political symbol of Hindu majoritarianism. See [[tradition-hindutva]].
