# Metztli

## Identity
Metztli is the Nahua (Aztec) **deity of the moon** — the personified moon and the night, associated with moisture, the agricultural night-sky, and the rabbit that the Mexica saw in the lunar face.

## Earliest attestation
Attested in the Nahua sources and codices of the Postclassic period; the moon's mythology is told above all in the **Teotihuacan creation** of the Fifth Sun.

## Mythological role — the moon and the rabbit
The Aztec moon-story is bound to the creation of the Fifth Sun: when the gods gathered at Teotihuacan to make a new sun, the humble [[nanahuatzin]] and the proud [[tecciztecatl]] cast themselves into the fire; both rose as suns, until a god flung a **rabbit** into the face of the second to dim it — and so it became the **moon**, the rabbit still visible upon it. Metztli is the moon thus born and personified, marking the lunar month and the rhythms of the night.

## Cross-tradition
Metztli is the Aztec member of the **moon-deity** neighborhood — the lunar god/goddess of the month and the tides — alongside Sin/Nanna, Khonsu, Selene, Luna, Mani, Chandra, Tsukuyomi, Mama Quilla, and Mēness.

## Iconography
The moon-disc, often with a rabbit; shell and water emblems; the dark of the night sky.
