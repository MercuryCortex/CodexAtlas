# Freyr

## Identity
Freyr ("Lord") is the principal male Vanir god — brother of Freyja, son of Njörðr; god of fertility, agricultural prosperity, peace, kingship, and the boar. His cult was especially strong in Sweden, where the kings of the Yngling dynasty claimed descent from him (Snorri's *Ynglinga saga* begins with him); Adam of Bremen's 11th-c. description of the great temple at Uppsala mentions Fricco (= Freyr) alongside Thor and Wotan with a notable phallic emphasis on his image. Freyr is the god of *peaceful prosperity*, contrasting with Thor's storm-protection and Óðinn's war-and-wisdom.

## Earliest attestation
Old Norse-Icelandic literary attestation; Adam of Bremen *Gesta Hammaburgensis* (c. 1075) on the Uppsala temple. The Anglo-Saxon *Ing* (Old English Rune Poem stanza for the *Ingwaz* rune) preserves the cognate.

## Mythological role
The *Skírnismál* narrates Freyr's wooing of Gerðr — he sees her from afar and falls into desperate love; his servant Skírnir is sent with the offer of his magical sword (and threats of cosmic curses) to win her consent. Freyr's giving away of his sword is fatal: at Ragnarǫk, he must fight Surtr without it and is killed.

## Iconography
Boar (the *Gullinbursti* "Golden-bristled" who shines like the sun and pulls his chariot); ship *Skíðblaðnir*; phallic emphasis in the Uppsala cult-image and in surviving small phallic figurines (Rällinge statuette).
