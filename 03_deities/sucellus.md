# Sucellus

Sucellus ("the Good Striker") is among the most frequently depicted gods of **Gaulish religion**, instantly recognizable by his **long-handled hammer or mallet** — the attribute that defines him — together with a **pot (olla)**, sometimes a **barrel**, and a dog at his side. A bearded, mature, dignified figure, he is a god of **agriculture, the forests, and above all the vine and wine**, and of the **prosperity** of the land and household; the mallet may strike the earth to fertility, or seal the wine-cask, and (more speculatively) knock at the door of death. His consort is the goddess **Nantosuelta** ("winding river" / "sun-warmed valley"), who carries a house-shaped sceptre, the pair forming a domestic-and-agrarian divine couple. Roman observers and modern scholars have compared him to **[[dis-pater|Dis Pater]]** (Julius Caesar reports the Gauls' claim of descent from a Dis-Pater-like underworld ancestor) and assimilated him in art to **[[silvanus|Silvanus]]**, the Roman god of the cultivated wild. Sucellus embodies the Gaulish sacralization of the working countryside — its wine, its fields, and its plenty.
