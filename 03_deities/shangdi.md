# Shangdi

## Identity
Shangdi ("the Lord on High") is the supreme deity of the Shang dynasty (~-1600 to -1046 BCE), attested above all in the oracle-bone inscriptions of Anyang. The Shang kings inquired of Shangdi about weather, harvest, battle, illness, dreams — burning turtle plastrons and ox scapulae and reading the cracks. Shangdi is conceived as a transcendent ancestral / cosmic sovereign, possibly the high ancestor of the Shang royal line elevated to cosmic status, possibly something more abstractly cosmic; the oracle bones do not depict him anthropomorphically. He controls weather, agriculture, and outcomes; he is approached through ancestral intermediaries (the Shang ancestors served as Shangdi's court).

After the Zhou conquest (~1046 BCE), Zhou ideology introduced the concept of *Tian* (Heaven) — partly overlapping, partly distinct from Shangdi — and the *Mandate of Heaven* (*tian ming*) legitimizing the Zhou victory. Through the Zhou period the two terms are used somewhat interchangeably, with Tian gradually predominating. By the time of Confucius and the Warring States philosophers, Tian is the standard supreme cosmic principle; Shangdi survives as a more archaic name. Christian missionary debates from Matteo Ricci (1583) onward over whether to translate "God" as "Shangdi" (Ricci's preference) or "Tian" or "Tianzhu" ("Lord of Heaven") drew on this dual heritage.
