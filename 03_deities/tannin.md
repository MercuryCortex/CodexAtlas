# Tannin

## Identity

Tannin (Hebrew **תַּנִּין**, *tannîn*, "dragon, sea-monster") is the sea-dragon of the Hebrew Bible — one of the named chaos-creatures of the deep that YHWH pierces and slays in the residual combat-cosmogony preserved in the Psalms, Job, and the Prophets. The word is grammatically masculine, forms a plural *tannînîm*, and has no cult, no genealogy, and no anthropomorphic iconography; like [[rahab|Rahab]] and [[leviathan|Leviathan]], Tannin survives only as a poetic trophy of divine victory. In the Atlas it is placed among the chaos-beings alongside [[leviathan]], [[rahab]], [[lotan]], [[yam]], and [[tiamat]], because it names the same primordial sea-chaos slot under a distinctively draconic by-name — *the Dragon* of the sea.

Two distinctions are built into the entry, because *tannîn* is a slippery word.

First — **the lexical caution: chaos-dragon vs. generic sea-creature.** Biblical Hebrew uses *tannîn* in two quite different ranges. (a) The **chaos-dragon** sense, the one relevant to the combat-cosmogony, where Tannin is a named cosmic enemy slain by YHWH (Isa 51:9; Ps 74:13; Isa 27:1; Job 7:12) or a figure for the Egyptian power (Ezek 29:3; 32:2). (b) A **generic** sense — a large reptile or sea-creature with no mythic charge: the "great *tannînîm*" simply created as fauna on day five (Gen 1:21), Aaron's staff that becomes a *tannîn* before Pharaoh (Exod 7:9–12), the serpents of the wilderness and the "venom of *tannînîm*" (Deut 32:33; Ps 91:13). Only sense (a) underwrites the placement in the Chaoskampf grid; the generic sense is, if anything, the demythologised residue of it (the chaos-dragon turned into an ordinary created beast at Gen 1:21).

Second — **the homograph trap**: *tannîn* (תַּנִּין, "dragon") must not be confused with *tannîm* (תַּנִּים, "jackals"), a separate plural noun. The two are repeatedly confused in the Masoretic vocalisation and the versions — several passages traditionally read as "dragons" (e.g. in Lam 4:3; Mal 1:3) are now generally taken as "jackals," and a few "jackal" pointings are emended back to "dragon." Standard lexica (TDOT, s.v. תַּנִּין) keep the two words apart.

## In the Hebrew Bible

The chaos-dragon Tannin is named in a small, tightly thematic cluster of poetic and prophetic texts:

- **Isaiah 51:9** — *"Awake, awake, put on strength, O arm of YHWH … Was it not you who cut Rahab in pieces (*hammaḥṣeḇet rahaḇ*), who pierced the dragon (*məḥôleleṯ tannîn*)?"* Here Tannin stands in **synonymous parallelism with [[rahab|Rahab]]**: the two cola name the one defeated chaos-power under two by-names, and the passage immediately invokes the drying of "the great deep" (*tĕhôm rabbâ*), fusing the primeval combat with the Exodus sea-crossing.
- **Psalm 74:13** — *"You divided the sea (*yām*) by your might; you broke the heads of the dragons (*tannînîm*) on the waters."* The very next verse crushes "the heads of [[leviathan|Leviathan]]" (Ps 74:14) — so the plural sea-dragons and the multi-headed Leviathan are the same combat in successive cola.
- **Isaiah 27:1** — *"In that day YHWH with his hard and great and strong sword will punish Leviathan the fleeing serpent, Leviathan the twisting serpent, and he will slay the dragon (*tannîn*) that is in the sea."* The eschatological re-doing of the combat, in which "the dragon in the sea" is a closing parallel by-name for the serpent Leviathan named just before.
- **Job 7:12** — *"Am I the Sea (*yām*), or a Tannin, that you set a guard over me?"* Job protests that he is being treated like a chaos-power needing divine restraint — Tannin in apposition with Yam, both as monsters God keeps under watch.

A second, **figurative** usage applies the dragon's name to **Egypt / Pharaoh**, the river-power whose arrogance YHWH humbles — closely parallel to the way Rahab is made a cipher for Egypt:

- **Ezekiel 29:3** — *"Thus says the Lord GOD: Behold, I am against you, Pharaoh king of Egypt, the great dragon (*hattannîm* / *tannîn*) that lies in the midst of his streams, who says, 'My Nile is my own, I made it for myself.'"*
- **Ezekiel 32:2** — *"You are like a dragon (*tannîn*) in the seas; you burst forth in your rivers, troubling the waters with your feet."* The mythic chaos-dragon recruited to name Israel's archetypal historical oppressor, with the Nile/sea imagery making the identification natural.

This figurative usage is theologically continuous with the cosmogonic one: the dragon that YHWH "pierces" in the deep (Isa 51:9) and the river-dragon Pharaoh whom YHWH hooks and hauls from his streams (Ezek 29:4) are the same mythic figure in cosmic and historical registers.

## Cross-tradition significance

Tannin is one of the **named survivals of the ancient Near-Eastern combat-cosmogony** within the Hebrew Bible — a fragment of the [[chaoskampf|Chaoskampf]] in which a divine warrior defeats the primordial chaos-sea and its dragon, establishing the ordered world. Since Hermann Gunkel's *Schöpfung und Chaos* (1895), the Tannin passages have been read — together with the Rahab, Leviathan, and Yam texts — as the poetic residue of a combat-myth that the Priestly Genesis 1 deliberately *demythologises* (the chaos-dragon reappearing, drained of menace, as the "great *tannînîm*" merely *created* at Gen 1:21). John Day's *God's Conflict with the Dragon and the Sea* (1985) is the canonical modern treatment, and Mary Wakeman's *God's Battle with the Monster* (1973) the monograph most directly organised around this chaos-figure.

The comparative claim must distinguish two very different kinds of link, and this is what makes Tannin instructive next to its sibling [[rahab|Rahab]].

**Westward — a secure linguistic cognate.** Unlike Rahab, whose name has *no* attested Ugaritic or Babylonian antecedent, Tannin has a genuine cognate: Hebrew *tannîn* and Ugaritic *tnn* are **the same Northwest-Semitic word** for the sea-dragon. In the Ugaritic Baal Cycle the goddess Anat enumerates the chaos-monsters she has vanquished, and *tnn* stands in that catalogue beside the seven-headed serpent that the Hebrew tradition knows as [[lotan|Lotan]]/Leviathan (KTU 1.3.iii). The Hebrew sea-dragon is therefore the **Israelite continuation of an attested Ugaritic chaos-monster**, with YHWH occupying the combat-role the Ugaritic texts give to Baal and Anat — exactly the relationship that holds between Leviathan (*lwytn*) and Lotan (*ltn*). This is a cognate **wire**, not a second home tradition: Tannin's origin is the Hebrew Bible, and its Ugaritic background and Bronze-Age substrate are edges, not memberships.

**Eastward and outward — structural parallel only.** Tannin's kinship to [[tiamat|Tiamat]] (Babylon) and to the wider chaoskampf grid (Typhon, Vritra, Illuyanka) is **structural / typological** — the convergent figure of "primordial sea-dragon defeated by the warrior-creator" — not a name-for-name etymology. *tannîn* is not a cognate of *Tiāmat*; the Hebrew cognate of Tiamat is [[tehom|tehom]], the deep. So the correct statement is: **Tannin is a native Hebrew chaos-dragon that (i) is a secure Northwest-Semitic cognate of the Ugaritic sea-dragon tnn, (ii) is interchangeable with Rahab / Leviathan / Yam by direct poetic synonymy inside the canon (Isa 51:9; Ps 74:13; Isa 27:1; Job 7:12), and (iii) occupies the same comparative slot as Tiamat by convergence on the shared combat-pattern, not by transmission of a name.**

In later tradition the chaos-dragon persists. The deuterocanonical **Bel and the Dragon** (Daniel additions, ~2nd c. BCE) has Daniel destroy a living *tannîn*/δράκων venerated by the Babylonians — a narrative play on the dragon that the prophets said YHWH alone could slay. Second Temple and rabbinic monster-lore folds Tannin into the same eschatological-banquet and sea-monster traditions that grow around Leviathan and Rahab — the demythologised biblical dragon re-mythologised in later Jewish imagination.

## Disputes

- **Secure cognate, contested transmission.** That *tannîn* and Ugaritic *tnn* are the same word is not in doubt; what follows from it is the usual Chaoskampf question. The mainstream (Gunkel 1895; Day 1985; Wakeman 1973) holds that the Hebrew dragon-poetry is a real survival of an inherited Northwest-Semitic combat-cosmogony, Canaanite-mediated (Day) rather than directly Babylonian. The cognate at least makes Tannin a *stronger* case for genuine inherited mythology than Rahab, whose name is a native coinage with no foreign anchor.
- **How "mythic" is the language? (Gunkel–Day vs. Tsumura).** Whether the Tannin passages preserve a once-told combat-*myth*, or merely deploy stock poetic imagery of God's mastery over a stormy, monster-filled sea, is contested. David Tsumura (*Creation and Destruction*, 2005) mounts the major dissent, arguing the "chaos" framework is overdrawn and much of the supposed combat-vocabulary is ordinary figurative poetry. The honest state of the question: **the imagery of a defeated sea-dragon is undisputed; whether it encodes a once-told myth or only a poetic topos is the live debate.**
- **Which sense in a given verse — dragon, sea-creature, or jackal?** Several occurrences are genuinely ambiguous between the chaos-dragon (a), the generic large reptile/sea-creature (b), and the homograph *tannîm* ("jackals"). Lam 4:3 and Mal 1:3 are now generally read as "jackals"; Gen 1:21 and Exod 7 are the generic reptile; only the poetic-prophetic cluster (Isa 51:9; Ps 74:13; Isa 27:1; Job 7:12; Ezek 29/32) carries the chaos-dragon force. The placement of this node rests only on that cluster.
- **The Egypt-cipher relation.** Whether Tannin-as-Pharaoh (Ezek 29:3; 32:2) is a *secondary historicising* of the chaos-dragon or evidence that the figure was always politically applicable is discussed but not decisive; most read it, as with Rahab=Egypt, as the mythic dragon recruited to name Israel's archetypal oppressor, the Nile standing in for the chaos-waters.
