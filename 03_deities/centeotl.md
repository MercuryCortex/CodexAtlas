# Centeotl

The **young male maize-god** of the Aztec — one node of a remarkable gendered maize-deity cluster that maps the male/female principle and the life-cycle of the corn plant onto distinct divine figures:

- **Centeotl** — the young male maize / the *teotl* of the corn plant itself
- **Chicomecoatl** ("Seven Serpent") — the mature female maize goddess; the established harvest
- **Xilonen** ("Tender Corn") — the young female goddess of the tender green ear
- **Mayahuel** — the agave/pulque mother; the fermented-drink complex

The Aztec mapping of one staple cultivar onto **four** gendered deities (with the maguey added as a fifth in [[mayahuel]]) is one of the most elaborated agricultural-deity systems in any religion. Centeotl is also tied to the male warrior cult through his depiction in spear-and-warrior regalia — corn was understood as the substance from which the Aztec people were made, so the maize-god participates in both agriculture and warrior identity.
