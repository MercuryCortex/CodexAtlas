# Apollo

## Identity
Apollo is the Greek Olympian of prophecy, music, poetry, healing, archery, and the arts of civilized order. Son of Zeus and Leto, twin of Artemis, born on Delos. His principal sanctuary at Delphi housed the most famous oracle of the ancient Greek world; the *Pythia* (priestess) gave responses on his behalf for over a millennium. He is the leader of the Muses (*Mousagētēs*) on Mount Parnassus and the patron of poetry. His healing aspect connects him to Asclepius (his son in some genealogies) and to the cult-shrine medicine of the classical world.

Apollo's origin is debated. The Homeric Hymn to Apollo (~700 BCE) gives two birth-narratives — Delian and Pythian — possibly reflecting two sources for the deity. Burkert and others argue for a partly non-Indo-European (Anatolian or Near Eastern) component in his early cult, given his unusually elaborate ritual apparatus and his standing apart from the Indo-European storm-warrior type. Linear B does not securely attest Apollo (some scholars read "*a-pe-ro2*" as proto-Apollo, debated). Later in antiquity Apollo is identified with the Sun (Helios), but this solar role is post-Classical; in Homer the Sun is Helios, distinct from Apollo. Herodotus identifies Apollo with the Egyptian Horus. Apollo's cult continued vigorously into Late Antiquity (Julian the Apostate reopened many Apollo sanctuaries in the 4th c. CE).
