# Holy Spirit (Christian)

The third person of the Christian Trinity — *homoousios* (one in being) with the Father and the Son per the Nicene Creed expanded at Constantinople (381 CE) against the Pneumatomachi ("Spirit-fighters") who denied the Spirit's full divinity. In mainstream Christian theology, the Holy Spirit is the divine principle of indwelling, sanctification, inspiration, and guidance into truth — the one who "proceeds from the Father" (Eastern formulation, John 15:26) or "from the Father and the Son" (Western *filioque* — added 589 CE Toledo, ratified 1014 Rome; the doctrinal flashpoint of the 1054 East-West Schism).

## The grammatical-gender drift

One of the most theologically consequential linguistic phenomena in the Christian transmission chain is the **grammatical-gender drift of the Spirit's name across translations**:

| Language | Term | Gender | Period |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biblical Hebrew | *ruach ha-qodesh* (רוּחַ הַקֹּדֶשׁ) | **feminine** | pre-exilic onward |
| Aramaic / Syriac | *ruḥa d-qudsha* (ܪܘܚܐ ܕܩܘܕܫܐ) | **feminine** | Second Temple → Late Antiquity |
| Koine Greek | *pneuma hagion* (πνεῦμα ἅγιον) | **neuter** | NT period onward |
| Latin | *spiritus sanctus* | **masculine** | Vulgate onward |
| English | "Holy Spirit" / "Holy Ghost" | implicitly masculine | KJV onward |

This is not random — it is the **linguistic fingerprint** of the suppression of the figure's feminine register. The Syriac Christianity that preserved Aramaic *ruḥa* (feminine) preserved the Spirit-as-Mother tradition; the Greek + Latin churches that translated the term into neuter + masculine grammatical genders progressively suppressed it. The Spirit-as-Mother citation Origen preserves from the lost Gospel of the Hebrews ("My mother the Holy Spirit took me by one of my hairs and carried me to the great mountain, Tabor") is the trace of what the Greek-Latin doctrinal mainstream lost.

See [[theme-holy-spirit-sophia]] for the full cross-tradition treatment of the suppressed-feminine-third pattern (Sophia in Gnosticism + Shekhinah in Kabbalah + Spirit-as-Mother in Syriac Christianity).

## The Persian-period substrate

The Hebrew Bible's *ruach ha-qodesh* concept appears in Isaiah 63:10–11 and Psalm 51:11 — Second-Temple-period passages, after the Persian contact. The structural and terminological parallel with Zoroastrian **Spenta Mainyu** ("Bounteous Spirit" / "Holy Spirit") is striking enough that Boyce & Grenet 1991 treat it as a likely Persian-period influence on developing Jewish (and through Judaism, Christian) pneumatology. See [[theme-zoroastrian-jewish-exchange]] and [[persian-period-injection]] for the broader six-channel exchange.

## The Pentecost event

Acts 2:1–4 narrates the descent of the Spirit on the 120 disciples at Pentecost — tongues of fire above each head, sudden glossolalia in all the languages of the assembled pilgrims, the inversion of Babel. The day of Pentecost (50 days after Passover) is the Jewish festival of Shavuot, the giving of the Torah at Sinai. The Christian Pentecost-as-Spirit-descent is structurally the **giving of the Spirit-as-new-Torah** — the Spirit replaces the written Law as the divine indwelling that guides the new covenant community. This Acts 2 narrative is the founding event of the Spirit's role in Christian ecclesiology.
