# Erlang Shen

## Identity
Erlang Shen (二郎神, "Second-Son Deity") is the supreme demon-subduer and divine engineer of the Chinese pantheon — a celestial general whose defining attributes are a supernaturally sharp third eye in the forehead that sees through all illusion and transformation, a three-pointed two-edged spear, and a divine hunting dog that can seize shape-changers mid-transformation. In his most widespread historical identification, he is Yang Jian (楊戩), nephew of the Jade Emperor ([[jade-emperor]]), and the commander of the Plum Mountain Six Brothers (a squad of elite celestial warriors). He is semi-autonomous within the celestial hierarchy — uniquely in Chinese mythology, he reportedly declines rewards and titles from the Jade Emperor, maintaining a proud independence that marks him as a liminal figure: powerful enough to be indispensable, proud enough to operate outside normal celestial bureaucracy.

## Earliest attestation
The historical stratum of the cult is ancient: Erlang Shen is most probably a euhemerized memory of Li Bing's son (**Li Erlang**), the engineer who built the Dujiangyan irrigation system in Sichuan (~256 BCE under the Qin), one of the greatest hydraulic engineering works in history. Li Erlang became a local flood-control deity of Sichuan before being absorbed into the pan-Chinese pantheon. The *Fengshen Yanyi* (Investiture of the Gods, 16th c.) and Wu Cheng'en's *Xiyouji* (c. 1592) give the deity his most famous literary forms. However, the folk cult in Sichuan predates the novels by over a millennium.

## Mythological role
**Flood Control (Historical Core).** As the son of Li Bing, Erlang is the god who tamed the Minjiang River through the Dujiangyan project — splitting a mountain with his divine spear to open the Min River gorge and redirect flood waters. This deed established his identity as the deity of hydraulic engineering and flood suppression, and temples at Dujiangyan (Erwang Miao, "Temple of the Two Kings") have been maintained for over two thousand years.

**Battle with Sun Wukong.** Erlang Shen's most famous episode in popular mythology is his epic battle with [[sun-wukong]] in the *Xiyouji*. When the Jade Emperor's celestial armies cannot defeat the Monkey King, Erlang Shen and his Plum Mountain Brothers descend to confront him. The battle becomes a contest of shapeshifting — Sun Wukong transforms through dozens of forms, and it is Erlang Shen's third eye that pierces each disguise. The fight is a draw until [[guanyin]] and Laozi intervene from above, trapping the Monkey King so he can be delivered to the Buddha. The episode positions Erlang Shen as uniquely capable among celestial forces — the only being powerful enough to match the Monkey King's shapeshifting through truth-seeing rather than brute force.

**Celestial Bureaucracy.** In the *Fengshen Yanyi* tradition, Yang Jian / Erlang Shen serves as supreme marshal of the celestial armies. His divine dog (the Roaring Celestial Dog) is famous for its own demon-catching capacity and appears as an independent story element in Chinese folk narrative.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[jade-emperor]]** — Erlang Shen serves as the Emperor's foremost general; his semi-autonomous refusal of celestial rewards creates a productive tension between service and independence that runs through the folklore.
- **[[guanyin]]** — in the *Xiyouji*, Guanyin observes the battle from above and ultimately assists in subduing Sun Wukong; their cooperation frames the divine order that Erlang Shen enforces.
- **[[sun-wukong]]** — chief opponent in the heaven-rebellion arc; Erlang Shen is the only celestial warrior capable of matching the Monkey King.
- **[[tu-di-gong]]** — local earth-deities of Chinese folk religion; Erlang Shen's flood-control and local Sichuan roots connect him to the strata of place-specific protective deities before his promotion into the pan-Chinese pantheon.
- **[[zao-jun]]** — another deity of the folk-religion layer who occupies a complementary domestic-protective role to Erlang Shen's martial-protective function.

## Iconography
Erlang Shen is depicted as a young, handsome celestial warrior in armored mandarin robes, carrying a three-pointed two-edged spear. Most distinctive is the **third eye** in the center of his forehead — a truth-seeing eye that cannot be deceived by transformations or illusions. His divine dog accompanies him. Temple iconography at Dujiangyan shows him in his engineering aspect, associated with the river-taming act.

## Disputes
The identity of Erlang Shen is debated in Chinese folklore studies: the three main candidates are (1) Li Erlang, son of the Qin-period engineer Li Bing; (2) Zhao Yu, a figure in medieval Sichuan local legend; and (3) Yang Jian, nephew of the Jade Emperor, as established in the 16th-century novels. These may represent the merging of distinct local cults under a single deity-name. The third-eye iconography may derive from Tantric Buddhist influence on Chinese folk religion.
