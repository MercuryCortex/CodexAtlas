# Samantabhadra (Primordial Buddha — Nyingma)

## Identity
Samantabhadra (Tibetan: *Kuntu Zangpo*, "All-Good") is the primordial Buddha (*Ādibuddha*) of the Nyingma school of Tibetan Buddhism. *Note*: this is distinct from the Mahāyāna bodhisattva Samantabhadra (also called Pu Xian / Fugen in East Asia), with whom this Tantric Samantabhadra shares a name but not a role. Iconographically: deep blue / sky-color, *naked* (without the regalia of other Buddhas — signifying the unadorned ultimate), seated in vajra-asana, in yab-yum union with his white consort Samantabhadrī. His nakedness is doctrinally important: where other Buddhas wear ornaments signifying particular qualities, Samantabhadra is the *ground itself*, without features. In Nyingma Dzogchen, Samantabhadra is identified with the *primordial purity* (*ka dag*) of awareness — the pre-personal, ever-pure ground from which all phenomena arise as the self-display of awareness (*rang shar*) and to which they return when their nature is recognized.

## Earliest attestation
Dzogchen tantras (the Seventeen Tantras of the Nyingma corpus, ~9th–11th c. Tibetan, often claimed to translate earlier Indian originals); systematic doctrinal treatment in Longchenpa (~1308–1364).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[vajradhara]]** — same primordial-Buddha function in Sarma schools.
- The Mahāyāna bodhisattva Samantabhadra — distinct figure with shared name; the Mahāyāna bodhisattva is one of the eight great bodhisattvas, depicted on elephant, associated with the *Bhadracarī* prayer (10 great vows).
