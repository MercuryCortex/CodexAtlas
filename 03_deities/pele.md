# Pele

## Identity
Hawaiian volcano goddess — most associated with Kīlauea on Hawai'i Island (the Big Island), but with cultic presence throughout the Hawaiian chain because her cosmogonic-volcanic activity shaped each island in succession. Pele is younger in the cosmogony than the four principal *akua* (Kāne, Kū, Lono, Kanaloa) and is a *separate* mythological complex of her own — Pele, her sister Hi'iaka, and their genealogical companions. Pele's religious presence remains live in contemporary Hawai'i — visitors to Volcanoes National Park are reminded not to take volcanic rocks home (Pele's curse on takers), and major eruptions (1959, 2018) generate continued Pele-religious observance.

## Earliest attestation
Polynesian-migration cosmology brought volcano-deity tradition (related but distinct figures attested in Tahiti, Samoa); Hawaiian-specific Pele narratives in Hawaiian oral tradition; documented in 19th-c. compilations (Emerson 1915; Malo).

## Mythological role
Pele and her family migrated from the ancestral Polynesian homeland *Kahiki* southward through the Hawaiian chain, settling first at Kaua'i and successively at each island, eventually establishing at Kīlauea on Hawai'i. Each migration corresponds to volcanic-geological progression: the Hawaiian Islands are progressively younger from northwest (Kaua'i) to southeast (Hawai'i), reflecting the Pacific tectonic plate's movement over the volcanic hotspot. The Pele mythological migration *describes the geological reality* — a striking case of Indigenous cosmogony encoding accurate earth-history.

The Pele-Hi'iaka cycle (extensive narrative material): Pele sends her younger sister Hi'iaka to fetch her lover Lohi'au from Kaua'i; complications, jealousies, betrayals; Hi'iaka ultimately resurrects Lohi'au twice from death-by-Pele's-wrath; the cycle is the longest connected narrative in Hawaiian oral tradition.

## Iconography
Volcanic eruption itself; red 'ohi'a lehua flower (the first vegetation to colonize new lava); appearances as old woman (testing humans' generosity) or beautiful young woman (in seduction narratives).
