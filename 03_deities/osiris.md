# Osiris

Osiris is the Egyptian god of the dead, judge of the deceased, archetypal divine king, and the principal Egyptian [[dying-rising-god]]. His mythological narrative — fragmentary in Egyptian sources but synthesized whole by Plutarch's *On Isis and Osiris* (~CE 100) — runs: Osiris reigns as Egypt's first king, civilizes humanity, is murdered by his jealous brother [[set]] (who tricks him into a coffin, seals it, and casts it into the Nile), his body is found and lost again (Set dismembers it into fourteen pieces and scatters them), his wife/sister [[isis]] reassembles the body and revivifies him long enough to conceive [[horus]], who avenges him; Osiris then rules in the netherworld as judge of the dead. His cult centered at Abydos (with the supposed relic of his head) and Busiris in the Delta. Greenness or blackness of his skin signals vegetative renewal and the Nile silt — his death and revival was tied to the annual Nile flood and grain cycle.

## Mythological role
The Osirian program is the dominant Egyptian afterlife framework from the [[phase-1-009-coffin-texts]] onward. The deceased is identified with Osiris ("Osiris-NN, the justified") and walks the netherworld path that Osiris walked. Judgment at the Hall of Two Truths — the weighing of the heart against the feather of [[maat]] — is presided over by Osiris seated on his throne, with [[anubis]] managing the scales and [[thoth]] recording. The Hellenistic Osiris-cult, mediated through Alexandria and the Ptolemaic syncretic Serapis, spread across the Mediterranean and may have influenced (the question of *how directly* is debated) later Mediterranean dying-rising-savior soteriologies. Smith (2017) is the definitive recent synthesis; Mettinger (2001) defends Osiris as a genuine dying-rising god against Frazer-skeptics.
