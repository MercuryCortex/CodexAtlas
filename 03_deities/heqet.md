# Heqet

Heqet (Heket) is the Egyptian **frog-goddess of childbirth** — the deity who presides over the final, quickening stage of labour and breathes life into the newborn. The frog was a natural emblem of teeming fertility and new life, appearing in vast numbers as the Nile flood receded, and the hieroglyph of the tadpole stood for the number 100,000 (abundance). In the divine-birth cycle Heqet partners the creator-potter **[[khnum]]**: as Khnum fashions the child's body and its *ka* on his wheel, **Heqet gives it the breath of life** and protects the mother — in the *Westcar Papyrus* she and a band of goddesses disguise themselves as travelling musicians to deliver the future kings of the Fifth Dynasty. Because she governs both the threshold of birth and (by extension) of rebirth, Heqet's amulets were worn by pregnant women, and her frog-form later carried into Coptic Christian Egypt as an emblem of resurrection.
