# Agni

## Identity
Agni is the Vedic fire-god, second only to Indra in number of Rig Vedic hymns addressed to him (~200 of ~1,028). He is the sacrificial fire personified, the divine mediator who carries human offerings up to the gods and embodies the gods' presence at the sacrifice. The opening hymn of the entire Rig Veda (1.1) is to Agni, marking his structural primacy: "Agni I praise, the household priest, the divine ministrant of the sacrifice." He is *jātavedas*, "knower of all beings"; he is born in many forms (lightning, sun, hearth-fire, ritual fire, the digestive fire within the body) — a unity in multiplicity.

In the Avestan world the cognate principle survives as *Ātar*, the holy fire central to Zoroastrian ritual; the relationship is one of clearly shared Indo-Iranian inheritance. Agni's structural role — the *zaotar* (Vedic *hotṛ*) priest's fire — and the haoma/soma-fire ritual complex link Avestan and Vedic ritual at the deepest level. Agni continues into classical Hinduism as a directional guardian (lord of the southeast) and as the principle of sacrificial efficacy.
