# Cybele

## Identity
Cybele is the great **mountain-mother goddess of Phrygia** (central Anatolia), whose cult spread across the Greek and Roman worlds through the Hellenistic and Imperial periods. Her name and core iconography (lions, mountain enthronement, mural crown) descend from the Bronze-Age Anatolian "Kubaba" of the Neo-Hittite city-states. In Greek territory she was identified with the mother of the Olympian gods, **Rhea**. In Rome she was imported officially in **-204 BCE** as **Magna Mater Deum Idaea** ("Great Idaean Mother of the Gods") on the recommendation of the Sibylline books, to help defeat Hannibal — the *first state-sponsored eastern mystery cult* in Rome.

## Earliest attestation
- Neo-Hittite "Kubaba" / *Mātar Kubileya* inscriptions from Carchemish, ~-1000.
- Phrygian rock-cut monuments at Pessinus, Midas City, the "Tomb of Midas" — ~-700/-500.
- Pindar's *Dithyramb 2* and Euripides' *Bacchae*: Greek-period attestations of Cybele as Mother of the Gods.
- **Rome -204**: the cult is officially transferred from Pessinus (Phrygia) to Rome; the cult-stone (a sacred meteorite) is processed up the Palatine and housed in a new temple (Livy 29.10–14).

## Mythological role
The defining Cybele-Attis narrative: Attis, a beautiful Phrygian youth (sometimes Cybele's lover, sometimes her consort, sometimes a self-mutilating priest figure), violates Cybele's wishes; in divinely-induced madness, he castrates himself under a pine tree and dies; Cybele mourns, and the resurrection or transformation of Attis is annually re-enacted. The Roman festival cycle of *Hilaria* (March 22–25), with mourning and ecstatic celebration of Attis's renewal, became one of the most prominent imperial-era religious cycles.

The cult's most controversial element was the **Galli** — eunuch priests who self-castrated in ecstatic ritual, then served as professional cult attendants. Roman aristocrats found this disturbing (early Roman citizens were forbidden by law from becoming Galli) but the cult flourished anyway.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[rhea]]** — *ancient-identification*: standard Greek interpretatio from late archaic onward.
- **[[demeter]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: both are mother / fertility goddesses, parallel but not equated.
- **[[isis-hellenistic]]** — *ancient-identification*: Apuleius *Met.* 11.5 has Isis name herself "Mother of the Gods" among many other names — explicit syncretic identification.
- **[[inanna-sumerian]]** / **[[ishtar-akkadian]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Eastern Mediterranean mother-goddesses with overlapping fertility-and-wild-nature domains.

## Iconography
- Throned, enthroned between two lions (or with lion at feet)
- Mural crown (turreted city-wall crown — *corona muralis*)
- Tympanum (frame drum) in hand
- Pine tree (Attis-associated)
- Sacred stone (the Pessinus betyl)

## Disputes
- **Continuity with Anatolian Kubaba.** Linguistic and iconographic continuity is well-documented (Roller 1999); the *theological* continuity (what cult content survived from the Neo-Hittites to Hellenistic-Roman times) is less clear.
- **Galli — historical practice.** Was self-castration the *normal* induction into the cult, or a rare-but-attention-getting feature? Mainstream: the practice was real and shocking but not universal among male cult-adherents.
- **Christian parallels.** The Attis dying-and-rising motif is sometimes invoked in popular literature as a Christian-parallel "mystery cult precursor." Modern specialists are cautious: *parallel-motif* not direct dependence; the Attis "resurrection" is more transformation/preservation than physical resurrection in the strict sense (Mettinger 2001 is judicious).
