# Tellus

Tellus (**Tellus Mater**, "Mother Earth"; also **Terra Mater**) is the Roman goddess of the **earth itself** — the productive ground from which crops rise and into which the dead return. She is invoked in the oldest strata of Roman agrarian religion: at the **Fordicidia** (15 April) pregnant cows were sacrificed to her to ensure the fertility of fields and herds, and she is paired with **[[ceres-roman|Ceres]]** in the sowing-festival (Feriae Sementivae), the earth and the grain together. Because the dead lie in her, she also receives chthonic and oath-binding associations — to swear by Tellus was to swear by the ground that holds all. Her most famous image is the serene, fruit-laden mother of the **Ara Pacis** relief, a child on each knee, embodying the peace and plenty of the Augustan earth. The Romans conventionally identified her with the Greek **[[gaia|Gaia]]**, the personified earth.
