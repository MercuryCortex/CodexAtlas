# Horus

Horus — the Egyptian falcon-god — is two distinct deities that merged in historical Egyptian theology. **Horus the Elder** (Haroeris) is an Early Dynastic sky-god, his name meaning "the Distant One"; he is the sky as falcon, with the sun and moon as his two eyes. **Horus son of Isis** (Harsiesis) is the avenging child of [[osiris]] and [[isis]]; conceived from his murdered father's revived corpse, hidden in the marshes by his mother, and grown to fight [[set]] for the kingship of Egypt. By the Old Kingdom these two Horuses had already merged, and "Horus" became the comprehensive Egyptian theological frame for divine kingship: **the living pharaoh is Horus**, the dead pharaoh is Osiris, and each new succession recapitulates Horus's victory over Set and inheritance of Osiris's throne. The very first sequence of Egyptian royal nomenclature is the "Horus name," attested on serekh-frames already in late Predynastic and Early Dynastic monuments.

## Mythological role
The **Contendings of Horus and Seth** — preserved in a New Kingdom papyrus (Chester Beatty Papyrus I, ~1160 BCE) — is the most fully narrative version of the inheritance dispute: Horus and Seth contend before the divine tribunal for 80 years; they undergo a series of physical and sexual contests; eventually Osiris from the underworld intervenes and Horus is awarded the kingship of Egypt, while Set is given the desert. The **Eye of Horus** (*udjat*) — the eye Set tore out and Thoth restored — became one of the most reproduced Egyptian protective symbols. At Edfu, the largest and best-preserved Ptolemaic-era Egyptian temple is dedicated to Horus; its annual festivals dramatized Horus's victory over Set in cultic re-enactment.
