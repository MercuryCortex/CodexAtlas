# Amitabha Buddha

The celestial Buddha of *Sukhāvatī*, the Western Pure Land — a paradise realm where conditions for the final attainment of Buddhahood are uniquely favorable. In the *Larger Sukhavativyuha Sutra* (~+100–200), the bodhisattva Dharmākara makes 48 vows; on attaining Buddhahood as Amitābha, he established Sukhāvatī, and his 18th vow promises rebirth there to anyone who calls his name in faith.

This soteriology — salvation by faith and devotional invocation, *not* by rigorous self-effort — is the foundation of East Asian Pure Land Buddhism (Chinese *Jingtu*, Japanese *Jōdo Shū* and *Jōdo Shinshū*). Hōnen (~+1175) and Shinran (~+1200) developed Japanese Pure Land into the dominant popular form of Buddhism in Japan. The *nembutsu* practice (chanting *Namo Amida Butsu*) is the primary spiritual discipline.

In Vajrayana, Amitabha is one of the Five Dhyāni Buddhas (associated with the western direction, the *padma* / lotus family, transformation of desire into discernment).

Pure Land Buddhism is the largest school of Buddhism by adherents globally. Its devotional / salvation-by-grace structure has been compared (carefully) to Christian soteriology; the comparative-religion literature is large.
