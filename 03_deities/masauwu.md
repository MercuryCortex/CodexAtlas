# Maasaw

Maasaw (also **Masauwu**; "Skeleton Man") is among the most important deities of the **Hopi** of the Arizona mesas — the god of **death, fire, and the earth's surface**, and the caretaker-owner of the present world. He is a paradoxical figure who stands exactly on the **boundary of life and death**: terrifying in his blood-spotted skull-mask and a being of the night and the underworld, yet also the giver of fire and of the planting-stick, a benefactor and a model of humility. In the Hopi **emergence** cosmology, when the people climb up from the previous worlds into this Fourth World, it is **Maasaw** whom they find already here as its owner; he grants them permission to live upon the earth and lays out the terms — a hard life of farming the high desert with a digging-stick, lived in humility and proper ceremony — and shows them the directions of their migrations. As lord of both the harvest of the surface and the death that waits below, Maasaw embodies the Hopi understanding that life on this world is borrowed, demanding, and bracketed by death.
