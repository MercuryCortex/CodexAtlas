# The Evil God (Cathar Rex Mundi)

## Identity
The **evil cosmic principle** in Cathar theology — the **King of the World** (*Rex Mundi*), creator of the material world, the trap of bodily existence, and identified by the **absolute Cathar dualists** with the God of the Old Testament. This deity-figure closes a 3-ref dead-link that the existing [[the-good-god-cathar]] node has pointed to since the Templar batch landed.

## Mythological role
**Two Cathar dualisms**:

1. **Absolute dualism** (the Albigensian school of John of Lugio, codified in *[[phase-5-023-liber-de-duobus-principiis|Liber de duobus principiis]]*) — Rex Mundi is **coeternal** and independent of the Good God; matter is intrinsically and irredeemably evil.
2. **Moderate dualism** (the Concorezzan / Bogomil-derived school, [[phase-5-020-interrogatio-iohannis|*Interrogatio Iohannis*]]) — Rex Mundi (= Satanael) is the **firstborn son** of the Good God who rebelled and was given the material world for a time; eventually he will be defeated.

**Cathar anti-Old-Testament polemic**: in absolute dualist hands, the Old Testament god — who creates matter, kills the firstborn of Egypt, commands the herem against the Canaanites — is *Rex Mundi*; the Good Father of Jesus in the New Testament is the Good God. This polemical identification is precisely **Marcionite** in form (~144 CE; cf. Marcion's rejection of the Old Testament god) — the medieval-Latin survival of one of the oldest Christian dualistic readings.
