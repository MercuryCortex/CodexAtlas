# Mēness

Mēness ("Moon") is the Baltic moon deity — masculine in the Baltic tradition, in contrast to the feminine moon deities of most other Indo-European branches (Greek Selene, Roman Luna, Vedic Chandra are all feminine or have feminine associations). This gender inversion is significant for comparative Indo-European mythology: paired with [[saule]] (the sun goddess, also exceptional for being feminine rather than masculine), the Baltic sun-female/moon-male system is the inverse of the Greek/Vedic norm and contributes key evidence to debates about the original PIE solar/lunar gender assignment.

The Saule-Mēness mythological relationship is one of cosmic domestic drama: they are paired as sun-wife and moon-husband, but Mēness is unfaithful (taking Auseklis, the morning star, as a companion), and [[perkunas]] punishes him by striking him with a sword — producing the waning moon phases as visible wounds. The celestial mechanics of the lunar cycle are thus explained through divine marital conflict and divine punishment.
