# Jupiter

## Identity
Jupiter (Latin *Iuppiter*) is the supreme sky-and-thunder god of Roman state religion and the tutelary deity of Rome and its empire. As **Iuppiter Optimus Maximus** ("Jupiter Best and Greatest") he presided from the Capitoline temple atop the Capitoline Hill in the **Capitoline Triad** with his consort Juno and the goddess Minerva. The Etruscan-period temple was traditionally dedicated in -509, the founding year of the Roman Republic; multiple rebuildings (after fires in -83, +69, +80) kept the Capitoline cult central to Roman state ceremony until the Theodosian dismantling of pagan state cult (+391/+392).

## Earliest attestation
- The name *Iuppiter* derives from a vocative formula *Iū-piter* = "Sky-Father", cognate with Vedic *Dyaus Pitar*, Greek *Zeus pater*, and the Proto-Indo-European *Dyēus phater*. This makes Jupiter linguistically the most directly preserved PIE inheritance among the major Roman deities.
- The Capitoline temple is traditionally dated to -509; its remains are partially excavated.
- Continuous attestation in Roman literature from Plautus (~-200) through Late Antiquity.

## Mythological role
Roman Jupiter's mythology is largely *imported from Greek Zeus* via *interpretatio Graeca*; the native Roman tradition emphasized state-cult functions over narrative myth. Jupiter sanctioned (a) Roman political authority — magistrates took their auspices from Jupiter; (b) oaths and treaties — *Iuppiter Lapis* (Jupiter as Stone, possibly a meteorite) was sworn upon for binding agreements; (c) military victory — Roman generals returning in triumph processed to the Capitoline temple to dedicate spoils to Jupiter; (d) the cosmic order of the heavens.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[zeus]]** — *ancient-identification*: complete identification by the Hellenizing late Republic; Roman literary mythology *is* Greek mythology under Latin names from Ennius onward.
- **[[dyaus-pita]]** (Vedic) — *scholarly-parallel*: shared PIE inheritance; both names derive from *Dyēus phater*. This is a *linguistic-reconstructive* identification, not an *ancient cultic* one.

## Iconography
- Mature bearded male in the Greek Zeus-iconographic type
- Thunderbolt in right hand
- Eagle as attendant
- Oak (sacred tree; the Capitoline crown of oak leaves)
- Throne with scepter

## Disputes
- **Native Roman vs. Hellenized.** Older scholarship (Wissowa) sharply distinguished "original" Roman religion from Hellenistic overlay; modern scholarship (Beard-North-Price, Rüpke) treats Roman religion as continuously syncretistic and developmental, resistant to a clean "pre-Greek" reconstruction.
- **Christian replacement.** With Theodosius's anti-pagan legislation, Jupiter's state cult ended; the Capitoline became a Christian site. Some scholars trace continuity from Iuppiter-Optimus-Maximus civic-supremacy iconography into early Christian Christ-Pantokrator imagery.
