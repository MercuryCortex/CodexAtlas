# Nezha

Nezha (哪吒) is one of the most beloved deities of Chinese folk religion — an eternally **youthful, rebellious warrior-god** who quells demons, depicted as a boy speeding on **Wind-Fire Wheels** and wielding the Cosmic Ring and a fire-tipped spear. His legend, fixed in the Ming novels *Investiture of the Gods* (*Fengshen Yanyi*) and *Journey to the West*, is a startling tale of filial rupture and restoration: the precocious Nezha slays a dragon-prince of the sea, brings the Dragon Kings' wrath on his family, and in atonement **returns his own flesh and bones to his parents** (suicide as the ultimate filial repayment) — only to be reborn from a **lotus** by his master Taiyi Zhenren, after which he furiously turns on his father Li Jing until the Buddha/Heaven reconciles them. Scholarship (Shahar) traces his ultimate origin to the **Buddhist guardian-yaksha Nalakūbara**, sinicized and folded into Daoist-folk worship — a vivid India-to-China transmission. As **Marshal of the Central Altar**, he is a frontline exorcistic protector invoked in temple processions and by spirit-mediums across the Chinese world.
