# Geshtinanna

## Identity
Geshtinanna ("Vine of Heaven") is the sister of [[dumuzi-tammuz]] and the divine scribe-and-record-keeper of the netherworld. Her best-known role is as the second-half-of-the-year substitute for Dumuzi: in *Inanna's Descent to the Netherworld* ([[phase-1-017-descent-of-inanna]]), after [[inanna-sumerian]] returns from the underworld and demands a substitute, Dumuzi is taken; his sister Geshtinanna offers herself, and in the resolution she and Dumuzi rotate — Dumuzi in the netherworld for half the year, Geshtinanna for the other half. This rotation is the etiology of the seasonal alternation of vegetation and the dual nature of the [[dying-rising-god]] motif in the Mesopotamian corpus: not one rising god but a sibling-pair in alternating descent. Her secondary identity as netherworld scribe (Akkadian Belet-Seri, "Lady of the Steppe") is documented in *Gilgamesh* Tablet VII where she records the names of the dead.

## Earliest attestation
- Early Dynastic god lists; she appears in Lagashite cultic records from the mid-third millennium.
- Multiple Sumerian compositions of the Dumuzi-cycle preserve her: *Inanna's Descent*, *Dumuzi's Dream*, *Dumuzi and Geshtinanna*, the lament *In the Desert by the Early Grass*.
- The Standard Babylonian Gilgamesh Tablet VII names Belet-Seri (= Geshtinanna) as scribe of the netherworld in Enkidu's dream of death.
- Old Babylonian lament-literature uses her as a paradigm of the mourning sister.

## Mythological role
- ***Inanna's Descent to the Netherworld*** ([[phase-1-017-descent-of-inanna]]): the central source. After Inanna's return, the demons sent to claim her substitute first encounter Geshtinanna and find her in mourning for Dumuzi; they pass over her. They then find Dumuzi in luxurious unconcern and seize him. In the closing lines, Geshtinanna petitions Inanna for the substitution arrangement that gives Dumuzi (and her) half a year above and half below.
- ***Dumuzi's Dream***: Dumuzi tells Geshtinanna a foreboding dream; she interprets it as foretelling his death. Her role as dream-interpreter parallels [[nanshe-sumerian]]'s.
- ***In the Desert by the Early Grass***: a long Sumerian lament in which Geshtinanna and the mother Duttur mourn Dumuzi. This composition gives Geshtinanna some of the most extended laments of any Sumerian deity.
- ***Netherworld scribe (as Belet-Seri)***: she keeps the tablets of the dead in the Akkadian afterlife, recording the dead as they arrive.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[belet-seri-akkadian]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Akkadian form, attested in *Gilgamesh* Tablet VII and Mesopotamian funerary contexts. The two are the same goddess at the boundary of Sumerian and Akkadian theology.
- The seasonal-substitution motif is sometimes scholarly-paralleled with Persephone (Greek) and Adonis (Greek/Phoenician/Egyptian) cycles; these are *scholarly-parallel* claims regarding the [[dying-rising-god]] complex.

## Iconography
Limited iconographic record. Where preserved, she appears as a seated goddess in horned crown; in some Lagashite seals she holds a stylus identifying her as netherworld scribe.

## Disputes
- **Mother's identity**. Duttur (Dumuzi's mother) versus Ninsun appears variably; the OB substitution texts more often name Duttur, the literary texts Ninsun.
- **Reading "Belili" as a name of Geshtinanna**. Some Akkadian sources name a "Belili" who appears in Dumuzi-substitution contexts; whether this is Geshtinanna under another name or a separate associated goddess is debated (Alster 1999 leans toward identity; older scholarship preferred distinction).
