# Turan

## Identity
Turan is the **Etruscan goddess of love, beauty, and vitality** — the patroness of the city of **Vulci (Velch)** and the Etruscan counterpart of the Greek [[aphrodite-greek]] and the Roman [[venus-roman]]. Her name carries the sense of "mistress" or "lady," and she embodies desire, fertility, and flourishing life.

## Earliest attestation
Best known from **engraved bronze mirrors** (6th–2nd c. BCE), where she appears amid scenes of love and myth, attended by the winged spirits called **Lasas**, and accompanied by swans and doves.

## Mythological role
Turan presides over love and its myths in the Etruscan repertoire: she appears at the **Judgement of Paris** in Aphrodite's role, and her youthful consort **Atunis** is the Etruscan **Adonis**, the beautiful youth beloved and mourned. The Lasas, her winged female attendants, are a distinctively Etruscan elaboration of the love-goddess's retinue.

## Cross-tradition
Turan is the Etruscan member of the **love-goddess** that runs Aphrodite → Turan → Venus across the ancient Mediterranean, ultimately reaching back to the Near Eastern Ishtar/Astarte. Her Atunis-Adonis is part of the dying-and-returning youth complex.

## Iconography
A beautiful winged or robed goddess with mirror, swan, and dove, surrounded by the Lasas.
