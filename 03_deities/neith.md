# Neith

## Identity
Neith (Egyptian *Nt*) is one of the **oldest attested Egyptian deities**, a Predynastic warrior- and creator-goddess whose cult at **Saïs** in the western Delta becomes politically dominant during the Saite Period (26th Dynasty, c. -664 to -525), the last great independent Egyptian dynasty before the Persian conquest. She is the **primordial weaver** who weaves the world into being, the **mother of [[ra]]** in some Saite theological traditions, and a goddess of warfare with crossed arrows and shield as her principal emblem. For the Codex Atlas's Greek-Egypt mission Neith is doubly important: (1) she is identified with **[[athena]]** by Plato's Saite priest in *[[phase-3-022-plato-timaeus-critias-atlantis]]* — the cross-tradition identification that frames the entire Atlantis dialogue; (2) the Saite Period of her cult-prominence is the era of **intensive Greek-Egyptian contact** (Naucratis, Greek mercenaries, Saite pharaohs employing Greek mercenaries against Assyria and Persia).

## Earliest attestation
- Earliest evidence: **Predynastic period (~-3200 to -3000)** — Neith's name appears on royal cartouches of First Dynasty queens (Neith-hotep, possibly wife of Narmer / Aha; Mer-Neith of the 1st Dynasty). She is among the very oldest attested Egyptian deities.
- Old Kingdom: cult-temple at Saïs continuously operative.
- **Saite Period (26th Dynasty, ~-664 to -525)** — Neith becomes Egypt's state high-goddess as Saïs becomes the capital. Massive temple-building; Saite theology articulates Neith as the primordial creator-mother whose ancient title is **"the one who is, who was, who will be"** (Esna temple text echoing Saite theology) — strikingly resonant with later Hellenistic and Christian self-identifications of the deity.
- Persian, Ptolemaic, and Roman periods: cult continues, deeply embedded in Greek-Egyptian intellectual exchange (Plato's Saite priest narrative).

## Mythological role
- **Primordial weaver**: Neith weaves the cosmos into existence; her hieroglyph (a weaver's shuttle) is itself her theological signature.
- **Mother of Ra**: in Saite theology she is the self-existent primordial waters from which Ra rises; her famous epithet "the one who came into being by herself" is one of the most explicitly **self-existent** divine statements in any ancient tradition.
- **Warrior**: she is depicted with crossed arrows and shield; the war-goddess role is well-attested from the earliest periods.
- **Funerary**: one of the four protective goddesses (with Isis, Nephthys, Selket) of the canopic jars containing the viscera of the embalmed dead.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[athena]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Saite priest in **Plato's *[[phase-3-022-plato-timaeus-critias-atlantis]]* 21E** explicitly identifies the Saite goddess Neith with the Athenian Athena: "The name of the goddess of the Egyptian city of Saïs in their language is Neith, in Greek, as they affirm, Athena. They are very friendly to the Athenians, and consider themselves in some sort akin to our people." [[herodotus]] *Histories* 2.59, 2.62, 2.83 also equates Neith with Athena. **This is one of the structurally most important Greek-Egyptian theological identifications**, because it sits at the foundation of the Atlantis narrative.
- **[[isis-egyptian]]** — *parallel-motif* + late *syncretic-identification*: in the late period and Greco-Roman Egypt, Isis absorbs Neith's universal-mother functions.
- **Self-existent ground of being** — *scholarly-parallel*: Neith's "the one who is, who was, who will be" epithet is structurally parallel to YHWH's "I am that I am" (Exodus 3:14) and to Revelation 1:8 ("him who is and who was and who is to come"). Direct genealogical transmission is unprovable but the formal parallel is striking and is regularly noted (Hornung 1982; Assmann 2002).

## Neith as Primordial Creator: A Matriarchal Creation Theology
Neith's creation theology at Saïs represents a **third Egyptian creation school** alongside the Heliopolitan (*Atum* masturbating/spitting the first gods into being) and the Memphite (*Ptah* creating by speech and thought) traditions. In Saite theology, **Neith is the primordial creator who existed before creation**, who weaves the world into being on her cosmic loom, and who speaks the names of things to make them real. This is a matriarchal creation framework: the first principle is feminine, self-existent, and generative through her own nature — not through an act of masculine self-fertilization (Atum) or masculine divine word (Ptah).

Her Esna temple inscription (Ptolemaic-Roman period, though encoding earlier Saite theology) describes her as **"the great cow who gave birth to Ra"** — she is the primordial generative medium from which the solar principle itself arises. The title "the one who came into being by herself" (*ḥr wsf n ḏs.f*) makes Neith one of the very few ancient deities who is fully **self-existent** — not born from a parent, not emanating from a prior principle, but simply existing prior to existence itself. The parallel with Atum's self-creation (who masturbates creation into being in the Heliopolitan theology) is striking: both are self-caused first principles. But Neith's self-existence is not narrated as an act; it is simply a property. She is the unarticulated prior ground.

## MASSIVE WIN: Neith → Athena — The Most Explicitly Documented Egyptian-Greek Divine Equation
The identification of Neith with [[athena]] is not an inference or a scholarly hypothesis — it is explicitly documented in two of antiquity's most authoritative sources:

1. **Herodotus** (*Histories* 2.28, 2.59, 2.62, 2.83, c. 430 BCE, Tier-1 primary source): explicitly identifies Neith as "Athena" at multiple points in his Egypt book. Herodotus's equation was based on direct observation of Egyptian cult and conversations with Egyptian priests.

2. **Plato** (*Timaeus* 21e, *Critias* 108e–109a, c. 360 BCE, Tier-1 primary source): the Saite priest Sonchis tells Solon (c. 590 BCE) that the patron goddess of Saïs is Neith, "whom in Greek you call Athena." Plato frames the entire Atlantis narrative within this identification: the Greeks and Saïtes are "kin" because they share the same patron goddess. **This cross-tradition divine identification is the structural hinge of the Atlantis dialogue** — the account of Atlantis is a Saite-priestly document that comes to Greece via the Neith-Athena identification authorizing the Saite priests to speak to Solon as to a kinsman.

The equation has three dimensions:
- **Functional parallel**: both Neith and Athena are war-goddesses AND wisdom-goddesses — an unusual combination that makes the identification compelling on first principles (most war-goddesses, like Ares/Mars, are not wisdom figures; most wisdom figures, like Apollo, are not war figures)
- **Documentary transmission**: the Solon→Plato chain gives the identification a traceable historical genealogy — it is not Plato's projection but a Saite-priestly identification that pre-dates Plato by c. 230 years
- **Institutional context**: Solon's visit to Saïs (c. 590 BCE) coincides with the Saite-period revival of Neith's cult; the great Saïs temple was built by Psamtik I (26th Dynasty); Greek merchants and mercenaries had been living in Naucratis (a Greek trading colony in the Nile Delta, c. 650 BCE onward) — the cultural contacts that produced the Neith-Athena identification are archaeologically attested

## Neith as Androgynous or Self-Generating Creator
Neith occupies a rare position in the typology of primordial beings: she is sometimes described as **androgynous or self-generating**. The title "great cow who gave birth to Ra" casts her in a purely feminine generative role; but other Saite texts describe her as encompassing both male and female principles, making her effectively gender-transcendent rather than gendered-feminine. This places her in the cross-tradition archetype of the **self-caused primordial being who is beyond the male/female distinction**:

- **Atum** (Egyptian, Heliopolitan): self-created by masturbation; explicitly both father and mother of the gods
- **Hermopolitan Ogdoad**: four male-female pairs (eight primordial principles), without a single gendered first creator
- **Aristotle's Unmoved Mover**: beyond all relational categories including gender
- **Neoplatonic One** (Plotinus *Enneads* VI.9): explicitly beyond all attributes including gender; the One does not *have* goodness, does not *have* being — it simply *is* the prior condition; similarly Neith simply *is* the prior condition of creation

Neith's androgyny is not a minor detail: it places her in the small set of first-principles in world theology that are explicitly declared beyond the gender that characterizes the beings they generate.

## Iconography
- Tall feminine figure in long sheath dress
- **Red crown of Lower Egypt** (the *deshret*) — Neith's signature crown
- **Crossed arrows and shield** above the head or held in the hand — her hieroglyphic signifier and martial attribute
- **Weaver's shuttle** as alternative symbolic emblem
- Sometimes holding bow and arrows

## Disputes
- **Plato's reliability.** Whether the Saite priest's identification (Neith = Athena) reflects genuine Saite-priestly theology or Plato's Athenian projection is debated. Most current scholars accept that the equation *was* made by Saite priests in contact with Greeks (Naucratis-era documentary evidence supports it); Plato's *use* of it for the Atlantis frame is his own.
- **"The one who is, who was, who will be" — date.** The Esna temple text bearing this Neith-epithet is Greco-Roman (Ptolemaic-Roman), so the epithet's age is uncertain; it may be Saite or earlier, or it may be a late synthesis influenced by intervening religious developments.
