# Neith

## Identity
Neith (Egyptian *Nt*) is one of the **oldest attested Egyptian deities**, a Predynastic warrior- and creator-goddess whose cult at **Saïs** in the western Delta becomes politically dominant during the Saite Period (26th Dynasty, c. -664 to -525), the last great independent Egyptian dynasty before the Persian conquest. She is the **primordial weaver** who weaves the world into being, the **mother of [[ra]]** in some Saite theological traditions, and a goddess of warfare with crossed arrows and shield as her principal emblem. For the Codex Atlas's Greek-Egypt mission Neith is doubly important: (1) she is identified with **[[athena]]** by Plato's Saite priest in *[[phase-3-022-plato-timaeus-critias-atlantis]]* — the cross-tradition identification that frames the entire Atlantis dialogue; (2) the Saite Period of her cult-prominence is the era of **intensive Greek-Egyptian contact** (Naucratis, Greek mercenaries, Saite pharaohs employing Greek mercenaries against Assyria and Persia).

## Earliest attestation
- Earliest evidence: **Predynastic period (~-3200 to -3000)** — Neith's name appears on royal cartouches of First Dynasty queens (Neith-hotep, possibly wife of Narmer / Aha; Mer-Neith of the 1st Dynasty). She is among the very oldest attested Egyptian deities.
- Old Kingdom: cult-temple at Saïs continuously operative.
- **Saite Period (26th Dynasty, ~-664 to -525)** — Neith becomes Egypt's state high-goddess as Saïs becomes the capital. Massive temple-building; Saite theology articulates Neith as the primordial creator-mother whose ancient title is **"the one who is, who was, who will be"** (Esna temple text echoing Saite theology) — strikingly resonant with later Hellenistic and Christian self-identifications of the deity.
- Persian, Ptolemaic, and Roman periods: cult continues, deeply embedded in Greek-Egyptian intellectual exchange (Plato's Saite priest narrative).

## Mythological role
- **Primordial weaver**: Neith weaves the cosmos into existence; her hieroglyph (a weaver's shuttle) is itself her theological signature.
- **Mother of Ra**: in Saite theology she is the self-existent primordial waters from which Ra rises; her famous epithet "the one who came into being by herself" is one of the most explicitly **self-existent** divine statements in any ancient tradition.
- **Warrior**: she is depicted with crossed arrows and shield; the war-goddess role is well-attested from the earliest periods.
- **Funerary**: one of the four protective goddesses (with Isis, Nephthys, Selket) of the canopic jars containing the viscera of the embalmed dead.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[athena]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Saite priest in **Plato's *[[phase-3-022-plato-timaeus-critias-atlantis]]* 21E** explicitly identifies the Saite goddess Neith with the Athenian Athena: "The name of the goddess of the Egyptian city of Saïs in their language is Neith, in Greek, as they affirm, Athena. They are very friendly to the Athenians, and consider themselves in some sort akin to our people." [[herodotus]] *Histories* 2.59, 2.62, 2.83 also equates Neith with Athena. **This is one of the structurally most important Greek-Egyptian theological identifications**, because it sits at the foundation of the Atlantis narrative.
- **[[isis]]** — *parallel-motif* + late *syncretic-identification*: in the late period and Greco-Roman Egypt, Isis absorbs Neith's universal-mother functions.
- **Self-existent ground of being** — *scholarly-parallel*: Neith's "the one who is, who was, who will be" epithet is structurally parallel to YHWH's "I am that I am" (Exodus 3:14) and to Revelation 1:8 ("him who is and who was and who is to come"). Direct genealogical transmission is unprovable but the formal parallel is striking and is regularly noted (Hornung 1982; Assmann 2002).

## Iconography
- Tall feminine figure in long sheath dress
- **Red crown of Lower Egypt** (the *deshret*) — Neith's signature crown
- **Crossed arrows and shield** above the head or held in the hand — her hieroglyphic signifier and martial attribute
- **Weaver's shuttle** as alternative symbolic emblem
- Sometimes holding bow and arrows

## Disputes
- **Plato's reliability.** Whether the Saite priest's identification (Neith = Athena) reflects genuine Saite-priestly theology or Plato's Athenian projection is debated. Most current scholars accept that the equation *was* made by Saite priests in contact with Greeks (Naucratis-era documentary evidence supports it); Plato's *use* of it for the Atlantis frame is his own.
- **"The one who is, who was, who will be" — date.** The Esna temple text bearing this Neith-epithet is Greco-Roman (Ptolemaic-Roman), so the epithet's age is uncertain; it may be Saite or earlier, or it may be a late synthesis influenced by intervening religious developments.
