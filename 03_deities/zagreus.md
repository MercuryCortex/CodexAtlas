# Zagreus

## Identity
Zagreus is the *first Dionysus* of Orphic theology — son of Zeus and Persephone, born in primordial time, marked by Zeus as heir of cosmic kingship. The Titans, prompted by Hera, distracted the child-god with toys (most famously a mirror), dismembered him, and ate his flesh. Zeus consumed the still-beating heart (or in some variants Athena rescued it), and from the Titans' ashes — after Zeus blasted them with thunder for the crime — humans were produced. Hence the dual nature of humanity in Orphic anthropogony: Titanic (heavy, evil substance) plus Dionysian (divine substance from the consumed god). The myth grounds the [[soul-as-spark]] anthropology of later Western religious imagination.

## Earliest attestation
- Hints in Pindar, Aeschylus, and Plato (oblique references to Orphic stories).
- Onomacritus (6th c. BCE Athens) is the traditional collator of Orphic poetry, though his texts are lost.
- Coherent narrative texts: late and fragmentary. The fullest version is in **Olympiodorus** (6th c. CE) *On Plato's Phaedo* 1.3 — a Neoplatonist witness.
- Indirect early evidence: Bacchic gold tablets (~-400/-200) presuppose an anthropogony in which the soul has divine origin; the Derveni Papyrus (~-340) and Olbia bone tablet inscriptions reflect Orphic-Bacchic milieu.

## Mythological role
- **Primordial child-god dismembered.** The dismemberment narrative is Zagreus's defining act; his death is the ground of human nature.
- **Reincarnation in Dionysus son of Semele.** Orphic theology identifies the Theban Dionysus (son of Zeus and Semele) as the resurrection / continuation of Zagreus. The single Dionysian substance persists through dismemberment and rebirth.
- **Salvation pattern.** Orphic and Bacchic mystery initiation provides the means for the divine spark in humans to recognize itself, escape the cycle of rebirth, and return to its source.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[dionysus]] / [[dionysus-mystery]]** — *ancient-identification*: Zagreus is the Orphic *first* Dionysus; the Theban Dionysus is the second instantiation.

## Disputes
- **Antiquity of the coherent myth.** Edmonds (2013) argues persuasively that the *systematic* Zagreus-anthropogony is a late (perhaps Neoplatonic) synthesis of older fragmentary motifs; what survives in classical-period sources may not yet have been combined. Bernabé and others defend substantial pre-Hellenistic provenance. The question turns on what one accepts as evidence for *narrative coherence* vs. allusion.
- **Christian-Dionysian parallel.** The dismembered-and-restored child-god has been claimed (Frazer 1890; popular comparative-religion writers) as a Christian-parallel "dying-and-rising god." Modern scholarship (Mettinger 2001) is cautious: Zagreus is dismembered, *the divine substance* persists, but a clear *resurrection of the same Zagreus-individual* is not in the well-attested sources.
