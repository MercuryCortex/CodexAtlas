# Eileithyia

Eileithyia (Greek *Eileíthyia*) is the Greek goddess of **childbirth** — the divine midwife who presides over labour and, crucially, who can either *bring on* or *hold back* the birth. Daughter of **[[zeus]]** and **[[hera]]** (Hesiod, *Theogony* 922), she is one of the **earliest-attested Greek deities**, named already in Mycenaean Linear B as *e-re-u-ti-ja* (Knossos tablet KN Gg 705), with an ancient cave-sanctuary at Amnisos on Crete. Her power over the threshold of birth makes her a tool of divine jealousy in myth: at Hera's command she **delays the labour of Alcmene** to imperil the birth of Heracles, and in the *Homeric Hymn to Apollo* she is kept away from Delos so that **[[leto]]** cannot deliver Apollo until the other goddesses bribe her to come. Her function overlaps **[[artemis]]** and Hera-as-Eileithyia, and the Romans called her **Lucina** ("she who brings to light"). The Greeks identified her with the Egyptian vulture-goddess **[[nekhbet]]**, renaming Nekhbet's city *Eileithyiaspolis*.
