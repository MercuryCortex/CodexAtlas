# Baron Samedi

## Identity
Head of the *Gede* (Ghede) nasyon — the *lwa* of the dead, the cemetery, and the ancestors. Baron Samedi (also Baron Saturday, also Bawon Lakwa "Baron of the Cross" — the cross of the cemetery — and Bawon Simityè "Baron of the Cemetery") is the most publicly visible of the *lwa* and the most beloved despite (or because of) his association with death. The Gede are humorous, obscene, irreverent — they tell rude jokes during possession, drink rum laced with chili peppers (which only a *lwa* could tolerate), and joke about death precisely because they have moved beyond fear of it. Baron is consort of Maman Brigitte, the white-skinned female *lwa* of the cemetery's first interred woman.

## Earliest attestation
Documented in Haitian sources from 19th c. onward; standard treatment in Métraux 1959; Cosentino's *Sacred Arts of Haitian Vodou* (1995) provides extensive iconographic and ethnographic detail.

## Mythological role
Baron Samedi waits at the entrance of every cemetery; no one can enter the realm of the dead without his permission. He is petitioned for terminally ill — Baron has the power to refuse to take the dying, restoring health, but the petitioner must convince him it's worth the candle. He is also patron of the November 1–2 Fèt Gede (Day of the Dead / All Saints / All Souls), when Haitians visit cemeteries with offerings.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Saint Martin de Porres** — *folk-syncretism* (some lineages): the Black Peruvian Dominican saint pairs with Baron.
- **Saint Gerard / Saint Expedite** — *folk-syncretism* (regional variation).
- **Anglophone Baron Samedi popular-cultural reception** (James Bond *Live and Let Die*, etc.) — *flag-misappropriation*: the Western popular depiction is a colonial-horror stereotype not representative of the actual *lwa*'s function in Vodou.

## Iconography
Top hat (often black, sometimes white); black tuxedo coat; dark sunglasses (often with one lens missing — Baron sees both worlds at once); cigar (rum-soaked); cane / cross; black and purple coloration. The Bawon Samdi iconography is one of the most globally recognizable Vodou images.
