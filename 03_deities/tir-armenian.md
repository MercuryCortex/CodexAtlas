# Tir

## Identity

**Tir** (Armenian: *Tir*, sometimes called **Grogh** = "the Writer") is the Armenian god of **writing, wisdom, prophecy, and scholarship** — the divine scribe who records human fate, serves as messenger of [[aramazd]], and presides over prophetic dreams. He is the Armenian equivalent of [[nabu]] (Babylonian) and [[mercury-roman]] (Roman), occupying the scribe-messenger-wisdom slot in the Armenian divine hierarchy.

## Role and Function

Tir's portfolio maps precisely onto the Mesopotamian/Greek messenger-scribe tradition:

- **Scribe of fate** — records the deeds and fate of human souls at Aramazd's command
- **Patron of scholarship** — scribes, scholars, and the arts fall under his protection
- **Dream-oracle** — Tir delivers prophetic dreams; his temple at Artashat was reportedly consulted for dream-interpretation
- **Messenger** — like Hermes/Mercury/Nabu, he carries communications between the divine and human realms
- **Patron of learning** — in later Armenian Christian tradition, the name "Tir" was sometimes used generically for "scribe"

## Temple at Artashat

Ancient Artashat (the Hellenistic-era Armenian capital) housed Tir's principal shrine. Agathangelos records its destruction during Gregory the Illuminator's Christianization campaign (c. 301 CE) alongside the shrines of [[anahit-armenian]] and [[nane-armenian]].

## The Name "Grogh"

The epithet **Grogh** ("the Writer") appears in Armenian folk tradition as a name for the divine recorder of human fate — a figure who writes your life-deeds in the celestial book. Some scholars see this as a survival of Tir's scribal function into Christian-era folk belief, where the "writer-angel" who records human fate substitutes for the pagan scribe-god.

## Cross-Tradition Position

Tir sits in the universal Scribe-Messenger deity cluster:

- [[nabu]] (Babylonian) — scribe of fate, tablet of destiny
- [[thoth]] (Egyptian) — divine scribe, measurer of souls
- **Tir** (Armenian) — scribe of Aramazd, dream-oracle
- [[mercury-roman]] / [[hermes-greek]] — messenger, patron of writing and commerce
- [[narada]] (Hindu) — divine messenger, sage, bearer of cosmic information

The presence of a scribe-messenger deity in every major pantheon is one of the strongest cross-traditional patterns in the vault.
