# Parashurama

Parashurama ("Rama with the axe") is the **sixth avatar of [[vishnu]]** and one of the most distinctive — a **Brahmin who is also a warrior**, fusing the priestly and martial castes that Hindu social theory keeps separate. Son of the sage Jamadagni and Renuka, he receives his great battle-axe (*paraśu*) from **[[shiva]]**. His defining myth is vengeance turned into a cosmic corrective: when the arrogant kshatriya (warrior-caste) king Kartavirya Arjuna steals his father's wish-cow and kills Jamadagni, Parashurama vows to rid the earth of tyrannical warriors — and is said to have annihilated the corrupt kshatriya class **twenty-one times**, filling five lakes with their blood, before retiring in penance. He is a **chiranjivi** (a deathless, long-lived avatar) who therefore appears across the ages — instructing Bhishma, Drona, and Karna in the *Mahabharata*, and (in regional tradition) reclaiming the western coastal strip of India (Konkan/Kerala, "Parashurama's land") from the sea. He embodies the dangerous righteousness of force wielded against the abuse of power.
