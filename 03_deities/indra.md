# Indra

## Identity
King of the *devas* in the Rig Veda, more hymns are addressed to Indra than to any other deity (~250 of ~1,028 hymns). He is the warrior storm-god — slayer of the dragon Vṛtra who held the cosmic waters captive, drinker of soma, granter of victory in cattle raids and battle. The split of his vajra (thunderbolt) into Vṛtra's body releases the waters that nourish the world: the foundational Vedic chaoskampf.

In the Avestan world Indra appears as a *daēva* — that is, a *demon* — in the Zoroastrian reform. The same name, opposite valence: a strong index that Zoroastrianism is a reform of the inherited Indo-Iranian system. In the Mitanni-Hittite treaty (~-1380 BCE, Boğazköy archives) Indra appears alongside Mitra, Varuṇa, and the Nāsatyas as divine witness — the earliest dateable attestation of Indra outside the Veda. Later, in classical Hinduism, Indra is gradually demoted as Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Śiva ascend; in the Buddhist and Jain texts he becomes Śakra, a respectful guardian deity but no longer supreme.
