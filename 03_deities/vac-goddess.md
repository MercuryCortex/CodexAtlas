# Vāc — Goddess of Sacred Speech

Vāc (Sanskrit वाच्; also **Vāk**, Vac) is the Vedic goddess of **sacred speech** — one of the oldest divine figures in the Rigvedic corpus and one of the most philosophically remarkable. Her supreme statement is the **Devīsūkta** (Rigveda 10.125), where she speaks in the **first person** as a cosmic power that *precedes and enables all the gods*: "I move with the Rudras, the Vasus, the Adityas… I am the queen, the gatherer of treasures… whom I love, him I make mighty." Vāc is not a messenger of the gods — she is the primal creative **Word** through which the gods themselves act; the named deities are secondary to sacred utterance.

This makes Vāc the Hindu instance of the cross-tradition **"creation by the Word"** pattern — a structural parallel (and the earliest dated witness) to the Memphite theology's **[[ptah]]** (who creates through his tongue), the Greek *Logos*, and the Islamic *Kalām Allāh* (the uncreated Word), the Rigvedic statement of Vāc's cosmic priority predating the Greek and Islamic formulations by a millennium or more. In the later tradition Vāc develops directly into the classical goddess **[[saraswati]]** — of speech, learning, and music — carrying the theology of the sacred Word into living Hindu devotion.
