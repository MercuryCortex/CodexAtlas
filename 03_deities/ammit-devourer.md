# Ammit — the Devourer of the Dead

Egyptian judgment-deity attending the weighing-of-the-heart (*wḏt-ib* / Greek *psychostasia*) ceremony described in the *Book of the Dead* chapter 125 and depicted across thousands of papyri and tomb paintings from the Middle Kingdom onward. Ammit is the *consequence* of moral failure: she waits beside the scale where the deceased's heart is weighed against the feather of [[ma-at]]; if the heart is heavier than the feather (weighted with moral failure), she devours it — the "second death" from which there is no resurrection.

## Role in the weighing-of-the-heart

At the [[ritual-weighing-of-the-heart]] tribunal:

1. **[[anubis]]** calibrates the balance scale
2. **[[thoth]]** records the verdict on his ibis-scribe palette
3. **[[ma-at]]** (the feather of truth) is the standard on the opposite pan
4. **Ammit** consumes the heart if the verdict is condemnation

The figure is iconographically distinctive: a composite of the three most-dangerous Nile animals — crocodile head, lion forequarters, hippopotamus hindquarters — fused into a single judgment-beast. The composite iconography signals that Ammit is *not a god to be worshipped* but a god-shaped consequence; she has no cult, no temple, no priesthood. She exists only at the moment of judgment.

## Cross-tradition position

Ammit anchors one pole of Egyptian-Hellenistic-Christian moral-judgment iconography:

- The weighing-of-the-heart pattern → Byzantine [[michael-archangel]] holding scales at the Last Judgment → Gothic cathedral *Last Judgment* tympana → secular Lady Justice (blindfold, scales)
- The "second death" theology — annihilation rather than punishment — appears in Christian Revelation 20:14 ("This is the second death, the lake of fire") as a structural echo

Hornung 1999 documents the Ammit iconography in the New Kingdom royal-funerary tradition; the figure persists into the Greco-Roman period and is preserved in [[plutarch]]'s *De Iside et Osiride*.
