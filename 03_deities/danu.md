# Danu

## Identity
Danu is the inferred Proto-Celtic mother goddess whose name survives not in direct mythology but in the collective name of the Irish divine race: the **Tuatha Dé Danann** ("Peoples/Tribes of the Goddess Danu"). She is one of the most tantalizing figures in Celtic religion — her existence is certain from her children, her name is embedded in the divine race-name, but almost no surviving myths record her deeds. This very absence is itself significant: it marks her as the most primordial stratum of the tradition, the ground from which the visible divine figures emerge, too foundational to require narrative. Linguistically, her name connects to the Proto-Indo-European root *dʰen-* ("river," "to flow"), placing her in the pan-Indo-European tradition of the primordial river-goddess as source of divine abundance.

## Earliest attestation
The term *Tuatha Dé Danann* is attested from the **Lebor Gabála Érenn** (Book of the Taking of Ireland, compiled ~11th c. CE from earlier oral/manuscript traditions) as the name of the divine race who inhabited Ireland before the Gaels. Danu herself is not named as a character in the surviving mythological texts — her existence is entirely inferential from the genitive in the tribal name. Irish medieval texts sometimes substitute *Anu* or *Anann* (a goddess of prosperity explicitly named in Cormac's Glossary, ~900 CE) for the *Dana*-form, leading to scholarly debate about whether Danu and Anu are variants of the same figure.

## Mythological role
Because Danu is not a protagonist in surviving myths, her role must be inferred structurally. The Tuatha Dé Danann arrived in Ireland in a cloud of mist (some texts say by ship, others by cloud), bringing four sacred treasures: the Lia Fáil (Stone of Destiny), the Sword of Lugh, the Spear of Nuada, and the Cauldron of the Dagda. These are the instruments of a divine tribe — a people, not merely a pantheon. Danu is the mother of this people: she is the divine matrix from which Irish mythology springs.

The river-goddess interpretation connects her to the tradition of rivers as goddesses throughout Indo-European religion: the Boyne ([[boann]]), the Shannon, the Danube, the Dōn (Britain), the Don (Russia), the Dnieper, and the Vedic *Dānu* (the flowing one, mother of the Danavas) all share the same root. The river is the flowing of divine life into the world — Danu as mother-of-gods is the river that gives the divine race their being.

**The Vedic Parallel.** In Vedic mythology, *Dānu* appears as the mother of the **Danavas** — a class of cosmic beings who stand in opposition to the Devas (the gods championed by Indra). This is a structural inversion of the Celtic usage (where the Tuatha Dé Danann are the *victorious* divine race), but the linguistic connection is one of the best-established Indo-European mythological cognates. Both the Celtic Danu and the Vedic Dānu are ancient feminine sources — rivers of divine generation that predate the individuated pantheons they produce. The parallel is the kind of striking cross-tradition connection comparativists prize: the same PIE root *dʰen-* produces both the mother of the Irish gods and the mother of the Vedic anti-gods, suggesting a common mythological archetype of the primordial flowing-mother at the origin of the divine order.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[varuna]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Vedic Dānu, mother of the Danavas, shares the PIE root *dʰen-* with Celtic Danu; Varuna's cosmological world includes Dānu as part of the primordial cosmic structure. Mallory and Adams (2006) document the *Dānu / Danu* cognate as among the clearest Indo-European mythological parallels.
- **[[dagda]]** — one of Danu's principal divine children in the Irish mythological tradition; the Dagda's cauldron of abundance echoes Danu's role as the source of divine nourishment.
- **[[lugh]]** — among the Tuatha Dé Danann, Lugh the long-armed is Danu's grandson or son depending on the textual strand.
- **[[nuada]]** — king of the Tuatha Dé Danann; divine child of Danu's race.
- **[[the-morrigan]]** — sovereignty-and-war figure among the Tuatha Dé Danann; of Danu's divine race.
- **[[aengus-og]]** — love-god among the Tuatha Dé Danann; son of the Dagda, thus Danu's grandchild.
- **[[manannan-mac-lir]]** — lord of the sea and the otherworld; among the Tuatha Dé Danann and closely associated with Danu's race.
- **[[boann]]** — goddess of the River Boyne; closest surviving Irish river-goddess parallel to Danu's river-source aspect.

## Iconography
No iconography of Danu survives from antiquity — she is not depicted on any known Gaulish or Irish artifacts. Her attributes are inferred: river-source, earth-abundance, the flowing nourishment that sustains a divine race.

## Disputes
The identification of Danu with Anu/Anann (the prosperity-goddess of Cormac's Glossary) is debated. The two hills called the "Paps of Anu" (Dá Chích Anann) in County Kerry are sometimes attributed to Anu/Danu as a fertility-earth-goddess. Some scholars (notably Carey, 1983) argue that "Danu" is a scholarly reconstruction from the genitive in *Tuatha Dé Danann* and that no goddess called Danu was ever directly invoked — the name in *Tuatha Dé Danann* may be the Old Irish word *dan* ("art, skill, craft") rather than a goddess-name. The debate is unresolved, but the PIE cognate with Vedic Dānu remains linguistically solid regardless of the Irish resolution.
