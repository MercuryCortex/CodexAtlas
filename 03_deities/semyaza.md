# Semyaza (Shemihazah)

## Identity
**Semyaza** (Aramaic *Šemiḥazah*, "my name has seen [God]"; Ge'ez *Semyaz*; Greek *Semiazas*) is the chief of the **Watchers** in the *Book of the Watchers* (1 Enoch 1–36) — the rebellion-leader of the two hundred angelic Watchers who, in 1 Enoch 6, descend to Mount Hermon, swear a binding oath together to take human women as wives, beget the violent **Nephilim** giants, and corrupt the antediluvian world. He is the single most important named individual fallen angel in Second-Temple-Jewish apocalyptic literature alongside [[azazel]] — and the textual stratigraphy of 1 Enoch (Reed 2005; Nickelsburg 2001) shows that the Semyaza-and-the-200-Watchers tradition and the Azazel-and-the-arts-of-civilization tradition are two parallel originally-independent narrative strands that the Book of the Watchers fuses.

## Earliest attestation
- **1 Enoch 6:1–8** (~3rd c. BCE Aramaic stratum, surviving in Qumran 4Q201, 4Q202; Greek Codex Panopolitanus; Ge'ez Ethiopian text) — the oath at Mount Hermon, the list of the chiefs-of-tens (Semyaza first, then Urakib, Ramiel, Kokabiel, Tamiel, Ramiel, Daniel, Ezeqeel, Baraqiel, Asael, Armaros, Batriel, Ananel, Zaqiel, Samsiel, Sartael, Turiel, Yomiel, Sariel — 20 chiefs each with 10 Watchers under them; lists vary by recension).
- **1 Enoch 7–8** — the consequences: Nephilim giants who eat everything, then humans, then each other; civilizational corruption.
- **1 Enoch 9–10** — God commissions Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, Uriel to respond: Semyaza is to be bound in the valleys of the earth for seventy generations, then cast into a fiery abyss.
- **1 Enoch 69** — the second Watchers-list, with elaborated teaching-roles (Semyaza is listed but specific teachings are assigned to others — Penemue writing, Kasdeja abortion/dark arts, Gadreel weapons, Kokabiel astrology).
- **Jubilees 4:15–22; 5:1–10** — recapitulates the Watchers narrative with Mastema-elaboration; canonical in [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]].
- **Qumran 4Q201, 4Q202** — Aramaic fragments of the Book of the Watchers naming Shemihazah directly.

## Mythological role
The leader of the angelic rebellion. The Watchers' oath at Mount Hermon is staged as a *binding* oath — *ḥerem*-style irrevocable — which is why, in the Enochic narrative, the rebellion cannot be undone by simple repentance; it requires the Flood and the binding-imprisonment of the Watchers as cosmic-historical response. The narrative is the foundational *exogenous* origin-of-evil thesis in Second-Temple-Jewish thought — evil enters creation through the fallen Watchers and their Nephilim offspring, *not* through Adam's transgression — and stands in tension with the *endogenous* Adamic origin-of-evil thesis that comes to dominate Pauline-Christian theology.

In the Hermeneia commentary (Nickelsburg 2001), Semyaza is the *priestly-illicit-union* archetype (the priests of post-exilic Judea who married foreign women — Ezra 9–10 — read mythologically) where Azazel is the *forbidden-arts-of-civilization* archetype. The two narratives crystallize together in the Book of the Watchers without full integration; modern scholarship reads them as parallel originally-independent traditions.

In Ethiopian Orthodox canonical reception, Semyaza is named in Ge'ez 1 Enoch as the principal rebellion-leader and is well-known in Ethiopian theological tradition — unlike in Western Christianity, where the Watchers tradition fell out of canonical use after Augustine and Semyaza becomes obscure until rediscovery via Bruce 1773 / Charles 1912 ([[event-bruce-recovers-enoch-1773]]).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[azazel]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the second of the two Watcher leaders in the Book of the Watchers stratigraphy. Reed 2005 demonstrates these are originally distinct figures conflated in 1 Enoch's redactional history.
- **[[satanael]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: medieval Slavonic Christian and Bogomil traditions develop a Satanael figure that absorbs Watchers material (Stuckenbruck 1997).
- **Iblis** ([[tradition-islam]]) — *parallel-form*: the Quranic Iblis-rebellion narrative (Q 7:11–18; 38:71–85) is structurally parallel-but-distinct; Islamic exegesis sometimes draws on Jewish Watchers traditions.

## Iconography
Semyaza is not iconographically standardized in Ethiopian Christian art (where iconographic emphasis is on saints and the Theotokos, not on demonic figures). Late-Western esoteric and Theosophical reception (post-Bruce 1773 / post-Charles 1912) sometimes assigns him visualizations, but these are modern reception, not ancient or Ethiopian iconography.

## Disputes
- **Semyaza vs. Azazel as the *single* Watcher chief.** The Book of the Watchers redactional history shows two leadership-traditions. Reed 2005 is the standard treatment.
- **Etymology of the name.** "My name has seen [God]" is one reading; alternatives propose connection to *šem* (name) + *ḥazah* (to see / behold). The name's exact theological resonance depends on the etymology.
- **Whether the Mount Hermon location is historical-cultic memory or pure narrative invention.** Mount Hermon was a regional pagan-cultic site; the Watcher tradition may encode polemic against neighboring pagan religion (Nickelsburg 2001).
