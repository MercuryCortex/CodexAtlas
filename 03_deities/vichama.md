# Vichama

Vichama is the central-coastal Andean god of **death and vengeance** — a solar avenger and the half-brother and rival of the great coastal creator **[[pachacamac]]**, both sons of the Sun. His story, preserved in the colonial chronicle of Antonio de la Calancha (1638), is one of the most vivid creation-and-vengeance myths of the Peruvian coast. After a primal woman is left starving and her firstborn son is killed and dismembered by Pachacamac (who plants the pieces to create the cultivated foods — maize from her teeth, gourds and roots from her bones), **Vichama**, a later son, grows up and sets out to avenge his murdered brother and mother. When he returns to find his mother killed too, he resurrects her, and in his fury appeals to the Sun, who turns the people of the coast to **stone** — and these petrified ancestors become the *huacas*, the sacred standing stones and headlands venerated up and down the shore. To repopulate the world the Sun then sends three eggs — of gold, silver, and copper — from which the nobles, noblewomen, and commoners are born. Vichama embodies the coastal Andean theology of death, retribution, and the sacred landscape of petrified ancestors.
