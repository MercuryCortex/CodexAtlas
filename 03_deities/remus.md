# Remus

## Identity
Remus is the slain twin of the Roman foundation myth — brother of [[romulus]], with whom he is suckled by the she-wolf and raised to found a city, but whom he does not live to see completed. Where Romulus becomes Rome's first king (and is deified as Quirinus), Remus is the brother who is killed at the threshold of the founding: the necessary, excluded double whose death consecrates the wall of the new city.

## Earliest attestation
The twins appear in Roman annalistic tradition (Fabius Pictor, 3rd c. BCE), and canonically in Livy (*Ab Urbe Condita* I) and Plutarch (*Life of Romulus*); the she-wolf and twins are depicted on Roman coinage from the 3rd c. BCE. T. P. Wiseman (*Remus: A Roman Myth*, 1995) argues that Remus was a relatively late, deliberate addition to the legend — the second twin embodying the plebeian half of a dual Rome.

## Mythological role — the founding fratricide
Sons of the Vestal Rhea Silvia by Mars, the infant twins are set adrift on the Tiber, suckled by the she-wolf (*Lupa*) at the Lupercal, and raised by the shepherd Faustulus. Grown, they resolve to found a city but quarrel over its site and name: Romulus takes the Palatine, Remus the Aventine, and they hold an augural contest of birds that ends in dispute. When Romulus traces the sacred boundary (*pomerium*) and raises the first wall, Remus leaps over it in mockery — and is struck dead (by Romulus, or by Celer). The fratricide is Rome's *foundation-sacrifice*: the brother's blood seals the wall, and the city is named for the survivor.

## Cross-tradition — the Indo-European Divine Twins
Romulus and Remus are the Roman reflex of the **Indo-European Divine Twins** — the paired brothers, often horse-associated and of contrasting fate (one mortal, one immortal; one slain, one raised). Their cognates: the Greek [[the-dioskouroi]] (Castor mortal, Pollux immortal) and the Vedic [[the-ashvins]] (the Nāsatya horse-twins). Beyond the Indo-European family, the twin-founder / paired-brother motif recurs worldwide as a structural parallel (no genetic contact): the Maya Hero Twins [[hunahpu]] and [[ixbalanque]] of the *Popol Vuh*, and the Yoruba [[ibeji]]. The motif anchors the [[divine-twins]] theme.

## Iconography
The she-wolf suckling the twins (the *Lupa Capitolina* type); the augural birds; the boundary-wall. In the dual scheme, Remus is the Aventine to Romulus's Palatine — the plebeian, the excluded, the sacrificed.
