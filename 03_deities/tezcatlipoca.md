# Tezcatlipoca

## Identity
"Smoking Mirror" — one of the four principal deities of late-Postclassic central-Mexican religion, the cosmic rival and dialectical-partner of Quetzalcoatl. Tezcatlipoca governs night, memory, destiny, kingship (the *tlatoani*'s royal authority is granted by Tezcatlipoca), sorcery, war, and the obsidian mirror by which diviners see hidden truth. He is the most all-encompassing of the Aztec gods — sometimes considered the most powerful or even, in some Sahagún passages, supreme. The four Tezcatlipocas (Black, Red, Blue, White) correspond to the four cardinal directions and the four cosmic ages preceding the Fifth Sun.

## Earliest attestation
Toltec period (~900–1100 CE) onward; Aztec central state religion ~14th–15th c.; iconographic continuity with Maya jaguar-god of the underworld (debated whether the same figure).

## Mythological role
In the five-suns cosmology, the four Tezcatlipocas (and Quetzalcoatl as one of them in some traditions) each preside over one of the four prior cosmic ages; the present Fifth Sun is the result of their cooperative-rival shaping. The Quetzalcoatl-Tezcatlipoca dialectic is fundamental: Tezcatlipoca brings down Topiltzin Quetzalcoatl in Tula by tricking him into drunkenness and incest; Quetzalcoatl flees east. In the *toxcatl* festival, a young man chosen as Tezcatlipoca's ixiptla (representative) lived as the god for a year — accorded divine honors, four wives — and was then sacrificed; this was the most-discussed of Mexica ritual practices in Spanish accounts.

## Iconography
Smoking obsidian mirror (in his foot, or in his hand, or on his chest — Tezcatlipoca has lost one foot in cosmic combat, replaced with the mirror); black-and-yellow horizontal stripes across the face; obsidian and jaguar imagery; the conch-shell ornament *anahuatl*.
