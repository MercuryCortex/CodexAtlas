# Beher (Aksumite)

## Identity
**Beher** is the sea / cosmic-water deity of the pre-Christian Aksumite pantheon, attested on Ezana's pre-conversion trilingual stelae alongside [[almaqah]] (moon / national high-god), [[mahrem]] (war-god), [[astar-aksumite]] (Venus / astral), and [[meder]] (earth-god). The name *Beher* is etymologically related to the Semitic *bḥr* / *baḥr* root for "sea" (preserved in modern Arabic *baḥr*, Amharic and Tigrinya *bahr*), and Beher functions in the pre-Christian Aksumite pantheon as the **sea-divinity** — particularly important for an Aksumite state whose **Adulis port** on the Red Sea was the economic engine of its trans-regional trade network (with Roman Egypt, Sasanian Persia, and Indian Ocean commerce).

## Earliest attestation
- **Pre-Christian Ezana stelae** — RIE 185, RIE 270, RIE 271 invoke Beher as one of the five pre-Christian deities of the Aksumite royal pantheon.
- The figure is *not* attested outside Aksumite epigraphy in the same way that Sabaean Almaqah is — Beher appears to be more specifically Aksumite-localized.
- After Ezana's conversion, Beher disappears from royal inscriptions along with the rest of the pantheon.

## Mythological role
Beher's role in the pre-Christian Aksumite frame is the **sea / cosmic-water** dimension of the pantheon. For a state whose wealth depended on Red Sea trade (Adulis was the principal port; the Aksumite navy and merchant fleet operated across the Red Sea and into the western Indian Ocean), the sea-god was politically and economically consequential. No surviving Aksumite-pre-Christian narrative texts elaborate Beher's mythology specifically; the documentation is epigraphic-cultic.

Some scholars (Marrassini 2003) propose that Beher may also have had a **sky / cosmic-water** dimension — the broader Semitic conceptual association of the sky with cosmic waters (the *mayim* above the firmament of Genesis 1) might give Beher a dual sea-and-sky role.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[poseidon]]** (Greek) — *parallel-form* (no ancient identification attested): structurally cognate sea-deity.
- **[[yam-canaanite]]** — *parallel-form*: Canaanite/Ugaritic Yam (the Sea-personification) is the Northwest-Semitic cognate of a sea-as-deity figure.
- The Ezana Greek version (RIE 270) does *not* explicitly identify Beher with Poseidon (unlike the explicit Mahrem-Ares identification), so the Beher-Poseidon parallel is scholarly inference rather than ancient identification.

## Iconography
Not standardized in surviving Aksumite material culture.

## Disputes
- **Beher's exact pantheonic role.** Whether sea, sky, both, or a more general regional patronage. The epigraphic record is brief and formulaic; conjecture predominates.
- **Whether Beher is Sabaean-derived or Aksumite-indigenous.** Unlike Almaqah (clearly Sabaean-imported), Beher appears more Aksumite-specific.
