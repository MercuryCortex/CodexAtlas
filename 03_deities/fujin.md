# Fūjin

## Identity
The Japanese wind deity, one of the oldest kami named in the *Nihon Shoki*, where he appears as **Shina-Tsu-Hiko** (male) and **Shina-To-Be** (female variant), born from the breath of [[izanagi]] and [[izanami]]. In later iconographic tradition — especially after Buddhist iconographic influence during the Nara and Heian periods — Fūjin became the fearsome green-skinned demon who carries an enormous bag of winds on his back, releasing storms when he opens it. He is inseparable from his partner [[raijin]] (thunder god), with the two deities forming the definitive storm-guardian dyad of Japanese religious art.

## Earliest attestation
- *Nihon Shoki* (720 CE) — named as Shina-Tsu-Hiko; born to give the first wind that dried the primordial ocean-mist between heaven and earth.
- *Kojiki* (712 CE) — less explicit but wind-deity generation embedded in Izanagi/Izanami creation sequence.
- **Tawara-ya Sōtatsu's screen painting** (c. 1620, Kennin-ji Temple, Kyoto) — the defining iconographic representation of Fujin and [[raijin]] as a complementary pair; considered one of the masterworks of Japanese art.
- Temple gate guardian role at many major shrine-temple complexes (syncretic *shinbutsu-shūgō* tradition).

## Mythological role
Fūjin's primary cosmogonic role is to dry and clarify the primordial atmospheric chaos — the first wind that separated the mist between heaven and earth and made the world inhabitable. This inaugural breath function mirrors the Sumerian Enlil's wind separating An (heaven) and Ki (earth), and the Egyptian Shu's air holding Nut above Geb.

In later tradition, Fūjin becomes a **guardian deity** posted at temple gates alongside Raijin — a function directly borrowed from Buddhist iconographic tradition (*Vāyu* as wind deity in Vedic-Buddhist transmission) but thoroughly naturalized into Shinto practice. The pair's fearsome appearance serves apotropaic function: warding off evil by their very terrible presence.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[enlil]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Sumerian wind-deity whose breath separates heaven from earth in the cosmogony; the wind-as-first-creative-breath is one of the vault's deepest cross-tradition patterns.
- **[[shu]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Egyptian personification of air who holds up the sky-goddess Nut above the earth-god Geb; structural parallel to Fūjin's atmospheric role in separating the primordial ocean-mist.
- **[[pazuzu]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Babylonian wind-demon; the convergent iconography of wind deity as fearsome demon/monster (rather than benign nature spirit) across Babylonian and Japanese traditions.
- **[[tlaloc]]** — *parallel-motif*: the Aztec storm deity whose fearsome appearance and domain over atmospheric forces parallels the storm-guardian function shared by Fujin and [[raijin]].
- **[[raijin]]** — *ancient-identification*: the wind-and-thunder paired guardians are theologically and iconographically inseparable in Japanese tradition; Fujin-Raijin as a divine dyad is one of the most visually prominent pairings in East Asian religious art.
- **[[susanoo]]** — *parallel-motif*: Susanoo as storm-god shares the wind/storm domain; Fūjin handles atmospheric wind specifically while Susanoo is the broader storm-and-sea deity.

## Iconography
Green-skinned demon with wild hair, bag of winds slung over his back (represented as a large sphere or elongated sack), flying through clouds. Always depicted in dynamic motion, in contrast to Raijin's drum-surrounded static pose. The bag-of-winds motif parallels Aeolus's bag of winds given to Odysseus in Homer's *Odyssey* — an iconographic coincidence of world significance.
