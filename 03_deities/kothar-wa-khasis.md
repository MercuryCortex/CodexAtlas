# Kothar-wa-Khasis

Kothar-wa-Khasis (Ugaritic *kṯr w ḫss*, "Skilled-and-Wise") is the divine craftsman of the Canaanite pantheon — smith, architect, weapon-maker, and magician. In the **[[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]]** he is the indispensable artisan of the gods: he forges the two enchanted maces **Yagrush** ("Driver") and **Aymur** ("Chaser") with which Baal defeats **[[yam]]** (the Sea), and he builds Baal's cedar-and-lapis palace with its contested window. His mythic workshops lie at the edges of the known world — *Kaphtor* (Crete) and *Ḥikupta* (Egyptian Memphis, *Ḥwt-kȝ-Ptḥ*) — embedding real Late-Bronze-Age craft-trade geography into theology.

## The craftsman-god type
Kothar belongs to the widely-attested ancient-Mediterranean type of the **divine smith** who both forges the gods' weapons and constructs their dwellings, inviting comparison with Greek **Hephaestus** and Egyptian **[[ptah]]** (whose Memphite seat is literally Kothar's mythic home). Philo of Byblos's Phoenician History preserves him as *Chusor*, a culture-hero inventor of iron and rhetoric. M.L. West (1997) treats the Kothar–Hephaestus parallel as part of the West-Asiatic substrate of Greek divine-craftsman mythology — a transmission-wire, not a shared membership.
