# Ebisu

## Identity
The jovial god of fishermen, luck, and prosperity — one of the **Seven Lucky Gods** (Shichifukujin) and the only member of that syncretic group of purely Japanese (Shinto) origin. Depicted as a rotund, bearded, laughing fisherman in an eboshi hat, holding a fishing rod in one hand and a plump red sea bream (*tai*, a fish whose name puns with *medetai* — "auspicious") under his other arm. He is one of the most beloved deities in Japanese folk religion, patron of fishermen, merchants, and the working prosperity of everyday life.

His mythological origin is unique and paradoxical: in one major tradition he is **Hiruko** (Leech-Child), the **first failed creation** of [[izanagi]] and [[izanami]] — a child born without bones, cast adrift on a reed boat because the cosmic marriage-ritual had been performed incorrectly (Izanami spoke first instead of Izanagi). This rejected first-born, abandoned to the sea, washes up and is deified as the patron of those who live from the sea. In another tradition he is identified as **Kotoshironushi**, a son of [[okuninushi]].

## Earliest attestation
- *Kojiki* Book 1: Hiruko narrative — the first defective creation of Izanagi and Izanami, set adrift as an infant.
- *Nihon Shoki* (720 CE) — parallel Hiruko account; the abandoned child who becomes the sea.
- **Shichifukujin iconographic tradition** (Muromachi period, 14th–16th c.) — Ebisu appears as part of the Seven Lucky Gods group; the Seven Lucky Gods tradition itself crystallizes in the Muromachi period as a deliberate inter-tradition synthesis.
- **Nishinomiya Shrine** (Hyogo Prefecture) — major center of Ebisu worship; the annual *Tōka Ebisu* festival (January 10) remains one of Japan's most widely attended religious festivals.
- **Yebisu Beer** (est. 1890) — named after Ebisu; the deity's name is now globally recognized through commercial culture.

## Mythological role
The **failed-creation-redeemed** narrative: Hiruko/Ebisu is the vault's primary Japanese instance of a cross-cultural mytheme: the rejected, imperfect, or cast-out first creation who becomes the most intimately beloved deity. The child cast into the sea for being born wrong becomes the patron of all those who live from the sea. The outsider becomes the insider's protector.

The **Shichifukujin synthesis**: Ebisu's most important theological context is his membership in the Seven Lucky Gods:
1. **Ebisu** (Shinto — Japan)
2. **Daikoku/Ōkuninushi** (Shinto-Buddhist — Japan)
3. **Benzaiten** (from Hindu [[saraswati]] via Buddhism)
4. **Bishamonten** (from Vedic [[kubera]] / Buddhist Vaishravana)
5. **Hotei** (Chinese Chan Buddhist sage)
6. **Fukurokuju** (Taoist immortal sage)
7. **Jurojin** (Taoist deity of longevity)

The Seven Lucky Gods are not a mythological group from any single tradition — they are a *deliberately assembled inter-tradition prosperity pantheon* created in medieval Japan to encode luck and abundance from every available divine source. Ebisu as the sole native Japanese member is the anchor of this group; the rest are imports from Hinduism, Buddhism, and Daoism. The entire Shichifukujin encodes Japan's synthesis of three major traditions into a single iconic grouping.

**Fishing and commerce**: the sea bream (tai) Ebisu holds is an auspicious fish precisely because *tai* sounds like *medetai* (congratulations, auspicious). Ebisu's whole iconography is a visual pun — the jolly fisherman holding his catch is simultaneously a statement about luck, abundance, and the blessing of good fortune on honest labor.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[sophia-gnostic]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Hiruko/Ebisu as rejected-first-creation-who-becomes-sacred follows the same arc as Gnostic Sophia — the divine that falls/is cast out and through that very exile becomes the redemptive mediator for the marginalized.
- **[[lilith]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: rejected first creation in some traditions (first wife of Adam cast out); the "failed/rejected first creation" pattern across traditions.
- **[[saraswati]]** — *folk-syncretism*: Saraswati → Benzaiten enters the Seven Lucky Gods alongside Ebisu; the Hindu-Buddhist-Shinto transmission is encoded structurally in the Shichifukujin grouping.
- **[[kubera]]** — *folk-syncretism*: Kubera → Bishamonten enters the Seven Lucky Gods; wealth-deity lineage from Vedic to Japanese pantheon.
- **[[inari]]** — *folk-syncretism*: overlapping commercial prosperity and luck domains; both widely venerated at merchant shrines.
- **[[demeter]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: food-abundance deity whose patronage of harvest and earthly prosperity runs parallel to Ebisu's prosperity domain (Demeter = grain abundance, Ebisu = sea harvest abundance).
- **[[okuninushi]]** — *folk-syncretism*: in the Kotoshironushi tradition Ebisu is Okuninushi's son; the prosperity-deity cluster of the Izumo tradition.

## Iconography
Round-faced, plump, smiling elderly fisherman with a white beard, wearing a formal eboshi court hat (indicating divine status despite the humble fishing context), holding a fishing rod in his right hand and a large red sea bream under his left arm. Often depicted seated, sometimes on a boat. The sea bream (*tai*) is inseparable from his iconography — to give someone a tai fish as a gift is to invoke Ebisu's blessing.
