# Enbilulu

## Identity

Enbilulu is the Mesopotamian god of canals, rivers, and irrigation — the divine overseer of the watercourses on which southern Mesopotamian agriculture depended. He is "the one who knows the secrets" of the dikes and channels, the god who keeps the river-water flowing where it is needed; his sphere is the managed water of the irrigated land, and he belongs to the watery domain of [[enki-ea]], the god of the fresh subterranean waters.

## Mythological role

Enbilulu's most prominent textual appearance is at the climax of the *Enuma Elish*. After Marduk defeats [[tiamat]] and orders the cosmos, the assembled gods proclaim his **fifty names**, each name folding an older god or function into Marduk's universal sovereignty. Among them, Enbilulu is invoked as the aspect of Marduk who "established the irrigation works," who set the Tigris and Euphrates in their courses, opened the springs, and apportioned the abundant waters to the fields. The incorporation of the canal-god Enbilulu into Marduk's names is a clear instance of how the rising imperial god of Babylon absorbed the powers and titles of the older Sumerian-Akkadian pantheon.

## Cross-tradition significance

Enbilulu is a useful node for two threads. As a **god of irrigation**, he reflects the deep Mesopotamian sacralization of the canal system — the engineered control of water that underwrote the world's first urban civilization. And as one of **Marduk's fifty names**, he illustrates the theological mechanism of *absorption-by-naming*, whereby a supreme god is exalted by gathering the identities of many lesser gods into himself — a move toward concentrated divine sovereignty that scholars have compared with the trajectory toward monotheism in the wider Near East.
