# Anubis

Anubis (Egyptian *Inpu*) is the jackal-headed Egyptian god of embalming, mummification, and the necropolis. He is among the oldest attested Egyptian deities — already present in late Predynastic and Early Dynastic iconography, predating the codification of the Osirian myth into which he is later incorporated as a junior figure. His association with the jackal probably derives from the literal sight of jackals at desert-edge cemeteries (where they would dig at fresh graves); the cult turns this anxiety into apotropaic patronage — the jackal-god protects the dead from the jackals. Cult centers at Cynopolis ("City of the Dog/Jackal") in Middle Egypt and at Asyut.

## Mythological role
Anubis presides over the embalming process: the embalmer-priest performing mummification rites wore an Anubis mask. He guides the deceased into the netherworld as **psychopomp** and tends the scales at the weighing of the heart in the Hall of Two Truths, with [[thoth]] recording the verdict and [[osiris]] enthroned as judge. In the Osirian myth as developed in the Middle and New Kingdoms, Anubis is presented as the son of Osiris by Nephthys, born of a clandestine encounter and raised by Isis — a mythological etiology grafted onto a much older independent jackal-god. The Hellenistic-Roman period generated the composite **Hermanubis**, fusing Anubis's psychopomp role with Hermes's — attested especially in Roman Egyptian funerary iconography.
