# Sulis

Sulis is the British goddess of the **hot healing spring at Bath** — *Aquae Sulis*, "the waters of Sulis," the only true thermal spring in Britain, whose steaming, mineral-rich water gushed up as an obvious marvel and was sacred long before the Romans monumentalized it. She is a goddess of two faces, both expressed at her spring: **healing and purification** (pilgrims came to her waters for cures), and **justice through cursing**. The latter is vividly attested by the great cache of lead **curse-tablets (defixiones)** thrown into the spring — petitions, often about stolen property, asking the goddess to seize and torment the thief until restitution is made — one of the richest bodies of everyday Latin (and Celtic naming) ever found. Under Roman rule she was fused with **[[minerva-roman|Minerva]]** as **Sulis Minerva**, the native name kept foremost — a textbook *interpretatio Romana* — and a temple and bath-complex rose around her spring, where a famous **gilt-bronze head** of the goddess survives. Sulis embodies the Celtic sacred spring: a living, personal divine power dwelling in healing — and dangerous — water.
