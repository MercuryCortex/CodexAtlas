# Phoebe

## Identity

Phoebe (Greek **Phoibē**, "the bright" or "radiant one") is a Titaness of the first generation — a daughter of [[uranus-greek]] and [[gaia]] — associated with prophecy, radiance, and the Delphic oracle. With her brother-consort [[coeus]] she forms one of the Titan couples, and through their daughter [[leto]] she is the grandmother of Apollo and Artemis.

## Mythological role

Phoebe's most important role is as a keeper of the **Delphic oracle**. In the prologue to Aeschylus's *Eumenides*, the Pythia recounts the orderly succession of the oracle's holders: first Gaia (Earth), then her daughter [[themis]], then the Titaness Phoebe, and finally — as a birth-gift from Phoebe to her grandson — Apollo. It is from this grandmother that Apollo takes his epithet **Phoebus** ("the bright"). Phoebe thus links the primordial, earth-born prophetic power of Gaia and Themis to the Olympian Apollo, giving the Delphic oracle a pedigree reaching back to the first generation of gods. By her union with Coeus she is also the mother of [[leto]] and of Asteria (mother of Hecate).

## Cross-tradition significance

Phoebe belongs to the Greek conception of **prophecy as an inheritance** passed down the divine generations — from the earth-mother to the Titans to the Olympians. The motif of the oracle peacefully transmitted (rather than seized) sets the Greek Delphic succession apart from the more violent successions of kingship in the Theogony, and her radiance-name, carried forward into Apollo's "Phoebus," makes her a quiet but structurally important node in the lineage of Greek prophetic and solar light.
