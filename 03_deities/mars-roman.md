# Mars (Roman)

## Identity
Mars is the **second-ranking** deity of the Roman state pantheon after Jupiter, but in cultic significance often the *most* important — he is the divine father of Romulus and Remus and thus the genealogical originator of Rome itself. Mars combines two seemingly disparate functions: (a) war and the Roman legions, especially their seasonal campaigns; (b) agricultural protection and spring fertility (his month, March / *Martius*, opens both the military and agricultural seasons in the old Roman calendar). The Roman Mars is notably *dignified* in contrast to the bloodthirsty Greek Ares; the interpretatio Graeca pairing did not erase the temperamental difference.

## Earliest attestation
- The **Carmen Arvale** (an archaic Latin liturgical chant preserved in inscriptions, ~-200 in form but containing material centuries older) invokes Mars.
- The **Salii** — priesthood that performed war-dances with the sacred shields (*ancilia*) — are traditionally established by Numa Pompilius (~-700) and are continuously attested.
- The Mars temple in the Campus Martius (Field of Mars) precedes the city wall, indicating his original residence *outside* the pomerium — fitting for a war-god whose violence must be kept outside the civic boundary.

## Mythological role
Native Roman myth: Mars impregnates the Vestal Virgin Rhea Silvia, producing the twins Romulus and Remus; their abandonment, suckling by a she-wolf (Mars's sacred animal), and Romulus's founding of Rome are the foundational national narrative. As a war-god, Mars receives the **spolia opima** (spoils stripped by a Roman commander who killed an enemy commander in single combat — only awarded three times in Roman history). The October Horse sacrifice and the Equirria horse-races honor him.

**Mars Ultor** ("Mars the Avenger"): Augustus vowed a temple to Mars Ultor before the Battle of Philippi (-42) to avenge Julius Caesar's assassination; the temple in the Forum of Augustus, dedicated in -2, became a major Augustan-era civic-religious site.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ares]]** — *ancient-identification* (with tonal divergence): standard *interpretatio Graeca* identification, but Mars is treated with cultic gravity while Ares is often disparaged in Greek literary mythology (Iliad 5: "most hateful of all the gods" — Zeus to Ares).

## Iconography
- Mature armored male
- Spear, shield, helmet
- The she-wolf (mother-suckling-twins motif)
- The woodpecker (*picus*; sacred to Mars)
- Bay laurel (associated with military triumph)

## Disputes
- **Original character — war or agriculture?** Older scholarship (Dumézil) argued Mars was originally a *general protector* whose war and agriculture aspects were both expressions of one tutelary function; some have argued he was originally agricultural and developed warrior aspects, or vice versa.
- **Etruscan origin.** Some etymological reconstructions link *Mavors / Mars* to the Etruscan deity *Maris*; a younger, war-specific Italian Mars then displaced an older Mars-the-protector. Contested.
