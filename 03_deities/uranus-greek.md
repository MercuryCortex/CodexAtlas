# Uranus

## Identity
Uranus (Greek *Ouranos* — "sky", "heaven") is the primordial Greek sky-god, husband-and-son of Gaia (Earth), and the oldest of the three generations of cosmic sovereigns in Hesiod's Theogony. In Hesiod, Gaia produces Uranus parthenogenetically to be her equal; from their union come the twelve Titans, the three Cyclopes, and the three Hundred-Handers (Hecatonchires). Uranus, fearful of his children, pushes them back into Gaia's body; in agony Gaia commissions the youngest Titan Kronos to castrate him; from the severed genitals fall blood (whence the Erinyes, the Giants, the Meliae) and into the sea (whence Aphrodite born of foam).

Unlike his successors Kronos and Zeus, Uranus has essentially no cult — he is mythologically active only in his castration narrative and then absent. His function in Greek religion is structural-mythic rather than worshipful: the displaced first sovereign of the succession myth. The Hittite Kumarbi cycle's "Song of Going Forth" preserves a striking parallel — the sky-god Anu is overthrown when Kumarbi bites off his genitals; the Hittite-Greek genealogical parallel is one of the cleanest mythological cognate-trees known. West (1997) treats Uranus as showing the strongest Near Eastern influence of any major Greek god. His name's etymology is unresolved; the "Indo-European Sky-Father" position is occupied not by Uranus but by Zeus / Jupiter / Dyauṣ Pitṛ.
