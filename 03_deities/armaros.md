# Armaros

## Identity
**Armaros** is one of the named **Watchers** of 1 Enoch, appearing in:
- **1 Enoch 6:7** — among the twenty chiefs-of-tens at Mount Hermon.
- **1 Enoch 8:3** — as the Watcher who taught humanity *"the loosing of spells"* (the dispelling of enchantments and counter-magical practices).

1 Enoch 8:3 (Nickelsburg-VanderKam 2004): *"Shemihazah taught spells and the cutting of roots; Hermoni taught the loosing of spells, magic, sorcery, and sophistry; Baraqel taught the signs of the lightning flashes..."*

Note: In some recensions, Armaros and Hermoni overlap or are identified; in others they are distinct Watchers. The Nickelsburg-VanderKam edition treats Hermoni as the principal teacher of magical-counter-spell knowledge; Armaros appears as a parallel-or-related figure in other Watcher-lists. This node treats Armaros as the figure principally associated with *counter-spells* and *the loosing of enchantments* — the inverse-magical knowledge.

## Earliest attestation
- **1 Enoch 6:7; 8:3**.
- **Qumran 4Q201, 4Q202** — Aramaic fragments attest the broader Watchers tradition with name-variation.

## Mythological role
Armaros teaches **counter-magic** — the dispelling of enchantments, the loosing of binding spells. Where [[semyaza]] (per 1 Enoch 8:3 in some recensions) teaches *spells* directly, Armaros (or Hermoni) teaches the *counter*-magical knowledge. The pattern is theologically interesting: both *casting* and *undoing* enchantments are coded as forbidden Watcher-knowledge in the Enochic frame — the Enochic theology rejects magical practice generally rather than approving counter-magic against malicious magic.

This contrasts with later Jewish, Christian, and Islamic traditions that sometimes distinguish *legitimate* counter-magic (apotropaic, exorcistic) from *illegitimate* offensive magic. The Enochic Watchers tradition is more thoroughgoingly anti-magical.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- Parallel structurally to figures associated with counter-magical and apotropaic knowledge across Mediterranean and Mesopotamian magical traditions.

## Iconography
Not standardized.

## Disputes
- **Identity vs. distinctness from Hermoni.** Some recensions of 1 Enoch 8:3 identify Armaros and Hermoni as the same figure; others treat them as distinct Watchers with related counter-magical portfolios. Nickelsburg-VanderKam 2004 maintains distinctness; older translations (Charles 1912) sometimes conflate.
