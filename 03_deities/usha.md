# Uṣas

## Identity
Uṣas is the Vedic goddess of dawn, one of the most poetically celebrated deities of the Rig Veda — ~20 hymns are addressed to her, including some of the most lyrical in the entire collection (RV 1.113, 1.123, 7.75–81). She is the daughter of the Sky (Dyauṣ Pitṛ), drawn across heaven in a chariot of red horses or red cows, revealing the world each morning. She is the perennial maiden — always young, always returning — yet by her cyclical appearance she also marks the passage of mortal time and ages humans toward death.

Uṣas is one of the clearest preservations of the Proto-Indo-European dawn-goddess *H₂éws-os, whose reflexes include Greek Eos, Latin Aurora, Lithuanian Aušrinė, and Old English Ēostre (the root preserved in "Easter"). The very name *Uṣas* / *Eos* / *Aurora* is cognate. The phrase "Daughter of Heaven" (*divaḥ duhitā*) used of her in the RV has direct cognates in Greek epic ("daughter of Zeus" applied to Aurora-like figures). She is the strongest Indo-European reconstructable goddess outside the Vedic-Greek-Latin reflex set.
