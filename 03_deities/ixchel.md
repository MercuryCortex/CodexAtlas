# Ixchel

## Identity
Yucatec Maya goddess of the moon, childbirth, weaving, medicine, and the rainbow. Ixchel is the principal female Maya deity in the surviving Yucatec sources (Landa, Chilam Balam, Dresden Codex). She manifests in both young (fertility-childbirth) and old (sorcery-weaving) aspects — the latter is the Dresden Codex's "Goddess O," frequently depicted with serpent in headdress and pouring water from a vessel (flood imagery). Her sanctuary at Cozumel was the principal pre-conquest Maya pilgrimage site, especially for women seeking fertility.

## Earliest attestation
Late Classic and Postclassic Maya iconography; Dresden Codex extensive attestation; Bishop Landa (16th c.) attests the Cozumel pilgrimage.

## Mythological role
The Dresden Codex shows Ixchel pouring water from an upturned jar — the world-destroying flood. She is also depicted with weaving implements, with serpents in her hair, and in childbirth-protective scenes. Her dual age-aspects (young-fertile / old-weaver) suggest a goddess of the full life-cycle.

## Iconography
Serpent in headdress (her oldest iconographic marker); upturned water-jar (flood/Moon-as-water-vessel); rabbit (Mesoamerican lunar associate); weaving loom.
