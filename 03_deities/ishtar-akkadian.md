# Ishtar

Ishtar is the supreme goddess of Akkadian-language Mesopotamia, inheriting and absorbing the roles of Sumerian [[inanna-sumerian]]. The two are essentially the same goddess in two languages, with the equation already explicit by the Sargonic period (~2300 BCE) when [[enheduanna]] wrote her Sumerian hymns as the Akkadian princess-priestess. In Assyrian theology Ishtar becomes the personal patron of kings — Ishtar of Nineveh and Ishtar of Arbela receive separate cults and were credited with battlefield victories; oracular pronouncements ("oracles of Ishtar") survive from Esarhaddon's and Ashurbanipal's reigns. Per the vault's splitting rule, Inanna and Ishtar are kept as separate nodes joined by `syncretic-edges` (ancient-identification) — the Akkadian Ishtar is *not* simply a translation of Inanna but the Sumerian goddess refracted through Semitic-language religious culture.

## Mythological role
In the **Descent of Ishtar** (a shorter, sharper Akkadian recension of Inanna's Descent), Ishtar descends to Kurnugi (the land of no return), is stripped at the seven gates, dies, and is restored by the gods' intervention through Asushunamir, with Dumuzi/Tammuz as her substitute. In the **Standard Babylonian Gilgamesh** Tablet VI, Ishtar proposes marriage to Gilgamesh; he refuses with a catalogue of her mistreated former lovers (including Tammuz), and she retaliates by demanding the Bull of Heaven from Anu. In the [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] she is a member of the divine council acclaiming Marduk's elevation. In the [[phase-1-007-code-of-hammurabi]] she is invoked among the curse-witnesses of the epilogue. The Hebrew Bible refers to her cult as "the queen of heaven" (Jer 7:18; 44:17–19, 25), attesting Ishtar-worship among Judahite women.
