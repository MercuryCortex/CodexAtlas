# Kauket

## Identity

Kauket ("the darkness" — feminine) is one of the eight primordial deities of the Hermopolitan Ogdoad and the female member of its darkness-pair, the counterpart of [[kek-egyptian]]. With Nun/Naunet (the primordial waters), [[heh-egyptian]]/[[hauhet]] (boundlessness), and [[amun]]/[[amaunet]] (hiddenness), Kek and Kauket describe the qualities of the world before the first sunrise — Kauket being the feminine articulation of the primordial dark, the obscurity that lay over the deep before any light.

## Mythological role

Like all the Ogdoad, Kauket is a being of presence rather than action: she constitutes one of the four conditions of the pre-cosmic abyss. She and Kek are so closely paired that Egyptian sources sometimes treat "Kek-Kauket" as a single androgynous darkness-force split into male and female modes. Her epithets mirror Kek's — associating her with both the bringing-on of night and, paradoxically, the turn toward day, since the primordial darkness is the condition out of which the first dawn breaks. In the daily cosmic cycle, this links Kauket to the night through which the sun-god travels before re-emerging at dawn.

## Cross-tradition identifications

- **[[kek-egyptian]]** — *ancient-identification*: Kauket is the female complement of Kek; together they personify the primordial darkness.
- **"darkness over the face of the deep" (Genesis 1:2)** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Hebrew creation account's darkness over the primordial waters occupies the same conceptual position as the Egyptian Kek/Kauket darkness-over-the-abyss (Allen 1988).
- **[[primordial-darkness]]** and the wider Ogdoad ([[nun]], [[amaunet]]) — Kauket operates within the eightfold Hermopolitan theology under the patronage of [[thoth]].

## Iconography

As one of the four female members of the Ogdoad, Kauket is depicted serpent-headed (the males are frog-headed), the convention distinguishing the female from the male primordial principles.

## Disputes

Because the darkness-pair is so often treated as a single force, Kauket — like the other Ogdoad females — is sometimes regarded as the grammatically required feminine of Kek rather than an independently developed goddess. Her epithets are reconstructed largely by analogy with Kek's, and the precise boundary between her "darkness" and Hauhet's "formlessness" within the abyss is an interpretive matter.
