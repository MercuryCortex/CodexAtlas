# Priapus

Priapus (Greek *Príapos*) is the Greco-Roman god of **fertility, gardens, and male generative power** — a rustic, minor, but ubiquitous deity, instantly recognized by his permanent and grossly exaggerated phallus. Most accounts make him the son of **[[aphrodite-greek|Aphrodite]]** and **[[dionysus]]**, born at **Lampsacus** on the Hellespont, which remained his chief cult-centre. His outsized member is not (primarily) erotic but **apotropaic**: a reddened wooden Priapus stood in countless Greek and Roman gardens and orchards as a scarecrow-guardian, threatening thieves with it in the crude, comic first-person verses of the Latin *Priapeia*. As a god of the fruitfulness of gardens, vines, herds, and bees — and a protector of sailors at his harbour-shrines — he embodies generative abundance under threat. In Greco-Roman Egypt he was identified with the ithyphallic fertility-god **[[min-egyptian|Min]]** (cf. Herodotus 2.51). He gives his name to the medical term *priapism*.
