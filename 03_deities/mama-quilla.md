# Mama Quilla

## Identity
Mama Quilla ("Mother Moon" in Quechua; *killa* = moon) is the Inca moon goddess, principal female deity of the Inca state pantheon, consort of Inti the sun-god, and protector of women, marriage, and the menstrual cycle. She was the third most important deity in the Inca state cult after Inti and Viracocha, and the Inca state calendar was lunar — making Mama Quilla not merely a mythological figure but the timekeeper of Andean ritual and agricultural life. Her cult center was within the Coricancha (the great temple complex at Cusco), where a massive silver disk served as her cult image. She was the special guardian of the *Aclla Cuna* (Chosen Women) — the consecrated women who served the state religion.

## Earliest attestation
Inca-period state cult (~1200–1533 CE); primary sources are Spanish colonial chroniclers (Cobo, Cieza de León, Molina, Polo de Ondegardo) who describe the Coricancha temple complex and its satellite shrines, including Mama Quilla's silver-disk idol. Pre-Inca Andean moon-goddesses were present in earlier cultures (Moche, Tiwanaku contexts), though the specific Mama Quilla form is Inca state-period.

## Mythological role
**Cosmic consort**: Mama Quilla is the wife of Inti (the sun) — a cosmic marriage that mirrors the marriage of the Sapa Inca (living son of the sun) to his *coya* (queen). The sky-couple Inti-Mama Quilla is the celestial template for royal marriage, and the Inca queen (coya) was understood as Mama Quilla's earthly representative.

**Lunar calendar**: The Inca ritual calendar was primarily lunar, with 12 lunar months organizing festivals, agricultural activities, and state ceremonies. Mama Quilla's movements governed this calendar. The *ceque* system (the radiating lines of sacred sites from Cusco) included shrines dedicated to Mama Quilla calibrated to lunar events.

**Lunar eclipses**: Solar and lunar eclipses were treated as emergencies in Inca religion. A lunar eclipse was understood as a puma or serpent attacking Mama Quilla — the population would shout, make noise, and beat dogs to drive the attacking creature away. The cosmological drama of the eclipse was a moment of divine crisis requiring human intervention.

**Protector of women**: Women's reproductive cycles (menstruation, pregnancy) were understood as under Mama Quilla's governance, making her the divine patron of women's health and fertility. The *Aclla Cuna* (chosen women, sometimes called "Virgins of the Sun" in colonial accounts) served both Inti and Mama Quilla.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[inti]]** — *consort-pair*: The sun-moon divine couple is one of the most widespread structural patterns in world religion; the Inca Inti-Mama Quilla pair is the Andean instantiation.
- **Virgin Mary** — *folk-syncretism*: Colonial Andean Christians assimilated Mama Quilla to the Virgin Mary, drawing on the shared lunar symbolism (Guadalupe's crescent moon, the Woman of Revelation crowned with stars), shared protection of women, and the position as consort to the solar male principle.

## Iconography
Her cult image in the Coricancha was a large silver disk — the moon as silver to Inti's gold. Spanish accounts describe it as a solid silver disk with a female face. The rabbit in the moon (a widespread Mesoamerican and Andean lunar figure) is associated with her. Silver was considered her metal, as gold was Inti's.
