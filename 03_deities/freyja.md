# Freyja

## Identity
Goddess of love, fertility, sorcery (*seiðr*), and the war-dead — Freyja takes half the slain to her hall *Fólkvangr*, with Óðinn taking the other half to Valhǫll. Freyja is of the *Vanir*, the second family of Norse gods (the Æsir being the first), absorbed into the Æsir after the Æsir-Vanir war as hostages-exchanged. She and her brother Freyr are the principal Vanir figures in Norse religion. Freyja taught *seiðr* (a form of trance-magic / sorcery) to the Æsir.

## Earliest attestation
Old Norse literary attestation from skaldic and Eddic poetry; weekday Friday < *Freyjudagr* preserves her name; her cult appears strongly attested in Sweden (place-names with *Frö-*) and Norway.

## Mythological role
The *Brísingamen* — her great necklace, won by sleeping with four dwarven smiths over four nights. In *Þrymskviða*, when Thor's hammer is stolen and Loki proposes sending Freyja as bride to the giant Þrymr, Freyja's outraged refusal (such that the gods' hall shakes) is the comedic setup for Thor's hammer-recovery in drag. She practices and teaches *seiðr*.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Venus / Aphrodite** — *ancient-identification*: weekday-calque evidence; Friday = *dies Veneris* = *Freyjudagr*. Freyja's fertility / love / sorcery / dispute-with-Hera (Sif/Frigg parallels) functions invite the interpretatio.
- **Frigg** — *scholarly-parallel* (intra-Norse): the relationship between Freyja and Frigg has been read by some (especially Grimm) as suggesting an original single goddess split by Norse-Vanir-Æsir reorganization; current consensus treats them as related but distinct.

## Iconography
The Brísingamen necklace; cat-chariot; falcon-feather cloak; the boar *Hildisvíni*. Often depicted weeping golden tears (her tears for the wandering Óðr).
