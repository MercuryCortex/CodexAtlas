# Tiki

## Identity
Tiki is the **first man** of Polynesian myth — across the islands the progenitor of humankind, and in the carved **tiki** figures (the Māori *hei-tiki* pendant, the great Marquesan and Hawaiian images) the ancestral human form made visible.

## Earliest attestation
Pan-Polynesian oral tradition (Māori, Tahitian *Ti'i*, Hawaiian *Ki'i*, Marquesan), recorded by 19th- and 20th-century ethnographers; the carved tiki is among the most widespread Polynesian sacred images.

## Mythological role — the first human
The traditions vary by island. In some, **Tāne** ([[tane]]) forms the first woman, **Hine-ahu-one**, from the red earth of Kurawaka and breathes life into her, and Tiki is the first man (or Tāne acting as Tiki). In others Tiki himself is the maker, shaping the first human from clay and quickening it — a Polynesian form of the **earth-formed first human**. From Tiki and the earth-woman the human line descends.

## Cross-tradition
Tiki is the Pacific member of the **first-man** neighborhood — the primordial human from whom humanity springs, often shaped from earth or clay — alongside [[adam]] (Hebrew), Manu (Vedic), Ymir and Ask (Norse), Purusha, and Gayōmard. The clay/earth-forming of the first human is one of the most widely-shared cosmogonic motifs.

## Iconography
The carved human figure — the Māori *hei-tiki* of greenstone, the monumental Marquesan and Hawaiian *ki'i* — the ancestral first form.
