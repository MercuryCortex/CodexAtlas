# Bacchus

## Identity
Bacchus is the Roman name for [[dionysus]] — wine-god, ecstatic mystery deity, and patron of vine and theater. The name *Bakkhos* (Greek Βάκχος) was originally a cult-epithet of Dionysus in Greek religion (associated specifically with his ecstatic-initiatory aspect, the *bakkheia*), and entered Latin as the primary name of the god under the Greek-to-Roman *interpretatio*. **Editorial decision for this vault:** Bacchus is given a separate deity-node from [[dionysus]] and [[dionysus-mystery]] not because the deities differ in identity — they do not — but to carry the specifically *Roman cultic-historical material* (the Bacchanalia and their suppression, the Liber Pater complex, the Roman-period mystery iconography) as a distinct evidentiary node. Readers interested in the Greek mythological-cultic Dionysus should consult [[dionysus]]; for the Greek and Hellenistic mystery-religion frame consult [[dionysus-mystery]]; for the Roman-cult-historical material (Bacchanalia, Liber, the iconographic-decorative Bacchic stock that pervaded Pompeian wall-painting and Roman sarcophagi), consult this node.

In native Roman religion the closest indigenous god was **Liber Pater** ("Father Liber" — "the Free One"), an Italic wine-and-fertility god whose cult was paired with that of Ceres and Libera (Persephone) in the Aventine plebeian triad (cult founded -493). From at least the 3rd century BCE Liber Pater was thoroughly assimilated to Bacchus-Dionysus. The most consequential Bacchus-event in Roman history was the **Bacchanalian affair of -186**, when the Senate, alarmed by reports of secret nocturnal Bacchic rites involving men and women together (*Livy* 39.8–18), issued the **Senatus Consultum de Bacchanalibus** (preserved on a bronze tablet from Tiriolo, Calabria, now the oldest surviving Roman senatorial decree) suppressing the cult. The suppression succeeded only partially; Bacchic cult continued in modified forms, and by the Imperial period Bacchic imagery was ubiquitous in elite decorative art.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[dionysus]]** / **[[dionysus-mystery]]** — *ancient-identification* (essentially total): Bacchus is Dionysus under his Roman name. See the editorial note above for why separate nodes exist.
- **Liber Pater** — *ancient-identification* / *syncretic-pre-existing-Italic*: the Italic Liber Pater was the pre-Bacchus Roman wine god; from ~-250 the two are fully assimilated.

## Disputes
- **What actually happened in the Bacchanalia of -186?** The Senate's account (preserved in Livy 39.8–18, written 200 years later) emphasizes sexual transgression, secret nocturnal rites, and conspiratorial cult-organization; modern scholarship (Pailler 1988, North 1979) reads the affair as much more politically motivated — the Senate's reaction to lower-class organized religious networks outside elite control. The factual core of the cult-practice is hard to recover through the polemic.
- **Roman senatorial reaction as proto-religious-persecution.** Some scholars see -186 as the first major Roman precedent for the legal-political suppression of mystery religions, later applied to Druids, Jews, Egyptian cults, and Christians. Others (Beard-North-Price) emphasize the specifically Roman-political nature of the action over religious-typological precedent.
