# Ayyappa

Ayyappa (Ayyappan; **Hariharaputra**, "son of Hari and Hara") is the celibate warrior-god of **Sabarimala** in Kerala — and a deity who *is*, in his very parentage, the reconciliation of Hinduism's two great theistic streams. He is born of the union of **[[shiva]]** (Hara) with **[[vishnu]]** in his female enchantress-form **Mohini** (Hari) — so that he embodies the **Shaiva-Vaishnava synthesis** otherwise depicted as the composite **[[harihara]]**. Abandoned and raised as the foundling **Manikandan** by the king of Pandalam, he grows into a divine hero who slays the buffalo-demoness **Mahishi** and then withdraws to the forest shrine of Sabarimala. His worship centers on one of the **largest annual pilgrimages in the world**: devotees observe a 41-day austerity (*vratam*) of celibacy, simplicity, and black dress, then climb the **eighteen sacred steps** to the hilltop shrine — a discipline whose male-celibate character became the focus of a major 2018 Indian constitutional controversy. Ayyappa is the South Indian god of *dharma*, ascetic discipline, and the unity of the divine.
