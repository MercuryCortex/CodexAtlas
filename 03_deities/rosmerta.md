# Rosmerta

Rosmerta ("the Great Provider," "Good Purveyor") is a Gaulish goddess of **abundance, plenty, and prosperity**, widely worshipped in eastern Gaul and the Rhineland. Her name, built on a Celtic root meaning "to provide / purvey" intensified by the prefix *ro-* ("great"), declares her function: she is the bringer of material well-being. In iconography she carries the classic emblems of plenty — the **cornucopia** (horn of plenty) and the **patera** (offering-dish) — together with a distinctive attribute of her own, a **stave or sceptre topped by a small bucket or butter-churn**, an emblem with no Roman parallel that marks her as a native goddess of stored abundance. She is most often found **paired with the Gallo-Roman [[mercury-roman|Mercury]]** as his consort, and here the Celtic religious pattern is revealing: even where the male god is given the Roman name and form of Mercury, the goddess keeps her **native Celtic name, Rosmerta**, and her own plenty-symbols — a sign that the indigenous goddess of the land's prosperity remained the senior, rooted partner. Rosmerta embodies the Gaulish veneration of provision and increase.
