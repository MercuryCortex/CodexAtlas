# Toyouke-Ōmikami

## Identity
The goddess of food, grain, and harvest, enshrined at the **Outer Shrine (Gekū)** of the Ise Grand Shrine complex — the second most sacred location in Japan, exceeded only by the Inner Shrine (Naikū) of [[amaterasu]] itself. Her name means "Abundant Food Great Deity." Her entire cultic identity is organized around a single theological relationship: she is Amaterasu's **sacred food-provider**. The *Nihon Shoki* records that Amaterasu expressed a desire to not be alone in her divine solitude and requested that a food-deity be brought to Ise — and so Toyouke was transferred from her original enshrinement in Tanba Province (modern Kyoto area) to become Amaterasu's perpetual culinary attendant at Ise.

## Earliest attestation
- *Nihon Shoki* (720 CE) — the divine oracle in which Amaterasu requests Toyouke's presence at Ise; her transfer from Tanba Province.
- **Ise Grand Shrine** — the Outer Shrine (Gekū) is attested from at least the late 5th century CE; the perpetual twice-daily food-offering ritual (*nikki sai*) has been maintained virtually without interruption for over 1,500 years.
- **Engishiki** (927 CE) — detailed listing of Ise ritual protocols including Toyouke's food-offering ceremonies.
- The **Geku** predates textual attestation in oral shrine tradition and may represent a pre-*Kojiki* stratum of agricultural kami veneration.

## Mythological role
Toyouke-Ōmikami's mythology is spare: she has no epic narrative, no drama, no conflict. She is the perpetual presence that feeds the supreme. This is itself theologically significant — the archetype of the **sacred feeder** who makes the transcendent deity's continued existence possible through the material act of nourishment.

The **perpetual offering ritual**: twice a day, every day, sacred food (*shinsen*) is prepared and offered to Toyouke at the Outer Shrine. This is then understood to enable the feeding of Amaterasu. The ritual has continued essentially without interruption for fifteen centuries, making it one of the longest-running ritual cycles in the documented world. This perpetuity is the theological statement: the feeding of heaven by earth is not a one-time mythological event but an ongoing cosmic necessity.

**Silkworm patronage**: Toyouke also governs silk production — *kaiko* (silkworms) are her domain because silk, like grain, is a product of agricultural civilization, and both sustain imperial and religious life. This domain cluster (grain + silk) precisely mirrors the Chinese agricultural-imperial system where silk and grain were the twin pillars of tributary civilization.

**Feeding heaven**: the theological structure is: earth (Toyouke) feeds heaven ([[amaterasu]]); the Outer Shrine (Gekū) enables the Inner Shrine (Naikū). This hierarchical but mutually dependent relationship — the supreme deity requires the earthly food-provider — appears across traditions.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[demeter]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the definitive grain-and-earth goddess of the Greek tradition; sustainer of human life through grain; the goddess whose domain is so fundamental that the supreme deity (Zeus) cannot do without her. Demeter's Eleusinian Mysteries parallel the secret rituals of Ise.
- **[[ceres-roman]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Roman state-cult grain goddess; the official feeding relationship between grain deity and the political/divine order.
- **[[nisaba]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Sumerian grain goddess who serves in Enlil's divine household — the food-goddess serving the supreme deity's household is a cross-cultural constant. Nisaba was scribe and grain-keeper; Toyouke is food-preparer; both are essential household members of the divine sovereign.
- **[[inari]]** — *folk-syncretism*: overlapping rice and harvest domains; widely venerated in parallel; regional folk conflation is common though shrine theology distinguishes them.
- **[[mama-sara]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Andean grain mother (Mama Sara / Sara Mama); the sacred female personification of grain as fundamental deity of agricultural civilization.
- **[[changing-woman]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Navajo goddess of seasons and agricultural renewal; the earth-mother who sustains human life through her seasonal generativity.

## Iconography
Rarely depicted anthropomorphically; the Outer Shrine at Ise, like the Inner Shrine, maintains strict aniconic practice. Symbolically associated with rice, grain sheaves, silk cocoons, and the ritual food-offerings (*shinsen*) prepared daily in her honor.

## Disputes
The relationship between Toyouke-Ōmikami and other food deities (*Ukemochi-no-Kami*, *Ōgetsu-Hime*) in the Shinto tradition is debated. Some scholars argue Toyouke is a Tanba-region deity who was elevated to Ise through political maneuvering; others see her as an independent and archaic stratum of grain worship predating the *Nihon Shoki*'s formalization.
