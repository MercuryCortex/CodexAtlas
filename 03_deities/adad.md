# Adad

Adad (Akkadian *Adad/Addu*; Sumerian **Ishkur**) is the Mesopotamian god of **storm, thunder, and rain** — and one of the clearest threads tying the religions of the ancient Near East together, for he is the same deity as West-Semitic **[[baal-hadad|Hadad]]**: the name *Hadad* underlies them both, and the great Storm-god temple of **Aleppo** served this shared Syro-Mesopotamian god for over a millennium. Like every Near-Eastern weather-god he has a **double face**: the bringer of the **life-giving rains** that make the dry land fertile, and the wielder of the **destructive storm and flood** that smites enemies and lays cities waste. His emblems are the **lightning-fork** and the **bull**, and his voice is the thunder. Alongside the sun-god **[[utu-shamash|Shamash]]** he is one of the two "lords of divination," presiding over **extispicy** (reading the entrails). Equated with Hurrian **[[teshub]]** and Hittite Tarhunna, and continuous with Canaanite Baal-Hadad, Adad sits at the centre of the great Near-Eastern storm-god family whose ramifications — through Baal's combat with the Sea, and beyond — reach into the Hebrew Bible's storm-theophanies and far across the ancient world.
