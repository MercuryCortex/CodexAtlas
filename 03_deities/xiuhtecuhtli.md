# Xiuhtecuhtli

Xiuhtecuhtli (Nahuatl, "Turquoise/Year Lord") — in his ancient aspect **Huehueteotl**, "the Old God" — is the Aztec god of **fire** and the lord of the **cosmic center**. Huehueteotl is among the very oldest deities of Mesoamerica: hunched, wrinkled old-man braziers bearing a fire-bowl on the head appear at Cuicuilco and Teotihuacan centuries before the Aztecs. As Xiuhtecuhtli he sits at the **navel of the universe** — the hearth-fire at the middle of the four directional quarters — and rules the turquoise diadem (*xiuhuitzolli*) of Aztec kingship, for the ruler is his earthly fire-keeper. His weapon and *nahual* is the **fire-serpent Xiuhcoatl**, the bolt that slays Coyolxauhqui. Most importantly he governs **time**: every 52 years, at the close of a "bundle" of years, all fires in the Aztec world were extinguished and, at the **New Fire Ceremony** atop the Hill of the Star, a new fire was drilled on the breast of a sacrificial victim to rekindle the sun and grant the cosmos another cycle — the rite over which Xiuhtecuhtli, lord of fire and the year, presided.
