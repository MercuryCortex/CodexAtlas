# Hokmah (Hebrew Wisdom)

## Identity
*Hokmah* (חכמה, "wisdom") is the personification of divine Wisdom in the Hebrew Bible and Second Temple literature — a semi-independent divine feminine figure who stands alongside God at creation, calls humans to her feast, and reveals the order embedded in the cosmos. She is not a fully separate deity (Hebrew monotheism prevents this), but she is considerably more than a poetic abstraction: she speaks in the first person, claims pre-existence before creation, and acts as God's artisan or companion at the fashioning of the world.

## Earliest attestation
**Proverbs 8:22–31** is the locus classicus: "The LORD possessed me at the beginning of his work, the first of his acts of old. Ages ago I was set up, at the first, before the beginning of the earth... when he marked out the foundations of the earth, then I was beside him, like a master workman, and I was daily his delight, rejoicing before him always." Proverbs 9 extends the figure: Wisdom builds her house, sets her table, and invites all who lack understanding to eat and drink.

**Sirach (Ben Sira) 24** (~180 BCE): Wisdom speaks from God's mouth, dwells in a pillar of cloud, and is commanded to "make your dwelling in Jacob" — an identification with Torah that becomes standard in rabbinic thought.

**Wisdom of Solomon** (~50 BCE, Alexandria): The most developed Hellenized account — Hokmah/Sophia is described as "a breath of the power of God, and a pure emanation of the glory of the Almighty... a reflection of eternal light, a spotless mirror of the working of God" (7:25–26). This is the text the Gnostics and Philo of Alexandria both develop.

## Why this matters: the ancestor-figure

Hokmah is the direct ancestor of three distinct theological traditions that the Codex Atlas traces:

1. **[[sophia-gnostic]]** — the Gnostic Sophia inherits the Hokmah-figure but radically transforms her: from God's wise partner in creation to a *fallen* aeon whose hubris generates the broken cosmos. Pearson 1990 argues this inversion is rooted in Jewish-Gnostic polemics with mainstream Judaism.

2. **[[logos-johannine]]** — the prologue of John's Gospel (1:1–14) is Wisdom-Christology: Jesus = the pre-existent Logos who was "with God in the beginning" and "through whom all things were made" — the same claims made for Hokmah in Proverbs 8. The Logos hymn follows the Wisdom pattern so closely that many NT scholars argue it was a Wisdom poem adapted for Christ (Dunn 1980, Witherington 1994).

3. **[[logos-philonic]]** — Philo of Alexandria (~20 BCE–50 CE) identifies Wisdom/Sophia with the Logos, the rational principle mediating between God and creation. His Logos theology is the direct bridge between Hokmah and both the Johannine Logos and the Neoplatonic Nous.
