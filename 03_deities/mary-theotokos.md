# Mary (Theotokos)

Mother of [[jesus-christ-deity|Jesus Christ]] in the canonical Gospels; dogmatically defined as *Theotokos* — "God-bearer" — at the Council of Ephesus (+431), against Nestorius who preferred *Christotokos* ("Christ-bearer"). The dogmatic stakes were Christological: if Mary bore the divine Logos in flesh, then the human and divine natures are united in a single subject; she is Mother of God, not merely mother of the man Jesus.

Mariology developed dramatically from the patristic period onward:
- **Perpetual virginity** (4th c. onward, dogmatic for Catholic and Orthodox)
- **Immaculate Conception** (Catholic dogma, defined 1854 by Pius IX — Mary conceived without original sin)
- **Assumption** (Catholic dogma, defined 1950 by Pius XII — bodily assumed into heaven)
- **Co-redemptrix / Mediatrix** (theological proposals, not formally dogmatized; Protestant rejection)

Eastern Orthodox veneration is structurally different: focused on liturgical commemoration and iconography (Hodegetria, Eleousa, Pokrov), not on the Western "Immaculate Conception" framework.

Marian apparitions (Guadalupe 1531, Lourdes 1858, Fatima 1917) — function in popular devotion as ongoing revelations; theological status varies by tradition.

Comparative-religion: Marina Warner and R.E. Witt argue for substantial late-antique Mediterranean continuity between [[isis-hellenistic|Isis-with-Horus]] iconography and Mary-with-Christ-child iconography, especially in Egypt and the Eastern Mediterranean. The Ephesus council was held in a city of long-standing Artemis devotion; the popular acclaim of Mary-as-Theotokos there was historically continuous with goddess-veneration patterns. Mariology fills the [[divine-feminine]] role otherwise absent from Trinitarian Christianity's masculine-coded persons.
