# Prajāpati

## Identity
Prajāpati ("Lord of Creatures") rises to prominence in the late Rig Veda (Maṇḍala 10's late hymns, including the famous Hiraṇyagarbha hymn 10.121 and the Puruṣa Sūkta 10.90) and becomes the dominant creator-god of the Brāhmaṇa period. He emerges from the cosmic waters or from a golden embryo (*hiraṇyagarbha*), creates the worlds and gods, and in many Brāhmaṇa narratives is *himself the original sacrifice* — he dismembers himself to make the cosmos, and the priestly sacrifice re-assembles him.

In the Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa and elsewhere Prajāpati is the cosmic principle that the sacrifice imitates and restores. His ascendancy reflects the Brāhmaṇa period's deepening interest in cosmogonic principles over the older Rig Vedic deva-pantheon of warrior-storm-fire-dawn personalities. In later classical Hinduism Prajāpati is partly displaced by Brahmā as creator (Brahmā assuming many of his functions), and in some texts the names are used interchangeably. The Puruṣa Sūkta (RV 10.90), which describes the cosmic Person's self-sacrifice giving rise to all things including the four varṇas, is the most influential single hymn for later Indian cosmogony and social theology.
