# Mímir

## Identity
Mímir is the wisest being in the Norse cosmos — not the wisest god, but the wisest *being*, a status shared or contested only with Odin himself (who obtained that wisdom from Mímir's well). He belongs to the oldest strata of Norse myth, associated with the primordial well *Mímisbrunnr* ("Mímir's well") that lies beneath one of the three roots of Yggdrasil. His name is cognate with Proto-Germanic *\*mēmaz* and Old High German *mîman* — all connected to the root for "memory" and "remembrance." Mímir *is* cosmic memory made hypostatic.

## Earliest attestation
*Vǫluspá* 28–29 (the Eddic poem recited to Odin by the Völva seeress) refers to Mímir's well and Odin's sacrifice of his eye: "I know that Óðinn, / where you hid your eye, / in the far-famed / well of Mímir" (*Larrington trans.*). *Ynglinga saga* (Snorri's *Heimskringla*, ~1230) provides the narrative of the Aesir–Vanir War and Mímir's fate as a peace-hostage. These are 13th-century CE manuscripts preserving oral traditions estimated to the Migration and Viking ages.

## Mythological role
**The Eye of Odin.** Odin traveled to Mímisbrunnr and asked Mímir for a drink from the well of wisdom. Mímir demanded Odin's left eye as payment. Odin gave it; Mímir keeps the eye submerged in the well, where it sees all things. This is the mythological explanation of Odin's one-eyed appearance and of the disproportionate wisdom he carries.

**The Aesir–Vanir War hostage.** When the two divine tribes, the Aesir and the Vanir, exchanged hostages as part of a peace treaty, the Aesir sent Mímir and Hœnir to the Vanir. The Vanir found Hœnir to be stupid and felt deceived; they decapitated Mímir and sent his head back to the Aesir. Odin embalmed the head with herbs and sang spells over it, preserving it as a living oracle that he could consult when he needed counsel. *Vǫluspá* 46 describes Odin going to consult Mímir's head on the eve of Ragnarǫk.

**The Well.** Mímisbrunnr is one of three cosmic wells at the roots of Yggdrasil: the Well of Urðr (fate, governed by the Norns), Hvergelmir (the source of all rivers, in the realm of Niflheim), and Mímisbrunnr (wisdom). The tripartite well structure suggests Mímir occupies the cosmic *sapiential* domain — the primordial source of knowledge as distinct from fate or life.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[bran-the-blessed]]** — *cross-tradition-parallel*: the Celtic severed head that continues to speak and counsel is the closest structural parallel in any other tradition. Bran's decapitated head accompanies his companions across the sea for eighty years, speaking wisdom and preventing decay. Mímir's head counsels Odin after decapitation. Both suggest an archaic Indo-European or North European motif: the wise head separated from the body retains — and perhaps concentrates — its cognitive power.
- **[[enki-ea]]** — *cross-tradition-parallel*: Mímisbrunnr as a primordial sub-terrestrial reservoir of wisdom parallels Enki's Abzu — the freshwater abyss beneath the earth from which all wisdom, craft, and divine order flow. Both are accessed by the supreme deity under exceptional conditions. The cosmological structure (primordial water beneath the world-axis = source of sacred knowledge) is one of the oldest religious topographies in the ancient world.
- **[[thoth]]** — *cross-tradition-parallel*: both are the living repositories of cosmic memory and the counselors of the divine assembly; Thoth holds the "secret of heaven and earth" as Mímir holds the submerged eye of Odin.
- **[[nabu]]** — *cross-tradition-parallel*: lord of all wisdom, keeper of the tablet of fates; the divine-counselor / wisdom-scribe archetype.
- **[[odin]]** — *mythological partner*: the Odin–Mímir relationship is generative: Odin's defining characteristic (one eye, extreme wisdom) is caused by Mímir's demands. The relationship also encodes the Norse theological principle that ultimate wisdom costs ultimate sacrifice.
