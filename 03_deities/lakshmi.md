# Lakshmi

## Identity
Lakṣmī (also Śrī) is the consort of Viṣṇu and the goddess of wealth, fortune, beauty, and auspiciousness in Hindu tradition. She emerges from the cosmic churning of the ocean (the *Samudra Manthana* myth, Mahābhārata) as one of the fourteen precious things gained by the gods. She accompanies Viṣṇu in his avatāric descents — as Sītā with Rāma, as Rukmiṇī (and the gopīs / Rādhā in later Bhāgavata-influenced theology) with Kṛṣṇa. In the [[phase-5-016-ramanuja-sribhasya]] tradition (Śrī Vaiṣṇavism), she is the eternal mediator between souls and Viṣṇu — *Śrī* being the founder-name of the school. Iconographically: seated on a lotus, holding lotuses, elephants beside her pouring water from raised trunks. The *Lakṣmī Pūjā* is the central rite of Diwali in much of Hindu India.

## Earliest attestation
Śrī Sūkta (a *khila* appendix to the Rig Veda, post-Vedic but ancient — ~-500); systematic Purāṇic narrative in Viṣṇu Purāṇa (~400 CE).
