# Michael (Archangel)

## Identity
Chief archangel of the Abrahamic angelic hierarchy. Hebrew *Mîkhāʾēl* — "Who is like God?" — a rhetorical name asserting divine incomparability, used as a war-cry against the dragon (Rev 12). Across Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, Michael is the prince of the heavenly host, defender of Israel/the people of God, and psychopomp of souls at the moment of death and at the Last Judgment.

## Earliest attestation
- **[[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]]** 10:13, 10:21, 12:1 — Michael is "one of the chief princes," guardian of Israel against the angelic princes of Persia and Greece; rises at the eschaton.
- **[[phase-3-004-1-enoch]]** 9–10, 20, 24, 40, 54, 60, 71 — Michael as one of the four (and later seven) archangels who intercede on behalf of humanity against the Watchers.
- **[[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]]** (1QM War Scroll) — "Prince of Light" leading the Sons of Light against Belial.
- **NT** — Jude 9 (dispute with Satan over the body of Moses), Revelation 12:7 (Michael and his angels vs. the dragon).
- **[[phase-4-034-quran]]** Surah 2:98 — Mikha'il named alongside Jibril/Gabriel as God's messengers, whoever is their enemy is the enemy of God.

## Mythological role
**Warrior**: leads the heavenly host against rebel angels and the dragon. **Psychopomp**: Eastern Orthodox and Catholic iconography depicts him weighing souls (*psychostasia*) at the particular judgment — a function inherited from Egyptian Anubis via Greco-Roman *psychopompos* iconography. **Protector**: guardian-prince of Israel in Daniel becomes guardian of the Church in Christian patristic exegesis; Michael appears in Constantinian dream-vision tradition (Sozomen 2.3) as defender of the new Christian empire. **Eschaton**: rises at the great resurrection (Daniel 12:1; 1 Thess 4:16 "with the voice of the archangel" is patristically identified as Michael).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[gabriel-archangel]]** — co-archangel; Quran 2:98 pairs them as primary angelic messengers.
- **[[raphael-archangel]] / [[uriel-archangel]] / [[saraqael-archangel]] / [[raguel-archangel]] / [[remiel-archangel]]** — the seven archangels of 1 Enoch 20.
- **[[anubis]]** — *parallel-motif* (psychopomp / weighing of the heart) — Egyptian *Book of the Dead* 125 weighing-scene as the iconographic source-stratum for Christian Michael-psychostasia (Hermann 1956; Mâle 1922).
- **[[hermes-greek]]** — *parallel-motif* (psychopomp); Greek *psychopompos* function transfers iconographically to Michael in Christian late antiquity.
- **[[the-elect-one-enochic]]** — *scholarly-parallel* (Hannah 1999): some scholars argue Michael-traditions are a substrate for the pre-Christian Son of Man figure.

## Iconography
Armored warrior with sword and shield; dragon (Satan/the devil) underfoot; scales for *psychostasia* in late-medieval Western art (Last Judgment tympana, Weighing of Souls scenes from c. 1100 onward). In Eastern Orthodox icons: military robes with spear, weighing scales, and rolled scroll. Mont Saint-Michel (France) and Sacra di San Michele (Italy) are on a famous "Sword-of-Michael" ley line of seven sanctuaries — folk-syncretism layer.
