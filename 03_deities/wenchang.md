# Wenchang

Wenchang (*Wénchāng* 文昌, "Flourishing of Letters"; as a deity, **Wenchang Dijun**, "Divine Lord Wenchang") is the Chinese god of **literature, culture, and above all success in the imperial civil-service examinations** — the system through which, for over a millennium, scholars rose to office. Hopeful candidates across late-imperial China prayed and offered to him for a passing grade and a fortunate official career, making him one of the most important deities of the **literati class** and a fixture of both Confucian-tinged civic religion and the Daoist pantheon. He is associated with the **Wenchang constellation** (near the Dipper) and was identified with a deified Sichuan figure, the spirit of **Zitong (Zhang Yazi)**, whose "Book of Transformations" recounts his many incarnations. In temple iconography he wears the robes of a **scholar-official** and is attended by **Kuixing**, the grotesque, one-legged personification of the examination-star, and by Zhu Yi ("Red-Coat"). As a patron of letters and learning he occupies, in the Chinese cosmos, a niche comparable to learning-deities elsewhere such as the Hindu **[[saraswati|Saraswati]]**. Wenchang embodies the Chinese sacralization of literacy, scholarship, and meritocratic ascent.
