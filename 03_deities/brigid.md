# Brigid

## Identity
Triple-goddess of the Tuatha Dé Danann — **Brigid of poetry, Brigid of smithcraft, Brigid of healing** (three sisters all called Brigid per Cormac's Glossary, or one goddess with three functional aspects). Daughter of [[dagda]], wife of Bres. The **single most legible Celtic-pagan-to-Christian-saint syncretism case in any tradition**: the pre-Christian goddess and the 6th-c. Christian Saint Brigid of Kildare share name, principal cult-center (Kildare = *Cill Dara* "Church of the Oak," continuous pagan-to-Christian sacred grove), perpetual flame (the Kildare flame, attended by nineteen nuns and never lit by male hands, was maintained continuously into the medieval period and revived in 1993 by the Brigidine Sisters), feast day (**February 1 / Imbolc**, the Celtic spring-quarter festival), iconographic attributes, and protective functions.

## Mythological role
**Imbolc**: her feast at the start of February marks the **return of light** in the Celtic year — a "Candlemas" equivalent that the Christian Candlemas (February 2) actually post-dates and reflects. **Triple aspect**: Brigid of *the poets, the file* (poetic inspiration as fire); Brigid of *the smiths* (forge-fire and metalcraft); Brigid of *the leeches/physicians* (healing fire). **Cath Maige Tuired**: she invents *keening* — the Irish lament for the dead — over her son Ruadán slain in the Second Battle of Mag Tuired.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Saint Brigid of Kildare** — *folk-syncretism*: the load-bearing case of pagan-goddess-becomes-Christian-saint.
- **[[hestia]] / [[vesta]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: hearth-fire with attendant virgins maintaining perpetual flame.
- **[[saraswati]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: goddess of poetic-creative flow.
- **Sulis** (Roman-British Bath) — *scholarly-parallel*: Celtic healing-fire-water goddess identified with Minerva (*Sulis Minerva*); structural cognate of Brigid's healing aspect.
