# Persephone

## Identity
Persephone (also *Kore*, "the Maiden") is the Greek goddess of the underworld, queen of Hades, and daughter of Demeter and Zeus. She has a double aspect — as the abducted maiden whose absence and return govern the agricultural cycle, and as the dread queen of the dead — that makes her one of the most polyvalent figures in Greek religion. Her cult is central to the Eleusinian Mysteries, the most prestigious religious institution of classical Greece.

## Earliest attestation
- Linear B fragments may attest a Mycenaean *Persa*; uncertain.
- Hesiod's *Theogony* and the *Homeric Hymn to Demeter* (~-650/-600) are the foundational literary texts.
- The Eleusinian sanctuary site is in continuous cultic use from the late Bronze Age through the Theodosian closures (~+392).

## Mythological role
- **Abduction by Hades.** While gathering flowers, Persephone is seized by Hades and carried to the underworld. Demeter, grieving, withholds growth from the earth. Zeus mediates a compromise: Persephone has eaten pomegranate seeds in the underworld (food of the dead), binding her there for part of the year; she returns to her mother for the rest. The seasonal cycle is grounded in this drama.
- **Queen of the underworld.** Persephone is the dreaded enthroned queen who receives the dead, presides at funeral rites, and answers prayers concerning the deceased.
- **Mystery-cult patroness.** With Demeter, Persephone is the central figure of the Eleusinian Mysteries — the *mystai* (initiates) participated ritually in the mother-and-daughter drama and were promised a better afterlife.
- **Orphic-Bacchic context.** In Orphic theology, Persephone (with Zeus) is mother of Zagreus / the first Dionysus. See [[zagreus]], [[dionysus-mystery]].

## Cross-tradition identifications
- Proserpina (Roman) — direct *interpretatio graeca*.
- **[[isis-hellenistic]]** — partial syncretism in Hellenistic mystery contexts via the wandering-grieving-mother / underworld-queen overlap.

## Iconography
- Pomegranate (the binding food)
- Torch (her descent and Demeter's search)
- Wheat sheaf (Eleusinian agricultural context)
- Crowned, often enthroned beside Hades
- Sometimes shown as the *Kore* maiden, sometimes as the dread queen
