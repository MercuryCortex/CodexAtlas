# Baiame

## Identity
Sky-father figure attested in the religious traditions of several southeastern Australian Aboriginal nations — the Wiradjuri, Kamilaroi, Wonnarua, Eora, and related nations of what is now New South Wales. Baiame is associated with the sky, with law-giving (he established the *bora* initiation ceremonies), with the bull-roarer (the ritual instrument that signals his approach), and with the Milky Way. The status of Baiame in comparative-religion history is theologically and politically contested: 19th-c. comparative religion (Andrew Lang especially, *The Making of Religion* 1898) seized on Baiame as evidence of "primitive monotheism," using him to argue against E.B. Tylor's evolutionary-animism account; W.E.H. Stanner partly continued this. Current scholarship (Swain 1993; Sutton 2003) emphasizes that Baiame is *specific* to certain southeastern Aboriginal nations, not pan-Aboriginal, and warns against assimilating him to Mediterranean-derived "High God" categories.

## Earliest attestation
First systematic documentation: A.W. Howitt *The Native Tribes of South-east Australia* (1904); R.H. Mathews's various papers (1890s–1900s); some earlier scattered missionary reports. Indigenous transmission much older — bull-roarer initiation traditions associated with Baiame are old by archaeological measures.

## Mythological role
Baiame is creator-and-law-giver: he made the rivers, mountains, animals; he established the *bora* (initiation grounds, recognizable in some surviving earthworks across NSW); he gave the laws by which Aboriginal society functions; he can hear and see all from the sky. In some traditions he is associated with Daramulun (sometimes son, sometimes alternate name); the bull-roarer's voice is Daramulun's (or Baiame's).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Christian God** — *folk-syncretism* (19th-c. missionary): missionaries (and Indigenous Christians like David Unaipon, an early-20th-c. Ngarrindjeri writer-evangelist) translated Christian God with Baiame in southeastern Australia. The translation has had complex effects on Indigenous Christian-Aboriginal religious synthesis.

## Disputes
- **"High God" status.** Andrew Lang (1898) and 19th-c. comparative-religion used Baiame to argue Aboriginal religion had an original High-God concept (against Tylorian evolutionary animism). Current scholarship treats Baiame as a *specific* southeastern figure and warns against the comparative-religion reading.
- **Pan-Aboriginal claims.** Some 20th-c. material treats Baiame as a pan-Aboriginal sky-father; current scholarship rejects this. Aboriginal religion is nation-specific; Baiame is southeastern.
- **Christian-period reshaping.** The southeastern Aboriginal nations experienced colonial violence (frontier killings, missionization) very early in the European colonization process; how much of post-1820s Baiame material reflects Christian-period reshaping is unclear and is intensely contested. Swain 1993 argues for significant Christian-period reshaping.

## Iconography
The sky; the Milky Way; the bull-roarer.
