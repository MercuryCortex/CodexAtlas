# YHWH (Yahweh)

## Identity
YHWH (the Tetragrammaton, traditionally vocalized "Yahweh" by modern scholars; pronounced "Adonai" / "the Lord" by Jews in liturgical practice from ~Hellenistic period onward) is the national God of Israel and Judah and, in the developed religion of post-exilic Judaism, the only God of the universe. The trajectory from "tribal storm-god of Iron Age Levantine highland clans" to "transcendent universal creator" is the major story Hebrew Bible scholarship reconstructs.

The earliest extra-biblical attestation is widely held to be Egyptian topographical lists from Soleb temple (~-1400 BCE) and Amarah West naming "Yhw3 of the Shasu" — Bedouin / nomad groups in the southern Levant / Edomite area. The biblical Sinai / Seir / Teman traditions (Deut 33:2, Judg 5:4–5, Hab 3:3) point to a southern desert origin for YHWH. By the Iron Age I (~-1200 onward) YHWH is the god of "Israel" emerging in the Levantine highlands; by the divided monarchy (-922 onward) YHWH is national god of both Israel and Judah. The Mesha Stele (~-840 BCE, Moabite) is the earliest dated extra-biblical attestation of YHWH by that exact spelling. Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions (~-800) attest "YHWH and his Asherah", confirming pre-exilic popular polytheism within Israelite religion that the prophets attacked.

YHWH was identified with the Canaanite high god El at some early stage (the names Israel — "El strives" — Bethel, Eliab, etc., preserve the El theology). Deut 32:8–9 (in the older Septuagint and Dead Sea Scrolls reading, "according to the number of the sons of God / Elohim", later Masoretically corrected to "sons of Israel") preserves a fossil verse in which El Elyon assigns nations to subordinate gods, with YHWH receiving Israel — a residue of an older Israelite-Canaanite theology in which YHWH was *one of the sons of El* rather than identical with El. The eventual identification of YHWH with El, then with all elohim, is the textual movement *toward* what later becomes monotheism. The exilic experience (586 BCE Babylonian conquest) and the Persian period (539–332 BCE) catalyze the full monotheizing reform; Second Isaiah ("I am YHWH, and there is no other") makes the move explicit.
