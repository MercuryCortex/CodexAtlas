## Narada — Divine Messenger and Musician

Narada (*Nārada*, Sanskrit नारद) is the pan-Hindu divine sage, messenger, and musician who travels freely between the human and divine realms. A mind-born son of **[[brahma]]**, he carries the *Mahati* vīṇā ("the great one") as his cosmic credential and instrument. In the *Natya Shastra* origin narrative, Brahma transmits music and the arts to Narada, who then teaches them to Bharata Muni — positioning Narada as the crucial intermediary in the divine-to-human transmission of sacred musical and dramatic knowledge.

Narada appears throughout the *Ramayana*, *Mahabharata*, and the Puranas as a storyteller, divine messenger, and occasionally a **trickster** who deliberately sets events in motion (carrying word, sowing fruitful discord, testing devotion). He is the patron of narrative itself as well as of music, embodying *both* sonic and textual transmission — and as the perfect *bhakta* (devotee) endlessly chanting "Nārāyaṇa," he is also the model of loving devotion to Vishnu. As the god who shuttles messages between heaven and earth, he is the Hindu instance of the **divine-herald** type found across traditions (compare **[[hermes-greek|Hermes]]**, Iris, and Gabriel).
