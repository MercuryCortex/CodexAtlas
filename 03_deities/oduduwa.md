# Oduduwa

## Identity
Oduduwa is the Yoruba deity of creation, the founding ancestor of all Yoruba royal dynasties, and the owner of the earth. He is simultaneously a deity (*orisha*) and the first ancestor — the distinction between the two blurs in Yoruba tradition, where the most powerful ancestors become orisha. The sacred city of **Ile-Ife** in southwestern Nigeria is considered the place where he created the earth and the point from which all Yoruba peoples and kingdoms ultimately derive their legitimacy.

The name "Odùduwà" likely means "the one who creates the substance of existence" or "he who owns the bottoms of the calabash" — both interpretations link to his creation function.

## Creation myth
In the most widespread version: Olodumare (the supreme deity) sent Oduduwa from heaven at a time when primordial waters covered everything. He descended on an iron chain, carrying a calabash of sand and a five-toed hen (*adiye arun ika*). He poured the sand onto the waters; the hen scratched and spread it, creating dry land. The chameleon (*ogiri-oko*) walked on the new land to test its firmness. Ile-Ife, where the chain touched down, became the first dry land — the navel of creation.

Multiple competing versions exist, reflecting the political use of Oduduwa mythology across different Yoruba kingdoms:
- In some versions, Obatala (a different orisha) was originally sent but got drunk on palm wine; Oduduwa completed the task
- In others, Oduduwa and Obatala are rivals who must cooperate
- In Ekiti and Ijesa traditions, Oduduwa is female rather than male

This multiplicity is not corruption but evidence of a living oral tradition where different communities adapt the founding myth to their own political and theological needs.

## Founding of Yoruba kingdoms
The 16 kings (*obas*) of the major Yoruba kingdoms trace their dynasties to Oduduwa's children and grandchildren who dispersed from Ile-Ife. The **Ooni of Ife** is considered Oduduwa's direct descendant and the spiritual head of the Yoruba nation. The Benin kingdom (Edo) also traces royal lineage to an Ile-Ife/Oduduwa source — making Oduduwa's genealogical reach one of the widest of any deity in West Africa.

This gives Oduduwa an unusual dual status: active cosmological deity AND historical royal ancestor, with the second function increasingly prominent in modern Yoruba nationalism (the Odua People's Congress uses his name).

## Diaspora reception
In Cuban Lucumí/Santería and Brazilian Candomblé, Oduduwa is an *orisha* associated with the deepest levels of creation, sometimes kept in a sealed container that is never opened. His presence in the Diaspora reflects his foundational status — some Lucumí traditions consider Oduduwa too fundamental to be directly approached in ordinary ritual.
