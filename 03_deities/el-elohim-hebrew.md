# El / Elohim (Hebrew)

## Identity
El is the high god of the early Israelite patriarchal tradition — the deity invoked under various epithets (El Shaddai, El Olam, El Elyon, El Bethel, El Roi) by Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in the Genesis patriarchal narratives. *Elohim* is the standard Hebrew word for "God" / "gods" — grammatically plural but used in the Hebrew Bible most often with singular agreement when referring to YHWH (a "plural of majesty" or fossilized plural usage). The E source of the Pentateuch uses *Elohim* before the revelation of the name YHWH at the burning bush (Exod 3); the P source likewise. The patriarchs in Genesis worship Elohim / El under his various epithets and do not (in the E and P chronology) know the name YHWH.

The Israelite El is — per the now-mainstream Mark Smith / Frank Cross / John Day reconstruction — substantially the same figure as the Canaanite El of Ugaritic and broader West Semitic religion: aged king of the divine council, "father of years", often depicted enthroned in a bull's-horn cap, often paired with Asherah as consort. The fossil verse Deut 32:8–9 (in its older textual reading, restored from LXX and Qumran 4QDeut^j) shows El Elyon as the high god dividing the nations among his sons, with YHWH receiving Israel as his portion. The eventual identification YHWH ≡ El, then YHWH ≡ "Elohim" universally, then YHWH as *the only* Elohim, is the developmental arc by which Israelite religion moves from a Canaanite-pattern polytheism with a high god to exclusivist monotheism. *Per the splitting rule of this vault, El-Canaanite and El-Elohim-Hebrew are separate nodes connected by a syncretic-identification edge.*

## Vault Connections

**HYPER WIN — Israel's name contains the proof.** The nation is called *Yisra-el* — the second element is unambiguously *El*, the Canaanite deity. The name means "El strives," "El rules," or "El prevails." Not "YHWH-strives" — *El*-strives. The nation was named for El before it identified its god with YHWH. Genesis 32:28 offers the name-etymology in the wrestling scene (Jacob wrestles "God" and is renamed) — but the etymology makes transparent what the name already contained. Israel = "the people of El" before it became "the people of Yahweh."

**MASSIVE WIN — "I am YHWH your God / I am El Shaddai" as the merger moment.** In the J source (Genesis), the patriarchs encounter God under El-names (El Shaddai, El Elyon, El Olam) and do not know the name YHWH. In the E source (also early), same picture. The P source makes the identification explicit: Exodus 6:2–3 — "God spoke to Moses and said to him, 'I am YHWH. I appeared to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob as El Shaddai, but by my name YHWH I was not known to them.'" This is the biblical text's own account of the El-YHWH merger: the God who spoke to the patriarchs as El Shaddai is now declaring himself to be YHWH. The Bible documents its own religious-historical development in plain sight: El and YHWH are being explicitly equated by the P author (c. 500 BCE) as part of the monotheistic consolidation.

**John 10:34 ← Psalm 82 ← Ugaritic El: three-tradition transmission in one Gospel citation.** Jesus quotes Psalm 82:6 ("I said you are gods") to defend himself against the charge of blasphemy. Psalm 82 is a Hebrew Bible divine-council scene (Elohim/El presides over a council of gods). The Ugaritic divine-council scene of El presiding over the *phr bn ilm* is the source tradition for Psalm 82. So: Ugaritic El's divine council (c. 1350 BCE) → Psalm 82's Hebrew council scene (c. 600–400 BCE) → Jesus citing Psalm 82 in a Johannine theological argument (c. 90 CE). The same theological image — God presiding over a council of divine beings — travels from Ugarit to the New Testament in 1,400 years. Three moments on one unbroken line.
