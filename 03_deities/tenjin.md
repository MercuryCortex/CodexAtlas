# Tenjin

Tenjin (天神) is the Shinto **kami of scholarship and learning** — and one of the clearest cases in any religion of a historical human becoming a great god. He is the deified **Sugawara no Michizane** (845–903), a brilliant scholar-official and poet of the Heian court who was slandered by the rival Fujiwara, demoted, and exiled to distant Dazaifu, where he died in disgrace. In the decades after his death, a cascade of disasters struck the capital — lightning strikes on the imperial palace that killed his accusers, plague, drought, the deaths of emperors — all attributed to his vengeful ghost (*goryō*). To **placate** this furious spirit the court posthumously restored his rank, burned the decrees of exile, and enshrined him as a god of thunder; once pacified, his learned aspect prevailed, and he became **Tenjin**, the benevolent patron of letters. Today the **Tenmangū** shrines (head shrines at Kitano in Kyoto and Dazaifu in Kyushu) are thronged by students praying for examination success, who hang plaques on his sacred **plum** trees — a vivid living cult born from the Japanese theology of the angry, then honored, dead.
