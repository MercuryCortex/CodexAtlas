# Shara

## Identity

Shara (Šara) is the tutelary warrior-god of the Sumerian city of **Umma**, where his great temple, the E-mah, stood. He is a young heroic god, named in the tradition as a **son of [[inanna-sumerian]]**, and his cult is bound up with the identity and military fortunes of Umma — the city whose long border-war with neighboring Lagash is among the earliest documented conflicts in human history.

## Mythological role

Shara's best-known mythological appearance is in the *Descent of Inanna to the Netherworld*. When Inanna returns from the dead, she is accompanied by the demons (*galla*) who must take a substitute back to the underworld in her place. She finds that several of her gods — among them Shara, her own son — have been faithfully mourning her, sitting in the dust and dressed in mourning rags. Moved by their devotion, Inanna refuses to surrender them to the demons. (It is the un-mourning [[dumuzi-tammuz]], found enthroned and untroubled, whom she finally hands over.) Shara thus appears as the loyal divine son, set in deliberate contrast to the negligent husband.

## Cross-tradition significance

Shara is a representative of the Sumerian **city warrior-god** type — the heroic young god who embodies and defends his city, of which [[ninurta]]/Ningirsu (Lagash) is the most prominent. The proliferation of such tutelary war-gods, one per city, is a structural feature of the Sumerian pantheon, reflecting a landscape of competing city-states each with its divine champion. As a son of Inanna and a mourner in her Descent, Shara also threads into the great dying-god and underworld-substitute complex that runs through Mesopotamian religion.
