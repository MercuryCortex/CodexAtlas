# Ninlil

## Identity
Ninlil ("Lady Wind" / "Lady of the Air") is the chief consort of [[enlil]] in the Sumerian pantheon and the mother of [[nanna-sin]] (the moon god), [[nergal]] (lord of the netherworld), and (in some traditions) Ninurta and Ninazu. Her principal cult center is Nippur — specifically the Tummal sanctuary within the Ekur temple complex — where she is queen alongside Enlil. In Shuruppak she appears under the pre-marriage name **Sud**, a grain goddess in her own right (per *Enlil and Sud*); the myth narrates Enlil's courtship and her elevation to consort under the new name Ninlil. She is one of the most stable members of the senior Sumerian pantheon, present from Early Dynastic god lists through the Akkadian-Babylonian transformation into Mullissu.

## Earliest attestation
- Early Dynastic god lists from Fara/Shuruppak and Abu Salabikh (~-2600 to -2500) list NIN.LÍL alongside Enlil among the senior gods.
- The Early Dynastic Zame Hymns (Abu Salabikh, ~-2500) include hymns to Sud at Shuruppak — likely the same goddess pre-marriage.
- The Kesh Temple Hymn ([[phase-1-001-kesh-temple-hymn]]) names her among the great gods present at Kesh.
- The myth *Enlil and Ninlil* and *Enlil and Sud* survive in Old Babylonian school copies; their compositional date is presumed Ur III or earlier.

## Mythological role
- ***Enlil and Ninlil***: the young Ninlil bathes in a canal at Nippur despite her mother's warning; Enlil sees her, propositions her, and forces himself on her — for which the divine assembly exiles him to the netherworld. Ninlil follows him; Enlil intercepts her in three disguises (gatekeeper, river-man, ferryman) and impregnates her three more times, producing Nergal, Ninazu, and (in some recensions) Enbilulu — thereby providing "substitute" netherworld-residents so that Nanna/Sin can ascend to heaven instead of remaining below. The myth is a netherworld-population etiology dressed as a courtship narrative.
- ***Enlil and Sud***: a courtship myth proper, in which Enlil approaches Sud at Eresh, is rebuffed for impropriety, sends a proper marriage embassy with gifts, and is accepted; Sud is renamed Ninlil and given cosmic offices.
- In [[phase-1-006-atrahasis]] she is present in the divine council though largely silent.
- In the Sumerian King List ([[phase-1-015-sumerian-king-list]]) Nippur — her city with Enlil — is the seat of cosmic kingship.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[mullissu-akkadian]]** — *ancient-identification*: the standard Akkadian rendering of Ninlil. By the Neo-Assyrian period Mullissu is consort of Ashur (a translation of the older Enlil-consort role to the new imperial high god). Herodotus's "Mylitta" (*Histories* 1.131, 1.199) is most likely a Greek reception of Mullissu, though he identifies her with Aphrodite — a Greek interpretatio.
- Distinguish from the Hurrian/Hittite Hebat, sometimes scholarly-paralleled but not an ancient identification.

## Iconography
No fixed independent iconography; Ninlil appears in seal images as a seated goddess in a horned crown beside Enlil, with no distinctive attribute of her own. This iconographic dependency is itself a theological statement.

## Disputes
- **Sud = Ninlil**. Most Assyriologists treat Sud and Ninlil as the same goddess at different cultic stages (per the explicit narrative in *Enlil and Sud*); a minority position holds they were originally distinct deities later merged.
- **Mullissu / Mylitta**. The Mullissu = Ninlil equation is uncontested; the Mylitta = Mullissu equation is the standard scholarly view but rests on a single Greek source (Herodotus).
