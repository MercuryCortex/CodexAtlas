# Nereus

Nereus (*Nēreus*), the **"Old Man of the Sea"** (*hálios gérōn*), is the ancient personification of the sea's kindly, truthful depths — eldest son of Pontus (Sea) and **Gaia**, and the father, by the Oceanid Doris, of the **fifty Nereids** (the sea-nymphs, among them **[[amphitrite]]** and Thetis, mother of Achilles). He is unfailingly **truthful and just** ("he does not lie, but speaks the truth," says Hesiod) and **prophetic** — but, like the related sea-elders Proteus and Phorcys, he must be **caught and held** through his shape-shifting evasions before he will answer, a motif Heracles uses to wring from him the way to the Garden of the Hesperides. Nereus represents an older, gentler stratum of Greek sea-divinity than the stormy Olympian **[[poseidon]]** who eclipses him.
