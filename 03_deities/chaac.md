# Chaac

Chaac (also Chac, Chahk) is the ancient Maya god of rain, lightning, storms, and water — one of the most important and widely worshipped deities in the Maya pantheon. He is iconographically distinctive: long, curling nose (sometimes depicted as a serpent); axe or lightning-bolt as his weapon; goggle-like eyes.

**Function**: As the deity controlling rainfall, Chaac was central to Maya agricultural religion. Maya cities frequently featured rain rituals addressed to Chaac, particularly in the Yucatan where surface water is scarce and rainfall is existentially important for agriculture. The Puuc-region Maya architecture (sites including Uxmal, Kabah, Sayil, Labna) features extensive Chaac masks on building facades — thousands of stone rain-deity faces integrated into the architectural program.

**Multiplicity**: Chaac often appears in groups of four (one for each cardinal direction) — the four Chaacs correspond to the four directional rain-winds. This quadripartite structure mirrors other fundamental Maya cosmological organizing principles.

**Relationship to Classic Maya script**: Chaac is extensively documented in Classic Maya inscriptions; his glyph-head is one of the most recognizable and fully deciphered head-variant signs in the Maya script system. See [[alphabet-mayan-glyphs]] for the decipherment context.

**Cross-tradition parallels**: Storm/rain deity wielding a lightning bolt — parallel forms include [[tlaloc]] (Aztec rain god, with similar goggle-eye iconography), [[indra]] (Vedic storm deity wielding thunderbolt), [[zeus]] (Greek storm deity), [[thor]] (Norse storm deity). The rain-deity wielding lightning is among the most widespread deity archetypes in world religion.
