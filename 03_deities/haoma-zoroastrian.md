# Haoma

## Identity
Haoma is the Avestan sacred plant deity and the personification of the ritual drink — the pressed juice used in the Yasna ceremony, the central liturgical rite of Zoroastrianism. He is simultaneously a divine being with personality and a material substance; worshippers who press and drink Haoma enter into direct communion with him. He is described in the Hōm Yašt (Yasna 9–11) as golden-eyed, conferring health, strength, healing, and righteous offspring on those who press him with devotion, and as an opponent of death and the lie (*druj*).

The paramount scholarly fact about Haoma is his **etymological identity with Vedic Soma**: both are reflexes of Proto-Indo-Iranian *sauma — the same word, the same plant-deity, the same ritual structure of pressing and consuming the sacred drink, separated only by the Indo-Iranian cultural and linguistic split (~2000–1500 BCE). Avestan phonology converts proto-Indo-Iranian *s > h (hence: *sauma > Avestan haoma); Sanskrit preserves *s (hence: *sauma > Sanskrit soma). This is documented to Tier 1 certainty by Manfred Mayrhofer's *Etymologisches Wörterbuch des Altindoarischen* (1992) and T. Burrow's *The Sanskrit Language* (1955). The Haoma-Soma cognate pair is the most precisely documented etymological-religious link in the entire cross-tradition vault.

The actual botanical identity of the haoma/soma plant remains contested: proposals include *Ephedra* (favored by Flattery & Schwartz 1989 for Haoma, and others for Avestan context), *Amanita muscaria* (fly agaric mushroom, proposed by R. Gordon Wasson for Soma), *Peganum harmala* (harmaline/Syrian rue), and various other psychoactive plants. The botanical question is unresolved at Tier 1-2; the ritual, linguistic, and theological facts are not.

## Earliest attestation
Haoma is attested in the Gāthās of Zarathustra (~1500–1000 BCE, with some scholars arguing earlier), where Zarathustra himself critiques certain aspects of the haoma ritual (the bloody sacrifice of cattle associated with the older Indo-Iranian haoma/soma cult). The younger Avesta elaborates the fully personified Haoma in the Hōm Yašt (Yasna 9–11), where Haoma speaks in the first person, recounting the historical figures (Vivaŋhant, Āθwyā, Θraētaona, Pouruŝāspa) who pressed him and received blessings. In living Zoroastrian practice (Parsi and Iranian Zoroastrian communities), the Yasna ceremony with haoma preparation is still performed regularly, making Haoma one of the oldest continuously practiced ritual deities on Earth.

## Mythological role
Key Haoma functions:
1. **The Hōm Yašt (Yasna 9–11)**: Haoma appears to Zarathustra during his ritual and identifies himself: "I am Haoma, the righteous, who wards off death. Hail to the man who presses me!" He recounts four great priests who pressed him and each received a divine reward (sons, cattle, long life, the birth of Zarathustra himself — Pouruŝāspa's pressing of Haoma is associated with Zarathustra's birth).
2. **Ritual function**: In the Yasna ceremony, the priest presses the haoma plant with mortar and pestle (or stone), mixes it with water and milk, and offers and consumes it as part of the cosmic ritual that sustains *asha* (truth/order) against *druj* (lie/chaos). The pressing of Haoma is a microcosmic reenactment of cosmic order-maintenance.
3. **Granting of gifts**: Haoma grants health, strength, victory over enemies, righteous children, immortality (in eschatological context), and wisdom to his devotees.
4. **Eschatological role**: In some texts, Haoma is associated with the preparation of the *frashokereti* (cosmic renovation) — the final rejuvenation of the world at the end of history.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[soma]]** — *ancient-identification* (etymological): THE definitive cognate pair. Haoma = Soma = Proto-Indo-Iranian *sauma. Tier 1 linguistic documentation (Mayrhofer 1992; Burrow 1955). The divine-plant-drink deity who grants immortality, strength, and cosmic order through priestly preparation and consumption is the same theological figure in two sister traditions. This is not a "parallel" or "influence" — it is identity through common ancestry. The divergence (Avestan /h/ < *s; Sanskrit /s/ preserved) is a textbook example of Indo-Iranian sound correspondence.
- **[[dionysus]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Dionysus is simultaneously the god of wine and the wine itself — worshippers consuming wine consume the god. Haoma is simultaneously the divine being and the sacred drink. Both instantiate the theology of the divine-intoxicant: the sacred substance that mediates between human and divine, and whose consumption is itself the religious act. Walter Otto's *Dionysus: Myth and Cult* (1933) articulates the Dionysus-as-the-wine-itself theology; the structural parallel to Haoma is exact.
- **[[bacchus]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Roman Bacchus as the sacralized wine-deity follows the same divine-drink pattern.
- **[[ninkasi]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: The Sumerian hymn to Ninkasi (~1800 BCE) is simultaneously a brewing recipe and a hymn to the goddess of beer herself — the most ancient written attestation of the divine-drink pattern. Haoma (Avestan), Soma (Vedic), Dionysus (Greek), and Ninkasi (Mesopotamian) form a cross-civilizational cluster of sacred-intoxicant-as-deity theologies.
- **[[ahura-mazda]]** — *direct-influence*: Haoma is a Yazata in the service of Ahura Mazda; he is the son of Ahura Mazda in some traditions. The Yasna ritual in which Haoma is pressed is performed in honor of Ahura Mazda.
- **[[mithra-zoroastrian]]** — *direct-influence*: Mithra is the yazata of the covenant/contract; both Mithra and Haoma are associated with the ritual bond between the human and divine spheres maintained through the Yasna ceremony.
- **[[anahita]]** — *direct-influence*: Anāhitā is the yazata of the waters (also a war-goddess); she is associated with the water used to prepare the haoma drink. The two yazatas are often invoked together in the Yasna.
- **[[the-amesha-spentas]]** — *direct-influence*: Haoma operates within the same Zoroastrian divine hierarchy as the Amesha Spentas, though he is classified as a Yazata (lower tier) rather than one of the seven great Amesha Spentas.

## Iconography
Haoma is described in the Avesta as golden (reflecting the color of the plant and its pressed juice), golden-eyed, and radiant. He is not anthropomorphically depicted in Zoroastrian tradition (consistent with Zoroastrianism's general restraint in divine iconography). In later Parsi tradition, haoma is represented by the mortar and pestle used in the ritual pressing and by the haoma twigs themselves. Modern Parsi Yasna ceremonies use *ephedra* twigs, consistent with Flattery & Schwartz's botanical identification.

## Disputes
- **Botanical identity**: *Ephedra* (Flattery & Schwartz 1989), *Amanita muscaria* (Wasson 1968 — for Vedic Soma; contested), *Peganum harmala* (Flattery & Schwartz alternate), or a mixture of plants. The botanical question is unresolved; the ritual-theological identity with Soma is not.
- **Zarathustra's attitude to haoma**: The Gāthās of Zarathustra contain passages (Yasna 48.10) that some scholars interpret as criticism of the haoma ritual. Boyce (1975) argues Zarathustra reformed but did not abolish the haoma cult; others (Herrenschmidt, Kellens) read the critique differently. The younger Avesta's enthusiastic haoma cult suggests any reform was not lasting.
