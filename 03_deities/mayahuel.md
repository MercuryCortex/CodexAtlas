# Mayahuel

The Aztec goddess of the **maguey** (agave) plant and of **pulque** (the fermented agave-sap intoxicant central to Mesoamerican ritual). Mayahuel is one of the clearest cases in world religion of a deity whose body IS the cultivated plant — she is depicted as a woman emerging from a maguey, nursing multiple offspring (the Centzon Totochtin, the "Four Hundred Rabbits," the collective pulque-gods). Her consort **Patecatl** discovered the fermentation process that turns maguey sap into pulque; together they produced the 400 rabbits.

Mayahuel's structural parallel with the [[corn-mother]] Selu (Cherokee) and with [[osiris]] (Egyptian grain-body) places her in the cross-tradition **body-of-the-deity-IS-the-food** pattern. The maguey was the principal source of fiber, food, drink, sweetener, and ritual intoxicant in the central Mexican highlands; Mayahuel's cult was therefore civilization-load-bearing in a way that few Aztec deities matched.
