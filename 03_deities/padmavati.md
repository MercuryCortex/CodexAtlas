# Padmāvatī

Padmāvatī is the most popular of the Jain **yakṣīs** — the attendant guardian-goddesses (*śāsana-devatās*) who protect the Tīrthaṅkaras and their teaching — serving as the divine guardian of the 23rd Tīrthaṅkara **[[parshvanatha|Pārśvanātha]]**. Her cult is woven from the great serpent-imagery of Pārśva: when the ascetic Pārśvanātha was assailed by a storm conjured by his enemy **Kamaṭha (Meghamālin)**, the **nāga king Dharaṇendra** and his queen rose up to shelter him with their hoods, and Padmāvatī is identified with this protective serpent-power, depicted with a **cobra canopy** above her, often **riding a cock-serpent (kukkuṭa-sarpa)** and bearing lotus, noose, goad, and fruit. Though theologically subordinate to the liberated, world-transcending Tīrthaṅkara (who grants no boons), Padmāvatī became an enormously popular **boon-granting protective goddess** in her own right — especially in the **Digambara** tradition and across South India, where the temple-complex at **Humcha (Hombuja)** in Karnataka is a major center of her worship. As a serpent-and-fortune goddess she runs parallel to Hindu figures like **[[manasa-devi|Manasā]]** and Lakṣmī, an instance of the shared South Asian goddess-substrate. Padmāvatī embodies the accessible, protective, this-worldly face of Jain devotion.
