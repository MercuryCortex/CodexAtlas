# Damu

## Identity

Damu is a Sumerian god of healing and of vegetation — specifically of the sap that rises in trees and plants — and one of the clearest Mesopotamian examples of a **dying-and-rising god**. He is the divine child of [[ninisina]], the healing goddess of Isin (later identified with [[gula-akkadian]]), and his name appears in incantations as a power that restores health. His more profound mythological role, however, is as the young god who "goes away" — whose disappearance is mourned in liturgy.

## Mythological role

Damu's cult centered on lament. In the dirges, the sap withdrawing from the trees and reeds is Damu departing to the underworld; his mother and sister search for him and weep, and the rituals enact the grief of the land at the loss of its vegetation-life. This pattern — the young vegetation-god who dies (or descends) and whose return is longed for — links Damu intimately with [[dumuzi-tammuz]], with whom he was eventually merged in the great cycle of laments for the dying god. Thorkild Jacobsen treated Damu as a primary witness to the Mesopotamian "dying god" of fertility, distinct in origin (a healing child-god of Isin) but absorbed into the Dumuzi complex.

## Cross-tradition significance

As a dying-and-rising vegetation deity, Damu belongs to the wider ancient Near Eastern complex that includes Dumuzi/Tammuz, the Levantine Baal and Adonis, and the Egyptian Osiris — figures whose death and return mythologize the agricultural year. He is therefore a useful Mesopotamian node for the [[dying-rising-god]] theme and for tracing how the seasonal-death-and-return pattern was expressed in healing- and vegetation-cult rather than (as with Dumuzi) in the shepherd's idiom.
