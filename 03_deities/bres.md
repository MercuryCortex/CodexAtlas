# Bres

Bres ("the Beautiful"; **Eochu Bres**, son of the Fomorian prince **Elatha** and the Tuatha Dé woman **Ériu**) is, in the Irish *Cath Maige Tuired*, the great cautionary figure of **failed kingship**. When **[[nuada|Nuada]]**, king of the **Tuatha Dé Danann**, is disqualified from rule by the loss of his hand in battle, the half-Fomorian Bres — beautiful and well-connected to both peoples — is made king in his place. But his reign is a disaster: he is **miserly and unjust**, exacting harsh tribute, failing in the sacred royal duty of **generosity** (the gods leave his hall without their knives greased or their breath smelling of ale), and a poet's satire against him — the first in Ireland — raises blemishes on his face that further unfit him to rule. Deposed in favour of the restored Nuada, Bres flees to his Fomorian kin and helps bring on the cataclysmic **Second Battle of Mag Tuired**. Captured at its end, he saves his life only by teaching the Tuatha the secrets of **ploughing, sowing, and reaping**. Bres is the mythic embodiment of the un-king — beauty without justice, sovereignty without generosity — against whom right rule is defined.
