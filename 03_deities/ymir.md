# Ymir

## Identity
The **primordial cosmic giant** of Norse mythology — formed from the dripping rime where the heat of Múspellsheimr met the cold of Niflheimr in **Ginnungagap**, the primal void between fire and ice. From his sweat and from his feet rubbing together the first generation of jötnar are produced; he is nursed by the cow Auðumla. Eventually slain by [[odin]] and his brothers Vili and Vé, who form the cosmos from his body:
- **flesh → earth** (Miðgarðr)
- **blood → sea**
- **bones → mountains**
- **teeth → stones**
- **skull → sky-vault** (held up by the four dwarves Norðri, Suðri, Austri, Vestri)
- **brain → clouds**
- **eyebrows → fence around Miðgarðr** (separating humans from jötnar)

## Mythological role
**Indo-European cosmic-body cosmogony**: Ymir's name is etymologically cognate with Vedic *Yama* and Avestan *Yima* — all from PIE *\*Yemo-* "twin." Lincoln 1986 argues that the cosmic-body-dismemberment cosmogony (Ymir → cosmos in Norse, [[purusha|Puruṣa]] → varṇa in Vedic, [[pangu|Pangu]] → cosmos in Chinese) is one of the deepest comparative-mythology templates — though the Sino-Norse parallel cannot be securely traced to transmission.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[purusha]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: IE cosmic-giant.
- **[[pangu]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: cosmic-body cosmogony.
- **[[tiamat]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: cosmic-body dismembered to form the world (Enuma Elish IV).
