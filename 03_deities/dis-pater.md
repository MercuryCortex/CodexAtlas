# Dis Pater

Dis Pater ("Wealthy Father" — *dis* contracted from *dives*, "rich") is the Roman god of the **underworld and the dead**, and at the same time of the **mineral wealth hidden beneath the earth**; Cicero explains his name precisely by the riches that rise from the ground. He is the Roman counterpart of the Greek **[[hades|Hades]]**, his name carrying the same idea of wealth as Greek *Plouton*, "the rich one" (whence **[[pluto-roman|Pluto]]**), and his consort is **[[proserpina-roman|Proserpina]]**. In one of Rome's most solemn rites, the **Ludi Tarentini** (later the *Ludi Saeculares*, the Secular Games marking the close of each *saeculum*), black victims were sacrificed to Dis and Proserpina by night at a subterranean altar in the Campus Martius. The grimmer name **Orcus** — a god (and personified force) of death who drags down the dying and punishes the breaking of oaths — overlaps heavily with Dis and is treated here as the same chthonic power under its sterner aspect. Dis Pater embodies the Roman fusion of death, the deep earth, and its concealed riches.
