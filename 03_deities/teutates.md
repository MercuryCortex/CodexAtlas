# Teutates

## Identity

Teutates (also Toutatis — the form preserved in the Gaulish inscription and the Asterix comic-series oath "By Toutatis!") is the Gaulish deity of tribal protection, war, and collective sovereignty. His name derives from Celtic *teutā ("people" / "tribe") — the same root as Germanic *þeudą* (people), Old Irish *túath* (tribe/district), and ultimately Proto-Indo-European *tewtéh₂. His name thus means literally "Father of the People" or "Protector of the Tribe." He is one of the three supreme Gaulish deities named in Caesar's *de Bello Gallico* (VI.16, c. 52 BCE), completed by [[taranis]] (thunder) and Esus (unclear function, possibly a woodsman-deity). Lucan's scholiasts describe sacrifices to Teutates as drowning in a cauldron or vat — a form of sacrifice connected to the cauldron-motif deep in Celtic religious practice.

## Earliest attestation

- Julius Caesar, *de Bello Gallico* VI.16 (c. 52 BCE): names the supreme Gaulish triad including Teutates.
- Lucan, *Pharsalia* I.444–446 (65 CE): *"et quibus immitis placatur sanguine diro / Teutates"* — "and Teutates, placated by grim bloodshed."
- Lucan's scholiast (later commentary): identifies Teutates with Mars or Mercury and specifies drowning sacrifice.
- Inscriptions: *Mars Toutatis* and *Mercury Toutatis* in Roman-period Britain and Gaul (e.g., Barkway, Hertfordshire, Britain — CIL VII.67).

## Mythological role

Teutates's mythology is reconstructed primarily from Roman-era sources and has no surviving extended Celtic narrative. Three functions are clear:

1. **Tribal protection**: his name being the people's own name makes him the divine embodiment of the community itself — worshipping Teutates is the tribe affirming its own existence and continuity. This is a near-universal sovereignty pattern (the deity *is* the people).
2. **War**: he receives martial sacrifice and is identified with Mars in Roman interpretatio — aligning him with the warrior function in Dumézil's tripartite scheme.
3. **Sacrifice by drowning**: Lucan's scholiast specifies this, connecting Teutates to the cauldron-motif prevalent across Celtic religion (the Dagda's cauldron, the Gundestrup Cauldron's drowning scene on the inner plate — possibly directly depicting Teutates sacrifice).

## Cross-tradition identifications

- **[[mars-roman]]** — *ancient-identification*: one stream of Lucan's scholiasts identifies Teutates with Mars; the *Mars Toutatis* inscriptions confirm this equation was used in Roman-Celtic religious practice. The warrior-protector function is the common ground.
- **[[dagda]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Irish Dagda ("Good God" = "Father of All") parallels Teutates as the divine embodiment of his people's welfare. Both are tribal father-figures with a protective-abundance function rather than a pure warrior role. Dumézil's trifunctional analysis places both in the first-function (sovereignty) tier, distinguished from pure war deities.
- **[[oduduwa]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Yoruba divine ancestor who *is* the origin of the Yoruba people parallels Teutates's "Father of the People" function exactly. The pattern — a deity whose name or nature is identical with the people-group itself — is a universal sovereignty mytheme.
- **[[nuada]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Irish sovereignty deity (king of the Tuatha Dé Danann before Lugh) whose fitness to rule is the tribe's fitness; the king's body = the tribe's body = the land's health is the Celtic sovereignty formula Teutates embodies.
- **Dumézil's triad (Teutates/Esus/[[taranis]])**: Teutates maps onto the first-function sovereign-warrior tier, paralleling Jupiter (Rome), Mitra-Varuna (Vedic), Odin-Tyr (Norse). The three-tier divine organization of the PIE religion — sovereignty/warrior/fertility — is Dumézil's central claim, and the Gaulish triad is one of his key exhibits.
- **Cauldron-sacrifice → [[dagda]]'s cauldron → Grail**: the drowning-in-cauldron sacrifice for Teutates connects to the broader Celtic cauldron complex (Dagda's *coire ansic*, the Cauldron of Annwn in Welsh tradition, the medieval Grail as Christianized cauldron) — a continuous transformation of the same ritual-mythological object across 1500 years.

## Iconography

Teutates has no consistently identified iconography independent of the Roman Mars-type. In the *interpretatio romana* he appears as a warrior with spear and shield (Mars-type) or occasionally as a traveler-figure (Mercury-type, reflecting the tribal-communications function). The Gundestrup Cauldron (2nd–1st c. BCE) inner plate showing a giant figure immersing a man headfirst in a cauldron is frequently identified as a depiction of Teutates sacrifice — one of the rare possible direct illustrations of the practice described by Lucan's scholiast.

## Disputes

The double identification — Teutates = Mars vs. Mercury — in the scholiasts may reflect genuine regional variation (different Gaulish tribes may have emphasized different functions), or it may reflect the scholiasts' confusion. Whether the Gundestrup Cauldron drowning scene depicts Teutates specifically, or a more generic cauldron-resuscitation motif (the reverse: cauldron that restores the dead to life), is unresolved. The historicity of Caesar's triadic organization of Gaulish religion has been challenged — some scholars view it as Roman ethnographic projection rather than native Gaulish theology.
