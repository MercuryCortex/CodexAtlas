# Yan Wang

## Identity
Yan Wang (*Yánluó Wáng*, "King Yanluo") is the Chinese Buddhist-Daoist ruler of the underworld and judge of the dead — the East Asian transformation of Vedic [[yama-vedic]], transmitted through Buddhism and thoroughly transformed by Chinese imperial administrative culture. Where Vedic Yama is a divine king who rules the dead, Yan Wang operates within a fully **bureaucratic afterlife court system**: the Ten Courts of Hell (*Shídiàn Yánwáng*), each presided by a different king, each processing specific categories of sin, with specific punishments administered before reincarnation.

## The Yama → Yan Wang transformation
The transmission pathway: Vedic *Yama* → Indian Buddhist *Yama* (who becomes a judge-deity in the Jātakas and the Pali suttas) → Chinese Buddhist *Yanluó Wáng* (2nd–5th c. CE translation period) → fully sinicized underworld bureaucracy (Tang dynasty onward, ~7th–10th c. CE).

The transformation reflects the encounter between Indian Buddhist cosmology and Chinese imperial-administrative culture. India's Yama is a divine king; China's Yan Wang is a **bureaucrat-judge** — with courts, registers, hearings, verdicts, sentences, and appeals. The Ten Courts of Hell operate like a Chinese magistracy writ cosmic. This is one of the most vivid examples in the vault of how a transmitted deity is transformed by the receiving culture's dominant institutional logic.

## The Ten Courts
Each of the Ten Courts processes different categories of karmic transgression:
- 1st Court: Yan Wang's own court (preliminary judgment; determines the basic karmic trajectory)
- Courts 2–9: specific punishments for specific sins (lying, theft, unfilial behavior, harm to life, etc.)
- 10th Court: the final processing before reincarnation, assignment of the next life-form, and administration of the "Soup of Forgetfulness" (*Mèng Pó Tāng*) that erases memory before rebirth

The 10th Court's **Soup of Forgetfulness** administered by Meng Po is one of the most distinctive elements of Chinese eschatology — a divine mechanism for ensuring that karmic justice operates without memory contaminating the new life.

## Connection to the yama-vedic spine
Yan Wang is the third node in the vault's Yama spine (alongside [[yama-vedic]] and [[yamantaka]]):
- **[[yama-vedic]]** — original Vedic lord of the dead
- **[[yamantaka]]** — Tibetan Buddhist polemic-inversion: Yama defeated and made into a wisdom deity
- **Yan Wang** — Chinese Buddhist bureaucratic transformation: Yama as cosmic magistrate

The three together show what happens to a single deity transmitted across Buddhist traditions: India keeps him as a terrifying divine king, Tibet kills him and absorbs him into the Vajrayana, China remodels him as an imperial judge.
