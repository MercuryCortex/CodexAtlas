# Tlaloc

## Identity
Pan-Mesoamerican rain-storm god, attested in central-Mexican religion under the name Tlaloc from at least the Teotihuacan period (~150–550 CE; Teotihuacan's so-called "Tlaloc" iconography is well-attested, though scholars caution that the name is reconstructed). Tlaloc governs rain, thunder, lightning, agricultural fertility, and mountains — clouds gather on the peaks, where Tlaloc's house *Tlalocan* is located, and from which rain descends. Tlalocan is also a paradise — the dead by drowning, lightning-strike, or certain waterborne diseases go to Tlalocan rather than Mictlan.

## Earliest attestation
Teotihuacan iconography (~150 CE onward); continuous attestation through Toltec, Aztec periods; pre-Aztec antecedents (the "Tláloc" complex) clearly span more than 1500 years.

## Mythological role
Tlaloc presides over the Third Sun in the Mexica five-suns cosmology (a sun destroyed by fire-rain); he is responsible for the proper rain-cycle that makes agriculture possible. His four-quadrant assistants (the *tlaloque*) carry pots of water that pour out as rain; lightning is the breaking of the pots. He shared the top of Tenochtitlan's Templo Mayor with Huitzilopochtli — the two shrines representing the agricultural-rain and warrior-state poles of Mexica state religion.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[chac]] (Maya)** — *scholarly-parallel*: structurally identical Maya rain-god; the two are clearly the same pan-Mesoamerican concept under different linguistic-cultural forms.
- **Cocijo (Zapotec)** — *scholarly-parallel*: Oaxacan rain-god in the same pan-Mesoamerican pattern.

## Iconography
The distinctive "Tlaloc mask": large goggle-eyes (often interpreted as snake-coils framing the eyes), prominent fangs / teeth, sometimes a curling upper-lip "moustache." Blue coloration; rain symbols (drops); lightning serpents.
