# Vithoba

Vithoba (*Viṭṭhala*, also **Panduranga**) is the presiding deity of **Pandharpur** in Maharashtra and the heart of the **Varkari** devotional tradition — one of the great living *bhakti* movements of India. Worshipped as a form of **[[krishna]]**/**[[vishnu]]**, his image is unmistakable: a dark figure standing **arms akimbo on a brick**, in the posture (the tradition says) of a god who came to reward his devotee **Pundalik** and, finding him busy serving his parents, simply *waited* on the brick Pundalik tossed him — the very emblem of an accessible, patient, devotee-loving deity. Scholarship (Dhere) traces him to a pastoral folk-god of the Deccan progressively identified with Vishnu, a textbook **Sanskritization** of a regional cult. His worship is carried by the beloved **sant-poets** of Maharashtra — Jnaneshwar, Namdev, Eknath, and Tukaram — whose Marathi *abhangas* are sung on the great **wari** pilgrimage, when millions walk to Pandharpur. With his consort **Rakhumai** (Rukmini), Vithoba is the warm devotional center of western Indian Hinduism.
