# Viracocha

## Identity
The Andean creator-god — pre-Inca in origin (probably Tiwanaku-era, ~500–1000 CE), absorbed into Inca state theology as the supreme deity preceding and grounding Inti. Viracocha created the cosmos, the previous humanities (which he variously destroyed by flood or by petrifaction), and the present humanity. He is also a *wandering* deity — the Huarochirí Manuscript (Cuni Raya Viracocha narratives) records him moving through the Andean landscape in disguise, often as a beggar, testing humans and creating landscape features through his actions. The wandering-trickster Viracocha of the Huarochirí material and the supreme-creator Viracocha of the Cusco state cult are differently weighted aspects of the same figure.

## Earliest attestation
Probable Tiwanaku origin (~500 CE onward); the Gateway of the Sun at Tiwanaku (~800 CE) shows a staff-bearing central figure conventionally identified as a Viracocha-type creator. Inca-period attestation in early colonial sources (Cieza, Polo, Garcilaso, Cobo). Huarochirí Manuscript (~1608) preserves the local-Andean wandering Cuni Raya Viracocha narratives.

## Mythological role
Inca tradition: Viracocha created the cosmos and the gods; rose at Lake Titicaca, made the sun and moon ascend from the lake; created the first humans from clay or stones; instructed them in civilization; then wandered across the Andes to the Pacific coast and disappeared westward across the ocean (the wandering-and-disappearance motif echoes Quetzalcoatl).

Huarochirí: Cuni Raya Viracocha appears in rags, encounters the maiden Cavillaca, magically impregnates her with a fruit; she bears a child, asks all the gods which is the father; only Cuni Raya in his ragged form does not protest his fatherhood claim; she flees with the child into the sea, becoming a rock. Cuni Raya pursues, encountering animals to whom he grants gifts or curses.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Christ** — *folk-syncretism* (early colonial): Andean Christians have intermittently identified Viracocha with Christ, drawing on the wandering, the bearded-iconography (Spanish reports of Viracocha as bearded), and the universal-creator dimension.

## Iconography
Pre-Inca / Tiwanaku: the staff-bearing central figure of the Gateway of the Sun. Inca-period iconography is less distinctive — he is the unrepresented or aniconic supreme.
