# Christos (Gnostic)

## Identity

In Sethian and Valentinian Gnosticism, the Christos is not primarily the crucified human Jesus of mainstream Christianity but an eternal *pneumatic* (spiritual) being of the Pleroma — the divine Fullness — who descends into the material world to awaken the sleeping sparks of light imprisoned in matter. The Gnostic tradition makes a fundamental split between the "heavenly Christos" (the eternal Pleroma-aeon, co-eternal with [[barbelo]] and [[the-monad-gnostic]]) and the "earthly Jesus" (a body, vessel, or simulacrum through which the Christos temporarily operated). This split — at once cosmological and soteriological — defines Gnostic Christology against the emerging orthodox tradition.

Two key Christological positions distinguish the Gnostic Christos:

1. **Docetism** (from Greek *dokein*, "to seem"): the Christos only *appeared* to have a physical body; the crucifixion was an illusion. The divine cannot genuinely suffer material death. Gospel of Philip (NHC II.3): "He said this on the day when he was crucified. [...] The perfect Man's body was hidden." The Christos laughs at the cross in some Sethian texts because the archons believe they are killing the divine — they are not.

2. **Revealer, not sacrifice**: the Gnostic Christos descends not to die for sins but to transmit *gnosis* — the self-knowledge that the soul is a divine spark, that the demiurge's cosmos is not ultimate, and that the Pleroma can be re-entered. Atonement theology is replaced by awakening theology. The cross is not central; the teaching and the revealed knowledge are central.

## The Sethian Christos (Apocryphon of John)

In the *Apocryphon of John* (NHC II.1), Christ/Autogenes ("Self-Begotten") is the third great emanation from [[the-monad-gnostic]] — after [[the-pleroma]] beings and [[barbelo]]. He is placed in the Ogdoad (the eighth sphere, above the seven archontic heavens) and given authority over the divine realm. Later, the Christos descends within the narrative itself: the revealer of the *ApocJohn* identifies himself as "the Father, the Mother, and the Son" — the whole divine Trinity appearing in one act of revelation.

The descent of the Christos in Sethian texts is a recurring narrative structure: the divine Pleroma sends the Christos downward precisely because [[sophia-gnostic]]'s fall and [[yaldabaoth]]'s creation have trapped the divine sparks in material bodies. The Christos is the Pleroma's rescue-mission, operating in disguise — the archons cannot perceive a truly divine being passing through their spheres.

## The Valentinian Christos

Valentinian Christology (Ptolemy's system, via Irenaeus *AH* I.1–8; Tripartite Tractate NHC I.5) is more philosophically elaborate:

- The Christos is an aeon produced by the *entire Pleroma* to resolve the crisis caused by Sophia's fall.
- He descends, takes on a "psychic body" (a body of soul-stuff, not matter), and teaches the Pneumatics.
- In the Gospel of Philip (NHC II.3): "Christ came to ransom some, to save others, to redeem others. He ransomed those who were strangers and made them his own." But this "ransom" is not blood-sacrifice — it is the transmission of gnosis that breaks the archons' hold.
- The Valentinian Christos passes through Mary "like water through a pipe" — he acquires no material contamination; his body is a functional vehicle, not a genuine human body.

## MASSIVE WIN: Docetism, the Quran, and the Buddhist Parinibbana

The Gnostic Christos's docetism — the claim that the divine figure only *appeared* to suffer and die — is not an isolated aberration but participates in a cross-tradition theological pattern:

| Tradition | Figure | The "apparent" death/vulnerability | The docetic claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sethian / Valentinian Gnostic (2nd c.) | Christos-Gnostic | The crucifixion is an illusion; the true Christos laughs above the cross | Gospel of Philip; Book of the Great Invisible Spirit |
| Islamic (7th c.) | [[jesus-christ-deity]] (Isa) | "They did not kill him, nor did they crucify him, but it was made to appear so to them" (Q 4:157) | The Quran's docetic reading of the crucifixion is structurally identical to Gnostic docetism |
| Mahayana Buddhism | The Buddha | The parinibbana (death) was a "skillful means" (*upaya*) — the Buddha did not truly die but demonstrated death for pedagogical reasons (Lotus Sutra ch. 16) | The divine teacher adopts apparent death as teaching device |

**The cross-tradition finding:** Three traditions, independently, conclude that the genuinely divine teacher cannot be subject to material death — and that any apparent death must be illusion, skillful means, or misperception. The theological logic is consistent: if the divine is truly transcendent of matter, then material suffering and death cannot touch it. The appearance of death is real; the divine remains unharmed. This is not mere coincidence of doctrine but a structural entailment of strong transcendence-theology applied to incarnate divine figures.

## MASSIVE WIN: Gnosis vs. Atonement — the fault line still running

The Gnostic Christos's purpose (awakening → gnosis) vs. the orthodox Christos's purpose (sacrifice → atonement) is the deepest fault line in Western theology of salvation:

| Model | Diagnosis of the human problem | Solution | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gnostic gnosis-model | Ignorance — humans do not know their divine origin | Awakening — gnosis restores knowledge | The Christos reveals; the spark recognizes itself |
| Orthodox atonement-model | Guilt — humans have sinned against God | Forgiveness — the sacrifice covers debt | Christ dies; the debt is paid |
| Neoplatonist illumination | Ignorance — the soul has forgotten its origin in the One | Contemplation — *theoria* restores the soul | The philosopher ascends through *nous* |
| Vedantic *avidya* model | Ignorance (*avidya*) — the individual does not know its identity with Brahman | Knowledge (*jnana*) — the illusion dissolves | The guru transmits the liberating insight |

The Gnostic position (human problem = ignorance, solution = revealed knowledge) aligns with Neoplatonism and Vedanta, not with Augustinian Christianity. The Augustinian tradition — which became Western Christian orthodoxy — diagnoses the human problem as guilt (*peccatum*) requiring redemption. The tension between these two anthropologies still drives contemporary Christian theological debate (e.g., liberation theology's critique of sacrificial atonement; the "moral influence" theory of the atonement).

## The Revealer-Savior Archetype: Cross-Tradition Grid

| Figure | Tradition | Knowledge transmitted | Forbidden quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[christos-gnostic]] | Sethian/Valentinian Gnostic | Gnosis of the Pleroma and the demiurge's lie | The archons suppress this knowledge; it breaks the cosmic prison |
| [[manda-d-hayyi]] | Mandaean | Knowledge of the light-worlds above the material cosmos | The world-rulers suppress the truth of the soul's origin |
| [[hermes-trismegistus]] | Hermetic | Cosmic secrets — the nature of God, world, and human soul | Revealed to the few; suppressed from the many |
| [[prometheus]] | Greek | Fire — divine creative power | Zeus forbids it; Prometheus transmits it anyway |
| [[lucifer]] | Christian re-reading | Knowledge of good and evil (Eden narrative inversion) | God forbids the fruit; the serpent/Lucifer offers it |

**The pattern:** In each case, a being from a higher realm crosses a divine boundary to transmit prohibited cosmic knowledge to humans. The establishment powers (Zeus, the Demiurge, the archons, the orthodox church) suppress or punish this transmission. The revealer is the cosmic outlier — the figure who breaks the hierarchy of ignorance-enforcement.
