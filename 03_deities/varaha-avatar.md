# Varaha (Boar Avatar of Vishnu)

Stub — to be expanded. The third avatar (*avatara*) of Vishnu in the Dashavatara (ten-avatar sequence). The cosmic boar dives into the primordial waters (*Garbhodaka* ocean) and lifts the Earth-goddess Bhudevi on his tusks from where the demon Hiranyaksha has submerged her.

**Earliest form**: In the *Shatapatha Brahmana* (c. 800–600 BCE), Prajapati (the creator) takes the form of a boar and dives into the primordial waters to raise up the earth — the oldest Vedic earth-diver narrative. This is later reinterpreted as Vishnu's avatar.

**Earth-diver parallel**: See [[earth-diver-creation]] for the full cross-tradition table placing Varaha alongside Haudenosaunee Muskrat, Finnish Ilmatar, and Cherokee Waterbeetle. The structural parallel — a divine being descending into cosmic waters to rescue / create earth — is one of the most widely distributed cosmogonic motifs in world religion.
