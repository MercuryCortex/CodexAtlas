# Tvashtar

## Identity

Tvashtar (Sanskrit **Tvaṣṭṛ**) is the divine artificer of the Vedic pantheon — the smith, carpenter, and shaper of forms among the gods. He fashions the weapons of the gods (above all the **vajra**, the thunderbolt of [[indra]]), and he is the maker of forms in a deeper sense: he shapes the embryo in the womb and gives every creature its distinctive shape, so that he is called the "lord of all forms." His name is connected with cutting, shaping, and fashioning.

## Mythological role

Tvashtar's role weaves directly into the central Vedic combat-myth. He forges the **vajra** with which Indra will slay the dragon Vritra. But Tvashtar is also the father of **Vishvarupa** (Trishiras), a three-headed priestly figure whom Indra, suspecting treachery, **kills** — beheading all three heads. Enraged at his son's death, Tvashtar creates the great dragon **Vritra** to avenge him, the monster who withholds the cosmic waters until Indra, with Tvashtar's own thunderbolt, destroys him. Thus the divine smith stands on both sides of the Vedic chaoskampf: he arms the storm-god and, in grief, fathers the chaos-dragon the storm-god must defeat. His daughter Saranyu, given in marriage to Vivasvat (the Sun), is the mother of the twins (the Ashvins) and of Yama and Manu.

## Cross-tradition significance

Tvashtar is the Vedic member of the widespread **divine smith / craftsman-god** type — the deity who forges the weapons and wonders of the gods. He stands beside the Greek [[hephaestus]], the Canaanite [[kothar-wa-khasis]] (who forges the clubs with which Baal defeats Yam), the Celtic Goibniu, and the Germanic Wayland. The recurring figure of the smith who **arms the storm-warrior for the chaoskampf** — Tvashtar's vajra for Indra, Kothar's clubs for Baal — is one of the clearest Indo-European and Near Eastern mythic correspondences.
