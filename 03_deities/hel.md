# Hel

## Identity
Queen of the Norse underworld (also called Hel — the place and the person are named the same). Hel is the daughter of Loki and the giantess Angrboða, sister of Fenrir and Jörmungandr. She rules over the part of the underworld where those who died of sickness, old age, or other non-warrior deaths arrive — the unhonored dead, contrasted with the elite warrior-dead at Valhǫll. Her appearance is striking: split vertically, half normal living woman, half corpse. The English word *hell* (and German *Hölle*) descends etymologically from her name; the Christian Hell was named for the Norse Hel, with the underworld's reception of the dead Christianized into the place of damnation.

## Earliest attestation
Old Norse literary attestation. Etymological cognates across Germanic.

## Mythological role
After Baldr's death, Hermóðr rides nine nights to Hel's hall to negotiate his return; Hel agrees if every being in the cosmos weeps for Baldr; the one giantess (Þǫkk / Loki) who refuses to weep keeps Baldr in Hel. Hel takes the dead at all ordinary deaths; she rules a vast realm beneath the world-tree's roots; at Ragnarǫk her dead-army sails with Loki on the ship *Naglfar* to attack the gods.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Christian Hell** — *manuscript-transmission* (etymological): the *name* of Christian Hell descends from Hel; the *concept* is significantly different (Hel is not principally a place of punishment).
- **Hades / Persephone / Ereshkigal** — *scholarly-parallel*: comparative underworld-deity category; the comparisons are typological.

## Iconography
Half-living half-dead appearance — vertical split, one side healthy, one side blue-black corpse. Hall Éljúðnir.
