# Pomona

Pomona is the Roman goddess of **fruit trees and orchards** — of the *poma*, the tree-borne fruit (apples, pears, and the like) whose cultivation she protects and whose ripening she grants. Hers is a strikingly specialized and purely Roman cult, without Greek antecedent: she had her own minor priest, the **flamen Pomonalis** (ranked at the bottom of the flamines, a sign of antiquity), and a sacred grove, the **Pomonal**, on the road toward Ostia. She is best known from Ovid's *Metamorphoses*, where she tends her walled orchard with the pruning-knife, indifferent to suitors, until the shape-shifting garden-god **[[vertumnus|Vertumnus]]** — having tried many disguises — woos and wins her. Pomona embodies the Roman delight in the disciplined, fruitful orchard, the cultivated counterpart to the wild wood.
