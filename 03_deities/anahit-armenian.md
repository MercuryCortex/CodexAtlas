# Anahit

## Identity
Anahit (Armenian: Անահիտ) is the supreme goddess of pre-Christian Armenia — goddess of fertility, love, wisdom, and war; called "the great mother of all virtue and benefactress of the human race" (Agathangelos) and "the golden mother" (*ոսկեմայր*). Daughter of [[aramazd]] in the Armenian pantheon. She derives from the Zoroastrian [[anahita]] (water and fertility goddess) but was radically transformed in Armenia into a fully anthropomorphic deity presiding over a golden-statue temple cult with independent priests and substantial religious authority. Her golden statue at the temple of **Erez (Acilisene)**, near modern Erzincan in eastern Anatolia, was the most celebrated divine image in ancient Armenia — described by Strabo, referenced by Pliny, and famous enough that both Artaxias II (c. 2nd c. BCE) and Mark Antony (c. 36 BCE) plundered it at different moments in Armenian history. The statue's gold was that famous.

## Earliest attestation
- **Artaxias II** (c. 190–159 BCE): the plundering of Anahit's golden statue by Artaxias II implies an established cult by at least the 2nd c. BCE.
- **Strabo**, *Geographica* XI.14.16 (c. 20 BCE): the most detailed ancient Greek account of the Erez temple — describes the cult practices, the goddess's identification with Aphrodite, and the social significance of temple service.
- **Pliny the Elder**, *Historia Naturalis* XXXIII.24 (c. 77 CE): records Mark Antony's seizure of the golden Anahit statue.
- **Agathangelos** (c. 400 CE) and **Movses Khorenatsi** (c. 470 CE): primary Armenian literary sources, both post-Christianization, preserving the tradition of Anahit as supreme goddess.

## Mythological role
In the Armenian pantheon Anahit occupies the role of supreme mother-goddess and divine queen — analogous to Hera/Juno as divine consort-queen, Athena as wisdom-and-war goddess, and Aphrodite as love deity, but with an independent cultic authority greater than any of these parallels. Her title "mother of all virtue" implies a moral cosmological function: she is the source from which human virtue flows, making her a theological predecessor to the later Armenian Christian theological exaltation of Mary. Her warrior aspects and sovereignty functions tie her to Zoroastrian [[anahita]]'s river-and-victory dimensions.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[anahita]]** — *ancient-identification*: direct linguistic and theological derivation; Anahit transforms the Zoroastrian water-fertility goddess into a fully anthropomorphic warrior-mother with golden cult statue (Russell 1987).
- **[[aphrodite-greek]]** — *ancient-identification* (Strabo): Hellenistic *interpretatio graeca* at the Erez temple.
- **[[athena]]** — *ancient-identification* (Agathangelos; Russell 1987): wisdom and war aspects.
- **[[mary-theotokos]]** — *folk-syncretism*: after 301 CE Armenian Christianization, Anahit's cult functions were substantially absorbed into Marian devotion — the "mother of all virtue" became the Theotokos. A textbook case of goddess-to-Mary continuity (Russell 1987).
- **[[isis-egyptian]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: both are supreme mother-goddesses described as "mother of all"; both underwent syncretization with Greco-Roman Aphrodite/Venus; both had their cult absorbed into Christian Marian devotion.
- **[[inanna-sumerian]]** / **[[ishtar-akkadian]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the triple-function goddess (love + war + sovereignty) is a Near Eastern pattern Anahit participates in.
- **[[astarte-canaanite]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Levantine parallel to the Near Eastern love-war goddess form.

## Iconography
Anahit's golden statue at Erez was described as the supreme cult image of ancient Armenia — likely a standing anthropomorphic golden figure. The details of the statue's appearance are not fully preserved; ancient sources emphasize its gold and its fame. After Christianization the physical cult images were destroyed (Gregory the Illuminator's mission involved systematic destruction of pre-Christian shrines). Some Armenian Christian icons of Mary Theotokos may preserve formal echoes of Anahit's iconography.

## Disputes
- **Cult-prostitution at Erez.** Strabo describes a form of sacred prostitution at Anahit's Erez temple, with Armenian noble families sending their daughters to serve before marriage. Modern scholarship is divided on whether this reflects actual cult practice or Strabo's Hellenocentric projection of Aphrodite-temple conventions onto an Armenian goddess he didn't fully understand (Russell 1987 treats this cautiously).
- **Statue identification.** Whether the plundered statues mentioned by different ancient sources (Artaxias, Mark Antony) are the same object or different cult images is unclear from the sources.
