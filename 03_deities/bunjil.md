# Bunjil

The **eaglehawk creator-ancestor** of the Kulin nation of south-central Victoria — the wedge-tailed eagle who made the land and the Kulin people, established the moral law, and now resides in the sky (in some tellings, as the bright star Altair). Bunjil is paired with **Waa the Crow** in the Kulin moiety system: every Kulin person is either Bunjil-moiety or Waa-moiety, and the two moieties intermarry. The complementary-binary creator-pair is one of the most consistently-attested structural features of south-eastern Australian Aboriginal religion.

**The All-Father pattern of south-eastern Australia.** Bunjil is one of several locally-named **All-Fathers** across south-eastern Australia: Bunjil (Kulin, Victoria), [[baiame]] (Wiradjuri/Gamilaroi/Kamilaroi, inland NSW), Daramulan (Yuin and others, coastal NSW), Nurrundere (Ngarrindjeri, Murray mouth). The pattern is so structurally consistent — sky-residing creator, lawgiver, ancestor, eagle-form — that 19th-century anthropologists initially read it as evidence of Christian missionary influence; subsequent scholarship (Howitt 1904; Stanner 1958-1959) established the pre-contact origin of the tradition firmly.

The **Bunjil's Shelter** rock-art site at Black Range (near Stawell, Victoria) preserves a painted Bunjil figure with two dingoes that is the principal documented Bunjil rock-art image. The site remains a sacred Kulin custodianship.
