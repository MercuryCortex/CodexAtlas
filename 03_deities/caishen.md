# Caishen

Caishen (*Cáishén* 財神, "God of Wealth") is the Chinese deity of **wealth and prosperity** — among the most universally worshipped figures of Chinese popular religion, welcomed into homes and shops above all at the **Lunar New Year**, when his image is hung and offerings made to draw in the year's riches. Caishen is less a single god than a **role** filled by several deities: a **"civil wealth god" (Wencaishen)**, often identified with the loyal minister **Bi Gan** or with Lord Zhao, and a **"martial wealth god" (Wucaishen)**, identified with **Zhao Gongming** — a fierce figure riding a **black tiger** and wielding a steel whip — and also with the deified hero-general **[[guan-yu|Guan Yu]]**, whom merchants venerate as a guarantor of loyalty, contracts, and profit. His emblems are the **golden ingots (yuanbao)** and the overflowing treasure-bowl. Caishen embodies the frankly this-worldly orientation of much Chinese folk devotion, in which divine favour is sought for concrete prosperity and a flourishing household.
