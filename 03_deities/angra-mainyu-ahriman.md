# Angra Mainyu / Ahriman

## Identity
Angra Mainyu ("Hostile Spirit") is the cosmic adversary of Ahura Mazda in Zoroastrian theology — the principle of the Lie (*druj*), darkness, deception, death, and disorder. In the Gathas of Zarathustra he is named in Yasna 30 as one of two primordial Spirits ("twin spirits") who chose, at the beginning, between Truth and the Lie; from this primordial choice flows all cosmic dualism. In the Younger Avesta and especially the Pahlavi commentaries (Bundahishn, Denkard) Angra Mainyu is mythologically developed into a fully personified Ahriman, a "dark mirror" of Ahura Mazda with his own demonic hierarchy (the *daēvas*, including Aēšma "Wrath", source of the apocryphal Asmodeus).

The structural impact of this figure on the history of religion is enormous. The pre-exilic Hebrew Bible knows a *śāṭān* who is a functionary of YHWH's court (Job 1–2; Zech 3) — an adversary-prosecutor, not a cosmic enemy. By the late Second Temple period, Satan is becoming an independent power, prince of demons, the adversary of God and humanity. This shift, occurring after Persian rule of Judah (539–332 BCE), is the most striking single piece of evidence cited for Zoroastrian theological influence on developing Jewish (and through Judaism, Christian and Islamic) demonology. The Pahlavi Bundahishn's cosmic dualism — Ahriman besieges the good creation, will be defeated at the end of time — provides the structural model for apocalyptic literature of all three Abrahamic religions.
