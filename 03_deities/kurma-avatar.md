# Kurma (Tortoise Avatar of Vishnu)

Stub — to be expanded. Kurma is the second of Vishnu's ten avatars (*Dashavatara*). In the *Samudra Manthan* (Churning of the Ocean) narrative, the gods and demons agree to churn the cosmic ocean to obtain the nectar of immortality (*amrita*). They use Mount Mandara as the churning rod and the cosmic serpent Vasuki as the rope. But Mount Mandara sinks into the ocean floor. Vishnu takes the form of a giant tortoise (Kurma) and dives to the bottom, providing his shell as the stable base for Mount Mandara — without which the churning cannot proceed.

**Turtle Island parallel**: The Kurma-as-cosmic-support is a Vedic-Hindu structural cognate of the Haudenosaunee **Turtle Island** cosmogony: in both traditions, a cosmic turtle provides the foundation of the world. The Haudenosaunee Great Turtle supports the newly-created earth after [[sky-woman-haudenosaunee]]'s fall; Kurma supports the cosmic mountain that generates the world's most precious substance. Both are **parallel-form** — no transmission documented; independent solutions to the same cosmological question (what is the ultimate foundation of the world?). The turtle's association with cosmic support appears in Chinese mythology (four turtles supporting the world's corners) as well. See [[earth-diver-creation]] for the full cross-tradition table.
