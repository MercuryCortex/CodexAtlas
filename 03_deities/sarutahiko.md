# Sarutahiko Ōkami

## Identity

Sarutahiko is the principal *kunitsukami* (earthly kami) who appears at the celestial crossroads at the moment of the *tenson kōrin* — the Heavenly Descent — when Amaterasu's grandson Ninigi-no-Mikoto is being sent down from the high plain of heaven (Takamagahara) to rule the Japanese islands. He stands at the fork in the road, an enormous figure radiating light from his eyes and mouth, and refuses to give way. The heavenly kami are intimidated; Amaterasu sends [[ame-no-uzume]] — the bold dancing-goddess of the rock-cave episode — to confront him. Her uninhibited approach (she bares her breasts and laughs at him) breaks the standoff; Sarutahiko reveals he is an earthly kami who has come specifically to guide the heavenly party to their destination. He escorts Ninigi safely down, and Uzume in turn escorts him back to his earthly home at the Isuzu River (the future site of the Ise Grand Shrine's Outer Shrine area). Folk tradition pairs Sarutahiko and Uzume as husband and wife from this point.

## Mythological role

Sarutahiko is the canonical *crossroads kami* (*chimata-no-kami*) — the deity who controls and guides movement at road-forks, mountain passes, and territorial boundaries. The Heavenly Descent narrative makes him the structural mediator between the heavenly and earthly orders: without his cooperation, the Yamato divine genealogy cannot enter the islands. His ritual continues to be invoked at modern Shinto crossroads festivals and travel-protection rituals.

His iconography — long red face, long nose, shining eyes, large size — is widely treated as the proto-tengu (the mountain spirits of later folklore). The connection between Sarutahiko and the tengu is iconographic and functional, not a direct identification.
