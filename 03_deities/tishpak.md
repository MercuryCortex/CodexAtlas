# Tishpak

## Identity

Tishpak (Tišpak) is the tutelary storm- and warrior-god of the city of Eshnunna in the Diyala region of Mesopotamia. He rose to prominence in the late third and early second millennium BCE, displacing the older god [[ninazu]] as Eshnunna's chief deity and taking over Ninazu's attributes — including association with the **mušḫuššu**, the horned snake-dragon that would later become the famous symbolic animal of [[marduk]] of Babylon.

## Mythological role

Tishpak's mythological importance rests largely on his identification as the hero of the fragmentary **Labbu Myth**. In that text a monstrous dragon — Labbu, "the raging one," fashioned by the Sea — rises up and devastates the land and its people. The gods, in alarm, seek a champion; the god commissioned to confront and slay the dragon is most plausibly Tishpak, who kills Labbu and restores order. Though the tablet is broken, the myth preserves a clear **chaoskampf**: a warrior-god slays a sea-born dragon to save the cosmos, the same pattern as Ninurta against [[anzu]] and [[asag]] and Marduk against [[tiamat]].

## Cross-tradition significance

Tishpak is a valuable node for tracing how the **dragon-slayer emblem migrated** within Mesopotamia. The mušḫuššu dragon belonged first to Ninazu, then to Tishpak at Eshnunna, and finally to Marduk when Babylon became the imperial capital — a concrete case of a sacred symbol following political power from city to city. He thereby links the local Eshnunna cult to the great Babylonian chaoskampf of the *Enuma Elish*, and to the broader ancient Near Eastern storm-god-versus-dragon complex.
