# Zhurong

## Identity
Zhurong is the Chinese **god of fire** — the bright, ordering power of flame, lord of the **south** and of summer, and in the cosmological scheme the minister of fire to the high god. Where fire civilizes (the hearth, the forge, the beacon), Zhurong presides.

## Earliest attestation
Named across the early corpus — the *Shanhaijing* (Classic of Mountains and Seas), the *Huainanzi*, and the *Shiji* — as a fire-deity and divine official, and as a culture-figure who taught the use of fire.

## Mythological role — the war with Gonggong
Zhurong's most consequential myth is his **battle with [[gonggong]]**, the monstrous god of water and floods. Zhurong (fire/order) defeats Gonggong (water/chaos); enraged, the vanquished Gonggong hurls himself against **Mount Buzhou**, the pillar that holds up the sky, cracking it and tilting the heavens and the earth — the disaster that the goddess [[nuwa]] must afterward repair by smelting five-coloured stones to patch the sky. Zhurong is thus the fire that wins the cosmic war but whose victory sets the world askew.

## Cross-tradition
Zhurong belongs to the type of the **divine fire** — the personified flame that orders the world and stands against watery chaos — paralleling the Vedic [[agni]] and the fire-gods of other traditions. The fire-versus-flood combat is a Chinese form of the cosmogonic struggle of order against chaos.

## Iconography
A fierce deity riding two dragons or a beast-drawn chariot; the colour red; the emblem of the south and of flame.
