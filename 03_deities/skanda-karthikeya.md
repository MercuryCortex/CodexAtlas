# Skanda / Kārttikeya

## Identity
The principal warrior-god of classical and medieval Hinduism — second son of Shiva and Pārvatī, commander of the divine armies (*Devasenāpati*), and the central deity of the Tamil-Murugan cultic tradition. In the Sanskrit-classical tradition, Skanda's most famous birth story has him born from Shiva's seed (separately ejected, gestated in fire, in the Ganges, and finally by the six Krittikā mothers — the Pleiades — who suckled the six-faced infant; hence *Kārttikeya*, "Son of the Krittikās") to serve the specific cosmic purpose of slaying the demon Tāraka, whose elimination required a son of Shiva and Pārvatī. In the Tamil tradition, Murugan is the principal cultic deity of pre-Aryan / Dravidian Tamil religion — attested in the earliest Tamil Sangam literature (~1st c. BCE – 3rd c. CE) as the youthful warrior-hunter god of the hill-country (*kuṟiñci* tiṇai). The two traditions — Sanskrit-Kārttikeya and Tamil-Murugan — were canonically identified by the early classical period and have been functionally unified within Tamil Shaivism, where Murugan is one of the supreme objects of devotion alongside Shiva himself.

## Earliest attestation
- **Mahābhārata** (Vana-parvan, Anuśāsana-parvan; ~late 1st c. BCE – 4th c. CE): the multiple Skanda birth narratives, the Skanda-versus-Tāraka mythology, the Skanda's adoption into the developed Shaiva mythological complex.
- **Tamil Sangam literature** (~1st–3rd c. CE): Murugan as the principal *kuṟiñci* (hill-country) deity. The *Tirumurukāṟṟuppaṭai* (~5th c. CE) is the foundational Tamil-Skanda devotional poem.
- **Kushan-period coinage** (~1st–3rd c. CE): the Kushan kings (especially Huviṣka) issued coins depicting Skanda; the deity had substantial Kushan-period cultic prominence.
- **Kālidāsa's *Kumārasambhava*** (~5th c. CE): the classical Sanskrit epic-poem on the birth of Kumāra (Skanda) — one of the supreme works of classical Sanskrit literature.

## Mythological role
- **Slayer of Tāraka**: the cosmic-purpose figure — the demon Tāraka has the boon of being slayable only by a son of Shiva; the entire mythological apparatus around Skanda's birth (Pārvatī's seduction of the ascetic Shiva, Kāma's burning by Shiva's third eye, the seed-deposit and the gestation, the final birth and slaying of Tāraka) is organized around this single cosmic-narrative end.
- **Commander of the divine armies**: *Senāpati* of the *devas*.
- **Youthful-warrior aesthetic**: Skanda is permanently youthful, perpetually pre-adult; the iconography emphasizes the unmarried young warrior.
- **Tamil Murugan-Vallī marriage**: in the Tamil tradition, Murugan's marriage to the hill-tribal hunter-girl Vallī is one of the central cultic narratives — a cross-class / cross-tribal romantic-divine marriage that grounds the Tamil-popular devotion to Murugan.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Tamil Murugan ↔ Sanskrit Skanda**: the principal cross-regional integration. The two have been canonically identified since the early classical period; the Tamil tradition reads them as identical, with Murugan as the indigenous Tamil form of the pan-Indian Skanda.
- **Kataragama Deviyo (Sri Lankan)**: the Kataragama temple in southeastern Sri Lanka is a major Murugan-Skanda pilgrimage center shared by Hindu Tamils and Sinhala Buddhists; both communities recognize the deity, with Buddhists treating him as a *deva* protector of the Buddha-Sasana. The shared cult is one of the most documented cases of Hindu-Buddhist cross-religious deity-sharing in South Asia.
- **Kushan-period transmission**: the Kushan coinage's depiction of Skanda (sometimes labeled *Skando*, *Kumāro*, *Mahāseno*) is one of the early documentations of the deity's pan-North-Indian / Central Asian cult; some scholars have noted parallels with the Greek Ares and the Iranian war-deity Verethraghna (*Bahram*) that may reflect Indo-Iranian shared substrate.
- **Mauritian / Malaysian / Singaporean diaspora Murugan worship**: the global Tamil diaspora's principal religious institution is generally the Murugan-Skanda temple; the Thaipusam festival (annual, principally honoring Murugan) is celebrated globally in Tamil diaspora communities.

## Iconography
- Six-faced (Shanmukha) young warrior, often seated on or accompanied by his peacock mount, holding the *vel* (spear) given by his mother Pārvatī.
- Younger, more youthful body than his brother Gaṇeśa.
- Multiple arms (variably six, twelve) holding weapons.

## Disputes
- **Indigenous Tamil Murugan vs. Sanskrit Skanda historical relation**: how much the Tamil Murugan is an independent indigenous Dravidian deity later identified with Sanskrit Skanda, vs. how much the identification reflects deeper cultural-religious cross-pollination across the Aryan-Dravidian divide, is a continuing scholarly question. The Sangam-era Murugan is firmly indigenous-Tamil; the canonical Skanda-Murugan identification is post-Sangam.
- **Kushan-period continuity**: how the substantial Kushan-era Skanda cult connects to the subsequent medieval Skanda-Murugan traditions is incompletely understood.
