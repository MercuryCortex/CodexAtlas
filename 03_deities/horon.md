# Horon

## Identity

Horon (Ugaritic **Ḥoron**, also Ḥawran/Hauron) is a Canaanite chthonic god — a deity of the underworld, of serpents and protection against snakebite, and of magic, exorcism, and healing. He is invoked in incantations both to inflict harm (curses) and to ward it off, and he stands, with [[resheph]], among the principal gods of the dark, plague-and-netherworld sphere of Canaanite religion.

## Mythological role and cult

In the Ugaritic texts Horon appears especially in connection with **serpents and venom**: a damaged mythological text recounts a struggle involving Horon and snakes, and he is called upon in anti-venom incantations. His curses are feared — to be "smitten by Horon" is a dire fate. His chthonic and apotropaic character made him a god of the boundary between the living and the dead, and of the magical control of dangerous powers.

## Cross-tradition significance

Horon is a striking case of a **Canaanite god exported into Egypt**. Under the name **Hauron** he was worshipped at Giza in the New Kingdom and became identified with the Great Sphinx — the colossus understood as **Hor-em-akhet** ("Horus in the Horizon," Greek Harmachis) — so that the Levantine chthonic god fused with the Egyptian falcon-god [[horus]] at one of Egypt's most famous monuments. He thus exemplifies the two-way traffic of deities across the Levant–Egypt frontier, and is a useful node for tracing how gods migrated and merged along the trade and imperial routes of the Late Bronze Age.
