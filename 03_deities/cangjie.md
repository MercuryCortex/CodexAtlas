# Cangjie

## Identity
Cangjie is the legendary **inventor of Chinese writing** — the four-eyed scribe of the Yellow Emperor ([[huangdi]]) who first devised the characters, and so is venerated as the patron-deity of the written word.

## Earliest attestation
Named in the *Xunzi*, *Han Feizi*, and *Huainanzi* (Warring States–Han) as the creator of script; later tradition gives him **four eyes** to see the patterns of heaven and earth.

## Mythological role — the maker of the characters
The story tells that Cangjie, charged by [[huangdi]] with finding a better record than knotted cords, studied **the tracks of birds and beasts**, the shapes of mountains and rivers, and from them devised the first written characters. So momentous was the act that — the *Huainanzi* says — "the sky rained millet and the ghosts wailed by night," for once humankind could write, the natural and spirit orders were forever changed. He is the Chinese instance of the civilization-founding gift of writing.

## Cross-tradition
Cangjie belongs to the **scribe / inventor-of-writing** neighborhood — the figure who gives humankind the written word and with it civilization — alongside the Egyptian [[thoth]] and [[seshat]], the Mesopotamian [[nabu]], and the Armenian [[tir-armenian]].

## Iconography
A sage with four eyes, holding or inscribing the first characters; associated with birds' tracks.
