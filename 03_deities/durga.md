# Durga

## Identity
Durgā — "the unassailable" / "the inaccessible" — is the great warrior goddess of Hinduism, embodied from the combined *tejas* (radiant power) of all the male gods in order to defeat the buffalo-demon Mahiṣa whom Brahmā's boon had made invulnerable to males. The central goddess of the [[phase-5-001-devi-mahatmya]] (~6th c. CE), where she is presented as the ultimate reality in feminine form (*prakṛti*, *māyā*, the Brahman of the Upaniṣads). Iconographically: many-armed (typically eight, ten, or eighteen arms holding weapons from each of the contributing gods); mounted on a lion or tiger; depicted in the moment of slaying Mahiṣa (the famous *Mahiṣāsuramardinī* iconography, with the demon's torso emerging from a beheaded buffalo body). The autumn festival *Navarātrī* / *Durgā Pūjā* (especially intense in Bengal) is her principal festival; ten-day worship culminates in her victory over Mahiṣa.

## Earliest attestation
Earlier Vedic and Hindu literature has only marginal mentions; the Goddess's coherent literary emergence is in the *Devī Māhātmya* (~400–600 CE). Iconographic representation: Gupta and post-Gupta period (~4th–7th c. CE) Mahiṣāsuramardinī sculptures across India.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[kali]]** — within the Devi Mahatmya, Kālī emerges from Durgā's forehead; later Shakta tradition treats them as related but distinct manifestations of the Goddess.
- **[[anat]]** (Canaanite warrior goddess) — *parallel-motif*: structurally parallel divine-female-warrior with no historical link.
- **[[athena]]** — *parallel-motif*: warrior goddess in armor.
