# Gabija

Gabija is the Lithuanian goddess of the **hearth-fire** — the living flame on the household hearth, divinized and treated as a sacred, almost personal presence at the heart of the home. Her name is linked to *gaubti*, "to cover," for the central rite of her cult was the careful **banking of the fire at night** — covering the embers so the fire would rest safely until morning, an act spoken of as **"putting Gabija to bed."** She was **fed** (offerings of bread and salt), kept clean, and never to be profaned: to spit in the fire, step on it, or let it be put out carelessly was to offend the goddess, who might then "go walking" and burn the house in anger. As the divinized sacred hearth she is the Baltic member of a deep **Indo-European family of hearth-and-fire deities** — the Roman **[[vesta|Vesta]]**, the Greek Hestia, the Vedic **[[agni|Agni]]** — each expressing the same intuition that the domestic fire is holy, the living center of the home, to be tended with reverence. Gabija embodies the sanctity of fire and the religious life of the Lithuanian household.
