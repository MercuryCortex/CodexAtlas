# Ek Chuah

Ek Chuah (Yucatec Maya, "Black Star/Scorpion"; "God M" in the codex catalogue) is the Maya patron god of **merchants, travelers, and cacao** — the deity invoked by the long-distance traders (*ppolom*) who carried obsidian, salt, jade, and cacao-bean currency along the routes of the lowlands. He is recognized by his **black body-paint**, a long drooping nose or lip, and the **merchant's pack** slung from a tumpline with a walking-staff. Diego de Landa records that travelers who camped on the road would set up three stones, place incense on each, and pray to Ek Chuah for safe passage. Because **cacao** was both a luxury and a money, the god of merchants was also the **god of the cacao tree and its growers**, honored in a festival of the month Muan by cacao-planters who sacrificed a spotted dog (the color of cacao) in his name. He has a darker, warlike aspect as well — fitting for the patron of those who ventured armed into hostile territory. He is the Maya equivalent of the central-Mexican merchant-god **Yacatecuhtli**.
