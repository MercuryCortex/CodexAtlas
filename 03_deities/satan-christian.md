# Satan (Christian)

The chief adversary in Christian cosmology — a fallen angel turned tempter, accuser, and ruler of the present age "from below." The figure assembles materials from several earlier sources:

- **Hebrew Bible *ha-satan*** — "the adversary," in Job and Zechariah a member of the divine council, not yet evil; his Christian transformation into a personal cosmic opponent is post-biblical
- **Zoroastrian [[angra-mainyu-ahriman]]** — the cosmic anti-god of evil; structurally the closest precedent, transmitted through Persian-period Jewish apocalyptic
- **1 Enoch's Watchers** — the [[phase-3-004-1-enoch|Book of the Watchers]] (~-300) develops the fall-of-angels mythology that Christian Satanology inherits
- **Isaiah 14 / Ezekiel 28** — pre-Christian Hebrew texts about fallen kings later allegorized as describing Satan's pre-cosmic fall (the *Lucifer* / "light-bringer" identification comes from the Latin Vulgate of Isa 14:12)

The Christian Satan is **NOT** a co-creator deity. Mainline Christianity is monotheistic: Satan is a creature, finite, will be defeated. The dualistic move — Satan as second principle equal to God — was made by [[tradition-bogomilism|Bogomils]] (10th c. Balkans), [[tradition-catharism|Cathars]] (12th–13th c. Languedoc), and earlier by [[tradition-manichaeism|Manichaeans]] (3rd c. onward). These groups were declared heretical; see [[phase-5-029-interrogatio-iohannis]] for Bogomil Satanology, and [[phase-4-016-manichaean-kephalaia]] for Manichaean dualism.

Modern reception:
- **Milton's *Paradise Lost*** (1667) gave the Satan-figure its enduring literary form
- **Modern Satanism** (LaVey 1966, Satanic Temple 2013) is largely *symbolic*: Satan as figure of individual liberty and reason, not literal worship
- **Anton LaVey** in [[phase-7-030-satanic-bible]] formalized atheistic-symbolic Satanism

The Christian Satan should not be confused with [[satanael]] (Bogomil/Cathar Satan-as-creator), with [[yaldabaoth]] (Sethian ignorant demiurge), or with the various trickster figures in non-Christian traditions.
