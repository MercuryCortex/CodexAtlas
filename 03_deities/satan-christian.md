# Satan (Christian)

## The serpent identification chain — the most consequential cross-tradition knot

The Christian Satan is assembled from at least **four distinct source figures** that were progressively merged. Understanding the merge-sequence is the key to the whole figure:

### Stage 1 — *ha-satan* (the Accuser) in Hebrew Bible
In Job 1–2 and Zechariah 3:1–2, *ha-satan* ("the adversary/accuser") is a **member of the divine council** — a prosecutorial figure who challenges Job's righteousness before Yahweh. He is not evil; he fulfills a judicial function within the heavenly court. No fall narrative, no cosmic opposition, no serpent identification. This is the earliest stratum.

### Stage 2 — Eden Serpent (Genesis 3)
The Genesis 3 serpent is described as "the most cunning of all the wild animals" (*ʿārûm*) Yahweh had made — a **created animal**, not a supernatural being. It speaks, it reasons, it deceives Eve. **The Hebrew text makes no identification of the serpent with Satan or any angel.** This is a critical point: the later Christian fusion is *exegetical*, not textual.

The Enochic tradition offers an alternative: [[gadreel]] is identified in 1 Enoch 69:6 as the Watcher who "led astray Eve" — a *Watcher-angel*, not the serpent itself. Two competing Second Temple traditions, then: (a) the serpent was a Watcher-angel; (b) the serpent *became* the identification of the cosmic adversary.

### Stage 3 — Wisdom of Solomon 2:24 (~50 BCE, Hellenistic Jewish)
The first explicit identification of the Eden serpent with the devil: *"but through the devil's envy death entered the world, and those who belong to his company experience it."* This Hellenistic Jewish text grafts the cosmic-adversary figure onto the Genesis 3 narrative. **This is the hinge text** — before it, no canonical identification; after it, the identification becomes standard in Christian exegesis.

### Stage 4 — Revelation 12:9 and 20:2 (first-century CE)
The canonical identification in Christian scripture: *"The great dragon was thrown down, that ancient serpent, who is called the Devil and Satan, the deceiver of the whole world."* Revelation explicitly names the **four-fold identity**: dragon + ancient serpent + Devil + Satan. The "ancient serpent" is a direct reference back to Genesis 3 — the first moment in canonical Christian scripture where the identification is made complete.

Revelation 20:2 repeats it identically: *"the dragon, that ancient serpent, who is the Devil and Satan."* This double attestation in the final canonical book locked the identification for all subsequent Christian theology.

### Stage 5 — Patristic telescoping: Leviathan, Lucifer, Eden
With the Revelation identification established, patristic theologians (Irenaeus, Tertullian, Origen, Augustine) retroactively read:
- **Genesis 3** as Satan in the serpent's form
- **Isaiah 14:12** (*Lucifer/Heylel*) as Satan's pre-cosmic fall (see [[lucifer]])
- **Job's Leviathan and Isaiah 27:1** as poetic names for Satan
- **Ezekiel 28** (taunt against the Prince of Tyre) as a further description of Satan's fall

This retroactive reading collapsed four distinct ancient figures — a divine council prosecutor, a garden animal, a chaos-serpent, and a star metaphor for a Babylonian king — into a single cosmic personality. The result is one of the most complex *composite figures* in world religion, assembled across roughly 600 years of exegetical accretion.

## What Satan is NOT in mainstream Christianity
- **Not co-eternal with God**: Satan is a fallen creature, finite, whose power is limited and temporary. The Christian position is strictly monotheistic.
- **Not the creator of the material world**: that dualist move is made by Bogomils ([[satanael]]), Cathars, and Manichaeans ([[prince-of-darkness-manichaean]]) — all declared heretical.
- **Not identical to the Eden serpent in the Hebrew source**: only in Christian exegesis via Wisdom of Solomon and Revelation.

## The Zoroastrian structural debt
The cosmic-adversary *function* of Christian Satan — a personal anti-divine being who opposes God's redemptive work throughout history — has no significant precedent within pre-exilic Hebrew religion but maps directly onto [[angra-mainyu-ahriman]] in Zoroastrian theology. The Persian period (~538–332 BCE) brought intense Zoroastrian-Jewish contact, and the 2nd Temple Jewish apocalyptic literature (1 Enoch, Daniel, the Qumran War Scroll) shows structural changes — cosmic dualism, a named cosmic adversary, eschatological battle, resurrection — that scholars (Boyce, Shaked, Russell, Hultgård) consistently attribute to Zoroastrian influence. This makes the Zoroastrian theological transmission possibly **the single most consequential non-Semitic influence on Western religious history**.

## Modern reception
- **Milton's *Paradise Lost*** (1667): the literary canonization of Satan as a psychologically complex tragic rebel — "Better to reign in Hell than serve in Heaven"
- **Romantic reception**: Blake, Shelley, Byron, Anatole France — Lucifer/Satan as the figure of enlightenment, rebellion, individual liberty (the [[prometheus]] equation)
- **Modern Satanism** (LaVey's *Satanic Bible* 1969; Satanic Temple 2013): largely symbolic/atheistic — Satan as a figure of rational individualism, not literal devil-worship
- **Horror and popular culture**: the medieval iconographic Satan (horns, tail, trident, goat-face) continues as the dominant popular image, largely disconnected from theological substance
