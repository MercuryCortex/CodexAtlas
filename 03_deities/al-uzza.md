# al-ʿUzzā

## Identity
al-ʿUzzā (Arabic *al-ʿUzzā*, العزى — "the Mightiest," feminine superlative of *ʿazīz* "mighty") is the **second of the three "daughters of Allāh"** named at **Quran 53:19-22** alongside [[allat|al-Lāt]] and [[manat|Manāt]] — the *Sūrat al-Najm* polemic and the so-called "Satanic Verses" / *gharānīq* incident context. She is the **principal goddess of the Quraysh tribe** per Islamic-tradition memory (Muhammad's own tribe), with her chief sanctuary in the **Wādī Nakhla** east of Mecca — a sacred grove of three acacia (*samurah*) trees where her aniconic cult-stone stood until its destruction by **Khalid ibn al-Walid in 8 AH (~630 CE)** on Muhammad's order. Her cult is also well-attested in the **Nabataean** sphere (Petra, Hegra, the broader North Arabian range) where Hellenistic-period *interpretatio graeca* identifies her with **Aphrodite-Venus** — the morning-star astral association consistent with the broader Northwest-Semitic ʿAṯtar / Inanna / Ishtar-Venus pattern. (Hoyland 2001; Hawting 1999; Healey 2001.)

## Earliest attestation
- **Nabataean inscriptions** (c. -100 onward) — al-ʿUzzā is one of the principal Nabataean goddesses, attested in Petra, Hegra, and the broader Nabataean realm. Healey 2001.
- **Safaitic / Hismaic graffiti** (c. -100 to 400 CE) — North Arabian rock-graffito attestation. Macdonald 2000 + OCIANA.
- **Quran 53:19-22** (~610-632 CE) — the principal Islamic-period textual attestation, naming al-ʿUzzā alongside al-Lāt and Manāt and rejecting their worship. ([[phase-4-034-quran]])
- **Ibn Ishaq *Sirat Rasul Allah* (~750 CE)** — narrates the Nakhla-sanctuary destruction by Khalid ibn al-Walid in 8 AH. ([[phase-5-043-ibn-ishaq-sirat-rasul-allah]] — this batch)
- **Ibn al-Kalbi *Kitāb al-Aṣnām* (~9th c. CE)** — detailed Islamic-tradition reconstructive description of the Nakhla three-tree sanctuary, the *sadana* (priestly caretakers — Banu Shayban of the Sulaym tribe), and the Khalid destruction-narrative including the apparition-of-a-naked-woman vignette. Treat source-critically per Hawting 1999.

## Mythological role
1. **Second of the three "daughters of Allāh."** With al-Lāt and Manāt, al-ʿUzzā is invoked in the Quranic polemic of Sura 53 as one of the goddesses associated with Allāh in pre-Islamic Meccan religion — a configuration the Quran emphatically rejects in favor of Allāh's unique sovereignty without consort or daughters.
2. **Principal Quraysh goddess.** Per Islamic-tradition memory (Ibn al-Kalbi, Ibn Ishaq), al-ʿUzzā was the **most-favored** of the three goddesses among the Quraysh; the Quraysh would invoke her specifically (*lā wa-l-ʿUzzā* "no, by al-ʿUzzā"; *wa-l-Lāt wa-l-ʿUzzā* "by al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā").
3. **The Nakhla sanctuary.** A sacred grove of three acacia trees in Wādī Nakhla east of Mecca, with an aniconic standing-stone cult-image, a *sadana* priestly caretaker-clan, and an associated *jinniyya* / female-spirit guardian narrated in Ibn al-Kalbi as a naked-black-woman apparition slain by Khalid ibn al-Walid at the destruction.
4. **Astral / Venus-planet association.** Hoyland 2001: al-ʿUzzā's astral profile fits the Northwest-Semitic Venus-goddess pattern (cognate semantically with the *ʿazz*-stem in Arabic and the Northwest-Semitic ʿAṯtar / Athtar Venus-deity); the Hellenistic-period Nabataean *interpretatio graeca* with Aphrodite is consistent with this.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Aphrodite-Venus** — *ancient-identification* (Nabataean / Hellenistic *interpretatio graeca*): the morning-star astral profile fits the Greek Aphrodite Urania / Venus pattern.
- **[[allat|Allāt]] and [[manat|Manāt]]** — close cultic associates; the triadic "daughters of Allāh."
- **Broader Northwest-Semitic ʿAṯtar / Athtar (Venus-deity)** — etymological and astral-functional cognate. Hoyland 2001.

## Iconography
- **Aniconic *baetyl* (sacred standing-stone)** at the Nakhla sanctuary — the canonical pre-Islamic Arabian form.
- **Three sacred acacia (*samurah*) trees** at Nakhla — the grove-sanctuary form.
- **Lion-throne / lion-iconography** in Nabataean / Palmyrene contexts where al-ʿUzzā receives Aphrodite-Venus type iconographic borrowings.
- **Morning-star / Venus-planet** as iconographic-cosmic association.

## Disputes
- **Source-critical reliability of the Nakhla-narrative.** The detailed Ibn al-Kalbi destruction-narrative (Khalid ibn al-Walid, the naked-black-woman apparition, the *sadana* Banu Shayban) is later Islamic-period reconstructive material with strong polemical interest in constructing pre-Islamic Quraysh idolatry as the foil for Islamic monotheism. The historical core (al-ʿUzzā as principal Quraysh goddess; Nakhla as her sanctuary; destruction at the conquest of Mecca) is secure; the iconographic and ritual details should be read source-critically per Hawting 1999.
- **The "Satanic Verses" / *gharānīq* incident** (Quran 53:19-22, with the interpolated *tilka l-gharānīq al-ʿulā wa-inna shafāʿatahunna la-turtajā* "those are the high-flying cranes whose intercession is to be hoped for" briefly accepted then retracted by Muhammad per Ibn Ishaq / Tabari). Historicity is contested in Islamic tradition (some sources include it, later sources reject it as inauthentic); modern critical scholarship is divided. Ahmed 2017 *Before Orthodoxy: The Satanic Verses in Early Islam* surveys.
- **al-ʿUzzā = Aphrodite identification.** The Hellenistic-period Nabataean identification is well-attested; whether the pre-Islamic Hijazi al-ʿUzzā retained a clean Venus-astral profile in the immediate-pre-Islamic Quraysh context is partly speculative.
