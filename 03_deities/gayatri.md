# Gayatri

## Identity

Gayatri (Sanskrit **Gāyatrī**) is the **personified Gayatri mantra** — the most sacred verse of the Vedas (Rig Veda 3.62.10) divinized as a goddess. Because that mantra is addressed to the solar impeller [[savitr]], the goddess is also called **Sāvitrī**; and because the recitation of the Vedas begins with her, she is honored as **Veda-mata**, "the Mother of the Vedas." She is the supreme instance in Hinduism of a **sacred sound become a deity**.

## Mythological role

Gayatri's identity is inseparable from the mantra she embodies — the verse that every initiated twice-born Hindu is taught to recite at dawn and dusk, the heart of the daily *sandhya* worship. As the embodiment of this supremely holy utterance, she is venerated as the mother of sacred knowledge and the consort of **[[brahma]]** the creator (a Puranic story tells how Brahma, needing a wife for a sacrifice, took Gayatri). In her developed iconography she is shown with five faces (representing the five elements or the five *pranas*) and ten arms bearing the emblems of the great gods, signifying that all divine powers are gathered in the sacred mantra. To "be given the Gayatri" is to be initiated into Vedic study; to recite her is to invoke the quickening of the mind by the divine light.

## Cross-tradition significance

Gayatri is the clearest Hindu example of the **divinization of sacred speech** — the mantra, the holy verse, worshipped as a goddess and regarded as the very substance of the Vedas. She belongs with [[vac-goddess|Vac]] (Speech) and [[saraswati]] (knowledge and eloquence) in the Indian theology of sound, which holds that the cosmos is sustained and made accessible through divinized utterance. This places her within the broad cross-tradition concern with the **creative and saving power of the sacred word** — the Egyptian Hu, the Hebrew *davar*, the Greek *Logos* — here uniquely focused on a single, supremely holy verse.
