# Asag

## Identity

Asag (Akkadian **Asakku**) is a Sumerian demon of sickness and chaos, best known as the great adversary of the warrior-god [[ninurta]] in the epic poem *Lugal-e*. As a common noun *asag/asakku* denotes a class of sickness-demon held responsible for fever and disease; as a mythological figure, Asag is a monstrous being of enormous power — in *Lugal-e* the offspring of [[an-sumerian]] (Heaven) and the Earth — who rises up in the mountains and threatens the order of the gods.

## Mythological role

*Lugal-e* ("O King!") recounts how Asag, dwelling in the mountains and supported by an army of stone-warriors, grows so powerful that the rivers run cold and the land is disordered. Ninurta marches against him. After a fierce struggle — and a moment of danger when Asag's onslaught nearly overwhelms him — Ninurta destroys Asag and scatters his stone army. In the aftermath Ninurta reorders the cosmos: he builds a barrier of stones (the *hursag*, the mountain range) to hold back the waters, channels the Tigris for irrigation, and pronounces destinies upon the stones, rewarding those that aided him and cursing those that sided with Asag. The myth thus joins a chaoskampf to an aetiology of the landscape and of stones.

## Cross-tradition significance

Asag is one of the three canonical Mesopotamian chaos-adversaries (with [[anzu]] and [[tiamat]]) whose defeat by the storm-warrior establishes and re-establishes cosmic order. The pattern — a young warrior-god subduing a chaotic, mountain- or sea-born monster and then organizing the world — is the Mesopotamian template behind the wider ancient Near Eastern **chaoskampf**, and feeds comparisons with Indra–Vritra, Baal–Yam, and Marduk–Tiamat.
