# Daramulan

The **one-legged sky-being** of the south-eastern Australian Aboriginal nations — principal agent of male initiation rites at the **Bora ground**. Daramulan's distinctive iconography (one leg) and his association with the **bullroarer** (the carved wooden slat swung on a cord to produce a low roaring tone) place him at the centre of the initiation-mystery tradition. In many tellings he is the son or junior of [[baiame]] (the supreme sky-father); in others they are alternate names for closely related figures.

**The bullroarer and the initiation-mystery.** The bullroarer's voice IS Daramulan's voice. Initiated men carved bullroarers from sacred wood and used them at the Bora ground to summon Daramulan's presence during young men's initiation; women and uninitiated men were forbidden to see the bullroarer or hear its origin under pain of death. The Australian initiation-mystery — secret men's knowledge protected by strict separation from women and the uninitiated — is one of the most-documented cases worldwide of **gendered religious knowledge as social structure**; Daramulan is the deity whose presence the mystery protects.

The Yuin coastal-NSW Daramulan tradition was extensively documented by Howitt (1904) and remains one of the most-detailed pre-contact records of south-eastern Australian Aboriginal religion in the colonial-anthropological corpus.
