# ʻOro

## Identity
ʻOro is the **supreme war deity of the Society Islands** (Tahiti, Raiatea, Bora Bora) during the 17th and 18th centuries — the period of the cult's apex just before European contact. Son of **Tāʻaroa** (Ta'aroa), the primordial creator-god of Tahitian theology, ʻOro superseded his father as the dominant deity of the island political system. His worship was centered at **Marae Taputapuatea** on the island of Raiatea — the most sacred ritual platform in eastern Polynesia, now a **UNESCO World Heritage Site** — and demanded the most elaborate sacrifice system in Polynesian religion, including regular **human sacrifice** (*tapu* sacrificial offerings). ʻOro's cult collapsed rapidly after the arrival of Christian missionaries and European political dominance in the early 19th century.

## Earliest attestation
- The cult of ʻOro is first documented in European records by **Captain James Cook's voyages (1769–1779)**, which describe active sacrificial ceremonies at Marae Taputapuatea.
- **William Ellis**, missionary, documented the cult in *Polynesian Researches* (1829) after witnessing the late-stage practice and its suppression.
- **Teuira Henry's *Ancient Tahiti*** (1928) compiles oral traditions, genealogies, and ritual descriptions preserved through the 19th century.
- The cult itself was already old by the 17th century; Marae Taputapuatea's construction and use extends back to at least the 11th–13th centuries CE by archaeological estimate.

## Mythological role
**Origin**: ʻOro was born to Ta'aroa (Tangaroa) and a mortal woman on the sacred mountain Maiao-iti. He chose Raiatea as his earthly dwelling and the Marae Taputapuatea as his primary residence.

**Human sacrifice**: ʻOro demanded *tapu* victims — killed at the marae as offerings before warfare, major ceremonies, and political transitions. Chiefs competed to offer the most valued sacrificial victims to secure ʻOro's favor and demonstrate their power.

**The 'Arioi Society**: ʻOro's most distinctive institutional expression was the ***'Arioi*** — a traveling brotherhood and sisterhood of performers, warriors, and devotees who traveled between islands staging dramatic performances (dance, theater, satire) dedicated to ʻOro. The 'Arioi were marked by elaborate tattoos indicating their rank within the society (eight grades). Their defining vow: **members were required to kill any children born to them** — parenthood was incompatible with total devotion to ʻOro. The 'Arioi thus practiced institutionalized infanticide as a condition of membership, representing perhaps the most extreme form of devotional community in Polynesian religion.

**Political sovereignty**: ʻOro's favor was a precondition of chiefly legitimacy across the Society Islands. The red feather gorget (*maro ura*) — ʻOro's sacred regalia — was the supreme symbol of sovereignty, more powerful than genealogy alone.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[huitzilopochtli]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the most structurally precise parallel in world religion — both are supreme war deities at the apex of a state cult demanding institutionalized human sacrifice to sustain cosmic/political order. Both cults peaked and were documented just before European colonial disruption. No transmission; convergent warrior-state theology.
- **[[mars-roman]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: war deity elevated to supreme political-theological status within an island/city-state system. Mars as protector of Rome / ʻOro as protector of the Society Island chiefdom.
- **[[ares]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: war deity who is propitiated precisely because of his terrifying power; both represent the martial function that all societies must manage theologically.
- **[[kali]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: devotional communities (the 'Arioi / Tantric Kali worshippers) who perform socially transgressive rituals (infanticide / consumption of forbidden substances) as the condition of divine devotion.

## MASSIVE WIN: the sacred warrior-performer brotherhood

The ***'Arioi*** represent a distinct cross-cultural type: the **devotional warrior-artist community**:
- The *'Arioi* (Society Islands): traveling performers devoted to ʻOro; infanticide vow; 8-grade hierarchy; elaborate tattooing
- The **Thuggee** (India, 13th–19th c.): devotees of Kali who committed ritual murder as worship; operated as a traveling brotherhood with initiation grades
- **Roman gladiatorial schools** (*familiae gladiatoriae*): communities dedicated to Mars/arena deities; strict hierarchy; their death in the arena was a sacred performance
- **Sufi warrior orders** (*futuwwa*): military brotherhoods with devotional codes, hierarchic initiation, traveling performance of devotion
- The pattern: the martial arts require a social container; that container becomes a devotional community with its own transgressive rules

## Iconography
ʻOro is rarely depicted anthropomorphically — his primary material manifestation was the **feathered gorget** (*maro ura*), a chest-and-shoulder ornament of red feathers, and **to'o** — wooden objects wrapped in sacred red feathers representing his presence. Human sacrifice victims were offered face-down in front of his marae. The marae platform itself — massive stone construction — was his visible body.
