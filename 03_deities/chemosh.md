# Chemosh

Chemosh (Moabite *Kmš*) is the national god of Moab, the Transjordanian kingdom east of the Dead Sea, and the closest extra-biblical analogue to the Israelite conception of a covenantal national deity. He is best known from the **Mesha Stele** (c. 840 BCE, KAI 181), the longest Moabite inscription, in which King Mesha of Dibon credits Chemosh with both Moab's earlier subjugation to Israel ("Chemosh was angry with his land") and its liberation ("Chemosh said to me, 'Go, take Nebo from Israel'"), and reports devoting the captured Israelite population to Chemosh as *ḥrm* (the ban) — the exact institution Israel applies to its enemies in Yahweh's name (cf. Deut 20; Josh 6). The structural symmetry between Mesha's Chemosh-theology and Israelite Yahwism is a central datum in the comparative study of West-Semitic national religion (Smith; Routledge).

## Appearance in the Hebrew Bible
Chemosh enters the biblical record as the paradigmatic *foreign* god — his home is Moab, but he appears in Israelite scripture as a tier-2 polemical figure (an appearance-wire, not a membership): Numbers 21:29 calls the Moabites "the people of Chemosh"; Solomon builds a high place for Chemosh "on the mountain east of Jerusalem" (1 Kgs 11:7), for which the Deuteronomistic Historian condemns him; Josiah defiles it (2 Kgs 23:13); and Jeremiah 48 is an extended oracle against Moab and "Chemosh… with his priests and officials." Possible human sacrifice to Chemosh is implied at 2 Kings 3:27, where the king of Moab offers his firstborn son on the wall and "great wrath came upon Israel." The compound theonym **Ashtar-Chemosh** (Mesha Stele line 17) links Chemosh to the West-Semitic Venus-star deity Athtar.
