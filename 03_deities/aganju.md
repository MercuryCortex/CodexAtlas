# Aganju

## Identity
Aganju is the Yoruba orisha of the **volcano**, the **wilderness**, and the vast uninhabited lands between human settlements. Where other orishas govern settled, cultivated, or navigable natural domains (Shango governs thunder, Yemoja rivers, Ogun forests/iron), Aganju governs the *trackless* — the desert, the volcanic field, the territory that cannot be domesticated or cultivated. He is the deity of transition through dangerous uninhabited zones.

## Role in Yoruba cosmology
Aganju's domain is the negative space between human habitations — the crossing-zones that must be traversed but cannot be settled. This makes him a deity of thresholds and transitions as much as a deity of natural forces. He is associated with great physical strength (the force required to traverse wilderness) and is sometimes said to be the father of [[shango]] (the thunder deity), creating a genealogical link between volcanic fire below and lightning-fire above.

In Cuban Lucumí Santería, he is syncretically identified with **San Cristóbal (Saint Christopher)** — the patron of travelers and safe crossings, which captures his wilderness-crossing function.

## Diaspora
In diaspora traditions, Aganju's volcanic/terrestrial fire aspect is sometimes emphasized in relation to Shango's aerial fire aspect — the two together encompass fire in its full range from volcanic earth to lightning sky.
