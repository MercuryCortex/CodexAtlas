# The Sefirot (Ten Divine Emanations)

## Identity
The ten *sefirot* are the divine emanations through which the *Ein Sof* (the Infinite) manifests itself in the structure of Kabbalistic theosophy. They are: **Keter** (Crown — the supernal will, almost identical with Ein Sof in many systems), **Chokhmah** (Wisdom — the primordial point of insight, masculine), **Binah** (Understanding — the womb of differentiated form, feminine), **Chesed** (Lovingkindness — the right-column expansive principle, masculine), **Gevurah** / Din (Severity / Judgment — the left-column constraining principle, feminine), **Tiferet** (Beauty — the central-column synthesis, masculine), **Netzach** (Eternity / Victory — right-column lower, masculine), **Hod** (Glory — left-column lower, feminine), **Yesod** (Foundation — the generative junction, masculine), and **Malkhut** / **Shekhinah** (Kingdom / Divine Presence — the receptive lower throne, feminine). The configuration is the Sefirotic Tree, with three columns and a vertical hierarchy of three triads plus Malkhut. The sefirot are *not* a polytheism — they are the inner articulation of the one Godhead — but their dynamic interactions, balances, and (especially) sacred-marriages (the union of Tiferet and Shekhinah) are the metaphysical drama that *Torah* and the human's performance of the *mitzvot* are designed to effect.

## Earliest attestation
The term *sefirot* originates in [[phase-5-012-sefer-yetzirah]] (~200–900 CE) but there refers to ten primordial *numbers* / dimensions, not divine emanations. The reinterpretation as divine emanations is the Kabbalistic move proper — first attested in [[phase-5-026-sefer-ha-bahir]] (~1180, Provence), systematized in [[phase-5-027-sefer-ha-zohar]] (~1280, Castile). Iconographic representation as the Sefirotic Tree (a diagrammatic configuration with three columns) crystallizes in 13th–14th c. Spanish Kabbalistic manuscripts.

## Mythological role
The sefirot are the operative apparatus of (1) creation — Ein Sof emanates through the sefirotic chain to produce the cosmos; (2) revelation — Torah's surface narrative encodes the sefirotic structure; (3) salvation — the human's performance of *mitzvot* effects *tikkun olam* (repair of the world) by harmonizing the sefirot, especially by uniting the upper masculine sefirot with the lower feminine Shekhinah; (4) eschatology — the final messianic redemption is the complete restoration of sefirotic balance.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[the-pleroma]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Scholem's contested thesis that the sefirotic structure shows structural parallels to Sethian Gnostic *pleroma* emanationism (especially the role of the lower feminine in tragic separation, parallel to Sophia). Cf. Scholem 1941, 1962; Idel 1988 (pushback).
- Plotinian / Procline hypostases — *parallel-motif*: emanative cosmogony from a transcendent One through structured emanations is structurally common; whether the Kabbalists drew on Arabic-mediated Neoplatonism is debated.

## Disputes
- **Origin of the emanative reading.** Did the *Bahir* and *Zohar*'s reinterpretation of *Sefer Yetzirah*'s sefirot draw on Gnostic / Neoplatonic substrate (Scholem), or develop from inner-rabbinic / Hekhalot materials (Idel)?
- **Gendering.** The strong gendering of the sefirot and the inner-divine sacred marriage is the most theologically bold feature of mature Kabbalah; modern feminist Jewish theology (Lynn Gottlieb, Tirzah Firestone) and feminist scholarship (Wolfson) engage critically.
