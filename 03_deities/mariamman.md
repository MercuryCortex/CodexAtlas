# Mariamman

Mariamman (*Māriyammaṉ*, "Mother Māri") is the great **village goddess (grāma-devatā)** of South India — above all of the Tamil country — and one of the most widely worshipped deities of popular Hinduism. She presides over **rain** (on which the village's life depends) and over **epidemic disease**: smallpox, chickenpox, measles, and fevers are understood as her presence, so that she is at once the **sender and the curer** of these afflictions, an ambivalent power to be honoured and cooled rather than simply feared. Her worship turns on the management of "heat" — the heat of disease, of drought, of divine anger — through cooling agents: **neem** leaves, turmeric, water-pots (*karagam*), and dramatic vows of **firewalking** and ordeal. Theologically she is understood as a local manifestation of the supreme **Goddess** (Devi/Shakti, identified with **[[parvati|Parvati]]**, Durga, or Kali). She is the southern counterpart of the North Indian smallpox-goddess **[[shitala|Shitala]]**, and remains a vivid, living focus of devotion across Tamil Nadu and the Tamil diaspora.
