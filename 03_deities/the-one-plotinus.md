# The One (Plotinus)

Plotinus' First Hypostasis — utterly simple, beyond being and beyond thought, the source of all that is. The One is not "a being" but the ground from which all beings derive by emanation. Plotinus describes it apophatically: it cannot properly be named, cannot be thought, cannot be experienced as an object; the soul can attain *henōsis* (union) with the One only by ceasing to think *of* it. Porphyry reports Plotinus achieved this union four times in his life (Life of Plotinus 23).

Plotinus' One inherits the first hypothesis of Plato's Parmenides and the Idea of the Good of Republic VI. It anticipates and shapes the apophatic theology of subsequent Neoplatonism (Iamblichus adds an even-higher Ineffable above the One; Proclus systematizes; Damascius radicalizes), of Christian mystical theology (Pseudo-Dionysius, Eriugena, Eckhart), and of late-antique Hermetism and Gnosticism (cf. parallels with the Sethian Monad).
