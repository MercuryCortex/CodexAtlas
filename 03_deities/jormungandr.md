# Jörmungandr

## Identity
Jörmungandr (Old Norse *Jǫrmungandr*, "great staff / great wand"; also called *Miðgarðsormr*, "the Midgard-Serpent") is the **world-encircling serpent** of Norse cosmology. He is one of the three monstrous children of [[loki]] with the jötunn Angrboða (the other two being the wolf [[fenrir]] and the death-goddess [[hel]]). Cast by Odin into the great sea surrounding Midgard, Jörmungandr grew so vast that he came to encircle the entire world, biting his own tail to close the encircling loop. He is fated to fight and kill [[thor]] at Ragnarök, dying from Thor's hammer-blows but inflicting fatal venom on the storm-god in turn. (Lindow 2001; Larrington 2014.)

His tail-biting world-encircling form is iconographically near-identical to the Egyptian-Hellenistic [[ouroboros]] — but the Norse attestation is **independent**. The Mediterranean-alchemical ouroboros iconographic line (Egypt → Hellenistic Alexandria → Greek alchemy → medieval Arabic alchemy) did not reach Iron Age Scandinavia. The Norse tail-biting world-serpent emerges from the Germanic-Indo-European chaoskampf inheritance — specifically from the inherited PIE *HERO SLAY SERPENT* poetic formula (Watkins 1995) that also generates Indra-Vritra in the Vedic tradition. Lindow 2001 makes the parallel-form-not-transmission point explicit.

## Earliest attestation
- **Viking-age stone-art (c. 950-1050 CE)** — the Hunnestad monument (Skåne, Sweden) and the Altuna runestone (Sweden) depict Thor's fishing-for-the-Midgard-Serpent expedition; the Gosforth Cross (Cumbria, c. 920-950 CE) appears to depict Ragnarök scenes including Jörmungandr.
- ***Hymiskviða*** (Poetic Edda, c. 13th c. manuscript of older oral tradition) — the eddic poem narrating Thor's fishing expedition with the giant Hymir, hooking and almost landing the Midgard-Serpent. ([[phase-8-014-poetic-edda]])
- ***Völuspá*** (Poetic Edda) — the cosmic prophecy describing Ragnarök, including Jörmungandr's role.
- ***Gylfaginning*** in Snorri's *Prose Edda* (c. 1220) — the systematizing prose treatment of the Loki-Angrboða children and Jörmungandr's role at Ragnarök. ([[phase-8-015-prose-edda-snorri]])

## Mythological role
Jörmungandr operates across three episodes:

1. **Birth and casting-out.** Loki and Angrboða produce three children: Jörmungandr, Fenrir, and Hel. Odin casts Jörmungandr into the world-encircling ocean, where he grows to encircle Midgard biting his own tail.
2. **Thor's fishing expedition.** In ***Hymiskviða*** (and recounted by Snorri), Thor accompanies the giant Hymir on a fishing expedition; Thor uses an ox-head as bait and hooks Jörmungandr; the two glare at each other across the boat's gunwale; Hymir, terrified, cuts the line and Jörmungandr sinks back. This is among the most celebrated and iconographically attested episodes in Norse mythology (Viking-age stone-art consistently depicts it).
3. **Ragnarök.** At Ragnarök Jörmungandr emerges from the ocean and battles Thor. Thor kills him with the hammer Mjölnir; but Jörmungandr's venom kills Thor, who walks nine steps and falls. (*Völuspá*; *Gylfaginning*.)

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ouroboros]]** — *parallel-form*: iconographically near-identical (tail-biting world-encircling serpent), but **NO documented transmission** from Mediterranean-alchemical ouroboros to Iron Age Scandinavia. The Norse attestation emerges independently from the Germanic-Indo-European chaoskampf inheritance. Lindow 2001 explicit on this point.
- **[[vritra]]** — *parallel-form*: Indo-European cognate via the inherited PIE *HERO SLAY SERPENT* poetic-formula (Watkins 1995). Thor-Jörmungandr instantiates the same inherited formula as Indra-Vritra.
- **[[leviathan]]** — *parallel-form*: chaos-serpent cosmic-enemy parallel; not direct transmission.
- **[[lotan]]**, **[[tiamat]]**, **[[apophis]]** — *parallel-form*: chaoskampf-cluster cognates.

## Iconography
- Tail-biting world-encircling serpent — canonical (matches [[ouroboros]] form).
- Vast scale — large enough to encircle Midgard / the world-ocean.
- Venomous (Thor dies from the venom at Ragnarök).
- Visual attestation in Viking-age stone-art: Hunnestad, Altuna, Gosforth Cross.

## Disputes
- **Strength of the parallel to ouroboros.** Mainstream scholarship (Lindow 2001, Larrington 2014) treats Jörmungandr's tail-biting form as Germanic-independent — emerging from the Indo-European chaoskampf inheritance rather than from Mediterranean-alchemical ouroboros transmission. Some popular comparative-religion literature attempts an Egyptian-to-Norse transmission, but this is not supported at the documentary level (no Mediterranean alchemical text reaches Iron Age Scandinavia).
- **Ragnarök outcome variants.** The Eddic sources are consistent on the Thor-Jörmungandr mutual-killing at Ragnarök; minor textual variation in walking-nine-steps detail.
- **Etymological reading of *Jǫrmungandr*.** "Great wand / great staff" is the consensus reading; some alternative readings ("vast / immense one") are minority positions.
