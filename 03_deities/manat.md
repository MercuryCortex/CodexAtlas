# Manāt

## Identity
Manāt (Arabic *Manāt*, مناة; Nabataean / Aramaic *Manawat*) is the **third of the three "daughters of Allāh"** named at **Quran 53:19-22** alongside [[allat|al-Lāt]] and [[al-uzza|al-ʿUzzā]]. She is the Arabian **goddess of fate, allotted-time, and death** — her name derives from the Semitic root *m-n-y / m-n-w* "to apportion, allot, count" (cf. Hebrew *manāh* "portion, fate"; the Quranic *al-maniyya* "death, fate" preserves the cognate semantics in standard Arabic usage). Her principal Hijazi sanctuary stood at **Qudayd** on the Red Sea coast between Mecca and Medina, with an aniconic cult-stone destroyed by **Saʿd ibn Zayd al-Ashhalī in 8 AH (~630 CE)** on Muhammad's order. She was the **principal goddess of the Aws and Khazraj tribes of Yathrib / Medina** per Islamic-tradition memory — the Medinan tribes that would form the *anṣār* (helpers) of Muhammad after the Hegira. In the **Nabataean** sphere she is *Manawat*, attested at Hegra (Madāʾin Sāliḥ) in funerary curse-formulas as protector-of-tomb / avenger-of-tomb-violation — preserving her fate-and-death theological profile. (Hoyland 2001; Hawting 1999; Healey 2001.)

## Earliest attestation
- **Nabataean inscriptions** (c. -100 onward) — Manawat is attested in Nabataean funerary contexts at Hegra (Madāʾin Sāliḥ), invoked in curse-formulas against tomb-violators. Healey 2001.
- **Safaitic / Hismaic graffiti** — North Arabian rock-graffito attestation.
- **Quran 53:19-22** (~610-632 CE) — the principal Islamic-period textual attestation, naming Manāt alongside al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā. ([[phase-4-034-quran]])
- **Ibn Ishaq *Sirat Rasul Allah* (~750 CE)** — narrates the Qudayd-sanctuary destruction. ([[phase-5-043-ibn-ishaq-sirat-rasul-allah]] — this batch)
- **Ibn al-Kalbi *Kitāb al-Aṣnām* (~9th c. CE)** — Islamic-tradition reconstructive description of the Qudayd sanctuary, its priestly caretakers (the al-Ghaṭārīf of the Azd), and the Aws / Khazraj of Yathrib as her principal devotees. Use source-critically per Hawting 1999.

## Mythological role
1. **Third of the three "daughters of Allāh."** With al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā, Manāt is invoked at Quran 53:19-22 as one of the goddesses associated with Allāh in pre-Islamic Meccan religion — rejected in favor of Allāh's unique sovereignty.
2. **Goddess of fate and death.** The *m-n-y / m-n-w* "apportion / count / allot" root associates Manāt with the apportioned span of life — the moment of death, the allotted portion of fortune, the limit of human striving. The Quranic *al-maniyya* "death, fate" preserves the semantic field. Hoyland 2001 reads Manāt as Northwest-Semitic Meni / *Mny* (Isaiah 65:11, "those who set tables for *Meni* / Fortune"), the Northwest-Semitic Fortune-deity.
3. **Principal goddess of the Aws and Khazraj of Yathrib.** Per Islamic-tradition memory, the pre-Hegira Medinan tribes were Manāt's principal devotees; the *talbiya* (pilgrimage-cry) "labbayka Allāhumma, labbayka, labbayka lā sharīka laka illā sharīkan huwa laka, tamlikuhu wa-mā malak" preserves a partial pre-Islamic Hijazi *talbiya*-formula invoking the goddess-companion alongside the high god — reframed in Islamic *talbiya* practice to the radical-tawhid *lā sharīka laka* "you have no partner."
4. **The Qudayd sanctuary.** A coastal sanctuary between Mecca and Medina with an aniconic standing-stone cult-image and a priestly caretaker-clan; destroyed by Saʿd ibn Zayd al-Ashhalī at the conquest of Mecca period.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[allat|Allāt]] and [[al-uzza|al-ʿUzzā]]** — close cultic associates; the triadic "daughters of Allāh."
- **Northwest-Semitic Meni / *Mny*** — etymological and functional cognate with the Isaiah 65:11 "Fortune"-deity (Hoyland 2001) — the Northwest-Semitic fate / fortune / allotment deity.
- **Nemesis (Greek)** — partial parallel via the fate-and-retribution semantic field; not an ancient identification.
- **Tyche (Greek) / Fortuna (Roman)** — partial parallel via the apportionment / fortune semantic field; not an ancient identification.

## Iconography
- **Aniconic *baetyl* (sacred standing-stone)** at the Qudayd sanctuary — the canonical pre-Islamic Arabian form.
- **The Nabataean Manawat at Hegra** appears in funerary inscription contexts; no anthropomorphic Manawat-image is firmly attested.
- **Funerary / tomb-curse association** in the Nabataean material.

## Disputes
- **The "Satanic Verses" / *gharānīq* incident** — Quran 53:19-22 named the three goddesses; the *gharānīq* interpolation briefly accepted then retracted per Ibn Ishaq / Tabari. Manāt's status in the rescinded verses is identical to al-Lāt and al-ʿUzzā. Ahmed 2017 *Before Orthodoxy*.
- **Manāt = Meni identification.** Hoyland 2001 makes the Northwest-Semitic Meni / *Mny* etymological connection; this is the cleaner Northwest-Semitic cognate, more direct than the Aphrodite-style Hellenistic identification proposed for al-ʿUzzā.
- **Source-critical reliability of the Qudayd-narrative.** Per Hawting 1999, the detailed Islamic-tradition narrative is later reconstructive material; the historical core (Manāt as principal Aws / Khazraj goddess; Qudayd sanctuary; destruction at the conquest of Mecca) is secure.
