# Eros (Primordial)

## Identity
The **primordial Eros** of Greek cosmogony is the **cosmic generative force** that comes into being at the very origin of the cosmos in **Hesiod's *Theogony* (line 120)** alongside [[chaos-primordial]], [[gaia]], and Tartarus, and that the Orphic theogonic tradition identifies with [[phanes-protogonos]] — the first-born from the cosmic egg. **This Eros must be distinguished from the more familiar Aphroditean Eros** (son of Aphrodite, Greek personification of erotic-love, Roman Cupid, mischievous winged boy with bow and arrows). The primordial Eros is **cosmic**, not erotic in the personal sense: he is the **principle of attraction and combination** that draws elements together to produce the cosmos. Where the Aphroditean Eros is a generative offspring of established gods, the primordial Eros is a *primordial cosmic principle* coeval with the first emergence of being.

## Earliest attestation
- **[[hesiod]]** *Theogony* 116–122 (c. -700): "First of all Chaos came into being, then broad-breasted Gaia... and dim Tartarus in the depths of the wide-pathed earth, and **Eros, the most beautiful among the immortal gods, who loosens the limbs, and overpowers the mind and prudent counsel in the breasts of all gods and all men**." — the foundational text.
- **Pre-Socratic reception**: [[parmenides]] DK B13 ("First of all the gods she devised Eros") and [[empedocles]]'s **Love (Philia/Aphrodite) as cosmic combining force** are pre-Socratic recastings of the primordial-Eros principle into cosmological systems.
- **Aristophanes** *Birds* 693–702 (-414) — parody of Orphic theogony: "First of all there was Chaos, Night, dark Erebus, and broad Tartarus... Black-winged Night first laid a wind-egg, from which... **golden-winged Eros** appeared" — this is Eros-Phanes hatching from the Orphic cosmic egg.
- **[[plato]]** *Symposium* 178b (Phaedrus's speech): "Eros is the most ancient of the gods, as Hesiod and Acusilaus testify" — Plato has Phaedrus argue from primordial-Eros tradition.
- **Orphic Hymn 58 (*to Eros*)** in [[phase-3-028-orphic-hymns]] — addresses Eros as cosmic generative principle.

## Mythological role
The primordial Eros has **no narrative biography** — he is purely a cosmogonic principle. His function:
1. **Cosmic attraction**: he draws the elements / primordial beings together so that the cosmos can come into being. Without Eros, Chaos and Gaia and Tartarus exist as separate; with Eros, they begin to combine and produce further generations.
2. **Generative impulse in all subsequent beings**: Hesiod's "loosens the limbs, overpowers mind and prudent counsel in the breasts of all gods and all men" connects the cosmic-generative function with the personal-generative experience.

In the Orphic *Rhapsodic Theogony* this cosmic-Eros is **identified with [[phanes-protogonos]]**: the first-born from the cosmic egg, the hermaphroditic shining figure, is the primordial Eros under another name. Zeus's swallowing of Phanes is therefore also a swallowing of Eros — the cosmos restarts with primordial Eros internalized in Zeus.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[phanes-protogonos]]** — *ancient-identification*: Orphic identification — same primordial being.
- **Empedoclean Love (Philia)** — *scholarly-parallel*: [[empedocles]]'s cosmic force of **Love** drawing elements together is a pre-Socratic philosophical re-expression of the Hesiodic primordial-Eros function. [[empedocles]] sometimes calls this force *Aphrodite* but its cosmic-combining structure is primordial-Eros structure.
- **Christian *agape* / cosmic-love theologies** — *scholarly-parallel*: Pseudo-Dionysius's *Divine Names* chapter 4 (cosmic-love as divine principle) and later medieval Christian-mystic engagements with cosmic-love-as-divine-principle (Bernard of Clairvaux's *bridal mysticism*; later [[meister-eckhart]]'s engagements) have structural resonance with primordial-Eros tradition mediated through Plotinus's *Enneads* 6.7–9.
- **Renaissance Hermetic-Platonist cosmic-Eros** — *appropriated-by*: [[marsilio-ficino]]'s *De Amore* commentary on Plato's *Symposium* makes the primordial-cosmic-Eros foundational to Florentine Hermetic-Platonist philosophy; [[giovanni-pico-della-mirandola]] continues this development.

## Iconography
- **No standard iconography for primordial Eros distinct from Aphroditean Eros.** Greek art conflates the two visually — both are winged youths, sometimes with bow-and-arrow (Aphroditean) or torch (more generic).
- In Orphic-Aristophanic cosmic-egg iconography (rare): a winged figure emerging from a split egg-shell, often with multiple-animal-head composite features (the Phanes-Eros type).

## Disputes
- **Primordial vs. Aphroditean Eros distinction.** Whether Hesiodic-primordial and Aphroditean-genealogical Eros are two distinct deities (early Greek view) or two aspects of one (later Greek-philosophical syncretism, especially after Plato's *Symposium*) is partly a question of historical period — distinct in Hesiod and the Orphics, increasingly merged in Hellenistic-Roman philosophical literature.
- **Hesiod's primordial-Eros and the Theogony's narrative logic.** Hesiod's primordial Eros performs his cosmogonic function but is then never mentioned again in the *Theogony*'s genealogical sequence (Aphrodite's-son Eros is a later distinct figure). Some scholars treat this as Hesiodic inconsistency; others (West 1966) treat it as deliberate — primordial Eros is the principle that makes genealogy possible, not a participant in it.
