# Naunet

## Identity

Naunet ("the primordial waters" — feminine) is one of the eight primordial deities of the Hermopolitan Ogdoad (*Khmunu*, "city of the eight"), the cosmogonic system of Hermopolis Magna that describes the world before creation as four male–female pairs, each personifying a quality of the pre-cosmic abyss: Nun and Naunet (the primordial waters), [[heh-egyptian]] and [[hauhet]] (infinity / boundlessness), [[kek-egyptian]] and [[kauket]] (darkness), and [[amun]] and [[amaunet]] (hiddenness). Naunet is the female half of the first and most basic pair — the inert, undifferentiated ocean out of which the first mound and the first sunrise emerge. Where her partner [[nun]] is the most prominent and frequently named of the Ogdoad (Nun outlived the system as a standalone cosmological principle), Naunet is his complementary feminine mode, completing the pair-structure on which the whole Ogdoad depends.

## Mythological role

The Ogdoad do not create by act but by presence: their eight forces constitute the conditions that make the first creative moment possible. Naunet supplies the feminine articulation of the primordial waters — the womb-like, generative aspect of the abyss, as against Nun's role as the watery expanse itself. In later cosmological geography Naunet is sometimes identified with the "counter-heaven" or nocturnal waters below the world, through which the sun travels at night to be reborn at dawn — making her the watery space of solar regeneration as well as of first creation.

## Cross-tradition identifications

- **[[nun]]** — *ancient-identification*: Naunet and Nun are the masculine/feminine pair of the primordial waters; the Pyramid and Coffin Texts treat them together as the watery substrate of creation.
- **the "deep" (tehom) of Genesis 1:2** — *scholarly-parallel*: the unformed watery abyss over which the spirit of God moves in the Hebrew creation account occupies the same cosmogonic position as Nun/Naunet; the Genesis text demythologizes the waters but preserves the structural role (Allen 1988).
- **[[tiamat]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Mesopotamian primordial salt sea is the feminine personified abyss of the *Enuma Elish*; Naunet is the Egyptian counterpart in the shared Bronze-Age Near Eastern primordial-waters vocabulary.

## Iconography

In Hermopolitan depictions Naunet, like the other three female members of the Ogdoad, is shown serpent-headed (the males are frog-headed), reflecting the convention that distinguished the female from the male principles. She otherwise carries the water-wave hieroglyph and appears in the company of the full eight.

## Disputes

Because Nun so dominated later theology as an independent principle, Naunet is comparatively thinly attested in her own right, and some scholars treat her as little more than the grammatically required feminine of Nun rather than an independently developed goddess. Her identification with the nocturnal counter-heaven is secondary and not present in the earliest Ogdoad material.
