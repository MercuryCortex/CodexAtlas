# Pangu

## Identity
The primordial cosmic giant whose body becomes the universe in Chinese creation mythology. Born from a **cosmic egg** containing primal chaos; awakens, breaks the egg, and pushes the heavy (yin) downward to form Earth and the light (yang) upward to form Heaven, growing taller each day for 18,000 years to keep them apart. On his death, **his body becomes the world**: breath → wind and clouds; voice → thunder; left eye → sun, right eye → moon; blood → rivers; bones → mountains; flesh → soil; hair → vegetation; sweat → rain; **fleas → human beings** (the slightly comic late-imperial elaboration).

## Earliest attestation
First in **Xu Zheng's *Sanwu Liji*** (3rd c. CE) — striking late date compared to most foundational mythologies. Modern scholarship (Bodde 1961) attributes this to Pangu being borrowed from southern non-Han (likely Miao-Yao) substrate cosmogony, only entering the literary Chinese mainstream when southern dynasties dominated. The pre-Pangu Chinese cosmogony of the classical Confucian texts is comparatively impersonal (*tai ji* → yin-yang → 10,000 things).
