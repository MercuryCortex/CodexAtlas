# Mictlantecuhtli

## Identity
Lord of Mictlan — the Aztec underworld, the destination of those who died ordinary deaths (not by drowning, lightning, childbirth, or in war, which have alternative paradises). Mictlantecuhtli is the skeletal lord of the dead; his consort is Mictecacihuatl, "Queen of Mictlan." Mictlan is reached only after a four-year journey through nine layers of underworld, navigating obstacles (a river crossed only with the help of a faithfully-buried dog; mountains that crash together; an obsidian wind that flays the dead). At journey's end, the dead present themselves to Mictlantecuhtli and dissolve into him.

## Earliest attestation
Aztec religion; iconographic continuity with earlier central-Mexican death-god imagery (the so-called "Death God A" of the Maya Dresden Codex is a related but distinct figure).

## Mythological role
Quetzalcoatl descends to Mictlan to retrieve the bones of past humanities. Mictlantecuhtli sets him a series of tests: blow a conch with no holes (Quetzalcoatl bores them with worms); circumambulate Mictlan four times; only then does Mictlantecuhtli reluctantly relinquish the bones. As Quetzalcoatl flees back to the upper world, Mictlantecuhtli's servants startle him; he drops the bones, which break into the different sizes of present humans, accounting for human variation. He carries the bones back to Tamoanchan, where Quilaztli grinds them and Quetzalcoatl bleeds onto them — creating present humanity.

## Iconography
Skull face; exposed liver/intestines (often dangling from the body); skeleton body with paper bow-knots; black-and-white coloration; owl, spider, bat associates. Highly recognizable in Mesoamerican codex art (especially Codex Borgia).
