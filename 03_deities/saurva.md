# Saurva

## Identity

**Saurva** (Avestan *Sauruua*, also *Sāuru*; Pahlavi *Sāwul* / *Sāwar*) is one of the **arch-daevas** (the *kamāretika* daevas, the "head-daevas") of Zoroastrianism — the demonized divine beings whom Mazdean ritual solemnly renounces. He is the **daeva of misrule and tyranny**: in the developed cosmological system he is the demonic counter-principle to the Amesha Spenta **[[khshathra-vairya|Khshathra Vairya]]** ("Desirable Dominion," rightly-exercised sovereignty), and so personifies bad government, anarchy, drunken disorder, and the perversion of legitimate power. Like the other daevas, Saurva has **no benevolent cult and no positive iconography**; he is named in the texts only to be rejected. In the Atlas he is placed among the named figures of the Indo-Iranian **[[asura-deva-inversion|daeva/asura inversion]]**, alongside the demonized Indra and **[[nanghaithya-daeva|Nāŋhaiθya]]**, because he is one of the specific divine beings that the Zoroastrian reform reassigned from the inherited pantheon to the demonic category.

## In the Avesta and the Pahlavi tradition

Saurva's securest attestations are in the **Videvdad** (Vendidad, "the law against the daevas"), the Younger-Avestan ritual text whose daeva-lists name specific demons. He appears in the demon-catalogues of **Videvdad 10.9** and **19.43** — the same passages that name Indra and Nāŋhaiθya. These lists are the heart of the evidence for the **point-by-point** character of the Zoroastrian reform: it is not abstract daeva-worship in general that is condemned but named beings, catalogued and renounced.

In the systematized **Pahlavi** cosmology of the 9th–10th centuries CE (the Greater Bundahishn and related works), the arch-daevas are arranged as a **mirror-heptad** opposing the Amesha Spentas — each "Bountiful Immortal" of the good creation is shadowed by a head-daeva of the evil creation. Saurva (Pahlavi *Sāwul*) stands as the opponent of **Khshathra Vairya**, whose domain is *desirable dominion* — rightly-ordered sovereignty and good government. Saurva is therefore the daeva of the **inverse**: tyranny, misrule, anarchy, and the disorder of drunkenness and violence. The pairing supplies his characteristic profile and is what fixes him, in the developed tradition, as the demon of bad rule.

## Cross-tradition significance

Saurva belongs to the small set of **named** divine beings — Indra, Saurva (Sauruua), and Nāŋhaiθya (Naonhaithya) of Videvdad 10.9 — through which the **[[asura-deva-inversion|asura/deva inversion]]** is documented at the level of the individual figure rather than the word-class. The general inversion is linguistic: Proto-Indo-Iranian *\*daiwá-* yields Sanskrit *deva* (good gods) but Avestan *daēva* (demons), while *\*asura-* yields Vedic *asura* (demons) but Avestan *ahura* (lords). What makes the Zoroastrian reform unmistakably **deliberate** rather than the product of slow semantic drift is that the Videvdad does not merely condemn "the daevas" in the abstract; it names figures that were central to the sibling Vedic pantheon and demonizes them one by one (West 2007; Boyce 1975; Skjærvø 1993). Saurva is one of those names.

The relationship of Saurva to Vedic religion is, however, of a **different and more disputed kind** than the cleaner cases. For Nāŋhaiθya the cognate is secure: Avestan *Nāŋhaiθya* and Vedic *Nāsatya* (the Aśvins) descend transparently from Proto-Indo-Iranian *\*nāsatya-*. For Saurva the proposed counterpart is the Vedic **Śarva**, a name used as an epithet of the dread archer-god **Rudra** (the prototype of Śiva), associated with disease, death, and the bow. A number of scholars (see the older comparative tradition reflected in Christensen 1941 and the etymological discussion in Mayrhofer's *EWAia* under *Śarva*) connect Avestan *Sauruua* to Vedic *Śarva* and read Saurva as the demonized counterpart of the Rudra/Śarva figure — which would make Saurva a fourth named case of the inversion. But the equation is **less certain** than the Indra and Nāŋhaiθya cases (it is flagged as such even in the Atlas's own [[theme-vedic-avestan-split|Vedic–Avestan split]] daeva-table, where the "less certain" cognates are marked), and it should be held as a **structural/typological** parallel rather than a securely demonstrated linguistic cognate.

## Disputes

- **The Saurva ↔ Śarva (Rudra) equation.** Whether Avestan *Sauruua* is the genuine etymological cognate of Vedic *Śarva* (an epithet of Rudra) — and therefore a demonized-Rudra parallel to the demonized-Nāsatya of Nāŋhaiθya — or whether the resemblance is partial and the identification over-reaches, is **not settled**. The connection is attractive because it would complete the pattern of named Vedic gods demonized in the Videvdad, but the phonological fit is debated and the Avestan figure's profile (misrule/tyranny, fixed by the opposition to Khshathra Vairya) does not obviously match Rudra's (archery, disease, death). The Atlas treats it as a flagged, not-secure parallel. **HOMONYM CAUTION:** the Vedic Śarva-as-Rudra/Śiva is a separate figure with its own Vedic and Hindu life; Saurva the Zoroastrian arch-daeva must not be conflated with it.
- **The meaning and domain of the name.** The standard interpretation makes Saurva the daeva of **misrule / bad government**, derived chiefly from his Pahlavi pairing against Khshathra Vairya (good government). How much of this domain is already implicit in the Younger-Avestan stratum, versus systematized only in the later Pahlavi mirror-heptad, is a question of textual layering; the explicit one-to-one opposition belongs most clearly to the developed Pahlavi cosmology (Greater Bundahishn 27; Christensen 1941).
- **Stratum and personification.** As with the other arch-daevas, the degree to which the Gathic stratum already conceives Saurva as an individuated being versus the Younger-Avestan and Pahlavi strata personifying and systematizing him is debated; the named-daeva catalogues are Younger-Avestan.
