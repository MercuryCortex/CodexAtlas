# Yeomra

## Identity
Yeomra (Yeomra-daewang, "Great King Yeomra") is the **king and judge of the underworld** in Korean belief — the ruler of *Jeoseung*, the realm of the dead, who weighs each soul's deeds against the register of life and assigns its fate.

## Earliest attestation
The figure enters Korea with Buddhism (from the early centuries CE), as the Korean form of the Buddhist **King Yama** (Sanskrit *Yama* → Chinese *Yanluo* → Korean *Yeomra* → Japanese *Enma*); he is also woven into shamanic *muga* (ritual songs) of the journey of the dead.

## Mythological role — the judge of souls
Yeomra presides over the courts through which the dead must pass, reading the ledger that records every deed; the just are sent on to rebirth or paradise, the wicked to punishment. In Korean shamanic narrative he dispatches his messengers (the *Jeoseung Saja*) to summon the dying, and the soul is guided before his throne — the Korean form of the universal reckoning at death.

## Cross-tradition
Yeomra is a node in one of the clearest documented transmissions in religion: the Buddhist **Yama** carried across Asia — [[yama-vedic]] (India) → Yanluo (China) → **Yeomra** (Korea) → Enma (Japan). Structurally he is the **judge of the dead**, kin to the Egyptian Anubis-Osiris weighing and the Greek Minos.

## Iconography
A crowned, robed king enthroned in judgment, with the register of the dead and the ten courts of the underworld.
