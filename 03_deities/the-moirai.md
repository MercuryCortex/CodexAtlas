# The Moirai (the Fates)

## Identity
Three goddesses who allot each mortal's destiny:
- **Klotho** ("Spinner") spins the thread of life.
- **Lachesis** ("Allotter") measures it.
- **Atropos** ("Inflexible") cuts it.

In Hesiod *Theogony* they appear twice with different parentage — 217–219 names them as daughters of Nyx (Night) alone (the older, darker, pre-Olympian generation); 904–906 has them as daughters of [[themis]] and [[zeus]] (the Olympian-integrated form, where fate is daughter of divine law).

## Mythological role
**The Spindle of Necessity**: Plato's *Republic* X.617c–620e — the *Myth of Er* — describes the Moirai sitting around the cosmic spindle of Necessity (Ananke), singing past, present, and future. **Fate as senior to Zeus**: in *Iliad* 16.431–461 even Zeus must yield to fate, unable to save his beloved Sarpedon — the moral that the gods themselves are subordinate to the Moirai is the philosophically explosive Homeric premise that drives Greek tragedy.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Norns** (Norse: Urðr/Verðandi/Skuld) — *scholarly-parallel*: Indo-European fate-triad (West 2007).
- **[[mokosh]]** — *parallel-motif*: Slavic spinner-fate.
- **Roman Parcae** (Nona, Decima, Morta) — *appropriated-by*: direct Roman absorption.
- **[[themis]]** — divine mother in Hesiod's Olympian-integrated recension.
