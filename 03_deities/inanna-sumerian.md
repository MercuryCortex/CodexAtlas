# Inanna

The Sumerian goddess of love and war, sovereign power (*nam-en*) and divine "powers" (*me*), and of the planet Venus as both morning and evening star. Her cult center is the Eanna ("House of Heaven") temple at Uruk; she is also worshipped at Zabalam, Kish, and the Akkadian royal city Akkad. She is the most fully developed female divine personality in Mesopotamian religion: she defies, persuades, seduces, fights, descends to the underworld and returns. Inanna's character holds together opposites (love and war, fertility and combat, gentleness and rage) in a way that no other Sumerian deity does. The earliest named author in human history, [[enheduanna]], composed hymns to Inanna ([[phase-1-003-enheduanna-hymns]]) that cemented her cosmic-supreme status.

## Mythological role
Central in: the sacred-marriage cycle with [[dumuzi-tammuz]] (basis of seasonal vegetation mythology); **Inanna's Descent to the Underworld** — she descends to her sister Ereshkigal's realm, is stripped of her seven *me* at seven gates, is killed and hung as a corpse on a hook, is revived by emissaries of Enki, and returns substituting Dumuzi as her replacement (the foundational ANE [[dying-rising-god]] narrative pattern with its substitution-of-the-lover variant); **Inanna and Enki** — Inanna seduces Enki, gets him drunk, and carries off the *me* from Eridu to Uruk (an etiology of Uruk's cultic supremacy). Across the Akkadian period and after, Inanna is increasingly identified with Akkadian [[ishtar-akkadian]] — same goddess in two language-cultures, the syncretism essentially complete by the Old Babylonian period.
