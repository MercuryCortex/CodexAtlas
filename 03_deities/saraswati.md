# Saraswati

## Identity
Sarasvatī is the Hindu goddess of speech (*Vāc*), learning, music, poetry, and the arts. She is one of the very few Vedic goddesses to retain (and even gain) prominence in classical and modern Hinduism. Originally associated with the Sarasvatī river (a mighty river in Vedic geography, identified with the now-dry Ghaggar-Hakra channel; the river itself was deified). She becomes the goddess of speech (*Vāc Devī*) and of learning; iconographically: seated on a lotus or peacock, four-armed, holding a *vīṇā* (lute), prayer beads, and a manuscript / book; sometimes depicted with a swan. Consort of Brahmā in Purāṇic theology. In Tantric Śaktism she becomes one of three principal goddesses (Sarasvatī of speech-power, Lakṣmī of fortune-power, Kālī / Durgā of warrior-power). The festival *Sarasvatī Pūjā* / *Vasanta Pañcamī* is centered on students and the arts. Through Buddhist transmission she became *Benzaiten* in Japanese tradition.

## Earliest attestation
Multiple Rig Vedic hymns (especially RV 6.61, 7.95–96, 10.17, 10.30, 10.64, 10.75, 10.131) — ~-1500 to -1000 BCE — first as river-goddess, then as goddess of inspired speech.
