# Hanuman

## Identity
Hanumān is the monkey-deity of Hinduism, the supreme devotee of Rāma, son of the wind-god Vāyu, and the master of devotion-as-service (bhakti). The principal Rāmāyaṇa narrative gives him the central role of the Sundara Kāṇḍa: he leaps from India to Lanka, finds the captive Sītā, identifies himself by Rāma's signet ring, sets Lanka ablaze with his burning tail, and returns. Iconographically: red-skinned simian form; carrying a club (gadā); often shown kneeling before Rāma or carrying a mountain (the medicinal Sañjīvanī mountain, brought to revive Lakṣmaṇa in the Yuddha Kāṇḍa); some images show his chest torn open displaying Rāma and Sītā within his heart. The Hanumān Cālīsā (~16th-c. devotional text, attributed to Tulsīdās) is the most-recited Hindu devotional poem after Sanskrit Veda excerpts. In Mādhva theology, Hanumān is the first incarnation of Vāyu, with Bhīma as second and Madhva himself as third.

## Earliest attestation
Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* (~-400 to +200, with the Sundara Kāṇḍa as the central Hanumān text). Iconographically: from ~5th c. CE onward.
