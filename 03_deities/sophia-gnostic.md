# Sophia (Gnostic)

## Identity

The fallen aeon of the Pleroma — divine Wisdom personified — whose attempted unilateral emanation (without her consort) produces the misshapen [[yaldabaoth]] and so triggers the existence of the material cosmos. The myth varies substantially across Gnostic schools: in the Sethian *Apocryphon of John* her error is purely cognitive (she "wished to bring forth a likeness from herself without the consent of the spirit"); in Valentinian systems her fall is a more complex emotional disruption (fear, grief, confusion, longing). In all versions, Sophia's redemption — and the recovery of the divine spark caught in matter — is the central drama of Gnostic soteriology.

The figure inherits [[hokmah-hebrew]] (Proverbs 8, Sirach 24, Wisdom of Solomon) and Hellenistic Sophia but transforms personified Wisdom from God's playmate at creation into the tragic agent of cosmic disaster and simultaneously the seed of cosmic redemption. Her tears become the primordial waters; her longing for the divine realm animates the spiritual drive of all humans who carry the divine spark (*pneuma*).

---

## The Apocryphon of John Narrative (Sethian version)

The fullest early Sethian account ([[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]], 2nd c. CE) unfolds in four movements:

### 1. The Pleroma — divine fullness before the fall
The supreme principle is the [[the-monad-gnostic]] — absolute, ineffable, incorruptible, beyond all predication. From the Monad emanates [[barbelo]] (the divine Mother/First Thought), then the divine Child (Christ/Autogenes), then a cascade of divine aeons forming the Pleroma ("fullness"). Sophia is the last and therefore lowest aeon.

### 2. Sophia's fall — the irregular emanation
Sophia "wished to bring forth a likeness from herself without the consent of her consort" — an act of autonomous self-generation that violates the syzygy principle (aeons emanate in male-female pairs; unilateral emanation is the cosmic error). The result is a "monstrous" being — *Yaldabaoth* — who bears the form of a serpent with a lion's face. He is Sophia's child but not the child of any Father. Sophia is horrified by what she has produced and conceals him in a cloud of light; she then repents and is partially restored, hidden below the Pleroma but above the material cosmos (the "ninth" or intermediate heaven).

### 3. Yaldabaoth's creation — the stolen cosmos
Yaldabaoth steals Sophia's divine power (the *pneuma* she inadvertently gives him), fashions seven archons from the divine light, and creates the material cosmos as an imperfect mirror of the Pleroma he dimly perceives. He declares "I am God and there is no other" (Isaiah 45:5 / Exodus 20:3) — the Sethian polemic re-reads this as proof of his ignorance. He then creates Adam in the image of the Anthropos (the divine Human figure he glimpsed in Sophia's light), and breathes his mother's stolen *pneuma* into Adam — inadvertently giving humans the divine spark that ensures their potential salvation.

### 4. Sophia's longing — the motive of cosmic redemption
Sophia's ongoing longing for the divine realm is the force that keeps the divine spark alive in human beings. The redeemer (Christ) descends precisely to awaken this spark: Gnosis is the recognition that the spark is real, that the demiurge's cosmos is not ultimate, and that the pneumatic soul can ascend back to the Pleroma. ApocJohn ends with Sophia's (and the sparks') eventual full restoration.

## The Valentinian Variant

Valentinus (~136–155 CE, Rome) and his school — especially Ptolemy (via Irenaeus *AH* I.1–8) and [[ptolemaeus-valentinian]] — develop a more complex, philosophically refined version:

**Two Sophias:**
1. **Higher Sophia** (sometimes called "Sophia" simply): remains within the Pleroma; her passion is healed by the "Christ" aeon and by the "Horos" (Limit) who separates her from the fullness.
2. **Lower Sophia** / **Achamoth** (Hebrew for "wisdom"): the passion of Higher Sophia, expelled outside the Pleroma, takes form as the lower aeon — confused, fearful, grieving. The Valentinian Demiurge is created from this lower Sophia's tears (water), her laughter (light), her grief (earth), her fear (air). He is not hostile but ignorant — a "psychic" craftsman who creates the cosmos thinking himself the supreme God.

**The Valentinian resolution:** the Pneumatics (humans who carry the divine spark) are gathered by Sophia Achamoth; at the end of the aeon, Sophia enters the Pleroma, the Pneumatics follow, and the Demiurge himself is elevated to the intermediate realm. Evil is a product of passion and confusion, not malice; redemption is restoration of the Pleroma's fullness.

This is a significantly more optimistic theology than the Sethian version: the Demiurge is not villainous but unwitting; Sophia's fall is recoverable; the cosmos is not merely a prison but a stage for pneumatic education.

| [[hokmah-hebrew]] | Wisdom of Solomon 7:25–26 | "Breath of the power of God... emanation of the glory of the Almighty" — direct emanation language |
| Philo of Alexandria | *On the Creation*, *Allegorical Interpretation* | Sophia/Logos as the intermediate divine mind; bridge to Neoplatonic emanationism |
| Gnostic transformation | Apocryphon of John, Valentinian texts | Sophia *falls* — the same figure who in Proverbs 8 joyfully assisted creation now generates the broken cosmos through her error |

**The inversion is the finding:** The Gnostics took the figure who symbolized divine-human cooperation in Proverbs 8 and made her the inadvertent author of cosmic catastrophe — a brilliant and devastating reinterpretation of Hebrew wisdom theology.

## MASSIVE WIN: Sophia / Shekinah / Jerusalem — the divine feminine exile spine

Three traditions, across 1,800 years, all posit a **divine feminine figure in exile**, whose longing for reunion drives cosmic and human history:

| Tradition | Figure | The exile | The longing | The restoration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gnostic (2nd c.) | [[sophia-gnostic]] | Falls below the Pleroma; her divine spark trapped in matter | Her tears / longing animate the pneumatics | Apokatastasis — return to Pleroma |
| Kabbalistic (13th–16th c.) | [[shekhinah]] | Driven into exile with Israel when the Temple falls; separated from the divine masculine | God mourns her exile; the Zohar's Shabbat-meals are re-unifications | Messianic gathering — Shekhinah returns to the divine seat |
| Blakean (1804–1820) | Jerusalem (Blake's Emanation of [[albion]]) | Separated from Albion when he turns toward Vala (nature); fragments into division | Los's eternal labor to rebuild Jerusalem in England | Albion's awakening — Jerusalem re-united with Albion at the end of Blake's poem |

**Cross-tradition anchor (MASSIVE WIN):** All three figures are divine-feminine principles exiled from a broken masculine divine order. The Gnostic Sophia → Kabbalistic Shekinah parallel was identified by Scholem (1941) as the single most structurally significant convergence between Gnosticism and Kabbalah. Blake independently reconstructed the same structure — Sophia/Shekinah → Jerusalem — receiving the Gnostic-Kabbalistic thread through [[jakob-boehme]]'s Sophia theology (the Shekinah mediated via Christian Kabbalah / Khunrath) and the KJV Wisdom literature.
