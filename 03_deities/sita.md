# Sītā

## Identity
Consort of [[rama]] in the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* — and the **avatar-form of [[lakshmi]]** who descends in each Vishnu-avatar cycle to be the consort of that age's Vishnu-incarnation. Daughter of King Janaka of Mithila, found by him as he ploughed a sacrificial field — hence *Sītā* = "the furrow," and *Bhūmijā* = "earth-born." Her central role in the Rāmāyaṇa is **paradigmatic wifely devotion (*pativratā*)** — accompanying Rama into 14 years of forest-exile rather than enjoy Ayodhya's palace, surviving her abduction by Ravana to Lanka, and undergoing the **agnipariksha (fire-trial)** to demonstrate the chastity she had maintained throughout captivity.

## Mythological role
**The fire-trial and the second exile**: after Rama recovers Sita from Ravana, public rumor in Ayodhya questions her chastity; Rama, as king prioritizing the appearance of dynastic legitimacy, exiles her again, pregnant. She raises Lava and Kusha (her twins, Rama's sons) at the ashram of Valmiki. **Her return to the Earth**: at narrative's close, asked to prove her purity a second time, Sita instead calls on her mother the Earth to receive her; the ground opens and she descends — the most morally devastating moment in the epic, often read as Sita's final assertion of dignity against patriarchal demand. (Tulsidas's later *Ramcharitmanas* softens this; modern feminist reception — Iravati Karve, Devdutt Pattanaik — has restored the original Vālmīki ending to scholarly center.)

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[lakshmi]]** — *ancient-identification*: Sita is Lakshmi-as-Rama-consort.
- **[[radha]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Sita-and-Rama / Radha-and-Krishna as paired Vaishnava divine couples.
- **Penelope** (Homeric) — *parallel-motif*: paradigmatic devoted wife awaiting hero-husband's return.
- **[[persephone-greek]]** — *parallel-motif*: earth-born, descends/returns to the underworld/earth.
