# Mulungu

Mulungu is one of the great Bantu names for the **supreme creator and high god** — used across a wide swath of **East and Central Africa** (among the Yao, Nyamwezi, Kamba, Nyakyusa, and many other Bantu peoples, and surviving in Swahili **Mungu**, now the ordinary word for "God"). He is the classic African **transcendent high god**: the **creator and source of all life**, associated with the **sky and the rain**, the upholder of the world's moral order and its final judge — yet **remote and withdrawn** from daily affairs, approached not directly but **through the ancestors and lesser spirits** who mediate between humankind and the distant Maker. Mulungu has no images and no elaborate cult of his own; he is rather the assumed ground of the whole spiritual world, invoked in times of crisis, drought, or death. He stands as one regional name among several — alongside **[[leza|Leza]]** in the south-central Bantu lands and **[[nyame|Nyame]]** among the Akan — for the same widespread African theological vision: a single supreme God over a populous world of spirits and ancestors. Mulungu embodies the Bantu conception of God as creator, sky, and ultimate source, near in power but far in person.
