# Sayīn

Sayīn (written **Syn**, often rendered "Sin of Hadramawt") was the **national god of the kingdom of Hadramawt**, one of the great caravan-states of pre-Islamic South Arabia, whose principal temple stood at the capital, **Shabwa**. Each South Arabian kingdom centered its cult on a tutelary national deity — Almaqah for Saba, Wadd for Maʿīn, ʿAmm for Qatabān — and Sayīn filled that role for Hadramawt, his name carried in numerous theophoric personal and royal names. His **character is debated**: the writing *Syn* has long invited comparison with the Mesopotamian moon-god **[[nanna-sin|Sîn]]**, yet many scholars read Sayīn as a **solar** deity, and the supposed link to Mesopotamian Sîn remains uncertain rather than demonstrated. What is clear is his political-religious function: the divine guarantor of the Hadrami state and dynasty, to whom the kingdom's monuments and dedications were addressed. He exemplifies the South Arabian pattern of a kingdom and its god bound together.
