# Manu

## Identity

Manu (especially **Vaivasvata Manu**, "Manu son of the Sun") is the progenitor of humankind in Hindu tradition — the **first man**, the survivor of the great flood, and the archetypal lawgiver to whom the *Manusmriti* ("Laws of Manu") is ascribed. The very word for "human" in Sanskrit (*mānava*, "descended from Manu") derives from his name, marking him as the ancestor of the human race. Each cosmic age (*manvantara*) has its own Manu; the Manu of the present age is Vaivasvata.

## Mythological role (the flood)

Manu's central myth is the **deluge**, told first in the *Shatapatha Brahmana* and elaborated in the *Mahabharata* and the Puranas. One morning Manu finds a tiny fish in the water he has brought for washing; the fish begs his protection and, in return, warns him that a great flood will sweep away all creatures. It instructs him to build a boat and to come aboard when the waters rise. As the fish grows to enormous size, Manu ties his boat to its horn, and it tows him through the deluge to a northern mountain peak, where he moors until the waters subside. Manu alone survives; through sacrifice and the offering that produces a woman, Idā, he re-founds the human race. In later tradition the saving fish is revealed to be **[[matsya]]**, the first avatar of [[vishnu]].

## Cross-tradition significance

Manu is the Indian member of one of the most widely distributed of all mythological complexes: the **flood-hero who survives the deluge in a vessel and re-founds humanity**. His story corresponds closely to the Mesopotamian flood-heroes [[ziusudra]], [[utnapishtim]], and Atrahasis, to the biblical [[noah]], and to the Greek [[deucalion]] — the righteous individual forewarned by a god, riding out the world-ending flood, and becoming the ancestor of all later humankind. Manu thus completes the cross-tradition flood-wire across the Near Eastern, Indo-European, and Indian worlds.
