# Hwanin

## Identity
Hwanin is the supreme heavenly ruler (*Sangje*, "Lord on High") in Korean founding mythology — the deity of the celestial realm who initiates the founding sequence of the Korean nation by permitting his son [[hwanung]] to descend to earth.

The *Samguk Yusa* (Iryeon, 1281 CE) identifies Hwanin with the Buddhist Sakra (Indra) — a 13th-century syncretic reading that maps indigenous Korean cosmology onto Buddhist cosmological categories. Whether Hwanin predates this Buddhist overlay as a purely indigenous sky-father figure is debated; scholars like Grayson (2002) suggest the basic sky-father theology is pre-Buddhist but the Indra identification belongs to the medieval Buddhist compilation.

## The founding sequence
Hwanin's role is initiating rather than directly active:
1. Hwanin looks down from heaven and sees that his son [[hwanung]] wishes to descend to earth
2. Hwanin surveys the three great mountains and determines that Mount Paektu (Taebaeksan) is the best place for the divine descent
3. He gives Hwanung the **Three Heavenly Seals** (*cheonbuín*, symbols of divine authority) and 3,000 followers
4. Hwanung descends, civilizes the human world, and ultimately fathers [[tangun]], the founder of the Korean nation

Hwanin's theological function is that of the sky-father who authorizes earthly divine activity — a common pattern in sky-father cosmologies (cf. the heavenly mandate in Chinese theology: *Tianming*).
