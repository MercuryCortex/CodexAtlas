# Gitche Manitou

## Identity
*Gichi-manidoo* / Gitche Manitou — "Great Spirit" or "Great Mystery" — is the totalizing-sacred concept of Anishinaabe and broader Algonquian religion. *Manitou* / *manidoo* is the underlying conceptual category — a *manidoo* is a being or power with spiritual potency; many beings have manidoo (water, animals, places, ancestors); *Gichi-manidoo* is the great or totalizing one. As with Lakota Wakan Tanka, the common English translation "Great Spirit" misleads if it suggests an anthropomorphic deity; Gitche Manitou is closer to a totalizing field of sacred power than a personal god.

## Earliest attestation
Algonquian oral tradition. Documented from 17th-c. Jesuit Relations onward (with significant Catholic-missionary interpretive filtering); modern ethnography from Frances Densmore, A. Irving Hallowell (1960 *Ojibwa Ontology*), Christopher Vecsey (1983).

## Mythological role
Approached through tobacco-offering (Anishinaabe sacred plant), the sweat-lodge (*madoodiswan*), drumming, and ceremony. The Midewiwin (Grand Medicine Society) ceremonial complex is the formalized initiatory tradition associated with Anishinaabe religion; degrees of Midewiwin initiation correspond to grades of access to manidoo knowledge.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Christian God** — *folk-syncretism* (post-contact): Anishinaabe converts to Christianity have variously translated and identified Gitche Manitou with the Christian God; some Anishinaabe Christians maintain a synthetic position, others sharply distinguish.

## Iconography
Aniconic.
