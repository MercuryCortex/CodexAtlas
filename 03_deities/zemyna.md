# Žemyna

Žemyna ("Earth") is the Lithuanian earth goddess — the most intimate deity in Baltic religion, addressed directly at every domestic threshold: births, deaths, meals, and household transitions all involved a libation poured onto the earth while calling her name. Where [[perkunas]] (the thunderer) and [[dievas-baltic]] (the sky-father) govern the distant celestial sphere, Žemyna governs the immediate — the ground underfoot, the bread in hand, the soil of the grave.

Her ritual presence in Lithuanian folk religion survived into the late 19th century despite Christian conversion, precisely because she was embedded in domestic practice rather than in a separate cult center. The *dainas* (Lithuanian folk songs) preserve hundreds of direct addresses to Žemynėlė ("dear little Earth").

West (2007) identifies Žemyna as one of the best-preserved inheritors of the PIE Earth Mother (*\*dʰéǵʰōm*) alongside Greek Gaia and Vedic Prithvi. The Lithuanian preservation is particularly valuable because Lithuanian is the most archaic living Indo-European language — making Žemyna evidence for a deep PIE religious stratum that Greek and Sanskrit attestations confirm independently.
