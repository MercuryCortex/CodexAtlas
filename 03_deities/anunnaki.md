# Anunnaki

## Identity

The Anunnaki (Sumerian **Anunna**, "offspring of An") are not a single deity but a **collective** — the body of the great gods of Mesopotamia, the descendants of the supreme sky-god [[an-sumerian]]. The term is used flexibly across the long history of Mesopotamian religion: sometimes for the great gods of heaven and earth in assembly, sometimes specifically for the gods of the netherworld, and sometimes paired against the [[igigi]] as the senior versus the younger generation of gods.

## Mythological role

In their celestial aspect the Anunnaki are the divine assembly that **decrees the destinies** — the council in which the great gods deliberate and pronounce the fates of cities, kings, and the cosmos. In their chthonic aspect they are the **judges of the underworld**: in the *Descent of Inanna*, the seven Anunnaki sit before [[ereshkigal]] and fix the deathly verdict upon Inanna. In the *Atra-ḫasīs* epic the relationship between the senior Anunnaki and the laboring Igigi frames the central drama: the younger gods, set to dig the rivers and canals, revolt against the toil, and the resolution — proposed by Enki and the mother-goddess — is the creation of humankind to bear the work in the gods' place.

## Cross-tradition significance

As the Mesopotamian "divine council," the Anunnaki are an important comparandum for the **assembly-of-the-gods** motif found across the ancient Near East — the Ugaritic council of El, the Hebrew *bənê ʾēlōhîm* ("sons of God") and the divine council of Psalm 82, and the Greek assembly of the Olympians. The image of a supreme god presiding over a council of lesser divine beings who decree fates is one of the deep structural inheritances that the biblical tradition shares with its Mesopotamian background.
