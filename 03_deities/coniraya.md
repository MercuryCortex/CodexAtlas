# Coniraya (Cuniraya Viracocha)

## Identity
Coniraya (also Cuniraya Viracocha) is the **Andean trickster-creator deity** of the Huarochirí region — a powerful god who disguises himself as a filthy beggar to move among mortals, concealing vast creative power within an abject exterior. He is the most vivid individual deity in the **Huarochirí Manuscript** (c. 1608 CE), the only surviving extended narrative text of indigenous Andean religion in the original Quechua language. His story combines divine power, unrequited desire, deceptive creation, and a picaresque journey of lamentation — making him one of the most narratively complex pre-Columbian deities documented in any Andean source.

## Earliest attestation
- Primary source: **Huarochirí Manuscript** (*Ritos y tradiciones de Huarochirí del siglo XVII*), compiled c. **1608 CE** by the Catholic priest Francisco de Ávila, who gathered these oral narratives from Quechua-speaking communities in the Huarochirí region of central Peru.
- The manuscript is the **only pre-conquest-derived Andean religious text preserved in the original Andean language (Quechua)** — all other extended Andean mythological accounts survive only in Spanish translation. This makes the Huarochirí Manuscript the Andean equivalent of the Popol Vuh.
- Translation: Salomon and Urioste (1991) is the standard scholarly edition.

## Mythological role
**The impregnation of Cavillaca**: Coniraya, disguised as a ragged beggar, desired the beautiful goddess **Cavillaca**, who refused all suitors. He placed his semen inside a ripe **lucuma fruit** and left it where she ate. Cavillaca became pregnant without understanding how — the child was born; she demanded the father reveal himself. Coniraya stood forward — filthy, contemptible. Cavillaca, in shame, fled to the sea with her child, where both transformed into **stone islands** (the Pachacamac islands off the coast of Lima). The child turned into stone while still an infant.

**The journey**: Coniraya pursued Cavillaca across the Andean world, asking each animal he encountered which way she had fled:
- **Condor**: told him she was near → Coniraya blessed the condor: "When anyone kills you, the one who did it shall die"
- **Skunk**: told him she was far → Coniraya cursed the skunk: "You shall walk only at night, and all who meet you shall be disgusted"
- **Puma**: gave hopeful news → blessed: "You shall eat the llamas of the guilty; when they kill you they must dance"
- **Fox**: gave bad news → cursed to stink and be despised

This "animal oracle" journey pattern — the deity asking creatures for directions, rewarding helpers and cursing hinderers — distributes attributes of animals as etiological explanations of their observed behaviors.

**Later exploits**: Coniraya continues as a wandering trickster throughout the Huarochirí narratives, seducing women and causing transformations; his association with **[[viracocha]]** in the manuscript's title suggests a theological merger between the regional trickster and the supreme creator of Inca state religion.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[viracocha]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Huarochirí Manuscript itself names him "Cuniraya Viracocha" — merging the regional trickster with the Inca supreme creator; a theologically significant identification that remains debated.
- **[[odin]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: both are high-god-level deities who disguise themselves as wandering beggars; both test mortals; both carry wisdom hidden beneath degraded appearance. Odin's "Grimnismál" wanderer / Coniraya's beggar-disguise are structurally identical.
- **[[zeus]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the divine impregnation-by-deception — Zeus impregnates through disguise (swan, bull, golden rain); Coniraya through a lucuma fruit. The high deity who fertilizes without consent through ingenuity rather than direct contact.
- **[[coyote-navajo]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: trickster-creator of the Americas whose deceptions generate the world's features; both Coyote and Coniraya combine creative power with unruly desire.
- **[[anansi]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the global trickster-creator archetype — cleverness as the primary generative force, operating through deception and unexpected consequences.
- **[[demeter]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the "which way did they go?" divine search — Demeter searching for Persephone (the old woman Baubo, the Eleusinian messengers) / Coniraya asking animals for directions. Both narratives distribute knowledge and punishment along the search path.

## MASSIVE WIN: the miraculous-impregnation-by-substance

Coniraya's impregnation of Cavillaca via a lucuma fruit belongs to the cross-cultural pattern of **divine conception without direct contact**:
- **Coniraya → Cavillaca** via lucuma fruit (Huarochirí Manuscript)
- **Zeus → Danaë** via golden rain (*Metamorphoses*)
- **Shiva's seed → Skanda** via fire / Ganges water (Mahabharata, Puranas)
- **The Virgin Mary → Jesus** via the Holy Spirit (without physical union — the Christian version of the miraculous conception)
- **Nana → Attis** via a pomegranate or almond (Phrygian myth)

The pattern: the deity's reproductive essence is transmitted through an intermediary substance (fruit, rain, fire, flower, spirit) — bypassing the direct erotic encounter. This "clean" or "hidden" conception both elevates the offspring (miraculous origin) and raises questions of consent, knowledge, and divine responsibility.

## Iconography
Coniraya's defining visual attribute is **deliberate absence of iconography** — he appears as a filthy, ragged beggar. His power is invisible, his divine nature hidden. This is itself an iconographic statement: the greatest creative force in the Huarochirí tradition looks like nothing at all.
