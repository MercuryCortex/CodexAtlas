# Tepeu

## Identity

Tepeu (K'iche' **Tepew**, "the Sovereign," "Majesty") is one of the creator-gods of the *Popol Vuh*. He is paired throughout with **Gucumatz**, the Feathered Serpent (the K'iche' counterpart of [[kukulkan]] / Quetzalcoatl), the two named together as the makers who, with Heart-of-Sky ([[huracan]]), bring the world into being.

## Mythological role

The *Popol Vuh* opens in the primordial stillness: there is only the empty sky and the calm, motionless sea, and within the water, gleaming in quetzal feathers, lie **Tepeu and Gucumatz**, "the Bearers, the Begetters." In the darkness they confer; "they spoke, they conceived," and by their thought and word they plan the creation — and at their utterance the earth arises from the sea, the mountains lift, the waters divide, and the world is furnished with creatures. The creation in the *Popol Vuh* is thus an act of **dialogue and speech** among the divine powers: Tepeu and Gucumatz think and say the world, and it comes to be. They then consult the diviner-grandparents [[xmucane-xpiacoc]] for the harder task of making beings who can speak and honor the gods — culminating, after failed attempts, in humanity formed from maize.

## Cross-tradition significance

Tepeu and Gucumatz's creation-by-speech places the Maya among the traditions in which the world is **brought forth by the divine word and thought** — a striking parallel to the Egyptian Memphite theology (creation by the heart's thought and tongue's command), the Genesis "and God said," and the Johannine *Logos*. The image of the creators floating in the calm primordial sea, conceiving the world out of the waters, also links the *Popol Vuh* to the broad family of **primordial-waters cosmogonies**. As the "sovereign" of the creating pair, Tepeu anchors the Maya end of the cross-tradition creator-and-feathered-serpent complex.
