# Soma

## Identity
Soma is both a deity and a ritual substance — the pressed juice of a plant, prepared and drunk in the Vedic soma-sacrifice, the central rite of the priestly tradition. The entire ninth book of the Rig Veda (Maṇḍala 9) is dedicated to Soma Pavamāna ("Soma being purified"), consisting of ~114 hymns describing the pressing, filtering, and divine ascent of the drink. As deity, Soma is the *king of the plants*, the lord of inspiration, the bestower of poetic vision and ecstasy. In later Hinduism, Soma is identified with Candra (the moon — the moon waxes and wanes as the gods drink the soma from it).

The Avestan cognate is *Haoma*, prepared and drunk in the Zoroastrian Yasna liturgy to this day. Both names derive from Indo-Iranian *sauma-* / *hauma-* ("pressed thing"); both are cognate plants prepared in cognate ways for cognate purposes. The botanical identification is unresolved: *Ephedra* (Falk 1989, current Avestan-studies favorite), *Amanita muscaria* (Wasson 1968, now largely rejected), *Peganum harmala* (Syrian rue, Flattery & Schwartz 1989), *Cannabis*, *Sarcostemma*, or now-extinct mountain plant. The soma-haoma question is the single best-documented case of Indo-Iranian ritual continuity.
