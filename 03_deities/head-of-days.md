# Head of Days / Antecedent of Days

## Identity
The **Head of Days** (Ge'ez *Re'esa Mawa'el*, literally "head of the days," "the antecedent / head of time") is the high-god figure in the **Book of Parables** (1 Enoch 37–71). He is the *enthroned eternal one* before whom the [[the-elect-one-enochic]] (Son of Man) is *named before creation* (1 Enoch 48:3), and on whose throne the Son of Man sits beside him at the eschatological judgment. The figure is the Enochic-canonical theological development of the high-god of Israelite tradition (YHWH), but with a distinct theological *title* that emphasizes pre-temporal eternity ("Head of the Days") rather than personal-name reference.

The figure is the direct Enochic parallel — and possibly the source — of **Daniel 7:9's "Ancient of Days"** (*ʿattîq yōmîn*), where the seer sees the white-haired enthroned judge of the eschatological court before whom "one like a son of man" approaches. The Enochic and Danielic figures are mutually illuminating; modern scholarship (Stone 1990; Boyarin 2004) generally treats them as variants of a single Second-Temple-Jewish heavenly-court vision.

## Earliest attestation
- **Daniel 7:9** (~2nd c. BCE, Maccabean-era) — *Ancient of Days* enthroned in white garments with hair like pure wool.
- **1 Enoch 46–48** — the *Head of Days* with the Son of Man approaching him; the Son of Man named before creation in his presence.
- **1 Enoch 60, 71** — additional Head-of-Days throne-room scenes.
- **Ge'ez Mashafa Henok** — only complete textual witness for the Parables.

## Mythological role
The Head of Days is the **supreme judge of the eschatological court** in the Enochic-canonical-Ethiopian theological frame. The structural pattern — supreme divine judge + adjutant heavenly figure (Son of Man / Elect One) enthroned beside him — is the **bi-throne** ("two powers in heaven") pattern that Alan Segal 1977 identified as a Second-Temple-Jewish theological structure: the high-god *plus* a closely-related divine-or-divine-adjacent agent. This bi-throne pattern is the load-bearing theological substrate for:

- Daniel 7:9–14's Ancient-of-Days + Son-of-Man pair
- 1 Enoch 46–48's Head-of-Days + Elect-One pair
- The Qumran 11QMelchizedek's heavenly-judge Melchizedek
- The Philonic Logos pattern (the Logos as second God / *deuteros theos*)
- Later Christian Father + Son pattern in Trinitarian theology
- Later rabbinic polemic against "Two Powers in Heaven" heresy (specifically targeting Christian and proto-Christian Jewish theology)

The Enochic Head-of-Days is the most-developed *named* instance of this pattern; the title's emphasis on pre-temporal eternity ("Head of the Days") makes the theological move explicit — this is the deity *prior to all temporality*, before whom the Elect One's pre-existence is meaningful.

The relationship to YHWH proper is *reverence-substitution*: Second-Temple-Jewish piety preferred indirect titles (Most High, Lord of Spirits, Head of Days, Ancient of Days) over the divine Name. The Enochic text uses *Head of Days*, *Lord of Spirits*, and *Most High* interchangeably or in close coordination.

## Cross-tradition identifications

- **[[yahweh]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Head of Days is the Enochic-canonical high-god, theologically continuous with the Israelite YHWH; the title is reverence-substitution.
- **Daniel 7:9's *ʿattîq yōmîn*** — *direct-parallel*: same throne-room figure under cognate title.
- **Christian *Father* (the first Person of the Trinity)** — *theological-continuity*: subsequent Christian Trinitarian theology absorbs the Head-of-Days + Son-of-Man bi-throne pattern into Father-Son theology, with the Son of Man identification of Jesus as the operative move.
- **Philonic *theos* + *Logos*** — *parallel-form*: Philo of Alexandria's *deuteros theos* / Logos pattern shares the bi-throne structure.

## Iconography
In Ethiopian Orthodox art, the Head of Days is iconographically continuous with the Father of the Trinity — typically depicted (when the Father is shown at all, which is doctrinally constrained) as an enthroned bearded elder, sometimes with white hair, following Daniel 7:9 iconography. The Ethiopian *kane-tabot* (Ark-of-Covenant tablet) inscriptions and some 17th–18th c. Gondarine paintings show the Father in this form alongside the Son.

## Disputes

- **Whether the Head of Days is *YHWH himself* or a distinct hypostasis.** Most scholarship treats Head of Days as a reverence-substitute for YHWH (= YHWH himself under indirect title); some (Boyarin 2004) emphasize the theological *productivity* of the title in opening space for the Two-Powers structure with the Son of Man.
- **Relationship to Daniel 7:9.** Whether 1 Enoch's Parables draw on Daniel or whether both draw on a common earlier tradition. Most current scholarship sees mutual dependence within a common Second-Temple-Jewish apocalyptic milieu.
- **The Trinitarian-Christian inheritance.** Whether subsequent Christian Father-Son theology is *direct development* from the Enochic-Danielic bi-throne pattern or *independent reading* of OT texts. Boyarin 2012 and the Enoch Seminar generally argue for direct continuity; conservative Trinitarian scholarship resists.
