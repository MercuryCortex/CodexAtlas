# Janus

## Identity
**The most distinctively Roman of all Roman gods** — no Greek equivalent — Janus is the god of **doorways and transitions**. His name shares root with *ianua* "door"; he looks **forward and backward simultaneously** with two faces, presiding over every passage from one state to another: dawn (beginning of day), the new year (beginning of the calendar — January is *Ianuarius*, his month), war and peace (his Forum temple's doors stood open in war, closed in peace), birth, marriage, harvest, and death.

## Mythological role
**The first invocation**: Janus was named first in every Roman religious ceremony, before even Jupiter — because all rituals are passages and all passages begin with him. **The Janus-temple gates**: a small bronze sanctuary in the Roman Forum whose doors were closed only in time of complete peace — closed **only three times** in the entire Roman Republic (under Numa, after the First Punic War, and after Actium under Augustus). **Janus Bifrons** iconography is the source of the figurative meaning of "Janus-faced" — looking both ways, holding past and future together.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[hekate]]** — *parallel-motif*: threshold-deity (Hekate is triple-faced, feminine, more chthonic).
- **Saint Peter (with the keys)** — *folk-syncretism* (disputed; Lactantius *Div. Inst.* 1.13 explicitly tries to dis-identify Janus from Christian saints; the iconographic parallel of key-holder-at-the-threshold-of-heaven is structural and was used in early-medieval polemic on both sides).
