# Crius

## Identity

Crius (Greek **Krios**, "the Ram") is one of the twelve Titans, a son of [[uranus-greek]] and [[gaia]]. He is among the most shadowy of the Titans, with essentially no myth of his own; his significance is genealogical and structural. His name, "the Ram," led later writers to associate him with the constellation Aries and to count him, with Coeus, Hyperion, and Iapetus, among the "pillar" Titans imagined to hold up the sky at the four corners or to mark the cosmic axes.

## Mythological role

Crius's role is as an **ancestor**. By Eurybia (a daughter of Pontus, the Sea, and Gaia) he fathers three sons who carry significant cosmic functions: **Astraeus** (father, by Eos, of the stars and the winds), **Pallas** (father of Nike, Kratos, Bia, and Zelus — Victory, Strength, Force, and Rivalry), and **Perses** (father of Hecate). Through these sons Crius stands at the head of the astral and "force"-personification lines of the Greek divine genealogy. Like the other Titans, he belongs to the generation displaced by Zeus and the Olympians in the Titanomachy.

## Cross-tradition significance

Crius exemplifies the Greek use of the Titans as a **genealogical scaffolding** — figures whose importance lies not in their deeds but in the cosmic powers and personifications they sire (stars, winds, victory, strength, Hecate). As one of the four Titans later read as the pillars of the cosmic quarters, he also reflects the ancient impulse to anchor the structure of the heavens to a set of primordial divine "supports," an impulse paralleled in other traditions' four-pillars-of-heaven and world-axis imagery.
