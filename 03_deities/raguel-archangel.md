# Raguel (Archangel)

## Identity
**Raguel** (Hebrew *Re'u'el* or *Ragu'el*, "Friend of God") is one of the **seven archangels** named in 1 Enoch 20 — the canonical list of the heavenly hierarchy in the Enochic tradition. He is *one of the three* major Enochic archangels not in the standard Western Catholic-Protestant short-list of four (Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, Uriel) but who has been canonically retained in [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]] (via Ge'ez 1 Enoch) and historically in [[tradition-coptic-orthodox]] liturgical practice — though Roman Catholic veneration of named angels beyond Michael, Gabriel, and Raphael was suppressed by Pope Zacharias's Roman Synod of 745 CE (which forbade veneration of angels other than the canonical three) and reinforced by Aachen 789.

1 Enoch 20:4 (Nickelsburg-VanderKam 2004): *"Raguel, one of the holy angels, who takes vengeance on the world of the luminaries."*

## Earliest attestation
- **1 Enoch 20:1–8** — the canonical naming of the seven Enochic archangels:
  1. **Uriel** — "set over the world and over Tartarus" (already in vault: [[uriel-archangel]])
  2. **Raphael** — "set over the spirits of humans" (already: [[raphael-archangel]])
  3. **Raguel** — "takes vengeance on the world of the luminaries" (this node)
  4. **Michael** — "set over the best part of humankind, over the people" (already: [[michael-archangel]])
  5. **Saraqael** — "set over the spirits of those who sin in spirit" ([[saraqael-archangel]])
  6. **Gabriel** — "set over the serpents and over paradise and over the cherubim" (already: [[gabriel-archangel]])
  7. **Remiel** — "set over those who rise" — also called **Jeremiel** in 4 Ezra 4:36 ([[remiel-archangel]])

- **1 Enoch 23:4** — Raguel reappears as one of the angelic guides showing Enoch the cosmic-eschatological geography.
- **Ge'ez Mashafa Henok** — the only complete textual witness; preserves the canonical-Ethiopian seven-archangel list.
- **Coptic liturgical tradition** retains Raguel-veneration (with the other three "extra" Enochic archangels) more strongly than the Latin West.

## Mythological role
Raguel's specific function in 1 Enoch 20 is to "take vengeance on the world of the luminaries" — that is, on the *stars-as-personal-beings* who disobey their divinely-ordained courses. This connects directly to the Enochic *Astronomical Book* (1 Enoch 72–82), which is fundamentally concerned with the *correctness* and *order* of the luminaries' movements. The stars are not pure mechanism in Enochic theology — they are angelic beings, and their movements can be transgressive; Raguel is the archangelic prosecutor of that transgression.

In Ethiopian Orthodox iconography Raguel appears in some seven-archangel paintings, particularly in 17th–19th c. Gondarine and post-Gondarine church art, alongside the other six. Modern Ethiopian Orthodox theology preserves the Enochic seven-archangel ranking more thoroughly than any other living Christian tradition.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- Mentioned in some Gnostic and Manichaean angelological texts (often with name-variants).
- Coptic-Egyptian liturgy retains Raguel-veneration in some monastic offices.

## Iconography
In Ethiopian Orthodox church-painting, Raguel typically appears as one of the seven archangels arranged around Christ-in-Majesty, with the others (Michael, Gabriel, Raphael, Uriel, Saraqael, Remiel). Specific iconographic attributes are less standardized than for the more-prominent four; sometimes Raguel is shown holding a balance or weight (the *takes-vengeance* role).

## Disputes
- **Identity with "Reuel" in the Pentateuch.** Reuel is the name of Moses's father-in-law in some Pentateuchal passages (Exodus 2:18); whether the Enochic archangel name draws on this human-character name is unclear. Most scholars treat them as separate.
- **Identity with "Suriel" in some Coptic and rabbinic angelologies.** Some traditions equate Raguel and Suriel; others keep them distinct.
