# Hine-nui-te-pō

Hine-nui-te-pō ("Great Woman of the Night") is the **Māori goddess of death and the night**, the dread but not malign sovereign of the underworld (**Rarohenga**) who **receives the spirits of all the dead**. Her origin myth defines mortality itself. She was first **Hine-tītama**, the radiant Dawn-Maiden, born of the forest-god **[[tane|Tāne]]** and the first woman (whom Tāne had himself formed); Tāne took her as wife, and when she discovered that her husband was also her **father**, she fled in shame down to the world of darkness, declaring that she would no longer draw their children up into the light of day but would **gather them to her in death** below. So she became Hine-nui-te-pō. Her second great myth is her killing of **[[maui-polynesian|Māui]]**: the trickster, seeking to win immortality for humankind, attempts to reverse death by crawling **through her sleeping body** from below — but a fantail bird, delighted, bursts out laughing; Hine-nui-te-pō wakes, and the **obsidian teeth set in her thighs** crush Māui to death. Because the great hero failed, **death became permanent for all people** — and Hine-nui-te-pō remains the goddess into whose night every human at last returns.
