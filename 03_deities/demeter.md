# Demeter

## Identity
Demeter is the Greek Olympian of grain, agriculture, and the great agricultural and mystery-religious cycle of Greece. Her name probably means "Grain-Mother" (*dē-* possibly cognate with the cereal grain, *-mētēr* mother), though some derive the *dē-* from the Earth (so "Earth-Mother"). The central mythic-religious complex around her is the Homeric Hymn to Demeter: her daughter Persephone (Kore, "the Maiden") is abducted by Hades; Demeter wanders the earth in grief, withholding the grain; the gods broker a compromise where Persephone spends part of each year above and part below, generating the seasonal cycle. This narrative grounded the Eleusinian Mysteries — the most prestigious initiatory mystery cult of Greek antiquity, performed annually at Eleusis from ~-1500 BCE through to the 4th c. CE, promising initiates a happier post-mortem condition.

Demeter's primary cult site Eleusis (~20 km from Athens) preserved a continuous ritual tradition from Mycenaean times until the Christian destruction by Alaric's Goths in 396 CE. The Mysteries' secrecy was so well kept that we still do not know exactly what was shown or said in the *telesterion*. The Greco-Egyptian syncretism Demeter-Isis became massive in the Hellenistic and Roman periods; Isis-Mysteries (Apuleius, *Metamorphoses* book 11) inherit and transform many features of Eleusinian Demeter-worship.
