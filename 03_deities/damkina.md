# Damkina

## Identity
Damkina (Akkadian; Sumerian *Damgalnuna*, "Great Lady of the Princely Place") is the consort of [[enki-ea]] and the mother of [[marduk]] in the Babylonian theological synthesis articulated in [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]]. Her Sumerian name *Damgalnuna* identifies her as the chief female partner ("great wife") of the prince-god of the Apsu, Enki. In her own right she has comparatively little extant mythology — her significance is genealogical-systematic, anchoring the Eridu-Marduk cult-line that *Enuma Elish* uses to ground Marduk's cosmic kingship in inherited divinity rather than usurpation. In *Enuma Elish* I.79–84 Marduk is born to "the princess Damkina" in the Apsu and immediately distinguished as exceptionally radiant; the Babylonian state theology that elevated Marduk's local cult to imperial supremacy required Marduk to descend from the senior gods Enki and Damkina rather than appear as a parvenu.

## Cross-tradition identifications
No standard *interpretatio* outside Mesopotamia. The Damkina-Enki pairing is internal to Sumero-Babylonian theology and was not widely Hellenized.

## Disputes
- **Independent cult.** Whether Damkina had a significant independent cult outside the Eridu-Esagila ritual context is debated. Black-Green (1992) suggest her presence is almost entirely consort/mother-of-Marduk; some scholars argue for an older Sumerian Damgalnuna with broader regional veneration prior to her absorption into the Babylonian Marduk-cult.
- **Name forms.** "Damkianna" appears in late texts and may be a folk-etymological reinterpretation as "Lady of Heaven-and-Earth" rather than the original "Lady of the Princely Place."
