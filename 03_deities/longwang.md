# Longwang

Longwang (*Lóngwáng* 龍王, "Dragon King") is the Chinese divine **ruler of the waters** — of the seas, rivers, lakes, and wells — and the great controller of **rainfall**, on whom both the farmer's harvest and the sailor's safety depend. Usually conceived as **four Dragon Kings**, one for each of the cardinal seas (the eldest being **Ao Guang** of the East Sea), each dwells in a magnificent **crystal palace beneath the waves**, commands armies of aquatic creatures, and can raise or withhold the rain-bearing clouds. In times of drought their temples received urgent petitions and processions; in flood they were blamed and even, in some tales, punished. The Dragon Kings figure largely in classic vernacular literature (*Journey to the West*, *Investiture of the Gods*), where they are powerful but not infrequently outwitted. Their form fuses the **native Chinese dragon (long)** — an auspicious water-and-sky power of great antiquity — with the **Indian Buddhist nāga-rājas**, the serpent water-kings transmitted into China with Buddhism. Alongside the sea-goddess **[[mazu|Mazu]]**, Longwang anchors the vast Chinese religion of water and weather.
