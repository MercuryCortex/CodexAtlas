# Abrasax

## Identity

Abrasax (also spelled Abraxas in Latin sources) is the supreme deity of Basilidean Gnosticism — the school founded by Basilides of Alexandria (c. 120–140 CE), one of the earliest and most philosophically ambitious Gnostic teachers. The name is a cosmological cipher: in Greek *isopsephy* (letter-value calculation, the Greek equivalent of gematria), the letters of ΑΒΡΑΣΑΞ sum to 365:

- Α = 1
- Β = 2
- Ρ = 100
- Α = 1
- Σ = 200
- Α = 1
- Ξ = 60
- **Total = 365**

The number 365 is simultaneously the number of days in the solar year and the number of aeons (divine spheres) in Basilides's cosmic system. By naming the supreme deity "Abrasax," Basilides encodes the cosmic architecture in the divine name: to know the god is to know the structure of time and the heavens. This is gematria as theology — the name *is* the cosmological diagram.

---

## Basilides's 365-Aeon System

Irenaeus (Adversus Haereses I.24, c. 180 CE) provides the principal account of Basilides's system (hostile but detailed). The structure:

- The supreme principle ("the Unborn Father," *Agennetos*) is above all — Abrasax may be this figure or the ruling mind of the highest aeon (Irenaeus's account is not perfectly consistent)
- Below the supreme principle: 365 aeons arranged in a descending hierarchy of divine spheres
- Each aeon is a heaven; each heaven is ruled by its own angelic power
- The lowest heavens are those familiar from the OT — ruled by the OT God (the demiurge of this world), who created the earth and Israel
- The supreme Abrasax-God is completely transcendent, unknown to the material-world rulers

**The 365-heaven system** is Basilides's answer to the Sethian 7-archon system: rather than seven planetary archons, Basilides posits 365 divine spheres — corresponding to the days of the year, the cycles of time, and the layers of the cosmic hierarchy. The material world is the lowest layer; the supreme principle is 365 degrees above it.

## Abraxas Gems: The Most Widespread Gnostic Artifact

Abraxas/Abrasax survives in the archaeological record more abundantly than almost any other Gnostic figure: hundreds of carved gemstone amulets (the "Abraxas gems" or "magical gems") survive from the 2nd–6th centuries CE, depicting the composite Abrasax figure. The standard iconography:

- **Rooster head**: solar symbol (the rooster greets the dawn); also Ares/Mars associations
- **Human torso with armor**: the divine warrior or ruler
- **Serpent legs**: chthonic power, underworld access, also solar-serpent (Ouroboros family)
- **Whip in right hand**: authority, command over the aeons
- **Shield in left hand**: protection; often inscribed with IAO (see [[iao-gnostic]])
- **Inscriptions**: typically "ΑΒΡΑΣΑΞ" plus other divine names (IAO, SABAOTH, ADONAI, MICHEAL)

The composite body — combining bird, human, and serpent in one figure — is explicitly syncretic: it combines Egyptian solar iconography, Greek warrior imagery, and Jewish-Gnostic divine names. The Abraxas gems were not exclusively "Gnostic" in a sectarian sense — they functioned as protective amulets across a broad Mediterranean market, used by people who may not have been Basilidean initiates but who recognized the name's magical power.

**"Abracadabra"**: the magical formula "abracadabra" (attested from the 2nd century CE in Quintus Serenus Sammonicus) is almost certainly derived from Abrasax — either directly or through the intermediate magical tradition that used the name as a supreme invocation.

## MASSIVE WIN: Gematria as Cosmological Architecture

Abrasax = 365 is the most famous and structurally important example of *gematria* (number-value of letters) deployed as *cosmological theology*. The divine name is not arbitrary — it is a cosmological equation. Knowing the name means knowing the number; knowing the number means knowing the structure of the heavens.

This principle — that the divine name encodes cosmic structure through numerical value — propagates across traditions:

| Tradition | Practice | Key example | What the number encodes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basilidean Gnostic (2nd c.) | Greek isopsephy | Abrasax = 365 | The number of aeons / days in the year |
| Jewish Kabbalistic (12th c. onward) | Hebrew gematria | *Sefer Yetzirah*'s 32 paths (10 sefirot + 22 letters) | The structure of divine emanation and creation |
| Islamic *abjad* (7th c. onward) | Arabic letter-values | Divine names (e.g., *Allah* = 66) interpreted numerically | Mystical properties of divine names |
| Pythagorean (6th–4th c. BCE) | Greek number-mysticism | The Tetraktys (1+2+3+4=10) as sacred cosmological figure | The number of fundamental cosmic principles |
| Revelation / Apocalyptic (1st c.) | Hebrew/Greek gematria | 666 as "number of the beast" (= Nero Caesar in Hebrew) | Hidden identity encoded in number |

**The convergence:** The belief that the divine name is a number, and that number encodes cosmic structure, is not a minor magical curiosity — it is a fundamental late-antique theological move that links Gnostic, Kabbalistic, Islamic, and Pythagorean traditions. Abrasax is the most explicit and famous example: the deity's name *is* the cosmological diagram.

## MASSIVE WIN: Composite Divine Iconography Across Traditions

Abrasax's hybrid body — rooster head, human torso, serpent legs — belongs to a cross-tradition pattern of composite divine iconography in which a single figure embodies multiple ontological orders:

| Figure | Composition | Tradition | What the composite encodes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abrasax | Rooster + human + serpent | Gnostic-Hermetic | Solar (rooster) + human (ruler) + chthonic (serpent) = complete cosmic sovereignty |
| [[narasimha]] | Lion + human | Hindu Vaishnava | Divine power that exceeds any single category; the avatar who defeats the demon precisely by being neither man nor animal |
| Egyptian composites (Thoth, Anubis, Sekhmet) | Ibis/jackal/lion + human | Egyptian | Each animal encodes a divine domain; the composite figure holds all domains at once |
| Seraphim / Cherubim (Isaiah 6; Ezekiel 1) | Human + lion + ox + eagle; six wings | Hebrew prophetic | The four living creatures that surround the divine throne encode the four quadrants of cosmic life |
| [[metatron]] / Merkavah figures | Human-divine composite; multiple faces | Jewish mystical | The divine body that mediates between infinite God and finite creation |

**The theological logic:** A composite body is a theological statement: no single natural form can adequately represent the divine. By combining forms from different ontological registers (celestial bird, earthly human, chthonic serpent), Abrasax iconography declares that the divine transcends any single category. The visual grammar of composite bodies is a cross-tradition assertion of divine transcendence-through-combination.
