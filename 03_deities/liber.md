# Liber

## Identity
Liber (Liber Pater, "Father Liber") is the old Italic god of **wine, fertility, and freedom** — the generative power of nature and the patron of the Roman plebs. With his consort **Libera** and the grain-goddess [[ceres-roman]] he forms the **Aventine Triad**, the plebeian counterpart to the patrician Capitoline Triad.

## Earliest attestation
His Aventine cult was instituted in **493 BCE** alongside Ceres and Libera; his festival the **Liberalia** (17 March) marked the coming-of-age of Roman boys, who took the *toga virilis*. His name links to *līber* ("free") and to liberty.

## Mythological role — wine, generation, and the plebs
Liber is the divinized force of growth — of the vine, of the seed, of generation itself (his rites included phallic emblems as signs of fertility). Old and Italic, he was readily identified with the Greek [[dionysus]] and fused with the imported [[bacchus]]; through this identification he acquired the mystery-cult associations and the promise of renewal that the wine-god carried across the Mediterranean. As patron of the plebs and of liberty, he also carried a political charge in the Republic.

## Cross-tradition
Liber is the Roman vine-and-vegetation god — Dionysus → Liber/Bacchus → the Etruscan Fufluns — a member of the wine-god and the **dying-and-rising** vegetation complex (the vine that dies back and returns).

## Iconography
The vine, ivy, and thyrsus; the wine-cup; phallic fertility emblems; the cap of liberty.
