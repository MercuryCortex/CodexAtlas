# Apām Napāt (Vedic)

Apām Napāt (Sanskrit, "Child / Grandson of the Waters") is a Vedic deity of striking paradox — **the fire that dwells hidden within the cosmic waters**. The Rigvedic hymn **RV 2.35** praises him as a golden, shining being who lives in the waters and is fed by them, yet is himself ardent and fiery, the lightning-spark concealed in the flood. He is closely identified with **[[agni]]** in his aquatic-celestial aspect (Agni *apām napāt*), the divine fire in its watery, lightning form. Apām Napāt is one of the **cleanest cases of Indo-Iranian religious continuity**: the very same name and the very same "fire-in-the-waters" theology reappear in the Younger Avesta as the Avestan **[[apam-napat-zoroastrian|Apąm Napāt]]** of *Yašt 19* (the Zamyād Yašt), where he guards the *khvarenah*, the cosmic glory, in the depths of the sea — a shared inheritance from Proto-Indo-Iranian religion. Comparative philologists further connect the formula to the Roman *Neptūnus* and the Celtic *Nechtán* (both "nephew/descendant of the waters"), making Apām Napāt a node in one of the most far-reaching Indo-European theonymic patterns.
