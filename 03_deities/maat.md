# Ma'at

Ma'at is both an Egyptian goddess and the central theological *concept* of Egyptian religion — *mꜣꜥt* designates **cosmic order, truth, justice, and right balance** as a single integrated principle. She is daughter of [[ra]], depicted as a seated woman with an ostrich feather on her head (or just the feather alone as her hieroglyph). The pharaoh's primary duty is to maintain ma'at against the encroaching disorder (*isfet*); every temple ritual is conceived as "presenting ma'at" — the offering of cosmic order back to the gods who sustain it. At the **weighing of the heart** in the Hall of Two Truths ([[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]] Spell 125), the deceased's heart is placed on one pan of the scales and Ma'at's feather on the other; a heart heavier than the feather has accumulated injustice and the deceased is consumed by Ammit.

## Mythological role
Ma'at has little narrative mythology — she is more a *condition of the cosmos* than a character. Her role is structural and pervasive: every pharaonic inscription declares the king to be "beloved of Ma'at" or "the one who establishes Ma'at"; every offering scene includes the small figure of Ma'at as the implicit "real" content of the offering. The negative confession of BD 125 ("I have not done X..." for each of forty-two sins) is a recitation of *ma'at*-conformity: the deceased lists the offenses against cosmic order he has not committed. Assmann (1990, 2001) argues that ma'at is the central organizing category of Egyptian religion — more fundamental than any single deity — and that its disappearance from later Egyptian religious thought (under Christian and Islamic eras) marks the genuine end of pharaonic religion as a coherent system.
