# Nane

## Identity

**Nane** (Armenian: *Nané*) is the goddess of war and wisdom in the pre-Christian Armenian pantheon — the daughter of the sky-father [[aramazd]] and the Armenian equivalent of [[athena]]. She is one of the two principal female deities of the Armenian pantheon, paired with the love-goddess [[anahit-armenian]] in a structure that directly mirrors the Greek pairing of Athena and Aphrodite as daughters of Zeus.

## Sources

Our primary evidence for Nane comes from **Agathangelos** (5th century CE), whose *History of the Armenians* recounts Gregory the Illuminator's conversion of King Tiridates III and the destruction of pagan temples in 301 CE. The account of Gregory destroying the statue of Nane at **Thil** in Ekeleats province is both a condemnation of paganism and an inadvertent preservation of the Armenian divine hierarchy.

## The Athena Identification

Greek and later Armenian-Greek authors identified Nane directly with Athena: warrior goddess, daughter of the sky-father, protector of wisdom and armed conflict. The structural match is tight — Nane carries spear and shield like Athena, her worship was centered on a major temple-precinct, and she occupies the war-wisdom slot in the Armenian pantheon exactly as Athena does in the Greek.

## Armenian Pantheon Position

Within the Armenian divine family rooted in [[aramazd]]:
- **[[vahagn]]** — son, thunder-hero, dragon-slayer (= Verethragna / Heracles)
- **[[anahit-armenian]]** — daughter, love-fertility goddess (= Aphrodite)
- **Nane** — daughter, war-wisdom goddess (= Athena)
- **[[astghik]]** — associated goddess of love and Venus-star
