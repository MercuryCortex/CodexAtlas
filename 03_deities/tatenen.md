# Tatenen

## Identity

Tatenen ("the risen land," *tȝ-ṯnn*) is the Egyptian primordial earth-god of Memphis — the personification of the **first land to emerge from the primordial waters** of [[nun]]. He is the dry ground rising out of the watery abyss at the dawn of creation, and by extension a god of the fertile silt deposited by the Nile's flood and of the mineral and vegetable wealth that lies within the earth. Androgynous and primeval, he is called "mother and father of the gods."

## Mythological role

Tatenen's central significance is cosmogonic and Memphite. In Egyptian creation thought, the decisive first event is the **emergence of the primeval mound** from the waters of Nun — the first solid ground on which creation can proceed. At Heliopolis this mound is the *benben* on which [[atum]] stands to create; at Memphis it is divinized as Tatenen, the risen land itself. Memphite theology then fused Tatenen with the great craftsman-creator [[ptah]] to form **Ptah-Tatenen**: the creator who is at once the emerged earth and the divine mind that shapes the world. In this composite, Tatenen supplies the material, chthonic, earth-rising aspect of creation, while Ptah supplies the intellectual, fashioning aspect (creation by the heart's thought and the tongue's command).

## Cross-tradition significance

Tatenen is the Egyptian personification of the **emergence of dry land from the primordial waters** — one of the most widespread of all cosmogonic motifs. The image of the first solid ground rising out of the watery deep parallels the "dry land" that appears when the waters are gathered in Genesis 1:9, and the earth-diver and primeval-hillock cosmogonies found across many cultures. Fused with Ptah, he also belongs to the distinctively Egyptian theology of **creation by divine intelligence and word** (see [[hu-sia]], [[heka-egyptian]]), making the Memphite Ptah-Tatenen one of the richest ancient anticipations of a creator who makes the world by mind and speech.
