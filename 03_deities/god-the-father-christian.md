# God the Father

The first person of the Christian Trinity, identified with YHWH of the Hebrew Bible and worshipped as the creator, sovereign, and judge. In Christian theology the Father is the *fons et origo* of the Trinity: the Son is eternally begotten of the Father, and the Spirit eternally proceeds (in Western theology, from the Father AND the Son — the *filioque*; in Eastern theology, from the Father alone — a fault line of the [[event-great-schism-1054]]).

The doctrine of the Father is inseparable from the Christology of [[jesus-christ-deity]] and the pneumatology of [[holy-spirit]]: defining the Father against the Arian denial of the Son's full divinity is the work of [[event-council-of-nicaea-325]] and [[athanasius]]. Sethian and Valentinian Gnostic systems (see [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]]) make a sharp distinction the Catholic tradition rejects: the OT creator is identified as the ignorant Yaldabaoth, while the true Father — strictly unknowable — is identified with [[the-monad-gnostic]].

Visually the Father is most often represented (against early aniconic resistance) as an ancient-of-days figure following Daniel 7:9; in Byzantine and Russian Orthodox tradition, depictions of the Father are doctrinally restricted.
