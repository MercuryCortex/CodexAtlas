# Aya

## Identity

Aya (Sumerian **Šerida**) is the Mesopotamian goddess of the dawn and of sexual love, best known as the consort of the sun-god [[utu-shamash]]. As the dawn-light, she is the radiance that accompanies the rising sun; as a goddess of love and fecundity, she is the bride (*kallatu*) celebrated in hymns alongside her husband. Her principal cult-centers were the great Shamash temples at Sippar (the Ebabbar) and Larsa, where she was worshipped beside the sun-god.

## Mythological role and cult

Aya's defining function in cult is **intercession**: as the beloved bride of the sun-god — the divine judge who sees all — she is repeatedly invoked to entreat Shamash on the worshipper's behalf, to "speak a favorable word" so that the just judge will look kindly on the petitioner. This intercessory role made her a frequently named goddess in prayers and in the dedicatory inscriptions of kings, and her marriage to Shamash was celebrated in sacred-marriage liturgy. Her association with the dawn ties her to themes of renewal, the daily rebirth of light, and the fecund power of the morning.

## Cross-tradition significance

Aya belongs to the Mesopotamian **dawn-and-love goddess** complex, overlapping at its edges with the morning-star aspect of [[ishtar-akkadian]] (Venus). The pairing of a sun-god with a dawn-bride, and the figure of the divine consort who intercedes with a stern judging god, are widely paralleled motifs; Aya is the Mesopotamian witness to the dawn-goddess type that recurs (under very different names) across Indo-European and Near Eastern theology.
