# Kʼawiil

K'awiil (Classic Maya *K'awiil*; "God K"; Yucatec **Bolon Dzacab**, "Nine Generations") is the Maya god of **lightning, royal lineage, and abundance** — one of the most important deities of Classic-period kingship. He is unmistakable in art: a god with a long upturned snout, a **smoking axe, torch, or obsidian mirror piercing his forehead** (the lightning-strike), and **one leg that transforms into a serpent**. That serpent-leg makes him the very emblem of dynastic continuity — Maya kings are repeatedly shown grasping the **"Manikin Scepter,"** a small effigy of K'awiil whose serpent-foot the ruler holds, declaring himself the legitimate channel of the lineage's divine descent and its lightning-power. His Yucatec name **Bolon Dzacab** ("Nine Generations / countless lineage") underlines his role as the god of **descent and increase** — of royal bloodlines, of sustenance, of the maize and wealth that flow from a legitimate king. K'awiil thus binds together three things at the core of Maya civilization: the **storm**, the **king**, and the **harvest**.
