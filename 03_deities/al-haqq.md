# Al-Haqq (The Real)

## Identity
*Al-Ḥaqq* — "the Real" / "the True" — is one of the 99 Beautiful Names of Allah in Qur'anic and Sunni tradition, but Sufi metaphysical theology, especially the Akbarī school of Ibn ʿArabī, gives the name distinctive technical force. In Ibn ʿArabī's *waḥdat al-wujūd* (oneness of being), *al-Ḥaqq* names God *qua* absolute Reality — that which truly is, as distinct from the *khalq* (creation), the merely apparent multiplicity which is the Real's self-manifestation (*tajallī*). The Sufi *ʿārif* (knower) realizes that the multiplicity of created things is, properly seen, the differentiated self-disclosure of *al-Ḥaqq*; nothing in creation has independent being. The famous (and theologically dangerous) ecstatic utterance of al-Ḥallāj — "*Anā al-Ḥaqq*" ("I am the Real / I am the Truth"), for which he was executed in 922 — was reread by later Sufis through Ibn ʿArabian metaphysics as: in the moment of *fanāʾ* (annihilation of the self), what speaks is the Real, since only the Real really is.

## Earliest attestation
The name *al-Ḥaqq* in the Qur'an (e.g., 22:6, 22:62, 24:25, 31:30): "Allah is the Real (*al-Ḥaqq*) and what they call upon besides Him is the false (*al-bāṭil*)" — already with metaphysical-ontological force. Sufi technical development: al-Ḥallāj's *Anā al-Ḥaqq* (~922); Ibn ʿArabī's systematic *waḥdat al-wujūd* (~1230).

## Cross-tradition identifications

- Plotinian One — *scholarly-parallel*: the absolute beyond differentiation.

## Disputes
- **Heresy charge.** Whether Ibn ʿArabī's *waḥdat al-wujūd* is pantheist heresy (Ibn Taymiyya, Salafism) or panentheist orthodoxy (mainstream Sufi tradition through Ottoman and Persianate Islam) — the perennial Islamic theological dispute.
- **Al-Ḥallāj.** Was al-Ḥallāj's *Anā al-Ḥaqq* claim genuinely heretical or merely ecstatically misunderstood? Massignon (1922) reads him as orthodox; some Sunni traditions agree; others continue to treat the execution as justified.
