# Ṛṣabha (Adinatha)

## Identity
Ṛṣabha (Sanskrit: "Bull"), also called **Adinatha** ("First Lord") and **Ādīśvara** ("First God"), is the **first Tirthankara** of the current Jain cosmic cycle — the primordial Ford-Maker whose teaching initiated the current era of Jain liberation history. In Jain cosmology, Rishabha lived in a period so remote as to be effectively mythological (the Jain cosmic time-scale dwarfs even Hindu cosmological time); his biographical dates are not reckoned historically but cosmologically. He is the **founder of human civilization** in Jain tradition: before Rishabha, humanity lived in a golden-age paradise where desire-trees (*kalpavrikshas*) provided everything without effort and there was no need for agriculture, writing, or social organization. As the cosmic cycle descended from its golden apex, these natural provisions failed; it was Rishabha who taught humanity **agriculture, cooking, pottery, writing, mathematics, and the complete structure of social organization** (including the institution of marriage) — making him the divine source of all civilizational arts.

His emblem among the 24 Tirthankaras is the **bull** (Sanskrit: *rishabha*); his most striking iconographic feature is **long rope-like matted hair** that falls to his shoulders — marking him as primordial, pre-shaving, the most ancient of all teachers. He attained liberation (*moksha*) at Mount Ashtapada (identified with the Himalayas in Jain tradition).

His son **Bharata** became the first *chakravartin* (world-monarch) — from whose name the Indian subcontinent is called **Bharatavarsha** in Jain accounts. His other son **Bahubali** attained liberation while standing in meditation so long that vines grew up his body — his colossal standing meditation statue at **Shravanabelagola** (Karnataka), 18 meters tall, is one of the largest monolithic statues in the world.

## Earliest attestation
- **Rig Veda 10.166** (c. 1000 BCE): mentions a *rishabha* in a context that some Jain scholars identify as a reference to the first Tirthankara; mainstream scholarship treats this as a bull-symbolism reference rather than a specific deity attestation.
- **Kalpa Sutra** (c. 3rd c. BCE): first canonical Jain listing of Rishabha as the first of 24 Tirthankaras.
- **Adipurana** (Jinasena, c. 870 CE): the major Jain Puranic biography of Rishabha, detailing his civilizational teachings.
- **Bhagavata Purana, Book 5** (c. 9th–10th c. CE): Hindu appropriation of Rishabha as a Vishnu avatar — the most significant cross-tradition attestation.

## Mythological role
Rishabha's mythological role has two dimensions:

1. **Civilizational founder.** In a world where the natural cosmic abundance has failed, Rishabha teaches humanity everything it needs to survive and organize itself: agriculture, food preparation, pottery, the social institutions of marriage and family, writing and counting, and the arts. He is the complete culture-hero — the deity who gives humanity its total civilizational package in a single teaching event.

2. **First Ford-Maker.** As the first Tirthankara, Rishabha rediscovers and re-teaches the eternal Jain dharma at the beginning of the current cosmic cycle. He establishes the four-fold community (*chaturvidha-sangha*) of monks, nuns, laymen, and laywomen that is the institutional backbone of Jainism. His personal liberation by radical asceticism — ultimately standing in *kayotsarga* (complete bodily renunciation posture) until attaining omniscience — is the exemplary act that all subsequent Tirthankaras replicate.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[vishnu]]** — *folk-syncretism*: the Bhagavata Purana (Book 5, chapters 3–6) incorporates Rishabha as an avatar of Vishnu — the Brahmanical tradition's systematic strategy of absorbing non-Vedic teachers by reframing them as avatars. The Buddha was subjected to the same move. This is one of the clearest documented cases of Brahmanical avatar-doctrine functioning as a tool of religious competition (Dundas 2002).
- **[[prometheus]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the civilization-giver who brings humanity its complete technical and social toolkit. Rishabha teaches writing + agriculture + cooking + social organization; Prometheus brings fire + all the technical arts (*technai*).
- **[[enki-ea]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Enki as possessor and dispenser of the *me* (all civilizational arts and divine decrees that constitute ordered human life). Both Enki and Rishabha are the divine source of everything that makes civilization possible.
- **[[thoth]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Thoth as inventor of writing, mathematics, and all learning; Rishabha specifically teaches writing and mathematics as part of his civilizational gift.
- **[[quetzalcoatl]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Mesoamerican culture-hero who gives humanity maize, the calendar, writing, and civilization. The structure of the divine teacher who gifts humanity its complete civilizational package is cross-culturally distributed.

## Iconography
Rishabha's canonical iconography:
- **Bull emblem** (his identifying symbol among the 24 Tirthankaras — depicted on his throne or pedestal).
- **Long rope-like matted hair** (unique among the Tirthankaras; markers of his primordial, pre-shaved antiquity).
- **Standing in *kayotsarga*** (complete renunciation posture: standing, arms by sides, eyes downcast, absolutely motionless).
- **Seated in padmasana** (lotus posture) in temple cult images.
- The **Gomateshvara / Bahubali statue** at Shravanabelagola (18 m, c. 981 CE) — while depicting Bahubali, not Rishabha himself, it is the architectural monument of Rishabha's family and the most famous Jain sacred image.

## Disputes
- **Rig Veda attestation.** Jain scholars have claimed the *rishabha* in RV 10.166 as the earliest attestation of Rishabha-worship; mainstream Vedic scholarship rejects this as a forced reading of a bull-symbolism passage.
- **Bhagavata Purana appropriation.** Whether the Hindu inclusion of Rishabha as a Vishnu avatar was intended as syncretism, absorption, or theological competition is debated. Dundas 2002 treats it as Brahmanical competitive absorption — an attempt to subordinate the Jain first Tirthankara within the Vaishnava framework, paralleling the Buddha's avatar-ization.
- **Civilizational teachings: historical or cosmological?** The account of Rishabha teaching agriculture, writing, and social organization is presented as mythological-cosmological (he lived billions of years ago); some modern Jain scholars have tried to connect Rishabha with IVC (Indus Valley Civilization) archaeology — a claim mainstream scholarship does not support.
