# Yarikh

## Identity

Yarikh (Ugaritic **Yariḫ**) is the Canaanite moon-god — the personified moon and its monthly cycle, "the illuminator of the heavens." The common Semitic word for "moon" and "month" (*yrḥ*) is his name, and he is the West-Semitic counterpart of the Mesopotamian moon-god [[nanna-sin]]. His consort is [[nikkal-akkadian]] (Nikkal, from Sumerian Ningal, "great lady"), the moon-god's traditional bride.

## Mythological role

Yarikh's principal text is the Ugaritic poem usually titled *The Marriage of Yarikh and Nikkal* (KTU 1.24), a hymn that recounts the moon-god's wooing and wedding of Nikkal — paying the bride-price to her father, with the birth-goddesses the [[kotharat]] (the swallow-like goddesses of conception and marriage) in attendance. The poem appears to have served a liturgical function in human marriage rites, the divine wedding providing the template for earthly ones. Elsewhere Yarikh appears in the pantheon lists and ritual texts of Ugarit as a regular recipient of offerings.

## Cross-tradition significance

Yarikh belongs to the shared ancient Near Eastern **moon-theology**: a male lunar god with the consort Nikkal/Ningal, paralleling Mesopotamian Nanna/Sin and his wife Ningal. His name is also embedded in the landscape of the Bible: the city of **Jericho** (Hebrew *Yərîḥô*) is widely understood to preserve the name of the moon-god, marking it as an old lunar cult-center — one of several Canaanite divine names (with [[shalim]] in Jerusalem) fossilized in the toponymy the Israelites inherited.
