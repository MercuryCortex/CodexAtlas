# Ishara

## Identity
**Ishara** (Akkadian *Išḫara*; Hurrian / Hittite *Išḫara*) is the principal **scorpion-goddess** of the Mesopotamian / Hurrian / Hittite religious zone, attested continuously from the third millennium BCE (Ebla and Mari archives) through the mid-first millennium BCE. Her divine emblem is the **scorpion**, and her central theological function is **oath-binding**: in Mesopotamian and Hurrian treaty-formulae she is invoked among the gods who guarantee the inviolability of sworn agreements. *Kudurru* boundary-stones (Kassite-period Babylonian land-grant monuments) regularly carry the scorpion among the divine emblems that magically enforce the grant. Ishara also has erotic / midwifery aspects in some traditions; the Hurrian goddess is associated with the netherworld in Hittite contexts.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[selket]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Egyptian Selket / Serqet is the structural counterpart in Egyptian religion (canopic-shrine protectress, venom-master, nurse of Horus). The parallel is **independent semantic isomorphism**, not documented transmission. Both traditions independently developed scorpion-as-feminine-divine-emblem from the same ecological reality. Black & Green 1992 p. 110; Wilkinson 2003 p. 233.
- **[[scorpion]]** (symbol-node) — Ishara is one of the canonical attestations in the cross-tradition scorpion-symbol cluster.
