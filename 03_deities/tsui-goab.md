# Tsui-Goab

Tsui-Goab (*Tsui-||Goab*, often glossed "**Wounded Knee**") is the great god of the **Khoikhoi (Nama)** peoples of southern Africa — a deity of **rain, thunder, and storm-clouds**, and so of the **health, prosperity, and life** that the rains bring to a dry land. He is invoked at the **red dawn**, facing east, as a benevolent ancestral hero-god: tradition holds he was a mighty **ancestor and chief** who, after death, became the supreme power, the giver of rain and the healer of the sick. His epithet comes from a foundational myth of **cosmic conflict**: Tsui-Goab does perpetual battle with **Gaunab**, the malevolent being who brings sickness and death, and though he repeatedly **defeats** Gaunab he is **wounded in the knee** in the struggle — the wound preserved in his name. Recorded in detail by the 19th-century scholar Theophilus Hahn, Tsui-Goab is one of the best-documented indigenous high gods of southern Africa, structurally akin to the Bantu rain-and-sky high gods **[[leza|Leza]]** and Mulungu of the same region, though belonging to the distinct **Khoe** (Khoikhoi) tradition. He embodies the southern African vision of the supreme being as rain-giver, healer, and victorious — if wounded — champion against the powers of death.
