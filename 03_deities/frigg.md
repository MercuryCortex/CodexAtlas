# Frigg

## Identity
Queen of the Æsir, wife of Óðinn, mother of Baldr. Frigg presides over marriage, motherhood, household, and possesses comprehensive foreknowledge (*forspár*) — she knows the fates of all but speaks them to no one. The Friday weekday name in English derives from her name (*Frīgedæg*); some other Germanic languages may preserve the alternative from Freyja, and the Frigg/Freyja relation is philologically contested.

## Earliest attestation
Old English (*Frīg* in royal genealogies); Old High German (*Friia* in the Merseburg charms, ~9th c.); Old Norse extensive literary attestation.

## Mythological role
The Baldr-death narrative: when Baldr begins to have ominous dreams of his death, Frigg exacts an oath from every being and substance not to harm him — except, fatefully, from the mistletoe, which she thought too young to bother with. Loki learns this; manipulates blind Hǫðr to throw a mistletoe-dart at the now-invulnerable Baldr; Baldr dies. Frigg sends Hermóðr to Hel to attempt to ransom Baldr; Hel agrees if every being in the cosmos will weep for him; one giantess (Þǫkk, probably Loki in disguise) refuses; Baldr remains in Hel.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[freyja]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Jacob Grimm (1844) and others have argued the two were originally one goddess; current consensus treats them as distinct but related Norse-Germanic feminine figures.

## Iconography
Distaff (spinning); hall *Fensalir* ("Fen-halls"); handmaidens.
