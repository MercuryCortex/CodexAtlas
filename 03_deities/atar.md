# Atar

## Identity
Atar (Avestan *Ātar*; Pahlavi-Persian *Ātaš* or *Adur*) is **the personified divine fire of Zoroastrian theology** — son of [[ahura-mazda]], the **visible-divine-presence** at the heart of every Zoroastrian temple-cult, the **agent of cosmic purification**, and the **ever-burning sacred fire** that has been ritually maintained in some Zoroastrian temples for **more than 1,500 years of unbroken combustion**. The current oldest continuously-burning Atar is the **Ataš Bahrām of Yazd, Iran** (the temple's principal fire is reported to have been continuously maintained since ~+470 CE, though parts of the institutional chain are folkloric). Atar is **directly cognate with the Vedic Agni** — both are inherited from Proto-Indo-Iranian as the personified divine-fire. For the Codex Atlas mission Atar is load-bearing as the **central cult-object of an entire religion**: Zoroastrian religion is sometimes mis-described as "fire-worship" by outsiders (it is not — Zoroastrians worship Ahura Mazda, with Atar as the visible-divine-presence facilitating that worship), but the centrality of the sacred fire is real and universally-attested.

## Earliest attestation
- **Gathas** ([[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]]): Yasna 31.3, 31.19, 34.4, 43.4, 43.9 — fire-as-divine-presence and as the means by which the cosmic order is upheld.
- **Younger Avesta**: extensively. **Yasna 62 (the Ataš Niyāyišn)** — the principal liturgical hymn to Atar, recited daily by every Zoroastrian.
- **Achaemenid period (-550 to -330)**: fire-altars on royal Achaemenid coinage; rock-relief depictions of the king praying before sacred fire (Naqsh-e Rustam, Persepolis). The Achaemenids systematically institutionalize fire-cult as state-religious practice.
- **Sasanian period (+224 to +651)**: the **three grades of consecrated fire** crystallize: **Ataš Bahrām** (Victory-fire, the highest grade — combined from 16 source-fires, year-long ritual purification), **Ataš Adaran** (intermediate grade), and **Ataš Dadgah** (household grade).

## Mythological role
- **Son of Ahura Mazda.** Atar is theologically a divine being subordinate to Ahura Mazda — the visible-divine-presence that mediates the cosmic-divine order to human worshippers.
- **Cosmic-purifier.** All offerings, all priests, and (at the end of time) all souls pass through fire as purification. The river-of-molten-metal at the end-of-time judgment (see [[saoshyant]], [[frashokereti-cosmic-renovation]]) is Atar's eschatological-cosmic form.
- **Daily-prayer focus.** Every Zoroastrian prayer is directed *toward* (but not *to*) the sacred fire — the fire is the visible-divine-presence facing which one prays to Ahura Mazda.

## Cross-tradition identifications

### Atar ↔ Vedic Agni
The **direct Indo-Iranian etymological-and-functional cognate** with Vedic Agni is one of the clearest shared-Indo-Iranian-theology cases:
- Both are **personified divine fire**.
- Both are **the means by which sacrificial offerings reach the gods** (Agni in Vedic *yajña*; Atar in Zoroastrian *yasna* — note the etymological cognate of the ritual-words themselves).
- Both are **central to temple/household cult** (the household hearth is divine in both traditions).
- Both have a **wide range of cult-applications** — domestic, sacrificial, purificatory, mortuary, royal-state.

The cognate-deity-pair Atar-Agni is one of the most-load-bearing pieces of Proto-Indo-Iranian religious-reconstruction.

### Atar and other fire-religions
- **Greek-Roman hearth-fire** (Vesta, Hestia, the sacred state-fire of Rome and Athens): structurally parallel cult-of-the-ever-burning-fire; no documented direct Persian-influence on Greek-Roman institutional-fire-cult (the parallel is Indo-European broadly, not Iranian-specific).
- **Christian altar-light (vigil lamp, sanctuary lamp).** Christian liturgical eternal-light tradition has structural parallels with Zoroastrian fire-temple practice; direct genealogical influence is unprovable but the structural resonance is real.
- **Jewish *ner tamid*** (the perpetually-burning eternal lamp in synagogues, Exodus 27:20–21): Hebrew Bible eternal-light commandment may have absorbed Persian-period Zoroastrian influence (the institutionalization of the *ner tamid* is post-exilic, in the Persian period).

## Iconography
- **Sasanian rock-reliefs**: fire-altars with the king-figure praying before them. Naqsh-e Rustam, Naqsh-e Rajab.
- **Achaemenid coinage**: fire-altar with two priestly attendants — a recurring iconographic-type.
- **Modern Parsi**: the temple-fire itself is iconographic-central; no anthropomorphic depiction. The famous *Ataš Bahrām of Yazd* and the Ataš Bahrāms of Mumbai, Surat, and Udwada (India) are pilgrimage sites.

## Disputes
- **Atar-Agni direction of derivation.** Standard view: both inherit from Proto-Indo-Iranian. Some older scholarship argued Iranian Atar borrowed from Vedic Agni; this is not the dominant view.
- **Continuous-combustion claims.** Several Atar Bahrāms claim continuous combustion across 1,500+ years (Yazd: ~+470 onward; others claim later but still 700+ years). The institutional-chain claims are partly folkloric and not always fully documentable, but the *practice* of careful continuous maintenance is real and remarkable.
- **"Fire-worship" mis-characterization.** Zoroastrians **do not worship fire** — they worship Ahura Mazda *facing* the sacred fire, which is the divine-visible-presence. The mis-characterization is a persistent outsider-prejudice (Greek, Roman, medieval Islamic, modern Western) that Zoroastrian apologists have consistently rejected.
