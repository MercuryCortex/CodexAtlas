# The Maruts

## Identity

The Maruts are a **troop** of Vedic storm-gods — a band of brilliant young warriors of the sky, all of one age and one splendor, who ride the winds amid thunder and lightning and bring the violent rains. They are the **sons of [[rudra-shiva-early]]** (and so are also called the *Rudras*) and of Prishni, the dappled cloud-cow. Numbering variously (commonly given as thrice-sixty, or seven troops of seven), they act always as a collective.

## Mythological role

The Maruts are above all the **companions of [[indra]]**. They ride in golden chariots drawn by spotted deer, armed with golden axes and lightning-spears, and as they rush across the sky they make the mountains tremble, split the trees, and loose the rains. In the central Vedic myth — Indra's slaying of the dragon Vritra to release the cosmic waters — the Maruts roar at Indra's side and lend him their strength, the storm-host backing the storm-king (though one hymn tells of a quarrel in which Indra, grown proud, briefly slights them). Fierce and beneficent at once, they bring both the destructive fury and the life-giving rain of the monsoon storm.

## Cross-tradition significance

The Maruts are the Vedic instance of the **divine storm-host / wild band of sky-warriors** — the collective of young storm-gods who ride the tempest. Scholars of Indo-European myth connect them to the broader pattern of the divine warrior-band (the *Männerbund* / the "furious host"), with comparanda in the Germanic Wild Hunt and the war-bands of other Indo-European traditions. As the storm-troop allied to the dragon-slaying Indra, they also belong to the cross-tradition **chaoskampf** complex, the host that accompanies the storm-warrior into his world-ordering battle.
