# Fufluns

## Identity
Fufluns is the **Etruscan god of wine and vegetation** — the ecstatic power of the vine, of natural growth, and of the mystery rites that promised renewal beyond death. The Etruscan city of **Populonia** (Etruscan *Fufluna*) bears his name, marking how central his cult was.

## Earliest attestation
Attested on engraved mirrors and in votive inscriptions from the 6th century BCE onward, often with his mother **Semla** (Semele) and his consort **Areatha** (Ariadne) — the Etruscan family of the Greek Dionysus.

## Mythological role — the wine-god and his mysteries
Fufluns is the vine made divine: god of intoxication, fertility, and the ecstatic cult. His Etruscan mythology mirrors that of [[dionysus]] — born of a mortal mother (Semla/Semele) and the sky-king [[tinia]], wedded to Areatha/Ariadne, surrounded by satyr-like figures. His **mystery cult** held out to initiates the hope of a happy afterlife, consonant with the deep Etruscan concern for the fate of the dead.

## Cross-tradition
Fufluns is the Etruscan instance of the **wine-and-vegetation god** — Dionysus → Fufluns → the Roman Liber/[[bacchus]] — and, as the vine that dies back and rises again with its promise of renewal, a member of the **dying-and-rising** vegetation complex.

## Iconography
A youthful god wreathed in vine and ivy, holding the thyrsus and wine-cup, attended by dancing companions.
