# Hunab Ku

A **post-Conquest Yucatec Maya supreme-being figure** — and one of the most contested deity-categories in Mesoamerican religious scholarship. The name appears in **colonial-period sources** (Landa's *Relación* 1566; the Books of Chilam Balam in colonial Yucatec Maya orthography) as *Hunab Ku* — "the only god" / "the one god" — sometimes identified as the father of [[itzamna]], sometimes as Itzamna's transcendent aspect.

**The historicity controversy.** Whether Hunab Ku represents an authentic **pre-Conquest** Yucatec supreme-deity category, or whether it is a **missionary projection** of the Christian One-God concept onto Maya religion (the missionaries needing to locate a Maya theological category they could equate with the Christian God for catechesis purposes), is sharply contested. Arguments:
- **Pro-authenticity:** the name appears in indigenous-authored colonial documents (Chilam Balam) not just in Spanish-missionary writing; some pre-Conquest Maya theological categories may have approached a supreme-being conception (cf. Itzamna).
- **Pro-projection:** the pre-Conquest Maya pantheon attested in the **Dresden and Madrid Codices** is polytheistic with no clear supreme-being category; the term *Hunab Ku* may be a 16th-century theological neologism for missionary purposes; the absence of pre-Conquest iconography for Hunab Ku is a striking gap.

**Modern non-academic appropriation.** Hunab Ku has been heavily appropriated by 20th-21st century **New Age** writers (most prominently José Argüelles, *The Mayan Factor* 1987, and the broader "Mayan calendar" industry) and by **Mexicayotl** identity-revival movements as a cosmic-source-of-everything figure. None of this New Age framing has any pre-Conquest textual or iconographic support.

This node is included in the vault not as an unproblematic Yucatec deity, but as a **contested theological category** whose existence-or-non-existence is a load-bearing question for understanding pre-Conquest Maya religion and the colonial-missionary process of religious-translation.
