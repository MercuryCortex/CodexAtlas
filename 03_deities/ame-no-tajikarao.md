# Ame-no-Tajikarao

## Identity
Ame-no-Tajikarao — "Heavenly-Hand-Strength-Male" — is the Shintō **kami of physical strength and power**, the divine muscle of the heavenly deities. Where [[omoikane]] supplies the plan, Tajikarao supplies the force to carry it out.

## Earliest attestation
Recorded in the *Kojiki* (712 CE) and *Nihon Shoki* (720 CE). His chief shrine is **Togakushi** in Nagano, named for the rock-door he is said to have thrown there; he is also a patron of sumo wrestlers and athletes.

## Mythological role — the opener of the cave
In the **Ama-no-Iwato** myth, when the dance of [[ame-no-uzume]] and the assembled gods' laughter draws [[amaterasu]] to peer out of the Heavenly Rock Cave at her own reflection in the great mirror, it is Ame-no-Tajikarao who waits hidden beside the door, **seizes her hand, and pulls her bodily out** into the world — restoring the sun. He then hurls the massive stone door far away, where it becomes a mountain. He is thus the agent of the light's physical return.

## Cross-tradition
Tajikarao is the Shintō instance of the **divine strongman** — the figure of overwhelming physical power who accomplishes the feat no one else can, kin in type to [[heracles]] in Greece and to the strongman-heroes of other traditions. The pairing of *cunning plan* ([[omoikane]]) with *brute strength* (Tajikarao) is itself a widespread mythic division of labour.

## Iconography
A powerfully-built kami gripping or hurling a great stone; associated with the torii and rock formations of Togakushi, and with sumo.
