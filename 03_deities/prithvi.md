# Pṛthivī

## Identity
Pṛthivī (Sanskrit पृथ्वी, "the Broad One," from PIE ***pl̥th₂wíh₂***) is the Vedic goddess of the earth — the ground itself, personified as a divine mother who sustains all life, holds all things in patient endurance, and partners with Dyaus Pitā (Sky Father) as one half of the primordial divine couple. The name is not metaphorical: Pṛthivī *is* the earth's breadth and flatness, the *wide-extending* quality of the ground underfoot — the same linguistic root that gives Sanskrit *pṛthu* (broad, wide) and that cognates in Greek *platus* (flat, wide), as in *Plataea* (the broad place).

The **Dyaus Pitā + Pṛthivī pair** is one of the best-attested Proto-Indo-European mythological reconstructions in comparative religion. Sky Father + Earth Mother appears across the Indo-European family: Sanskrit *Dyauṣ Pitṛ* + *Pṛthivī* = Greek *Zeus* + *Gaia* = Latin *Iuppiter* (*Diēspiter*) + *Tellus* = Norse *Odin* + *Jörð* = Hittite *Tarhunt* + earth deity. The pattern is approximately 5,000 years old. No other divine pairing in the vault is reconstructable to this antiquity with this level of philological certainty.

Her most important textual monument is the **Bhūmi Sūkta** (Atharvaveda 12.1) — with 63 verses, one of the longest single hymns in the Vedic corpus, and one of the most ecologically comprehensive: it addresses the earth as mother of all creatures, source of all food, sustainer of all stability, the one who bears all that is done upon her with patience (*kṣamā*). The prayer is addressed to the earth before anything is done *to* her — before plowing, building, digging — which makes it one of the earliest documented examples of ritual ecological acknowledgment.

In later Puranic and Vaishnava tradition, Pṛthivī merges with **Bhūmi Devī** (or Bhū Devī), who becomes one of Vishnu's two primary consorts (alongside Lakshmi/Śrī). In the Varāha avatar myth, Vishnu as the cosmic boar dives to the bottom of the cosmic ocean to rescue Bhūmi Devī (the earth) who has been seized by the demon Hiraṇyākṣa — the earth-mother requiring divine rescue from cosmic catastrophe.

## Earliest attestation
- **Rig Veda** (~-1500 to -1200 oral) — RV 1.185 is the canonical Dyaus-Pṛthivī hymn, addressing the divine couple jointly; additional references throughout as the paired cosmic parents. ([[phase-2-001-rig-veda-family-books]])
- **Atharva Veda 12.1** (~-1200 to -900) — the **Bhūmi Sūkta** (Earth Hymn), 63 verses, the most extensive single treatment of the earth goddess in any Vedic text. "O Earth, my mother, set me securely in a place well-poised; and in the sky above let me not sway" (AV 12.1.17). ([[phase-2-003-atharva-veda]])
- **Brāhmaṇas** (~-800 to -600) — earth rituals and the *bhūmi pūjā* pattern; the earth's consent before plowing or construction.
- **Vishnu Purana, Bhāgavata Purana** (~400–1000 CE) — Bhūmi Devī as Vishnu's consort; the Varāha avatar rescue; Prithvi's lament to Brahma about the earth's burden (which triggers the Vishnu avatars cycle).

## Mythological role
1. **The patient supporter.** Pṛthivī holds all things — mountains, oceans, forests, cities, all human activity — with inexhaustible patience. The epithet *kṣamā* (the patient, the forgiving) captures this: she endures everything done upon her. The Bhūmi Sūkta asks her to bear the weight of what the worshipper does, to sustain and not crack.

2. **The primordial couple.** Dyaus + Pṛthivī are the sky-earth pair that generate the cosmic order; they are rarely separated in early Vedic hymns, addressed as *dyāvāpṛthivī* (a compound dual). Their union/separation is implicit in Vedic cosmology: the sky and earth were once joined and were separated at creation — an almost universal creation pattern (Rangi and Papa in Maori tradition; Geb and Nut in Egypt; An and Ki in Sumerian).

3. **Earth as the trigger for divine intervention.** In Puranic mythology, when the weight of evil becomes too great for Pṛthivī/Bhūmi to bear, she appeals to the gods — and this appeal triggers Vishnu's avatars. Earth's suffering is the *cause* of divine descent. Every Vishnu avatar is ultimately a response to earth's prayer.

4. **Ritual ecological acknowledgment.** The Bhūmi Pūjā (earth-worship) before any construction — breaking ground, digging, plowing — is a living ritual practice that descends directly from the Atharva Vedic tradition. One apologizes to the earth before disturbing her.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[gaia]]** — *scholarly-parallel* (PIE cognate): Greek Gaia is the earth-mother; paired with Uranos (sky) who parallels Dyaus. The Greek Zeus (< PIE *Dyauṣ*) + Gaia pair is cognate at the name level (Watkins 1995; Macdonell 1897). This is not just structural parallel but linguistically demonstrable shared inheritance.
- **[[ranginui]]** + **[[papatuanuku]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Maori sky father (Ranginui) + earth mother (Papatuanuku) locked in embrace, separated by their children — the most structurally identical parallel to the Dyaus-Pṛthivī pair outside the Indo-European family. Independent convergence or very early Pacific transmission.
- **[[ki-sumerian]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Sumerian Ki (earth) paired with An (sky, An = Dyaus cognate?); sky-earth divine pair in Mesopotamia; independent of PIE but structurally identical.
- **[[ninhursag]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Sumerian earth/mother-goddess; the fertility-earth complex, parallel to Pṛthivī as life-sustainer.
- **[[pachamama]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Andean Pachamama is the earth-mother who receives offerings before plowing and construction — the closest functional parallel to the Bhūmi Pūjā tradition outside the Indo-European world. Independent convergence of the "ask the earth's permission before disturbing her" ritual logic.
- **[[asase-yaa]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Akan (West African) earth goddess; parallel earth-mother theology; independent.

**The Bhūmi Pūjā / Earth-permission ritual pattern** is one of the vault's most striking cross-cultural convergences:
- Vedic Bhūmi Pūjā before construction → Roman Terminalia (boundary-god propitiation before moving stones) → Australian Aboriginal smoking ceremonies before building → Andean *despacho* offerings to Pachamama before plowing. Four independent traditions performing the same ritual logic: *ask the earth's permission / forgiveness before disturbing her*.

## Iconography
- Golden or dark complexion; matronly form.
- Seated on a lotus; carrying a pot of grain, fruit, or flowers.
- In Vaishnava iconography as Bhūmi Devī: paired with Lakshmi flanking Vishnu; holding a lotus, crowned, royal bearing.
- In Varāha iconography: rescued by the boar-form Vishnu, she clings to his tusk as he lifts her from the cosmic ocean.

## Disputes
- **Pṛthivī vs. Bhūmi Devī**: Are these the same deity in different phases, or should they be separate nodes? The Vedic Pṛthivī is cosmic-mother and earth-sustainer; the Puranic Bhūmi Devī is specifically Vishnu's consort and a Vaishnava theological construct. This node treats them as one with phase evolution; separate-node splitting may be warranted as the vault expands Vaishnava theology.
- **Dyaus's eclipse**: Unlike the Greek Zeus (who retained supreme sovereignty), Vedic Dyaus almost entirely disappears from the post-Vedic Hindu pantheon, taking Pṛthivī with him from prominence. Why the sky-father/earth-mother pair collapsed in Hindu tradition while surviving in Greek religion is unexplained.
- **Scope of the PIE reconstruction**: The Dyaus-Pṛthivī = Zeus-Gaia equation is accepted in comparative linguistics; how much mythology (beyond the names) was shared is debated.
