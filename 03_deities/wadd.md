# Wadd

## Identity
Wadd (Arabic *Wadd*, ود; Old South Arabian / Minaean *Wdm* with the -m mimation) is the **principal high god of the Minaean kingdom** (Maʿīn, north-central Yemen, c. -800 to -100), a South Arabian moon-god with extensive Sabaean and broader South Arabian attestation. His name preserves the Semitic *w-d-d* "love / friendship / affection" root (cf. Arabic *wadūd* "loving" — one of the Islamic *al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā* "beautiful names of God"; the Northwest-Semitic personal-name element *Yedid* / *Dawid* "beloved / David"). He is **uniquely preserved in the Quran at 71:23 (Sūrat Nūḥ)** as one of the five gods named as worshipped in Noah's time — *Wadd, Suwāʿ, Yaghūth, Yaʿūq, Nasr* — the Quranic "antediluvian pantheon" recasting these South Arabian / Hijazi gods as the gods of Noah's polytheist contemporaries, with the implicit Islamic-tradition narrative (developed in Ibn al-Kalbi) that these five idols passed from Noah's time through to the immediate-pre-Islamic Arabian sanctuaries and were re-discovered / re-erected by ʿAmr ibn Luḥayy at Mecca. (Hoyland 2001; Hawting 1999; Beeston Sabaic dictionary.)

## Earliest attestation
- **Minaean and Sabaean inscriptions** (c. -800 onward) — Wadd is the principal high god of the Minaean kingdom (capital Maʿīn / Qarnāwu in north-central Yemen); attested as *Wdm* in dedicatory inscriptions, oath-formulas, and royal-titulary epigraphy. The Minaean trading network across the Hadramaut frankincense routes carries Wadd's cult into broader South Arabian and even some North Arabian contexts.
- **Quran 71:23 (Sūrat Nūḥ)** (~610-632 CE) — the principal Islamic-period textual attestation, naming Wadd in the antediluvian pantheon. ([[phase-4-034-quran]])
- **Ibn al-Kalbi *Kitāb al-Aṣnām* (~9th c. CE)** — Islamic-tradition reconstructive description: Wadd as cult-image of the **Banu Kalb tribe at Dūmat al-Jandal** (northern Hijaz / southern Syria), described as a male standing-figure with armor, sword, and bow. Use source-critically per Hawting 1999.
- **Safaitic / Thamudic graffiti** — North Arabian personal-name attestation in the *Wdd*-element (theophoric names — "servant of Wadd").

## Mythological role
1. **Minaean high god.** Wadd is the principal deity of the Minaean kingdom in northern Yemen — a South Arabian moon-god of the broader Almaqah / Sīn / Wadd / ʿAmm regional family of South Arabian moon-deities, each associated with a particular South Arabian kingdom.
2. **Father-god / patron of oaths.** The *w-d-d* "love / affection" root; the Minaean royal epithet *Wadd-ab* "Wadd-the-father" presents him in protective-paternal aspect.
3. **Antediluvian-pantheon-name in the Quran.** Quran 71:23 places Wadd in the mouth of Noah's polytheist contemporaries: "They said: do not abandon your gods, do not abandon Wadd, nor Suwāʿ, nor Yaghūth, nor Yaʿūq, nor Nasr." The Islamic-tradition reception narrative (Ibn al-Kalbi) bridges this Quranic antediluvian framing to immediate-pre-Islamic Arabian cult by claiming these five gods passed from Noah's contemporaries through to Arabian sanctuaries.
4. **Banu Kalb cult at Dūmat al-Jandal.** Per Ibn al-Kalbi, the immediate-pre-Islamic Wadd-cult was located at the northern Hijazi / southern Syrian oasis-town of Dūmat al-Jandal under the Banu Kalb tribe. Whether this north-Hijazi Wadd-cult is continuous with the older south Arabian Minaean Wadd-cult or is a late-antique north-Arabian appropriation of the South Arabian name is unclear.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Almaqah (Sabaean) / Sīn (Hadramaut) / ʿAmm (Qatabanian)** — close South Arabian moon-deity cluster; each is the principal deity of its respective Yemeni kingdom.
- **Northwest-Semitic moon-god type** — Yaraḥ (Ugaritic), Akkadian Sîn (Ur, Harran); Wadd shares the astral-functional class.
- **No firm ancient identifications with Greek / Roman / Iranian deities.**

## Iconography
- **Crescent moon** — astral-iconographic association, consistent with the South Arabian moon-god type. ([[crescent-moon-star]])
- **Snake** — Wadd's sacred animal in some Minaean iconographic contexts; a serpent appears on coins and stelae associated with him.
- **Anthropomorphic standing-figure with armor / sword / bow** — per Ibn al-Kalbi's description of the Banu Kalb cult-image at Dūmat al-Jandal. Islamic-tradition reconstructive description; use source-critically.

## Disputes
- **Continuity of the south Arabian Minaean Wadd-cult with the north Arabian Banu Kalb / Dūmat al-Jandal Wadd-cult.** Mainstream view: probable continuity via the Minaean trading network's northward reach. Alternative: the north-Arabian Wadd-cult is a late-antique appropriation of the older South Arabian name with potentially different cultic content. (Hoyland 2001.)
- **The antediluvian-pantheon framing of Quran 71:23.** The Quranic placement of Wadd / Suwāʿ / Yaghūth / Yaʿūq / Nasr in Noah's polytheist contemporaries' mouths is a theological-typological move (the unrighteousness of Noah's generation as type for unrighteousness of Muhammad's Meccan opponents). The historical content of the list is the late-antique Hijazi memory of these specific god-names; the antediluvian framing is Quranic. (Reynolds 2018.)
- **Wadd vs. Allāh in pre-Islamic Yemen.** By the late-antique period (4th-6th c. CE), the South Arabian moon-deity cults were substantially replaced or absorbed by the Himyarite Jewish-style monotheism centered on *Rahmanan* (Robin 2015), and then by Christian and Jewish overlays. The Quran 71:23 framing recalls a deeper-past Wadd-cult, not a contemporary-to-Muhammad one.
