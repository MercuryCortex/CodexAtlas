# Vajrapāṇi

The bodhisattva **"Bearer of the Vajra"** — one of the three principal protectors of Mahāyāna Buddhism, embodying the **power** of all the Buddhas as [[manjushri]] embodies their wisdom and [[avalokitesvara]] their compassion. Vajrapāṇi is the wrathful protector who clears obstacles for the Dharma and guards the secret tantric transmissions.

The figure carries two extraordinary transmission stories:
- **Indra absorbed into Buddhism.** Vajrapāṇi inherits the *vajra* (thunderbolt) and the warrior-protector role directly from the Vedic [[indra]], who is institutionally demoted in Buddhism into the role of Buddha-attendant (Sakra) and into Vajrapāṇi himself. This is one of the clearest documented cases of a Vedic deity being absorbed into Buddhism.
- **Heracles-iconography in Gandhāran reliefs.** The Greek-Buddhist art of Gandhāra (1st-3rd c. CE, modern Pakistan/Afghanistan) depicts Vajrapāṇi with the **muscular nude torso, lion-skin, and club of [[heracles]]** — possibly the most striking single visual fusion between Greek and Buddhist religion. The Heraclean Vajrapāṇi iconography then travels east and becomes the **Niō (Kongōrikishi)** temple-gate guardians of Japanese Buddhism — a documented 2,000-year visual-cognate transmission chain from a Greek hero in the Mediterranean to a Japanese temple gate.
