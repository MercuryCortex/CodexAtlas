# Kama (Vedic/Hindu)

## Identity

Kama (also *Kamadeva*, "god of desire") is the Hindu god of erotic love and desire — structurally the nearest parallel to Roman Cupid and Greek Eros. He is armed with a bow of sugarcane strung with a line of bees, and fires five flower-tipped arrows that cause the five stages of desire. His consort is Rati (passion).

**Kama as purushartha**: In Hindu thought, *kama* (desire/pleasure) is one of the four legitimate goals of human life (*purusharthas*): *dharma* (righteousness), *artha* (material prosperity), *kama* (pleasure/love), and *moksha* (liberation). This taxonomic inclusion of desire as a legitimate life-goal distinguishes Hindu ethics from Stoic or Platonic frameworks that treat desire as a problem to be overcome.

**The Shiva episode**: Kama was burned to ash by Shiva's third eye when he attempted to distract the meditating god into falling in love with Parvati (at the gods' request, so that Shiva would produce a son to defeat the demon Taraka). Shiva incinerated Kama with a burst of fire from his third eye. Thereafter Kama is called **Ananga** ("the Bodiless") — he continues to function as the power of desire without a physical form. This myth encodes a profound tension: desire is necessary for cosmic reproduction yet is incompatible with ascetic liberation. See **[[shiva]]** and **[[parvati]]**.

See **[[cupid-roman]]** for the parallel-motif comparison; the structural resemblance (child-deity, flower/golden arrows, erotic compulsion, divine mother) is among the strongest Indo-European parallel-motif pairs, though no transmission is documented.
