# Utu / Shamash

The Mesopotamian sun god — Utu in Sumerian, Shamash in Akkadian — and the principal divine judge. Each morning he rises through gates in the eastern mountain, traverses the sky, sets in the west, and travels through the underworld at night where he judges the dead. His ability to see everything that happens on earth makes him the natural patron of oaths, judicial procedure, and law. The relief at the top of the [[phase-1-007-code-of-hammurabi]] stele shows Shamash seated on his throne, handing the rod and ring of authority to King Hammurabi — the most famous image of Shamash as the divine source of law. His principal cult centers were Larsa and Sippar, both with temples called Ebabbar ("White House").

## Mythological role
Shamash is a frequently invoked but rarely narratively-central deity. He appears in the [[phase-1-004-gilgamesh-old-babylonian]] as the patron of the heroes, aiding Gilgamesh and Enkidu against Humbaba; in the Standard Babylonian Gilgamesh he is the deity addressed by the dying Enkidu. His role as divine judge becomes the central theme of Babylonian wisdom literature: the *Hymn to Shamash* (Lambert 1960) celebrates his omniscience and justice ("The criminal you make wear his crime"); the *Babylonian Theodicy* turns to him as the only possible referent of righteous suffering. Shamash's structural position in Mesopotamian theology — solar omniscience grounding legal authority — closely parallels the role the Hebrew Bible eventually centralizes in Yahweh as judge.
