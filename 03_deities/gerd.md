# Gerd

## Identity

Gerd (Old Norse **Gerðr**) is a giantess of Norse myth — the daughter of the giant Gymir, famed for a beauty so radiant that "her arms gave light to all the sky and sea." She is best known as the bride of the Vanir fertility-god [[freyr]], whose wooing of her is one of the most celebrated love-stories of the Eddic corpus.

## Mythological role

Gerd's story is told in the Eddic poem **Skírnismál**. Freyr sits in Odin's high seat Hlidskjalf, from which all worlds can be seen, and catches sight of Gerd; he is at once sick with love. Because a god cannot easily court a giantess, Freyr sends his servant **Skírnir** to woo her, lending him his horse and his magical sword. Skírnir offers Gerd golden apples and a ring, which she refuses; only when he resorts to dire threats — curses of barrenness, monstrous marriage, and madness carved in runes — does she relent and agree to meet Freyr in nine nights at the grove Barri. Freyr wins her, but at the cost of his sword, which he gives away in the bargain — a loss that will doom him at Ragnarök. Many scholars read the union of the bright fertility-god and the earth-giantess as a **fertility hieros gamos**, the sacred marriage that brings the quickening of the land.

## Cross-tradition significance

Gerd is the Norse instance of the **god-weds-earth/giantess** sacred-marriage motif — the union of a fertility or sky deity with a figure of the earth or the elder, chthonic race, enacting the fructifying of the land. The pattern parallels the Mesopotamian sacred marriage of [[inanna-sumerian]] and [[dumuzi-tammuz]] and the wider ancient *hieros gamos* in which the bond of divine bride and groom secures fertility. As a giantess married into the Vanir, she also belongs to the distinctively Norse web of god–giant marriages that bind the warring races of the cosmos.
