# Yima

## Identity
The first mortal sovereign in Iranian mythology — Avestan **Yima Xšaēta** ("Yima the Radiant"), later Persian **Jamshid**. Where his Indo-Iranian cognate [[yama-vedic|Yama]] becomes lord of the dead in India, Yima in Iran becomes the **first king of a paradisal golden age**: under his rule humans and animals do not die, food never spoils, and the earth grows so populous it must be enlarged three times. Already in the [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra|Gāthās]] (Yasna 32.8) Yima is treated as problematic — Zarathustra rebukes him for some primordial sin (variously: pride, claiming divinity, slaughtering the ox, taking lying speech).

## Mythological role
**The Vara**: in **Vendidad 2** Ahura Mazda warns Yima of a coming devastating winter (*malkōsa*) and instructs him to build an underground refuge — the **Vara of Yima** — in which to preserve paired specimens of all species, the most beautiful humans and animals, and seeds. This is the Iranian variant of the **flood/ark mytheme**, with winter substituted for flood (climatically reasonable for highland Iran), and is structurally cognate to Noah, Utnapishtim, Atrahasis, and Deucalion. The **Jām-e Jam** ("Cup of Jamshid") of later Persian Sufi poetry — a divinatory mirror-cup showing the seven climes of the world — is named for Yima/Jamshid.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[yama-vedic]]** — *ancient-identification*: the cleanest Indo-Iranian deity-cognate.
- **[[noah]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: ark/refuge mytheme.
- **[[gilgamesh-king]] / Utnapishtim** — *scholarly-parallel*: golden-age king who confronts mortality.
