# Venus (Roman)

## Identity
Originally a relatively minor Italian goddess of gardens, vegetation, and seasonal blooming (the etymological connection to *venia*, "favor, charm" is suggestive), Venus underwent a dramatic expansion of role through (a) early identification with the **Greek Aphrodite** via *interpretatio Graeca*; (b) the importation of the **Astarte cult of Eryx (Sicily) under the name *Venus Erycina*** in -217 during the Second Punic War; and (c) Julius Caesar's claim that his Julian gens descended from Venus through Aeneas — making Venus the divine ancestress of the Caesars and, by extension, of Rome. As **Venus Genetrix** ("Venus the Mother / Begetter"), she received Caesar's temple in the Forum Iulium in -46.

## Earliest attestation
- The earliest attested Venus temple in Rome dated to ~-295 (Quintus Fabius Gurges).
- Venus Erycina temple on the Capitoline: -217.
- Venus Genetrix temple in the Forum Iulium: -46.

## Mythological role
Roman Venus's mythology is largely (but not entirely) the Greek Aphrodite's mythology under Latin name. **Distinctively Roman** is her role as **mother of Aeneas** (Vergil, *Aeneid* — Aeneas is *natus dea*, "born of a goddess"), making her the divine ancestress of (a) the Trojan-Roman foundation lineage; (b) the gens Iulia specifically; (c) through Augustus's Julian self-presentation, the divine matrix of the Augustan principate.

The cult of **Venus Erycina** brought Phoenician fertility-and-prostitution-cult practices into Roman religion via Sicily; the Roman version was sanitized but retained sexual-fertility associations.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[aphrodite-greek]]** — *ancient-identification*: complete from late Republic onward; the literary Venus IS Aphrodite.
- **[[astarte-canaanite]]** — *ancient-identification* (via Eryx route): Venus Erycina imports a genuinely Phoenician-Punic cult.
- **[[ishtar-akkadian]]** / **[[inanna-sumerian]]** — *scholarly-parallel* (via Astarte-Aphrodite chain): the Mesopotamian Inanna→Ishtar→Astarte→Aphrodite→Venus lineage is a classic case-study in Mediterranean syncretism.

## Iconography
- Greek Aphrodite-typology: idealized nude or partially draped female
- Scallop shell (referencing sea-birth)
- Dove (sacred bird)
- Myrtle (sacred plant)
- Rose, apple
- Frequently shown with infant Cupid (Eros)

## Disputes
- **Native Roman content.** Was Venus a meaningful pre-Hellenized Italian goddess, or essentially an imported Aphrodite from the start? Schilling argued for a substantive Italian core; Wissowa for near-total Greek import.
- **Phoenician-Roman cult transmission via Eryx.** The Eryx cult's character (genuine sacred prostitution? cultic exaggeration in Roman sources?) is debated.
