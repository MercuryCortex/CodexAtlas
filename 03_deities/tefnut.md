# Tefnut

## Identity
Tefnut is the Egyptian goddess of moisture, dew, rain, and the fluid atmosphere; sister-consort of Shu (Air); mother of Geb (Earth) and Nut (Sky). With Shu she is the first-generated pair of the Heliopolitan Ennead from Atum's self-generative act, embodying the fluid principle paired with Shu's airy principle. The pair is the first sexual dyad of the Egyptian cosmogony — before Shu and Tefnut, there is only the autogenous Atum.

## Earliest attestation
- [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] — appears in Old Kingdom Heliopolitan formulas alongside Shu.
- [[phase-1-009-coffin-texts]] further develop her role.

## Mythological role
- **First daughter of Atum.** Atum produces Shu and Tefnut in the primordial moment — typically by self-fertilization (the texts use various images: masturbation, sneezing, spitting); they are the first cosmic differentiation from undifferentiated Atum-Nun.
- **Mother of the second generation.** With Shu she begets Geb and Nut, who beget the Osiris-Isis-Set-Nephthys quartet, completing the Ennead.
- **The "Distant Goddess" myth.** In a Late Period and Greco-Roman tradition (preserved in the Demotic *Myth of the Sun's Eye*), Tefnut as the Solar Eye departs Egypt in anger to Nubia; Thoth (or Shu) must persuade her to return. The myth is a paradigm for the wandering-and-recovered-goddess.

## Iconography
- Most often lion-headed (paired with Shu's masculine form)
- Solar disc with uraeus
- Sometimes fully leonine
- Occasionally fully anthropomorphic woman
