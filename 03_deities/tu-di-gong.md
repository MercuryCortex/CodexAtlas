# Tu Di Gong (土地公)

The hyper-local Earth God of Chinese folk religion — every neighborhood, village, rice paddy, and building has its own Tu Di Gong with his own small shrine. He is the lowest rank in the divine bureaucracy (the divine equivalent of a village headman or beat policeman), responsible for the welfare of his small territory and for reporting annually to the [[jade-emperor]] on local affairs.

His ubiquity makes him statistically the most common deity in the Chinese religious world — there are more Tu Di Gong shrines than any other religious site in Chinese civilization. His small red-and-gold roadside shrines are present everywhere from rural Fujian to urban Singapore.

He exemplifies the **imperial-metaphor structure** of [[tradition-chinese-folk-religion]]: a divine official with a defined jurisdiction, upward reporting obligations, and accountability to the supreme deity.
