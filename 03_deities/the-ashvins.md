# The Aśvins

## Identity
The **divine twins** of the Vedic pantheon — **Nāsatya** and **Dasra** — sons of the sun-god Vivasvant by Saraṇyū. Healers of the gods, riders of a three-wheeled chariot drawn by horses (or swans, or birds), the first to greet the dawn-goddess Uṣas each day. The third-most-addressed deities in the Rig Veda (after Indra and Agni), with over **50 hymns** dedicated to them; their dawn-rescue mythology — rescuing the drowning sage Bhujyu from the sea, the blind Ṛjrāśva, the aged Cyavāna whom they restore to youth — is the most-elaborated Vedic dawn-healing complex.

## Mythological role
**The Indo-European Divine Twins**: Ward 1968 and West 2007 establish the Aśvins as one of the cleanest cross-IE deity-cognates. The Vedic Aśvins, Greek Dioskouroi (Castor and Pollux), Lithuanian Aśvieniai, and Latvian Dieva Dēli all share: (1) twin-pair structure; (2) descent from the Sky-Father; (3) horse-association; (4) dawn-association; (5) rescue-at-sea function (Dioskouroi save sailors from storm, Aśvins save Bhujyu from the sea); (6) functional split (mortal/immortal in Greek, healer/horseman in Vedic).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Dioskouroi** (Castor & Pollux) — *scholarly-parallel*: IE divine-twins.
- **Aśvieniai / Dieva Dēli** (Baltic) — *scholarly-parallel*: IE cognate.
- **Marassa** (Vodou) — *parallel-motif*: divine-twins type (non-IE).
- **[[romulus]] & Remus** — *parallel-motif*: legendary founder-twins.
