# Hercules

The Roman reception of the Greek hero [[heracles]] — distinct from his Greek source in that he is a fully integrated **state cult** at the Ara Maxima in the Forum Boarium (one of Rome's oldest cult sites, allegedly founded by Heracles himself during the Cacus episode). The Roman Hercules is venerated as a divine model of *virtus* — strength, endurance, and apotheosis through labor — and is the patron of merchants, victory, and oath-taking. The cult passed through Etruscan intermediaries (the Etruscan **Hercle**, attested on bronze engraved mirrors) before its Latin form crystallized.

See [[heracles]] for the Greek source and his Twelve Labors mythography; this node tracks specifically the Roman cult, the Etruscan stratum, and the Hercules-Melqart-Heracles Mediterranean triple-identification documented as early as Herodotus.
