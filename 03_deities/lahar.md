# Lahar

## Identity

Lahar (Laḫar) is the Sumerian deity of cattle and the flocks — the personified power of herding, of sheep and cattle and the wool, milk, and meat they yield. The name is closely bound to the word for "ewe," and Lahar stands for the pastoral half of the Mesopotamian food economy, paired against [[ashnan]], the goddess of grain, who stands for the agricultural half.

## Mythological role

Lahar is known above all from the Sumerian disputation poem usually called *Lahar and Ashnan* (or *Ewe and Grain*). In its mythological prologue, the gods of the Anunnaki, in the holy mound called the **Duku**, did not yet have proper food or clothing; so Lahar (the flocks) and Ashnan (the grain) were created there to supply them. The two were then sent down to the earth to benefit humankind. Once among humans, Lahar and Ashnan fall into a dispute — each boasting of her gifts and disparaging the other's — in the classic Mesopotamian "disputation" form that pits two complementary goods against each other (herding versus farming, exactly the tension dramatized elsewhere in *Inanna prefers the shepherd* with the shepherd [[dumuzi-tammuz]] and the farmer [[enkimdu]]). The gods finally adjudicate the quarrel.

## Cross-tradition significance

The Lahar–Ashnan disputation belongs to the Mesopotamian theology of the **divine provision of subsistence** — agriculture and herding established by the gods for the feeding of gods and humans alike. The herder-versus-farmer motif it dramatizes is one of the oldest such literary debates in the world, and its underlying tension — between the pastoral and the agricultural ways of life — echoes through later Near Eastern literature, including the Cain (farmer) and Abel (herder) narrative of Genesis.
