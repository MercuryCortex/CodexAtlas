# Hermoni

## Identity
**Hermoni** is the named **Watcher** of 1 Enoch 8:3 who taught humanity *"the loosing of spells, magic, sorcery, and sophistry."* The name *Hermoni* etymologically echoes **Mount Hermon** — the location where [[semyaza]] led the 200 Watchers to swear their binding oath (1 Enoch 6:5–6) — making Hermoni the Watcher whose name encodes the geography of the original rebellion.

1 Enoch 8:3 (Nickelsburg-VanderKam 2004): *"Shemihazah taught spells and the cutting of roots; Hermoni taught the loosing of spells, magic, sorcery, and sophistry..."*

## Earliest attestation
- **1 Enoch 8:3** — the Book of the Watchers teaching-list.
- **Ge'ez Mashafa Henok** — only complete textual witness.

## Mythological role
Hermoni teaches the **magical-sophistical complex**: magic (manipulation of reality through esoteric means), sorcery (malicious magical practice), sophistry (deceptive verbal argument), and the loosing of spells (counter-magic). The clustering of magical-and-sophistical knowledge under one Watcher's portfolio is theologically suggestive — the Enochic tradition treats *magic* and *sophistry* (false-but-persuasive argument) as cognate species of illegitimate manipulation.

The name's geographic echo of Mount Hermon may indicate Hermoni was the original or chief teacher at the oath-site itself, with [[semyaza]] as the political-organizational leader and Hermoni as the magical-pedagogical leader. The Aramaic original at Qumran 4Q201 / 4Q202 (the Watchers fragments) preserves enough of the name to confirm the Hermon-eponymous reading.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- The clustering of *magic + sophistry* parallels Plato's polemics against rhetorical sophistry; the Enochic theology may be structurally related to Hellenistic-period Jewish polemics against Greek rhetorical-philosophical practices, though direct dependence is not established.

## Iconography
Not standardized.

## Disputes
- **Hermoni vs. Armaros.** Some recensions of 1 Enoch 8:3 collapse the two figures; others maintain distinctness. The Aramaic Qumran fragments support Hermoni as the principal teacher of magic-sorcery; Armaros may be a parallel name for the same or an adjacent figure.
