# Leviathan

## Identity
Leviathan (Hebrew *liwyāṯān*, from the root *lwy*, "to twist, coil") is the primordial sea-serpent / chaos-dragon of the Hebrew Bible, defeated by Yahweh in cosmogonic combat. The name is cognate with Ugaritic *ltn* (Lotan), and the Hebrew Leviathan inherits the iconographic and onomastic content of the Canaanite chaos-serpent with Yahweh substituted for Baal as the conquering warrior. Principal attestations: **Job 41** (extended description as God's untameable creature — the redacted, domesticated Leviathan); **Psalm 74:13-14** (Yahweh "crushed the heads of Leviathan" — plural, *rāʾšê liwyāṯān*, presupposing a multi-headed chaoskampf); **Isaiah 27:1** ("In that day Yahweh with his hard, great, strong sword will punish Leviathan the fleeing serpent, Leviathan the twisting serpent, and he will slay the dragon that is in the sea"); **Psalm 104:26** (Leviathan reduced to a sea-creature Yahweh plays with). In later Second Temple and rabbinic tradition, Leviathan becomes paired with **Behemoth** (the land-counterpart) as the eschatological banquet to be eaten by the righteous at the end of days (1 Enoch 60; b. Bava Batra 74b–75a).

## Earliest attestation
- Hebrew Bible — pre-exilic poetic core (Psalms 74, 89, 104; Isaiah 27, 51; Job 40-41), composed across the late Iron II to Persian periods (-700 to -400). Day 1985 reconstructs the pre-Israelite mythic substrate.
- Ugaritic *ltn* — the direct cognate — attested at KTU 1.5.i.1–3 (~-1400 to -1180), is the older Northwest Semitic attestation. See [[lotan]].
- Second Temple expansion: [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] (1 Enoch 60:7-10) develops the Leviathan / Behemoth pairing for eschatological banquet imagery.

## Mythological role
In the surviving Hebrew Bible, Leviathan operates at three levels:

1. **Cosmogonic combat (pre-domesticated).** Psalm 74:13-14, Isaiah 51:9-10, and (more obliquely) Job 26:12-13 preserve fragments of a Yahweh-vs.-Leviathan combat at the foundation of the cosmos — the Yahwistic recension of the Ugaritic Baal-vs.-Lotan combat (Day 1985).
2. **Eschatological adversary.** Isaiah 27:1 places Leviathan's final defeat at the end of days; the chaoskampf is recapitulated at the eschaton. This pattern feeds directly into the New Testament Revelation 12-13 dragon and the rabbinic eschatological-banquet tradition.
3. **Job-domesticated.** Job 41 (Hebrew Bible Job 40:25–41:26) presents Leviathan as God's creature, untameable by humans but firmly within Yahweh's creative jurisdiction — the chaoskampf domesticated into theodicy. This is the latest stage of the Hebrew Leviathan tradition and represents the Priestly / Wisdom redactional move that takes the sting out of the older combat-myth.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[lotan]]** — *ancient-identification* (linguistically and iconographically): Ugaritic *ltn* = Hebrew *lwytn*; same Northwest Semitic root *lwy*; same seven-headed (Ps 74:14 — plural heads) chaos-serpent type. Isaiah 27:1's epithet pair *nāḥāš bāriaḥ* / *nāḥāš ʿăqallātôn* is the direct Hebrew calque of Ugaritic *bṯn brḥ* / *bṯn ʿqltn* (KTU 1.5.i.1–3). Day 1985 is canonical.
- **[[yam]]** — *ancient-identification*: Leviathan is repeatedly associated with the sea (*yām*) and the deep (*tehom*); Psalm 74:13-14 pairs sea and Leviathan.
- **[[tiamat]]** — *parallel-form*: Mesopotamian primordial sea-chaos; structurally parallel but not directly inherited.
- **[[apophis]]** — *parallel-form*: Egyptian cosmic-serpent parallel.
- **[[vritra]]** — *parallel-form*: Indo-European-Vedic parallel via the inherited PIE *HERO SLAY SERPENT* poetic formula (Watkins 1995).

## Iconography
- Multiple heads (Psalm 74:14).
- Serpentine / dragon body; scales (Job 41:15-17 — "rows of scales").
- Fire from mouth, smoke from nostrils (Job 41:18-21) — a poetic intensification of the chaos-monster type.
- Coiling / twisting (the *ʿăqallātôn* / *bāriaḥ* pair).
- Habit: sea, deep waters, the *tehom*.
- Later Jewish iconography (rare in pre-modern Jewish art) treats Leviathan as a great sea-serpent or whale.

## Disputes
- **Strength of the Lotan → Leviathan transmission.** Day 1985 argues for *direct* ancestor-of relation at the philological-mythological level — the verbal calque at Isaiah 27:1 is the strongest evidence. Some critics treat the relation as *parallel-form* (shared West-Semitic chaoskampf vocabulary rather than direct borrowing). Mainstream consensus follows Day.
- **Number of heads.** Psalm 74:14 uses plural *rāʾšê* ("heads") — implying multiple heads, consistent with Lotan's seven heads at KTU 1.5.i.3. Some readings treat the plural as poetic / collective rather than literal.
- **Domestication chronology.** Whether Job 40-41's domestication of Leviathan is a deliberate polemic against earlier chaoskampf mythology or a Wisdom-tradition repurposing of inherited imagery is debated.
