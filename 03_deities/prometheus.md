# Prometheus

## Identity
The Titan **"Forethought"** — son of [[iapetus]] and the Oceanid Clymene, brother of Atlas and Epimetheus ("Hindsight"). His three principal mythological acts together constitute one of the densest culture-hero narratives in any tradition:

1. **The Mecone trick** (Hesiod *Theogony* 535–560): at the meal that establishes the sacrificial division between gods and humans, Prometheus arranges two portions — bones wrapped in glistening fat, vs. meat hidden in the stomach — and tricks Zeus into choosing the deceptive bones-portion, thereby securing the meat-portion for humans forever after.
2. **The theft of fire** (*Theogony* 561–569; *Works and Days* 50–58): Zeus, enraged by the Mecone trick, withholds fire from humans; Prometheus steals it back **in a hollow fennel-stalk** and delivers it.
3. **The clay-anthropogony** (Apollodorus *Library* 1.7.1): Prometheus shapes the first humans from clay; Athena breathes life into them.

For these gifts Zeus chains him to a peak in the Caucasus where an eagle devours his ever-regenerating liver every day; Heracles eventually frees him.

## Mythological role
**Aeschylus's *Prometheus Bound*** (~450 BCE) transforms the figure from Hesiod's clever trickster into the **tragic divine rebel against tyrannical sovereignty**, accusing Zeus of being a new tyrant of the cosmos. **Romantic reception** — Goethe's "Prometheus" (1774), Byron's "Prometheus" (1816), Mary and Percy Shelley's *Frankenstein, or the Modern Prometheus* (1818) and *Prometheus Unbound* (1820) — established Prometheus as **the foundational mythological figure of modernity**: the rebel-benefactor whose theft of divine fire is the type of human creative, technical, and intellectual self-assertion against received authority.
