# Ehecatl

## Identity
Ehecatl is the wind-god of Aztec religion — not a fully independent deity but the wind-aspect / wind-manifestation of [[quetzalcoatl]], consistently named in Aztec sources as "Ehecatl-Quetzalcoatl." The duck-billed mask (a projecting beak-like mouthpiece) is his signature iconographic attribute and is the marker that identifies a [[quetzalcoatl]] image as specifically in its wind manifestation. He governs wind, breath, and life-force — in Aztec cosmology these are not metaphorically but literally the same thing: *ehecatl* means both "wind" and "breath" in Nahuatl, and breath is the animating force of life.

## Earliest attestation
- Round temples dedicated to Ehecatl-Quetzalcoatl appear at multiple Aztec sites; the round plan is the architectural signature of Ehecatl worship (circular structures have no corners to impede the wind).
- **Teotihuacan** (c. 200 BCE–600 CE): feathered-serpent imagery at the Temple of the Feathered Serpent may include proto-Ehecatl wind associations, though the wind-mask specifically is a later Postclassic development.
- **Codex Borgia** and **Florentine Codex** (Late Postclassic, compiled c. 1569) provide the fullest textual and iconographic treatment of Ehecatl-Quetzalcoatl.

## Mythological role
Ehecatl's central cosmogonic act is **setting the Five Suns in motion**. In the Aztec creation account (*Leyenda de los Soles*, compiled c. 1558), after the gods sacrificed themselves at Teotihuacan to create the sun and moon, the newly-made celestial bodies refused to move — they sat motionless in the sky. Ehecatl blew the wind with his breath, and the sun and moon began their courses. Without Ehecatl's wind, creation would have been a still, unmoving, non-functioning cosmos — his breath is what gives the created world its dynamism.

He is also the figure who, as Ehecatl-Quetzalcoatl, descended to Mictlan (the underworld) to retrieve the bones of previous humanities and brought them back to create the current human race. Ehecatl's breath animated the reconstituted bones: wind = breath = life.

## Cross-tradition identifications

- **[[quetzalcoatl]]** — *ancient-identification*: Ehecatl IS Quetzalcoatl in his wind manifestation; the compound name Ehecatl-Quetzalcoatl is the standard term in primary sources. The duck-billed mask distinguishes this aspect from other Quetzalcoatl manifestations (the serpent, the morning star, the culture-hero). This is not a syncretism between two deities but an internal Aztec theological relationship.
- **[[tlaloc]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: wind and rain are paired atmospheric forces in Aztec agricultural theology; Ehecatl brings the winds that carry the clouds, and Tlaloc releases the rain. Their pairing at Templo Mayor (Ehecatl-Quetzalcoatl's round temples are found near Tlaloc shrines at multiple sites) encodes this atmospheric complementarity architecturally.
- **[[tonatiuh]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Ehecatl set Tonatiuh (the sun) in motion with his breath; the two are in a cosmogonic dependency relationship — Tonatiuh would not move without Ehecatl's wind.
- **Wind-as-breath cross-tradition** — *scholarly-parallel*: the identification of wind and life-breath as a single divine force appears in multiple independent traditions. **Genesis 1:2**: *ruach Elohim* ("the breath/wind/spirit of God") moving over the waters precedes creation — the divine wind activates the formless. **John 3:8**: "The wind (*pneuma*) blows where it wishes... so is everyone born of the Spirit (*pneuma*)." **Vedic Vāyu** (wind god) is directly linked to *prāṇa* (life-breath). The Aztec Ehecatl pattern — wind as the animating force that sets creation in motion — is a near-universal theological-cosmogonic category.

## Iconography
The duck-billed wind mask is the unique and unmistakable marker: a protruding tubular or beak-shaped mouthpiece in front of the deity's face, designed to represent wind blowing. The *ehécacozcatl* (wind jewel) is a pectoral ornament made from a cross-section of a conch shell — the spiral shape represents the spinning of wind. Black body paint. The round temple plan is Ehecatl's architectural signature: Aztec sacred architecture is otherwise angular and rectilinear; round temples (at Calixtlahuaca, Quetzalcoatl's temple at Tenochtitlan, and other sites) mark Ehecatl-Quetzalcoatl's presence.

## Disputes
- **Ehecatl as independent deity vs. Quetzalcoatl-aspect.** Primary sources consistently give the compound name; some modern scholars discuss Ehecatl as if he were a fully separate deity for analytic purposes. The ancient Aztec position seems to have been that Ehecatl is [[quetzalcoatl]]'s wind-manifestation, not a separate divine being.
- **The cosmogonic wind-breath narrative.** Different Aztec creation texts assign different roles to Ehecatl in the cosmogony. The *Leyenda de los Soles* version (Ehecatl blows the sun and moon into motion) is the most cited; Sahagún's *Florentine Codex* treatment differs in some details. The core of the narrative — that the wind was the animating cosmic force at the moment of creation — is consistent.
