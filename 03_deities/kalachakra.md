# Kalachakra (Wheel of Time)

## Identity
Kālacakra — "the Wheel of Time" — is the central deity of the Kālacakra-tantra system, the last great Indian Vajrayāna tantric tradition to develop (~11th c., possibly in eastern India / Kashmir). The system is encyclopedic: it incorporates astronomy, calendrical computation, alchemy, medicine, and an elaborate eschatological narrative of the kingdom of Shambhala (a hidden land of dharma-practitioners that will, in 2424 CE according to traditional Kālacakra timing, emerge to overcome a final cycle of darkness). The Kālacakra deity-image: dark blue, four-faced (white, red, yellow, black faces), with twenty-four arms, in yab-yum union with the four-faced consort Viśvamātā. The doctrinal core: time itself is the cosmic non-dual reality; ordinary time is the projection of unrecognized awareness; the Kālacakra sādhana is the operative practice for realizing the identity of time and awareness. The Kālacakra initiation has been one of the principal public Tibetan Buddhist rites of the 20th–21st c. — the Dalai Lama has performed it more than thirty times to large international audiences.

## Earliest attestation
Kālacakra-tantra and its commentary *Vimalaprabhā* (~1027) — the only major tantric system whose composition can be relatively closely dated. Tibetan translations from ~11th–12th c.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- The Shambhala mythology of the Kālacakra system entered Western esoteric imagination via Helena Blavatsky and Theosophy (Phase 7); modern New Age "Shambhala" rhetoric descends in part from this transmission, much corrupted from the original tantric context.
