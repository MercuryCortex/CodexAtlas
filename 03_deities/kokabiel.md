# Kokabiel

## Identity
**Kokabiel** (Hebrew *kokab* = "star" + *El* = "God"; "Star of God" or "God's Star") is one of the named **Watchers** of 1 Enoch. He appears in both the chiefs-of-tens list at 1 Enoch 6:7 (the original Book of the Watchers list of the twenty chiefs who swore the oath at Mount Hermon under [[semyaza]]) and in the teaching-roles list at 1 Enoch 8:3 as the Watcher who **taught humanity astrology and the constellations**.

1 Enoch 8:3 (Nickelsburg-VanderKam 2004): *"Shemihazah taught spells and the cutting of roots; Hermoni taught the loosing of spells, magic, sorcery, and sophistry; Baraqel taught the signs of the lightning flashes; Kokabel taught the signs of the stars; Zikiel taught the signs of the shooting stars; Arteqof taught the signs of the earth; Shamsiel taught the signs of the sun; Sahriel taught the signs of the moon. And they all began to reveal mysteries to their wives and to their children."*

## Earliest attestation
- **1 Enoch 6:7** — included in the Mount Hermon oath-list of twenty chiefs.
- **1 Enoch 8:3** — the teaching-of-astrology role.
- **Qumran 4Q201, 4Q202** — Aramaic fragments of the Book of the Watchers attesting the broader Watchers tradition.
- **Ge'ez Mashafa Henok** — the only complete textual witness, preserving the role canonically in [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]].

## Mythological role
Kokabiel is the **inventor-and-illicit-transmitter of astrology** in the Enochic frame. The Enochic theology treats astrology — along with metallurgy (Azazel), abortifacients (Kasdeja), and writing (Penemue) — as part of the corrupting Watcher-knowledge transmitted to pre-Diluvian humanity. Where most Mesopotamian, Egyptian, and Greek cultures treat star-divination as legitimate priestly knowledge, the Enochic tradition theologically marks it as *transgressive* — celestial knowledge that belongs to the heavenly courts and was *stolen* from them. The polemic likely targets the prestige of Babylonian astrological-priestly traditions (the *bārû* diviners) which dominated late-Persian and Hellenistic-period Near Eastern intellectual culture.

The Enochic *Astronomical Book* (1 Enoch 72–82, the "Book of the Heavenly Luminaries") is a different theological move: it presents Enoch as *legitimately* receiving celestial-astronomical knowledge from the archangel Uriel — sharply distinguishing this divinely-sanctioned cosmography from the Watcher-transmitted *astrology* that Kokabiel illicitly taught. The same Enochic corpus thus polemicizes against astrology while simultaneously transmitting a cosmographical astronomy.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- The Mesopotamian astrological-priestly *bārû* tradition is the implied polemical target.
- Hellenistic astrology (well-developed in the same 3rd–1st c. BCE period as the Enochic literature) is parallel cultural context.

## Iconography
Not standardized.

## Disputes
- **Identity with "Kakabel" / "Kawkabel."** Different recensions and translations vary the form; all refer to the same figure.
- **Order in the chiefs-of-tens list.** Different manuscript traditions order the twenty chiefs slightly differently; Kokabiel appears consistently in the seventh-or-eighth position.
