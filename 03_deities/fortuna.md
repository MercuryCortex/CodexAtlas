# Fortuna

## Identity
The Roman goddess of **fate, fortune, and chance** — and the principal Roman attempt to **personify what cannot be controlled**. Her great cult-center at **Praeneste** (modern Palestrina, Lazio) was a massive terraced sanctuary built into a hillside (~120 BCE) where her oracle was consulted by oak-tablet *sortes* (lots) drawn by a child — among the most important Italian oracle-sites alongside Cumae and Dodona. **Servius Tullius**, the sixth king of Rome, was credited with introducing her cult (traditionally ~570 BCE).

## Mythological role
**Rota Fortunae (Wheel of Fortune)**: the iconographic image of Fortuna with a wheel becomes — via **Boethius's *Consolation of Philosophy*** (early 6th c. CE, written in prison awaiting execution) — the great medieval Christian moral image of the unreliability of worldly success. Boethius's Lady Philosophy explicitly *demotes* Fortuna and her wheel beneath the Christian Providence — but the wheel survives Christianization and becomes one of the most-depicted motifs in medieval manuscripts and cathedral rose-windows (Amiens, Beauvais, Basel).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Tyche** — *ancient-identification*: Greek cognate.
- **[[the-moirai]]** — *parallel-motif*: rival principle of fate.
- **[[lakshmi]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: feminine-divine variable-fortune.
- **Boethian Providence** — *polemic-inversion*: Christian re-subordination of pagan Fortune.
