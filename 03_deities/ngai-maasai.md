# Ngai

## Identity
Supreme being of Maasai traditional religion (Nilotic pastoralist people of Kenya and Tanzania). Ngai is the sky, the giver of rain, and the original donor of cattle — which the Maasai trace as Ngai's gift specifically to them. Ngai resides on Kilimanjaro (called Ngai-Naiponi) and is sometimes identified with Mount Kenya as well, sharing the high-mountain residence with the related Kikuyu high-god Ngai/Kirinyaga (Kenyatta 1938). The dual aspect Ngai-Narok (the "Black Ngai" — beneficent, rain-giver) and Ngai-Nanyokie (the "Red Ngai" — wrathful, drought-giver) is a distinctive feature of Maasai theology.

## Earliest attestation
Pre-colonial Maasai oral tradition; first European documentation 19th c. (Thomson, Krapf); modern ethnography Spencer (1988); missionary documentation Donovan (1978) in *Christianity Rediscovered*.

## Mythological role
A foundational Maasai narrative: at the beginning, the heavens and earth were connected by a long cord. Ngai sent cattle down the cord to the Maasai, establishing them as the original cattle-owning people. Other peoples who possess cattle therefore possess what was originally given by Ngai to the Maasai — a theology that has historically justified Maasai cattle-raiding as the recovery of misappropriated divine property. The dual Black-Red Ngai pattern accounts for the variability of Ngai's response: the rains come (Black Ngai), the drought punishes (Red Ngai). The two are not separate gods but two faces of the one Ngai.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Kirinyaga / Ngai of Kikuyu** — the Kikuyu (Bantu, agriculturalist) and Maasai (Nilotic, pastoralist) live adjacent and share the high-god name; Kikuyu Kirinyaga is identified with Mount Kenya, Maasai Ngai-Naiponi with Kilimanjaro. The names are cognate; whether this represents Maasai → Kikuyu loanword, Kikuyu → Maasai loanword, or shared sub-Saharan East-African substrate is debated (Kenyatta 1938; Mbiti 1969).
- **Christian God** — Donovan's *Christianity Rediscovered* (1978) is the canonical methodological reflection on missionary translation of Christian theology into Ngai-categories among the Maasai; the work has been widely read in missiology circles.

## Cross-tradition parallels — the sky-mountain pastoralist high-god
Ngai sits within the East-African pastoralist pattern of a sky / mountain / cattle / rain high-god. Adjacent traditions (Kikuyu Ngai/Kirinyaga; Kalenjin Asis; Luo Nyasaye) follow similar patterns. The pastoralist + mountain + cattle pattern in East Africa has structural parallels (parallel-form, no transmission) to other pastoralist high-gods: PIE *dyḗws ph₂tḗr (sky-father, source of Zeus / Jupiter / Dyaus Pita), various Inner-Asian Tengri high-gods of nomadic pastoralists, and the Hebrew Bible's pastoralist-period YHWH theology. The pattern is consistent enough that Burkert (1996) and others have argued for a deep-cognitive connection between pastoralist subsistence and sky-mountain high-god theology.

## Iconography
No anthropomorphic image. Kilimanjaro is the visual representation. Cattle themselves are theologically charged as Ngai's gift. Maasai religious specialists (*oloiboni* — diviner-prophet) mediate Ngai's communication.
