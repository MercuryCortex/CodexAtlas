# Samsin

Samsin (most affectionately **Samsin Halmoni**, "Grandmother Samsin") is the Korean **household goddess of childbirth** — the spirit (or, in her name, the *three* spirits) who governs **conception, pregnancy, safe delivery, and the health of infants and small children**. She is among the most intimately important deities of Korean folk religion and shamanism (**Muism**): a couple hoping for a child prays to her, a safe birth is credited to her, and after a delivery the family offers her **seaweed soup (miyeok-guk) and rice** in the inner room of the house, her special domain. Folk belief holds that she watches over each child until about the age of seven, and even the bluish **infant birth-mark** (the "Mongolian spot") is sometimes explained as the mark of Grandmother Samsin's parting slap, hurrying the baby out into the world. As a domestic protective power she belongs to the same indigenous tradition as the mountain-god **[[sansin|Sansin]]** — the everyday Korean spirits of home and land that have persisted alongside Buddhism, Confucianism, and Christianity. Samsin embodies the sacralization of birth and the protective grandmother at the threshold of every Korean life.
