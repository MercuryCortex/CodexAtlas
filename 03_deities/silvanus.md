# Silvanus

Silvanus is the Roman god of **woods and uncultivated land** (*silva*, "forest"), and of the **boundary between the wild and the farmed** — the field-edge, the plantation, the estate's margin. He is one of the most widely attested deities of Roman *folk* religion: hundreds of humble dedications survive, overwhelmingly from **soldiers, freedmen, and small farmers** rather than the state cult, making him a vivid index of popular piety. He guards herds and plantations, presides over the clearing of forest into farmland, and is invoked in three aspects — *domesticus* (of the household), *agrestis* (of the fields), and *orientalis* (of the boundary plantings). In character and image he shades into **[[faunus-roman|Faunus]]** and the Greek **[[pan-greek|Pan]]**, the half-wild powers of the rustic margin, though his cult is distinctly his own. Silvanus embodies the ambivalent Roman relationship with the forest: a power to be propitiated wherever cultivation pressed against the wild.
