# Wakan Tanka

## Identity
*Wakȟáŋ Tȟáŋka* — usually translated "Great Spirit" or "Great Mystery" — is the totalizing-sacred concept of Lakota religion. Walker's *Lakota Belief and Ritual* records (from his early-20th-c. interviews with Oglala holy men) that *Wakan Tanka* is the totality of all the *wakȟáŋ* (sacred powers), structured hierarchically: the four superior wakȟáŋ (Wí — Sun, Skáŋ — Sky, Makȟá — Earth, Iŋyaŋ — Rock), the four associated wakȟáŋ, the four subordinated, the four wakȟáŋlapi — sixteen in all, but conceptually one. Wakan Tanka is therefore not a person but a structured-yet-unified field of sacred power; the common English translation "Great Spirit" misleads if it suggests an anthropomorphic deity.

## Earliest attestation
Lakota oral tradition; first systematic ethnographic documentation: James R. Walker's interviews with George Sword, Thomas Tyon, and other Oglala holy men, 1896–1914 (Walker 1980, ed. DeMallie & Jahner); Joseph Epes Brown's collaboration with Nicholas Black Elk (Brown 1953).

## Mythological role
Wakan Tanka is approached through the *čaŋnúŋpa* (the sacred pipe), the central religious technology of Lakota practice; through the seven sacred rites (Inípi sweat lodge, Haŋbléčheyapi vision quest, Wiwáŋyaŋg Wačhípi sun dance, Huŋkápi kinship-making, Išnáthi Awíčhalowaŋpi puberty rite, Tȟápa Waŋkáyeyapi throwing-the-ball, Naǧí Glúhapi spirit-keeping); and through the embodied symbolism of the *káŋǧí Tȟokáhe* (first Buffalo Calf Woman / White Buffalo Calf Woman, who in Lakota tradition brought the pipe and the rites).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Christian God** — *folk-syncretism* (post-contact): Lakota converts to Christianity (Black Elk became a Catholic catechist) sometimes identified Wakan Tanka with the Christian God; sometimes not. The translation is theologically contested within Lakota tradition.

## Iconography
Aniconic. The *čaŋnúŋpa* and ritual paraphernalia are mediating objects, not images of Wakan Tanka.
