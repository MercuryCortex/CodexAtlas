# Tonatiuh

## Identity
The Sun — specifically the present Fifth Sun, *Nahui Ōlin* ("Four Movement") in the Mexica cosmological count. Tonatiuh is one of the most-petitioned and most-feared Mexica deities; the heliocentric face at the center of the so-called Aztec Calendar Stone (the *Piedra del Sol*, excavated 1790 from Mexico City's Zócalo) is Tonatiuh (or Tlaltecuhtli, the earth-monster — debated). Mexica state theology held that the sun required human blood as nourishment to continue its daily journey across the sky; without sacrifice, the sun would weaken and the cosmos collapse. This theological justification of human sacrifice is the most-controversial element of Mexica religion and has been variously analyzed (as theological-functional reality; as exaggerated Spanish projection; as state-political instrument).

## Earliest attestation
Iconographic attestation in Aztec sculpture, especially the *Piedra del Sol* (~1502, in the reign of Moctezuma II). Earlier antecedents in Toltec and Teotihuacan solar imagery.

## Mythological role
At the creation of the Fifth Sun (Sahagún Book VII), the gods assembled at Teotihuacan and asked who would sacrifice himself to become the new sun. The proud Tecuciztecatl volunteered; the humble, sick Nanahuatzin also volunteered. Tecuciztecatl, faced with the sacrificial fire, hesitated four times; Nanahuatzin leapt directly in and became the Sun. Tecuciztecatl then jumped and became the Moon. The two rose at the same brightness, until a god threw a rabbit at the Moon to dim it — which is why the Moon has a rabbit-shape on its face. But the new Sun would not move — it stood still, demanding more sacrifice. The gods one by one offered themselves; Ehécatl-Quetzalcoatl finally sacrificed them all, and the Sun began its journey.

## Iconography
Solar disk with eight rays (or twenty, in the calendar-stone version); red coloration; eagle associate (eagles represent the sun and warrior-sacrifice); the calendar-stone face with protruding tongue (which may be Tlaltecuhtli rather than Tonatiuh — disputed).
