# Buri

## Identity

Buri (**Búri**) is the first of the gods in Norse cosmogony — the primeval ancestor from whom the Aesir descend. He has no parents: he is **produced directly from the ice** of [[ginnungagap]], licked free over three days by the primordial cow [[audumla]]. He is the grandfather of [[odin]].

## Mythological role

Snorri's *Gylfaginning* tells the story compactly. In the beginning, the cow Audumla — herself formed from the melting rime — nourishes the frost-giant [[ymir]] with her milk, and feeds herself by licking the salty rime-stones. As she licks, a man begins to emerge from the ice: on the first day his hair appears, on the second his head, and on the third the whole man steps forth. This is Buri, "fair, great, and mighty." Buri has a son, **[[bor-norse]]** (Bor), who marries Bestla, daughter of the giant Bölthorn; and Bor and Bestla's sons are **[[odin]], Vili, and Ve** — the gods who will slay Ymir and fashion the world from his body. Buri thus stands at the head of the divine genealogy, the first link in the chain that leads to Odin and the creation of the cosmos.

## Cross-tradition significance

Buri belongs to the Norse account of **origins from the primordial ice** — the distinctive fire-and-ice cosmogony in which life quickens in the void of Ginnungagap. As a first being produced spontaneously from the primal substance (here ice, licked by the cosmic cow), he is the Norse counterpart to the various "first man / first god" figures that emerge directly from the primordial matter across creation mythologies. His line (Buri → Bor → Odin) provides the genealogical bridge from the primeval state to the active, world-shaping gods.
