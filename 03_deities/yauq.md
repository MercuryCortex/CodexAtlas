# Yaʿūq

The pre-Islamic deity of the **Hamdān confederation** (north-western Yemeni highlands) — one of the five idols named in **Quran 71:23** as the deities of Noah's people that the pre-Islamic Arabs inherited. The name *Yaʿūq* in Arabic means *"He Who Restrains / Holds Back"* — implied protective or apotropaic function. The theophoric name *ʿAbd Yaʿūq* is attested in pre-Islamic Yemeni epigraphy.

Ibn al-Kalbi's *Kitāb al-Aṣnām* describes the idol as **horse-formed**, though other sources offer alternate descriptions. The Hamdān cult of Yaʿūq is one of the better-documented South Arabian tribal cults in the early Islamic compendia, partly because the Hamdān became major Yemeni converts and partly because the post-conversion genealogical literature preserved their pre-Islamic theophoric naming patterns.

This node completes the Quran 71:23 antediluvian-pantheon set in the vault: [[wadd]], [[suwa]], [[yaghuth]], Yaʿūq, [[nasr-pre-islamic]] — all five named in a single Quranic verse as the original pre-Islamic Arabian idolatry condemned by Noah.
