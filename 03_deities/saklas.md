# Saklas

## Identity

Saklas — "the Fool" or "the Blind One" in Aramaic — is one of the three primary appellatives for the Sethian Gnostic demiurge, used alongside [[yaldabaoth]] ("child of chaos") and Samael ("Blind God" or "Poison of God"). The three names are not three different beings but three faces of the same entity, each encoding a theological judgment:

- **Yaldabaoth**: his origin — born from [[sophia-gnostic]]'s irregular emanation without a consort; a child of chaos or shame
- **Saklas**: his epistemic condition — a *fool*, one who does not know his own nature, does not know his mother, does not know the Pleroma above him
- **Samael**: his visual condition — the *blind god*, one who cannot perceive the higher divine reality that exists above his cosmos

The name-cluster is the theological point: this being is not evil in the sense of malicious — he is cognitively incapacitated. He is a fool, blind, born of chaos. The Gnostic insistence on *ignorance* (not malice) as the primal cosmological condition is encoded in the very names given to the creator.

## Gospel of Judas: Saklas as the Name Jesus Reveals

The *Gospel of Judas* (Codex Tchacos, c. 300 CE; probably composed late 2nd c.) is the most explicit use of "Saklas" as a revelatory name. In the text, Jesus speaks privately with Judas and reveals the cosmic truth: the god the other disciples worship — the god of sacrifice, of the Temple cult, of blood and atonement — is not the true God but Saklas, the blind creator. The twelve disciples, who serve this god, will be "destroyed" along with his cosmos; Judas alone is given the knowledge of the true God above Saklas.

This is the most radical deployment of the Saklas/demiurge theology: it explicitly names the mainstream Jewish-Christian god-of-sacrifice as a cosmic fool and identifies orthodox Christian eucharistic practice (the "sacrifice" of the Mass) as service to the blind creator, not the true divine. The theological shock of the *Gospel of Judas* is not the "rehabilitation of Judas" but the identification of the sacrificial cult with Saklas-worship.

## MASSIVE WIN: The "Jealous God" Self-Refutation

The theological polemic crystallized in the Saklas/Yaldabaoth figure is one of the most significant instances of internal scripture-critique in the history of religions. The Sethian move: taking the Hebrew Bible's own language and turning it against the mainstream interpretation.

**The Exodus texts at issue:**

- Exodus 20:5: "I the LORD your God am a jealous God"
- Exodus 34:14: "the LORD, whose name is Jealous, is a jealous God"
- Isaiah 45:5: "I am the LORD, and there is no other; apart from me there is no God"

**The Gnostic reading:** A jealous God is, by definition, aware of other gods — otherwise, jealousy has no object. A God who must declare "I am the only God" implicitly reveals that he is uncertain of this fact. The declaration of exclusive divinity, for the Sethians, is precisely the evidence of ignorance: a truly supreme being does not need to assert its supremacy, and a truly omniscient being does not experience jealousy.

This interpretive move propagates across traditions:

| Tradition / Thinker | The "jealous God" reading | The conclusion drawn |
|---|---|---|
| Sethian Gnostic (2nd c.) | "I am God and there is no other" = proof of ignorance | The God who speaks these words is Saklas/Yaldabaoth, not the supreme God |
| Marcion (c. 144 CE) | The OT God is wrathful, jealous, violent, legalistic; the NT God is loving and transcendent | The OT and NT describe two genuinely different Gods; Jesus came from the higher one |
| Islamic critique (7th c. onward) | The God who "repents" of the flood (Gen 6:6) and "walks in the garden" (Gen 3:8) cannot be truly omniscient or transcendent | Such passages demonstrate Jewish anthropomorphism that Islam corrects with strict *tanzih* (divine incomparability) |
| Modern atheistic reading | A jealous, wrathful God who demands exclusive worship is evidence of anthropomorphism — a human-scale tribal deity, not the ultimate principle | Religion projects human tribal psychology onto the cosmos |

**The convergence:** The *form* of the critique — using the biblical God's own self-description as evidence against mainstream theology — recurs from the 2nd century Gnostics to 7th century Islamic *kalam* to 18th century Enlightenment deism. Each tradition uses the same textual evidence to reach a different conclusion, but the logical structure is identical: the God who declares exclusive divinity and experiences jealousy is anthropomorphically limited, not truly ultimate.

## Triple Name as Theological Architecture

Saklas / Yaldabaoth / Samael as a name-cluster parallels other triple-name divine figures in which multiple names encode multiple aspects of a single entity:

| Figure | Name 1 | Name 2 | Name 3 | Tradition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gnostic Demiurge | Yaldabaoth (origin) | Saklas (folly) | Samael (blindness) | Sethian |
| The accuser-being | [[satan-christian]] (the adversary) | [[lucifer]] (the light-bearer, fallen) | [[beelzebub]] (Lord of Flies) | Jewish-Christian |
| The Islamic rebel | [[iblis-shaytan]] (Iblis the refuser / Shaytan the adversary) | — | — | Islamic |
| The Hindu supreme | Brahma (creator) | Vishnu (sustainer) | Shiva (destroyer) | Hindu Trimurti |

The Gnostic triple-name is notable because each name encodes a *defect* (origin in chaos, foolishness, blindness) — it is a negative triple-name that damns its bearer rather than honoring him. This inversion — giving a divine figure three names that together constitute an accusation — is unique in its systematic theological deployment.
