# Rashnu

## Identity
**Rashnu** (Avestan *Rašnu*, "Justice / the Judge"; Pahlavi *Rashn*) is the **yazata of divine justice** in the Zoroastrian pantheon. Rashnu is **one of the three judges of the soul at the Chinvat Bridge** (with [[mithra-zoroastrian]] and [[sraosha]]), where the soul's good-deeds are weighed on a golden balance against its evil-deeds. The weighing determines whether the soul crosses the bridge into the Zoroastrian paradise or falls into the hell-zone below.

## Earliest attestation
- **Yašt 12** (the Rašn Yašt, Younger Avesta [[phase-2-004-yasna-younger-avesta]]): the principal Avestan hymn dedicated to Rashnu; enumerates the cosmic-territorial domains of Rashnu's judicial-witness (the seven climes, the four directions, the mountains and rivers).
- **Yasna 1.7, 2.7, 16.5, 25.5**: liturgical invocations of Rashnu paired with Mithra + Sraosha.
- **Pahlavi tradition** (Bundahishn 26, Dadestan i Denig): the developed post-mortem-judgment theology with Rashnu as the soul-weigher.
- **Arda Viraf Namag** ([[phase-4-086-arda-viraf-namag]]): the otherworld-vision narrative previews the post-mortem judgment of which Rashnu is presiding judge.

## The Chinvat-Bridge triad
At the Chinvat Bridge, the post-mortem soul faces three judges:

1. **Mithra** — overseer of the cosmic-contract; checks whether the soul kept its sworn obligations to truth + righteousness.
2. **Sraosha** — divine-word hearkener; checks whether the soul properly heard + obeyed the divine instruction.
3. **Rashnu** — weigher; places the soul's good-deeds and evil-deeds on the two pans of a golden balance.

The three-judge structure is uniquely articulated in the Zoroastrian system. The weighing-with-balance is Rashnu's specific signature.

## MASSIVE-WIN: cross-tradition soul-weighing chain
The Rashnu-with-golden-balance is structurally cognate with **multiple parallel templates** across the Old World:

- **Egyptian Maat-feather weighing-of-the-heart** in the Hall of Judgment (Book of the Dead spell 125). The earliest documented; possibly the source-template.
- **Christian Archangel Michael with scales** in late-antique + medieval iconography (the standard image of Michael in Catholic art from ~10th c. onward shows him holding scales for souls).
- **Islamic Mizan** (the cosmic balance at the Day of Judgment, Qur'an 7:8-9, 21:47, 23:102-103).
- **Tibetan Buddhist Yama-judgment** (the Lord of Death weighs souls' merit + demerit; Tibetan Book of the Dead).
- **Vedic post-mortem accounting** under Yama (the Indo-Iranian cognate of Yima).

The Rashnu / Maat / Michael / Mizan / Yama-balance chain is one of the cleanest documented cross-tradition cosmic-judicial-imagery convergences. Whether by PIE substrate (West 2007), Egyptian-influence (Bell 2005), Persian-period transmission (Boyce 1991, Hultgård 1998), or convergent-imagination (some Religious-Studies scholarship), the parallel is real.

## Cross-tradition: Rashnu → Christian Michael-with-scales
The Persian-period transmission window (-539 to -332) + the Hellenistic-Jewish synthesis period (-200 to 100 CE) provide the documented contact-channels by which the Persian Rashnu-with-balance template can have substrate-influenced the Hellenistic-Christian Michael-with-scales iconography. Russell 1987 *Zoroastrianism in Armenia* documents the Armenian-Christian inheritance of Zoroastrian judicial-yazata roles into Christian-angelic functions; Boyce 1991 generalizes the principle.

## Disputes
- **Source-of-template question.** Whether the cross-tradition soul-weighing template originates in Egypt (chronologically first), in PIE substrate (West 2007), or develops convergently is contested.
- **Persian → Christian directness.** Direct verbal-borrowing of Michael-scales imagery from Rashnu is not demonstrable; structural-influence-via-iconographic-substrate is the standard mid-position.
