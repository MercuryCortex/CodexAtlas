# Wākea

## Identity
Wākea is the Hawaiian **Sky Father** (*Wākea-nui-a-Kāne*, "The Great Expanse of Kāne") — the primordial male principle of Hawaiian cosmology, paired with **Papa** (Papatūānuku, Earth Mother) as the founding dyad from whom the Hawaiian Islands, the taro plant, and the Hawaiian people were created. His myth is the primary cosmogonic narrative of the **Kumulipo** (the great Hawaiian creation chant, composed for the birth of the chief Kalaninuiʻīamamao, c. 18th century), and its theological implications governed Hawaiian social life through the **kapu** (taboo) system until 1819.

## Earliest attestation
- Primary source: **Kumulipo** (Hawaiian creation chant), composed c. 18th century CE; preserved orally and first transcribed in the 19th century. Translated by Martha Beckwith (1951).
- Also attested in **Kamakau's *Ruling Chiefs of Hawaii*** (1961, compiled from 19th-century oral traditions) and **Malo's *Hawaiian Antiquities*** (1903).
- The underlying cosmogonic structure (sky-father + earth-mother) is shared across Polynesia and reflects ancestral Polynesian religion reconstructable to c. 1000–500 BCE.

## Mythological role
**The primordial union**: Wākea (Sky) and Papa (Earth) were the first pair. Their union was the generative act from which reality proceeds. In this, Wākea performs the same function as Ranginui and Papatūānuku in Māori cosmology (the closest structural parallel) — the sky-earth couple whose embrace is the world before differentiation.

**The creation of the Islands**: The Hawaiian Islands were born as children of Wākea and Papa — the islands emerge from their union as the first generation of offspring. The land is therefore divine in a literal genealogical sense: the earth of Hawaii is the body of Papa, and its form is Wākea's child.

**The taro as first child**: Before the human lineage, Wākea and Papa conceived a child who was born incomplete — a stillbirth from which the **kalo** (taro, *Colocasia esculenta*) plant grew. Taro is thus the **elder sibling of humanity**: the first child of Sky and Earth. This cosmogonic fact is the theological foundation of taro's sacred status in Hawaiian culture. **To cultivate, pound, and eat poi (pounded taro) is to participate in the founding act of creation — to be nourished by your elder sibling.** The head of the taro plant (*oha*) is ritually equated with the head of the family; the practice of farming taro in flooded paddies is a participation in cosmic genealogy.

**The incest and the kapu**: Wākea's union with **Hōʻohōkūkalani**, his own daughter (by Papa), produced a second miraculous child (the taro-child) and then human offspring. This transgression of the incest prohibition — committed by the primordial father himself — is the **etiological foundation of the kapu system**: the elaborate network of Hawaiian taboos (gender separation in eating, sacred-object restrictions, spatial prohibitions) are understood as the divine order established in the wake of Wākea's violation. The kapu system runs the civilization; its origin is the primordial transgression.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[ranginui]] + [[papatuanuku]]** — *ancient-identification*: the Māori Ranginui (Sky Father) / Papatūānuku (Earth Mother) pair is the direct ancestral cognate. Hawaiian Wākea + Papa and Māori Ranginui + Papatūānuku descend from the same Ancestral Polynesian cosmogony (~1000 BCE). Linguistic and structural evidence confirms direct genealogical relationship, not merely parallel structure.
- **[[dyaus-pita]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Vedic sky-father (Dyaus Pitā, cognate with Zeus, Jupiter) paired with Prithvī (Earth Mother) replicates the Wākea + Papa structure. The reconstructed Proto-Indo-European sky-father (*Dyēus Ph₂tēr*) is the IE equivalent of the Polynesian Wākea pattern — two independent instances of the sky-father/earth-mother cosmogonic pair.
- **[[uranus-greek]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Greek Uranus (Sky) + [[gaia]] (Earth) — the primordial sky-earth couple whose separation generates the world. No transmission; convergent cosmogonic structure across Pacific and Mediterranean.
- **[[tangaroa]]** — *tradition-internal*: in Hawaiian cosmology, Kāne (not Tangaroa/Kanaloa) is paired with Wākea; but across Polynesia, the Tangaroa-cognate (Tāʻaroa in Tahiti, Tagaloa in Samoa) sometimes occupies the role of supreme creator that Wākea holds in Hawaii.

## MASSIVE WIN: the sky-father/earth-mother pair

The **Wākea + Papa** cosmogony is one of the most widely documented structural patterns in world mythology:

| Sky Father | Earth Mother | Tradition | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wākea** | **Papa** | Hawaiian | Kumulipo, 18th c. |
| **Ranginui** | **Papatūānuku** | Māori | Ancestral Polynesian |
| **Dyaus Pitā** | **Prithvī** | Vedic | Rigveda |
| **Uranus** | **[[gaia]]** | Greek | Hesiod's *Theogony* |
| **Tengri** | **Etugen** | Mongol/Turkic | Steppe oral tradition |
| **An** | **Ki / Ninhursag** | Sumerian | *Eridu Genesis* |

These are neither all transmitted nor all invented independently — the truth is complex: some (Vedic/Greek) share a PIE ancestor; some (Hawaiian/Māori) share a Polynesian ancestor; others (Sumerian/Mongol) are independent convergences. The pattern is robust enough to suggest a very deep mythological structure.

## Iconography
Wākea is largely aniconic — Sky Father is the sky itself. His presence is expressed through the **upward openness** of the *heiau* (sacred platform) oriented to receive the sky. The taro plant as his first child is his primary visible emblem. The **feather capes** of Hawaiian chiefs encode his genealogical authority — each chief descends from Wākea, and the feathers that cover the cape represent the sky that is his body.
