# Itzpapalotl

Itzpapalotl (Nahuatl *Ītzpāpālōtl*, "Obsidian Butterfly" or "Clawed Butterfly") is a fearsome Aztec **warrior- and star-goddess** — a skeletal figure whose butterfly wings are edged with **obsidian and flint knives** and whose hands and feet end in jaguar claws. She is one of the *tzitzimimeh*, the terrifying star-demons who, at the death of the sun (an eclipse, or the feared end of the world at the close of a 52-year cycle), descend to devour humanity. As a mother-goddess of the nomadic **Chichimec** peoples of the north she ruled the lost paradise of **Tamoanchan**, the place of the broken flowering tree from which the gods were expelled. Her myths emphasize transformation and danger: she can appear as a beautiful woman to lure and destroy, and in the Chichimec legend of the Mimixcoa she is slain and burned, her ashes yielding the white and dark flint blades bound as a sacred bundle and carried as the tribal god. Itzpapalotl belongs with **[[coatlicue]]** and Cihuacoatl among the **devouring mother / earth-death goddesses** at the heart of Aztec religion's vision of life feeding on death.
