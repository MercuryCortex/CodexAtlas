# Shennong

## Identity

Shennong (神農, "Divine Farmer") is one of the Three Sovereigns of Chinese mythology — a divine culture-hero credited with teaching humanity both agriculture and herbal medicine. In the received mythological tradition, Shennong personally tasted hundreds of plants, minerals, and animals to classify their medicinal properties, suffering seventy-two poisonings in a single day (cured each time by tea — the *cha* plant, *Camellia sinensis*, being the antidote). This act of personal self-experimentation for the benefit of humanity is the founding myth of the Chinese pharmacopeia.

## The Tasting of the Herbs — Sacred Pharmacology's Paradigm Case

The Shennong myth encodes what the vault calls **sacred pharmacology**: the divine or spiritually charged investigator who personally ingests substances to map their effects for the community's benefit. Shennong is depicted with a transparent abdomen through which he could observe herbs' effects on his organs — the mythological image of **the physician whose body is the laboratory**.

He classified 365 substances (matching the 365 days of the year) into three tiers:
- **Upper** — tonics for longevity, non-toxic, sustainable (ginseng as the supreme plant, king of all tonics)
- **Middle** — therapeutic for specific conditions
- **Lower** — powerful, toxic, short-term medicines only

This tripartite pharmacological classification — empirically derived through divine self-experimentation — became the structure of the **Shennong Bencao Jing** (Divine Farmer's Materia Medica), the canonical pharmacopeia of Chinese medicine.

## Cross-Tradition Archetype: The Divine Pharmacologist

Shennong exemplifies the **divine pharmacologist archetype** — the divine or semi-divine being who acquires pharmacological knowledge through personal ordeal so that humanity has access to it:

- **Dhanvantari** (Hindu): emerges from the churning of the cosmic ocean bearing amrita (the complete medicine)
- **Soma** (Vedic): the plant-deity that must be pressed, filtered, and consumed to yield its divine knowledge
- **Asklepios** (Greek): the divine physician whose personal knowledge (including of the boundary between life and death) establishes the canon of healing
- **Shennong** (Chinese): tastes 365 herbs at personal risk to classify the complete pharmacopeia

All four are divine beings who function as the **first and authoritative pharmacologist** of their tradition — the one who knows what every substance does because they have personally experienced it.
