# Los

## The Figure

**Los** is Blake's creative-prophetic giant — the Imagination-faculty of the cosmic human **Albion**, fallen into semi-autonomous operation in the divided world, who works within time and history to forge the conditions of liberation. His unfallen name is **Urthona** (Earth-One, the creative generative power of the deep); in the fallen world he is called Los, and he labors at his **Anvil of Los** through the night of history, weeping and hammering, to prevent final chaos.

In the fallen cosmos created by **[[urizen]]**'s seizure of power, Los performs a double function:
1. He **binds Urizen** — encases the tyrant in a body of flesh, creating the human form as a container for the fallen reason. This is simultaneously imprisonment and compassion: without the body, Urizen's reason would dissolve into infinite abstraction; the body makes redemption possible.
2. He **forges time** — the Mundane Shell, the structure of history, which Los hammers out on his anvil. Time in Blake is not a prison but the *vehicle of redemption* — the series of moments through which the renovating act can occur. Los is the creator of duration.

> "Los was the fourth immortal starry one, & in the Earth / Of a bright Universe Empery attended day & night / Days & nights of revolving joy, Urthona was his name / In Eden; in the Auricular Nerves of Human life / Which is the Earth of Eden, he his Emanations propagated"
> — *The Four Zoas* (~1797)

## Los as Logos / Tikkun

The structural parallel to the Logos tradition is not incidental — it is the deepest architecture of Blake's mythology:

**Los as Logos:**
- The divine creative principle working *within* time (not from above it)
- The "Word" that creates and names — Los is the figure through whom Blake the poet speaks; the prophetic word IS Los's creative act
- Like the Johannine Logos who becomes flesh, Los descends into the conditions of the fallen world to work within them
- Like the Stoic immanent Logos, Los is the rational creative structure that prevents complete chaos

**Los as Tikkun:**
- Los's forge-work is explicitly cumulative and redemptive — each hammer-blow adds to the structure that will eventually enable Albion's awakening
- Los weeps as he works — he suffers within the repair process, as the Lurianic God contracts and suffers in the tzimtzum
- The final goal (Albion's awakening, Jerusalem's reunion) is Los's tikkun — the cosmic repair accomplished through ages of prophetic-creative labor
- Human creative acts (poetry, painting, engraving — Blake's own work) are *participations in Los's forge* — exactly as human Torah-practice participates in tikkun in Lurianic Kabbalah

---

## Enitharmon: Los's Emanation

Los's feminine counterpart and Emanation is **Enitharmon** — the Spirit of Nature in its ambivalence. She is simultaneously Los's creative partner (when united) and, when separated by the Fall, the principle that enforces female dominion and sexual repression over the Male. The Los-Enitharmon tension mirrors the Tiferet-Shekinah dynamic of Kabbalah: the divine masculine creative principle and his separated Feminine, whose reunion is the telos of the cosmic drama.

---

## Los in *Milton*

Blake's poem *Milton* (1804–1811) narrates the most direct encounter with Los: the poet John Milton descends from heaven to correct his own errors (Puritan rigidity that had made Urizen into God), enters Blake's foot, and in so doing, Blake encounters Los directly:

> "And I became One Man with him arising in my strength: / Twas too late now to recede. Los had entered into my soul."

This passage dramatizes the prophetic identification: the living poet IS Los, the creative-imaginative principle working through human art toward cosmic repair. Blake's entire oeuvre — the illuminated books, hand-engraved and hand-colored — is Los's forge-work.
