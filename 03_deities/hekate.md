# Hekate

## Identity
Greek goddess of liminal places — crossroads, doorways, thresholds — and of magic, witchcraft, the moon, and the dead. Hekate's earliest substantial Greek attestation is the famous hymn in Hesiod's *Theogony* (411–452), where she is given honors in earth, sea, and heaven; her Carian origin is widely argued. Hellenistic and Roman-era Hekate becomes the patroness of magical operations (the magical papyri, the *defixiones* — curse tablets — invoke her); she is the *prōtophanēs* and central cosmological figure of the *Chaldean Oracles*, the Neoplatonic theurgic-oracular collection. The triple-form *trimorphos* Hekate (three bodies joined at the back, looking three ways at a crossroad) is a Hellenistic iconographic development.

## Earliest attestation
- [[phase-2-009-hesiod-theogony-works-and-days]] — the *Theogony* hymn to Hekate.
- [[phase-4-022-chaldean-oracles]] — the central figure of the Chaldean theological-cosmological system.

## Mythological role
Liminal goddess, divider of realms, escort across thresholds. In the Demeter-Persephone Eleusinian cycle, Hekate hears Persephone's abduction and informs Demeter; thereafter Hekate is companion to the returning Persephone (Homeric Hymn to Demeter).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Artemis** — *ancient-identification*: Hellenistic syncretism frequently fuses Artemis and Hekate as moon-goddesses.
