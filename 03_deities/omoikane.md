# Omoikane

## Identity
Omoikane is the Shintō **kami of wisdom, intellect, and good counsel** — the divine planner whose name ("thought-combining") means the power to gather and weigh many thoughts at once. He is the strategist of the heavenly deities (the *Takamagahara* council), the one consulted when the gods face a problem they cannot solve by force.

## Earliest attestation
Recorded in the *Kojiki* (712 CE) and *Nihon Shoki* (720 CE) as the son of [[takamimusubi]], one of the primal creative kami. He is enshrined principally at Chichibu Shrine and at Togakushi, and is today invoked by students for success in study and examinations.

## Mythological role — the architect of the cave-opening
Omoikane's signature act is in the central Shintō myth of the **Ama-no-Iwato** (the Heavenly Rock Cave): when the sun-goddess [[amaterasu]] withdraws into the cave and the world goes dark, it is Omoikane who **devises the plan** to lure her out — assembling the long-crowing cocks, the great mirror, the jewels, and staging the dance of [[ame-no-uzume]] so that the gods' laughter rouses Amaterasu's curiosity. He likewise plans the pacification of the Central Land and the descent of Ninigi. Where other kami act, Omoikane *thinks*.

## Cross-tradition
Omoikane is an instance of the **divine wise-counsellor** — the intellect among the gods whose plans the others carry out, structurally akin to [[thoth]] (Egypt), to Enki/Ea (Mesopotamia), and to the counselling wisdom-figures of other pantheons. The resemblance is typological, not a documented borrowing.

## Iconography
Rarely depicted figurally; represented by his shrines and by the act of deliberation itself; associated in modern devotion with scholarship.
