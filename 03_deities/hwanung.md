# Hwanung

## Identity
Hwanung ("Son of Heaven") is the son of [[hwanin]] (the heavenly ruler) who descends to Mount Paektu (Taebaeksan, "Great White Mountain") with 3,000 divine followers to govern and civilize the human world. He is the **culture-hero** figure of Korean founding mythology — the deity who directly teaches humanity the 360 arts and practices of civilized life.

## The descent and civilizing mission
Per the *Samguk Yusa*:
- Hwanung descends to earth under a sandalwood tree (*sindan* — the World Tree axis of Korean cosmology)
- His three ministers govern **wind** (Pungbaek), **clouds** (Usin), and **rain** (Usa) — the three primary determinants of agricultural success
- He rules over agriculture, life-span, illness, justice, virtue, and evil — all the essential categories of human civilization
- He governs 360 matters of human affairs in total

The number 360 is significant — corresponding to the days of the year and implying that Hwanung's civilization encompasses the complete temporal cycle of human life.

## The bear-woman and the birth of Tangun
The most famous episode: a bear and a tiger both ask Hwanung to be transformed into humans. He gives them garlic and mugwort and instructs them to stay in a cave for 100 days without sunlight. The tiger gives up after 20 days and leaves. The bear persists for 21 days and is transformed into a woman (*ungnyeo*, "bear-woman"). She prays under the World Tree for a child; Hwanung temporarily takes human form and she conceives [[tangun]], who will become the founder of Gojoseon and the ancestor of all Koreans.

The bear-transformation narrative is one of the most distinctive elements of Korean mythology. The bear's patient persistence (in contrast to the tiger's impatience) is a founding statement about the Korean national character in Korean mythology.

## Mount Paektu
Hwanung's descent point, Mount Paektu (Baekdu Mountain, 2,744m, on the China-North Korea border), is the most sacred mountain in Korean cosmology. It is the spiritual source of Korean civilization and identity — both North Korean state mythology (Kim Il-sung's claimed birth near the mountain) and Korean shamanic tradition treat it as the cosmic axis of the Korean world.
