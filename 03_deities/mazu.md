# Mazu (Tianhou, Empress of Heaven)

## Identity
The principal sea-goddess of Chinese maritime culture, originally **Lin Moniang** — a historical woman of Meizhou Island, Fujian, traditionally dated 960–987 CE — who reputedly used shamanic powers to save sailors and was venerated after her death. Imperial canonization steps her progressively up: from *Lady* (Song 1156) → *Princess* (Song 1192) → *Empress of Heaven / Tianhou* (Qing 1737). The most prominent diaspora-tutelary deity of the Chinese maritime world: ~1,500 Mazu temples in Taiwan alone; major cult centers in Hong Kong, Macao, the Philippines, Singapore, Malaysia, and every Chinatown.

## Mythological role
**Maritime rescuer**: appears as a red light over storm-tossed ships; her two attendants Qianliyan (Thousand-Mile-Eye) and Shunfeng'er (Following-Wind-Ear) scout for sailors in distress. **Diaspora-cohesion**: Mazu pilgrimages cross the Taiwan Strait annually with hundreds of thousands of devotees, the most politically significant religious mobilization in modern Sinophone society.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[guanyin]]** — *folk-syncretism*: co-enshrined, iconographically conflated.
- **[[isis-egyptian]]** (as *Isis Pelagia*) / **[[mary-theotokos]]** (as *Stella Maris*) — *parallel-motif*: the trans-Mediterranean / trans-Pacific feminine-divine sea-protector type.
- **[[yemoja]]** — *parallel-motif*: Yoruba/Afro-Atlantic maritime mother-goddess.
