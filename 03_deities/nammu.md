# Nammu

## Identity

Nammu (also Namma) is the Sumerian goddess of the primordial sea — the engendering waters that existed before the cosmos and from which the first gods were born. Her name is written with the cuneiform sign **ENGUR**, the cosmic groundwater (closely associated with the Abzu / [[apsu]], the fresh subterranean ocean). In the Sumerian cosmogonic tradition she is "the mother who gave birth to heaven and earth" — that is, the mother of An (the sky) and [[ki-sumerian]] (the earth), whose separation produces the structured cosmos — and the mother of [[enki-ea]], the god of wisdom and the fresh waters. Unlike most of the great Mesopotamian deities, Nammu has no developed iconography and little active cult: she is a cosmological principle, the watery origin behind the visible world.

## Mythological role

Nammu's most important narrative appearance is in the myth *Enki and Ninmah*, where she is the agent who sets the creation of humankind in motion. When the lesser gods complain of their labor, it is Nammu who rouses her son Enki from sleep and instructs him to fashion human beings from clay drawn over the Abzu, so that they might bear the gods' burden. This places Nammu at the headwater of two of the deepest Mesopotamian cosmogonic themes: the emergence of the ordered cosmos from primordial water, and the creation of humanity to serve the gods.

## Cross-tradition significance (the upstream of "the deep")

Nammu is the **Sumerian prototype of [[tiamat]]**. The Babylonian *Enuma Elish* opens with Tiamat, the primordial salt sea, as mother of the first gods — a role that maps directly onto Nammu's. Because the *Enuma Elish* is a relatively late (second-millennium) Akkadian composition drawing on far older Sumerian conceptions, Nammu represents the older stratum of the primordial-sea-mother who recurs across the Near East: **Nammu (Sumer) → Tiamat (Babylon) → the *tehom* "deep" of Genesis 1:2 → Nun (Egypt)**. For an investigation of how the Hebrew creation account's "the deep" carries an inheritance of older Near Eastern primordial-waters theology, Nammu is the load-bearing upstream node — the earliest named form of the primordial sea-mother.

- **[[tiamat]]** — *prototype-ancestor*: the Babylonian primordial sea-mother developed from the Sumerian Nammu-type.
- **[[nun]]** / **[[naunet]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Egyptian primordial waters occupy the same cosmogonic slot.
- **[[enki-ea]]** — *genealogical*: her son, god of the fresh waters and of the creation of humankind.

## Disputes

The exact relationship between Nammu and the Abzu/Engur is debated — whether Nammu *is* the personified groundwater or a goddess closely associated with it. The directness of the Nammu→Tiamat development is also a matter of degree: the two are not simply equated, but Nammu is the best-attested earlier Sumerian figure occupying Tiamat's cosmogonic role, and the genealogical resemblance is widely accepted.
