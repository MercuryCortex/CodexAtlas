# Resheph

Resheph (Ugaritic *ršp*, "the burner / flame") is the West-Semitic god of plague and war — the archer whose arrows are pestilence. Attested from Ebla (c. 2400 BCE, as *Rasap*) through Ugarit, the Phoenician coast, Cyprus, and into New Kingdom Egypt (where Resheph is adopted as a warrior-god under Amenhotep II), he is one of the most widely diffused Levantine deities. In the Ugaritic god-lists he is equated with Mesopotamian **[[nergal]]** (plague, war, underworld), and the ritual texts make him a gatekeeper of the netherworld ("Resheph, the doorkeeper of the sun-goddess Shapash").

## Cross-tradition reach
Resheph's reach across the ancient Mediterranean is a textbook case of the wiring this Atlas exists to surface. On Cyprus he is identified with **Apollo** (Apollo-Resheph at Idalion and Kition, in bilingual Phoenician–Greek inscriptions), and the plague-shooting Apollo of *Iliad* I — whose arrows bring pestilence on the Achaean camp — is widely read against this Levantine background (Münnich 2013). In the Hebrew Bible, by contrast, Resheph is *demythologized*: he survives only as the common noun *rešep* ("plague," "flame," "spark") and, in the archaic theophany of Habakkuk 3:5, as a faint personified attendant in Yahweh's war-march ("before him went Deber/pestilence, and Resheph followed at his feet") — a tier-3 appearance, not a god worshipped in Israel.
