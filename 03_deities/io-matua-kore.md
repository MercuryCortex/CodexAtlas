# Io-matua-kore

## Identity
A supreme being recorded in some Māori iwi traditions — most prominently in material gathered by Stephenson Percy Smith from the tohunga Te Matorohanga (via Hoani Te Whatahoro) and published as *The Lore of the Whare-wananga* (1913–15). Io is said to be the absolute first being, parentless, hidden, the source from whom Ranginui and Papatūānuku and all the other atua descend. The Io tradition is *not* present in all Māori cosmogonic accounts — many iwi versions begin with Te Kore → Te Pō → Ranginui-Papatūānuku without an earlier Io. The status of the Io tradition is the most-contested question in Māori religious-studies historiography.

## Earliest attestation
Published attestation: Smith / Whatahoro / Te Matorohanga, *The Lore of the Whare-wananga* (1913–15). Specific iwi traditions (Tūhoe, Ngāti Kahungunu) hold Io to be an authentic pre-contact tradition transmitted in restricted *whare wānanga* (esoteric schools).

## Mythological role
In the Io tradition, Io thinks the cosmos into being; commands the genealogical descent from Te Kore (Nothing) through Te Pō (Night) through to the named gods (Ranginui, Papatūānuku, and their children); is approached only by senior tohunga, never by ordinary people, who address the lower atua. Io's name and full epithets (*Io-matua-kore*, *Io-mata-ngaro*, *Io-taketake*) are themselves objects of restricted knowledge.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **Christian God** — *folk-syncretism* (contested): some scholars (Simmons 1976; Mikaere 2011) argue Io is at minimum a post-contact tradition shaped by exposure to Christianity, possibly an attempt by senior tohunga to present Māori religion to European audiences as proto-monotheistic. Other scholars (some Tūhoe sources; some defenders of the Whatahoro material) argue Io is authentically pre-contact in those iwi where he is attested, with the structural parallel to Christian theism a genuine but independent feature.

## Disputes
- **Pre-contact authenticity.** The Sambridge-Simmons-Mikaere critical position holds Io is post-contact or substantially post-contact-shaped. Defenders point to: specific iwi insistence on transmission lineage; structural distinction from Christian theology; recorded ceremonial vocabulary. Current scholarly consensus: Io is iwi-specific, not pan-Māori; pre-contact attestation in some iwi is plausible but documentation is post-contact and contamination is impossible to rule out.

## Iconography
Aniconic. Io has no representation.
