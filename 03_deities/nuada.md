# Nuada

## Identity
First king of the Tuatha Dé Danann; loses his right arm at the **First Battle of Mag Tuired** against the Fír Bolg, which under Celtic kingly-purity requirements (the king's body must be unmaimed) forces him to abdicate in favor of the Fomorian half-blood Bres. The smith **Dian Cécht** fashions him a **silver arm** that fully functions; his son Miach later replaces the silver with regrown flesh, allowing Nuada to retake the kingship. He yields to Lugh at the *Samildánach* moment before the Second Battle, recognizing the multi-skilled hero as the right leader for the war.

## Mythological role
**The one-handed sovereign**: Dumézil 1948 famously interprets the recurring one-handed sovereign motif (Nuada / Tyr / Roman one-handed Cocles / Scaevola) as expressing the Indo-European theology of sovereignty-by-sacrifice — the king who must lose part of himself to be king. **Nodens at Lydney**: a major Romano-British healing temple in Gloucestershire dedicated to *Nodens* (~ Nuada-form) was active in the late 4th c. CE, with inscriptions documenting healing-incubation practices — the only Celtic deity with a substantial Romano-British dedicated healing temple.
