# The Dagda

## Identity
**"The Good God"** — chief of the Tuatha Dé Danann in Irish mythology. Father of [[brigid]] and Aengus; consort (at Samhain ford) of [[the-morrigan]] and lover of Boann (the river-goddess of the Boyne). His three principal attributes:
- **The club** (*lorg mór*): so heavy it must be dragged on wheels; one end kills, the other restores life.
- **The cauldron of plenty** (*coire ansic*): one of the Four Treasures of the Tuatha Dé; no one leaves it unsatisfied — the structural ancestor of the **Grail / horn-of-plenty** cycle of medieval Christian Celtic-substrate romance.
- **The harp Uaithne**: controls the order of the seasons; the Dagda must reclaim it from the Fomorians.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[odin]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: chief, magician, father.
- **The Grail** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Dagda's cauldron is the deepest Celtic-pagan ancestor of the medieval Christianized Grail / Cauldron of Annwn (Welsh) cycle (Loomis 1949; Mac Cana 1970).
