# Coyote (Mąʼii)

The Navajo trickster figure — one of the Holy People but profoundly different in character from the beneficent figures like [[changing-woman]] or [[spider-woman-navajo]]. Coyote (Mąʼii) is not evil but *necessary*: his disruptive, impulsive, often sexually motivated actions introduce complications into cosmic order that the other Holy People must then resolve, thereby driving the creation narrative forward. The origin of death in Navajo tradition is attributed to Coyote: in the primordial world, people debated whether the dead should return to life; Coyote threw a stone into the water and declared that if it sank, death would be permanent — it sank. His presence in Navajo ceremonial narrative is consistently disruptive and frequently comic, but he is not to be ignored or dismissed. Navajo tradition holds that if you dream of Coyote, you must have a Coyote ceremony performed to avoid its ill effects.

For the cross-tradition trickster archetype (Coyote / Eshu / Hermes / Loki / Anansi), see [[eshu]] and [[tradition-navajo]].
