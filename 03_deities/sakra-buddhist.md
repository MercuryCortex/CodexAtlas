# Śakra

Buddhist king of the gods, ruler of the Trāyastriṃśa ("Heaven of the Thirty-three") on the summit of Mount Sumeru — the second of the six Kāmadhātu heavens in Buddhist cosmology. Śakra is the Buddhist re-instantiation of Vedic [[indra]], preserved across the canonical tradition with the Vedic name intact in his honorific (*Śakra Devānām Indra*, "Powerful One, Lord of the Gods").

## The doctrinal demotion

Buddhism's handling of Indra is the cleanest comparative-religion case study in how an absorbing tradition can subordinate rather than reject a source-tradition's chief god. Where Zoroastrianism demonized Indra as a daēva (see [[asura-deva-inversion]] and [[verethraghna-zoroastrian]]), Buddhism kept him — but moved him below the Buddha and the bodhisattvas in the cosmic hierarchy. The *Sakka-pañha Sutta* (Dīgha Nikāya 21) narrates Śakra approaching the Buddha as a disciple to ask questions about Dharma. The image is doctrinally load-bearing: the king of the Vedic gods becomes a student of the Awakened One.

## Iconography

Śakra carries Indra's vajra (thunderbolt) and rides Indra's white elephant Airāvata. In East Asian Esoteric Buddhism (Mikkyō, Mizong) he wears warrior armor and bears the vajra, often paired with [[brahma]]/Bonten as the Buddha's flanking guardians. In Japanese Tendai and Shingon iconography Taishakuten is one of the Twelve Devas (Jūniten).
