# The Four Sons of Horus

The Four Sons of Horus — **Imsety, Hapy, Duamutef, and Qebehsenuef** — are the Egyptian funerary genii who **guard the internal organs of the mummified dead**, and so among the most familiar of all Egyptian religious figures, for their faces top the **canopic jars** found in every well-equipped burial. When a body was embalmed, the major viscera were removed and placed in four jars, each protected by one of the sons, each son in turn watched over by a **protective goddess**, and each assigned to a **cardinal direction**: **Imsety** (human-headed; the **liver**; with **[[isis-egyptian|Isis]]**; south); **Hapy** (baboon-headed; the **lungs**; with **[[nephthys|Nephthys]]**; north — not to be confused with Hapi the Nile-flood god); **Duamutef** (jackal-headed; the **stomach**; with **[[neith|Neith]]**; east); and **Qebehsenuef** (falcon-headed; the **intestines**; with **[[selket|Selket]]**; west). Themselves sons of **[[horus|Horus]]**, they figure already in the **Pyramid Texts** as helpers who assist the king's ascent, and throughout Egyptian history they ensure that the dismembered body is kept whole and protected so that the deceased may live again. The Four Sons of Horus embody the Egyptian funerary logic of **fourfold protection** — body, organ, goddess, and direction bound together to guard the dead.
