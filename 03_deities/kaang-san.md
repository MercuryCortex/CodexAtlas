# Kaang

## Identity
Creator-trickster god of the San (Bushman) traditions of southern Africa. Kaang takes the form of the praying mantis — his most-iconic animal-form — and through his ambiguous, often comically inept, often profound actions, he creates and shapes the cosmos. The /Xam-San corpus (collected by Bleek and Lloyd 1870s–1880s in the Cape Colony from /Xam-language informants whose language is now extinct) is the richest documented source of Kaang/Kaggen narratives. Lewis-Williams (1981, 2002) integrates the textual corpus with San rock-art interpretation.

## Earliest attestation
San traditions are among the oldest continuous religious systems on Earth — San rock-art in southern Africa is securely dated to at least 27,000 years BP (Apollo 11 cave, Namibia), with some sites pushing earlier. The specific Kaang/Kaggen narrative-corpus comes from the Bleek-Lloyd /Xam archive (1870s) — a salvage-anthropology corpus that preserves /Xam testimony before /Xam language and culture went extinct in the late 19th c.

## Mythological role
Kaang created the eland (the antelope he loved most) and was angered when humans hunted it; he sometimes makes things and breaks them; he gives the moon its phase-cycle from the slipper he threw; he is killed and resurrected in various narratives. He is simultaneously the cosmic creator and the trickster-fool whose mistakes shape the world that humans must live in. The morally-ambiguous-creator pattern is unusual in world religion (most creator-figures are either purely-good or purely-cosmic-impersonal); Kaang is BOTH the supreme creator AND the trickster, which makes him one of the most theologically distinctive figures in comparative religion.

## MASSIVE WIN — One of the oldest documented religious figures + creator-trickster fusion
San religion is among the oldest documented continuous religious systems on Earth, with rock-art attestation pushing into the late Pleistocene. The Kaang/Kaggen narrative corpus offers a window into a creator-figure type that is rare cross-culturally: the creator who is ALSO the trickster, the supreme who is ALSO the fool. Most world traditions split these into different figures (creator-high-god + trickster-intermediate-figure — see [[olodumare]] vs [[eshu]], [[odin]] vs [[loki]], etc.). The Kaang fusion of the two suggests a possibly-older theological stratum, before the high-god/trickster differentiation that characterizes most agricultural-and-pastoralist religion. Hyde (1998) explicitly notes this — Kaang/Kaggen is one of the case studies in his theoretical argument that the trickster is fundamental to the religious imagination, not a later corruption.

Lewis-Williams (1981, 2002) integrates the Kaang corpus with San rock-art interpretation — much of the rock-art (specifically the trance-dance and rain-animal imagery) is theologically Kaang-oriented, depicting the shamanic *!gi:xa* in altered-state contact with Kaang and the spirit-world. This connects the Kaang theology to the broader San *!gi:xa* shamanic practice, one of the best-documented contemporary hunter-gatherer shamanic systems.

## Iconography
The mantis above all; the eland (Kaang's favorite animal); the bow and arrow; rock-art trance-dance imagery showing shamans contacting Kaang in altered states (Lewis-Williams 1981).
