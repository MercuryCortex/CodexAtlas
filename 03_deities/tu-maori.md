# Tūmatauenga

## Identity
Māori god of war and the patron of humanity. Tūmatauenga ("Tū of the angry face") was, in the cosmogonic narrative, the most fiercely active of the children of Ranginui and Papatūānuku — he wanted to *kill* his parents to free his siblings, while Tāne advocated separating them. After the separation, the brothers fought each other in various ways; Tūmatauenga overcame Tāwhirimātea (storm) and afterwards "ate" all his brothers' children — that is, humans (the descendants of Tūmatauenga) consume the products of Tāne (trees, birds), Tangaroa (fish), Rongo (cultivated plants), Haumia-tiketike (uncultivated plants). Tūmatauenga is therefore particularly the *human* god — the patron of warrior-humans whose appetite supplies all human ritual life.

## Earliest attestation
Māori oral tradition; documented in 19th-c. compilations.

## Mythological role
In the Rangi-Papa separation narrative, Tūmatauenga proposes killing the parents; he is overruled by Tāne. He then fights his brothers, asserting the human-dominance position in the cosmos. Pre-contact Māori warfare was conducted under Tū's auspices; *tangata whenua* (humans, "people of the land") are particularly his.

## Iconography
Weapons; fierce facial expression (the *pūkana* / *whetero* grimaces of haka derive from Tū's facial aspect).
