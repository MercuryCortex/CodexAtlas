# Azazel

## Identity
A double-natured figure in the Hebrew Bible and Second Temple Jewish tradition. In Leviticus 16, on Yom Kippur, the high priest places the people's sins on a goat *la-Azazel* — "for Azazel" — and dispatches it into the wilderness. Whether Azazel here is a demon (the dominant ancient and modern reading), a place-name, or an abstract "removal" is debated; the apocalyptic-Enochic literature settles the question by making Azazel a *fallen-angelic leader*: in 1 Enoch 6–11 (Book of the Watchers), Asael / Azazel is one of two chief Watcher-leaders (with Shemiḥazah) who lead 200 angels in transgressing the divine order — they take human wives and teach forbidden knowledge (metallurgy, weaponry, cosmetics, sorcery). The Watcher transgression introduces evil into humanity; the resulting Nephilim, the giants, devastate the earth and require the divine flood to remove.

## Earliest attestation
- [[phase-2-011-hebrew-bible-d-source]] — Leviticus 16 (P) is the foundational biblical text.
- [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — the Book of the Watchers gives the developed Watcher-Azazel narrative.
- [[phase-4-034-quran]] — the related Islamic figure of *Iblīs / Shayṭān* who refused to bow to Adam may functionally parallel some Azazel traditions.

## Mythological role
- Scapegoat-recipient (Lev 16).
- Watcher-leader teacher of forbidden civilization arts (1 En 8).
- Bound in the wilderness until eschatological judgment (1 En 10:4–8).

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[satanael]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: some Slavonic-Bogomil cosmologies map Azazel-Asael-related material onto Satanael.
- Some traditions later conflate Azazel with Iblis (Islamic tradition).

## Disputes
- The Leviticus text: demon, place, or process?
- The Enochic Watcher narrative's relation to Genesis 6:1–4.
