# Cabracan

## Identity

Cabracan (K'iche' **Kab'rakan**, "Earthquake" — the word is still used for "earthquake" in some Maya languages) is the younger son of the false "sun" [[vucub-caquix]] and the brother of the mountain-maker [[zipacna]]. Where Zipacna raises the mountains, Cabracan **shakes them down**: he is the personified earthquake, the toppler and leveler of the great hills, boastful of his world-shaking strength.

## Mythological role

Cabracan is the third and last of the arrogant powers the Hero Twins remove in the first part of the *Popol Vuh* (after Vucub Caquix and Zipacna). Hearing of a mighty mountain that Cabracan has not yet brought down, the Twins [[hunahpu]] and Xbalanque lead him toward it, promising to show him a peak worthy of his strength. Along the way they hunt birds with their blowguns and roast one, **coating it with white earth (or a poison)**; the gluttonous Cabracan eats it, and the tainted food saps his power. Weakened, he is easily bound and buried by the Twins. With his fall, the prideful false powers are gone, and the world is made ready for the rising of the true sun and the proper order of things.

## Cross-tradition significance

Cabracan completes the *Popol Vuh*'s opening cycle of the **humbling of false and arrogant powers** — the clearing-away of the boastful pretenders (the false sun Vucub Caquix and his earth-shaping sons) that must precede the establishment of true cosmic order and the real sun. As the personified earthquake, he reflects the Mesoamerican awareness of seismic catastrophe woven into mythology; and as a brute force overcome not by strength but by the Twins' **cunning**, he belongs to the widespread mythic theme of the trickster-hero who defeats the giant by wit rather than might.
