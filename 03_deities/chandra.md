# Chandra

## Identity
Chandra (Sanskrit चन्द्र, "the Shining One") is the Vedic and Hindu lunar deity — at once the moon as celestial body, the ruler of months and time, and, in one of the most distinctive equations in Vedic cosmology, identical with **Soma**: the ritual drink pressed from the Soma plant in sacrifice *is* the moon. The moon's waxing phase represents the Soma vessel filling; the waning phase represents the gods drinking it down over the fortnight. This identification — astronomical body = ritual substance = divine person — is a uniquely Vedic theological compression with no close parallel in other ancient traditions.

In the Rig Veda, Soma dominates (~120 hymns; the entire Book IX is the Soma Mandala). The figure most clearly "Chandra as moon-god" emerges in RV 10.85, the famous marriage hymn where Sūryā (Surya's daughter) is given in marriage to Soma/Chandra — establishing the moon as divine bridegroom and the 27 Nakshatras (the lunar mansions through which the moon travels) as his wives, daughters of the progenitor Daksha. The Sanskrit equation **manas = moon** (both *man-* root) is not merely poetic: in Vedic and later Āyurvedic thought the moon literally governs mental activity, emotional rhythms, and the fluid body.

In Puranic tradition Chandra becomes more individuated and distinct from the Soma-drink, and his mythology expands: he is born from the churning of the cosmic ocean (Samudra Manthan), he illicitly loves Tārā (wife of the planet-deity Bṛhaspati/Jupiter), and from that union Budha (Mercury) is born — creating the lunar dynasty (*Candravamśa*) that includes the Pandavas and the Kauravas of the Mahābhārata.

## Earliest attestation
- **Rig Veda Book X** (~-1200 to -1000 oral) — RV 10.85, the Sūryā wedding hymn, is the canonical early attestation linking Soma and the moon as bridegroom. ([[phase-2-001-rig-veda-family-books]])
- **Rig Veda Book IX** (~-1200 to -1000) — the entire Soma Mandala; Soma here is still primarily the ritual drink, but the moon identification is implicit and becomes explicit in later Brāhmaṇa texts.
- **Atharva Veda** (~-1200 to -900) — lunar timing hymns; Chandra governs the lunar fortnight rhythms for ritual timing. ([[phase-2-003-atharva-veda]])
- **Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa** (~-800 to -600) — the Chandra-Soma = moon equation is made theologically explicit; the waxing/waning = filling/drinking equation is formalized. ([[phase-2-006-brahmanas-aranyakas]])
- **Vishnu Purana, Bhāgavata Purana** (~400–1000 CE) — the Candravamśa (lunar dynasty genealogy), the Tārā-harana myth, the Samudra Manthan birth of Chandra are elaborated.

## Mythological role
Chandra operates across four registers:

1. **Lord of the Nakshatras.** The 27 Nakshatra wives receive Chandra in turn, one per day of the sidereal lunar month. He is said to favor Rohiṇī above the others, angering Daksha, who curses him with waxing and waning — a myth that gives cosmological meaning to the lunar cycle.

2. **Time-keeper.** The Sanskrit calendar (*pañcānga*) is lunar-solar; Chandra's cycle defines the month (*māsa*). The names of the twelve months are named after the Nakshatra in which the full moon occurs — Chandra is the clock of the cosmic schedule.

3. **Soma-equation.** The ritual Soma drink *is* Chandra; when priests press and offer Soma, they are feeding the gods with the substance of the moon, which depletes it, and when the moon refills it is because the plant regenerates. This is a closed cosmological loop that makes ritual sacrifice continuous with astronomical observation.

4. **Lord of the Mind (manas).** In Vedic-Āyurvedic physiology, the moon governs the mind, the fluid humors, the menstrual cycle, emotional tides, and the *ojas* (vital fluid). The moon-mind equation (*candras tato manaḥ*) in the Puruṣa Sūkta (RV 10.90) is the canonical statement.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[thoth]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Both are lunar deities governing the mind, measurement, writing, and wisdom. Thoth is the Egyptian moon-god who invented writing and measured time; Chandra governs *manas* (mind) and defines the calendar. No documented Bronze Age Egypt → India transmission; independent convergence on the moon-as-mind pattern. (Macdonell 1897)
- **[[nanna-sin]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Nanna/Sin is the Mesopotamian lunar deity governing time-keeping, wisdom, and the calendar — structurally parallel to Chandra's role as lord of months and mind. Nanna-Sin's waxing/waning were also associated with divine favor and curse, paralleling the Daksha-curse myth. (Pingree 1976 — no direct transmission)
- **[[tsukuyomi]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: The Shinto moon-god who rules a separate realm (night) in tension with the solar Amaterasu; structural parallel to Chandra's distinct domain from Surya, and the cosmic separation of sun and moon after a quarrel.
- **[[selene]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: Greek lunar goddess; lunar chariot motif shared with Chandra's silver chariot; both govern the night sky and tides; Indo-European parallel without demonstrated direct transmission.

**The Chandra-Soma cosmological identification** is a uniquely Vedic pattern with structural analogies in two other traditions:
- **Eucharist / Dionysus**: the ritual drink *is* the divine body — wine is the blood of Christ (Christianity) or the embodiment of Dionysus (Greek mysteries). The pattern "sacred drink = living god" bridges all three.
- **27 Nakshatra mansions ↔ 28 Arabic Manzil ↔ 28 Chinese Xiu**: three independent traditions mapped the moon's path through the sky into 27–28 lunar stations, creating a shared astronomical-mythological infrastructure from India to China to Arabia. The number and approximate positions converge despite the traditions' independence — strong evidence for early astronomical diffusion along trade routes by ~-500 BCE.

## Iconography
- White or silver complexion; the crescent moon as crown attribute.
- Silver chariot drawn by ten white horses (in Puranic iconography) or by white antelopes in some regional traditions.
- Two arms holding the Soma cup and a mace (gadā); in some traditions a lotus.
- Accompanied by a crescent above his head; his vāhana (vehicle) in some traditions is a white antelope.

## Disputes
- **Chandra vs. Soma distinction**: In the Rigveda the two are effectively one identity; the Puranic tradition individuates them. The question of when and how the distinction emerges is debated in Vedic philology (Gonda 1986).
- **Nakshatra count — 27 vs. 28**: Most Indian traditions list 27 Nakshatras; some list 28 (adding Abhijit). The relationship of this count to the Arabic 28-Manzil system and whether there was early transmission or convergent observation remains debated.
- **Soma plant identity**: The botanical identity of the original Soma plant (pressed for the ritual drink) is unsettled — candidates range from *Ephedra* species to *Amanita muscaria* to *Peganum harmala*. The Chandra-Soma identification means that this unsettled botanical question has cosmological implications.
