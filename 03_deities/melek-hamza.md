# Hamza ibn Ali (as Universal Intellect)

## Identity
**Hamza ibn Ali ibn Ahmad** (fl. 996–1021 CE) is, in the Druze theological system, not merely a historical person but the historical incarnation of the **al-ʿaql al-kullī** (Universal Intellect) — the highest cosmic hypostasis, the divine Mind that mediates between the absolute One and all creation. The Druze tradition is a syncretic offshoot of Ismaili Shia Islam that crystallized around the reign of the Fatimid caliph **al-Hakim bi-Amr Allah** (r. 996–1021 CE) in Cairo; the Druze hold that al-Hakim is the divine manifestation (*tajallī*) of the One — God appearing in history as a ruler — and that Hamza ibn Ali is the Universal Intellect who appeared alongside him as cosmic guide and teacher.

The primary text is the **Rasa'il al-Hikma** ("Epistles of Wisdom"), a collection of 111 epistles attributed to Hamza and his associates, preserved in the [[phase-5-059-rasail-al-hikma]] and considered the Druze sacred scripture (though its full contents are restricted to initiated members of the community, *uqqal* = "the knowers").

The theological system maps explicitly onto Neoplatonic emanation: the divine One (al-Hakim as *tajallī*) → Universal Intellect (Hamza as al-ʿaql al-kullī) → Universal Soul (a second cosmic principle) → the Word (*kalima*) → the Preceding One (*al-sābiq*) — five cosmic principles corresponding to the five-colored Druze star. This is Plotinus's *One → Nous → World Soul* structure, translated into Islamic theological language and rooted in a specific historical moment (Fatimid Cairo, 996–1021 CE).

The historical [[hamza-ibn-ali]] (as person) is distinct from Hamza's theological role as cosmic hypostasis documented here.

## Earliest attestation
- **Rasa'il al-Hikma** (c. 1017–1021 CE): the epistles were written and circulated during the reign of al-Hakim; they constitute the primary attestation of the entire theological system.
- The Druze community itself: founded c. 1017 CE in Fatimid Egypt; the living community in Syria, Lebanon, Israel, and Jordan is the continuous attestation.
- **Druze community restrictions**: the full text of the Rasa'il al-Hikma has been restricted to initiated members (*uqqal*); scholarly access has been partial. The most complete scholarly editions are in Arabic.

## Mythological role
In the Druze theological system, Hamza ibn Ali's role is to be the **cosmic intellect made historical** — the rational ordering principle of creation that took human form to reveal the esoteric truth (*hikma*) that underlies all exoteric religion (Islam, Christianity, Judaism). The Druze theology posits that all previous prophets and divine manifestations were partial revelations; the al-Hakim / Hamza pair constitutes the final and complete disclosure of divine reality.

The five cosmic principles (Hamza's system) form the structure of all being:
1. **Universal Intellect** (*al-ʿaql al-kullī*) — Hamza ibn Ali
2. **Universal Soul** (*al-nafs al-kulliyya*) — Ismail al-Tamimi
3. **The Word** (*al-kalima*) — Muhammad ibn Wahb al-Qurashi
4. **The Preceding One** (*al-sābiq*) — Salama ibn Abd al-Wahhab
5. **The Following One** (*al-tālī*) — Baha al-Din al-Samuki

This five-fold hierarchy of cosmic principles instantiating as historical persons is the defining innovation of Druze cosmology — a Neoplatonic emanation theory made concrete as biography.

The Druze also practice **reincarnation** within the Druze community: Druze souls reincarnate as Druze, and liberation from the reincarnation cycle (*tanasukh*) comes through accumulation of virtue and knowledge over multiple lives.

## Cross-tradition identifications
- **[[nous-plotinus]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the most direct parallel; Hamza as al-ʿaql al-kullī occupies precisely the position of Nous in the Plotinian scheme — the first emanation from the One, the pure cosmic intellect (De Smet 1995).
- **[[nous-hermetic]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Hermetic Nous as the divine mind that descends to reveal saving knowledge to humanity; Hamza's role as Universal Intellect who appears historically to teach the *hikma* (wisdom) parallels the Hermetic Nous-as-revealer.
- **[[logos-stoic]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Stoic Logos as the rational cosmic ordering principle — precisely what Hamza represents as Universal Intellect (Leaman 2002).
- **[[sophia-gnostic]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Gnostic Sophia (divine Wisdom descending from the Pleroma to mediate and reveal); the Druze hikma (wisdom tradition) is a direct parallel in structure and function to Sophia's cosmic-wisdom-mediating role (De Smet 1995).
- **[[the-one-plotinus]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the relationship Hamza-as-Nous maintains to al-Hakim-as-the-One replicates the Plotinian One → Nous structure.
- **[[melek-taus]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Yazidi Melek Taus as the supreme angelic intermediary between hidden God and creation; Hamza fills an analogous intermediary-cosmic-intellect role in another closed esoteric Middle Eastern tradition.
- **[[nur-muhammadi]]** — *scholarly-parallel*: the Islamic concept of the Muhammadan Light (nur muhammadi) as the primal cosmic light from which creation flows, which certain Sufi and Shia traditions treat as a cosmic hypostasis — parallel to Hamza's Universal Intellect as the primal rational principle from which all being emanates.
- **[[iblis-shaytan]]** — *scholarly-contrast*: the Druze theology explicitly posits a cosmic anti-intellect principle opposing Hamza's Universal Intellect — the cosmic adversary who generates ignorance and delusion as Iblis generates evil. The Druze eschatology involves the final defeat of this opposing principle.
- **[[allah]]** — *theological-context*: Waheguru and Allah are both the absolute divine reality that Hamza's role is to mediate; the Druze theological system builds on and radically reinterprets Islamic monotheism.

## Iconography
The Druze have no anthropomorphic divine images — consistent with Islamic aniconism and the esoteric tradition's focus on inner wisdom rather than outward form. The primary visual emblem is the **five-colored star** (yellow, green, red, blue, white), representing the five cosmic principles — with Hamza's Universal Intellect as the first principle (corresponding to one of the star's points). The colors have esoteric correspondences within the Rasa'il.

## Disputes
- **Historical vs. theological Hamza.** Scholarly debate centers on separating the historical Hamza ibn Ali (a real Ismaili religious figure active in Fatimid Cairo) from his theological elaboration as Universal Intellect. The historical figure may have genuinely believed in al-Hakim's divinity; the cosmic-hypostasis interpretation is a theological development of his own writings.
- **Al-Hakim's death.** Al-Hakim disappeared in 1021 CE (likely murdered). The Druze interpret this as an occultation (*ghayba*) rather than death — the divine manifestation withdrew and will return. This eschatological structure parallels Twelver Shia Islam's hidden Mahdi.
- **Druze and Islam.** Whether the Druze are Muslims is disputed. Orthodox Sunni Islam does not recognize the Druze as Muslim. The Druze themselves have maintained a distinct identity while formally claiming Islamic identity for centuries of political survival. The Rasa'il clearly goes far beyond any recognizable Islamic orthodoxy.
- **Ismaili influence.** The Druze system builds on Ismaili philosophy (especially the Neoplatonic emanation theology of figures like al-Sijistani and al-Kirmani); the degree to which Druze theology represents an independent innovation vs. a radicalization of Ismaili philosophy is a matter of ongoing scholarly discussion.
