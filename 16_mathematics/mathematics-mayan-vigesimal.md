# Mayan vigesimal mathematics

The Classical Maya developed a fully positional base-20 numeral system with an explicit zero — one of only three independent inventions of positional zero in human history (the other two: the Babylonian use of a placeholder, which was not a true zero in our sense; and the Indian *śūnya*, which became the modern decimal zero — see [[mathematics-zero-india]]).

## The numerals and the zero

The basic glyphs are radically compressed: a dot = 1, a bar = 5. Numbers 1-19 are written as combinations: ●●● = 3, ▬●● = 7, ▬▬▬▬ = 20-(would be) but is written instead as 1 in the next-higher place. The zero is a separate glyph — most commonly drawn as a stylized shell or a four-petaled flower — and functions as a true place-value zero, allowing distinct expression of 20 (1 in the 20s place + 0 in the 1s place) vs. simply 1 in the 1s place.

The system is principally calendrical-astronomical. Classic-period inscriptions perform exact astronomical computations across vast scales: the Dresden Codex Venus tables (see [[astronomy-venus-tables-dresden]]) track Venus synodic cycles for hundreds of years; date-arithmetic spans intervals of millions of days; the Long Count calendar runs in baktuns (144,000 days each) for cosmological epochs.

## The calendar-mathematics integration

The Long Count uses an *almost* pure vigesimal place-value system, with one explicit exception: the third place is 18 (not 20), so that 1 *tun* = 18 × 20 days = 360 days — approximating the solar year. The vigesimal-with-tun-modification structure is internally consistent and produces a notation suited to calendrical computation. The [[mayan-calendrical-system|interlocking calendars]] (Tzolk'in 260-day, Haab' 365-day, Long Count) all depend on this arithmetic.

## Independent invention

Mayan zero is one of the strongest cases of independent mathematical invention in human history. The Long Count and the place-value zero are both attested by the late-Preclassic / early-Classic; transmission from any Old-World source is chronologically and geographically implausible. The achievement is the principal counter-example to any unilineal account of mathematical development.
