# Magic squares

An *n* × *n* grid of distinct integers in which every row, every column, and both main diagonals sum to the same constant — the *magic constant* (= *n*(*n*²+1)/2 for the standard 1-to-*n*² fill). The simplest non-trivial case is the 3×3, which has essentially one solution up to symmetry: the Chinese *Lo Shu* grid, attested in Han-dynasty references to a much older Yu-the-Great myth.

## The Lo Shu

The 3×3 grid:
```
4 9 2
3 5 7
8 1 6
```
— rows, columns, and diagonals each sum to 15 — is associated with the legendary appearance of a divine turtle from the river Luo, whose shell-pattern the sage-king Yu interpreted as the cosmic ordering principle. The figure is the foundational text of the [[yi-jing-divination|I Ching trigram-system]] divinatory mathematics: the 8 trigrams of the *I Ching* arrange around the central 5 (the earth-axis) in the same pattern. The figure is also the prototype of *feng shui* spatial-ordering and Chinese imperial five-phases cosmology (*wuxing*).

## Islamic mathematics

Independent of (and substantially more mathematically developed than) the Chinese tradition, Arabic and Persian mathematicians from the 9th-10th century onward built a deep theory of magic squares of arbitrary order. The 4×4 *jinn squares* and 6×6, 7×7, ... constructions appear in the works of Abū al-Wafāʾ Būzjānī, ʿAlī ibn Aḥmad al-Anṭākī, and later in al-Būnī's 13th-c. *Shams al-Maʿārif*. Islamic magic-square practice is more strongly amuletic than the Chinese tradition: specific squares are inscribed on objects for protection, fertility, healing, or as theosophical contemplation.

## Kabbalah and Hermetism

Magic squares enter Western esoteric tradition through Islamic-Spanish transmission (12th-13th c.) and become associated, in [[tradition-hermeticism|Renaissance Hermetism]], with the seven classical planets — Saturn (3×3), Jupiter (4×4), Mars (5×5), Sun (6×6), Venus (7×7), Mercury (8×8), Moon (9×9). The system is consolidated in Heinrich Cornelius Agrippa's *De Occulta Philosophia* (1531), Book II, where each square is given a planetary name, sigil, and ritual use. Albrecht Dürer's engraving *Melencolia I* (1514) embeds the Jupiter (4×4) square in the upper right of the image — the earliest Western artistic citation of the Islamic-Hermetic magic-square corpus.

## Cross-tradition connections

- **[[mathematics-tetractys]]**: parallel as cosmologically-loaded numerical-geometric figure.
- **[[mathematics-gematria]]**: shares the principle that specific numerical relationships are loci of theological meaning.
- **[[mathematics-sacred-geometry]]**: 2D arrangements of number as cosmological diagrams.
