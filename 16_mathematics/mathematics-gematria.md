# Gematria

The Hebrew system in which each letter of the alphabet bears a fixed numerical value (aleph=1, bet=2, ... yod=10, kaf=20, ... qof=100, resh=200, shin=300, tav=400). Words and phrases acquire numerical totals; equivalence of totals is read as a clue to hidden meaning. The system is one of three classical [[alphabet-gematria-hebrew|Hebrew sacred-arithmetic methods]] (the other two are *notarikon*, acronymic exposition, and *temurah*, letter-substitution).

## The basic mechanism

The Hebrew alphabet has 22 consonants. The decimal expansion of letter-values runs:
- 1-10: aleph through yod
- 20-90: kaf through tsade (the ones place exhausted, the tens begin)
- 100-400: qof, resh, shin, tav

The standard form (*mispar hechrachi*) gives a clean total per word; variant systems (small gematria *katan*, ordinal *seder*, square *miluy*) generate alternative readings. Of the hundreds of Talmudic and midrashic gematria-readings recorded, the most famous: *Elohim* and *ha-teva* (the natural world) both total 86 — read as identifying God with nature; *ahavah* (love) and *echad* (one) both total 13 — read as identifying love with divine unity.

## Antiquity

The Sefer Yetzirah (c. 200 BCE - 200 CE) is the first extant Hebrew text systematically treating letters as cosmologically operative — though it does not yet use the term *gematria*. The term itself is Greek-derived (*geōmetria*, geometry), reflecting the Hellenistic milieu in which letter-arithmetic crystallized in both Greek (*isopsephia*) and Hebrew traditions. The parallel Greek *isopsephy* is securely Hellenistic (5th-3rd c. BCE Greek inscriptions); the systematization in both traditions is roughly contemporary.

## Apocalyptic-prophetic use

The most famous single instance is Revelation 13:18 — "let him that hath understanding count the number of the beast: for it is the number of a man; and his number is six hundred threescore and six" — almost certainly a gematria-coded reference to *Neron Caesar* (נרון קסר in Hebrew letters = 666). The same passage demonstrates the technique was understood by John of Patmos as a transparent cryptographic technique whose key was the standard Hebrew-letter-value table.
