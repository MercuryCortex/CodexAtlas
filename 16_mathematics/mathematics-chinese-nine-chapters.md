# Jiuzhang Suanshu — Nine Chapters on the Mathematical Art

The *Jiuzhang Suanshu* (九章算術, c. 200 BCE – 100 CE, with Liu Hui's commentary 263 CE) is the most important mathematical text in Chinese history — the Chinese *Elements*, establishing the methods and problems that Chinese mathematics would develop for 1,500 years. Unlike Euclid's deductive axiomatic structure, the Jiuzhang presents **practical problem types and solution procedures** — 246 problems organized into nine chapters by problem type.

## The Nine Chapters

1. **Fangtian** (Surveying of Land) — area computation for various land shapes; fractions
2. **Su Mi** (Millet and Rice) — proportional exchange; rule of three
3. **Cuifen** (Distribution by Proportion) — taxation by rank; weighted proportions
4. **Shao Guang** (Short Width) — square and cube roots; finding dimensions from areas
5. **Shang Gong** (Consultations on Works) — volumes of three-dimensional shapes
6. **Jun Shu** (Equitable Taxation) — labor allocation; transport problems
7. **Ying Bu Zu** (Excess and Deficit) — false position method; linear problems
8. **Fang Cheng** (Rectangular Arrays) — **systems of simultaneous linear equations; negative numbers**
9. **Gou Gu** (Base and Altitude) — **the right-triangle theorem; its applications**

## MASSIVE WIN — Three Independent Right-Triangle Discoveries

Chapter 9 (*Gou Gu*) applies the right-triangle theorem (*gou² + gu² = xian²*, or base² + altitude² = hypotenuse²) to practical problems — measuring heights of buildings, depths of pools, distances of objects. The theorem is stated as a known result, not derived; it has been part of Chinese mathematical knowledge since at least the Zhou Bi Suanjing (c. 100 BCE, possibly earlier).

The three independent discoveries of the same theorem:

| Civilization | Date | Context | Term |
|---|---|---|---|
| Babylonian | c. 1800 BCE | Temple accounting, land surveying | (no term — used without naming) |
| Vedic Indian | c. 800 BCE | Sacred fire-altar construction | Baudhayana's theorem |
| Chinese | c. 100 BCE | Surveying, construction | Gou-gu theorem |
| Greek | c. 530 BCE | Pythagorean school / Euclid 300 BCE | Pythagorean theorem |

The "Pythagorean theorem" naming is a historical convention based on Greek philosophical celebrity, not discovery priority. The Babylonians had it first by over a millennium. The Vedic and Chinese traditions developed it independently for practical purposes. The Greeks embedded it in a sacred-philosophical framework and gave it a proof. Four civilizations, one mathematical fact.

## MASSIVE WIN — Gaussian Elimination 1,800 Years Early

Chapter 8 (*Fang Cheng*, Rectangular Arrays) solves systems of simultaneous linear equations using a procedure functionally equivalent to what Carl Friedrich Gauss systematized in 1809 CE. The Chinese procedure:

1. Write the coefficients of the equations as columns in a rectangular array
2. Use column operations (multiply one column by a constant, subtract from another) to eliminate variables
3. Solve by back-substitution

This is Gaussian elimination. It appears in the *Jiuzhang* c. 100 BCE or earlier — 1,900 years before Gauss. The discovery is complete and general; Chapter 8 includes negative numbers (debts vs. assets) because the procedure requires subtracting larger from smaller quantities.

This is the clearest case in the history of mathematics of a major procedure being discovered, used for over a millennium, then independently re-discovered in a different tradition.

## MASSIVE WIN — Negative Numbers Before India

Chapter 8's treatment of *fu* (negative, "debt") numbers alongside *zheng* (positive, "asset") numbers is the **first documented use of negative numbers in any mathematical tradition**. Brahmagupta's treatment of negative numbers (India, 628 CE) is 700+ years later. The Chinese practical context — accounting — is precisely the context that makes negative numbers naturally necessary: a debt is real, it has quantitative effect, it must be tracked.

The cognitive step from "no number corresponds to -3 in nature" to "we can nonetheless compute with -3 and get valid results" is one of the deepest abstractions in the history of number. China made it first, for the most practical of reasons.
