# The Tetractys

The Pythagorean triangular figure of 10 points arranged in 4 rows (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10) — at once a numerical, geometric, musical, and theological diagram. The Pythagorean oath, as preserved by Sextus Empiricus and reproduced through late antiquity, swears by "the one who handed down to our generation the tetraktys, which contains the fount and root of ever-flowing nature."

## The four rows

- **Row 1** (a single point): the *monad* — undivided unity, source of all number.
- **Row 2** (two points): the *dyad* — first division, principle of opposition, the line.
- **Row 3** (three points): the *triad* — surface, harmony emerging from the opposed pair, the plane.
- **Row 4** (four points): the *tetrad* — solid, the material world, the four elements.

Summing the rows yields 10 — the *decad*, which the Pythagoreans treated as the perfect number containing all other numbers within it. The figure simultaneously encodes the geometric progression of dimension (point → line → plane → solid), the four foundational musical ratios (1:2 octave, 2:3 fifth, 3:4 fourth, both visible as differences among consecutive numbers in the diagram), and the cosmological progression from undifferentiated unity to differentiated matter.

## Transmission

The tetractys passed from the Pythagorean school to Plato (the soul-stuff division in *Timaeus* 35-36 uses tetractys ratios), to the Neoplatonists (Iamblichus's *On the Pythagorean Way of Life* devotes a chapter to it), to the medieval Latin West via Boethius's *De Institutione Arithmetica*, and into Renaissance Christian Kabbalah where the Pythagorean decad was systematically aligned with the [[sefirot|ten Kabbalistic sefirot]] (Pico della Mirandola, Reuchlin) — a syncretism that the late Renaissance treated as evidence of a *prisca theologia* uniting Greek and Hebrew wisdom.
