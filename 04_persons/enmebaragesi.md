# Enmebaragesi

## Identity
Enmebaragesi was a king of the late First Dynasty of Kish in the Early Dynastic II period, conventionally dated ~-2625 to -2575 BCE. He is the **earliest Mesopotamian ruler with surviving contemporary inscriptional evidence** — and one of the earliest historical figures of any kind attested by primary documentation in human history. Two inscribed alabaster vases (Edzard 1959), excavated near Khafajah (ancient Tutub in the Diyala region), bear his name and the title "King of Kish," providing direct contemporary evidence independent of the King List. He is also named in the Sumerian King List ([[phase-1-015-sumerian-king-list]]) as the penultimate king of the First Dynasty of Kish — attributed a 900-year reign, mythologically inflated, but with a real historical anchor — and is named in the Tummal Chronicle as the king who built the Tummal at Nippur. His son Aga (Akka), also named in the King List as the last king of the First Dynasty of Kish, appears as the antagonist of [[gilgamesh-king]] in the Sumerian composition *Gilgamesh and Aga*.

## Historical evidence
The evidence is converging and unusually strong for the period:
1. **The two inscribed vases** (Edzard 1959), now in the Iraq Museum and at Yale, bearing "Mebaragesi, King of Kish" in archaic Sumerian script. These are *contemporary primary inscriptions* — the earliest known historical-royal inscriptions.
2. **The Sumerian King List** entry, despite the inflated 900-year reign.
3. **The Tummal Chronicle**, which embeds him in a sequence of construction-attributions at Nippur (Enmebaragesi → Aga → Mes-anepada → Gilgamesh → Ur-Nammu).
4. **The *Gilgamesh and Aga* composition**, naming Aga son of Enmebaragesi as the antagonist.

The vase inscriptions are the decisive evidence. Combined with the literary anchors, Enmebaragesi is reliably **documented** — the King List's 900-year reign is mythological accretion on a real historical kernel.

## Works
None preserved beyond the votive vases.

## Role in the tradition
In the Sumerian historical-literary memory, Enmebaragesi belongs to a small cluster of "founder-figure" pre-Akkadian rulers who anchor the historical tradition: Enmebaragesi, [[mes-anepada]] of Ur, [[gilgamesh-king]] of Uruk. The Tummal Chronicle and the Sumerian King List together coordinate these figures into a continuous historical sequence. In modern Assyriology Enmebaragesi has special importance as the **earliest historical anchor** — the figure whose contemporary inscription provides the floor on which Sumerian historical chronology rests.

## Disputes
- **Date**. Conventional range ~-2625 to -2575 BCE; the Middle Chronology dates the ED II period within these bounds. Lower-chronology proposals shift dates somewhat later.
- **Identity of inscriptions**. The two inscribed vases are universally accepted as referring to the King List's Enmebaragesi; no serious challenge in current scholarship.
