# Naropa and Tilopa (Indian Mahāsiddha Kagyu Founders)

## Identity
Combined entry for the Indian *mahāsiddhas* Tilopa (~988–1069) and his disciple Nāropā (~1016–1100), foundational figures of the Kagyu transmission of Vajrayāna Buddhism. **Tilopa**: a Bengali Tantric master associated with sesame-pressing as occupation (*Tila*-+ profession suffix); systematized the "Six Yogas" (inner heat, illusory body, dream, clear light, transference, intermediate state) that became the operative meditation curriculum of Kagyu Vajrayāna. **Nāropā**: a high-born Brahmin Buddhist scholar at Nālandā, abbot of the monastery at one point, who left the academic life in search of direct experiential realization, undertook the famous "Twelve Trials of Naropa" under Tilopa (humiliations and seemingly absurd commands that broke down conceptual fixation), and received the full transmission. Nāropā taught **Marpa** the Translator (~1012–1097), who carried the lineage to Tibet, where it was transmitted to **Milarepa** and then through Gampopa into the formal Kagyu institutional school.

## Historical evidence
The historical Tilopa and Nāropā are widely accepted; the elaborate Twelve Trials narrative and many specific anecdotes are substantially hagiographic. The transmission lineage Tilopa → Nāropā → Marpa → Milarepa is the foundational Kagyu *paramparā*.

## Works
Tilopa: short instruction-songs (the *Gangā Mahāmudrā Upadeśa* is famous); the *Mahāmudropadeśa*. Naropa: the *Sekkodesh-Tika* (a Kālacakra commentary); various instruction texts; the *Six Yogas of Nāropā* corpus is associated with him though substantially developed and transmitted by his disciples.

## Role in the tradition
Foundational for Kagyu Vajrayāna; the Indian mahāsiddha lineage from which Tibetan Kagyu derives. The Six Yogas of Nāropā are central to Kagyu and (in transmuted form) Gelug Tantric practice.
