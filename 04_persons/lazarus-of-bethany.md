# Lazarus of Bethany

## Identity
Brother of [[mary-of-bethany]] and [[martha-of-bethany]]; the disciple whom Jesus raises from a four-day tomb in John 11. The Synoptic Gospels do not mention him; the *only* canonical attestation is the Johannine Lazarus-narrative (Jn 11) and the banquet that follows (Jn 12). Distinct from the **parable** of Lazarus the beggar at Lk 16:19–31 (likely the *literary origin* of the Johannine name).

## Historical evidence
Single-source (Johannine). The Synoptic silence about so dramatic an event is the principal historical-critical problem with the narrative. Multiple readings: (a) the Johannine story is a literary creation building on the Lukan parable; (b) the event is historical but did not survive into Markan-Q tradition; (c) the underlying event is a healing or other miracle that the Fourth Evangelist has reframed.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
The Lazarus-raising is the climactic miracle of the Fourth Gospel, the seventh and greatest of the "signs," and the trigger that motivates the priestly decision to kill Jesus (Jn 11:53). Patristic tradition makes Lazarus a bishop (Cyprus or Marseille per competing legends). Bauckham (2017) revives the patristic-medieval proposal that Lazarus is the **Beloved Disciple** of the Fourth Gospel — minority but defensible reading.

## Disputes
- **Historicity.** Single-source canonical attestation; Synoptic silence; possible literary derivation from the Lukan parable.
- **Beloved Disciple identification.** Bauckham's case is internally coherent but does not command consensus.
