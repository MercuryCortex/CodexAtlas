# Julian the Apostate

Roman emperor 361–363; nephew of Constantine. Raised Christian but converted to philosophical paganism, studying with Iamblichean Neoplatonists (Maximus of Ephesus). His brief reign attempted a pagan restoration — restoring the temples, removing Christian privileges from imperial service, beginning to rebuild the Jerusalem Temple — cut short by his death in a Persian campaign at age 31. His theological writings (especially *Hymn to King Helios*) are major sources for late-antique pagan philosophical theology in the Iamblichean register. The "Apostate" label is Christian; pagans called him *Philosophos* (philosopher).
