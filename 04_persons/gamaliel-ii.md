# Gamaliel II

## Identity
Grandson of [[gamaliel-the-elder|Gamaliel I]], **president of the academy of Yavneh** (~+90 onward, succeeding [[yohanan-ben-zakkai]] in academic leadership). Under his presidency the Tannaitic curriculum, liturgy, and halakhic precedence were substantially reorganized. Rabbinic tradition (Bavli Berakhot 28b-29a) credits him with authorizing **Shemuel ha-Qatan** to formulate the *Birkat ha-Minim* — the synagogue benediction against *minim* (heretics) — traditionally read as a key moment in the formal separation of Christians (or Jewish-Christians) from the synagogue community.

## Historical evidence
Rabbinic. Multiply attested in Mishnah, Tosefta, and Talmuds. The Birkat ha-Minim authorization narrative is contested by modern scholarship: Lieu 1996, Boyarin 2004, Becker-Reed 2003 have argued the traditional "Jamnia council expelled the Christians" reading is anachronistic; the Birkat ha-Minim's earliest text targets *minim* (heretics generally) and *notzrim* (Christians) is a probably-later addition.

## Works
None. Halakhic rulings and synagogue-liturgy reorganization preserved across the rabbinic corpus.

## Role in the tradition
- **Consolidator of Yavnean Judaism**: institutional patriarchate-formation under Gamaliel II.
- **Birkat ha-Minim**: traditionally a key text in the Jewish-Christian separation; modern reading: the separation was more gradual, geographically uneven, and not formally completed at Yavneh.
- **Conflict with Joshua ben Hananiah**: rabbinic tradition records moments of overreach by Gamaliel II and rebuke from the academy — important data about the limits of patriarchal authority.

## Disputes
- **Birkat ha-Minim and Christian-Jewish separation**: long held to be a "council of Jamnia" moment; modern scholarship (Boyarin, Lieu, Becker-Reed) has substantially complicated this picture.
- **Date of the benediction's *notzrim* clause**: probably later addition, not Gamaliel-II-period.
- **Reconstruction of his patriarchate**: rabbinic sources are theologically shaped.
