# Seth

## Identity
Seth — Hebrew *Shēt* (שֵׁת, "appointed / granted") — is the third son of Adam and Eve in the biblical narrative (Gen 4:25–26; 5:3–8), born after Cain killed Abel, and the ancestor of Noah, and so of all post-flood humanity (the genealogy of Gen 5 runs Adam → Seth → Enosh → … → Noah). In the canonical Hebrew Bible Seth is a minor genealogical figure; his theological importance is constructed retroactively. In *Sethian Gnostic Christianity* (~2nd–4th c. CE) Seth becomes a cosmic-redemptive figure of enormous importance: he is the "third begetting" — Adam → Cain (rejected line) → Seth (the saved seed) — and the heavenly Seth (or *Allogenes* / *the Stranger*) is the cosmic-mythic origin of the saved-spiritual race ("the seed of Seth") into which the Sethian Gnostic is incorporated by gnosis. The Mandaean tradition makes Seth (as Šitil / Shīth) one of the great Light-prophets. Islamic tradition makes Shīth a prophet given fifty scrolls of revelation by Allah, intermediate between Adam and Idrīs/Enoch.

## Historical evidence
None — Seth is a *biblical-narrative* and *post-biblical-religious* figure. The historical-critical question is about *the development of Seth-traditions*, not about a historical individual. The Sethian elevation of the biblical Seth into a cosmic-redeemer is a striking case of post-biblical reception remaking a minor biblical figure into a theological centerpiece.

## Role in the Sethian tradition
The Sethian Gnostic mythology (in [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]], [[phase-4-006-hypostasis-of-the-archons]], [[phase-4-007-on-the-origin-of-the-world]], [[phase-4-008-trimorphic-protennoia]], the *Gospel of the Egyptians*, the *Three Steles of Seth*, *Allogenes*, *Marsanes*, *Zostrianos*) makes Seth the divine son of the heavenly Adam-as-Image, and the source of the "seed of Seth" — the Gnostic spiritual elect. The "seed of Seth" is a cosmological race-concept that organizes Sethian soteriology: the Gnostic recognizes themselves as belonging to the seed of Seth, not to the line of Cain or the merely-psychic line of Abel. Seth is thus a *cosmic-redeemer-figure*, in some texts identified with the heavenly Christ and in some texts as Christ's prefigurement. Turner 2001 is the definitive scholarly reconstruction of the Sethian system; Pearson 1990 documents the Jewish-heterodox roots of the seed-of-Seth concept.
