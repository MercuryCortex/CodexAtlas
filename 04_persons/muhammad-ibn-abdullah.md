# Muhammad ibn Abdullah

Founder of Islam in the historical-religious sense; in Islamic theology, the final Prophet to whom God revealed the Quran via the angel Jibrīl (Gabriel) over twenty-two years (610–632). Born in Mecca (~570) to the Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh tribe; orphaned young; worked as a trader; married the older businesswoman [[khadija-bint-khuwaylid|Khadīja]] (~595); received his first revelation (Sura 96:1–5) ~610 at age forty in a cave on Mount Hira (per the Sira-tradition, Khadīja's older Christian-Arab cousin Waraqa ibn Nawfal — one of the named [[hanif-monotheism|*ḥunafāʾ*]] — confirmed the revelation as authentic prophecy). After persecution in Mecca (and the disputed [[satanic-verses-incident|*gharānīq* incident]]), migrated with his community to Yathrib (Medina) in 622 — the Hegira ([[event-hegira-622]]), the canonical Year 1 of the Islamic calendar. United the Arabian peninsula under Islamic authority in the decade before his death in 632 — including the [[event-conquest-of-mecca-630|conquest of Mecca in 630]] with destruction of the Kaaba idols ([[hubal]] + the [[tradition-pre-islamic-arabian|pre-Islamic Hijazi pantheon]]), and his [[event-death-of-muhammad-632|death and the Saqīfa succession in 632]].

The vault catalogs Muhammad with the same care it applies to other founders of religions — the historical record from outside Islamic tradition is sparse for the early years and richer for the Medinan period; biographies (*sīra*) by Ibn Ishaq (~767, surviving in Ibn Hisham's recension) and Tabari (early 10th c.) are the principal Islamic sources; revisionist scholarship has questioned details, but the early-7th-century Hijazi historical core is now broadly accepted across critical Western scholarship (Donner, Watt, Hoyland).
