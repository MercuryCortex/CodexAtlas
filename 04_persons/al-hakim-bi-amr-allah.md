# al-Ḥākim bi-Amr Allāh

**al-Ḥākim bi-Amr Allāh** (985–1021 CE) — sixth Fatimid Ismaili caliph of Egypt (r. 996–1021) — is venerated by the Druze as the most recent theophanic manifestation of the divine principle in human history. His reign was marked by erratic policies (alternating severity and indulgence toward non-Muslims, destruction of the Church of the Holy Sepulchre in 1009, banning of chess and women from appearing in public). He disappeared in February 1021 while on a night walk in the Muqattam Hills and was never found; the Druze interpret his disappearance as occultation (*ghayba*) rather than death, with his return expected eschatologically.

The Druze founder-theologians Hamza ibn ʿAlī and Bahāʾ al-Dīn al-Muqtanā composed the [[phase-5-059-rasail-al-hikma|Rasāʾil al-Ḥikma]] around al-Ḥākim's theophany during 1017–1043. See [[tradition-druze]].
