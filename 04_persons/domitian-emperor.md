# Domitian

## Identity
Third Flavian emperor (81–96), youngest son of Vespasian, brother and successor of Titus. Reigned for 15 years before being assassinated in a palace conspiracy. His insistence on the honorific *dominus et deus noster* ("our lord and god") and his pious devotion to traditional Roman religion are well-attested. Christian tradition (from Hegesippus via Eusebius onward) treats him as the **second great persecutor** after Nero; modern scholarship is much more cautious.

## Historical evidence
Pagan sources (Tacitus, Suetonius, Dio, Pliny) attest his autocratic style but say little about anti-Christian persecution specifically. The principal datum for *targeted* persecution is the **execution of Flavius Clemens (his cousin, consul in 95) and exile of Clemens's wife Flavia Domitilla** in 95 on charges including *atheotēs* (atheism) and "Jewish customs" — possibly Christianity. Hegesippus (~+170 via Eusebius HE 3.20) reports that Domitian had grandsons of Jude (the brother of Jesus) brought before him as Davidic claimants; Domitian, unimpressed by their farming-calloused hands, released them.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
- **Second persecutor in Christian historiography**: Eusebius's narrative establishes him as such, but the *actual* scope of his anti-Christian policy is much disputed by modern scholarship.
- **Apocalyptic context of Revelation**: traditional dating of [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john|the Book of Revelation]] is ~+95, Domitian's reign — Revelation's vision of empire-as-beast is read against Domitian's autocratic style. The dating is increasingly contested (some prefer ~+68 Neronian, or even later Trajanic) but the Domitianic dating remains mainstream.
- **Atheism charge against Christians**: the *atheotēs* charge against Flavius Clemens is the earliest clear instance of the term being applied to monotheists; it becomes a stock charge through the 2nd–3rd c.

## Disputes
- **Reality of an empire-wide Domitianic persecution**: Moss (2013) and others argue that Christian tradition retrofitted a coherent Domitianic persecution onto isolated events. Mainstream view: limited persecution, real but exaggerated by later tradition.
- **Revelation dating**: ~+95 (mainstream) vs. ~+68 (a substantial minority).
- **Flavius Clemens's religion**: Christian or Jewish — sources do not clearly distinguish, but both were treated by the Romans as forms of *atheotēs*.
