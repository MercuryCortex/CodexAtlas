# Alcuin of York

## Identity
Northumbrian Anglo-Saxon cleric and scholar. Educated at York Minster under Archbishop Egbert (himself a student of Bede); became master of the York cathedral school. Met Charlemagne at Parma in 781 and was recruited to head the palace school at Aachen — the educational centerpiece of the Carolingian Renaissance. Lay abbot of St Martin's at Tours from 796 until his death in 804.

## Historical evidence
Approximately 310 of his letters survive, addressed to Charlemagne, members of the royal family, bishops, abbots, and former students — a major primary archive of the Carolingian court. His didactic works, biblical work, and theological treatises are extensively transmitted.

## Works
- **Didactic dialogues** — *De grammatica*, *De rhetorica et virtutibus*, *De dialectica*, *De orthographia*. Question-and-answer pedagogical texts for the palace school and other Carolingian foundations.
- ***Vita Willibrordi*** (prose and verse) — life of the Northumbrian missionary to Frisia (his kinsman); ***Vita Vedasti*** — life of an earlier Frankish bishop.
- **Anti-Adoptionist** — *Adversus Felicem*, *Adversus Elipandum* (against the Spanish Adoptionists Felix of Urgell and Elipandus of Toledo, who taught that Christ was the Son of God only by adoption in his human nature).
- ***De fide sanctae Trinitatis*** — a Trinitarian textbook.
- **Liturgical work** — the *Supplementum* to the Gregorian Sacramentary; widely used votive masses.
- **Biblical revision** — Alcuin oversaw the production at Tours of a corrected (and standardized) Vulgate text that became influential throughout the Carolingian world.

## Role in the tradition
The intellectual architect of the Carolingian Renaissance. The standardization of script (Carolingian minuscule), of biblical text, of liturgical books, and of educational curriculum that issues from the Aachen-Tours axis owes more to Alcuin than to any other single figure. His pupils (Rabanus Maurus, Hatto of Fulda, Hildebald of Cologne) staffed the next generation of Frankish bishoprics and abbeys.

## Connections — substrate and influence
- Alcuin transmits the **Anglo-Saxon learning of York** — which was itself an inheritance from [[bede]] and ultimately from the Wearmouth-Jarrow library — to the Frankish empire. He represents a clean documentable transmission line: Roman / patristic library → Benedict Biscop and Ceolfrith's book purchases → Wearmouth-Jarrow → [[bede]] → Egbert → Alcuin → Aachen and Tours.
- His curriculum codifies the late-antique liberal arts (*trivium* + *quadrivium*) inherited from [[cassiodorus]], [[isidore-of-seville]], and ultimately Martianus Capella and [[boethius]]. The Carolingian school is the late-antique school, restored — a direct connection between [[cassiodorus]]'s Vivarium curriculum and Alcuin's palace school at Aachen under [[charlemagne]].
- Alcuin reads Augustine extensively; the Trinitarian theology of his *De fide* is essentially Augustinian.
- The Carolingian-era promotion of *filioque* (the addition to the Nicene Creed of "and from the Son" in the procession of the Spirit) in which Alcuin was active foreshadows the great East-West dispute culminating in 1054.

## Disputes
- **Alcuin's authorship of specific Carolingian texts.** A number of works are confidently or doubtfully attributed; Bullough 2004 reassesses the corpus.
- **Alcuin and the Saxon wars.** A famous letter (#111 of his corpus) urges Charlemagne to convert pagan Saxons by teaching and persuasion rather than by force — implicit criticism of Charlemagne's violent campaigns. Whether this constitutes serious dissent or a pious literary topos is debated.
