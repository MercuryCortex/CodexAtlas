# Basil of Caesarea

Bishop of Caesarea in Cappadocia from 370 to his death in 379. Elder of the three Cappadocian Fathers; brother of [[gregory-of-nyssa]] and [[macrina-the-younger]]; close friend of [[gregory-of-nazianzus]]. Articulated the orthodox Trinitarian formula of "one *ousia* in three *hypostases*" that resolved decades of fourth-century Trinitarian controversy. His monastic Rules became foundational for Eastern Christian monasticism. His *On the Holy Spirit* and the *Hexaemeron* (a series of homilies on Genesis 1) are among the most-read patristic Greek texts.

## Substrate connections
- His monastic *Asketikon* was composed after a visit to the Egyptian and Syrian desert communities (~357) and is methodologically Egyptian-Coptic monasticism (especially Pachomian cenobitism — see [[pachomius]]) Hellenized for Greek-speaking Anatolian communities. The desert tradition substrate is direct.
- His Trinitarian vocabulary (*ousia* / *hypostasis*) deploys Aristotelian and Stoic substance-metaphysics in Christian service.
- His *Address to Young Men on the Right Use of Greek Literature* gives the classical defense of Christian engagement with pagan classical literature — a methodological warrant for the late-antique Christian appropriation of the Greek-philosophical inheritance.
