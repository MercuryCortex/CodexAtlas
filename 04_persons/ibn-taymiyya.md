# Ibn Taymiyya (Taqī al-Dīn Aḥmad ibn ʿAbd al-Ḥalīm ibn Taymiyya)

## Identity
**Taqī al-Dīn Aḥmad ibn ʿAbd al-Ḥalīm ibn Taymiyya al-Ḥarrānī al-Dimashqī (1263–1328 CE)** — universally honored by his Ḥanbalī and modern-Salafi devotees as ***Shaykh al-Islām*** (Master of Islam) — is the **single most consequential medieval Sunni intellectual root of modern Salafism, Wahhābism, and Sunni-revivalist political Islam**. A Mamlūk-era Ḥanbalī jurist-theologian born in Ḥarrān (modern Şanlıurfa, Turkey) and forced westward to Damascus by the Mongol invasions when he was 5, Ibn Taymiyya spent his career in **systematic polemical-juristic-theological combat** on multiple fronts simultaneously: against the **Ashʿarī kalām** establishment for figurative-interpretation of the divine-attribute Quran verses; against the **Twelver Shīʿī** tradition (especially Ibn al-Muṭahhar al-Ḥillī's apologetic); against the **Sufi shrine-veneration** practices (especially the tomb-veneration practices of his contemporary Damascene Sufi *ṭuruq*); against the **falsafa** tradition (especially the Avicennian intellect-theory and emanational cosmology, and the Sufi-philosophical synthesis of Ibn ʿArabī); against **Christianity** (in his *al-Jawāb al-Ṣaḥīḥ* refutation of a Cypriot Christian apologetic letter); against **mainstream contemporary Sunni political theology** (especially the *kufr dūna kufr* / "taghut" political-theological framework allowing jihad against insufficiently-sharīʿa-implementing Muslim rulers — including the **Mongol Ilkhanate** despite their nominal conversion to Islam, in the 1299-1303 Mongol invasion fatwas). His polemical aggressiveness and his theological-political program **earned him repeated imprisonment** by Mamluk authorities (most consequentially the 1306 Cairo imprisonment after the *al-ʿAqīda al-Ḥamawiyya* controversy; a series of subsequent Damascus and Cairo imprisonments; he died in the Damascus citadel prison in 1328 after being denied access to writing materials).

The **modern reception-history of Ibn Taymiyya is the principal reason for his vault-status as a load-bearing wedge node**: his juristic-theological positions on (1) **strict divine-attribute realism against Ashʿarī figurative interpretation**, (2) **rejection of the kalām-falsafa rationalist program**, (3) **rejection of Sufi shrine-veneration and intercession-practices**, (4) **strict commitment to the *aslāf* (the early Salaf) as exclusive doctrinal-juristic authority**, and especially (5) **the *Mardin fatwa* political-theological framework** authorizing jihad against insufficiently-sharīʿa-implementing Muslim rulers, are the **direct intellectual foundation of the Wahhābī revival of the 18th c.** ([[event-rise-of-political-islamism-1928-present|Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Wahhāb (1703-1792)]] is explicitly Ibn Taymiyya-formed), the **modern Salafi reformist movement of the late 19th-20th c.** (Rashīd Riḍā substantially Ibn Taymiyya-formed), and the **modern jihadi-Salafi political-Islamist movement** (Sayyid Qutb's [[phase-7-028-milestones|*Milestones*]] uses Ibn Taymiyya's political-theological framework; al-Qāʿida and ISIS rhetoric centrally cites Ibn Taymiyya's Mardin fatwa). (Hoover 2019; Rapoport & Ahmed 2010.)

## Historical evidence
Documented extensively. Ibn Taymiyya's massive corpus survives (the *Majmūʿ al-Fatāwā* in the modern critical edition runs to ~37 vols.). Independent contemporary biographical evidence in Mamluk-era chronicles (al-Dhahabī's *Tārīkh al-Islām*; al-Bidāya wa-l-Nihāya by Ibn Kathīr, who was Ibn Taymiyya's student; Ibn Ḥajar al-ʿAsqalānī's *al-Durar al-Kāmina*; substantial polemical-counter-literature by contemporary Ashʿarī and Sufi opponents — Taqī al-Dīn al-Subkī, al-Akhnāʾī, etc.).

## Works
- ***Majmūʿ al-Fatāwā*** — the principal corpus of Ibn Taymiyya's juristic, theological, and polemical writings. ~37 vols. in the modern Riyadh critical edition.
- ***Minhāj al-Sunna al-Nabawiyya*** — major refutation of Twelver-Shīʿī theology (~9 vols.). The single most influential medieval Sunni anti-Shīʿī polemic; the foundational text for modern Salafi anti-Shīʿī positions.
- ***Darʾ Taʿāruḍ al-ʿAql wa-l-Naql*** — major theological-epistemological work arguing that the kalām claim of conflict-between-reason-and-revelation is fundamentally mistaken; **reason properly used cannot conflict with revealed religion**, and apparent conflicts arise from kalām misunderstandings of reason or of revelation. The work is a strict-Ḥanbalī alternative to the falsafa harmony-thesis of Averroes's [[phase-5-046-ibn-rushd-tahafut-al-tahafut|*Tahāfut al-Tahāfut*]] / *Faṣl al-Maqāl*.
- ***al-ʿAqīda al-Wāsiṭiyya*** — short systematic creed (~20 pages); the foundational statement of the strict-Ḥanbalī / proto-Salafi theological position. **Read continuously in modern Salafi study-circles**; one of the most-read brief creeds in modern Sunni religious education.
- ***al-ʿAqīda al-Ḥamawiyya*** — defends a strict-Ḥanbalī divine-attribute realism against Ashʿarī figurative interpretation; the work that led to Ibn Taymiyya's 1306 imprisonment after Ashʿarī jurists charged him with anthropomorphism (*tajsīm*).
- ***al-Siyāsa al-Sharʿiyya fī Iṣlāḥ al-Rāʿī wa-l-Raʿiyya*** (Governance according to the Sharīʿa) — Ibn Taymiyya's principal political-theological treatise. The foundational medieval text for **modern Sunni political Islam** — read centrally by Sayyid Qutb, Abū al-Aʿlā Mawdūdī, Ḥasan al-Bannā, and the Saudi-Wahhābī religious establishment.
- ***al-Jawāb al-Ṣaḥīḥ li-man Baddala Dīn al-Masīḥ*** — refutation of Christianity (~5 vols.). Major medieval anti-Christian polemic.

## Role in the tradition
- ***Modern reception-history* is the load-bearing reason for Ibn Taymiyya's vault-importance.** His own medieval reception was mixed (he was repeatedly imprisoned by Mamluk authorities; his positions were rejected by mainstream Ashʿarī Sunni scholars; some of his juristic positions were treated as eccentric even within the Ḥanbalī school). The decisive turn is the **18th-c. Wahhābī revival**: [[event-rise-of-political-islamism-1928-present|Muḥammad ibn ʿAbd al-Wahhāb (1703-1792)]] is **explicitly Ibn Taymiyya-formed** — the Wahhābī rejection of shrine-veneration, the Wahhābī divine-attribute realism, the Wahhābī polemic against Shīʿīsm and Sufism, all come from Ibn Taymiyya. The Saudi-Wahhābī pact c. 1744 brings Ibn Taymiyya's positions into political-state implementation. The **late-19th-20th-c. Salafi reformist movement** (Jamāl al-Dīn al-Afghānī, Muḥammad ʿAbduh, Rashīd Riḍā) is more philosophically eclectic but absorbs Ibn Taymiyya as a foundational authority. **Modern Sunni political Islam (Sayyid Qutb, [[phase-7-028-milestones|*Milestones*]]; Mawdūdī; al-Bannā; the Muslim Brotherhood) reads Ibn Taymiyya's *Siyāsa al-Sharʿiyya* and the *Mardin fatwa* as the foundational political-theological framework**. **Modern jihadi-Salafism (al-Qāʿida, ISIS)** deploys the *Mardin fatwa* — Ibn Taymiyya's ruling on insufficiently-sharīʿa-implementing Muslim rulers — in revisionist form to authorize jihad against secular Muslim governments. The 2010 Mardin Conference (Yahya Michot et al., with Mauritanian scholar Bin Bayyah) was a contemporary Sunni-scholarly response: a re-examination of the original Mardin fatwa demonstrating that the jihadi-Salafi deployment substantially mis-reads Ibn Taymiyya's actual position.
- **Strict divine-attribute realism.** Ibn Taymiyya's theological position: the Quranic divine-attribute verses (God's *yad* "hand," *wajh* "face," *istiwāʾ ʿalā al-ʿarsh* "settling on the throne," etc.) must be affirmed *bi-lā kayf* (without specifying how) but *not figuratively reinterpreted* (against the Ashʿarī *taʾwīl* of these verses as metaphorical for power, presence, etc.). This is the canonical strict-Ḥanbalī position, but Ibn Taymiyya pushed it more aggressively than mainstream Ḥanbalism, leading to the *tajsīm* (anthropomorphism) charges and the 1306 imprisonment.
- **Rejection of falsafa.** Ibn Taymiyya's *Darʾ Taʿāruḍ al-ʿAql wa-l-Naql* and other works are systematically critical of the Avicennian falsafa tradition — the emanational cosmology, the intellect-theory, the Necessary-Being argument as Ibn Taymiyya reads it. The *Naqḍ al-Manṭiq* (Refutation of Logic) attacks Aristotelian logic itself as deployed in kalām and falsafa.
- **Rejection of Sufi shrine-veneration.** Ibn Taymiyya does not reject Sufism wholesale (he had Qādirī affiliations himself in some accounts), but **systematically rejects tomb-visitation as religious practice** (the *ziyārat al-qubūr* polemic), shrine-intercession theology, and the Ibn ʿArabī Sufi-philosophical synthesis (*waḥdat al-wujūd*). His positions on shrine-veneration are directly implemented by the Wahhābī movement (the famous Wahhābī destruction of shrines in the Hijaz at the 1803-1812 first-Saudi-state period).
- **The *Mardin fatwa*.** Issued in response to a question about Muslims living in Mongol-controlled Mardin (then theologically-deficient Mongol rule): Ibn Taymiyya's ruling distinguished *dār al-Islām* (the land of Islam, with full sharīʿa-implementation) from *dār al-ḥarb* (the land of war, openly non-Muslim) and a third category — *dār al-kufr al-marakkab* (compound non-Muslim land, where Muslims may live under non-fully-Islamic rule under various conditions). The fatwa was deployed by Ibn Taymiyya himself for the Mongol-era context; **modern jihadi-Salafism redeployed it as authorizing jihad against insufficiently-sharīʿa-implementing Muslim rulers** (the Saudi state, Egypt under Nasser, etc.). The 2010 Mardin Conference response argues this is a revisionist mis-reading.

## Disputes
- **Anthropomorphism (*tajsīm*) charges.** Mamlūk-era Ashʿarī charges; modern Salafi defense: not anthropomorphism, but strict scriptural-realism. The dispute is alive.
- **Modern jihadi-Salafi deployment of the Mardin fatwa.** Jihadi-Salafi: legitimate extension of Ibn Taymiyya's framework. 2010 Mardin Conference + mainstream Sunni scholarship: revisionist mis-reading.
- **Was Ibn Taymiyya himself a Sufi?** Some accounts attribute Qādirī affiliation; he rejects Sufi shrine-veneration but not Sufism wholesale. Position is contested.
- **Ibn Taymiyya's status in modern Sunni Islam.** Wahhābī / Salafi: Shaykh al-Islām, foundational authority. Mainstream Ashʿarī Sunni: respected jurist with controversial positions; not foundational. Modern progressive Sunni: foundational figure but problematic for modern reception (especially the political-theological positions).
