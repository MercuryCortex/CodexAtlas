# Orpheus

## Identity
Orpheus is the legendary Thracian singer-poet, son of the Muse Calliope (and either Apollo or the Thracian king Oeagrus), whose lyre charmed beasts, trees, and stones; companion of Jason on the Argonautic voyage; husband of Eurydice, whose loss he attempted to reverse by descending to Hades and persuading Persephone with his song to release her — only to lose her by looking back at the threshold. He was later torn apart (*sparagmos*) by the Thracian Maenads; his severed head, still singing, floated to Lesbos where it became an oracular relic. Around this mythological figure crystallized **Orphism** — a diffuse but identifiable strand of Greek mystery-religious literature and practice from at least the 6th century BCE onward, centered on the Zagreus-Dionysus dismemberment myth, the soul's transmigration and divine origin, ritual purification, and salvation through initiation.

## Historical evidence
There is **no historical evidence for an individual person Orpheus**. He is a legendary founder-figure to whom a tradition of religious poetry was ascribed pseudepigraphically over many centuries. The earliest secure reference to Orpheus by name is a fragment of the Hesiodic-school *Catalogue of Women* (~-700 to -600); Orpheus is established in Greek literature by Pindar and the Athenian dramatists. The **Derveni Papyrus** (~-340 papyrus carbonized in a Macedonian funeral pyre, recovered 1962, fully published 2006) preserves an Orphic-attributed theogony with allegorical commentary, our oldest direct manuscript evidence of an Orphic theological text; the theology itself is older, datable to ~-500 or earlier. The **Orphic gold tablets** (40+ inscribed gold lamellae from burials in Magna Graecia, Crete, Thessaly, ~-400 to +200) preserve afterlife-instructions for the initiated soul. Two main scholarly positions on Orphism's historicity: (1) Linforth (1941) skeptical — "Orphism" is largely a modern scholarly construct on a thin and diverse ancient body of pseudepigraphical literature; (2) Burkert (1985), West (1983), Bernabé — there is a recognizable Orphic literary-religious complex with identifiable family resemblances even if no unified institutional "Orphic religion."

## Works
All works ascribed to Orpheus are pseudepigraphical. The major Orphic textual corpora:
- **Orphic theogonies** — at least four distinguishable theogonic poems are reconstructible from quotation: the Derveni theogony, the Eudemian theogony, the Rhapsodic theogony (the most fully developed; perhaps Hellenistic), and the Hieronymian-Hellanican theogony (Damascius's late-antique sources). West (1983) reconstructs the textual history.
- **Orphic Hymns** — a collection of 87 hexameter hymns invoking various deities; in their present form composed by anonymous Orphic communities ~+200/+400 in Asia Minor.
- **Orphic Argonautica** — late-antique pseudo-Orphic retelling of the Argonautic voyage from Orpheus's perspective.
- **Lithica** — a poem on the magical properties of stones, ~+300, pseudo-Orphic.
- **Orphic gold tablets** — ritual-funerary inscriptions, ~-400/+200, often Orphic-themed but not strictly "Orphic-attributed."

## Role in the tradition
Orpheus's tradition has three major load-bearing afterlives:

1. **Influence on Pythagoreanism.** [[pythagoras]] (or the early Pythagorean tradition) and Orphism share key doctrines — transmigration of souls (*metempsychosis*), the soul's divine origin and exile in the body (*sōma sēma*, "the body is the tomb"), purification through ritual and lifestyle, dietary asceticism (vegetarianism). The directional question — did Orphism influence Pythagoreanism or vice versa? — is debated; Burkert (1972) argued for substantial mutual influence with primary Orphic priority on the soul-doctrine; West (1983) treats the two as parallel currents with overlapping personnel. By the Hellenistic period the two are often functionally inseparable in popular religion.

2. **Influence on [[plato]] and Platonism.** Plato repeatedly cites Orphic-and-Pythagorean doctrines, especially in the *Phaedo* (the *sōma sēma* image at 62b, 67b; the *katharsis* doctrine 67c–d; the cosmic-river afterlife at 113a–114b), the *Cratylus* (400c on the body as the soul's prison/tomb), the *Republic* (364e–365a on Orphic books used by itinerant priests), and the *Laws* (782c on the "Orphic life" of vegetarianism). The myth of Er at *Republic* 614b–621d is structurally Orphic. The mature Platonic doctrines of the soul's immortality, pre-existence, and divine origin are deeply Orphic-Pythagorean in genealogy; from Plato these doctrines pass into Middle Platonism, Neoplatonism, and through Plotinus and Augustine into Christian theology.

3. **The Zagreus-Gnostic edge.** The **Zagreus myth** — Dionysus-Zagreus as the divine child of Zeus and Persephone, dismembered and consumed by the Titans, from whose ashes Zeus blasts the Titans and creates humanity (so that humanity carries within it both the divine spark from the consumed Dionysus and the Titanic earth-substance) — is the load-bearing Orphic anthropogony. This dual-substance anthropology — *humanity carries a divine pneumatic spark within a Titanic / hylic shell* — is structurally identical to the Gnostic anthropology of the divine spark trapped in the demiurgic-Titanic body. Whether the Gnostic anthropology is *directly* Orphic-derived is contested (the channels go through Middle Platonism rather than direct dependence), but the deep-structural parallel is real. See [[zagreus]] for the deity-side of this connection.

## Disputes
- **Was there a single "Orphic religion"?** Linforth (1941) and now Edmonds (2013) argue against; Burkert, West, and Bernabé argue for at least a recognizable "Orphic" literary-religious complex even if not institutional.
- **Date of the Zagreus dismemberment myth.** The full Zagreus-dismemberment-anthropogony narrative is securely attested only from late-antique sources (Olympiodorus, Damascius); whether it goes back to the early Orphic stratum (so West, Burkert) or is a late-Hellenistic / late-antique elaboration (so Edmonds 2013) is one of the most active controversies in Orphic scholarship.
- **Orphic priority over Pythagoreanism on soul-doctrine.** Burkert (1972) is the canonical modern statement of Orphic priority; some scholars dissent.
