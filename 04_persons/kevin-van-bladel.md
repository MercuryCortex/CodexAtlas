# Kevin van Bladel

## Identity
American Arabist and historian of late antiquity; Professor of Near Eastern Languages and Civilizations at Yale University (previously USC). Author of *The Arabic Hermes* (OUP 2009), the standard scholarly reconstruction of how Hermetic literature was transmitted from Greek into Arabic — primarily via the **Sabian community of Harran** in the Abbasid period (8th–10th centuries) — and how the figure of Hermes Trismegistus came to be read in the Arabic philosophical-religious tradition as the prophet Idris and as the founder of astrology, alchemy, and natural science.

## Historical evidence
Living scholar; Yale faculty page; OUP and Brill catalogs document his works.

## Works
- *The Arabic Hermes: From Pagan Sage to Prophet of Science* (OUP 2009). The standard study of Hermetic-Arabic transmission.
- *From Sasanian Mandaeans to Ṣābians of the Marshes* (Brill 2017). On the late-antique Iraqi religious-cultural matrix.
- Numerous articles on Arabic philosophy, late-antique transmission, Sabian religion, and the medieval Arabic reception of Greek-Egyptian material.

## Role in the tradition
van Bladel completed the modern scholarly map of Hermetic transmission. Where Fowden 1986 covered the Greco-Egyptian origins, Mahé 1978–82 added the Coptic and Armenian recoveries, and Copenhaver 1992 standardized the English translation, van Bladel 2009 documented the Arabic-Sabian-Harranian channel — the medieval transmission line that carried Hermetic alchemy, astrology, and theurgy into the Latin West via 12th-c. Toledo-school translation, where it intersected with the Renaissance Hermetic recovery centuries later.

His work decisively dismantled older speculative reconstructions (notably Massignon, Plessner) that had projected an essentially modernist syncretic Hermetism backward onto inadequately-documented medieval Arabic sources. The post-van-Bladel picture is more conservative and better documented.
