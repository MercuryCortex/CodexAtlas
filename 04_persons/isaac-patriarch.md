# Isaac

## Identity
Second of the three patriarchs; son of Abraham and Sarah; father of Esau and Jacob. The defining biblical narrative is the *Aqedah* / "Binding of Isaac" (Gen 22) — Abraham's near-sacrifice of Isaac at YHWH's command, halted at the last instant by the angel of YHWH and a substitute ram. This narrative is among the most theologically dense passages in the Hebrew Bible and a primary substrate for Jewish, Christian, and Islamic sacrificial-theological reflection.

## Historical evidence
None extrabiblical. The Isaac-cycle (Gen 21–27) is shorter than either the Abraham- or Jacob-cycle, and source-critical analysis treats Isaac as the structurally lightest of the three patriarchal figures.

## Role in the tradition
- **Jewish tradition.** The *Aqedah* is invoked liturgically on Rosh Hashanah; Levenson 1993 traces the structural pattern (the firstborn nearly killed, then restored) as the ancestor of the Israelite firstborn-redemption theology.
- **New Testament.** Hebrews 11:17-19 uses the *Aqedah* as a typological foreshadowing of resurrection ("considering that God was able to raise even from the dead"). Romans 4 deploys Abraham-and-Isaac as the paradigm of justification by faith. Galatians 4:21-31 reads Isaac and Ishmael allegorically as the children of the two covenants. Matthew's genealogy (1:2) and Stephen's speech (Acts 7) carry him.
- **Islamic tradition.** Ishaq is a prophet (Quran 19:49); in Islamic tradition the near-sacrifice involves *Ishmael* not Isaac (a long-debated tafsir question; the Quran does not actually name the son in 37:99-113, but the dominant Sunni tradition reads it as Ishmael).
