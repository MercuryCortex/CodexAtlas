# Chaim Vital

## Identity
Sephardic-Calabrian rabbi and Kabbalist (1542–1620), Isaac Luria's principal disciple and the canonical recorder of Lurianic Kabbalah. Born at Safed; studied with Moses Alshich (halakhah) and Moses Cordovero (Kabbalah) before attaching himself exclusively to Luria 1570–1572. After Luria's death, claimed authority over the Lurianic transmission against the other students; controlled access to the manuscripts; spent later life in Damascus and finally Jerusalem (~1593). Composed *Etz Chayyim* and the *Shemonah She'arim* (the Eight Gates, edited by his son Shmuel after his death) as the canonical Lurianic exposition. His *Sefer ha-Hezyonot* (Book of Visions) is a remarkable Kabbalistic autobiography preserving his dreams, mystical experiences, and (in candid detail) his anxieties about reputation and authority — a rare first-person mystical document of the early modern period.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own substantial autobiography, his Kabbalistic writings, contemporary Safed and Damascus rabbinic correspondence.

## Works
- *Etz Chayyim* (the principal Lurianic systematic).
- *Shemonah She'arim* (Eight Gates): including *Sha'ar ha-Hakdamot*, *Sha'ar Ma'amrei Rashbi*, *Sha'ar ha-Pesukim*, *Sha'ar ha-Mitzvot*, *Sha'ar ha-Kavvanot*, *Sha'ar Ruach ha-Kodesh*, *Sha'ar ha-Gilgulim* (Gate of Reincarnations), *Sha'ar ha-Likkutim*.
- *Pri Etz Chayyim* (on Lurianic kavvanot for prayer and commandments).
- *Sha'arei Kedushah* (the only major work Vital himself published in his lifetime, a more accessible meditative ethical-Kabbalistic manual).
- *Sefer ha-Hezyonot* (Book of Visions).

## Role in the tradition
Without Vital, no Lurianic Kabbalah. The canonical Lurianic corpus is his composition; the Sabbatean, Hasidic, and Christian-Kabbalist receptions of Luria all flow through his writings.
