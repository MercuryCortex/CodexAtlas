# Basilides

Alexandrian Christian Gnostic teacher under Hadrian and Antoninus Pius (~117–145). His twenty-four-book *Exegetica* (commentary on the Gospels) is lost except for fragments preserved in Clement, Hippolytus, and others. Irenaeus and Hippolytus give competing accounts of Basilidean theology — Irenaeus attributes a complex emanationist system with 365 heavens; Hippolytus attributes a more philosophically refined theology centering on a non-existent god (*ouk ōn theos*) and an ineffable seed. Löhr 1996 makes the case that Hippolytus's account is closer to Basilides himself and Irenaeus's account closer to a later Basilidean school. Either way, Basilides is the earliest named Christian Gnostic teacher we can place historically.
