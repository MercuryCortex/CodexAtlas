# Garth Fowden

## Identity
British classical and religious historian; Princeton-educated, long career at Cambridge University; later moved to the Academy of Athens and the University of Cyprus. Author of *The Egyptian Hermes* (1986) — the single most influential modern monograph on Hermetic literature, which decisively restored the Egyptian-priestly substrate of Hermetic religion against the earlier Reitzenstein (Iranian-Babylonian) and Festugière (Greek-philosophical) reconstructions. Subsequent work on late-antique monotheism and the Mediterranean first millennium.

## Historical evidence
Living scholar; works are publicly available; Cambridge UP and Princeton UP catalog his monographs. Cited 5+ times across the Codex Atlas Hermetic node-cluster.

## Works
- *The Egyptian Hermes: A Historical Approach to the Late Pagan Mind* (Cambridge UP 1986). The book.
- *Empire to Commonwealth: Consequences of Monotheism in Late Antiquity* (Princeton UP 1993).
- *Before and After Muhammad: The First Millennium Refocused* (Princeton UP 2014).

## Role in the tradition
The current scholarly consensus on Hermetism is essentially Fowden's: Hermetic literature is a late-antique Egyptian phenomenon — pagan-priestly, philosophically-Platonist, religiously-initiatory — preserved in Greek and Coptic and Latin and Armenian and Arabic, that interacts with but does not derive from Christianity, Gnosticism, or Judaism. Every contemporary Hermetic study works from Fowden's framework or argues against it.
