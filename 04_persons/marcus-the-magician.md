# Marcus the Magician

## Identity
A second-century Valentinian teacher (Marcosian sub-school) active in Asia Minor and the Rhone valley of Gaul, where Irenaeus encountered his disciples. Irenaeus's account (AH 1.13–21) is **the longest single passage** in *Against Heresies* devoted to any one figure — suggesting Marcus posed a substantial pastoral threat in Irenaeus's own diocese. Irenaeus presents Marcus as a *charlatan-magician* who manipulated the eucharistic cup to appear to bubble blood, seduced wealthy women into giving him money, taught a numerological-alphabetic mysticism, and conducted a controversial **eucharistic rite that involved women priests prophesying** (AH 1.13.3–4).

## Historical evidence
Single-source heresiological (Irenaeus, with Hippolytus dependent). Irenaeus is detailed but obviously hostile; he charges Marcus with magical, sexual, and economic exploitation. The numerological-alphabetic Marcosian theology Irenaeus reports — letters of the Greek alphabet as ontological building blocks of the Pleroma — is sophisticated and not implausible as 2nd-c. Valentinian speculation.

## Works
None survives. Irenaeus quotes Marcosian "prophecies" and ritual texts in summary.

## Role in the tradition
- **Heresiological centerpiece**: AH 1.13–21 is the textbook for what 2nd-c. proto-orthodox bishops thought wrong with the Valentinians — magical-ritual focus, women's prophecy, alphabetic mysticism.
- **Women's ministry evidence**: even through Irenaeus's hostile lens, Marcus's eucharistic rite included women prophesying and (apparently) consecrating the cup — significant data for women's liturgical roles in the period.
- **Magical-ritual Valentinianism**: where Heracleon represents intellectual exegesis and Theodotus represents sacramental theology, Marcus represents *operative magic* — closer to later Theurgy than to philosophy.

## Disputes
- **Reliability of Irenaeus**: hostile single source; the women-priests material is taken seriously by feminist scholarship as authentic data about a Marcosian practice, even after discounting Irenaeus's framing.
- **Relation to mainstream Valentinianism**: was Marcus a deviation from Valentinianism or a representative? Heresiological taxonomy is unreliable here.
- **Geographic spread**: Irenaeus's testimony shows him active in Gaul; whether Marcus himself or only his disciples were there is unclear.
