# David Brakke

## Identity
American historian of early Christianity (b. 1961); Joe R. Engle Chair in the History of Christianity and Professor of History, Ohio State University; previously at Indiana University. Editor of the *Journal of Early Christian Studies* (2005–2015).

## Works
See texts-authored. *Athanasius and the Politics of Asceticism* (1995) is a major study of [[athanasius]] as ascetic theorist and church-political actor. *Demons and the Making of the Monk* (2006) reads demonology as a constitutive ascetic technology. *The Gnostics* (2010) is the historiographically pivotal book.

## Role in the field
The 2010 *Gnostics* makes a counter-move against the post-1996 dissolution of the "Gnostic" category (Williams 1996 *Rethinking "Gnosticism"*; [[karen-king]] 2003 *What Is Gnosticism?*). Brakke argues that "Gnostic" should be retained as a *narrower* historiographical term applied to the specific Sethian-Barbeloite school attested by [[irenaeus-of-lyon]] in *Against Heresies* I.29 and matching the Nag Hammadi Sethian corpus. This restricts but does not abolish the category, providing a middle path between the older Jonas/Pagels/Rudolph "world religion" usage and the Williams/King dissolution. The position has been widely cited and is one of the dominant active framings.

## Connections
- Continues the historiographical conversation begun by [[hans-jonas]] (*The Gnostic Religion* 1958) → [[elaine-pagels]] (*The Gnostic Gospels* 1979) → [[karen-king]] (*What Is Gnosticism?* 2003) → Brakke (*The Gnostics* 2010).
- Builds on [[john-d-turner]]'s philological reconstruction of the Sethian corpus.
- Continues the ascetic-history line connecting late-antique monastic culture ([[phase-4-028-augustine-confessions]], desert-father literature) into modern academic study of Christian asceticism.

## Disputes
- **Restoring vs. dissolving "Gnostic."** Brakke 2010 vs. King 2003 / Williams 1996 is the principal active dispute. The field has not converged.
