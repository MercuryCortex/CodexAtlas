# John the Apostle

## Identity
John the Apostle, son of Zebedee, was one of the **Twelve Apostles** of Jesus and — with his brother James and with Peter — a member of Jesus's inner circle in the Synoptic Gospels. A Galilean fisherman, he became a leading figure of the earliest Jerusalem community. Christian tradition (from the 2nd century) identifies him with the unnamed "Beloved Disciple" of the Fourth Gospel and, in later tradition, as the author of the Gospel of John, the three Johannine epistles, and (in some traditions) Revelation — attributions that modern critical scholarship treats as a complex and debated question.

## Historical evidence
The historical John is attested in the Synoptic Gospels, Acts (where he appears beside Peter in the Jerusalem church), and Paul's letter to the Galatians (Paul names John among the "pillars" of the Jerusalem church). His later association with Ephesus rests on 2nd-century tradition (Irenaeus, Polycrates).

## Role in the tradition
John gives his name to the **Johannine tradition** — the Gospel of John, the Johannine letters, and the community behind them — whose distinctive high Christology (the pre-existent Logos made flesh), realized eschatology, and theology of love profoundly shaped later Christian doctrine. In Gnostic literature such as the [[phase-4-009-pistis-sophia|Pistis Sophia]], John appears among the disciples to whom the risen Jesus reveals esoteric teaching.

## Cross-tradition findings
- **The Johannine Logos.** The prologue attributed to the Johannine tradition ("In the beginning was the Word [Logos]…") is the New Testament's most direct point of contact with Greek and Hellenistic-Jewish *logos* speculation (Stoic *logos*, Philo of Alexandria's *Logos*), and one of the Atlas's key transmission junctions between Greco-Egyptian philosophy and Christian theology. This connection is carried by the Johannine literature rather than by the apostle as a person.

## Disputes
- **Authorship.** Whether John son of Zebedee wrote the Gospel of John, the epistles, and/or Revelation is heavily debated. Critical scholarship generally distinguishes the apostle, the Beloved Disciple, the evangelist, and the seer of Patmos ([[john-of-patmos]]), which earlier tradition merged into one. The Atlas keeps the apostle as a distinct node from John of Patmos.
