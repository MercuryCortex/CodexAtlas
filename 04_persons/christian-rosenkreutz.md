# Christian Rosenkreutz

## Identity
Legendary founder of the Rosicrucian Brotherhood, as narrated in the *Fama Fraternitatis* (1614) and *Confessio Fraternitatis* (1615). According to the *Fama*, Rosenkreutz was a German noble born 1378, educated in a monastery, traveled in his late teens to Damascus, Fez, and Egypt where he received Arabic-Egyptian wisdom from the "wise men of the East," returned to Germany at age 35–36, founded the Brotherhood with four initial brothers, established a building called the House of the Holy Spirit (*Sancti Spiritus*), and lived to 106, dying in 1484. The *Fama* narrates that his tomb was rediscovered 120 years after his death (i.e., in 1604), undecayed, with miraculous inscriptions and the founder's intact body — the discovery of the tomb being the announced reason for the Brotherhood's 1614 public emergence.

## Historical evidence
None. Modern scholarly consensus (Gilly, van Dülmen, Brecht) is that Christian Rosenkreutz is a literary-allegorical construction by the Tübingen circle of Andreae, Hess, and Besold, possibly designed as a deliberate *ludibrium* (Andreae's later term) to provoke universal-reformation discussion. The dates of the legend (1378 birth, 1484 death, 1604 tomb-rediscovery, 1614 emergence) are themselves significant: the 120-year interval between death and rediscovery is the Christian Kabbalist symbolic period for the cycle of the Holy Spirit's manifestation, marking the legend's symbolic-allegorical character.

## Works
None. The first-person narration of the *Chymical Wedding* (1616) is a literary device; the actual author is Andreae.

## Role in the tradition
As a *legendary* figure: the symbolic founder of the Rosicrucian movement and the entire Western esoteric tradition that traces itself back to the 1614 manifestos. Every modern Rosicrucian order claims symbolic descent from Christian Rosenkreutz. As a *literary construction*: an early example of the trope of the hidden adept and the secret brotherhood, which has shaped Western esoteric and occult fiction from the Rosicrucian novels of the 17th–18th c. through the present.

## Disputes
- **Historicity.** None. Even the most generous esoteric-internal readings treat Rosenkreutz as primarily symbolic; mainstream scholarship treats him as wholly literary.
