# ʿUzayr

Quranic figure mentioned in a single verse: **Surah 9:30** — "The Jews say: ʿUzayr is the son of God. The Christians say: The Messiah is the son of God. This is their spoken claim; they imitate the speech of those who disbelieved before. May God destroy them; how they are deluded!"

## The identification question

The traditional identification with **Ezra the Scribe** (Hebrew: *ʿEzrāʾ*, the post-exilic priest and Torah-transmitter of the books of Ezra and Nehemiah) is ancient in Islamic exegesis but historically puzzling, since mainstream Jewish tradition does not attribute divine sonship to Ezra. The verse has generated extensive scholarly debate:

1. **Traditional Islamic exegesis** identifies ʿUzayr with Ezra and explains the "son of God" claim as a hyperbolic veneration of Ezra's role in restoring the Torah after Babylonian exile.
2. **Modern critical scholarship** (including Reynolds 2018) has explored whether the verse reflects a specific sectarian Jewish community known to the Quran's milieu (possibly a sect that venerated Ezra unusually highly), or whether it reflects a polemic against Jewish Torah-devotion metaphorically expressed as "sonship."
3. **Wheeler 2002** notes that no surviving Jewish source describes Ezra as "son of God," and suggests the verse may be engaging with a specific late-antique Near Eastern Jewish community or tradition no longer extant.

## Theological significance

The verse's primary function in the Quran is to place Jewish veneration of ʿUzayr structurally parallel to Christian veneration of Jesus as "son of God" — both are presented as forms of *shirk* (associating partners with God). This makes ʿUzayr a crucial node for the Quran's comparative monotheological polemic against both Judaism and Christianity.
