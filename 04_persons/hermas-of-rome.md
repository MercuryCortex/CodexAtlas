# Hermas of Rome

## Identity
**Hermas** (c. 95–165 CE) was a Christian visionary and author at Rome — by his own account in the *Shepherd of Hermas*, a *freed slave* who had been sold to a Roman Christian woman named Rhoda, who appears to him in a vision that begins the work. The text he composed — the **Shepherd of Hermas** ([[phase-4-065-shepherd-of-hermas]]) — became one of the most widely-read Christian texts of the 2nd century and was considered canonical scripture by significant 2nd–4th-c. Greek and Latin Christian authorities (Irenaeus, Clement of Alexandria, Origen, Tertullian in his earlier work all cite it as scripture) before being demoted to *useful but not canonical* status in the Western canon-formation of the 4th–5th c. It remains **canonical scripture in [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]]** to the present — one of the most distinctive Ethiopian-canonical retentions of a pre-Nicene Christian text dropped elsewhere.

The Muratorian Fragment (~170 CE) identifies Hermas as the brother of **Pope Pius I** (~140–155 CE) — making the *Shepherd* a Roman-Christian production of the mid-2nd century. The Origenian identification of Hermas with the *Hermas* greeted in Romans 16:14 (Paul's letter to Rome, ~57 CE) is now considered chronologically untenable (the Romans-16 Hermas would have been ~120 years old by the *Shepherd*'s composition); modern scholarship treats the two as distinct, with the Muratorian Pius-brother identification accepted.

## Historical evidence
- **The Shepherd of Hermas itself** — surviving in Greek (Codex Sinaiticus, Athos manuscripts, papyri), Latin, Ethiopic, Coptic, and Middle Persian-Manichaean. Hermas's first-person narration provides the principal biographical sketch: freed slave, sold to Rhoda, married with children, businessman in Rome.
- **Muratorian Fragment ~170 CE** — Latin canon-list naming Hermas as Pius I's brother.
- **Origen, Clement, Irenaeus, Tertullian (early), Eusebius** — extensive 2nd–4th-c. Christian citation of Hermas as authority.
- **Ge'ez canonical preservation** — Ethiopian Tewahedo retains the Shepherd as canonical NT-adjacent scripture, making Hermas a *canonical author* in Ethiopian Christian tradition.

## Works
- **[[phase-4-065-shepherd-of-hermas]]** — divided into three major parts: 5 Visions, 12 Mandates (Commandments), 10 Similitudes (Parables). The work is one of the longest extant 2nd-c. Christian texts; its principal theological concern is *the possibility of post-baptismal repentance* — a question central to early-Christian sacramental development.

## Role in the tradition
Hermas is one of the most-read 2nd-c. Christian authors after the canonical NT writers themselves. His distinctive contribution is the *literary form* of the visionary-allegorical-pastoral apocalypse, combining moral teaching, ecclesial discipline, and apocalyptic-eschatological framing. The personification-cluster of his visions — [[the-shepherd-angel-of-repentance]], [[the-lady-ecclesia-hermas]], the Tower-as-Church-allegory, the twelve mountains-as-Christian-types — is the most-developed personified-angelology in pre-Nicene Christian literature.

In Ethiopian Orthodox tradition Hermas is a *canonical author* — his work is scripture, not apocrypha. This is the most consequential single canonical-status divergence between Ethiopian Tewahedo and other Christian traditions for a NT-era figure (alongside the canonical retention of 1 Enoch and Jubilees for OT-era pseudepigrapha).

## Disputes
- **Identity with Romans 16:14 Hermas.** Origen's identification is chronologically unworkable; modern scholarship treats the two as distinct.
- **Single author or multi-stage composition?** The *Shepherd* shows possible multi-stage compositional history (the Visions may be an earlier stratum than the Mandates and Similitudes). Most scholarship (Osiek 1999) treats it as substantively single-author with some redactional layering.
- **Date.** The Muratorian dating (~140–155 CE, Pius I era) is the consensus; some scholarship pushes earlier (~90–100 CE).
