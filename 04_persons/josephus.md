# Flavius Josephus

## Identity
Yosef ben Matityahu (~+37 to ~+100 CE), known as Flavius Josephus after his Roman naturalization, was the leading **Jewish historian of antiquity** and the single most important non-Christian source for 1st-century Jewish history, Second Temple Judaism, the Hasmonean and Herodian periods, and the early Roman procuratorial era. A priestly aristocrat, Pharisaically trained, he commanded Jewish forces in Galilee at the outbreak of the First Jewish War (+66), surrendered at Jotapata (+67), prophesied Vespasian's accession to the imperial throne (which came true two years later), was granted Roman citizenship under Flavian patronage, and spent the rest of his life in Rome writing the four major works that survive.

## Historical evidence
Self-documented (the autobiographical *Vita*); independently corroborated through Roman imperial records; quoted and engaged with by patristic and rabbinic-adjacent literature. The works survive complete through Christian transmission — Eusebius treated Josephus as nearly canonical for OT-historical purposes, and the *Antiquities* in particular preserved Christian-interest passages (most famously the disputed Testimonium Flavianum).

## Works
- ***The Jewish War*** — eyewitness/participant history of +66/+73, including the destruction of the Temple, Masada, and the human cost of the revolt. Composed first in Aramaic for an Eastern Jewish audience (lost), then in Greek for the Roman elite.
- ***Antiquities of the Jews*** — a 20-volume Greek history paralleling Roman antiquarianism (Dionysius of Halicarnassus): from Creation, through the Hebrew Bible (extensively paraphrased with extra-biblical traditions), to the outbreak of the war.
- ***Against Apion*** — apologetic defense of Jewish antiquity, monotheism, and ethical superiority against pagan critics.
- ***Life*** — self-justifying autobiography responding to charges of Galilean misconduct.

## Role in the tradition
**For Jewish history**: indispensable. Without Josephus, knowledge of the Hasmonean dynasty, Herod the Great's reign, Pontius Pilate's tenure, the Sadducean-Pharisaic-Essene division, the Jewish War, and Masada would be drastically reduced. Rabbinic Judaism treated him ambivalently as a Roman collaborator and forgot him for nearly a millennium; he was rediscovered as a Jewish historical resource only in modern times.

**For Christian-origins study**: the *Antiquities* contains:
- The ***Testimonium Flavianum*** (18.63–64) — partially interpolated reference to Jesus; original Josephan core remains historically valuable.
- The ***James reference*** (20.200) — the undisputed mention of "James the brother of Jesus who was called Christ"; one of the firmest non-Christian attestations of Jesus's existence.
- The ***John the Baptist passage*** (18.116–119) — fully Josephan, independent of Christian framing.
- Material on the Essenes, Sadducees, Pharisees, the procurators, Herodian dynasty.

## Disputes
- **Reliability.** Josephus is *not* a neutral historian: he is an apologist for both Judaism (to Roman audiences) and his own conduct (in the *Vita*). His Galilean-command narrative is self-contradictory between *War* and *Vita*. Mason (1992, 2003) emphasizes reading him as a *literary-political* author, not as a transparent reporter.
- **Testimonium Flavianum.** Three main positions: total interpolation (rare now); partial Christian interpolation around a Josephan core (mainstream consensus — Meier, Vermes, Feldman); fully authentic (conservative minority). The James-reference at 20.200 is universally accepted as authentic and is the strongest evidentiary anchor.
- **Numbers.** Josephus's casualty figures (e.g., 1.1 million dead in the Jewish War) are widely judged exaggerated for rhetorical effect.
