# Marcion of Sinope

A wealthy Christian shipowner from Sinope on the Black Sea who came to Rome ~140 and proposed the most radical theological revision in early Christianity: the God of the Hebrew Bible (the just, vengeful creator) is a *different and lesser God* from the Father of Jesus Christ (the alien God of pure love). Marcion rejected the Hebrew Bible entirely and produced the first known fixed Christian canon — ten letters of Paul plus an edited Gospel of Luke, his *Evangelion* — explicitly excluding what he considered Judaizing material.

Marcion was expelled from the Roman church ~144 and founded a parallel Marcionite Christianity that spread widely across the Mediterranean and Asia (lasting in pockets into the medieval period). His provocation of the proto-orthodox church catalyzed the development of the four-Gospel canon (against his single-Gospel canon), the explicit affirmation of the Hebrew Bible as Christian scripture, and the rule of faith identifying the OT creator with the Christian Father. Whether Marcion was a Gnostic (Harnack: no, a Pauline radical) or proto-Gnostic (Lieu: theologically adjacent) is debated. His two-Gods theology, though not Sethian-style emanationism, parallels the Gnostic demiurge / Father split.
