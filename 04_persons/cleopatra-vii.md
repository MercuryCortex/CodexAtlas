# Cleopatra VII Philopator

## Identity
Cleopatra VII Philopator (-69 to -30) was the **last active ruler of Ptolemaic Egypt** and the **last Pharaoh** in the dynastic line going back to [[ptolemy-i-soter]]. Daughter of Ptolemy XII Auletes, she ruled Egypt jointly with her brothers and then alone from -51 to -30. Her political-religious significance for the Codex Atlas mission is **fourfold**: (1) she is the **terminal political figure of the Ptolemaic Greek-Egyptian synthesis** — her death in -30 marks Egypt's absorption into the Roman Empire; (2) she **ritually identified herself with [[isis]]** ("Nea Isis" — the New Isis) in elaborate religious self-presentation, making her the most consequential Hellenistic-period institutional embodiment of the [[isis-hellenistic|Hellenistic Isis cult]]; (3) her **political-religious alliance with Mark Antony** institutionalized the Greco-Egyptian-Roman religious synthesis at the highest political level for two decades; (4) the **Augustan propaganda construction** of Cleopatra — Octavian's anti-Cleopatra publicity campaign generating massive Latin-language literary response — is the **founding instance of Western anti-Egyptian and anti-feminine political-religious polemic**, with consequences running through Roman, medieval Christian, and modern Orientalist constructions of "the Eastern queen."

## Historical evidence
Cleopatra is among the most thoroughly documented figures of antiquity, but the surviving documentation is **overwhelmingly hostile** (Augustan-era Latin sources) or **legendary-romantic** (Plutarch's *Life of Antony*, written ~150 years later). Primary documentary evidence:
- **Greek and demotic-Egyptian papyri** from her reign (administrative documents, tax records, decrees) — substantial corpus.
- **Coinage** — extensive issues bearing her portrait.
- **The Edfu temple inscriptions** — Cleopatra and Caesarion (her son by Julius Caesar) shown as Pharaonic divinities in classical Egyptian iconography.
- **Egyptian-priestly stelae** — Cleopatra's Pharaonic regnal-cult inscriptions.
- **The "Cleopatra-as-Isis" cult-imagery** — sculptural and numismatic.

Narrative sources (all hostile or later): Plutarch *Life of Antony*; Cassius Dio *Roman History* 41–51; Appian *Civil Wars*; Suetonius *Augustus* and *Julius Caesar*; Velleius Paterculus.

## Works
None historically authored. Cleopatra was reputedly **multilingual** (Plutarch *Antony* 27 reports she spoke nine languages including Egyptian — the first Ptolemaic ruler to bother learning the native language of her own kingdom) and reputedly a learned woman engaged with Alexandrian intellectual culture. Late-antique magical-medical papyrology preserves works attributed to "Cleopatra" — the *Cleopatra Cosmetics*, the *Cleopatra Weights and Measures*, the *Cleopatra Letter on Childbirth*, and alchemical-Hermetic *Cleopatra-letters* — all pseudepigraphic.

## Role in the tradition
For the Codex Atlas mission Cleopatra's role unfolds in four registers:

1. **Terminal Ptolemaic Pharaoh.** Her death by suicide in -30 (the famous asp at her breast, per Plutarch — modern scholarship is skeptical of the snake-specifically but accepts suicide) ends 275 years of Ptolemaic Greek-Macedonian rule and begins Roman provincial rule of Egypt. The institutional infrastructure her ancestors built — Library of Alexandria, Serapis cult, Greco-Egyptian temple religion — continues under Roman rule, but the **Pharaonic dynastic-religious institution ends with her**.

2. **Nea Isis — the New Isis.** Cleopatra deployed elaborate religious self-presentation as **the living incarnation of [[isis]]** — wearing Isis-attributes in public ceremonies, taking the cult-title *Nea Isis* ("the New Isis"), being depicted on coinage with Isis-iconography. This is the highest-political-level expression of the **Hellenistic Isis cult** and contributes substantially to Isis's spread across the Roman world in the subsequent century. The Isis-Mary-Theotokos influence-edge ([[isis-hellenistic]] → Marian iconography in [[clement-of-alexandria]]'s Alexandria) runs through the Cleopatran institutional moment.

3. **Antony alliance and Greco-Egyptian-Roman religious synthesis.** Cleopatra's political-religious partnership with **Mark Antony** (-41 to -30) involved Antony's self-presentation as **Dionysus / Osiris** to Cleopatra's Isis — institutionalizing the **Greek-Egyptian-Roman triple synthesis** at the highest political-religious level for two decades. The "Donations of Alexandria" (-34) — in which Antony distributed Roman eastern territories to Cleopatra and their children — was framed in Hellenistic-Egyptian religious-political language and was a principal Augustan-propaganda target.

4. **Augustan anti-Cleopatra construction.** Octavian's defeat of Antony and Cleopatra at the **[[event-battle-of-actium-31-bce|Battle of Actium]] (-31)** institutionalizes an extensive propaganda campaign portraying Cleopatra as the **decadent Eastern queen** corrupting Roman virtue — Horace's "*fatale monstrum*" ode (*Odes* 1.37); Virgil's *Aeneid* 8.685–713 (Actium-shield ekphrasis); the entire Augustan-poet construction of *Aegyptus* as the threatening other. **This is the founding instance of Western anti-Egyptian and anti-feminine political-religious polemic**, with downstream consequences in Roman, medieval Christian, and modern Orientalist constructions of Egypt-and-Eastern-women.

## Disputes
- **Suicide details.** Whether Cleopatra died by snakebite (the famous asp tradition) or by poison-mixture is debated — modern scholarship leans poison; the snake is plausibly Augustan-propaganda embellishment (the asp = Egyptian *uraeus* = royal cobra-emblem, making the asp-suicide image symbolically loaded).
- **Native-Egyptian engagement.** How seriously Cleopatra engaged with native-Egyptian religion (versus Hellenistic-Egyptian Greek-language Alexandrian religion) is debated. Plutarch's report that she learned Egyptian is taken seriously by modern scholarship; her Edfu and Dendera temple-inscriptions show genuine Pharaonic-religious engagement.
- **Cleopatra and Alexandrian intellectual culture.** Whether Cleopatra was actively engaged with Library scholars (Manetho-era institutional patronage being mostly her ancestors' work) or was a passive inheritor of the institutions is debated. Recent scholarship leans toward more active engagement.
- **The Caesarion question.** Cleopatra's son by Julius Caesar (Ptolemy XV Caesarion, "Little Caesar") was the legitimate Ptolemaic heir; Octavian's execution of Caesarion in -30 (after Cleopatra's death) was politically necessary to eliminate the dynastic rival but morally embarrassing — Augustan sources are evasive about it.

## The Oniad Temple connection
During Cleopatra VII's reign (and throughout the broader Ptolemaic period), the **Temple of Onias at Leontopolis** — a Jewish Zadokite temple ~40 km from Alexandria — was operating simultaneously with the Alexandrian intellectual milieu Cleopatra inherited. The [[event-temple-leontopolis-foundation-c150bce]] was founded by [[onias-iv]] (~150 BCE) under Ptolemy VI Philometor; it operated until 73 CE. The Oniad priestly community and the Alexandrian Jewish intellectual culture (Philo, the Septuagint translators) occupied the same geographic and social space during much of Cleopatra's lifetime. The Ptolemaic dynasty is thus the political framework within which the Zadokite priestly tradition preserved itself in Egyptian exile — see [[theme-aaronide-priestly-continuity]].
