# Zhu Xi (朱熹)

The most influential Confucian thinker after Confucius himself. Born in Fujian 1130, died 1200. Zhu Xi synthesized the Song Confucian masters into a comprehensive philosophical system — the **li-qi** framework:
- *Li* (理, Principle): the unchanging moral-metaphysical pattern embedded in all things; the source of goodness in the cosmos
- *Qi* (氣, Vital force): the dynamic material-energy that actualizes li in particular forms; variable, corruptible, the source of evil through turbidity

He compiled and wrote commentaries on the **Four Books** (Analects, Mencius, Great Learning, Doctrine of the Mean), elevating them above the Five Classics. From 1313 onward, his Four Books commentaries defined the curriculum for China's imperial civil service examinations — the longest-running meritocratic institution in world history. Every educated Chinese person for 600 years was trained in Zhu Xi's synthesis.

**The paradox:** Zhu Xi claimed to purify Confucianism of Buddhist and Daoist contamination. He absorbed Buddhist Huayan metaphysical vocabulary (*li-qi* directly parallels Huayan's *li-shi* ontology) while insisting on Confucian social ethics as the ultimate framework. The same move Thomas Aquinas makes with Aristotelian-Islamic philosophy.
