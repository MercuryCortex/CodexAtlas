# Lydia of Thyatira

## Identity
A Gentile **God-fearer** (Acts 16:14) and *porphyropōlis* (dealer in purple cloth — a luxury commodity, implying wealth) originally from Thyatira in Asia Minor but resident at Philippi in Macedonia. Acts presents her as **the first convert in Europe** in the Pauline mission, baptized with her whole household, and as the host of the nascent Philippian house-church.

## Historical evidence
Single-source (Lukan), but Thyatira was indeed a major center of purple-dye production (corroborated by inscriptions), and a Thyatiran trade-diaspora at Philippi is geographically and economically plausible. The Pauline letter to the Philippians is famously affectionate toward this community, and Lydia is often considered (silently) one of the patrons behind that warmth.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
A foundational figure in feminist NT scholarship as a documented female patron and house-church host. The Philippian church (the Pauline community that financially supported Paul, Phil 4:14–18) was likely sustained in significant part by her household. Catholic and Orthodox calendars commemorate her.

## Disputes
- **Real personal name or trade-epithet?** "Lydia" may be the personal name *or* a regional epithet ("the Lydian woman"). Lukan composition makes it irresolvable.
- **Absence from Pauline letters.** Striking; possibly under a different name (Euodia? Syntyche? — Phil 4:2) — speculative.
