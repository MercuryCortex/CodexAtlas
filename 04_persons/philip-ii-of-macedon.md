# Philip II of Macedon

King of Macedon 359-336 BCE; father of [[alexander-the-great|Alexander III]]. Reformed the Macedonian army (the long sarissa-armed phalanx; combined-arms tactics with cavalry; engineering and siege capability) that his son would use to conquer the Achaemenid Persian Empire. Established Macedonian hegemony over Greece by victory at Chaeronea (338 BCE); founded the League of Corinth (337 BCE) as the political vehicle for the planned Panhellenic war of revenge against Persia for Xerxes's invasions. Was assassinated at Aegae (336 BCE) at his daughter's wedding by his bodyguard Pausanias of Orestis, leaving the campaign and the kingship to his twenty-year-old son Alexander.
