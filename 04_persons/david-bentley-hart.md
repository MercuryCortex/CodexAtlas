# David Bentley Hart

## Identity
American theologian, philosopher, cultural critic, and translator (b. 1965). Eastern Orthodox; PhD in theology, University of Virginia 2001 (dissertation supervised by Robert Jenson). Held positions at Loyola College, Duke, Providence College, and Notre Dame (Institute for Advanced Study); has been largely an independent scholar. Younger brother of theologian Addison Hart; older brother of theologian Robert Hart.

## Works
See texts-authored. *The Beauty of the Infinite* (2003) is the dissertation-derived dogmatic-trinitarian-aesthetics monograph engaging with Hans Urs von Balthasar, Karl Barth, and the postmodernists (Derrida, Levinas, Milbank). *The Experience of God* (2013) defends classical theism (the convergent metaphysics of God across Christianity, Judaism, Islam, Vedanta) against both atheist and theistic-personalist mischaracterizations. *That All Shall Be Saved* (2019) argues for Christian universalism (universal final salvation) on Greek-patristic, scriptural, and philosophical grounds — controversially within Orthodox and Catholic theology. *The New Testament: A Translation* (2017) is a deliberately literal, often strange Greek-Bible rendering.

## Role in the field
A high-profile Anglophone theological intellectual operating across academic theology, philosophy of religion, and Christian public discourse. Continues the patristic-resourcement line ([[gregory-of-nyssa]], [[maximus-the-confessor]], [[origen]]) and engages with Orthodox theologians like Sergei Bulgakov and modern Catholic resourcement (de Lubac, Balthasar, [[hans-urs-von-balthasar]]). His translation work continues the Tyndale-onwards Anglophone Bible-translation tradition with a deliberately patristic-philosophical sensibility.

## Disputes
- **Universalism (*apokatastasis*).** Hart's defense of universal salvation has been contested by Catholic and Orthodox theologians (e.g., Michael McClymond's *The Devil's Redemption* 2018; Edward Feser).
- **Translation choices.** His NT translation is widely criticized by Greek-NT specialists for idiosyncratic renderings (e.g., *aiōnios* as "of the Age" rather than "eternal," which is methodologically defensible but reads strangely).
- **Combative public style.** Hart's polemical mode (against the New Atheists in *Atheist Delusions*, against analytic theistic-personalism, against American evangelical eschatology) is itself a subject of methodological discussion.
