# Jan Hus (c. 1369–1415)

## Identity
Jan Hus was a Bohemian theologian, preacher, philosopher, and rector of Charles University in Prague whose execution at the Council of Constance (July 6, 1415) — in violation of an imperial safe-conduct promise — triggered the Hussite Wars and made him the founding martyr of the Bohemian Reformation and a proto-Reformation figure acknowledged by Luther himself.

## Life
Born in Husinec, Bohemia (~1369); studied theology and philosophy at Prague; ordained ~1400; appointed rector of Bethlehem Chapel, Prague (1402) — a Czech-language preaching center with massive popular reach. Master of Arts and later rector of Charles University (1409). His preaching drew on John Wyclif's writings, which had reached Prague through Czech students at Oxford. He demanded:
- Communion in **both kinds** (*sub utraque specie* — bread AND wine for the laity), where Rome gave the laity only bread
- Scripture's authority over papal decrees
- Reform of clerical corruption and simony
- **Vernacular preaching** in Czech, making theology accessible to the laity

Excommunicated by the Archbishop of Prague (1411) and later by Pope John XXIII. Traveled to the Council of Constance (1414) under Holy Roman Emperor Sigismund's letter of safe-conduct to defend his views — and was immediately imprisoned. After a trial in which he was not permitted to speak systematically in his own defense, he was condemned on 30 counts and burned on July 6, 1415. His ashes were thrown into the Rhine to prevent veneration.

## Wyclif connection
Hus is often called "Wyclif's pupil," which oversimplifies. He did absorb Wyclif's ecclesiological views (*De Ecclesia* directly mirrors Wyclif's work of the same name) but was less radical on transubstantiation. The Prague connection shows that reform ideas circulated internationally within the Latin Church before Luther.

## Hussite Wars aftermath
Hus's execution triggered an immediate Czech national-religious uprising. The Hussites — fighting under Jan Žižka — defeated five imperial crusades (1420–1431). The Council of Basel (1436) conceded communion in both kinds to the Bohemian church — Hus's central demand, won 21 years after his death.

## Luther's explicit acknowledgment
At the Leipzig Debate (1519), Martin Luther declared: "We are all Hussites without knowing it." Luther had read *De Ecclesia* in Erfurt. The Hus → Luther genealogy is not retrospective — it was consciously owned by Luther. See [[event-death-of-jan-hus-1415]] for the full persecution-as-legitimation sequence.
