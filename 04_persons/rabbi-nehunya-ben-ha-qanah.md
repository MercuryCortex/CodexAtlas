# Rabbi Nehunya ben ha-Qanah

## Identity
Rabbi Nehunya ben ha-Qanah was a **Tanna** of the late 1st / early 2nd century CE who, like [[rabbi-ishmael-ben-elisha|Ishmael ben Elisha]] and [[rabbi-akiva|Akiva]], became a central pseudepigraphic figure in [[tradition-merkavah-mysticism|Merkavah and Hekhalot mysticism]]. In *Hekhalot Rabbati* he is portrayed as the master who, seated in a theurgic trance, dictates the secrets of the heavenly ascent to a circle of disciples. Later Kabbalistic tradition attributed to him the *Sefer ha-Bahir* and the 42-letter-Name prayer *Ana be-Khoaḥ*.

## Historical evidence
The historical Nehunya is named in Tannaitic sources (he was a teacher of R. Ishmael by some accounts) and is remembered for a brief prayer recorded in the Babylonian Talmud (Berakhot 28b). As with the other Hekhalot heroes, the elaborate mystical persona is a later literary attribution that borrowed his authority.

## Context
In the Hekhalot literature Nehunya functions as the *revealer* of ascent techniques — the disciplinary practices, angelic names, and seals by which the adept passes the gatekeepers of the seven palaces. The *Sefer ha-Bahir*, the earliest text of the emergent Kabbalah (redacted in Provence ~1170s but drawing older material), opens under his name, making him a bridge figure between late-antique Merkavah mysticism and medieval Kabbalah.

## Cross-tradition findings
- **Divine-name theurgy.** The practice of ascent and protection through recited divine names and angelic seals — strongly associated with Nehunya in the Hekhalot texts — parallels the broader late-antique magical-theurgic milieu (the Greek Magical Papyri, Hermetic ascent, Gnostic passwords for the archons). The Atlas treats this as shared late-antique religious technology, wired as a parallel.
