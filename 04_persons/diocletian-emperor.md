# Diocletian

## Identity
Roman emperor 284–305, principal architect of the late-3rd-c. imperial reorganization (the Tetrarchy: two senior *Augusti* and two junior *Caesares* dividing administrative responsibility), tax and monetary reformer, and the emperor who initiated the **Great Persecution** of 303–311 — the most systematic empire-wide anti-Christian campaign in Roman history. Voluntarily abdicated (305) and retired to his palace at Split (Spalatum) on the Dalmatian coast.

## Historical evidence
**Lactantius** (*De mortibus persecutorum*, ~+315) is the principal contemporary narrative source; **Eusebius HE 8** is the second; archaeological and inscriptional evidence is abundant. The edicts of 303 (24 February, against churches; spring, against clergy; March, against Christian books; summer, against Christian laity) and 304 (universal sacrifice) are all narratively preserved though the original texts are lost.

## Works
- Edict against churches (303): destroy church buildings, burn scriptures, deprive Christians of legal rights.
- Edict against clergy (303): arrest clergy.
- Edict against scriptures (303): hand over scriptures to be burned — created the **traditor crisis** in North Africa (Christians who handed over books) that would erupt into the Donatist schism after the persecution.
- Edict against laity (303): require sacrifice.
- Edict of universal sacrifice (304): empire-wide sacrifice required, on the Decian model.

## Role in the tradition
- **The Great Persecution**: the most severe, lasting (303–311 in the East), and theologically formative persecution. Christian survivors held a *theological* status approximating that of the apostolic martyrs.
- **Traditor / Donatist crisis aftermath**: the persecution created post-persecution disputes about how to treat *traditores* (those who handed over scriptures) — central to North African ecclesiology and the Donatist schism through the 4th c.
- **Tetrarchic religious framing**: Diocletian's self-identification as **Jovian** (under Jupiter's protection), his colleague Maximian as **Herculian** (under Hercules's), was an explicit re-paganization of the imperial idea — a deliberate counter-Christian theological move.
- **Abdication**: in itself unprecedented; framed by Diocletian as religious-philosophical retreat.

## Disputes
- **Personal vs. ministerial responsibility**: Lactantius blames Galerius (Diocletian's Caesar) as the principal anti-Christian driver; Diocletian may have been pushed.
- **Galerius's edict of toleration (311)**: ended the persecution in the East; preceded the Edict of Milan (313) by two years.
- **Death in retirement**: Diocletian died ~+311/+313 in his palace at Split, possibly by suicide as Lactantius and others suggest.
