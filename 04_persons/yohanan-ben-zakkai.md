# Yohanan ben Zakkai

## Identity
Pharisaic-rabbinic leader of the generation that survived the Roman destruction of the Second Temple (70). Per the famous rabbinic narrative (Bavli Gittin 56a-b; Avot de-Rabbi Natan A 4; Lam. Rabbah 1.5), at the climax of the Roman siege he had himself smuggled out of besieged Jerusalem in a coffin, approached Vespasian, and obtained imperial permission to **establish an academy at Yavneh (Jamnia)** — the institution that re-grounded Judaism on Torah-study after the Temple was no longer available as the cult-center. He is conventionally the **founder of rabbinic Judaism** in its post-Temple form.

## Historical evidence
Rabbinic only; no extra-Jewish attestation. The narrative is theologically and literarily shaped — the coffin-escape is a folkloric topos — but the *fact* of a Yavneh-centered Pharisaic reorganization after 70, with Yohanan ben Zakkai as principal figure, is widely accepted by critical scholarship (Neusner's life is the standard treatment).

## Works
None directly. Sayings and rulings preserved in the Tannaitic corpus.

## Role in the tradition
- **Founding figure of post-Temple rabbinic Judaism**: re-centered worship on Torah-study, prayer, and synagogue rather than priestly sacrifice — the **transition that made post-70 Judaism survivable**.
- **Vespasian negotiation**: the rabbinic tradition that he prophesied Vespasian's elevation to emperor and obtained his sponsorship is structurally parallel to [[josephus|Josephus]]'s career-defining prophecy of the same — possibly historical kernel underlying both.
- **The five Tannaitic disciples**: Eliezer, Joshua, Yose, Shimon, and Elazar — the second generation of rabbinic authority traced through him.
- **Christian-Jewish boundary period**: contemporary with the development of the **Birkat ha-Minim** (the synagogue benediction against heretics) — a critical period for Christian-Jewish separation, though Yohanan ben Zakkai's personal role in this is debated.

## Disputes
- **Historicity of the coffin escape**: legendary; the underlying mission to Vespasian is plausible.
- **Date of Yavneh foundation**: ~+70/+73; precise date unknown.
- **Role in Christian-Jewish separation**: rabbinic sources do not record his anti-Christian polemic specifically; the Birkat ha-Minim's authorship is debated.
