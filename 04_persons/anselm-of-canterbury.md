# Anselm of Canterbury

## Identity
Anselm of Canterbury (1033–1109) was the principal proto-scholastic theologian — abbot of Bec (Normandy) and from 1093 Archbishop of Canterbury in tense circumstances with Kings William II and Henry I. Architect of the "ontological argument" for God's existence (*Proslogion* chapter 2: *id quo maius cogitari nequit* — that than which nothing greater can be thought) and of the substitutionary-satisfaction theory of atonement (*Cur Deus Homo*, "Why God Became Man," ~1098). His method — "*fides quaerens intellectum*" (faith seeking understanding) — set the stylistic template for the entire scholastic tradition that followed.

## Works
*Monologion*; *Proslogion*; *Cur Deus Homo*; *De Veritate*; *De Libertate Arbitrii*; *De Casu Diaboli*; *De Concordia*; substantial correspondence.

## Role in the tradition
Bridges Augustine and the scholastic synthesis; the ontological argument and the satisfaction-atonement theory are his two most influential single contributions to Western theology.
