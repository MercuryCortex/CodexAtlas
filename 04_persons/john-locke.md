# John Locke

English philosopher whose *Two Treatises of Government* (1689) argued that political authority is conditional — government derives legitimacy from its service to the people's natural rights (life, liberty, property), and the people have a right to resist and replace a government that violates that trust. His *Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1689) argued that the mind at birth is a *tabula rasa* (blank slate), directly opposing innate-idea theories — placing him on the opposite side from Mengzi on the innate-goodness question even as his political theory parallels Mengzi's conditional Mandate of Heaven. Key cross-tradition parallel: Mengzi 7B:14 (people are most important; rulers can be removed) ↔ Locke's right to revolution (~1,950 years later, independent).
