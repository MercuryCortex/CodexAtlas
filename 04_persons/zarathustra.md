# Zarathustra

## Identity
Zarathustra (Greek: Zoroaster) is the founding prophet of Zoroastrianism, a Bactrian / eastern Iranian *zaotar*-priest who, by his own account in the Gathas, experienced direct visionary commissioning by Ahura Mazda and founded — or radically reformed — what became the Iranian state religion. Trained as a sacrificial priest in the inherited Indo-Iranian tradition, he turned against the daēva-cults of his time and proclaimed Ahura Mazda as the sole supreme god, with cosmic dualism (Truth vs. the Lie) as the structuring principle of existence.

## Historical evidence
Direct first-person voice in the Gathas (Yasna 28–34, 43–46, 47–50, 51, 53) — the strongest evidence for a historical author of any "Axial Age" prophetic founder. The Gathas name him repeatedly, describe his social circumstances (priestly training, persecution, eventual royal patronage by Vishtaspa), and convey a coherent personal theological voice. Linguistic dating of Old Avestan places these hymns at roughly the same era as the early Vedic Sanskrit (~1500–1000 BCE). No archaeological inscription or contemporary record outside the Gathas survives.

## Works
- Authored: the 17 Gathas embedded in the Yasna.
- Some Younger Avestan hymns are traditionally attributed to him but are later school products.

## Role in the tradition
Founder, prophet, and exemplar. Zoroastrian liturgy is structured around his hymns. Later tradition (Pahlavi books) mythologizes his birth, life, and miracles. Greek tradition (from at least the 5th c. BCE) recognized him as a great Eastern sage — Plato, Aristotle, and later Hellenistic philosophers cite him, sometimes as a paradigmatic figure of ancient wisdom.

## Disputes
- **Date.** Boyce / Skjærvø / Kellens linguistic camp: ~1200 BCE. Late traditional dating (~600 BCE) preserved by Greek sources is largely rejected by current philological scholarship. We carry -1200/-1000.
- **Historicity.** Mainstream: a single historical individual. Some scholars (Molé, Kellens at times) have problematized the single-historical-author position; the personal voice of the hymns remains decisive for most.
- **Place.** Bactria / Sogdiana / Khwarezm — all plausible eastern Iranian locales.
