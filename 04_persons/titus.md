# Titus

## Identity
A Greek (uncircumcised) Gentile companion of Paul, taken to the **Jerusalem conference** (Gal 2:1–5) as a deliberate test case of the Gentile-conversion question. Paul records pointedly that Titus was *not* compelled to be circumcised — making him a decisive piece of evidence in the Pauline corpus for the Gentile mission's terms. Later he is Paul's troubleshooter and emissary, especially to Corinth during the painful conflict reflected in 2 Corinthians.

## Historical evidence
Paul's letters give the principal attestation. The puzzling **silence of Acts** about Titus (who never appears by name) is a longstanding scholarly crux — explanations range from Luke's editorial choices to manuscript transmission to underlying source-critical issues.

## Works
None genuine. The Epistle to Titus is one of the three Pastoral Epistles (mainstream critical: early-2nd-c. pseudonymous compositions promoting nascent episcopal-presbyteral order).

## Role in the tradition
**Bishop of Crete** per the Pastoral Epistle and patristic tradition; a major saint in the Cretan and broader Orthodox tradition. **Historical Paul studies**: a key test-case figure for the Gentile-mission origin.

## Disputes
- **Acts silence.** Major scholarly puzzle.
- **Pastoral authorship.** As with 1–2 Timothy, pseudonymous in mainstream critical view.
