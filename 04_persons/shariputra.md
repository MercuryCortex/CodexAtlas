# Śāriputra

## Identity
Śāriputra (Pali Sāriputta; personal name Upatissa, born to a brahmin family near Nāḷandā ~-490) is the principal "right-hand disciple" of the Buddha — paired with his lifelong friend Mahā-Moggallāna (Maudgalyāyana) as "the chief disciples." Where Moggallāna is foremost in psychic-power (*iddhi*), Śāriputra is foremost in wisdom (*paññā*) and is given the formal title *Dharma-sēnāpati* ("General of the Dharma") — the chief expositor and systematizer of the Buddha's teaching. He is the most-named individual interlocutor in the early canon after Ānanda, and his discourses (especially the *Sāriputta-saṃyutta* SN 28) treat the systematic doctrinal questions that became the Abhidhamma. He pre-deceased the Buddha by ~6 months. His relics were enshrined in a stūpa at Nāḷandā recovered by Cunningham 1851 and now in the V&A London.

## Historical evidence
Śāriputra's historicity is robustly multi-attested across Pali, Sanskrit Sarvāstivāda, Chinese Āgama, and Mahāyāna sources. The biographical narrative (conversion via meeting Aśvajit and hearing the *ye dhammā* verse; lifelong friendship with Moggallāna; pre-decease of the Buddha at his birth-village; relic-stūpa) is consistent across recensions to a degree that suggests substantial historical core. Cunningham's 1851 archaeological recovery of a relic-stūpa at Sāriputta's natal village with inscribed reliquaries naming Sāriputta and Moggallāna is the most concrete material attestation of any named Buddhist figure besides the Buddha himself.

## Works
- **Theravāda attribution**: the first two books of the Abhidhamma-piṭaka (*Dhammasaṅgaṇī*, *Vibhaṅga*) are by tradition transmitted through Śāriputra; the broader Abhidhamma project is associated with his systematizing intelligence.
- **Critical assessment**: modern scholarship does not regard the Abhidhamma-piṭaka as composed by Śāriputra in any literal sense — it is a post-Buddha systematization (~-300 to -100). Śāriputra's *role* is that of the canonical figurehead of analytic-systematic Buddhism.
- **Theragāthā verses**: 37 verses (981-1017) in the *Theragāthā* are attributed to him.

## Role in the tradition
- **In Theravāda**: Śāriputra is the patron of Abhidhamma and the model of the wisdom-disciple. The Pali commentarial literature treats him as the second authority after the Buddha for points of doctrine. The Nāḷandā monastic university (founded ~5th c. CE) is traditionally located at his birth-village.
- **In Mahāyāna**: Śāriputra is the canonical interlocutor of the Buddha in many Mahāyāna sūtras — but he is also frequently the **foil** to the new Mahāyāna teaching. In the Lotus Sūtra ch. 3, the Buddha tells Śāriputra the parable of the burning house and the three vehicles, framing the bodhisattva-vehicle as the One True Vehicle of which the earlier Buddhist teaching (Śāriputra's vehicle, the *śrāvakayāna*) is a skilful-means precursor. The same pattern recurs in the Vimalakīrti-nirdeśa (where Śāriputra is mildly mocked by the lay-bodhisattva Vimalakīrti) and the *Heart Sūtra* (where Avalokiteśvara, the Mahāyāna bodhisattva of compassion, *teaches Śāriputra* the prajñāpāramitā doctrine — the role-reversal is deliberate: the senior śrāvaka now receives instruction from the bodhisattva).
- **In Chan/Zen**: Śāriputra appears in koan collections as a foil to the bodhisattvic spontaneity (e.g., the goddess-and-Śāriputra exchange of Vimalakīrti ch. 7 is a standard reference).

## Disputes
- **Authorship of the Abhidhamma**: as noted, the canonical attribution to Śāriputra is traditional but not historically supported. The Abhidhamma is a post-Buddha systematization (Frauwallner 1995).
- **Cause of death**: traditional accounts say he died of natural illness (chronic dysentery) at his birth-village. Some Mahāyāna sources elaborate; the Pali account is sober.
- **Mahāyāna polemical use**: how to read the Mahāyāna sūtras' use of Śāriputra as foil? Williams 2008 reads this as a deliberate Mahāyāna rhetorical strategy to claim the Buddha's authority for new doctrines: by having the Buddha *correct* the senior śrāvaka, the new teaching is positioned above the early-Buddhist canon without abandoning the framing-figure of the Buddha.
