# Suhrawardi

## Identity
Shihāb al-Dīn Yaḥyā al-Suhrawardī (1154–1191) — known posthumously as *al-Maqtūl* (the Executed One) and as *Shaykh al-Ishrāq* (the Master of Illumination) — founded the Illuminationist (*Ishrāqī*) school of Islamic philosophy. Born Suhraward in northwestern Iran; studied at Marāgheh under Majd al-Dīn al-Jīlī (also Fakhr al-Dīn al-Rāzī's teacher); traveled in Iran, Anatolia, and Syria; settled in Aleppo under the patronage of Saladin's son al-Malik al-Ẓāhir. Executed in 1191 at the order of Saladin himself, transmitted through the Aleppo governor, after Sunni ulama brought heresy charges. Cause of execution variously reported: claims to direct revelation; metaphysical doctrines too close to *waḥdat al-wujūd*; or political risk of his influence at a Sunni court that needed to display orthodoxy.

## Historical evidence
Documented through his own works (substantial autobiographical material in the visionary recitals); contemporary references in Ayyubid sources; later biographical literature.

## Works
*Ḥikmat al-Ishrāq* (Philosophy of Illumination, 1186 — the principal systematic work); three earlier Peripatetic-style systematic works (*al-Talwīḥāt*, *al-Muqāwamāt*, *al-Mashāriʿ wa-l-Muṭāraḥāt*); Persian visionary recitals (*Tale of Western Exile*, *On the State of Childhood*, etc.); shorter Arabic and Persian treatises.

## Role in the tradition
Founder of Illuminationist philosophy — the second great post-classical Islamic philosophical tradition after Avicennan Peripateticism. His school flourished in Iranian, Anatolian, and Indian contexts. Mullā Ṣadrā (Phase 6) synthesizes Suhrawardī with Avicenna and Ibn ʿArabī to produce the dominant late-Safavid Iranian philosophy. Corbin (1903–1978) made Suhrawardī central to Western reception of "spiritual Iran."
