# Joseph Campbell

## Identity
American comparativist (1904–1987); Sarah Lawrence College faculty 1934–1972; popular-philosophical popularizer of comparative mythology. His *Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949) developed the "monomyth" / "hero's journey" model that became influential in film, literature, and popular spirituality. The 1988 PBS series *The Power of Myth* with Bill Moyers reached an audience of tens of millions.

## Works
*Hero with a Thousand Faces* (1949), *The Masks of God* (4 vols 1959–1968), *Myths to Live By* (1972), *Power of Myth* (1988).

## Role in the tradition
The most-read popularizer of comparative mythology in 20th-century America. The Jungian-Frazerian-Eliadean comparative method reached mass audiences largely through Campbell. The monomyth's influence on Hollywood (especially George Lucas's *Star Wars*) is well documented.

## Disputes
- **Antisemitism allegations.** Maurice Friedman, Brendan Gill, and others have reported personal antisemitic statements by Campbell; the New York Review of Books debate (1989) is the principal documented exchange. Defenders contest interpretation.
- **Monomyth as universal.** Critics argue Campbell forced disparate narratives into a single template; his selection of materials is criticized as Eurocentric and gendered.
