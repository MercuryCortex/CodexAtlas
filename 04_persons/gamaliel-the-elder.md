# Gamaliel the Elder

## Identity
Grandson of [[hillel-the-elder]] (per the rabbinic dynastic genealogy), Pharisaic leader at Jerusalem in the generation before the destruction of the Temple. **Acts 5:34–39** presents him as a moderate Sanhedrin voice counseling tolerance toward the early Jesus-movement; **Acts 22:3** has Paul claim that he "was brought up... at the feet of Gamaliel, educated strictly according to our ancestral law."

## Historical evidence
Multiple-source attestation (rabbinic + Acts), which is unusually strong for a 1st-c. Pharisee. Rabbinic literature treats him as a major authority and successor of Hillel's leadership. Mishnah Sotah 9:15 famously laments his death: "When Rabban Gamaliel the Elder died, the glory of the Torah ceased and purity and abstinence died."

## Works
None. Sayings and rulings preserved in Tannaitic corpus.

## Role in the tradition
- **Hillelite Pharisaic succession**: the grandfather-to-grandson line Hillel → Shimon → Gamaliel I → Shimon ben Gamaliel I → Gamaliel II establishes the patriarchal dynasty.
- **Acts portrayal**: as the moderate Pharisaic voice who saved the early Christian movement from a more aggressive Sanhedrin response — used by Christian apologists for the *natural-affinity* of Pharisaic Judaism with Christianity.
- **Paul's teacher claim**: Acts 22:3 — historicity debated. Paul's own letters never name Gamaliel; Fredriksen 2017 and others doubt the Lukan claim; conservative scholarship accepts it. If true, it places Paul within a moderate Pharisaic intellectual lineage.

## Disputes
- **Paul = Gamaliel's student**: Acts-only claim; not corroborated by Paul; mainstream critical doubt.
- **Acts 5 speech historicity**: shaped by Lukan theology; the *type* of moderate-Pharisaic position is historically plausible.
- **Identification with later rabbinic authorities**: Gamaliel I vs. Gamaliel II is the main distinction; the rabbinic tradition is sometimes ambiguous.
