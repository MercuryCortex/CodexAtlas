# Zhuangzi (Zhuang Zhou)

## Identity
Zhuang Zhou, conventionally known as Zhuangzi ("Master Zhuang"), was a Daoist philosopher of the late Warring States period, traditionally dated ~-369 to -286. He is the author of the Inner Chapters (1-7) of the eponymous *Zhuangzi*, and the literary inspiration of the rest of the school's accreted Outer and Miscellaneous Chapters. Where Laozi (if he existed) is gnomic, brief, and political-oracular, Zhuangzi is rhapsodic, narrative, and personally vivid — the great anti-philosopher of philosophical Daoism, the master of paradox and the butterfly dream.

## Historical evidence
Sima Qian's *Shiji* ch. 63 (~-90 BCE) gives a brief biography: Zhuang Zhou was a native of Meng (in Song state, modern Henan), held office briefly as an official at the *qiyuan* (lacquer-tree grove) in Meng, was a contemporary of King Hui of Liang (r. -370 to -319) and King Xuan of Qi (r. -319 to -301), and declined an offer of a state ministership from King Wei of Chu. The biographical detail is thin but the historicity is generally accepted. The Inner Chapters themselves contain numerous anecdotes about Zhuang Zhou (the discussion of fish-pleasure with Hui Shi on the Hao bridge; the death of Zhuang Zhou's wife; the encounter with the skull on the road) that have the flavor of school-preserved teacher-lore.

## Works
- *Inner Chapters* (1-7) of the *Zhuangzi* — the only chapters confidently attributable to the historical Zhuangzi. Includes *Xiao Yao You* (Free and Easy Wandering), *Qi Wu Lun* (On the Equality of Things, the great relativistic-skeptical essay), *Yang Sheng Zhu* (Nourishing the Lord of Life, including Cook Ding), *Ren Jian Shi* (In the Human World), *De Chong Fu* (The Sign of Virtue Complete), *Da Zong Shi* (The Great and Venerable Teacher), and *Ying Di Wang* (Responding to Emperors and Kings).
- *Outer* and *Miscellaneous Chapters* — composite school work over the following 100-150 years (Primitivist, Yangist, Syncretist branches; Graham 1981's apparatus). Not by Zhuangzi himself but in his name.

## Role in the tradition
The other half of the classical Daoist canon, alongside the Daodejing. In Wei-Jin "Dark Learning" (*xuanxue*), Zhuangzi became the central text of literati metaphysics; in the Sinification of Buddhism (~2nd-6th c. CE), Zhuangzi's vocabulary provided the conceptual scaffolding for the early Chinese translation of Indian Buddhist concepts (*geyi* practice). Chan/Zen Buddhism's anti-textual, anti-conceptual rhetoric, its valorization of spontaneity and skill-stories, and its irreverent encounter-dialogues are deeply continuous with Zhuangzi. Tang and Song literati (Li Bai, Su Dongpo, Tao Yuanming) made Zhuangzi the patron sage of eccentric-recluse-poet self-image for the next millennium.

## Disputes
- **Historicity.** Generally accepted as historical; the Shiji biography, while brief, is consistent with the Inner Chapters' references to him and to his contemporaries (especially Hui Shi).
- **Inner Chapters vs. rest.** Graham 1981 is the foundational scholarly argument that the Inner Chapters are by Zhuangzi himself while the rest is school work. The position is now mainstream.
- **Dates.** Conventional ~-369 to -286. The precision is partly conjectural but the *period* is secure (late Warring States, contemporary with Mencius and slightly earlier than Xunzi).
- **Reading: mystic or skeptic?** Whether Zhuangzi is primarily a mystic of cosmic union with Dao or a skeptic dismantling fixed perspectives is a long-running interpretive debate. Graham emphasizes the skepticism; the broader tradition emphasizes the mysticism; the text itself contains both.
