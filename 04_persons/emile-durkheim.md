# Émile Durkheim

## Identity
Alsatian-Jewish French sociologist (1858–1917), founder of academic sociology in France. Professor at Bordeaux (1887), then Sorbonne (1902). Founder of the *Année sociologique* journal (1898) and its associated school (Mauss, Hertz, Hubert, Halbwachs). His *Elementary Forms of Religious Life* (1912) is the foundational text of sociology of religion.

## Historical evidence
Extensively documented through Sorbonne university records, his own correspondence, the *Année sociologique* archive, and Steven Lukes's 1973 standard biography.

## Works
- *Division of Labour in Society* (1893) — doctoral thesis; mechanical vs. organic solidarity.
- *Rules of Sociological Method* (1895) — methodological treatise establishing sociology as autonomous discipline.
- *Suicide* (1897) — first major statistical-empirical sociological monograph.
- *Elementary Forms* (1912) — the religion book; the last major work before his death.

## Role in the tradition
The most influential sociologist of religion of the 20th century alongside Max Weber. The sacred/profane dichotomy, the analysis of collective effervescence, and the "religion = society in transfigured form" reduction are foundational. His school's later work (Mauss's *The Gift*, 1925; Lévi-Strauss as a partial heir) extended the program.
