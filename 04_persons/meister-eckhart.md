# Meister Eckhart

## Identity
Eckhart von Hochheim (~1260–1328) was the most adventurous Latin-Christian mystical theologian of the high medieval period — a Dominican friar from Thuringia, twice Paris doctor (the "Master"), Provincial of the Dominicans in Germany 1303–1311, and from ~1314 the leading vernacular preacher of the Rhineland. His Latin academic *Opus Tripartitum* (incomplete) and his German *Predigten* (sermons) present a radically apophatic theology of the *Godhead* (*gotheit*) beyond God-Trinity, the *birth of the Son in the soul*, the uncreated *spark* (*Funke*) of the soul, and the operative discipline of *detachment* (*Abgeschiedenheit*). Heresy investigation opened by the Cologne Archbishop in 1326; Eckhart appealed to Avignon and died there in early 1328. Pope John XXII condemned 28 propositions in the bull *In agro dominico* (March 1329). The Dominican order formally affirmed his place in its tradition in 1992.

## Works
*Predigten* (~110 authentic German sermons + many attributed); *Opus Tripartitum* (Latin theological summa, incomplete); Latin Scripture commentaries (Genesis, Exodus, Wisdom, Sirach, John); *Quaestiones Parisienses*; the German *Reden der Unterweisung* (Counsels of Discernment); the *Defense* before the Cologne inquisitors.

## Role in the tradition
The vernacular Rhineland mystical school's principal figure; influenced [[johannes-tauler|Tauler]] and [[henry-suso|Suso]] (his Dominican juniors), the anonymous *Theologia Germanica*, Luther (who praised Tauler and the *Theologia*), Nicholas of Cusa, Hegel, Schopenhauer, Heidegger, and the 20th-c. Christian-Buddhist dialogue.

## Predecessors — the Beguine vernacular-mystical milieu
McGinn 1998 *Flowering of Mysticism* establishes that **Eckhart's vernacular mystical theology has substantial Beguine antecedents**, with the most striking conceptual continuity being his *Gottheit* / Godhead-language and the prior Beguine articulation:
- [[mechthild-of-magdeburg]] *Das fliessende Licht der Gottheit* (~1250–1280) — already uses *Gottheit* for the divine essence-beyond-distinction in vernacular Middle Low German.
- [[hadewijch-of-antwerp]] *minne*-mysticism — vernacular Middle Dutch mystical-erotic register.
- [[marguerite-porete]] *Mirror of Simple Souls* (~1290–1300, burned 1310) — *annihilation* of the soul into God, vernacular Old French; **Eckhart was in Paris 1311–1313, concurrently with the posthumous Vienne 1311–1312 condemnation of Porete's positions**; the conceptual continuity between Marguerite's *l'ame qui est Dieu par condition d'amour* and Eckhart's *Gottheit*-language is striking, and the geographic-temporal overlap is suggestive even though a direct-citation smoking gun is not yet established (Hollywood 1995, McGinn 2001 ch. 7).
