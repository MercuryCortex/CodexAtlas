# Amos

## Identity
Amos is the earliest of the named "writing prophets" of the Hebrew Bible. A Judahite from Tekoa (a small town in the Judean hill country south of Jerusalem), he describes himself as a shepherd / herdsman (Amos 7:14) and "dresser of sycamore trees" — a rural agricultural worker, not a professional prophet. He traveled north to the Israelite kingdom in the prosperous reign of Jeroboam II (~786–746 BCE) and prophesied at Bethel, the royal sanctuary, denouncing the social injustice of the northern elite, the empty cult, and predicting destruction. His confrontation with the priest Amaziah at Bethel (Amos 7:10–17) is the most vivid biographical scene.

## Historical evidence
The book of Amos itself is the primary evidence; the biographical details (Tekoa, sycamore-dressing, Bethel confrontation, reign of Jeroboam II) are internally coherent and consistent with the archaeological and historical reconstruction of the period (8th-c. Israelite prosperity preceding the Assyrian crisis).

## Works
The book of Amos — 9 chapters, prophetic oracles plus narrative/biographical sections — is the main artifact, edited and accreted over time by disciples and later scribes but with a substantial 8th-c. core.

## Role in the tradition
Inaugurates the "Day of YHWH" theology (Amos 5:18–20). His "let justice roll down like waters" (5:24) became iconic in later Jewish and Christian social ethics; famously invoked by Martin Luther King Jr. in 1963. Cited in the New Testament (Acts 7:42–43, 15:16–17).

## Disputes
- **Editorial layers.** The opening oracles against the nations (1:3–2:16) and some closing material show later editorial expansion.
- **Tekoa identification.** The Judahite Tekoa is standard; a hypothetical northern Tekoa has been argued but lacks evidence.
