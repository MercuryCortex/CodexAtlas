# Giordano Bruno

## Identity
Italian Dominican friar, Renaissance Hermetic philosopher, and martyr of free thought (1548–1600). Born at Nola near Naples; took Dominican orders at age 17; fled the order in 1576 after dropping religious images from his cell and being charged with heresy. Lived a peripatetic European life — Geneva (briefly Calvinist, then excommunicated), Toulouse, Paris, London (1583–1585, under French ambassador's protection; visited Oxford to mixed reception), Wittenberg, Helmstedt, Frankfurt — publishing along the way the six Italian dialogues of his London period (the *Cena*, *Spaccio*, *Cabala*, *De la Causa*, *De l'infinito*, *De gli Eroici Furori*) and the Latin philosophical and mnemonic works. Returned to Italy 1591 at a Venetian noble's invitation; was denounced, arrested by the Venetian Inquisition 1592, extradited to Rome 1593, imprisoned for seven years, refused to recant, and was burned at the Campo de' Fiori on 17 February 1600. Bruno's metaphysical pantheism, infinite cosmos, animated universe, denial of Trinitarian orthodoxy, and Hermetic-magical commitments make him the most radical figure of the Renaissance Hermetic current.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own substantial published corpus, the Roman Inquisition records (partly extant; Cardinal Caetani's principal volume is lost), the Venetian denunciations and trial records, contemporary references.

## Works
Italian London dialogues: *Cena de le Ceneri*, *De la Causa, Principio et Uno*, *De l'infinito universo et mondi*, *Spaccio de la bestia trionfante*, *Cabala del cavallo Pegaseo*, *De gli Eroici Furori* (1584–1585). Latin works on memory and magic: *De umbris idearum*, *Ars memoriae*, *De magia*, *De vinculis in genere*, *De compendiosa architectura*. The Frankfurt Latin trilogy: *De minimo*, *De monade*, *De immenso* (1591).

## Role in the tradition
Bruno is at once a central figure of Renaissance Hermeticism (Yates' magus reading), a serious early-modern philosopher who extended Copernicus to an infinite animated cosmos (Gatti's scientific reading), and a permanent symbol of free thought against ecclesiastical persecution (the modern Italian republican reading; the 1889 Campo de' Fiori monument). German Idealism's reception (Jacobi, Schelling, Hegel) made him the precursor of identity-philosophy and pantheism.

## Disputes
- **Hermetic magus vs. proto-scientist.** The Yates-Gatti debate.
- **Charges at execution.** Multiple — Antitrinitarianism, eternity of the world, infinite worlds, Hermetic claims, denial of the Eucharist; which were decisive is debated due to lost trial records.
