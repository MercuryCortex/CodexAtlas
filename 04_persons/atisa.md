# Atiśa Dīpaṃkara Śrījñāna

## Identity
Atiśa Dīpaṃkara Śrījñāna (~982-1054) was a Pāla-Bengali Buddhist scholar-monk who became the principal Indian master of the Tibetan **Phyi-dar** ("Later Diffusion") — the 11th-c. revival of Buddhism in Tibet after the collapse of imperial patronage following the assassination of King Langdarma (~841). At ~60 he accepted the invitation of King Yeshe Ö and his great-nephew Jangchub Ö of the western Tibetan kingdom of Guge, traveled to Tibet in 1042, and spent his final 13 years there teaching, translating, and reforming. His central manifesto *Bodhipathapradīpa* (*Lamp for the Path to Awakening*, written for Jangchub Ö) became the foundational text of the Tibetan *lamrim* ("stages of the path") literature — the integrated Mahāyāna-Vajrayāna soteriological progression structure that [[tsongkhapa]] would later expand into the great *Lamrim Chenmo*. Atiśa's principal disciple Dromtönpa (Brom-ston Rgyal-ba'i 'byung-gnas, 1004-1064) founded the Kadampa (*bKa'-gdams-pa*) school, which became the parent lineage of the later Gelug (*dGe-lugs-pa*) school of Tsongkhapa and the Dalai Lamas (see [[dalai-lama-14]]).

## Historical evidence
- Tibetan historians from the 12th-15th c. (Tāranātha, Bu-ston, Gö Lotsawa) provide extensive overlapping biographical accounts.
- His own works survive in Sanskrit (a few) and in Tibetan translation (the bulk).
- Indian Pāla-period evidence: the Bengal Buddhist universities of Vikramaśīla and Nālandā at this period are well-documented; Atiśa is mentioned in their teacher-lists. He served as the abbot (*upādhyāya*) of Vikramaśīla before his Tibet journey.
- His route to Tibet via Nepal is documented; the Kadampa lineage preserves a continuous transmission claim.

## Works
- ***Bodhipathapradīpa*** (~1042) — his Tibet manifesto: a 67-verse Mahāyāna soteriological summary integrating sūtra and tantra paths and articulating the three-tier *lamrim* structure (lesser / middling / great person motivations). The foundational text of all subsequent Tibetan *lamrim* literature.
- ***Bodhipathapradīpa-pañjikā*** — his own commentary on the above.
- ***Caryāsaṃgrahapradīpa*** — a short treatise on bodhisattva training.
- ***Madhyamaka-upadeśa*** — instruction on the Madhyamaka philosophical view.
- Numerous shorter Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna treatises, ritual manuals, sādhanas, and pith-instruction texts preserved in the Tibetan Tengyur.

## Role in the tradition
- **As the Phyi-dar founder-figure**: Atiśa stabilized the Tibetan revival by establishing (a) the priority of monastic discipline (Vinaya) as the foundation for tantric practice, (b) the integrated *lamrim* structure organizing the entire Buddhist path, and (c) the centrality of the bodhicitta motivation. He is widely credited with rescuing Tibetan Buddhism from the perceived antinomian-tantric excesses of the late Indian-into-Tibet transmission.
- **As founder of the Kadampa lineage**: through his principal disciple Dromtönpa, Atiśa's teaching becomes the Kadampa school's foundational charter. Kadampa was eventually absorbed into the Gelug school ([[tsongkhapa]], ~14th-15th c.), which carries forward the Atiśa lineage as its core.
- **In Tibetan religious memory**: Atiśa is one of the most-venerated figures of Tibetan Buddhism. His name in Tibetan, *Jo bo rje* ("the Lord Lord"), is used with maximum reverence. The Reting / Radreng monastery north of Lhasa, founded by Dromtönpa on Atiśa's instructions, was a principal Kadampa-Gelug center.
- **In comparative reception**: Atiśa's *Bodhipathapradīpa* is the canonical model of an integrated Mahāyāna-Vajrayāna manual. Tsongkhapa's *Lamrim Chenmo* (~1402) and the Dalai Lama's many modern *lamrim*-style works are direct descendants.
- **Tārā-cult introduction**: Atiśa's systematic Tārā practice and devotion was a major channel by which the Tibetan Tārā-cult (see [[tara]]) was institutionalized.

## Disputes
- **Date of arrival in Tibet**: most sources give 1042; some Tibetan chronologies give 1040 or 1041. Modern consensus: 1042.
- **Initial vs. extended stay**: he had agreed to a three-year stay; he ended up remaining 13 years and dying in Tibet (at Nyethang). The reasons (political instability in Bengal under Pāla collapse + persistence of Tibetan demand) are partly speculative.
- **Vajrayāna inheritance**: how "tantric" was Atiśa's own teaching? Davidson 2002 emphasizes the Vikramaśīla tantric milieu; some Tibetan-tradition presentations downplay this in favor of the lamrim-monastic image. Both are partially correct.
- **Slug**: canonical vault slug is `atisa` (no h), per the existing reference in [[tradition-vajrayana-buddhism]] and the canonical-slugs registry.
