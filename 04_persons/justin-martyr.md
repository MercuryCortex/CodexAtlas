# Justin Martyr

## Identity
Greek-speaking Christian philosopher and apologist (~+100/+165), born at Flavia Neapolis (Nablus) in Samaria, converted to Christianity ~+135 on the Palestinian coast (per *Dial.* 3–8), established a Christian philosophical school at Rome where he taught wearing the *tribōn* (philosopher's cloak), beheaded under [[marcus-aurelius-emperor|Marcus Aurelius]] (~+165) with six companions — his trial dialogue is preserved in the *Acts of Justin*.

## Historical evidence
Strong: three extant works (*1 Apology*, *2 Apology*, *Dialogue with Trypho*), the *Acts of Justin* trial-record, citation in [[tatian-the-syrian|Tatian]], [[irenaeus-of-lyon|Irenaeus]], Eusebius. Justin's own autobiographical narrative in *Dial.* 1–8 — describing his philosophical wandering through Stoicism, Aristotelianism, Pythagoreanism, and finally Platonism before encountering an aged Christian on the seashore — is one of the most influential conversion-narratives of antiquity.

## Works
- ***First Apology*** (~+155, addressed to Antoninus Pius): defense of Christianity as the true philosophy.
- ***Second Apology*** (~+155/+160): shorter follow-up.
- ***Dialogue with Trypho*** (~+160): an extended fictional debate with a Jewish interlocutor (Trypho) on the Christian reading of the Hebrew Bible.

## Role in the tradition
- **Founder of Christian Platonism**: the first major Christian writer to engage Greek philosophy positively. His *logos spermatikos* doctrine — the seed of the Logos present in all rational humans — makes Greek philosophy (especially Plato, Socrates, Heraclitus) a *partial revelation* preparatory to Christ. The Platonic-Christian synthesis runs through Justin → Clement → Origen → Augustine → all subsequent Western Christian theology.
- **The Logos transmission chain**: Justin is a critical node in the *Stoic Logos → Philo (Logos as instrument of God) → John 1:1 (Logos as pre-existent Christ) → Justin's Logos-spermatikos* tracing edge — one of the cleanest cross-tradition transmission chains in early Christianity.
- **Anti-Marcionite**: Justin's lost *Against Marcion* (referenced in *1 Apol.* 26) was the earliest substantive anti-Marcionite work.
- **Anti-Samaritan-heresiology**: as a Samaritan himself, Justin is the principal early source on [[simon-magus]] and his Samaritan followers.

## Disputes
- **Authenticity of the *Second Apology***: occasionally questioned but generally accepted; may be an appendix or postscript to the First.
- **Date of martyrdom**: ~+165 standard.
- **Original audience of the *Dialogue with Trypho***: addressed to Marcus Pompeius (a friend); the *Trypho* dialogue partner is sometimes identified with Rabbi Tarphon, but the identification is contested and largely literary.
