# Ur-Nammu

## Identity
Ur-Nammu (r. ~-2112 to -2095 BCE) was the founder of the **Third Dynasty of Ur** ("Ur III"), the last great Sumerian-language imperial state. He came to power in the wake of the Gutian period, the post-Akkadian dark age that followed the collapse of the Akkadian Empire of [[sargon-of-akkad]] and [[naram-sin]]. Either as governor under Utu-hengal of Uruk or independently, Ur-Nammu broke with Uruk, established his own dynasty at Ur, and over a 17-year reign restored centralized Sumerian rule across southern Mesopotamia. He is principally remembered for three accomplishments: (1) the **Code of Ur-Nammu**, the earliest preserved law collection (~150 years before Hammurabi), survived in Old Babylonian copies; (2) the systematic restoration and construction of major temples and ziggurats — most spectacularly the **Great Ziggurat of Ur** (Etemennigur), the best-preserved ziggurat in Mesopotamia; (3) the consolidation of the administrative bureaucratic state that his son [[shulgi]] would extend into the most extensively documented administrative regime of the ancient world.

## Historical evidence
Robust:
1. Hundreds of contemporary royal inscriptions on bricks, votive objects, and statues (Frayne RIME 3/2).
2. The Code of Ur-Nammu in Old Babylonian school copies — the prologue identifies him as author in royal voice.
3. The Ur-Nammu hymn cycle (Ur-Nammu A through F) — Sumerian royal hymns composed during or shortly after his reign.
4. Date formulae of his 17 regnal years.
5. The Ur III administrative archives (~120,000 tablets), beginning in his reign and exploding under Shulgi.
6. Archaeological remains — the Etemennigur ziggurat itself (excavated by Woolley) stands as the most visible physical legacy.

He is one of the best-documented kings of the entire third millennium.

## Works
- **The Code of Ur-Nammu** (~-2100): prologue + ~57 surviving laws (the original full collection was longer; preserved on Old Babylonian school copies, principally tablets from Nippur and Ur). The prologue rehearses Ur-Nammu's establishment of cosmic and social order; the laws treat marriage, slavery, property, bodily injury, and capital offenses. The law-form is the casuistic *šumma X — Y* structure (later canonized in the Code of Hammurabi).
- The **Ur-Nammu hymn cycle** — six royal hymns (Ur-Nammu A–F) including the long *Death of Ur-Nammu* (Ur-Nammu A), a striking first-person netherworld-descent narrative attributed to the dead king.
- Royal inscriptions: ziggurat-construction texts, votive dedications, boundary stones.

## Role in the tradition
Ur-Nammu is the founder-figure of the Sumerian *renaissance* — the last era of unambiguously Sumerian-language political dominance in Mesopotamia. His dynasty (Ur III) under his son Shulgi (r. -2094 to -2047) is the most administratively elaborated ancient state we know, the "best-documented century in pre-modern history" (Sallaberger). His Code of Ur-Nammu was canonized as the first in the chain of Mesopotamian law-collections (Ur-Nammu → Lipit-Ishtar → Eshnunna → Hammurabi) and is the principal foundation-stone of the comparative study of ancient law. The Great Ziggurat of Ur is the most reproduced image of Mesopotamian architecture.

## Disputes
- **Relationship to Utu-hengal of Uruk**. Whether Ur-Nammu was Utu-hengal's brother (the dominant view) or his governor or an independent claimant is debated. The Sumerian King List makes the dynastic transition Uruk-V → Ur-III but is uninformative on family relations.
- **Authorship of the Code**. Whether Ur-Nammu personally composed the Code, or whether his son Shulgi (who continued the legal-administrative project) was the actual promulgator with later attribution to the founder, is debated. Some scholars (Wilcke; Lieberman) have argued for Shulgi authorship; Roth 1995 retains the traditional Ur-Nammu attribution.
- **Manner of death**. The *Death of Ur-Nammu* hymn implies death in battle (against unspecified enemies), which would be unusual for a 17-year reign ending at advanced age. Whether the hymn is historical or stylized is unclear.
