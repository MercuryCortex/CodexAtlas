# Cyprian of Carthage

## Identity
Born ~+200 to a wealthy pagan Carthaginian family, classically educated in rhetoric and law, **converted to Christianity ~+246**, elected bishop of Carthage ~+248/+249, **martyred under [[valerian-emperor|Valerian]]'s second edict in 258**. Cyprian's eleven-year episcopate spans the Decian persecution (250–251) and the run-up to Valerian's persecution, with the consequence that his 65-letter corpus and treatises are **the principal documentary archive of 3rd-c. Western Christianity**.

## Historical evidence
Cyprian's own letter-corpus is the principal source — supplemented by Pontius's *Vita Cypriani* (~+260, the first datable Christian biography), the *Acta Proconsularia Cypriani* (the trial-record), and brief notices in Eusebius and Jerome.

## Works
- ***De ecclesiae unitate*** — foundational Western ecclesiology: outside the unified episcopal church there is no salvation; "he cannot have God as Father who does not have the church as mother" (*Unit.* 6).
- ***De lapsis*** — pastoral theology of the lapsed (those who sacrificed under Decius): rigorist but mercy-allowing readmission after appropriate penance, against both the schismatic-laxists (Felicissimus) and the rigorists (Novatian).
- ***De habitu virginum*** — on women's dress and the consecrated-virgin life.
- 65 letters — exceptionally rich documentation of Mediterranean Christian communication.

## Role in the tradition
- **Western ecclesiology**: Cyprian establishes the *catholica* / *non-catholica* boundary as constitutive of salvation. The phrase "salus extra ecclesiam non est" (no salvation outside the church) is Cyprianic.
- **Lapsi crisis**: his pastoral theology of restoring lapsed Christians (with penance and bishop's authority) shaped subsequent Western penitential practice.
- **Rebaptism dispute with Rome**: Cyprian held that those baptized by heretics required *re*baptism upon entering the church; **Pope Stephen I held the opposite** (one baptism, valid wherever administered). The dispute prefigured the later Donatist crisis. Stephen's position eventually prevailed in Western Catholic doctrine; Cyprian's prevailed in Eastern Orthodox.
- **Martyrdom**: Cyprian's *Acta Proconsularia* — preserving the trial dialogue with the proconsul Galerius Maximus — is among the most-circulated and -admired martyrologies of Latin Christianity.

## Disputes
- **Personal authorship of *De ecclesiae unitate***: two recensions exist; the difference centers on a passage about the Petrine primacy. The "primacy text" recension may be Cyprian's first draft, the alternative his revision (Bévenot 1961 reconstruction). Used in later Petrine-primacy controversies.
- **Rebaptism dispute resolution**: Cyprian and Stephen died before resolution; Pope Stephen's position is later canonical Western Catholic doctrine but was not so during the original dispute.
- **Conduct during the Decian persecution**: Cyprian's flight was criticized by some at the time; his self-defense is theologically elaborated in his letters.
