# Pope Clement V

## Identity
Bertrand de Got, born 1264 in Villandraut (Gascony, then English-ruled Aquitaine). Archbishop of Bordeaux from 1299. Elected pope at Perugia on 5 June 1305 after a year-long conclave deadlock, after which the cardinals selected him as a compromise candidate acceptable to King [[philip-iv-of-france|Philip IV]] of France. Crowned at Lyon (14 November 1305) rather than Rome — the **inauguration of the Avignon Papacy** (eventually fully relocated to Avignon in 1309). Reigned 1305–1314.

His pontificate is dominated by the **Templar suppression** ([[event-trial-of-templars-1307-1314]], [[event-council-of-vienne-1311-1312]]) and by his structural dependence on Philip IV. The traditional historical verdict — "weak pope who abandoned the Templars under royal pressure" — has been substantially complicated by Barbara Frale's 2001 rediscovery of the [[phase-5-038-chinon-parchment-1308|Chinon Parchment]], which shows that Clement *did* conditionally absolve [[jacques-de-molay|Molay]] and the senior Templar officers in 1308 but lacked the political weight to defend them publicly. Died 20 April 1314 — one month after Molay's burning. (The legendary "Molay curse" is anchored on this proximity.)

## Historical evidence
Strong contemporary documentation: papal registers, the *Processus Contra Templarios*, the [[phase-5-038-chinon-parchment-1308|Chinon Parchment]], chronicle attestation across Latin Christendom.

## Works
- The Templar-suppression bulls: *Pastoralis praeeminentiae* (1307), *Faciens misericordiam* (1308), *Vox in excelso* (1312), *Ad providam* (1312), *Considerantes dudum* (1312).
- The *Clementinae* — Clement's canon-law decrees, posthumously collected and promulgated by John XXII in 1317 as part of the *Corpus Iuris Canonici*.
- Numerous additional bulls and letters.

## Role in the tradition
- **First Avignon pope.** The relocation of papal residence to Avignon (formally 1309) inaugurates the **Avignon Papacy** (1309–1377), often called the *Babylonian Captivity* of the Church by hostile contemporaries (Petrarch coined the phrase). The structural dependence on the French crown initiated under Clement persists for seven decades.
- **Templar suppressor.** The single most-remembered act of his pontificate. Suppressed the Order by apostolic provision in 1312 despite the Council of Vienne's reluctance to convict.
- **Patron of universities.** Founded universities at Orléans (1306) and Perugia (1308).

## Connections
- **→ [[philip-iv-of-france]]** *structurally-dependent*: the formative political relationship of his pontificate.
- **→ [[jacques-de-molay]]** *trial-judge-and-absolver*: conditional absolution at Chinon 1308 (the Chinon Parchment); did not / could not save Molay from the secular-arm execution 1314.
- **→ [[tradition-knights-templar]]** *suppressor*: the institutional end of the Order.
- **→ [[event-trial-of-templars-1307-1314]]** + **→ [[event-council-of-vienne-1311-1312]]** *principal-pope-actor*.

## Disputes
- **Was Clement complicit or coerced?** The Chinon Parchment (Frale 2004) substantially complicates the older "complicit Clement" reading: he absolved the senior officers privately. But he did *not* defend them publicly, and he did *not* prevent the 1314 execution. The honest reading: structurally coerced, individually face-saving, ultimately complicit in the institutional suppression even if not in the heresy verdict.
- **Was the Avignon move *because of* French pressure?** Mixed — Clement initially planned to return to Italy after the 1309 settlement of Italian politics; the move to Avignon was meant to be temporary. The contingent political situation (continuous Italian instability, Avignon's papal-fief status making it not technically *in* France though surrounded by France) made the move sticky.
