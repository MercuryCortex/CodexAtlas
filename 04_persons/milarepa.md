# Milarepa

## Identity
Jetsun Milarepa (1052–1135) is the most beloved figure of Tibetan Buddhism — the yogi-poet whose biography (a young man corrupted into sorcery by maternal grief; vengeful murder of his uncle's family; repentance and apprenticeship to Marpa; arduous trials before instruction; solitary mountain practice in cold caves wearing only cotton; eventual realization and lifelong teaching through extemporaneous song) became the foundational narrative of the Kagyu school and one of the most-told life-stories in Tibetan culture. The standard biography (*Mi la rnam thar*) and the song-collection (*Mgur-bum*) were compiled by the eccentric tertön Tsangnyön Heruka (~1452–1507) — substantially later than Milarepa's life, drawing on earlier oral and partial-written traditions.

## Historical evidence
The historical Milarepa is well attested; specific biographical episodes vary across earlier and later biographical compilations. Tsangnyön Heruka's late-15th-c. compilation is the canonical version.

## Works
No systematic doctrinal works; his teaching is preserved entirely in the song-collection and in biographical anecdotes.

## Role in the tradition
Foundational for the Kagyu school; the most-recognized Tibetan religious figure after Padmasambhava; the Kagyu lineage transmission flows Tilopa → Naropa → Marpa (Milarepa's teacher) → Milarepa → Gampopa → the Four Major and Eight Minor Kagyu sub-schools.
