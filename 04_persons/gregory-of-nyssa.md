# Gregory of Nyssa

Youngest of the three great Cappadocian Fathers (with brother [[basil-of-caesarea]] and friend [[gregory-of-nazianzus]]). Older sister [[macrina-the-younger]] figures in his *Life of Macrina* and as "the Teacher" in *On the Soul and Resurrection*. Bishop of Nyssa from ~372. His theology is the most philosophically adventurous of the Cappadocians — Christian apophatic mysticism (the *Life of Moses* presents the soul's ascent into the divine darkness), an early statement of universal salvation (*apokatastasis*) in dialogue with Origen, and the sophisticated Trinitarian theology that secured Nicene orthodoxy at Constantinople in 381.

## Substrate connections
- His [[via-negativa]] / apophatic theology — Moses entering the cloud on Sinai as the soul's mystical ascent — is structurally Plotinian (apophasis of the One in *Enneads* V.3, VI.9). The substrate-Platonism reaches him through Origen, Philo of Alexandria, and direct reading of [[plotinus]]. Daniélou 1944, Mühlenberg 1966.
- His doctrine of *epektasis* (the soul's endless stretching into God) is a Christian Platonist development of Plotinian ascent doctrine, made distinctive by the Christian insistence on divine infinity.
- His *apokatastasis* (universal restoration) doctrine is Origenist; condemned (in Evagrian form) at Constantinople 553 but preserved through Gregory's authoritative status.
- His *Life of Moses* is generically modeled on [[philo-of-alexandria]]'s *Life of Moses* — Hellenistic-Jewish allegorical biography Christianized.
