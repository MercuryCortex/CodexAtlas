# Dinis I of Portugal (1261–1325)

## The man
Dinis I (*Dom Dinis*, 1261–1325) was the sixth king of Portugal — one of the most effective rulers in medieval Portuguese history and, oddly, also a gifted troubadour poet. His reign (1279–1325) saw the establishment of the University of Coimbra (1290, the first Portuguese university), the demarcation of Portugal's borders with Castile (Treaty of Alcanices, 1297), the planting of the Pinhal de Leiria (a vast pine forest to stabilize coastal dunes and provide shipbuilding timber — directly enabling the later caravels), and the institutional act that set the stage for the Age of Discoveries.

## The founding act: Order of Christ (1319)

When Pope Clement V dissolved the Knights Templar (*Vox in excelso*, 1312), every Iberian kingdom faced the same problem: what to do with the Templar properties, personnel, and wealth on their territory? The Crown of Aragon created the Order of Montesa (1317). **Dinis I of Portugal negotiated the creation of the Order of Christ** — receiving papal approval from Pope John XXII in the Bull *Ad ea ex quibus* (14 March 1319).

The Order of Christ was, functionally, the Portuguese Templars reconstituted:
- **Same personnel:** former Templar knights who had not been condemned were admitted directly
- **Same properties:** the Templar holdings in Portugal (including the magnificent Convento de Cristo at Tomar — the Templar headquarters) transferred to the new Order
- **Same headquarters:** Tomar (first at Castro Marim, then moved to Tomar)
- **New name, new mission:** the crusading mission was retained but now directed toward the African coast

This institutional continuity is why the Templar cross — the *Pátea* cross — appears on Portuguese caravel sails a century and a half later: **it is, literally, the same institution**.

## The documentary chain
The complete institutional transmission:
1. **[[event-council-of-troyes-1129]]** — Templars formally founded under papal authority
2. **[[event-trial-of-templars-1307-1314]]** — Suppression
3. **[[event-order-of-christ-foundation-1319]]** — Dinis founds Order of Christ
4. **[[henry-the-navigator]]** — Grand Master of the Order of Christ (1420), funds exploration with Order revenues
5. **Vasco da Gama's caravel sails** — carry the cross of Christ (the Order's symbol) to India

Dinis I is the **pivot point** — the person through whom Templar institutional survival became Discoveries institutional capacity. Without Dinis's negotiation in 1319, there is no Order of Christ; without the Order of Christ, there is no institutional funding mechanism for Henry's exploration program; without Henry, there is no Vasco da Gama; without Vasco da Gama, there is no [[phase-6-041-camoes-os-lusiadas]].

He is the least-known person in one of the most consequential institutional chains in European history.
