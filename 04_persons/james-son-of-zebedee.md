# James, son of Zebedee

## Identity
James (Yaakov), elder son of the Galilean fisherman Zebedee and brother of [[john-evangelist]] (in traditional identification), one of the inner three of Jesus's disciples (with [[peter-apostle]] and John) — present at the raising of Jairus's daughter, the Transfiguration, and Gethsemane. Surnamed by Jesus *Boanerges* ("Sons of Thunder"). The first apostle martyred: beheaded by Herod Agrippa I in Jerusalem ~+44 (Acts 12:1–2). Conventionally distinguished as **James the Greater** from [[james-brother-of-jesus]] (James the Just) and James son of Alphaeus.

## Historical evidence
Strong Gospel attestation across all four; Acts gives the death notice with date-anchor (Agrippa I's reign, ~+41/+44 per Josephus). Among the most securely datable martyrdoms of any 1st-century Christian figure — Agrippa's death (Acts 12:21–23; Josephus *Ant.* 19.343–352) provides the *terminus ad quem*.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
Patron of Spain (the *Santiago de Compostela* tradition, originating in the 9th century on the basis of a legendary translation of his relics — historically unsupported but liturgically and pilgrimage-wise enormous). In early Christianity his early death gave him symbolic importance as the proto-martyr-apostle, paired with [[stephen-protomartyr]] (first non-apostolic martyr).

## Disputes
- **Spanish mission.** The legend that James preached in Spain and was buried at Compostela is medieval (9th c. onward), with no early attestation; it is regarded by all critical scholarship as devotional rather than historical.
- **Brother of John identification.** That the Zebedee brothers were James and John, and that this John is the Evangelist, is traditional but the second identification is heavily disputed in modern critical scholarship (see [[john-son-of-zebedee]]).
