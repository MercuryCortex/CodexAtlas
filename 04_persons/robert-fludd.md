# Robert Fludd

## Identity
English physician, Hermetic-Kabbalist-Paracelsian philosopher, and principal English Rosicrucian apologist (1574–1637). Educated at St John's College, Oxford; studied medicine on the continent (1598–1604) where he absorbed the Paracelsian and Hermetic-Kabbalist currents; Fellow of the Royal College of Physicians from 1609; maintained a successful London medical practice. From 1616 onward he wrote a major series of Latin works defending Rosicrucianism, articulating a systematic Hermetic-Paracelsian cosmology in the *Utriusque Cosmi Historia* (1617–1621), and engaging in celebrated controversies with Marin Mersenne, Pierre Gassendi, and Johannes Kepler. The de Bry engravings illustrating his works — the chain of being, the celestial monochord, the Tetragrammaton at the cosmic source — are among the iconic images of late-Renaissance esoteric cosmology.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own substantial published corpus, Royal College of Physicians records, the printed Mersenne-Gassendi-Kepler controversies, his medical case notebooks.

## Works
*Utriusque Cosmi Historia* (Macrocosm 1617–1618; Microcosm 1619–1621; continuations 1626, 1629, 1633). *Apologia* (1616) and *Tractatus Apologeticus* (1617). *Anatomiae Amphitheatrum* (1623). *Sophia cum Moria Certamen* (1626). *Philosophia Moysaica* (1638, posthumous).

## Role in the tradition
Fludd is the principal English representative of the Hermetic-Kabbalist-Paracelsian synthesis on the eve of the Scientific Revolution. The Fludd-Mersenne controversy 1623–1630 is one of the canonical debates between Renaissance Hermetic-magical natural philosophy and the emerging mechanical philosophy.
