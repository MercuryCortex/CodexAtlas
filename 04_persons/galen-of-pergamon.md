# Galen of Pergamon

## Identity
The most prolific and influential physician of antiquity — court physician under Marcus Aurelius, Commodus, and Septimius Severus, philosophically Platonist-Aristotelian-eclectic, author of an enormous medical-philosophical corpus that defined the medieval medical curriculum from Baghdad to Salerno. Among his ~150 surviving works there are **brief but historically valuable references to Christians (and Jews)** — making him one of the few learned-pagan witnesses of the late 2nd / early 3rd c.

## Historical evidence
Walzer 1949 collected the Galenic references to Christianity. The principal passages (some preserved only in Arabic translation, since their Greek originals are lost) include:
- A comment that "the followers of Moses and of Christ" assume scriptural authority rather than philosophical demonstration — but that the Christian moral life is nonetheless admirable;
- A passage praising Christian *self-discipline*: "in their contempt for death and abstention from sexual relations... and earnestness in self-control they have attained a level not inferior to that of true philosophers";
- A passage critical of Christian (and Jewish and Mosaic) reliance on faith rather than reason.

## Works
~400 works composed, ~150 survive in Greek or Arabic.

## Role in the tradition
- **Sympathetic learned-pagan testimony to Christian *moral practice*** — distinct from Celsus's hostile philosophical-systematic attack and from Pliny/Tacitus/Suetonius's administrative-procedural notices. Galen judged Christian ethics from the standpoint of philosophical asceticism and found them admirable.
- **Medical-philosophical critique of faith-without-proof**: Galen's complaint that Christians and Jews demand assent without demonstration is a *philosophical*-not-cultural critique, prefiguring later rationalist objections.
- **Medieval transmission**: Galen's reputation made these passages widely read in the medieval Arabic and Latin medical-philosophical tradition.

## Disputes
- **Authenticity of the Arabic-preserved passages**: largely accepted but textual verification is ongoing.
- **Galen's exact theological views**: he was personally philosophically theist (the *Demiurge* of Plato's *Timaeus* was his theology) but a sharp critic of biblical literalism.
