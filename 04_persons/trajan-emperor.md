# Trajan

## Identity
Second of the "Five Good Emperors" (98–117), military-emperor of Spanish (Italica) origin, conqueror of Dacia and Parthia, generally regarded by both ancient and modern observers as among the best Roman emperors. His significance for Christianity rests on his **brief rescript to Pliny the Younger** (Ep. 10.97) setting the legal procedure for handling Christians — a procedure that remained substantially in effect for ~140 years until the Decian universal-sacrifice edict of 250.

## Historical evidence
The Pliny-Trajan correspondence (*Ep.* 10) is intact and uncontested. **Trajan's reply**:

> "You have followed the right course, my dear Secundus, in investigating the cases of those who were brought before you as Christians. For no general rule can be laid down. They are not to be sought out; if they are accused and convicted, they must be punished, but with this reservation — that he who denies that he is a Christian and proves this by his act (that is, by adoring our gods) shall obtain pardon for his repentance, however suspect his past may be. Anonymous accusations should have no place in a criminal case; they are a very bad precedent and unworthy of our times."

## Works
Imperial correspondence (preserved in Pliny *Ep.* 10), edicts (variously preserved). Nothing of his own composition apart from documentary.

## Role in the tradition
- **Trajan's rescript**: foundational legal-procedural framework for Christian persecution: (a) not to be sought out; (b) if denounced and convicted, punished; (c) sacrifice to the gods constitutes proof of non-Christianity; (d) anonymous denunciations excluded.
- **The "name itself" question**: Trajan does not say whether being a Christian is itself the crime, leaving the legal logic incoherent — a feature [[tertullian]] later exploits brilliantly (*Apol.* 2.6–8): why investigate and convict if there is no specific crime, but on the other hand not seek out — incoherence diagnostic of the regime's discomfort.
- **Ignatius's martyrdom**: traditionally under Trajan ~+108.
- **Medieval legend**: Trajan's posthumous salvation through the intercession of Gregory the Great — a touchstone of medieval theological speculation on virtuous-pagan salvation.

## Disputes
- **Date of Pliny's tenure**: ~+111/+113 mainstream.
- **Scope of Trajan's policy**: applied empire-wide or only to Pliny's province? Mainstream view: the rescript was treated as binding precedent.
