# Baruch Spinoza

## Identity

Baruch (Benedict) Spinoza (1632–1677) was a Dutch philosopher of Portuguese-Sephardic background, the most rigorous early-modern monist, and one of the principal architects of post-Cartesian European philosophy. Born in Amsterdam to a Portuguese-Jewish merchant family that had fled the Iberian Inquisition; educated in the Amsterdam Talmud Torah school and (later) in Latin and contemporary philosophy with the dissenting Latinist Franciscus van den Enden. Excommunicated from the Amsterdam Sephardic community by formal *cherem* (decree of *herem*, religious ban) on 27 July 1656, at age 23 — the harshest such decree the community ever issued, the exact theological reasons for which remain debated. Adopted the Latin name *Benedictus* (translation of Hebrew *Baruch*); supported himself as a lens-grinder for optical instruments while writing philosophy; lived modestly in successive Dutch towns (Amsterdam, Rijnsburg, Voorburg, The Hague). Died of a lung disease (probably exacerbated by glass-dust from lens-grinding) in The Hague 21 February 1677, age 44.

His *Tractatus Theologico-Politicus* (1670), published anonymously and immediately banned and put on the Index, argued for freedom of philosophical inquiry, separation of philosophy from theology, naturalistic interpretation of biblical miracles, and historical-critical reading of scripture — a founding document of biblical criticism. His *Ethica* (composed during the 1660s and early 1670s, published posthumously 1677) presents in geometric (Euclidean) form a metaphysics of *one substance with infinite attributes* (*deus sive natura* — "God or Nature"), a parallelist solution to the mind-body problem, a deterministic account of human action, and an ethics culminating in the *intellectual love of God* (*amor Dei intellectualis*).

## Historical Evidence

Densely documented. The Amsterdam Portuguese-Jewish community archive preserves the *cherem* decree. Spinoza's own letters (~88 surviving); contemporaneous correspondence by Leibniz (who visited him November 1676), Henry Oldenburg, Christian Huygens; early biographies by Colerus (1705) and Lucas (~1719); civic records from Amsterdam and The Hague.

## Works

- *Renati Descartes Principiorum Philosophiae Pars I et II* (1663) — the only work published under his own name.
- *Tractatus Theologico-Politicus* (1670, anonymously) — political-theological-biblical-critical treatise.
- *Ethica* (composed 1660s–1670s, posthumous 1677) — the principal metaphysical work.
- *Tractatus Politicus* (unfinished, posthumous 1677).
- *Opera Posthuma* (1677) — posthumous collection edited by friends.

Standard critical text: Gebhardt 1925 edition. Standard English translation: Curley's *Collected Works* (Princeton 1985–2016).

## Role in the Tradition

Spinoza's substance-monism provoked the 18th-century *Pantheismusstreit* (Jacobi vs. Mendelssohn 1785–86) that inaugurated the Romantic-Idealist movements (Goethe, Schelling, Hegel). His biblical hermeneutics in the *TTP* founded modern historical-critical Bible scholarship. His political theory of free speech and religious tolerance shaped Enlightenment liberalism. His geometric ethics provoked sustained engagement from Leibniz, Hume, Kant, Schopenhauer, Nietzsche, and (20th century) Deleuze.

## MASSIVE WIN — The 2,500-Year Chain: Pythagoras → Spinoza → Einstein

The most consequential long-range connection in the vault's mathematics cluster:

**Pythagoras** (c. 530 BCE): Number is the arche of all things; the cosmos is mathematical harmony; mathematics IS reality.

**Plato** (c. 390 BCE): The Forms are mathematical; the Good is the principle of the Forms. The universe is constructed by the Demiurge according to mathematical ratios.

**Plotinus** (c. 254 CE): The One generates all being through emanation; mathematics is the structure of Intellect (the second hypostasis). The rational soul participates in mathematical order as return to the One.

**Spinoza** (1677): There is one substance. That substance is God. That substance is Nature. Its mathematical attributes are directly knowable by reason. The *Ethica* proves this in Euclidean form. *Amor Dei intellectualis* (the intellectual love of God) is the highest human good — achieved through comprehending the mathematical necessity of all things.

**Einstein** (1929, letter to Rabbi Goldstein): "I believe in Spinoza's God, who reveals himself in the lawful harmony of the world, not in a God who concerns himself with the fate and actions of human beings."

**Einstein** (1931): "The most incomprehensible thing about the universe is that it is comprehensible."

This sentence — the most quoted statement in philosophy of physics — is Pythagorean sacred mathematics restated in 20th-century terms. Why should the universe be mathematically ordered? Why should human minds be capable of grasping that order? The Pythagoreans had an answer: because number is divine, and human souls are mathematical. Einstein had no answer — but the question is the same question.

The chain: **Pythagorean number-theology** → **Platonic Form-world** → **Neoplatonist emanation** → **Spinoza's Deus sive Natura** → **Einstein's cosmic religious feeling** → **the implicit theology of modern theoretical physics** (every physicist who invokes "mathematical beauty" as a criterion for physical theories is in this tradition).

## Disputes

- **Reasons for the *cherem* (1656).** The exact grounds are not stated in the decree; modern reconstruction: denial of personal afterlife, Mosaic authorship, election of Israel, transcendent personal God. Nadler 2018, Popkin 2004.
- **Pantheism vs. *deus sive natura*.** Whether best classed as pantheism or a more nuanced position requires precise interpretation of the substance-attribute-mode metaphysics. Most contemporary scholarship (Della Rocca, Garrett, Curley) reads Spinoza as strictly identifying God and Nature.
- **Connection to Jewish tradition.** Whether post-Jewish (Strauss, Yovel) or retaining substantial Jewish content (Wolfson, Melamed) is contested.
