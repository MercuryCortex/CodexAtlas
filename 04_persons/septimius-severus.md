# Septimius Severus

## Identity
Roman emperor 193–211 (the founder of the Severan dynasty), of Punic-African background from Lepcis Magna in Tripolitania. Married to Julia Domna, a Syrian. The *Historia Augusta* (*Severus* 17.1) reports a Severan rescript prohibiting conversion to Judaism or Christianity; the historicity of this decree is **contested by modern scholarship** — the HA is a notoriously unreliable late-4th-c. source. But the persecutions of 202–203 in Egypt and North Africa, including the famous **martyrdom of [[perpetua-and-felicity|Perpetua and Felicity]] at Carthage in 203**, are securely historical.

## Historical evidence
The *Passio Perpetuae* (~+203) — partly first-person diary, one of the earliest extant Christian autobiographical texts — anchors the Severan persecution in Carthage. Eusebius HE 6.1–6 records the martyrdom of Origen's father Leonides at Alexandria and the cessation of catechetical instruction under the persecution. The general Severan rescript on conversion is doubted, but the *fact* of local persecution under Severus is not.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
- **Conversion-ban rescript** (historicity contested): if real, the first imperial measure specifically targeting *converts* rather than existing adherents.
- **North African persecutions** (203): produce the *Passio Perpetuae*, one of the most important early Christian texts — the first known Christian women's autobiographical writing, the visions of Perpetua in prison, the martyrdom narrative composed (per its prologue) "by us, your unworthy brothers" but framing Perpetua's diary as primary.
- **Origen's catechetical context**: Severus's reign breaks up the Alexandrian catechetical school under Clement, sending young Origen into prominence by ~+203.

## Disputes
- **HA *Severus* 17.1 rescript**: historicity contested; many scholars treat as 4th-c. fabrication.
- **Personal disposition**: Severus's reign included Christians and Jews in his court (Julia Domna was sympathetic to philosophical-religious eclecticism). Probably ad-hoc rather than systematic persecutor.
