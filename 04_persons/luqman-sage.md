# Luqmān

Quranic wisdom figure who gives his name to **Surah 31** (Luqmān). Unlike most Quranic figures, Luqmān is explicitly not a prophet — the Quran presents him as a man granted *ḥikma* (wisdom, understanding) by God directly (Surah 31:12). The surah preserves Luqmān's extended instruction to his son: monotheism, gratitude, prayer, patience, and social ethics (31:13–19).

## Quranic profile

- **Surah 31:12** — God grants Luqmān wisdom (*ḥikma*)
- **Surah 31:13** — Luqmān forbids associating partners with God (*shirk*) — "the greatest injustice"
- **Surah 31:14–19** — instruction to his son: parental duty, prayer, right conduct, forbearance, moderation in walking and speech

The father-to-son instruction genre (*Weisheitslehren*) is the oldest attested literary genre in the world, found in Egyptian Instructions (Ptahhotep ~2400 BCE), Mesopotamian wisdom texts, and the Hebrew wisdom literature (Proverbs 1–9). Luqmān's surah participates in this international wisdom genre.

## Identity debates

Islamic tradition contains extensive speculation about Luqmān's identity. Some traditions identify him with the Greek Aesop or with an Ethiopian / Nubian slave who received wisdom. Others identify him as a pre-Islamic Arabian wise man. The Quran itself provides no genealogy or ethnic origin. Wheeler 2002 (ch. 12) surveys these traditions. The scholarly consensus is that the Quran is engaging with a pre-Islamic Arabian wise-man tradition rather than a biblical figure.

## Wisdom-figure in Islam

Luqmān is exceptional in Quranic typology as a recipient of *ḥikma* who is neither a prophet nor a messenger. This places him in the same conceptual category as Solomon ([[solomon-king]]) — both receive divine wisdom as a distinct gift — but Luqmān's wisdom is explicitly private and ethical rather than royal and political.
