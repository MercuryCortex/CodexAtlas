# Giyorgis of Sagla

## Identity
**Giyorgis of Sagla** (1365–1425), also called *Abba Giyorgis II* in distinction from his earlier hagiographic namesake, is the greatest systematic theologian of medieval Ethiopian Christianity and the principal author of the [[phase-5-036-mashafa-mistir-giyorgis]] (*Book of the Mystery*) — a comprehensive defense of Tewahedo doctrine against ten named heretical positions, written in elaborate Ge'ez prose with extensive citation of patristic and Old/New Testament sources. Born in the Sagla region of Amhara, educated in the Hayq Estifanos / Debre Libanos monastic networks, Giyorgis served at the court of *negus* Dawit I and later Zara Yaqob's father Yeshaq I; he was briefly imprisoned for theological disputes but rehabilitated, and he died (traditional) 1425 at the monastery of Gasecca / Däbrä Bahrey.

## Historical evidence
- **The *Mashafa Mistir* itself** — survives in many Ge'ez manuscripts; critical-edited Yaqob Beyene 1990 (CSCO, 4 vols).
- **Multiple other authored works** — the *Saatat* (Book of Hours), *Arganona Maryam* (Marian poetry, *The Harp of Mary*), *Wəddase Mäsqäl* (*Praise of the Cross*), *Fəkkare Haymanot* — all in continuous Ethiopian liturgical use.
- **Royal-court documentation** — Giyorgis's service under Dawit I and Yeshaq I is documented in Solomonic chronicles.
- **Gadla Giyorgis** — Ge'ez hagiography, post-15th c. but with documentary contemporary substrate.

## Works
- **[[phase-5-036-mashafa-mistir-giyorgis]] — *Book of the Mystery*** (Ge'ez *Maṣḥafa Mesṭir*, ~1390s–early 1400s). 30 discourses systematically refuting ten heresies (named including Arian, Sabellian, Nestorian, Macedonian, etc.) and expounding Tewahedo doctrine. The most ambitious and learned single theological work of medieval Ethiopian literature; comparable in scope to Latin scholastic *summae* of the same period and to Eastern-Orthodox dogmatic florilegia.
- ***Arganona Maryam*** (*The Harp of Mary*) — Marian liturgical poetry, organized by the seven days of the week; in daily Ethiopian Orthodox monastic use.
- ***Saatat*** (Book of Hours).
- ***Wəddase Mäsqäl*** (*Praise of the Cross*).
- ***Fəkkare Haymanot*** (*Exposition of the Faith*).
- A *Gadla Sämaʿǝtat* (Acts of the Martyrs) collection is also sometimes attributed.

## Role in the tradition
Giyorgis is the most systematic theological mind Ethiopian Christianity produced in the medieval period. The *Mashafa Mistir* is *the* canonical statement of Tewahedo Christological orthodoxy from inside the tradition. His Marian devotional corpus (Arganona Maryam in particular) shaped the extraordinarily strong Marian piety of Ethiopian Orthodoxy — load-bearing for the Marian dimension of the tradition (33 Marian feasts annually; Saint Mary of Zion at Aksum as spiritual center). His authored texts are continuously preserved in Ge'ez monastic-liturgical use, making his corpus among the most-read of any single Ethiopian author across six centuries.

His position is *theologically* close to that of Zara Yaqob — the great 15th-c. emperor-theologian — and the two are sometimes treated together as the dual architects of the Solomonic-era systematization of Tewahedo doctrine; Giyorgis died (1425) before Zara Yaqob's reign began (1434), so the influence is one-directional, with Zara Yaqob building on Giyorgis's theological framework.

## Disputes
- **Exact authorship of all attributed works.** The *Mashafa Mistir* attribution is secure; some of the smaller liturgical pieces (e.g., the precise edition of the *Wəddase Mäsqäl*) are less so.
- **Date of birth.** Conventionally 1365; some sources give slightly earlier.
