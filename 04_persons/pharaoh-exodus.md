# Firʿawn (Pharaoh of the Exodus)

The Quranic Firʿawn (Arabic transcription of Hebrew *Parʿōh*) is the paradigmatic tyrant-king who oppressed the Israelites and opposed Moses (*Mūsā*). The Pharaoh narrative is the single most frequently repeated story in the Quran — occurring in some form in more than twenty surahs — making Firʿawn the Quran's central exemplar of political and theological tyranny.

## Quranic profile

The Quran significantly expands the Pharaoh figure beyond the Exodus account:

- **Surah 2:49–50** — Pharaoh oppresses Israel; Moses rescues them through the sea
- **Surah 7:103–141** — extended account of the plagues and confrontation
- **Surah 10:75–92** — Pharaoh's deathbed declaration of faith (too late): "I believe that there is no god except the one in whom the Children of Israel believe" — the Quran notes this faith came only when he saw destruction
- **Surah 20:24–79** — Moses's mission and the magic-vs-miracle confrontation
- **Surah 28:1–43** — includes the birth of Moses, his adoption by Pharaoh's household (his wife, in the Quranic version, pleads for the infant)
- **Surah 40:24–46** — the "Believer of Pharaoh's family" (*muʾmin āl Firʿawn*) — a secret believer in Pharaoh's court who argues for Moses

## Cross-tradition significance

Firʿawn appears in the Quran's Middle and Late Meccan surahs as the paradigmatic opponent of God's messenger, functioning as a rhetorical mirror for the Meccan polytheists' rejection of Muhammad. Reynolds 2018 documents how the Quranic Firʿawn narrative reworks the Exodus account, drawing on both the Hebrew Bible text and post-biblical Jewish midrashic elaborations.

The deathbed-faith episode (Surah 10:90–92) has no Pentateuchal parallel and represents Quranic theological expansion: even Pharaoh could have been saved, but his faith came too late — a lesson in the eschatological deadline.

## Asiya (wife of Pharaoh)

The Quran presents **Āsiya bint Muzāḥim**, wife of Pharaoh (not named but implied), as one of the four perfect women in Islamic tradition (Surah 66:11: "God has set forth an example for those who believe — the wife of Pharaoh, when she said: My Lord, build for me near You a house in Paradise..."). This represents a striking positive figure within the Pharaoh household.
