# Al-Shafi'i

## Identity
Muḥammad ibn Idrīs al-Shāfiʿī (767–820) was the architect of classical Sunni legal theory (*uṣūl al-fiqh*) and the founder of the Shāfiʿī school of jurisprudence — one of the four canonical Sunni *madhāhib*. Born Gaza; studied at Mecca, Medina (under Mālik ibn Anas), and Baghdad (under Muḥammad al-Shaybānī, Abū Ḥanīfa's leading student); spent his final years in Egypt where he died. His foundational contribution: the systematic theory of the four sources of Islamic law (Qur'an, Sunnah, *ijmāʿ* / consensus, *qiyās* / analogical reasoning) and the doctrine that authentic prophetic Sunnah (preserved in canonical hadith) is the second source of law, equivalent in authority to the Qur'an. This was a sharp innovation: pre-Shāfiʿī Iraqi jurisprudence had been more flexible about Sunnah-vs-rational-judgment, while Medinan jurisprudence had emphasized local customary practice.

## Works
*al-Risāla* (the foundational treatise on *uṣūl al-fiqh*, several editions and recensions); *Kitāb al-Umm* (legal compendium, ~7 vols).

## Role in the tradition
Foundational for Sunni Islamic law. The Shāfiʿī school is the dominant madhhab of Southeast Asia, East Africa, southern Egypt, and Kurdish areas. Al-Shāfiʿī's systematic theory of Sunnah was decisive in the canonization of hadith — al-Bukhārī, Muslim, and the other Six-Books compilers operated within the framework Shāfiʿī established.
