# Philo of Alexandria

## Identity
Philo of Alexandria (~-20 to ~+50 CE) was the leading Hellenistic Jewish philosopher of the early Roman period and the most consequential bridge between Greek philosophical theology and Jewish revealed religion. Born into one of Alexandria's wealthiest Jewish families, trained in the full Greek paideia (rhetoric, geometry, philosophy, music), and committed to Torah-observant Judaism, Philo produced ~50 surviving treatises that fuse Middle Platonist, Stoic, and Pythagorean philosophy with allegorical exegesis of the LXX Torah. His **Logos** — at once the cosmic Stoic Reason, the Platonic world of Forms, and the Hebrew creating Word — feeds directly into the Johannine prologue, patristic Trinitarian theology, and Christian allegorical biblical interpretation.

## Historical evidence
Strong documentation. His writings survive in substantial numbers via Christian transmission (Eusebius's preservation was decisive). External attestation: Josephus describes his leadership of the Jewish embassy to Caligula in +40 (*Antiquities* 18.259–260); his nephew Tiberius Julius Alexander is well-documented in Roman administrative records. His own *In Flaccum* and *Legatio ad Gaium* are eyewitness accounts of the Alexandrian Jewish-Greek conflicts of +38 and the embassy to Rome.

## Works
- The **Allegorical Commentary** (~20 treatises) — running allegorical exegesis of Genesis 2–17.
- The **Exposition of the Law** (~10 treatises) — more accessible treatment of Mosaic legislation.
- **Quaestiones in Genesim et Exodum** — catechetical Q&A; mostly Armenian.
- Two historical-political works (*In Flaccum*, *Legatio ad Gaium*).
- *On the Life of Moses* — biographical-theological.

## Role in the tradition
For Jewish tradition: forgotten for over a millennium; rediscovered in the 16th century; remains marginal in normative Judaism.

For Christian tradition: foundational. Eusebius called him "almost a Christian." Clement of Alexandria and Origen built their allegorical-Platonic theology on him. The Johannine Logos prologue is unimaginable without his groundwork. Ambrose's biblical commentary draws on him directly. Through these channels, Philo silently shaped Western Christian thought far more than his nominal status as a "Jewish philosopher" would suggest.

For Gnostic studies: the Philonic Logos-Sophia framework is the structural template that Gnostic systems (Sethian, Valentinian) inherit and *invert* — splitting Philo's harmonious High-God + Logos + Sophia + cosmos into a fallen-Sophia, ignorant-demiurge, redeemable-pneumatic-self schema.

## Disputes
- **Date of birth/death.** Approximations only; the embassy in +40 is the firmest fixed point.
- **Relationship to "common Judaism" of Palestine.** Older view (Wolfson): entirely Hellenistic, unaware of rabbinic categories. Recent (Niehoff, Birnbaum): more aware of and engaged with proto-rabbinic discussions than previously thought.
- **Direct influence on John's Gospel.** Hengel and Borgen: substantial. Boyarin and Dodd: shared Hellenistic-Jewish synagogue discourse rather than direct literary dependence.
