# Philo of Alexandria

## Identity
Philo of Alexandria (~-20 to ~+50 CE) was the leading Hellenistic Jewish philosopher of the early Roman period and the most consequential bridge between Greek philosophical theology and Jewish revealed religion. Born into one of Alexandria's wealthiest Jewish families, trained in the full Greek paideia (rhetoric, geometry, philosophy, music), and committed to Torah-observant Judaism, Philo produced ~50 surviving treatises that fuse Middle Platonist, Stoic, and Pythagorean philosophy with allegorical exegesis of the LXX Torah. His **Logos** — at once the cosmic Stoic Reason, the Platonic world of Forms, and the Hebrew creating Word — feeds directly into the Johannine prologue, patristic Trinitarian theology, and Christian allegorical biblical interpretation.

## Historical evidence
Strong documentation. His writings survive in substantial numbers via Christian transmission (Eusebius's preservation was decisive). External attestation: Josephus describes his leadership of the Jewish embassy to Caligula in +40 (*Antiquities* 18.259–260); his nephew Tiberius Julius Alexander is well-documented in Roman administrative records. His own *In Flaccum* and *Legatio ad Gaium* are eyewitness accounts of the Alexandrian Jewish-Greek conflicts of +38 and the embassy to Rome.

## Works
- The **Allegorical Commentary** (~20 treatises) — running allegorical exegesis of Genesis 2–17.
- The **Exposition of the Law** (~10 treatises) — more accessible treatment of Mosaic legislation.
- **Quaestiones in Genesim et Exodum** — catechetical Q&A; mostly Armenian.
- Two historical-political works (*In Flaccum*, *Legatio ad Gaium*).
- *On the Life of Moses* — biographical-theological.

## Role in the tradition
For Jewish tradition: forgotten for over a millennium; rediscovered in the 16th century; remains marginal in normative Judaism.

For Christian tradition: foundational. Eusebius called him "almost a Christian." Clement of Alexandria and Origen built their allegorical-Platonic theology on him. The Johannine Logos prologue is unimaginable without his groundwork. Ambrose's biblical commentary draws on him directly. Through these channels, Philo silently shaped Western Christian thought far more than his nominal status as a "Jewish philosopher" would suggest.

For Gnostic studies: the Philonic Logos-Sophia framework is the structural template that Gnostic systems (Sethian, Valentinian) inherit and *invert* — splitting Philo's harmonious High-God + Logos + Sophia + cosmos into a fallen-Sophia, ignorant-demiurge, redeemable-pneumatic-self schema.

## Disputes
- **Date of birth/death.** Approximations only; the embassy in +40 is the firmest fixed point.
- **Relationship to "common Judaism" of Palestine.** Older view (Wolfson): entirely Hellenistic, unaware of rabbinic categories. Recent (Niehoff, Birnbaum): more aware of and engaged with proto-rabbinic discussions than previously thought.
- **Direct influence on John's Gospel.** Hengel and Borgen: substantial. Boyarin and Dodd: shared Hellenistic-Jewish synagogue discourse rather than direct literary dependence.

## The Logos Architecture

Philo's Logos is a *composite* entity, assembled from at least four independent traditions:
1. **Stoic logos spermatikos** — the immanent rational principle pervading the cosmos
2. **Platonic intelligible realm** — the world of Forms as the blueprint of creation (Timaeus)
3. **Hebrew Bible's *dabar YHWH*** — the divine Word that creates in Genesis 1, that goes out and accomplishes things (Isaiah 55:11)
4. **Wisdom personification (*ḥokhmah/sophia*)** — the feminine cosmic Wisdom of Proverbs 8, Sirach 24, Wisdom of Solomon 7–9

Philo hypostasizes these into a single theological entity he calls:
- *Logos* (reason/word)
- *Prōtogonos huios* (first-born Son)
- *Deuteros theos* (second God)
- *Arkhiereus* (High Priest of the cosmos)
- *Eikon tou theou* (image of God)
- *Angelos* (messenger/mediator)

The function is invariant across all these names: the Logos is the *principle that makes transcendent God accessible to finite creation without requiring God to touch matter*. This is the theological problem of the encounter between infinity and finitude — Philo's solution is the Logos-mediator. 

**This is the direct theological precondition for the Johannine prologue**. When John 1:1 says "the Logos was with God and the Logos was God" — and then says the Logos "became flesh" — it deploys Philo's entire architecture and makes one additional, unprecedented claim: the Logos-mediator is *historically identifiable* as Jesus of Nazareth.

## The Christian Reception

Philo was forgotten by Jews after ~100 CE (he was not engaged by the Mishnah or Talmud) and preserved *entirely by Christians*. Eusebius of Caesarea (*Ecclesiastical History* 2.4–18, ~313 CE) quotes him extensively and calls him "almost a Christian," suggesting that the monastic communities of Alexandria he describes (*De Vita Contemplativa*) were actually the earliest Christians. This is historically dubious but reveals how naturally Philo's theology mapped onto early Christian self-understanding.

Direct Christian users of Philo:
- **Clement of Alexandria** (~150–215 CE): draws on Philo's allegorical method and Logos theology to synthesize Christianity with Greek philosophy. The *Stromata* is unimaginable without Philo.
- **Origen** (~185–254 CE): extends Philo's allegorical exegesis and Logos-mediation theology into the systematic Trinitarian framework that prefigures Nicaea.
- **Ambrose of Milan** (~340–397 CE): borrows so extensively from Philo (often without acknowledgment) that scholars have reconstructed lost Philonic texts from Ambrose.
- **The Nicene Creed (325 CE)**: "Begotten not made, of the same substance as the Father" — the *homoousios* formula is the Nicene resolution of exactly the theological tension Philo's Logos-mediator created.

## MASSIVE WIN: The Gnostic Inversion

Philo's Logos-theology is *harmonious*: the Logos mediates perfectly between a good God and a good creation. Gnostic systems take the same structural framework and *invert the valences*: what Philo calls the Logos/Demiurge is not a good mediator but an *ignorant creator* who mistakes himself for the highest God; what Philo calls material creation is not a good image of the divine but a *prison*. The Gnostic innovation is the inversion of Philo, not an independent discovery. See [[tradition-gnosticism]], [[sophia-gnostic]].
