# Cainan (son of Arpachshad)

## Identity
**Cainan (the second; son of Arpachshad)** is a post-Diluvian patriarchal figure who is present in the **Septuagint** Greek text of Genesis 10:24 and 11:12–13 (where he is son of Arpachshad and father of Shelah), in [[phase-3-009-jubilees]] 8:1–5 (with an extensive narrative), and in the New Testament Lucan genealogy of Jesus at **Luke 3:36** — but **completely absent from the Hebrew Masoretic Text** of Genesis (where the genealogy goes directly Arpachshad → Shelah, omitting Cainan). The figure is canonical scripture in [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo]] (which follows the LXX-tradition genealogy via both its Geʼez OT translation and its canonical retention of Jubilees) and in any Christian Bible that follows Luke's NT genealogy — meaning **all** mainstream Christian Bibles since they accept Luke 3 as canonical.

This makes Cainan one of the most **canonically-textually consequential** figures of the biblical tradition: he constitutes a clean documentary proof that:

1. The **Septuagint Greek text differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text** at the Genesis-genealogical level (not merely in vocalization or word-order but in the *number and names of patriarchs*).
2. The **New Testament (Luke specifically) followed the LXX-tradition canonical text, not the proto-Masoretic Hebrew** — Luke 3:36 includes Cainan, proving the evangelist used the LXX.
3. The **Ethiopian Bible's broader-canonical retention of Jubilees** (which gives Cainan an entire pre-Diluvian-civilization-rediscovery narrative — Jubilees 8:1–5) is theologically continuous with the LXX-Lucan retention rather than the Masoretic omission.

## Historical evidence
- **LXX Genesis 10:24; 11:12–13** — retains Cainan in both genealogies.
- **Hebrew MT Genesis 10:24; 11:12** — omits Cainan; Arpachshad begets Shelah directly.
- **Jubilees 8:1–5** — substantial narrative: Cainan as a learned man who **rediscovers pre-Diluvian inscribed knowledge from the Watchers** carved on stones — a striking Watchers-tradition link making Cainan a *transmitter of antediluvian forbidden knowledge into the post-Diluvian world* (the Jubilean narrative is theologically negative; Cainan is *not* praised for this).
- **Qumran 4Q252 (Genesis Commentary), 4Q156, 1QGenesis Apocryphon, 1QSamP** — Cainan present in several Qumran biblical witnesses; the LXX-tradition has Hebrew-Aramaic substrate at Qumran.
- **Luke 3:36 (NT)** — *"the son of Shelah, the son of Cainan, the son of Arpachshad, the son of Shem, the son of Noah..."* — Cainan retained in the genealogy of Jesus.

## Works
None. Jubilees attributes to him the *rediscovery* of pre-Diluvian-Watchers writing — a transmission-of-forbidden-knowledge act, not an authored composition.

## Role in the tradition
Cainan is theologically consequential out of proportion to his narrative prominence because his presence-vs-absence is a **clean documentary marker of the LXX-vs-MT divergence** in the post-exilic Jewish biblical-textual tradition. The patristic Christian tradition extensively noted the divergence (Jerome wrote about it; the Vulgate's relationship to the MT vs LXX is in part shaped by it) and the modern critical scholarship on the Septuagint (Hiebert 2003) treats Cainan as one of the key data points for the OG-vs-MT textual question.

In **Ethiopian Orthodox tradition** Cainan is straightforwardly part of the patriarchal genealogy — both via the LXX-derived Geʼez Old Testament and via the canonical-Ethiopian Jubilees' extensive Cainan-narrative. His role as *transmitter of Watchers-forbidden-knowledge* in Jubilees 8 connects him to the broader Ethiopian-canonical Watchers / 1 Enoch tradition.

The Jubilees-Cainan narrative is also one of the most striking *antediluvian-civilization* texts in canonical scripture — the trope that pre-Flood humanity had knowledge later partially recovered survives medieval and early-modern speculation (Pseudo-Berossus, Annius of Viterbo, John Dee) and reaches modern alternative-prehistory speculation (Hancock, ancient-aliens reception of Enochic material). The Ethiopian-canonical Cainan is the documentary substrate of this entire tradition.

## Disputes
- **Original LXX retention or LXX harmonization?** Whether Cainan was originally present in the Hebrew Vorlage of the LXX and dropped from the MT, OR was added to the LXX from a separate tradition. Modern scholarship (Hiebert 2003) generally accepts that Cainan was present in the pre-Masoretic Hebrew tradition and dropped from the proto-MT, with the LXX retaining the earlier reading.
- **Theological reception.** Jerome's Vulgate Latin Christian Bible retained Cainan in Luke 3:36 while omitting him in Genesis (following the Hebrew there) — creating an internal tension in the Latin Christian biblical text that some patristic and medieval commentators discussed.
- **Two Cainans.** The Hebrew Bible has *Cainan / Kenan son of Enosh* (Genesis 5:9, antediluvian, present in both MT and LXX) and the post-Diluvian Cainan-son-of-Arpachshad (LXX/Jubilees/Luke only). The two Cainans are distinct figures; this node is the post-Diluvian one.
