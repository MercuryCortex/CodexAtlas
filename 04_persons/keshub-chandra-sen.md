# Keshub Chandra Sen

## Identity
Bengali religious reformer and the most charismatic 19th-c. Brahmo Samaj leader after [[ram-mohan-roy]] (1838–1884). Born to a Vaidya (medical-caste) Vaishnava family in Calcutta; joined the Brahmo Samaj under Debendranath Tagore (Ram Mohan Roy's successor) in 1857; emerged as the movement's most powerful orator; broke with Debendranath in 1866 over the question of caste (Sen abolished the sacred thread for Brahmo Samaj ministers; Debendranath was more conservative); founded the *Brahmo Samaj of India* (the radical branch). His own break in 1878 — over the question of his daughter Sunity Devi's marriage to the Hindu Maharaja Cooch Behar against Sen's own previously-stated reform principle of *no child-marriage* — produced the next Brahmo schism: his followers split, the *Sadharan Brahmo Samaj* forming under reformist opponents. From 1879 Sen reframed his teaching as the *Nava Vidhāna* / "New Dispensation" — an explicitly universalist-syncretic synthesis of Hindu Vaishnava bhakti, Christian devotional Christology, Islamic *tawḥīd*, and Buddhist ethical-meditative discipline. Traveled to England in 1870, where he met Queen Victoria, Max Müller, Frances Power Cobbe, John Stuart Mill (in correspondence), and the Unitarian-Universalist intellectual world.

## Works
- ***Lectures in India*** (collected, multiple editions, definitive 1901): the principal collected corpus of his oratorical religious-philosophical work. Includes "Asia's Message to Europe" (1883), "Christ and Christianity" (1879), "We Apostles of the New Dispensation" (1881), and the famous "Behold the Light of Heaven in India" (1875).
- ***That Marvellous Mystery — the Trinity*** (1882): Sen's Hindu-Vaishnava interpretive reconfiguration of the Christian Trinity (Father / Son / Holy Spirit ↔ Saccidānanda: Being / Consciousness / Bliss). One of the boldest 19th-c. cross-tradition theological constructions.
- ***Jeebon Veda*** (Bengali, 1879): a Bengali-language anthology of devotional-philosophical instruction.

## Role in the tradition
- **Brahmo Samaj's most influential 19th-c. evangelist**: Sen's oratory (Bengali, English, sustained both) carried the Brahmo Samaj's reformed-Hindu message across India and into the British and American Unitarian-liberal world more thoroughly than any other 19th-c. Hindu reformer before Vivekananda's 1893 Chicago appearance.
- **Nava Vidhāna / New Dispensation**: his late-1870s and 1880s synthesis treats all the great religions as legitimate dispensations, with the Brahmo Samaj's "New Dispensation" as their synthesizing fulfillment. The position prefigures [[ramakrishna]]'s practical multi-tradition *sādhana* (Sen visited Ramakrishna at Dakshineswar from 1875 — the encounter is documented in the Ramakrishna corpus; the cross-fertilization is real but contested in directionality).
- **Bridge figure to Ramakrishna**: Sen's visits to Ramakrishna brought the unknown Bengali Tantric-mystic of Dakshineswar to the attention of educated Calcutta society; Sen's *Paramahamser Ukti* (1878) is the first published account of Ramakrishna in English; the trajectory by which Ramakrishna entered Bengali public consciousness runs substantially through Sen.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Hindu-Christian-Islamic-Buddhist explicit syncretism (Nava Vidhāna)**: Sen's late teaching is one of the most explicit 19th-c. theological attempts to construct a religion that affirms multiple traditions simultaneously as legitimate divine dispensations. The Brahmo *trinity-as-Saccidānanda* identification, the Brahmo incorporation of Christian hymnody, the Brahmo *sangkirtan* (devotional singing) drawing on Vaishnava bhakti, and the formal liturgical recognition of Buddha and Muhammad as prophets together constitute one of the most fully-realized 19th-c. religious-pluralist programs.
- **Direct British-Unitarian connection**: Sen's 1870 England visit, his correspondence with Frances Power Cobbe and others, and his sustained engagement with the British Unitarian-Universalist movement constitute one of the most documented 19th-c. Hindu-Christian liberal-reform alliances. Müller's 1884 memorial study is one document of this relationship.
- **Anticipation of 20th-c. comparative-religion / interfaith universalism**: the *Nava Vidhāna* synthesis prefigures the Theosophical Society's perennial-philosophy program (1875), the 1893 Parliament of World Religions (see [[event-parliament-of-world-religions-1893]]), Vivekananda's universalist Vedanta, and 20th-c. interfaith movements generally.
- **Vaishnava-Sufi-Christian bridal-devotional shared idiom**: Sen drew explicitly on the Bengali Vaishnava *madhura-rasa* (sweet erotic) devotional tradition, on Sufi *‘ishq* / *firāq*, and on the Christian Song-of-Songs / *Sponsa Christi* bridal mysticism in his preaching. The cross-tradition lexical-thematic resonance was for Sen a sign of underlying religious unity; for his orthodox Brahmo critics, an unprincipled syncretic eclecticism.

## Disputes
- **The 1878 Cooch Behar marriage**: Sen's permission of his minor daughter's marriage to the Cooch Behar Maharaja (against the explicit Brahmo reform principle of *no marriage below the age of consent*, which he had himself championed) was the central scandal of his career. Critics treat it as cynical opportunism (the Cooch Behar throne was valuable); sympathizers as a personal-pastoral compromise.
- **Mental state in late years**: Sen's heightened ecstatic-mystical register in the 1880s, with self-presentations as *Mātā Bāp* ("Mother-Father" — a divine bisexual identity) and as a prophet of the New Dispensation, was variously read by contemporaries as authentic religious experience or as deterioration; the documentary record allows both readings.
- **Direction of Ramakrishna-Sen influence**: who influenced whom in the Sen-Ramakrishna relationship is contested; the standard position is that the influence ran in both directions.
