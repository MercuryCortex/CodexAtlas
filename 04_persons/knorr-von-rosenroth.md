# Christian Knorr von Rosenroth

## Identity

Christian Knorr von Rosenroth (1636–1689) was a German Lutheran diplomat and Christian Kabbalist who compiled and translated the *Kabbala Denudata* ("Kabbalah Unveiled," 2 vols., 1677–1684) — the most important Latin compilation of Kabbalistic texts produced in the early modern period. The work included substantial portions of the Zohar in Latin translation, selections from the Lurianic corpus (including material on Adam Kadmon and the *tzimtzum*), and summaries of Kabbalistic theology. It was the primary means by which Lurianic Kabbalah became available to non-Hebrew-reading European intellectuals.

## Significance for the Atlas

The *Kabbala Denudata* is the **transmission vector** through which Lurianic Adam Kadmon ideas (the Cosmic Human, shevirat ha-kelim, tzimtzum) entered the English intellectual tradition. Newton owned a copy and annotated it. Leibniz read it. Henry More (Cambridge Platonist) engaged with it. Through the English Hermetic tradition — specifically through Jakob Boehme's reception of Christian Kabbalism and through Thomas Taylor — these ideas eventually reached William Blake's generation. Blake did not read the *Kabbala Denudata* directly, but the Cosmic Human doctrine that Blake independently reconstructed as "Albion" had been transmitted to his intellectual environment through Knorr von Rosenroth as an intermediary.
