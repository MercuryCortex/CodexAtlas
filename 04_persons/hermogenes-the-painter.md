# Hermogenes the Painter

## Identity
A late-2nd-century Christian theologian (also a painter by profession — hence the cognomen), probably resident at Carthage, who taught that **matter is eternal and uncreated**, co-existing with God, and that creation is God's *ordering* of pre-existent matter rather than creation *ex nihilo*. Tertullian's *Adversus Hermogenem* (~+200) is the principal extant attack on his position. Theophilus of Antioch had earlier written against him (work lost).

## Historical evidence
Tertullian's *Adversus Hermogenem* preserves substantial quotations and paraphrases. The matter-eternity position is recognizably Platonist-Stoic in genealogy — similar to the [[plato]]-*Timaeus* doctrine that the Demiurge orders pre-existent chaos rather than creating from nothing — and Hermogenes appears to have explicitly invoked philosophical reasoning to defend a theologically respectable interpretation of the Old Testament's "tohu wa-bohu" (Gen 1:2) as evidence for pre-existent matter.

## Works
None survives. Reconstruction from Tertullian.

## Role in the tradition
- **Eternal matter doctrine**: a critical test case for the developing Christian doctrine of *creatio ex nihilo*. Tertullian's *Adversus Hermogenem* is the first sustained Christian theological defense of *creatio ex nihilo* as a *necessary* doctrinal commitment.
- **Christian-Platonist tension**: Hermogenes represents the trajectory where Christian Platonism was *not* yet committed to *ex nihilo* creation; the orthodox decision against him sets a path-dependent theological direction.

## Disputes
- **Whether Hermogenes was a "Gnostic"**: Tertullian groups him with the heresies but his position is more straightforwardly philosophical-Platonist than Gnostic-mythological.
- **Cultural-philosophical genealogy**: how directly he borrowed from Plato's *Timaeus*, Stoic physics, or Middle-Platonist sources is contested.
