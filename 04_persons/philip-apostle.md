# Philip the Apostle

## Identity
Philip, native of Bethsaida (the home of Peter and Andrew per Jn 1:44), one of the Twelve. The Synoptics list him by name but never narrate his actions; the Gospel of John gives him substantial dramatic presence (calling of Nathanael; bread-of-life setup; Greek-inquirers; Last Supper "show us the Father"). Critical scholarship maintains a careful distinction between Philip the Apostle and [[philip-the-evangelist]] (Acts 6, 8, 21) — but the earliest traditions (Papias via Eusebius) already conflate them.

## Historical evidence
Reliable as a name on the apostle-list. Beyond that, attestations diverge. Papias-via-Eusebius says "Philip the apostle and his daughters" lived and were buried at Hierapolis — but the daughters are an Acts-derived feature of the *Evangelist*, not the Apostle, suggesting Papias (~+125) is already conflating. The *Acts of Philip* (4th c.) elaborates a missionary tale through Asia Minor culminating in upside-down crucifixion at Hierapolis, with the legendary subplot of the apostle's sister Mariamne.

## Works
None genuine. The pseudepigraphic *Gospel of Philip* (Nag Hammadi II,3) is a Valentinian anthology with no contact with the historical apostle — the attribution is generic. The *Acts of Philip* and the *Letter of Peter to Philip* (NHC VIII,2) are likewise pseudonymous.

## Role in the tradition
Important as a Gnostic-favored apostle: the *Gospel of Philip* and *Letter of Peter to Philip* both use his name, suggesting that — like Thomas, James, and Mary Magdalene — Philip was a marker of a particular early-Christian intellectual current.

## Disputes
- **Conflation with Philip the Evangelist.** Papias's testimony, and consequently much of the Hierapolis tradition, may belong instead to the Evangelist (the Hellenist deacon of Acts 6) and have been attached to the Apostle by later tradition.
- **Authorship of Johannine Philip-scenes.** Whether these reflect early memory or are a literary device of the Fourth Evangelist is debated.
