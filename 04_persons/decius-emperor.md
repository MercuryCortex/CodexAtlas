# Decius

## Identity
Roman emperor 249–251 (killed in battle at Abrittus against the Goths), a Pannonian general elevated by his troops. His brief reign produced **the first systematically empire-wide anti-Christian policy**: an edict ~+250 requiring **every inhabitant of the Empire to sacrifice to the gods**, take a sacrificial meal, and receive a written certificate (*libellus*) confirming compliance. While not explicitly directed at Christians, the edict was *de facto* a comprehensive enforcement test, since Christians (and to a lesser extent Jews) were uniquely unable to comply.

## Historical evidence
The edict itself is lost, but **physical *libelli*** — papyrus certificates — have been recovered, principally from Egypt; ~45 examples are known. The certificates follow a standard form: declaration that the bearer has always sacrificed, did sacrifice and tasted the sacrificial food, plus a witness signature. The recoverable evidence vindicates the historicity of the comprehensive policy.

## Works
The edict of 249/250 (lost in original); *libelli* (physical examples preserved).

## Role in the tradition
- **The first comprehensive Christian persecution**: distinct from previous local or court-driven persecution patterns. Decius's policy created the **lapsi crisis** — the post-persecution problem of how to handle Christians who had sacrificed (or obtained false certificates) to escape death. This crisis produced the foundational discussions of [[cyprian-of-carthage|Cyprian]]'s *De lapsis* and *De ecclesiae unitate*.
- **Death of Pope Fabian** (January 250) — under Decius. Fabian's death and the consequent Roman bishopric vacancy plays a role in Cyprian's correspondence.
- **Origen's torture**: Origen was imprisoned and tortured (~+250) but survived; he died shortly after, possibly from the after-effects.
- **End of the persecution**: Decius's death in battle at Abrittus (June 251) terminated the persecution; subsequent emperors (Trebonianus Gallus, Valerian) varied in their policy.

## Disputes
- **Was the edict targeted at Christians specifically or universal**: mainstream view is universal sacrifice with Christians as the principal incidentally-affected group, possibly with hostile intent.
- **Scale of compliance and mortality**: high compliance with sacrifice (with the *lapsi* problem); martyrdom counts likely in low hundreds, contra later hagiographic inflation.
