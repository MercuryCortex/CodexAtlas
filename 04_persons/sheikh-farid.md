# Sheikh Farid (Bābā Farīd Ganjshakar, 1173–1266 CE)

Punjabi Muslim Sufi saint of the Chishti order; the dominant Chishti master in Punjab in the 12-13th century; founder of the Pakpattan khānaqāh that became the spiritual center of Punjabi Islam for several centuries. Farid is theologically Muslim, ritually Sufi-Chishti — and culturally one of the most important figures in pre-Sikh Punjab.

## MASSIVE WIN — Muslim Sufi poetry canonized as Sikh scripture

134 of Farid's Punjabi devotional verses (*shaloks*) are included in the [[phase-6-016-guru-granth-sahib]], the central scripture of Sikhism, compiled by Guru Arjan in 1604. They are part of the *bhagat bani* — the canonical compositions of the holy ones — alongside [[kabir]], Ravidas, Namdev, and other pre-Sikh sants and Sufis. Farid is the second-most-represented non-Guru voice in the Granth.

This is one of the cleanest documented cross-tradition canonization events in world religious history: the central scripture of one tradition (Sikhism) contains, *as canonical*, the devotional poetry of a saint from another tradition (Islamic Sufism) — recited daily in gurdwaras worldwide. The cross-tradition incorporation is institutional and active, not merely historical.

See the "Sheikh Farid's Poetry Canonized in a Sikh Scripture" section of [[phase-6-016-guru-granth-sahib]]; Singh 2000 documents the canonization in detail.

## Lineage

Chishti silsila: Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti of Ajmer → Quṭbuddīn Bakhtiyār Kākī → Bābā Farīd. Farid's principal successor was Niẓāmuddīn Awliyā of Delhi (d. 1325), through whom the Chishti order became the dominant Sufi tradition in northern India.
