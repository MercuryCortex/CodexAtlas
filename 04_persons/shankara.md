# Adi Shankara

## Identity
Ādi Śaṅkarācārya (~788–820 traditional; some scholars 700–750) is the founder of the Advaita Vedānta philosophical school and the most influential single figure in medieval Hindu intellectual history. Born Kāladī in Kerala; took saṃnyāsa young; organized the four *Daśanāmi* monastic orders; founded the four cardinal *maṭhas* at Sringeri (south), Dwarka (west), Badrinath (north), and Puri (east); died ~820 (traditional) at age 32 at Kedarnath. The biographical *Śaṅkara-Digvijaya* (hagiographic country-wide debating tour) is largely legendary.

## Works
*Brahmasūtra-Bhāṣya* (his definitive systematic statement); commentaries on the ten principal Upaniṣads; *Gītā-Bhāṣya*; *Upadeśasāhasrī* (introductory treatise on Advaita method); shorter Sanskrit hymns. Many works attributed to him are now widely held by scholars to be later (especially *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi*).

## Role in the tradition
Foundational architect of Advaita Vedānta — the doctrine that Brahman alone is real, that *māyā* causes the apparent multiplicity, and that liberation is the realization "I am Brahman." His system has been the dominant philosophical position in subsequent Hindu intellectual culture, though [[ramanuja]]'s Viśiṣṭādvaita and [[madhva]]'s Dvaita are the principal alternative Vedāntic streams. Subsequent Advaita systematization by [[vidyaranya]] (14th c.) under the Vijayanagara empire's intellectual program; the post-Shankara *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* ([[phase-5-034-vivekachudamani]]) is the principal popular-pedagogical Advaita introductory manual. Modern Neo-Vedānta ([[swami-vivekananda]], [[sri-aurobindo]]) is built on Shankarian foundations; Schopenhauer's interest in Hindu philosophy was substantially Advaita-mediated.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Shankara-Meister Eckhart parallel**: Otto 1923 *Mysticism East and West* is the classic comparative study; both are radical non-dualist mystics articulating the identity of self with ultimate reality (Brahman / Gottheit). The parallel has been one of the most-studied cross-tradition mystical comparisons.
- **Shankara-Plotinian Neoplatonism structural-parallel**: the non-dual one-as-source position; Halbfass 1988 *India and Europe* treats the comparison rigorously, treating the parallels as structurally significant but historically independent (no documented historical transmission).

## Disputes
Dates contested between traditional 788–820 (most accepted) and revisionist 700–750 (Hacker, Mayeda). Attribution of many works is disputed; the *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* is now widely held to be later.
