# Homer

## Identity
"Homer" is the name traditionally attached to the Iliad and the Odyssey, the two great Archaic Greek epics. Whether he was a single historical poet, two historical poets (one for each epic), an oral tradition coalescing under a name, or a substantially fictive name attached to a monumental redaction of oral material — the "Homeric Question" — has been debated since at least the 18th c.

## Historical evidence
Ancient biographical traditions (the *Vitae Homeri*, Pseudo-Herodotus etc.) supply many details: blind, Ionian (Chios or Smyrna), wandering rhapsode. These are widely regarded as later legendary accretions. No contemporary inscription, no documentary record, exists. Herodotus (2.53) places Homer ~400 years before himself, i.e., ~9th c. BCE; modern linguistic and stylistic dating tends to ~750–700 BCE for the Iliad and ~720–650 for the Odyssey.

## Works
- Authored or compiled: Iliad and Odyssey. Substantial unity within each poem suggests at least monumental redactor-poets behind each.
- Falsely attributed: the Homeric Hymns (collection of hexameter hymns, in fact composed by various poets, 7th–4th c. BCE), the *Margites* (a lost comic epic), the *Batrachomyomachia* (parodic later work).

## Role in the tradition
Foundational author of Greek literature; "the Poet" (*ho poiētēs*) by antonomasia in later Greek. Plato's *Republic* (book 10) treats Homer as the educator of the Greeks and famously banishes him from the ideal city. The Hellenistic Alexandrian scholars (Zenodotus, Aristophanes of Byzantium, Aristarchus of Samothrace) edited the Homeric text and developed literary criticism around it. Roman education made Homer central; Vergil's Aeneid imitates him; the Western literary tradition is unimaginable without him.

## Disputes
- **Single author vs. tradition.** Wolf (1795) opened the question; Parry/Lord's oral-formulaic theory (1930s–50s) reframed it. Current consensus: oral tradition with substantial monumental shaping, single or near-single shaper plausible for each poem.
- **Date.** Iliad ~750–700; Odyssey slightly later. Some place both later (-7th c.).
- **Blindness.** Legendary; no historical basis.
