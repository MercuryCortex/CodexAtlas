# John Mark

## Identity
A Jerusalem Christian whose mother (Mary, distinct from the better-known Marys) hosted a house-church there (Acts 12:12). Cousin of [[barnabas]] (Col 4:10). Distinct from [[mark-evangelist]] in critical scholarship, though traditional Christianity identifies them — making him the author of the Gospel of Mark on the basis of Papias's testimony that "Mark, the interpreter of Peter, wrote down accurately, but not in order, the things which were said and done by the Lord."

## Historical evidence
Strong NT attestation: in Acts as Paul-Barnabas's young assistant (Acts 12–15), as the cause of their split, then in the disputed and undisputed Paulines (Col 4:10; Phlm 24; 2 Tim 4:11) reconciled with Paul. 1 Peter 5:13 sends greetings from "my son Mark" in "Babylon" (= Rome). The **Papias tradition** (~+125, in Eusebius *HE* 3.39.15) is the bridge between this Mark and the Gospel; mainstream critical scholarship accepts the Papias tradition as the historical link, though some scholars (Ehrman, Crossan) treat the connection as later attribution.

## Works
Traditional author of the **Gospel of Mark** (~+65/+75), the earliest of the canonical Gospels. The Papias attribution is foundational to this identification.

## Role in the tradition
**Founder-evangelist of Alexandria** per Eusebius (HE 2.16) — possibly the earliest extant tradition for Alexandrian Christian origins, important if Apollos (also Alexandrian) was already a Christian when he arrived in Ephesus. **Pauline biography**: the dispute over him split Paul and Barnabas. **Petrine biography**: as Peter's interpreter, the link between Petrine memory and the Markan Gospel.

## Disputes
- **John Mark = Mark the Evangelist?** Traditional yes; critical scholarship: probable but not certain.
- **Papias's testimony.** Whether it reliably reflects 1st-c. memory or is itself a 2nd-c. reconstruction is debated.
- **Alexandria mission.** Eusebius is our earliest source; not independently corroborated.
