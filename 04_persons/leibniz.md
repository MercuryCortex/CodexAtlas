Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz (1646–1716) — German mathematician, philosopher, and diplomat; one of the most intellectually wide-ranging figures of the 17th century. He independently developed calculus (his notation became the standard over Newton's), invented binary arithmetic, proposed the first modern notion of kinetic energy, designed calculating machines, and produced a complete philosophical system — the *Monadology* — in which ultimate reality consists of irreducible immaterial units called **monads**.

## The Monadology — Pythagorean Inheritance

The *Monadology* (1714) is a 90-proposition metaphysical treatise arguing that reality consists of infinitely many indivisible, immaterial, non-spatial units — "monads" — each of which is a windowless (non-interacting) mirror of the entire universe, arranged in a perfect pre-established harmony by God. The names comes directly from the Pythagorean **Monas** (unity), and the doctrine — that ultimate reality is irreducible spiritual-mathematical units whose mutual proportionality constitutes the cosmos — is the Pythagorean program in 17th-century dress.

Leibniz knew the Pythagorean-Neoplatonic tradition well; he read Plotinus, was familiar with Renaissance Neoplatonism, and explicitly engaged with the Pythagorean doctrine of harmony. His "pre-established harmony" (God arranges all monads to correspond perfectly without interaction) is the Pythagorean *harmonia sphairon* (music of the spheres) transposed to metaphysics: the cosmos is in perfect harmony because God the Demiurge geometrized/arithmetized it from the beginning.

## Binary Arithmetic and the Yi Jing — The MASSIVE WIN

In 1679, Leibniz developed binary arithmetic — representing all numbers using only 0 and 1. In 1701, the Jesuit missionary Joachim Bouvet sent him the Fu Xi arrangement of the 64 hexagrams. Leibniz saw immediately that the hexagram arrangement encoded binary numbers 0–63 in sequence. He published this finding in "Explication de l'Arithmétique Binaire" (1703):

> "It is certainly a remarkable thing that this arithmetic of mine, which I had invented only from now on, was already among the ancient Chinese... I found that what the Chinese emperor Fu Hsi, who lived more than 3,000 years ago, had set down in their canonical scripture, the *Book of Changes*, is exactly my binary arithmetic."

The modern digital computer, which operates entirely in binary, is built on a mathematical system that Leibniz named and formalized — and that the Yi Jing encoded 3,000 years earlier in a different cosmological vocabulary (yang/yin = 1/0, fullness/emptiness, being/non-being).
