# Hadrian

## Identity
Roman emperor 117–138, deeply Hellenophile, traveler-emperor (the only emperor to systematically tour the provinces), founder of **Aelia Capitolina** on the ruins of Jerusalem (~+130 — the act that precipitated the [[event-bar-kokhba-revolt-132-136|Bar Kokhba revolt]]). Issued a **rescript to the proconsul Minucius Fundanus** (~+125) on Christians, preserved verbatim by [[justin-martyr|Justin]] (*1 Apol.* 68) — moderate Trajanic-style policy: trial required, sacrifice acceptable as proof, false accusations punished.

## Historical evidence
The Hadrianic rescript is widely accepted as authentic (despite occasional minority challenges). The Aelia Capitolina foundation is independently attested in coins, inscriptions, rabbinic sources, and Christian historiography.

## Works
- The rescript to Minucius Fundanus on Christians.
- The famous death-bed poem *Animula vagula blandula*.

## Role in the tradition
- **Moderate-Trajanic procedural framework**: confirms and refines Trajan's rescript; Christians take it as relatively favorable.
- **Founder of Aelia Capitolina**: the renaming of Jerusalem and the prohibition of Jewish residence (Aelia was a *gentile* city with a temple to Jupiter on the Temple Mount) is the immediate cause of [[event-bar-kokhba-revolt-132-136|Bar Kokhba's revolt]]. The political reorganization permanently changed the demography of Roman Palestine and produced lasting consequences for Jewish-Christian relations (Christians at the time were generally pro-Roman per Justin, while Jews fought).
- **Apologetic addressee**: [[quadratus-the-apologist]] and [[aristides-of-athens]] addressed apologies to him.

## Disputes
- **Authenticity of the rescript**: occasionally questioned but generally accepted.
- **Hadrian's personal religion**: Hellenophile and initiate into the Eleusinian mysteries; less interested in Christianity per se.
