# Heracleon

## Identity
A leading second-century **Valentinian** theologian, classed by Hippolytus among the *Italian school* of Valentinians (with [[ptolemaeus-valentinian]]), the author of the **earliest known commentary on any Christian biblical book** — a commentary on the Gospel of John (~+170). His commentary survives only in fragments quoted by [[origen]] (~50 years later, in Origen's own Johannine commentary) — but those fragments give us substantial access to one of the most sophisticated 2nd-century Gnostic theological readings of a canonical text.

## Historical evidence
Strong: Irenaeus (~+180) names him; Hippolytus (~+220) assigns him to the Italian Valentinian wing; Clement of Alexandria (~+200) cites him; Origen (~+240) preserves the most material — about 48 quotations of varying length, used as targets for Origen's own anti-Gnostic interpretive program.

## Works
**Commentary on John** — lost; ~48 fragments preserved in Origen's *Commentary on John*. The fragments cover Jn 1, 2, 4, and 8 substantially. They show Heracleon: (a) using sophisticated philological-allegorical method ahead of Origen's own; (b) distinguishing *pneumatic* (spiritual), *psychic* (soulish), and *hylic* (material) classes of humans in his interpretation; (c) reading John 4 (the Samaritan woman) as a programmatic allegory of *Sophia* and *salvation*.

## Role in the tradition
- **First Christian biblical commentator**: a date-priority claim of considerable importance for the history of Christian exegesis. The genre Origen would perfect was pioneered by his Valentinian opponent.
- **Italian Valentinian theology**: per Thomassen 2006, the Italian school taught a *psychic* Christ (in distinction from the Eastern Valentinian school's *pneumatic* Christ at conception), with implications for soteriology of psychic Christians.
- **Origen's interlocutor**: Origen's *Commentary on John* is structured in part as an answer to Heracleon — meaning Heracleon's exegetical agenda *shaped* the orthodox commentary tradition through opposition.

## Disputes
- **Italian vs. Eastern school assignment.** Hippolytus's testimony is the basis; whether the East/West Valentinian split was real-and-Heracleon-as-Italian or a heresiological construction is contested.
- **Date and place.** ~+170 in Rome is mainstream but not certain.
- **Whether the *Gospel of Truth* (NHC I,3) is Heracleon's work.** A minority hypothesis; mainstream view: more likely Valentinus's own.
