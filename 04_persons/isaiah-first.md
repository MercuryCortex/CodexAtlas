# Isaiah of Jerusalem (First Isaiah)

## Identity
Isaiah son of Amoz, prophesying in Jerusalem during the second half of the 8th century BCE under the Judahite kings Uzziah, Jotham, Ahaz, and Hezekiah. A Jerusalem aristocrat with court access; married, with two sons whose symbolic names appear in the prophecies (Shear-jashub "A remnant shall return", Maher-shalal-hash-baz "Spoil speeds, prey hastens"). His vocation-vision (Isaiah 6, the seraphim and "Holy, holy, holy") is one of the most influential theological texts in the Hebrew Bible. He confronted King Ahaz during the Syro-Ephraimite war (~734); advised King Hezekiah during Sennacherib's invasion (701) and the siege of Jerusalem.

## Historical evidence
Internal autobiographical material in the book (his call vision in ch. 6 dated to the year of Uzziah's death, ~739; his sons named symbolically; his interactions with Ahaz in ch. 7 and Hezekiah in chs. 36–39). Independent attestation in 2 Kings 19–20 (parallel to Isaiah 36–39) and 2 Chronicles. Sennacherib's invasion of 701 is directly confirmed by the Taylor Prism and the Lachish reliefs in the British Museum.

## Works
Isaiah 1–39 core material (with extensive Deuteronomistic, exilic, and post-exilic editorial accretion). Chapters 40–55 are by an anonymous exilic prophet ("Second Isaiah" / "Deutero-Isaiah", ~540s BCE); chapters 56–66 by post-exilic prophetic circles ("Third Isaiah"); all are bound into the single book of Isaiah by tradition but are demonstrably from later authors.

## Role in the tradition
One of the most influential prophets in Jewish, Christian, and Islamic tradition. His Immanuel oracle (Isa 7:14) and Servant Song passages (in Second Isaiah, but combined with First Isaiah in tradition) are major Christological proof-texts in the New Testament. The Holiness vocabulary of Isaiah 6 grounds much of subsequent Jewish liturgy (the *Kedusha*).

## Disputes
- **Date of death.** Tradition (Martyrdom of Isaiah, in the Ascension of Isaiah) records his martyrdom under Manasseh by being sawn in half. Historicity legendary.
- **Editorial layers in chs. 1–39.** Even within the core, ch. 24–27 (the "Isaiah Apocalypse") is widely held to be much later (probably 4th c. BCE).
