# Aristotle

## Identity
Aristotle of Stagira (-384 to -322) was Plato's most accomplished student, Alexander the Great's tutor, and founder of the Peripatetic school at the Lyceum in Athens. His corpus — surviving as "esoteric" school-internal lectures recovered from a damp cellar and edited by Andronicus of Rhodes in the 1st century BCE — covers logic, physics, biology, metaphysics, ethics, politics, rhetoric, poetics. His influence on medieval Islamic philosophy (where he was *al-mu'allim al-awwal*, "the First Teacher") and Christian Scholasticism (where Aquinas calls him simply "the Philosopher") is matched by his influence on the natural sciences through the 17th century.

## Historical evidence
Excellent documentation: continuous philosophical reception, named author with biography, named teacher (Plato), named pupil (Alexander), well-attested institutional foundation (the Lyceum). His connection to the Macedonian royal house and his Stagirite (Macedonian) origin made him politically vulnerable in Athens; after Alexander's death (-323) he fled to Chalcis "lest the Athenians sin twice against philosophy" (referencing Socrates), dying there in -322.

For this vault: Aristotle is the *philosophical alternative track* to Plato — his **unmoved mover** is the philosophical "God of the philosophers" who reaches into Christian and Islamic theology (Aquinas's Five Ways, Avicenna's Necessary Being). His *De Anima* on the soul, and the active-intellect doctrine, shape both Christian and Islamic anthropology. His role as Plato's principal critic shaped *all* subsequent Platonist apologetics, including the Neoplatonic synthesis that ultimately fed Christian and Gnostic theology.
