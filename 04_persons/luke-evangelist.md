# Luke the Evangelist

## Identity
Traditional author of Luke and Acts, identified in Christian tradition with **Luke the physician**, the otherwise-obscure New Testament figure mentioned in three Pauline letters (Col 4:14; Phlm 24; 2 Tim 4:11) as a companion of Paul. He would have been a Gentile (Col 4:11–14 distinguishes Luke from those "of the circumcision"), a medical professional, and a traveler with Paul. Antiochene origin is the traditional report (Anti-Marcionite Prologue; Eusebius).

## Historical evidence
The figure of Luke the physician is securely attested in the Pauline letters (Col 4:14 is undisputed for purposes of *mentioning* him, even if Colossians's Pauline authorship is itself disputed; Phlm 24 is in the undisputed Pauline corpus). Whether the Greek author of the two-volume Luke-Acts is *this* Luke is the debated question. The **we-passages** of Acts (sections in 1st-person plural during sea voyages: 16:10–17; 20:5–15; 21:1–18; 27:1–28:16) are sometimes read as evidence of eyewitness participation (Bauckham, Marshall, Hengel) and sometimes as a Hellenistic literary convention of sea-narrative (Vielhauer, Pervo). Mainstream scholarship is divided. The two-volume work itself is securely datable to ~+80/+100 (with a minority pushing later to ~+115 per Pervo). For this vault: hold the authorship as **disputed**, the work itself as datable.
