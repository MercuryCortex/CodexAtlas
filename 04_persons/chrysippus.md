# Chrysippus of Soli

## Identity
The third scholarch of the Stoa (after Zeno of Citium and Cleanthes), c. -279 to -206. "Without Chrysippus there would have been no Stoa" was the ancient proverb (Diogenes Laertius VII.183). Chrysippus systematized Stoic logic (propositional logic, conditional analysis, modal logic — work without which the discipline would not have existed in antiquity), defended Stoic compatibilism (free action within universal causation), and refined Stoic ethics. He wrote some 705 books according to Diogenes Laertius; not one survives as a complete text.

## Historical evidence
Substantial: Diogenes Laertius VII has an extensive doxographic biography; Cicero, Plutarch, Galen, and the later Stoic-doxographic tradition (Stobaeus, Sextus Empiricus) preserve hundreds of fragments. The Long-Sedley *Hellenistic Philosophers* (1987) collects the major texts in translation.

## Works
- *Logical Investigations* — the central body of Stoic propositional logic.
- *On Providence* — defending the rational-providential ordering of the cosmos.
- *On the Soul* — the materialist-pneumatic Stoic psychology.
- *On Fate* — the compatibilist account of universal causation and human responsibility.
None survive as complete works.

## Role in the tradition
Chrysippus is *the* systematizer of Stoicism. Subsequent Roman Stoa (Seneca, Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius) presupposes his work even when not citing it. Modern logic recognized his propositional system as anticipating much of what Frege would re-invent in the late 19th century.

## Disputes
- Reconstructions of the lost works (Stoic logic, the theory of *lekta*, conditionals).
- The compatibilist account of fate and the "lazy argument."
