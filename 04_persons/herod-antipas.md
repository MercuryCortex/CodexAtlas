# Herod Antipas

## Identity
Son of Herod the Great; tetrarch of Galilee and Perea 4 BCE – 39 CE; the Herod of the gospel narratives that mention "Herod" during Jesus's adult ministry (NOT his father Herod the Great of the Matthew 2 nativity, NOR Herod Agrippa I of Acts 12, NOR Herod Agrippa II of Acts 25-26). The political backdrop of most of the Galilean ministry; the executor of John the Baptist (Mark 6, Matthew 14); the figure to whom Pilate sends Jesus during the trial in Luke 23:6-12.

## Historical evidence
Extensively documented by Josephus (*Antiquities* 18.5.1-2 — the execution of John the Baptist; *Antiquities* 18.7 — Antipas's eventual exile by Caligula in 39 CE to Gaul). Coinage struck under his rule survives (founded Tiberias on the lake in 19 CE in honor of the emperor Tiberius). His marriage to Herodias (his half-niece, formerly the wife of his half-brother Herod II) — the moral occasion of John the Baptist's denunciation — is the well-attested historical core behind Mark 6.

## Role in the tradition
- **Execution of John the Baptist.** Mark 6:14-29 and Matthew 14:1-12 narrate the banquet, Salome's dance, the rash oath, and the beheading. Josephus *Antiquities* 18.5.2 gives an alternate-and-more-political account (Antipas executed John out of fear of his crowd influence, at the fortress Machaerus). Mark and Josephus are widely treated as triangulating the same historical event from different angles.
- **The Lukan Pilate-and-Herod episode.** Luke 23:6-12 — Pilate sends Jesus to Antipas because the latter is in Jerusalem for Passover; Antipas mocks Jesus and returns him.
- **Acts 4:27, 13:1** also references him.
