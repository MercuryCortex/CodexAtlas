# Muṣliḥ al-Dīn Saʿdī Shīrāzī

## Identity

Muṣliḥ al-Dīn Saʿdī Shīrāzī (~1210–1292 CE) — known universally as Saʿdī — is the most widely-read prose stylist in the Persian literary tradition and one of the two or three supreme Persian poets. He was born in Shiraz (Fars province) but spent approximately 30–45 years traveling across the Islamic world — Khorasan, Anatolia, the Levant (including, by his own account, the Crusades-era Holy Land, where he was reportedly captured by Crusaders and ransomed), North Africa, Central Asia, and India. He returned to Shiraz and in 1257–58 composed his two masterworks in rapid succession: the *Bostān* ("The Orchard," 1257, verse mathnawi) and the [[phase-5-052-sadi-gulistan|*Gulistān*]] ("The Rose Garden," 1258, prose-verse compendium).

His *takhallus* "Saʿdī" derives from Saʿd ibn Zangī, the Atābeg of Fars who patronized him. He is the author of the *Bānī Ādam* quatrain inscribed at the **United Nations** entrance in New York — making him one of the handful of medieval Persian poets known to modern secular audiences globally.

## Historical evidence

Saʿdī's biography is documented through his own extensive autobiographical material in the *Gulistān* and *Bostān* (though some anecdotes are likely shaped for literary effect), through the testimony of his student Qāsim ibn ʿAlī Fārisī (who compiled biographical notes), and through court documents from the Salghurid Atābegs of Shiraz. His travels are generally accepted as historical; the Crusader-captivity episode is consistent with known conditions in the Levant in the 1230s–1240s.

## Works

**Texts authored:**
- [[phase-5-052-sadi-gulistan]] (*Gulistān*, 1258 CE) — prose-poetry compendium in 8 chapters; the most widely-read Persian prose text after the Quran across the Persianate world.
- *Bostān* ("The Orchard," 1257 CE) — verse mathnawi companion to the *Gulistān*; ethical poetry organized by virtue-themes. Often studied alongside the *Gulistān* as a unified diptych.
- *Ghazals* — a *Dīvān* of lyric poems, less celebrated than the *Gulistān* but contributing to his Sufi-lyric reputation.
- *Qaṣīdas* (formal odes) and *Rubāʿiyyāt* (quatrains).

## Role in the tradition

Sa'di occupies a unique position in the Persian tradition: he is simultaneously the supreme Sufi ethical teacher and the supreme prose stylist. His work is more accessible than [[farid-ud-din-attar]]'s esoteric mysticism or [[rumi]]'s ecstatic theology; the *Gulistān* synthesizes Sufi spiritual insight with practical worldly wisdom in a form usable by rulers, scholars, merchants, and students alike. This accessibility was the key to the *Gulistān*'s extraordinary cultural reach: it was the standard Persian-language teaching text in Ottoman, Mughal, and Safavid schools for ~600 years.

**The *Bānī Ādam* (Children of Adam) quatrain:**
> *Human beings are members of one whole* / *Since in creation from one essence they were made* / *When the age afflicts one member with pain* / *The other members will remain restless too* / *If you have no sympathy for human pain* / *You cannot be called a human being.*

(Transliteration of the Persian opening: *Banī ādam aʿżāye yak dīgarand / Ki dar āfarīnesh ze yak gawharand*.) This was carved in marble and installed at the United Nations entrance in New York in 2005. Its theological grounding is the Sufi *tawḥīd* (divine unity implies human unity) — making it simultaneously a 13th-century Islamic theological statement and a 21st-century human-rights text.

## Disputes

- **Saʿdī's Sufi affiliation.** Scholars disagree on how deeply committed Sa'di was to formal Sufism vs. how much his Sufi vocabulary was the culturally expected mode of elevated Persian poetry. The consensus is that his ethics are genuinely Sufi-grounded (rooted in *tawḥīd*), but he is not a *ṭarīqa*-aligned mystic in the way Attar or Rumi were.
- **Historicity of the travel anecdotes.** The Crusader-captivity story in particular has been questioned as literary embellishment; the general scholarly view is that Sa'di genuinely traveled widely and the core of the autobiographical material is reliable.
