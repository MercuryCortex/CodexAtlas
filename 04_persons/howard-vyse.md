# Colonel Richard William Howard Vyse

**Richard William Howard Vyse** (1784–1853) was a British Army officer who conducted extensive (and frequently destructive) investigations of the Giza pyramids in 1837, using gunpowder to blast open sealed passages. His primary discoveries were the **five relieving chambers** above the King's Chamber (see [[relieving-chambers-great-pyramid]]), including Campbell's Chamber where he found the **Khufu cartouche** — the primary inscriptional evidence connecting the Great Pyramid to Khufu.

His methods were typical of 19th-century Egyptology but were later criticized as unnecessarily destructive. He also forced entry into the **[[pyramid-of-menkaure-giza|Pyramid of Menkaure]]** and found the famous basalt sarcophagus (subsequently lost at sea) and the wooden coffin with Menkaure's cartouche.

His field notes were published in *Operations Carried On at the Pyramids of Gizeh in 1837* (1840) — a primary source still consulted by researchers. Allegations by Zecharia Sitchin that Vyse forged the Khufu cartouche have been rejected by virtually all Egyptologists.
