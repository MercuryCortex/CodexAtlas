# Noah

## Identity
The pre-Mosaic patriarch of Genesis 5–9 — son of Lamech, father of Shem, Ham, and Japheth — chosen as the righteous one to be spared from the divinely-sent flood, becoming progenitor of all post-diluvian humanity and partner of God's "Noahide" covenant (Gen 9). The biblical Noah is the Hebrew adaptation of the Mesopotamian flood-hero (Utnapishtim in Gilgamesh; Ziusudra in the Sumerian Flood Story; Atrahasis). See [[flood-motif]].

## Historical evidence
None. The figure is a narrative-foundational character within an inherited Near Eastern myth-complex.

## Role in the tradition
- Judaism: the *Noahide laws* (Gen 9; Tosefta Avodah Zarah 8:4) are the rabbinic universal-moral framework binding on all humanity (not just Israel). The "ger toshav" status of righteous Gentiles is grounded here.
- Christianity: Noah is a type of baptism (1 Pet 3:20–21) and of the eschatological judgment ("as the days of Noah were," Mt 24:37).
- Islam: Nūḥ is a major pre-Islamic prophet; Qur'an Sura 71 is named after him; his flood narrative is shorter and more moralized than the biblical.
- Apocalyptic-pseudepigraphic: Noah-traditions develop in 1 Enoch (the Birth-of-Noah narrative), Jubilees (Noah's deathbed instructions, the Noahide covenant), and the *Genesis Apocryphon* from Qumran.

## Disputes
- Source-critical: Genesis 6–9 contains parallel flood accounts woven together — J and P each have their own narrative; the combined text preserves doublets and contradictions (different counting of animals, different durations).
- Comparative: the Mesopotamian-Israelite literary dependence is now standard; Israel's text is theologically transformed.
