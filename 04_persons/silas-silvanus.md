# Silas / Silvanus

## Identity
A Jerusalem-Christian leader sent (with Judas Barsabbas) by the Jerusalem council to convey the Apostolic Decree to Antioch (Acts 15:22), who then joined Paul as his principal companion on the **second missionary journey** (after Paul and Barnabas's split). Identified with the **Silvanus** of Paul's letters and 1 Peter — the Greek/Latin form of the same name.

## Historical evidence
Strong: Acts and Paul's letters independently attest him as Paul's co-worker (Paul: 1 Thess 1:1, 2 Thess 1:1, 2 Cor 1:19; Acts: extensive narrative through the second journey). The identification of "Silas" in Acts with "Silvanus" in Paul is generally accepted on linguistic grounds.

## Works
None genuine. The 1 Peter mention (*"By Silvanus I have written..."*) is traditionally read as scribal/courier note; some critical scholars have proposed compositional responsibility (Silvanus as the actual author of 1 Peter on Peter's behalf), but this is contested.

## Role in the tradition
Bishop of Corinth or Thessalonica per varying late traditions. A major character in the Acts second-journey narrative (Philippi, Thessalonica, Beroea, Corinth).

## Disputes
- **Silas = Silvanus identification.** Standardly accepted; mainstream critical view.
- **1 Peter compositional role.** Possible but minority hypothesis.
