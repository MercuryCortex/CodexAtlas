# Johann Valentin Andreae

## Identity
Lutheran pastor, theologian, educational reformer, and esoteric author (1586–1654); the consensus principal author of the foundational Rosicrucian texts and the most influential single figure of the early Rosicrucian movement. Born to a Württemberg pastoral family; educated at Tübingen where he joined the Tübingen circle with Tobias Hess and Christoph Besold; ordained Lutheran 1614; held various pastoral positions ending as senior court chaplain at Stuttgart. Composed the *Chymische Hochzeit* in his teens (~1605, printed 1616); jointly produced the *Fama Fraternitatis* (1614) and *Confessio Fraternitatis* (1615) with the Tübingen circle. Later in life Andreae substantially distanced himself from the Rosicrucian movement (calling the *Fama* a *ludibrium*), focused on practical Christian reform, and wrote *Christianopolis* (1619) — a Lutheran utopian-pedagogical city plan that anticipates Comenius's pansophic project.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own substantial published corpus, autobiography, official Württemberg church records, contemporary correspondence (Andreae's correspondents included Comenius and Hartlib).

## Works
*Chymische Hochzeit Christiani Rosencreutz* (1616). *Fama Fraternitatis* (1614, co-authored). *Confessio Fraternitatis* (1615, co-authored). *Christianopolis* (1619). *Mythologia Christiana* (1619). *Theca Gladii Spiritus* (1616). *Vita ab Ipso Conscripta* (autobiography, posthumously published).

## Role in the tradition
Andreae is the principal historical author of the Rosicrucian foundational texts. His later disavowals and pivots have made him a complicated figure for the modern Rosicrucian movements that look back to 1614; current scholarship (Brecht, Edighoffer) treats him as a serious Lutheran reformer for whom the Rosicrucian moment was a youthful project he later partly disowned.
