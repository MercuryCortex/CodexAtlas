# Padmasambhava (historical figure)

## Identity
Padmasambhava (~8th c., dates uncertain) was an Indian Tantric Buddhist master invited to Tibet by Emperor Trisong Detsen (r. 755–797) to consolidate Buddhism in Tibet. The historical figure was probably born in the Swat / Oḍḍiyāna region (modern Pakistan); known in Tibetan tradition as Lopön Rinpoche or Guru Rinpoche. Legend credits him with overcoming the indigenous Bön deities (subduing rather than expelling them, converting them into Buddhist dharma-protectors) and founding the first major Tibetan Buddhist monastery at Samye (~779). The deified-cosmic Padmasambhava — central to Nyingma terma doctrine, in which Padmasambhava is treated as an ongoing transcendent presence who hid scriptures for predestined revealers — is in some respects continuous with the historical figure and in other respects an elaborate posterior development. See also [[padmasambhava]] in 03_deities/ for the deified aspect.

## Historical evidence
8th-c. Tibetan imperial records (the imperial chronicles, Dunhuang manuscripts) attest a figure named Padmasambhava active at Trisong Detsen's court. The hagiographic biographies (*Padma Kathang* cycle, 12th–14th c.) substantially elaborate.

## Works
Traditional attribution: a massive corpus of terma scriptures revealed by later *tertöns* (Karma Lingpa for the *Bardo Thödöl*, Nyangral Nyima Özer, Guru Chöwang, the "Five Sovereign Tertöns" and many others). The actual textual composition of these terma texts is substantially later than the 8th c.

## Role in the tradition
Foundational; revered as a "Second Buddha" in Tibetan Buddhism. The Nyingma school traces itself entirely back to his transmissions. Bhutan's national identity is substantially tied to the Padmasambhava-tradition.
