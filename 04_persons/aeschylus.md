# Aeschylus of Eleusis

## Identity
Aeschylus (c. -525 to -456) is **the founder of Greek tragedy as a literary form** — the eldest of the three great Athenian tragedians, whose ~80–90 plays (7 surviving) effectively invented the tragic-dramatic genre as we know it by adding the second actor (Aristotle *Poetics* 1449a) and developing the choral-dramatic-religious form that Sophocles and [[euripides]] inherit. He was **born at Eleusis** — the very town of the Mysteries — and was almost certainly an **initiate of the Eleusinian Mysteries**, a connection his ancient biographers stressed and that the religious gravity of his surviving plays substantiates. He **fought as a hoplite at Marathon (-490) and Salamis (-480)** — the Greco-Persian War battles that saved Greek independence — and his self-composed epitaph mentioned **only Marathon** ("the Mede with his long hair knows his valor") and **not** his playwriting, by his own choice. His ***Oresteia*** trilogy ([[phase-2-031-aeschylus-oresteia]]) — *Agamemnon*, *Libation Bearers*, *Eumenides*, produced -458 — is **the only surviving complete Greek tragic trilogy** and **the foundational dramatic articulation of Greek theology of cosmic justice**: the movement from blood-vengeance (the Furies / Erinyes) to civic-rational law (the Areopagus court).

## Historical evidence
- **The plays themselves** — 7 surviving (of ~80–90).
- **Aristophanes** *Frogs* (-405) — extensive engagement, where Aeschylus is restored from Hades as the better tragedian (vs. Euripides) by Dionysus the judge.
- **Aristotle** *Poetics* 1449a — credits Aeschylus with adding the second actor.
- ***Vita Aeschyli*** (Hellenistic biography) — extensive but legendary.
- **Self-composed epitaph** preserved by Athenaeus *Deipnosophistae* 14.627c-d: "Aeschylus, son of Euphorion, an Athenian, lies buried here in Gela rich in wheat. His celebrated valor the grove of Marathon could tell, and the long-haired Mede knew it well."

## Works
**Surviving 7 plays:**
- ***Persians*** (-472). The earliest surviving Greek tragedy. The **only surviving tragedy on a historical event** — the defeat of the Persian fleet at Salamis (-480), in which Aeschylus had personally fought, told from the *Persian* perspective (a remarkable empathetic-imaginative move).
- ***Seven Against Thebes*** (-467).
- ***Suppliants*** (c. -463).
- ***Oresteia*** trilogy (-458): **Agamemnon**, **Libation Bearers** (*Choephoroi*), **Eumenides**. The only surviving complete Greek tragic trilogy. See [[phase-2-031-aeschylus-oresteia]].
- ***Prometheus Bound*** — authorship disputed; West 1990 and others argue post-Aeschylean.

**Lost works** — Aeschylus is reported to have written a **lost play group on Eleusinian themes** that may have included content the Mysteries' secrecy-rules prohibited. The *Vita* reports Aeschylus was **prosecuted for revealing the Mysteries** in his plays but acquitted (he successfully argued he had not known the rites were secret — implying he had received the content from a non-initiatic source). The story is probably legendary but reflects ancient awareness of Aeschylus's deep Eleusinian background.

## Role in the tradition

### For the Codex Atlas's mystery-cult-Christianity mission

1. **The *Oresteia* and the theological structure of cosmic justice.** [[phase-2-031-aeschylus-oresteia]] articulates **the foundational Greek-religious theology of justice**: blood-vengeance (the Erinyes / Furies pursuing Orestes for matricide) is **transformed into civic-rational law** (the Areopagus court vote, deadlocked, with Athena's tie-breaking vote acquitting Orestes; the Erinyes transformed into the **Eumenides** — "kindly ones" — and integrated into Athenian civic religion). **This is the foundational structural pattern of Western religious-juridical thought** — the movement from archaic-vendetta justice to civic-rational law-and-order, with religious-mythic resources transformed and preserved rather than destroyed. Christian-theological reception of this pattern — through Augustine *City of God*'s contrast of *civitas Dei* and *civitas terrena*, through medieval canon-law and natural-law theology, through Hegel's reading of *Antigone* and the *Oresteia* in the *Phenomenology* — makes Aeschylean cosmic-justice theology load-bearing for **the entire Western religious-political tradition**.

2. **Eleusinian-religious depth.** Aeschylus's birth at Eleusis and probable initiation make his plays **the principal surviving Greek-tragedy window into Eleusinian religious sensibility**. The *Oresteia*'s climactic move — old chthonic powers (Erinyes) transformed into civic-religious benefactors (Eumenides) through ritual-juridical action — is structurally **the Eleusinian initiation pattern** transferred to civic-political theology: the dark chthonic mother-goddess world (Demeter-Persephone-Erinyes) is not denied or overcome but **integrated** into the higher-order civic-religious cosmos. This is **deep Eleusinian theology in dramatic form**.

3. **Religious experience as dramatic-ritual.** Aeschylus's plays were **performed at the City Dionysia as religious ritual**. The *Eumenides* premiere staged the Erinyes appearing **for the first time visibly on the Athenian stage** in such terrifying form that (the *Vita* reports) audience members had heart attacks and pregnant women miscarried — the story is probably folkloric-amplified, but it reflects the **genuine religious-experiential intensity** of Aeschylean tragedy. This is **religious ritual whose form is drama**, not entertainment-with-religious-coloring.

4. **The Marathon-and-Salamis veteran's theological seriousness.** Aeschylus's plays are written by a man who had **personally fought the Greco-Persian Wars** — who had experienced civic-religious-political crisis at the existential level. The religious weight of *Persians*, *Suppliants*, *Oresteia* derives partly from this existential ground. **Christian comparative-religion reception** (Eric Voegelin's *Order and History* most famously) treats Aeschylus as the principal articulator of **archaic-into-classical Greek religious-political consciousness**, a figure of comparable theological weight to the Hebrew prophets.

## Disputes
- ***Prometheus Bound* authorship.** Traditional Aeschylean attribution is disputed; West 1990 and others argue it is post-Aeschylean (4th-c. or later). The dominant view is now non-Aeschylean.
- **Eleusinian-revelation prosecution.** The *Vita*'s report that Aeschylus was prosecuted for revealing Eleusinian Mystery content in his plays (and acquitted by pleading ignorance of the rites' secrecy) is probably legendary but reflects genuine ancient awareness of his Eleusinian background and the religious-political seriousness of mystery-cult secrecy laws.
- **Death-by-falling-tortoise.** The famous Hellenistic legend that Aeschylus died in Sicily when an eagle dropped a tortoise on his bald head (mistaking it for a stone) is folkloric and untrustworthy; the historical-archaeological record places his death and burial at Gela in Sicily in -456 but the cause is unknown.
