# Apelles

## Identity
A second-century Christian theologian who began as a student of [[marcion-of-sinope|Marcion]] in Rome, broke from him (per the sources, over a moral issue), traveled to Alexandria, and returned to Rome to lead a modified-Marcionite school. Apelles abandoned Marcion's strict two-gods dualism in favor of a more moderate position: **one good God** who created via a *single demiurgic angel*, with the OT scriptures partially-but-not-wholly rejected. He was closely associated with a **female prophetess named Philumene**, whose ecstatic revelations he transcribed in the *Phaneroseis*.

## Historical evidence
Tertullian (*De praescriptione* 30; *De carne Christi* 6, 8) is the most extensive source; **Rhodon's debate with Apelles**, preserved in Eusebius HE 5.13, gives a strikingly personal portrait — Rhodon's questioning, Apelles's refusal to be systematic, Apelles's deflection ("I don't see the need to enquire too closely"). Ambrose's *De paradiso* preserves fragments of the *Syllogismoi* — Apelles's logical arguments against the OT (e.g., if God said "Adam, where are you?" he was either ignorant or lying — neither befitting God).

## Works
- ***Syllogismoi*** — anti-OT logical demonstrations.
- ***Phaneroseis*** — visionary material from Philumene.
Both lost; fragments only.

## Role in the tradition
- **Post-Marcionite theological moderation**: Apelles softened Marcion's stark dualism, possibly making the Marcionite tradition more palatable; his school competed with the strict Marcionites for two centuries.
- **Prophetic-revelatory authority**: the Philumene-Apelles partnership is a striking 2nd-c. example of female revelatory authority being authoritative for a male teacher — parallel to (and slightly preceding) [[montanus|Montanus]]-[[priscilla-maximilla|Priscilla and Maximilla]].
- **Bridge from Marcionism toward proto-Gnosticism**: the demiurgic-angel theology has affinities with Sethian and Valentinian theologies.

## Disputes
- **Cause of break with Marcion**: Tertullian says moral (an affair with a woman in the community); Apelles is more likely to have broken on theological grounds.
- **Philumene's relation to Marcus the Magician's prophetesses, or to Montanus's prophetesses**: contemporary, parallel but distinct phenomena.
- **Whether Apelleans count as a distinct sect**: Tertullian and Hippolytus treat them so; some modern scholarship treats them as a Marcionite school.
