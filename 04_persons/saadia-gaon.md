# Saadia Gaon

## Identity
Saʿadia ben Yosef (882–942) was the leading Jewish scholar of the early 10th century — head (Gaon) of the great Babylonian yeshiva at Sura (928–942, with deposition and restoration), pioneer of Jewish philosophical theology, foundational Hebrew grammarian, principal opponent of the Karaite schism, translator of the Hebrew Bible into Judeo-Arabic, and author of the first comprehensive Jewish prayer-book.

## Historical evidence
Well-documented through Geonic responsa, contemporary chronicles, and a substantial body of his own surviving writings.

## Works
*Emunot ve-Deot* (Judeo-Arabic ~933, the first systematic Jewish philosophical theology); *Tafsīr* (Arabic Bible translation with commentary, the standard Arabic Jewish Bible for centuries); *Sefer ha-Galuy* (polemical); a Hebrew grammar (the first); the *Siddur* (prayer book); polemical works against the Karaite founder Anan ben David and his successors.

## Role in the tradition
Saʿadia is the founding figure of medieval Jewish philosophy — Maimonides treated him as the first major Jewish philosopher, though dismissive of his Kalām-method philosophy as philosophically unsophisticated by his own Aristotelian standard. Saʿadia's defense of Rabbanite Judaism against Karaism was decisive in containing Karaite influence within the mainstream tradition.
