# Paracelsus (Theophrastus von Hohenheim)

## Identity
Swiss physician, alchemist, theologian, and Renaissance magus (1493–1541). Born in Einsiedeln; educated at the universities of Vienna and Ferrara; held a brief and tempestuous appointment as Basel city physician and university lecturer (1527) during which he lectured in German rather than Latin and reportedly burned Avicenna's *Canon* publicly; thereafter spent his life as a wandering physician and prolific vernacular writer across the Holy Roman Empire and Switzerland. Paracelsus reformulated medical theory around three principles (sulphur, mercury, salt) instead of four humors, founded chemical pharmacology (iatrochemistry), introduced opium tincture (laudanum) and chemical mineral remedies, articulated a doctrine of signatures and a microcosm-macrocosm anthropology, and produced a substantial body of heterodox-Christian theological and mystical writings that remained largely unprinted until the 20th century. His chaotic, polemical, vernacular German style established him as one of the most singular personalities of the Renaissance.

## Historical evidence
Documented through his own writings, a substantial body of contemporary references (mostly hostile from academic medicine), the Huser print edition of his works (1589–1591), modern critical editions by Sudhoff and Goldammer.

## Works
- Medical-philosophical: *Volumen Paramirum*, *Opus Paramirum*, *Liber Paragranum*, *Astronomia Magna* (= *Philosophia Sagax*), *De Natura Rerum*, *Archidoxis*, *Labyrinthus Medicorum*.
- Theological-mystical: corpus of biblical commentaries, *De Genealogia Christi*, christological writings (mostly unprinted in his lifetime).
- *Liber de Nymphis, Sylphis, Pygmaeis et Salamandris et de Caeteris Spiritibus* — the canonical text on elemental spirits.

## Role in the tradition
Paracelsus is one of the most consequential single figures in the early modern history of medicine, chemistry, and esoteric Christianity. The Paracelsian tradition (17th-c. iatrochemistry: van Helmont, Fludd, Sennert) is a major root of modern biochemistry and medicine; the Paracelsian theology fed into Rosicrucianism and Boehmian theosophy; the Jungian rediscovery of alchemical psychology takes Paracelsus as central.
