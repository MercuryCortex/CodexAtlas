# Hua Tuo

## Identity

Hua Tuo (華佗, c. 145–208 CE) is the most celebrated physician of the Eastern Han dynasty and the figure whom later Chinese tradition calls the "Father of Chinese Surgery." His biography in the *Sanguozhi* (Records of the Three Kingdoms, c. 280 CE) — one of the most detailed biographies of a physician in ancient world literature — records dozens of clinical cases with specific diagnoses, treatments, and outcomes. He is best known for performing abdominal surgeries and organ repairs under general anesthesia using **mafeisan** (麻沸散), a herbal drug mixture that rendered patients insensible to pain.

## MASSIVE WIN: First Documented General Anesthesia — 1,640 Years Before Ether

Hua Tuo administered **mafeisan** ("hemp boiling powder") — a compound of cannabis (*dama*), Datura (*mantuoluo*), aconite, and other herbs — to render patients unconscious for abdominal surgeries. The *Sanguozhi* records:

> "He first made them drink *mafeisan* dissolved in wine; shortly their senses were numbed as if drunk, or as if they had lost consciousness. He then cut open [the affected area], removed the diseased matter, sutured the incision, and applied a medicated ointment. Within four or five days, the pain had gone; within a month, the patient was completely recovered."

This is the **first documented record of general anesthesia in the history of medicine**, predating:
- European ether anesthesia (Crawford Long / William Morton, 1842–1846) by 1,640 years
- Arabic anesthesia with opium sponges (10th c. CE) by ~800 years
- European soporific sponges (11th c. CE) by ~900 years

The pharmacological basis is sound: cannabis contains cannabinoids with analgesic and sedative effects; Datura contains scopolamine and atropine, potent anticholinergic agents that produce amnesia and sedation. Hua Tuo independently discovered the combination that modern anesthesiology would validate.

## The Cao Cao Case

Hua Tuo treated the warlord **Cao Cao** for severe headaches (possibly migraines, possibly a brain tumor). Hua Tuo's proposed treatment — craniotomy under mafeisan — frightened Cao Cao, who suspected assassination and imprisoned Hua Tuo. Hua Tuo was executed in 208 CE. Cao Cao later watched his son die of the same disease Hua Tuo might have cured, and reportedly wept saying: "If Hua Tuo were still alive, my son would not have come to this."

The story encodes a tragic irony in the history of medicine: the practitioner who could perform brain surgery died because his patient's political paranoia eliminated the only person who could have saved him.

## Wuqinxi — Physical Medicine

Hua Tuo also developed the **Wuqinxi** (五禽戲, "Five Animal Frolics") — a system of physical exercises imitating five animals (tiger, deer, bear, monkey, crane) as preventive medicine. This is the earliest documented exercise-as-medicine system in Chinese tradition, predating modern physical therapy by 1,800 years.
