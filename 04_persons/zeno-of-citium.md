# Zeno of Citium

## Identity
Zeno of Citium (-334 to -262) founded **Stoicism**, the most influential of the Hellenistic philosophical schools. Born in Citium, a Greek-Phoenician city in Cyprus, he came to Athens as a young man (according to legend, after a shipwreck on a trading voyage), studied with the Cynic Crates, then with the Megaric Stilpo and the Academic Polemo, and eventually founded his own school by teaching in the *Stoa Poikilē* — the "Painted Porch" on the north side of the Athenian Agora — which gave his school its name.

## Historical evidence
Documented through Diogenes Laertius's *Lives* VII (a sympathetic and detailed account drawing on multiple Hellenistic sources, including Zeno's pupil Persaeus). His writings are lost; only fragments survive in later quoters (von Arnim's *SVF* I collects them). His Phoenician background was remarked upon in antiquity (he is described as "thin, dark-skinned" — *phoinikidion*), and his outsider status may have informed Stoicism's universalism. He never accepted Athenian citizenship despite his decades there. The Athenians honored him at death with a public funeral and a golden crown.

For this vault: Zeno is the *origin point* of the Stoic logos-doctrine that becomes one of the three load-bearing threads of Phase 3 — feeding into Philo, then Johannine Christology, then patristic theology. He also originated the **cosmopolitan** doctrine: humans are *kosmopolitai* (citizens of the cosmos) by virtue of shared participation in cosmic *logos*, an idea that quietly fed early Christian universalism. His **utopian *Republic*** (lost) advocated common spouses, unisex dress, and the abolition of money, gymnasia, and law-courts — a remarkable early-Stoic radicalism that the later Stoa quietly retired.
