# Georges Dumézil

**Georges Dumézil** (1898–1986) was the French philologist who, more than any other single
figure, built the modern reconstruction of Proto-Indo-European religion. His **trifunctional
hypothesis** — that Indo-European societies and their pantheons are organized around three
functions (sovereignty, martial force, and productivity/fertility) — reorganized the
comparative study of Indian, Iranian, Roman, Norse, and Celtic religion into a single
inherited system.

He is a principal modern terminus of the storm-god branch of this Atlas: the reconstructed
**[[perkwunos|*Perkʷunos]]** and the reading of the thunderer-reflexes (**[[indra]]**,
**[[thor]]**, **[[perun]]**) as one Indo-European inheritance, and of the
**[[chaoskampf|storm-god-vs-serpent combat]]** as a shared warrior-function myth, are the work
of the comparative-mythology tradition (**[[tradition-comparative-religion-academic]]**) that
Dumézil, alongside Calvert Watkins and Jaan Puhvel, brought to maturity. He is where the deep
Indo-European transmission lands in the modern academy.
