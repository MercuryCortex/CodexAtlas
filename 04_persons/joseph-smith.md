# Joseph Smith Jr.

## Identity
American religious leader (1805–1844); founder of the Latter Day Saint movement. Reported a series of visions beginning ~1820 (the "First Vision") culminating in the 1827 delivery of the golden plates by the angel Moroni; translated the Book of Mormon (publ. 1830); founded the Church of Christ in Fayette, New York, 6 April 1830 (later the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints, 1838). Led the Saints through successive migrations (Kirtland, Ohio; Independence and Far West, Missouri; Nauvoo, Illinois) marked by escalating conflict with neighboring communities, sometimes violent. Murdered by a mob at Carthage Jail, Illinois, 27 June 1844 along with his brother Hyrum.

## Role in the tradition
Founder; "Prophet, Seer, and Revelator" in LDS terminology. Practiced and taught plural marriage (polygamy) from ~1841 (debated earlier dates); succession after his death produced multiple branches (Brigham Young's Utah-based LDS, Joseph Smith III's Reorganized LDS / Community of Christ, smaller groups).

## Disputes
- **Translation methodology** (peep stone in hat vs. Urim and Thummim breastplate) — both attested in different sources.
- **Polygamy practice and disclosure timing** — extensively documented in 20th-century scholarship; the 2014 LDS-published essays acknowledge polygamy and its disclosure history with new candor.
- **Book of Abraham translation** — Smith claimed to translate Egyptian funerary papyri (recovered from the original manuscripts in the Metropolitan Museum 1966) as the Book of Abraham; mainstream Egyptology identifies them as standard Book of the Dead and Hor Book of Breathing papyri, with no relation to the Book of Abraham content. The translation methodology remains a Mormon-studies subject (LDS scholarly responses include "catalyst theory" and other accommodations).
