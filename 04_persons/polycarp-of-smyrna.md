# Polycarp of Smyrna

## Identity
Bishop of Smyrna in Asia Minor for ~50+ years (the entire first half of the 2nd c.), martyred ~+155/+156 at the age of 86 by stabbing after the pyre allegedly refused to consume him. Polycarp is a critical **chain-of-memory** figure: Irenaeus, who as a youth heard him at Smyrna, claims that Polycarp claimed to have heard the apostle [[john-son-of-zebedee|John]] — making him a putative living-memory bridge between the apostolic age and late-2nd-c. proto-orthodoxy.

## Historical evidence
Multiple early sources:
- **Ignatius's letter to Polycarp** (~+108) — direct correspondence;
- **Polycarp's *Letter to the Philippians*** (~+115/+135) — his own composition;
- **The *Martyrdom of Polycarp*** (~+156) — by the Smyrnaean Christian community, the earliest extant Christian *martyrology* in its own right (a literary genre);
- **Irenaeus** (~+180) — provides the *direct testimony* chain John → Polycarp → Irenaeus;
- **Eusebius** (~+325) compiles the tradition.

## Works
- *Letter to the Philippians* (heavily quoted from the Pauline epistles and 1 Clement; an early datapoint for proto-canonical New Testament use).
- Possibly two letters fused (P.N. Harrison's hypothesis, defended by Hartog 2013) — one acknowledging Ignatius's letters shortly after his death, one addressed to a later Philippian crisis.

## Role in the tradition
- **The John-Polycarp-Irenaeus chain**: foundation of the apostolic-succession argument and of Western traditions about the Johannine corpus.
- **Quartodeciman controversy**: at Rome ~+155 he and Anicetus failed to agree on the date of Easter — but parted in peace; this becomes a template for later ecumenical disagreement-without-breaking-communion.
- **Martyrology as literary genre**: the *Martyrdom of Polycarp* establishes the form (arrest, refusal-to-recant, execution-with-miraculous-details, communal commemoration of the *birthday into eternal life*).

## Disputes
- **Date of martyrdom.** ~+155/+156 traditional; some scholars argue ~+167 (later in Marcus Aurelius's reign). The Eusebian chronology favors the earlier date.
- **Reliability of Irenaeus's claim** that Polycarp heard John. Critical scholarship varies: some accept; others see it as Irenaean construction.
- **One letter or two.** The Harrison-Hartog two-letter hypothesis is plausible but not consensual.
