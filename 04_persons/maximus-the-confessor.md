# Maximus the Confessor

## Identity
Maximus the Confessor (~580–662) was the supreme Byzantine theologian of the 7th c. — synthesizer of Cappadocian theology, Pseudo-Dionysian apophaticism, and Origenist cosmology through Chalcedonian Christology. Aristocratic Constantinopolitan; briefly secretary to Emperor Heraclius; entered monasticism; fled the Persian and then Islamic conquests, residing in Carthage and Rome. Doctrinal leader of the resistance to Monothelitism; at the Lateran Synod of 649 he was the principal theological architect of the condemnation. For this, the imperial regime in 662 had his tongue cut out and his right hand cut off; he died in exile in Lazica. Vindicated posthumously at Constantinople III (680–681).

## Works
*Ambigua* (commentaries on Gregory of Nazianzus and Pseudo-Dionysius); *Mystagogia*; *Questions to Thalassius*; *Centuries on Love* and *Centuries on Theology* (Sufi-paralleling spiritual aphorisms); *Disputation with Pyrrhus* (Monothelite controversy).

## Role in the tradition
Foundational for all subsequent Byzantine theology; transmitted to the Latin West by Eriugena (~862–864) and central to the Christian Neoplatonic transmission Eriugena → Eckhart → Cusa. Palamas's essence/energies distinction is structurally indebted to Maximian *logoi* doctrine.
