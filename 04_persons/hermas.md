# Hermas

## Identity
A Roman Christian (former enslaved person, per the Shepherd's narrative self-presentation) and the author of **The Shepherd of Hermas** — a sprawling Christian apocalypse in five Visions, twelve Mandates, and ten Similitudes. The work concerns *post-baptismal repentance* — whether Christians who have sinned after baptism can be restored — to which it answers, controversially for its day, *yes, once*. The work was enormously popular and was considered scripture by some 2nd–4th-c. Christians; it appears in Codex Sinaiticus immediately after the New Testament.

## Historical evidence
The **Muratorian Fragment** (~+170/+180) dates and locates him precisely: "The Shepherd was very recently in our own times written by Hermas, while his brother Pius was occupying the bishop's seat of the church of the city of Rome." If reliable, this places the completed work in the 140s, with Pius I (~+140/+155) as Hermas's brother. The work itself, however, mentions "Clement" (Vis. 2.4.3) as a contemporary courier-to-foreign-churches — making the earliest Visions look ~+95. Mainstream view: **composed in stages over ~+95 to ~+150** by a single author over several decades, with the final form appearing under Pius.

## Works
*The Shepherd of Hermas* — single work, but lengthy and stratified.

## Role in the tradition
- **Near-canonical**: included in Codex Sinaiticus; cited as scripture by Irenaeus (AH 4.20.2) and Clement of Alexandria; rejected from canon by the Muratorian Fragment on the *explicit grounds* that it was written too recently.
- **Roman Christian sociology**: rare datum on a Christian who is or was a slave, a freedman struggling with business and family ethics — a window into non-elite Christian Rome.
- **Repentance theology**: the controversial "one post-baptismal repentance" rule shaped early Roman penitential practice; later loosened, then reformed in Latin penitential tradition.
- **Christian prophecy**: Hermas presents as a *receiving prophet* — direct visionary access — placing him in the spiritual-charismatic stream that Montanism would later push further.

## Disputes
- **Single author or composite.** Mainstream: single author over time. Some older scholarship: multiple authors.
- **Identification with Rom 16:14's Hermas.** Origen makes the link; modern scholarship considers it implausible given the dating.
- **Canonical status.** Used as scripture for 2 centuries; never settled.
