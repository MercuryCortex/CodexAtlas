# Plato

## Identity
Plato (~-428 to -348) was an aristocratic Athenian philosopher, student of Socrates, teacher of Aristotle, and founder of the Academy — the institution that gave its name to academic life and operated continuously for nearly 900 years. He is the single most consequential philosopher in the Western tradition; Whitehead's famous remark that the European philosophical tradition is "a series of footnotes to Plato" overstates only in form.

## Historical evidence
Plato is among the best-documented figures of antiquity. His works are nearly all preserved continuously through Byzantine and Renaissance transmission. External attestation includes his nephew Speusippus's account, Aristotle's extensive engagement with his teaching, and Diogenes Laertius's *Lives* (3rd c. CE) compiling earlier biographies. His three Sicilian voyages, his relationship with Dion and Dionysius II of Syracuse, and his Athenian aristocratic lineage are all securely documented. His role in the religious-political ferment following the Peloponnesian War — especially the trial and execution of Socrates in -399, which shaped his life-long alienation from democratic politics — is a vivid historical episode.

His thought reached every subsequent layer of the vault: Aristotle was his student; the Platonic Academy ran continuously to 529 CE; Middle Platonism (~-100/+250) and Neoplatonism (~+250/+650) extended the tradition; Philo, the Christian Fathers, and Gnostic theologians built on Platonic categories; Islamic philosophy and Christian Scholasticism received him through Arabic and Greek transmission; the Renaissance recovered the full corpus and reignited Platonism. The Gnostic demiurge (with valuation inverted) descends from his *Timaeus*. The Christian Logos (via Stoa, Philo, John) descends from his cosmic Reason. He is the most load-bearing pre-Christian figure for the strands this vault tracks.
