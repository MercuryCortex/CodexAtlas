# Zacchaeus

## Identity
A *chief tax-collector* (*architelōnēs*) at Jericho — a major customs and trade hub — described as wealthy and short of stature. The Lukan vignette has him climbing a sycamore-fig to see Jesus pass, being called down personally, and pledging fourfold restitution of anyone he has defrauded.

## Historical evidence
Single-source (Lukan); the narrative is theologically shaped to illustrate Luke's themes of inclusion-of-the-marginalized and repentance-of-the-wealthy. Patristic tradition (Clement of Alexandria, Apostolic Constitutions, *Recognitions* pseudo-Clementine) identifies him as the first bishop of Caesarea Maritima — late and devotional.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
A standard figure of *unexpected-grace* preaching; the sycamore-tree image is enormously productive in art and Sunday school. Eastern Christian tradition makes him a saint and bishop.

## Disputes
- **Historicity.** Lukan-only; no critical scholar treats the narrative as straightforward history, but the underlying figure is plausible.
