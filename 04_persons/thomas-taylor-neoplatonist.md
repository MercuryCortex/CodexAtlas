# Thomas Taylor

## Identity

Thomas Taylor (1758–1835) was the first person to translate the complete works of Plato and most of the Neoplatonists into English — a scholar so devoted to Greek paganism that he was dubbed "the English Pagan" and "Thomas Taylor the Platonist." Working largely alone in poverty, Taylor produced translations of Plato, Plotinus, Iamblichus, Porphyry, Proclus, and the Orphic hymns, making the entire Neoplatonic tradition available to English readers for the first time.

## Significance for the Atlas

Taylor is the **primary channel** through which Neoplatonic and Hermetic ideas — including the Anthropos/Cosmic Human concept — reached the English Romantic generation. William Blake knew Taylor personally and drew on his translations for the concept of **Albion** as the Cosmic Human whose fall into matter generates the broken world (the Hermetic *Poimandres* Anthropos, available in Taylor's translation). Kathleen Raine's *Blake and Tradition* (1968) demonstrated this channel definitively.

Taylor also transmitted: the Orphic cosmogony, the Platonic doctrine of the soul's descent through the spheres, the Iamblichan theurgy that influenced Romantic concepts of imagination as cosmic-redemptive force, and the Eleusinian mystery theology that prefigures Christian sacramental theology.
