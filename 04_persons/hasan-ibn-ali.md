# Hasan ibn Ali

## Identity
Elder son of [[ali-ibn-abi-talib]] by Fatima al-Zahra, daughter of the Prophet [[muhammad-ibn-abdullah]]; older brother of [[husayn-ibn-ali]]. Born in Medina ~625; the elder of the two grandsons whom the Prophet held in special affection. For Shi'i Islam, the **Second Imam**, succeeding his father after Ali's assassination in January 661. Briefly proclaimed caliph in Kufa after Ali's death, but in summer 661, facing the military superiority of Mu'awiya's Syrian forces and internal Kufan fragility, signed an abdication treaty with Mu'awiya ceding the caliphate. Retired to Medina, where he lived another ~9 years and died (poisoned, per Shi'i tradition, at the instigation of Mu'awiya) in 670.

## Historical evidence
Documented in the Sunni and Shi'i hadith corpora, the *sira* tradition, and the early historiographers ([[al-tabari]], Baladhuri, Ya'qubi). The abdication of 661 is a particularly well-attested political event; the precise terms (which scholars debate — Madelung 1997 is the principal critical reconstruction) included Hasan's renunciation of caliphal claim, financial provisions, and (per some sources) provisions about the succession after Mu'awiya — provisions Mu'awiya then violated by designating his son Yazid I.

## Role in the tradition
The figure of Hasan poses a permanent interpretive question for Shi'i theology. If Husayn is the martyr-archetype, Hasan is the *peace-maker / accommodator* — the Imam who chose abdication over a militarily-unwinnable war. Shi'i exegesis of Hasan's choice has varied: some traditions present the abdication as forced strategic necessity (the Kufans had failed Hasan as they would later fail Husayn; further fighting would have annihilated the Prophet's line); other traditions read Hasan's choice as deliberate *taqiyya* (pious dissimulation in danger) preserving the line for later. Critical historiography (Madelung 1997) reads Hasan as a politically realistic figure who recognized that his support in Kufa was unstable and that Mu'awiya could not be militarily defeated in 661.

In Sunni Islam, Hasan's abdication is sometimes invoked positively as a model of Muslim-on-Muslim conflict resolution (the *hadith* of Hasan as "the lord of the youth of Paradise" and as "a chief through whom God will reconcile two great parties of Muslims" — the latter in Bukhari — is read in Sunni tradition as theological vindication of the abdication).

The line of Imams in Twelver Shi'ism continues *not* through Hasan but through Husayn — though Hasan's descendants (the *Hasanid* line) remain genealogically central to Shi'i identity, produced the Zaydi imamate in Yemen (claiming descent from Hasan as well as Husayn through various branches), and were politically active across the early Islamic centuries (the Hasanid revolts of the 8th c. against the Abbasids; Idrisid Morocco; the various Hasani-shariff dynasties — Hashemites of Jordan and the Moroccan ruling house both claim Hasanid descent).

## Disputes
- **Cause of death.** Shi'i tradition uniformly holds Hasan was poisoned by his wife Ja'da bint al-Ash'ath at the instigation of Mu'awiya. Sunni historiography is divided: some accept the poisoning narrative (Baladhuri, Mas'udi) while differing on responsibility; others present a more ambiguous account. The poisoning is plausible but not securely demonstrable.

- **The terms of the abdication treaty.** Sources differ on whether the treaty included Mu'awiya's promise not to designate a successor (which Mu'awiya then violated with Yazid). Madelung 1997 argues a non-designation clause existed; the Umayyad-favoring tradition obscures it.

- **Number of wives.** A late and politically-motivated tradition styles Hasan as *al-mitlaq* (the much-divorcing), with implausibly inflated marriage-and-divorce counts. The tradition is widely treated by both classical and modern scholarship as Umayyad-anti-Hasanid polemic, not historical (Madelung 1997).

## Cross-tradition connections
- ↔ [[ali-ibn-abi-talib]] (father), [[husayn-ibn-ali]] (brother) — the central genealogical triad of early Shi'ism.
- ↔ [[muhammad-ibn-abdullah]] — maternal grandfather.
- ↔ [[event-karbala-680]] — Hasan's earlier abdication is the political-theological pre-condition for the Husayn-Yazid confrontation at Karbala; Hasan's accommodation was repudiated by Husayn's confrontation.
- ↔ [[tradition-shia-islam]], Zaydi Shi'ism — Hasanid descent is the alternative claim-line; Zaydi imams have included Hasanids alongside Husaynids.
