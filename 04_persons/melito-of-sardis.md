# Melito of Sardis

## Identity
Bishop of Sardis in Lydia (Asia Minor), the leading **Quartodeciman** theologian — i.e., the Asian Christian party that celebrated *Pascha* on the 14th of Nisan (the Jewish Passover date) regardless of the day of the week, in tension with the Roman/Alexandrian practice of celebrating on the following Sunday. His *Peri Pascha* — rediscovered in 1936 in a Chester Beatty papyrus — is a stunning Quartodeciman paschal homily, theologically rich and rhetorically extraordinary, but containing **virulent anti-Jewish polemic** ("It is he who has been murdered. And where has he been murdered? In the middle of Jerusalem! By whom? By Israel!") that has made it a key text in scholarship on early Christian anti-Judaism.

## Historical evidence
**Eusebius HE 4.26** preserves substantial fragments of Melito's lost *Apology to Marcus Aurelius* and his important **list of the Old Testament canonical books** — the **earliest extant Christian list** of OT books (compiled by visit to Palestine to ascertain the Jewish canon, per Eusebius). **Polycrates of Ephesus** (~+190, in Eusebius HE 5.24) lists Melito as one of the great Quartodeciman authorities. The *Peri Pascha* was recovered in 1936 (Chester Beatty papyrus XII) — among the most important 20th-century early-Christian discoveries.

## Works
- ***Peri Pascha*** — extant; the great Quartodeciman paschal homily.
- ***Apology to Marcus Aurelius*** — lost; fragments in Eusebius.
- **OT canon list** — preserved in Eusebius HE 4.26.
- Various other titles (*Eclogae*, *On the Truth*, *On Faith*, etc.) — lost.

## Role in the tradition
- **Quartodeciman Christianity**: the leading figure of the Asian paschal tradition that survived into the 4th c. before being suppressed by the Council of Nicaea.
- **Early Christian anti-Judaism**: Melito's *Peri Pascha* is one of the most rhetorically violent early-Christian denunciations of "Israel" for the crucifixion — a critical document for the genealogy of Christian anti-Judaism (Lieu 1996).
- **OT canon-list pioneer**: his canon list is the earliest extant Christian one and matches the Jewish (Hebrew) canon except for the omission of Esther.

## Disputes
- **Date of *Peri Pascha*.** ~+160/+170; precise date debated.
- **Anti-Judaism interpretation.** Whether his rhetoric is "supersessionist" in the later sense or polemic-within-still-fluid Jewish-Christian boundaries (Lieu's reading) is the major modern scholarly question.
