# Augustine of Hippo

Latin Christian theologian (354–430), bishop of Hippo Regius in Roman North Africa from 396 until his death during the Vandal siege of Hippo. Augustine's intellectual trajectory passed through Manichaeism (~373–382, as a Hearer), Academic skepticism, Neoplatonism (via Marius Victorinus' Latin translation of Plotinus), and finally Catholic Christianity under Ambrose at Milan (baptized 387). His autobiographical *Confessions* (~397–401) and political-theological *City of God* (~413–426) are the two most influential Latin texts of late antiquity. His later anti-Pelagian writings systematize the doctrine of original sin and grace; his anti-Donatist writings the ecclesiology of the catholic church; his anti-Manichaean writings provide our best external evidence for late-antique Manichaean teaching. The Western theological tradition in both its Catholic and Reformation registers is unimaginable without him.
