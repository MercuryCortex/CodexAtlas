# Ralph Waldo Emerson

## Identity
Ralph Waldo Emerson (1803–1882) was an American essayist, lecturer, poet, and the central figure of New England **Transcendentalism**. A former Unitarian minister who left the pulpit in 1832, he became the most influential American thinker of the 19th century, articulating a religion of self-trust, direct intuition of the divine, and the immanence of one universal Spirit ("the Over-Soul") in nature and in every human mind. His essays "Nature" (1836), "Self-Reliance," and "The Over-Soul" (1841) reshaped American intellectual and spiritual culture.

## Historical evidence
Fully documented through his published essays, lectures, an extensive journal (16 volumes), and voluminous correspondence. His lecture circuit made him a public intellectual; his circle at Concord (Thoreau, Margaret Fuller, Bronson Alcott) is among the best-attested literary communities of the period.

## Thought
Emerson's core claim is that the individual soul is continuous with a single divine Spirit pervading all things — what he named the **Over-Soul**. Knowledge of the divine comes not through institutional authority or revelation mediated by scripture, but through immediate intuition. This grounds his ethics of "Self-Reliance" (trust the inward voice) and his reading of nature as a transparent symbol of spirit.

## Cross-tradition findings
- **Bhagavad Gita → Emerson's Over-Soul (documented East→West transmission).** Emerson read Charles Wilkins's 1785 English translation of the [[phase-2-027-bhagavad-gita|Bhagavad Gita]] in the 1820s–30s. The Gita's *ātman*–Brahman identity — the individual self as identical with the cosmic Self — became the bedrock of his Over-Soul doctrine, and his poem *Brahma* (1856) is a near-direct paraphrase of Gita 10:20 / 9:16 ("They reckon ill who leave me out; / When me they fly, I am the wings"). This is one of the founding events of the modern Western reception of Hindu scripture (Halbfass 1988; Versluis 1993). Wired as a `direct-borrowing` transmission edge.
- **Neoplatonic channel.** Emerson absorbed [[plato]] and [[plotinus]] partly through [[thomas-taylor-neoplatonist|Thomas Taylor's]] translations and through [[emanuel-swedenborg]] — making him a confluence point where Indic, Platonic, and Swedenborgian mysticism merge into American Transcendentalism.
