# Dogen

## Identity
Dōgen Zenji (1200–1253) was the founder of the Japanese Sōtō Zen school and one of the most original Buddhist philosophical voices in Japanese intellectual history. Born to aristocratic family in Kyoto; orphaned young; entered Tendai monasticism on Mount Hiei at age 13; troubled by the doctrinal problem of why practice is needed if Buddha-nature is already present; traveled to China 1223–1227 where he received Sōtō Caodong-school transmission from Tiantong Rujing; returned to Japan and founded the monasteries Kōshōhōrinji and Eiheiji (the latter remaining the Sōtō head temple). His doctrine of *shikantaza* (just sitting) — that zazen is itself enlightenment-conduct, not a means to enlightenment — and his sophisticated metaphysical-philosophical writing (the *Shōbōgenzō*'s ~95 fascicles) are foundational for Sōtō Zen and increasingly studied in modern philosophy.

## Works
*Shōbōgenzō* (Treasury of the True Dharma Eye, ~95 fascicles in Japanese — Dōgen's principal work, extraordinarily difficult Japanese prose); *Eihei Kōroku* (collected Chinese-style discourses and verses); *Fukan Zazengi* (Universal Recommendation of Zazen, early manifesto); *Tenzo Kyōkun* (Instructions for the Tenzo, on the monastic role of cook).

## Role in the tradition
Foundational for Sōtō Zen; via the *Shōbōgenzō*'s 20th-c. recovery (by Watsuji Tetsurō, then by Hee-Jin Kim, Tanahashi, and others), foundational also for modern Japanese philosophy (the Kyoto School) and Western philosophical engagement (Heidegger comparisons; Critical Buddhism debates).
