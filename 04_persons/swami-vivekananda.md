# Swami Vivekananda

## Identity
Bengali monk (1863–1902); principal disciple of Ramakrishna; first major Hindu teacher to address mass Western audiences. Delivered the famous "Sisters and Brothers of America" address at the World's Parliament of Religions in Chicago, 11 September 1893, marking the modern public emergence of Hindu philosophy in the West. Founded the Vedanta Society in New York (1894) and the Ramakrishna Mission in India (1897).

## Role in the tradition
The principal vector by which Advaita Vedanta and a universalist-Hindu position entered Western religious-philosophical discourse; the institutional founder of Hindu modernism's public face. Direct successor to [[ramakrishna]]; co-organizer (with [[sarada-devi]]) of the [[event-ramakrishna-mission-founded-1897|Ramakrishna Mission (founded 1897)]]; the principal figure of the [[event-parliament-of-world-religions-1893]].

## Cross-tradition connections
- **The 1893 Parliament of Religions** — Vivekananda's "Sisters and Brothers of America" address inaugurated the modern public emergence of Hindu philosophy in the West; the subsequent Vedanta Society of New York (1894) was the first Hindu institutional presence in the United States.
- **Vivekananda's *Raja Yoga* (1896)** brought [[patanjali-yoga-sutras-author|Patañjali]]'s [[phase-3-023-yoga-sutras-of-patanjali|Yoga Sutras]] into the modern global yoga discourse; Singleton 2010 has shown that modern global yoga's authority-text status for the Yoga Sutras is principally Vivekananda's mediation.
- **Hindu modernism's lineage from Brahmo Samaj precursors**: Vivekananda's universalist-Vedantic framing builds on [[ram-mohan-roy]]'s Brahmo Samaj synthesis and [[keshub-chandra-sen]]'s *Nava-Vidhāna* — though Vivekananda's specifically-Vedantic-Hindu (rather than Christian-Unitarian-Hindu syncretic) framing is institutionally distinct.
