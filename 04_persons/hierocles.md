# Hierocles of Bithynia (~250–310 CE)

Roman provincial governor + Neoplatonist polemicist; one of the principal pagan intellectual architects of the Diocletianic Persecution (303-311 CE). Served as governor (*praeses*) of Bithynia, then later of Egypt; both posts involved active prosecution of Christians during the persecution period.

## The *Philalethēs / Truthful Discourse* (~303 CE)

Hierocles's principal anti-Christian work — *Logoi Philalētheis pros tous Christianous* ("Truthful Words to the Christians") — is lost, but its argument survives via Eusebius of Caesarea's refutation *Contra Hieroclem* (~312 CE). Hierocles's central polemical move: argue that [[apollonius-of-tyana]] (the 1st-c. Neo-Pythagorean wonder-worker) was a pagan figure structurally parallel to Christ — same miraculous-cures, prophetic teachings, divine commission, persecution — but treated by Christians as a charlatan while Christ is treated as God incarnate. This is the *parallel-pagan-Christ* polemic in its earliest extant form. The structural argument anticipates the [[executed-divine-claimant]] cross-tradition pattern Justin Martyr had positively framed 150 years earlier (Socrates as Logos-Christian); Hierocles deploys the same structural parallel polemically rather than synthetically.

## Cross-tradition position

Hierocles sits at the late-3rd-c. peak of the [[third-century-collision-zone]] — Neoplatonism, Hermetism, reformed Roman state-religion, and emerging Christianity colliding institutionally. The *Philalethēs* is one of the four major anti-Christian works of the Diocletianic period (alongside [[porphyry]]'s *Against the Christians*, [[celsus]]'s *True Word*, and Julian's later *Against the Galileans*).
