# Joshua bin Nun

## Identity
Moses's assistant and successor; the figure to whom the Book of Joshua attributes the conquest of Canaan. His Hebrew name *Yehoshua* is the same name as that of Jesus (*Iēsous* in Greek = Yehoshua); the typological link is exploited by Hebrews 4:8 ("if Joshua had given them rest..."). In the Deuteronomistic structure he is paired with Moses as the wilderness-to-land hinge.

## Historical evidence
None extrabiblical. The archaeological record (Finkelstein, Dever, Mazar) consistently shows that the conquest-narrative of Joshua 6-12 does NOT correspond to a 13th-12th-c. BCE invasion-and-destruction event; the cities listed (Jericho, Ai, Hazor) show no consistent destruction-horizon datable to a single conquest moment. The mainstream scholarly position treats Joshua as a late-7th-c. (Josianic) or post-exilic theological construction of national-religious identity, projected onto a heroic conquest-figure.

## Role in the tradition
- **Deuteronomistic History.** Joshua's farewell address (Josh 24) is the rhetorical-theological climax of the conquest-and-settlement cycle.
- **New Testament.** Hebrews 4:1-11 deploys Joshua as a typological forerunner of Christ's rest (*katapausis*). Acts 7:45 (Stephen) names him in the wilderness-to-tabernacle narrative. Acts 13:19 sketches the conquest as backstory.
- **Islamic tradition.** Yusha' ibn Nun is named in *hadith* (and extracanonically) as Moses's young companion (the unnamed companion of Sura 18 — Moses and the servant-of-Khidr — is identified in tafsir as Joshua).
