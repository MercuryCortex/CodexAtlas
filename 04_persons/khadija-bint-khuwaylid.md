# Khadīja bint Khuwaylid

## Identity
**Khadīja bint Khuwaylid ibn Asad ibn ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā (~555–619 CE)** — known by the canonical Sunni-Shīʿī honorific *Umm al-Muʾminīn* (Mother of the Believers) — is the **first wife of Muhammad** and **the first Muslim convert** (the first person to accept Muhammad's prophethood after he received the initial revelation in the cave of Mount Ḥirāʾ c. 610). A Meccan widow of the Banu Asad ibn ʿAbd al-ʿUzzā clan of the Quraysh, Khadīja was a successful **merchant in the Meccan caravan trade**, twice widowed before her marriage to Muhammad. Per the canonical Sira-tradition (Ibn Ishaq), Khadīja's older Christian cousin [[hanif-monotheism|Waraqa ibn Nawfal]] (one of the named *ḥunafāʾ*) confirmed Muhammad's first revelation as authentic prophecy — establishing the **Christian-Arab → Muhammad recognition edge** in Islamic-tradition memory itself. The 25-year marriage (~595-619) was monogamous on Muhammad's side; **Khadīja was Muhammad's only wife during her lifetime**; all of Muhammad's surviving children except Ibrāhīm (born to Māriya the Copt, d. infancy) were Khadīja's. She died c. 619 in Mecca during the difficult late-Meccan period (the Quraysh boycott of the Banu Hāshim); her death plus Abū Ṭālib's death the same year marks the *ʿām al-ḥuzn* ("Year of Sorrow") and frames the immediate pre-Hegira context. Khadīja is venerated across all major Islamic traditions as the first Muslim and the *Umm al-Muʾminīn*; she has special importance in Shīʿī tradition as the mother of [[fatima-bint-muhammad|Fāṭima]] (whose marriage to Muhammad's cousin and son-in-law [[ali-ibn-abi-talib|ʿAlī]] founded the Ahl al-Bayt).

## Historical evidence
Documented in Islamic-tradition sources unanimously; the biographical details (her father's identity, her mother's identity, her prior marriages, her age at marriage to Muhammad, the number and order of her children) vary across early sources. The traditional account of Khadīja being 40 at the time of her marriage to a 25-year-old Muhammad is reported in Ibn Ishaq but is disputed in some early sources (some giving her age as 28 or 30); the figure 40 is the canonical Sunni-tradition number but the historicity is contested by modern critical scholarship.

## Works
- None — Khadīja is a person of historical action rather than authorship.

## Role in the tradition
- **First Muslim.** The first person to accept Muhammad's prophethood — affirmed by mainstream Sunni and Shīʿī tradition. Some Shīʿī traditions instead identify [[ali-ibn-abi-talib|ʿAlī]] as first male Muslim and Khadīja as first female / first overall.
- **Spousal foundation of early Islam.** Khadīja's wealth, social position in the Quraysh, and unwavering personal support of Muhammad during the early years of revelation are foundational to the early Meccan Muslim community's survival. The Sira-tradition (Bukhārī and Muslim) records Muhammad's continued grief over her death in his later years, including his refusal to marry until after her death and his consistent honoring of her memory in the presence of subsequent wives (especially Ḥafṣa and ʿĀʾisha).
- **Mother of [[fatima-bint-muhammad|Fāṭima]].** Through Fāṭima's marriage to [[ali-ibn-abi-talib|ʿAlī]], Khadīja is the grandmother of [[hasan-ibn-ali|al-Ḥasan]] and [[husayn-ibn-ali|al-Ḥusayn]] and thus the great-grandmother of the **Twelve Shīʿī Imams** (the Ahl al-Bayt genealogy descends through Khadīja → Fāṭima → al-Ḥasan / al-Ḥusayn → the subsequent Imams).
- **Waraqa-ibn-Nawfal cousin-link.** Waraqa ibn Nawfal, Khadīja's older paternal cousin, is one of the named [[hanif-monotheism|*ḥunafāʾ*]] in Islamic-tradition memory — a Christian Arab who recognized Muhammad's first revelation as authentic prophecy when Khadīja brought Muhammad to him after the Mount Ḥirāʾ revelation. This is the **canonical Christian-Arab → Muhammad recognition narrative** in the Sira tradition.

## Disputes
- **Khadīja's age at marriage.** Traditional figure: 40, vs. Muhammad at 25. Modern critical scholarship: probably younger (28-30), based on the number and ages of her children. The "40" figure may be a later Islamic-tradition narrative embellishment to emphasize her maturity and seniority.
- **Date of death.** Canonical: 619 CE (Year of Sorrow). Some sources give earlier or later; 619 is the standard.
- **Status as "first Muslim."** Sunni: yes, first overall. Shīʿī: ʿAlī is first (male / overall); Khadīja is first (female). Both consistent with her foundational role.
