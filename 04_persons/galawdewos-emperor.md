# Galawdewos (Emperor Claudius)

## Identity
**Galawdewos** ("Claudius"; r. 1540–1559) became negus on the death of his father Lebna Dengel at the height of the **Ahmad Gragn invasion** (1529–1543) that had reduced Ethiopian Christianity to its lowest historical point. With the assistance of the **Portuguese-Christian expedition of Cristóvão da Gama** (son of Vasco da Gama, landed 1541; killed 1542 fighting Gragn), Galawdewos defeated and killed Ahmad Gragn at the **Battle of Wayna Daga (1543)**, recovered the highland heartland, and began the slow rebuilding of Ethiopian Christianity. The war had destroyed enormous quantities of manuscripts, churches, and royal infrastructure; many monasteries were sacked; the Aksum cathedral was burned in 1535. Galawdewos's reign restored political continuity and inaugurated the difficult Ethiopian-Portuguese-Catholic engagement (which would culminate in the Jesuit mission and its 1632 expulsion under Fasilides).

His most enduring authored work is the ***Confessio Claudii*** (1555) — a Ge'ez apologetic responding to the Portuguese Catholic missionary critiques of Tewahedo Christianity. The text defends Tewahedo Christology (the Miaphysite *one united nature*), Sabbath-observance, Old Testament practices (including circumcision and dietary observance), and other distinctive Ethiopian practices — addressed to King John III of Portugal, but circulated widely. It is one of the most rhetorically and theologically careful Ge'ez polemics ever produced and is regularly cited in modern ecumenical theological discussions as a model statement of the Tewahedo position.

## Historical evidence
- **Royal Chronicle of Galawdewos** — Ge'ez prose chronicle; critical-edited Conzelman 1895.
- **Confessio Claudii** — surviving in many Ge'ez manuscripts; translated into Latin (early modern) and modern European languages.
- **Portuguese sources** — Miguel de Castanhoso's *Dos Feitos de D. Cristóvão da Gama* (1564), eyewitness account of the 1541–1543 campaign; later Pero Páez's *História da Etiópia*.
- **Material destruction** — independently corroborated by archaeology of mid-16th-c. Ethiopian sites; the cataclysmic loss of Ethiopian manuscript heritage in 1529–1543 is the largest single discontinuity in Ge'ez literary transmission.

## Works
- **Confessio Claudii** (1555) — Ge'ez apology. The text became a load-bearing reference for Ethiopian Orthodox self-definition vis-à-vis Catholic and (later) Protestant Christianity.

## Role in the tradition
Galawdewos is honored as the king who saved Christian Ethiopia from extinction. The post-Gragn rebuilding of the church was slow; many ancient manuscripts were never recovered (the loss of the pre-1529 Ethiopian library is comparable in scale to the loss of the Alexandrian library, though less culturally visible to Western historiography). His *Confessio* defines the Tewahedo position against the Catholic critique and prefigures the issues that would drive the Jesuit-period crisis (Susenyos's 1622 conversion to Catholicism and Fasilides's 1632 expulsion of the Jesuits).

## Disputes
- **Authorship of the Confessio.** Probably Galawdewos's own composition with theological-court assistance; the text is consistent in voice and the dating is secure.
- **Galawdewos's death.** Killed in battle 1559 against the Adal Sultanate (the same political-religious force that had produced Gragn); the immediate post-Galawdewos period sees renewed Adal pressure.
