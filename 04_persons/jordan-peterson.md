# Jordan B. Peterson

## Identity
Canadian clinical psychologist (b. 12 June 1962, Alberta). PhD Psychology, McGill 1991 (on alcoholism and aggression). Assistant Professor at Harvard 1993–1998; Professor of Psychology, University of Toronto 1998–2018 (active research on personality, especially the Big Five and a sub-trait Big-Five model). Reached very large public prominence from 2016 through a YouTube video critiquing Canadian Bill C-16 (compelled-speech argument concerning gender-identity protected categories); rose to global cultural-commentary celebrity 2017–2018 with the *12 Rules* book.

## Works
See texts-authored. *Maps of Meaning* (1999) is his major Jungian-evolutionary-psychological synthesis: religion and myth as cognitive adaptations encoding hard-won wisdom about the structure of the world (Order/Chaos, Father/Mother, Hero, Dragon) refined over deep evolutionary time. *12 Rules for Life* (2018) and *Beyond Order* (2021) are popularizations of that framework as advice. The Jewish-Bible and Genesis lecture series (YouTube, 2017–) and now the New Testament lecture series, plus *We Who Wrestle with God* (2024), apply Maps-of-Meaning readings to biblical narratives. Lectures and podcast appearances (Daily Wire from 2023) are a major part of his current output.

## Role in the field
Peterson sits at the intersection of: (a) genuine academic-psychology background (personality research) — Tier 2; (b) popular self-help — Tier 3; (c) culture-war public-intellectual commentary on gender, free speech, and Western civilization — Tier 4 (contested and politicized); (d) idiosyncratic Jungian-mythological readings of biblical texts — Tier 3/4. The vault flags his religion-and-myth work as **popular-controversial** rather than mainstream religious-studies scholarship.

- Cites [[iain-mcgilchrist]]'s hemispheric thesis as a scientific scaffold for his cultural-historical claims.
- Engages [[carl-gustav-jung]] / [[mircea-eliade]] / [[joseph-campbell]] / Northrop Frye reading of the Bible as archetypal-mythological corpus rather than (only) historical witness.

## Disputes
- **Biblical scholarship.** Specialist biblical scholars and religious-studies scholars have substantially criticized Peterson's biblical readings as (a) Jungian-psychologizing rather than historical-critical; (b) selectively combining motifs across traditions without philological discipline; (c) re-coding religious narratives as universal evolutionary-psychology lessons in ways that bypass each tradition's internal historical context. See Burns 2020 and the broader academic-review literature.
- **Public-intellectual controversy.** Peterson's interventions on gender identity (Bill C-16, 2016), university culture, climate change, and political-ideological controversies are highly politicized; his religious commentary is now read in that polarized frame from both sides.
- **Stability of personal voice 2020–.** A widely-discussed 2020 hospitalization and medical-detox episode (benzodiazepine-withdrawal) was followed by a more theological and increasingly explicitly Christian register in his public work; readers debate whether this represents an underlying continuity or a substantive shift.
- **Methodology.** Peterson's lectures and books mix clinical-psychology citation, mythological reading, evolutionary-psychology speculation, and biblical-narrative commentary in ways that no single academic discipline (psychology, religious studies, biblical studies) accepts as methodologically rigorous in its terms.
