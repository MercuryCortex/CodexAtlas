# Hemiunu

## The architect of the Great Pyramid

**Hemiunu** (~2570–2530 BCE) is the probable chief architect of the **[[great-pyramid-of-khufu|Great Pyramid of Khufu]]** — the most technically ambitious construction project in human history. He held the title **"Overseer of All Royal Works"** (*imy-r kȝt nbt nt nswt*) and was **Khufu's nephew** (son of Prince Nefermaat, himself a son of Sneferu).

The identification of Hemiunu as the Great Pyramid's architect is based on:
- His mastaba tomb **G4000** sits in the western cemetery at Giza, adjacent to the Great Pyramid — the burial site of the highest-ranking officials of Khufu's court
- His title "Overseer of All Royal Works" is the exact administrative position responsible for royal construction projects
- A **large seated limestone statue** of Hemiunu was found in his mastaba by the Pelizaeus Expedition (1912) — now in the Roemer-Pelizaeus Museum, Hildesheim. The statue shows a heavyset man in administrative dress; it is among the finest Old Kingdom portrait statues and the primary image of the man who built the largest monument in the ancient world

No contemporary document names Hemiunu as the pyramid's architect — this is an inference from his title and tomb location, and is accepted by virtually all Egyptologists.

## The construction problem

Hemiunu inherited the accumulated knowledge of two generations of pyramid-building failures and successes under his grandfather Sneferu:
- **Meidum pyramid**: outer casing collapsed (probably while under construction or shortly after)
- **Bent Pyramid**: angle changed mid-construction; internal chambers cracked and were packed with emergency cedar buttressing
- **Red Pyramid**: first successful true smooth-sided pyramid at 43° — the prototype

Hemiunu's innovation was to increase the angle to **51°50'** (steeper, more imposing) while engineering all the structural solutions to prevent the failures seen at Dahshur:
- The **Ascending Passage** (punched upward through the original Descending Passage ceiling) to allow an upper burial chamber
- The **Grand Gallery's corbelled vault** to create a large interior space without collapse risk
- The **five relieving chambers** above the King's Chamber to distribute the ~5,000 tonne load
- The **coffer placed before the walls were built** — it could not fit through the Ascending Passage afterward

The resulting structure has stood for 4,500 years. No ancient stone structure anywhere in the world has survived more completely.

## What we don't know

Almost nothing about Hemiunu survives beyond his titles, tomb, and statue. We have no text in his voice, no description of the construction process from his perspective, and no inventory of his design decisions. The entire planning and engineering intelligence behind the Great Pyramid is archaeological inference.

This is the central irony of the Great Pyramid: the most impressive engineering achievement of the ancient world was designed by someone whose personal record is essentially blank.
