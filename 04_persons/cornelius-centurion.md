# Cornelius the Centurion

## Identity
A centurion of the Italian Cohort stationed at Caesarea Maritima, described by Acts 10 as a *theosebēs* ("God-fearer" — Gentile sympathizer with Judaism) who, after a vision, sent for Peter from Joppa. Peter's preaching to Cornelius's household and the consequent descent of the Holy Spirit on uncircumcised Gentiles is, in Luke's theology, the *programmatic* moment of Gentile inclusion in the Christian community.

## Historical evidence
Single-source (Lukan); Luke is shaping the Gentile-mission origin story around this episode for theological reasons. The historical-critical question is whether the Cornelius episode reflects a *specific* historical first-conversion or is a literary template for a broader development. Acts 11:1–18 has Peter narrating the event to Jerusalem critics — reinforcing its programmatic role.

## Works
None. Later tradition makes him the first bishop of Caesarea (in competition with [[zacchaeus]] in other traditions).

## Role in the tradition
The biblical *prototype Gentile convert*. Theologically crucial in Lukan ecclesiology as the precedent for the [[paul-of-tarsus|Pauline]] Gentile mission and the [[event-council-of-jerusalem-c-49|Jerusalem Council]] decision.

## Disputes
- **Historicity.** Critical scholarship reads the Cornelius episode as Lukan literary-theological construction with possible historical kernel.
- **Theological priority of Peter.** Acts gives Peter the *first* Gentile conversion in tension with Paul's Galatians narrative which makes Peter Judaizing.
