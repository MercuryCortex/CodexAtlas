# Theophilus of Antioch

## Identity
Sixth bishop of Antioch in Syria (per Eusebius's succession), author of *Ad Autolycum* — three books addressed to a learned pagan friend Autolycus, presenting Christianity as both philosophically defensible and biblically grounded. Theophilus is theologically significant for two reasons: (a) **the first attested use of the Greek term *Trias* ("Triad") of God, Word, and Wisdom** (Ad Autol. 2.15) — a vocabulary precursor of later trinitarian theology; (b) his careful treatment of **Christianity as the original, primordial wisdom** that the Greeks plagiarized from Moses — an apologetic move that shapes [[clement-of-alexandria|Clement]] and [[origen]].

## Historical evidence
*Ad Autolycum* is securely extant. Eusebius gives the chronology; Jerome the brief biography. His lost anti-heretical and anti-Marcionite treatises are mentioned by Eusebius but not preserved.

## Works
- ***Ad Autolycum*** (3 books) — apologetic with substantial biblical-chronological argumentation; the chronology section is influential in setting Christian world-chronology genres.
- *Against the heresy of Hermogenes* (lost)
- *Against Marcion* (lost)
- Gospel commentaries (lost)

## Role in the tradition
- **First *Trias* terminology**: an important early step in trinitarian linguistic development.
- **Antiquity-of-Christianity argument**: Theophilus contends that Greek philosophy and mythology are derivative of much older Hebrew wisdom — a move that hands [[clement-of-alexandria|Clement]] and [[origen]] the tools for treating Greek philosophy as preparation for Christianity.
- **Antiochene exegetical tradition**: forerunner of the later Antiochene school's preference for literal-historical reading (though Theophilus himself uses allegory at points).

## Disputes
- **Date of *Ad Autolycum*.** Likely ~+177/+185 — composed during or after Commodus's accession.
- **Lost works' content.** *Against Hermogenes* especially is mourned for what it might tell us about [[hermogenes-the-painter|Hermogenes]]'s actual positions.
