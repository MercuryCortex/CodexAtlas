# Reza Aslan

## Identity
Iranian-American writer (b. 3 May 1972, Tehran); Shia Muslim by background, with a documented conversion to evangelical Christianity in adolescence and a subsequent return to Islam. MA Harvard Divinity (2000); MFA Iowa Writers' Workshop (2003); PhD Sociology of Religions, UC Santa Barbara (2009, on global jihadism). Associate Professor of Creative Writing at UC Riverside.

## Works
See texts-authored. *No god but God* (2005) is a widely-used popular Islam-history book reaching general American readers. *Zealot* (2013) is his most-discussed work — a popular historical-Jesus reconstruction presenting Jesus as a zealous Jewish nationalist whose Kingdom-of-God preaching is a political-revolutionary program in continuity with the earlier-1st-century anti-Roman Jewish resistance.

## Role in the field
Aslan's primary contribution is as a *popularizer* who brings Anglophone non-specialist readers into contact with religious-studies questions. He is not a specialist in New Testament studies in particular; his historical-Jesus reconstruction is in the older "Jesus the Zealot" lineage (S.G.F. Brandon 1967, *Jesus and the Zealots*) that mainstream NT scholarship has generally moved away from. The book's enormous trade success and the controversial 2013 Fox News interview (in which an interviewer questioned a Muslim writing about Jesus) gave *Zealot* a public profile out of proportion to its specialist reception.

## Connections
- Continues the popular-historical-Jesus genre vector alongside [[bart-ehrman]], [[reza-aslan]]'s less specialist methodology — Aslan represents the trade-press end of the historical-Jesus market.
- His Islamic-religious-history writing parallels but is more journalistic than academic Islamic studies (cf. Karen Armstrong's similar genre, [[karen-armstrong]]).

## Disputes
- **Specialist NT reception.** Mainstream NT scholarship (Dale Allison, Maurice Casey, Anthony Le Donne, Greg Carey, et al.) has criticized *Zealot* on multiple grounds: anachronistic political-revolutionary projection, selective use of Josephus and Tacitus, neglect of the Q and pre-Pauline traditions, dismissal of the *Realgeschichte* of historical-Jesus methodology. The book's revival of the Brandon-zealot thesis is widely regarded as historiographically retrograde.
- **Disciplinary credentialing.** Aslan was widely (and inaccurately) described in promotional contexts as "PhD in religion" / "historian of religions"; his doctorate is in sociology of religion and his expertise is on contemporary global jihadism, not first-century Judaism. This was the substantive criticism the 2013 Fox News interview attempted (badly) to make.
