# Aristides of Athens

## Identity
One of the **two earliest known Christian apologists** (with [[quadratus-the-apologist|Quadratus]]). Eusebius reports that Aristides addressed an apology to Hadrian; the surviving Syriac manuscript heads the text "To Antoninus Pius" — the discrepancy is a long-standing puzzle, possibly resolved if the work was redirected to the new emperor after Hadrian's death (138). The text is a defense of Christian monotheism and ethical practice through a contrast with Greek, Egyptian, and Jewish religion — concluding that Christians alone have rightly known God.

## Historical evidence
The work was considered lost until **1889**, when J. Rendel Harris identified a Syriac version in a Sinai manuscript and recognized that the Greek philosophical romance ***Barlaam and Josaphat*** (an Indian-Buddhist tale Christianized through Manichaean and Christian transmission) preserves the Apology embedded as a speech in its narrative. The Greek fragment and the Syriac version largely agree, with notable variants. An Armenian fragment was also identified.

## Works
- ***Apology***: organized around the four "races" (barbarians, Greeks, Jews, Christians), evaluating each by its theology. The Greek and Syriac versions show some restructuring.

## Role in the tradition
- **Earliest extant Christian apology** (with Quadratus's fragment): a foundational moment in the Christian-apologetic genre.
- **Transmission curiosity**: preserved by being embedded in *Barlaam and Josaphat*, a hagiographic romance with Indian-Buddhist origins — one of the most striking East-West literary-transmission stories in early Christian literature.
- **Apologetic theology**: argues from "natural theology" (incorruptibility, omnipotence, oneness of God) to Christian monotheism, characteristic of the philosophical-apologetic stream.

## Disputes
- **Date and addressee.** Hadrian or Antoninus Pius — manuscript and Eusebian evidence diverge.
- **Greek text reconstruction.** The *Barlaam and Josaphat* version represents an adapted text; the Syriac is closer to original.
