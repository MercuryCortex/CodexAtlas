# Rabbi Akiva ben Joseph

## Identity
Major Tannaitic rabbi (c. 50–135 CE), one of the towering figures of early rabbinic Judaism. Traditionally said to have begun Torah study at age 40, became one of the most influential teachers of the Yavneh-Bnei-Brak generation; his hermeneutical method (every textual feature — including particles, repetitions, plene/defective spellings — carries halakhic significance) shaped the developing rabbinic system; his students (including Rabbi Meir and the editors of the Mishnah's substrate) carried forward the Akivan tradition. Hailed Bar Kokhba as the messiah (a position later regretted by the tradition after the revolt's failure); martyred under Hadrian during or just after the suppression of the revolt, traditionally said to have died reciting the Shema as his flesh was raked by iron combs.

## Historical evidence
Substantial: extensive Mishnaic and Talmudic references; martyr narrative preserved in *Berakhot* 61b among others; Hadrianic-period chronology fits the historical record of the revolt.

## Role in the tradition
- **Mishnah**: Akiva is the single most-cited Tannaitic authority; the editing of the Mishnah's substrate is traditionally attributed to his school.
- **Hermeneutics**: the "Akivan" approach to Scripture (maximally exploiting textual features) is contrasted with Rabbi Ishmael's more restrained approach.
- **Mysticism**: traditionally a participant in early *merkavah*-mystical speculation; *Hagigah* 14b narrates the "four who entered the Pardes" with Akiva as the only one to enter and exit in peace.
- **Martyrology**: the *Eleh Ezkerah* liturgy of Yom Kippur counts him among the Ten Martyrs of the Kingdom.

## Disputes
- Historicity of biographical traditions (especially the late-start narrative).
- Bar Kokhba endorsement: ancient tradition, modern question about extent.
