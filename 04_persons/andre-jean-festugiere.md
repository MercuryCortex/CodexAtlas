# André-Jean Festugière

## Identity
French Dominican priest, classical philologist, and historian of late-antique religion and philosophy; member of the Saulchoir / Le Saulchoir Dominican studium; lifelong scholar of the Hermetica and of pagan religious philosophy. Author of *La révélation d'Hermès Trismégiste* (4 vols., 1944–1954), the foundational scholarly synthesis on the Hermetic corpus, and co-editor with A.D. Nock of the Budé critical edition of the Greek Hermetica (1945–1954). The two together — Festugière's *Révélation* and the Nock-Festugière Budé text — define modern Hermetic studies.

## Historical evidence
Documented Dominican career; ordained 1930; long publication record at Lecoffre/Gabalda and Les Belles Lettres. Cited 5+ times across the Codex Atlas Hermetic node-cluster.

## Works
- *La révélation d'Hermès Trismégiste* (Gabalda, 4 vols., 1944–1954). The Hermetic study.
- (with A.D. Nock) *Hermès Trismégiste: Corpus Hermeticum* (Budé, 4 vols., 1945–1954). The critical Greek edition.
- *Personal Religion Among the Greeks* (UC Press 1954). The Sather Lectures.
- *Hermétisme et mystique païenne* (Aubier-Montaigne 1967).
- *Études de philosophie grecque* (Vrin 1971).
- (translator) *Vie de Plotin* of Porphyry; *De Mysteriis* of Iamblichus.

## Role in the tradition
The conceptual vocabulary of contemporary Hermetic studies is Festugière's. The "optimist gnosis" / "pessimist gnosis" distinction, the typology of "popular" vs. "philosophical" Hermetica, the careful separation of the Hermetic *eusebeia* (cosmos-affirming piety) from the Gnostic anti-cosmic posture — all are Festugière's contributions. Subsequent scholarship (Fowden 1986, Mahé 1978–82, van den Broek) modifies but does not replace him.

## Originator of
- [[hermetic-piety]] — Festugière is the principal modern articulator of *eusebeia* as the central Hermetic religious orientation, distinct from Gnostic *gnōsis*.

## Disputes
- Festugière weighted the Hermetica strongly toward Greek-philosophical (Middle Platonist) origins; Fowden 1986 corrected this toward an Egyptian-priestly substrate. Mahé 1978–82's recovery of the Coptic and Armenian Hermetica also corrected the more purely-philosophical reading.
- His "optimist" / "pessimist" Hermetic-gnosis bifurcation is sometimes criticized as too neat (e.g., van den Broek 2013). The distinction is still widely used but with caveats.
