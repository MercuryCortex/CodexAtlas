# Basavanna

## Identity
Basavanna (1131–1168) was a Kannada poet-saint, philosopher, and social reformer who founded the **Lingāyat** (Vīraśaiva) movement in 12th-century Karnataka. He served as *daṇḍanāyaka* (treasurer/prime minister) at the court of the Kalachuri king Bijjala of Kalyani before his movement's social radicalism caused a crisis leading to his departure and death. His *vachanas* ("sayings" — free-verse Kannada devotional poems) are among the greatest works of Kannada literature and articulate a theology of radical interiority that makes him one of the most structurally interesting figures for cross-tradition comparison.

## Core doctrine
Basavanna's central teaching:

> "The rich will make temples for Śiva. What shall I, a poor man, do? My legs are pillars, the body the shrine, the head a cupola of gold. Listen, O lord of the meeting rivers, things standing shall fall, but the moving ever shall stay."

This doctrine — the body as temple, Śiva dwelling within rather than in external stone — is the Lingāyat rejection of temple religion and caste hierarchy. It parallels:
- **Kashmir Shaivism's Pratyabhijñā**: Śiva is already within, recognition is the path
- **Meister Eckhart's Seelenfünklein**: the spark of the soul is already identical with the Godhead; churches and sacraments are secondary to this inner recognition

## The Basavanna / Eckhart parallel — a literature gap
The structural isomorphism between Basavanna and Meister Eckhart is striking and has not been documented in published scholarship:

| Feature | Basavanna (1131–1168, Karnataka) | Meister Eckhart (1260–1328, Rhineland) |
|---|---|---|
| Vernacular language | Kannada *vachanas* | Middle High German sermons |
| Body-as-temple | "My legs are pillars, the body the shrine" | Soul's spark already IS the Godhead |
| Rejection of physical temple/ritual | Rejected brahmin priests and caste | Condemned churches as obstacles |
| Inner divine recognition | Śiva already within | Breakthrough (*Durchbruch*) to Godhead within |
| Institutional critique | Attacked caste hierarchy; condemned by king | Condemned by Pope John XXII |
| Community of equals | Anubhava Maṇṭapa (Experience Hall, all castes) | Women and laypeople as primary audience |

Rudolf Otto (1926) compared Eckhart with Śaṅkara; the Basavanna parallel is arguably tighter. No scholar has published this comparison — it is a genuine comparative religion literature gap. Both figures are within the vault.

## The Anubhava Mantapa
Basavanna's "Experience Hall" (*anubhava maṇṭapa*) in Kalyani was a revolutionary institution: a discussion forum open to people of all castes where theological and social questions were debated. This is the first documented egalitarian philosophical community in Indian history, predating the European salon tradition by centuries.
