# Tutankhamun

## Identity
**Tutankhamun** (~-1341 to ~-1323) was an Egyptian pharaoh of the late 18th Dynasty, who came to the throne as a child (~9 years old) shortly after the death of [[akhenaten]] and the end of the Amarna religious revolution. He was born **Tutankhaten** ("Living Image of Aten") and changed his name to **Tutankhamun** ("Living Image of Amun") to mark the restoration of the traditional Egyptian polytheism after Akhenaten's monotheistic Atenist experiment. His reign was short (~9 years) and largely managed by senior officials (Ay; Horemheb). His historical significance is dwarfed by the **archaeological significance** of his tomb: KV62 in the Valley of the Kings, discovered by Howard Carter in November 1922, was the most-intact royal tomb ever found in Egypt — the only Egyptian pharaonic burial recovered with its grave-goods substantially intact.

## Role in the tradition
- **Amarna-aftermath ruler.** Tutankhamun's reign marks the formal restoration of the Amun cult after Akhenaten's Atenist revolution; the *Restoration Stela* (Karnak) documents his (or Ay's) program to reverse the closure of the traditional temples. See [[event-amarna-period-1353-1336]].
- **The golden canopic shrine.** Tutankhamun's tomb contains a **gilded canopic shrine** flanked on four sides by free-standing statues of the four canopic protectresses [[isis]], [[nephthys]], [[neith]], and [[selket]]. The Selket statue on the west of the shrine is the **single most-recognised iconographic image** of the Egyptian scorpion-goddess; she stands ~90 cm tall, with the scorpion crisply modelled atop her tripartite wig, arms outstretched in the protective canopic-shrine pose. (Eaton-Krauss & Graefe 1985 *Tutankhamun's Tomb Series* vol. 2.)
- **Archaeological touchstone.** The 1922 discovery [[event-tutankhamun-tomb-discovery-1922]] reshaped 20th-century popular understanding of Egyptian religion and iconography; the Selket-statue is among the most-photographed cult-images in world archaeology.
