# Atrahasis (the flood-hero)

## Identity
**Atrahasis** (Akkadian *Atra-Hasis*, "exceedingly wise") is the **Akkadian renaming of the Sumerian flood-hero [[ziusudra]]** — the same legendary antediluvian king of Shuruppak, the same narrative role (warned by Enki/Ea, builds the boat, survives, sacrifices afterward), but in the **Akkadian-language Atrahasis Epic** (~-1800 BCE, Old Babylonian period) rather than the Sumerian Eridu Genesis.

This is the *person* node — distinct from the document node [[phase-1-006-atrahasis]] (the *epic* named for him). The Atrahasis Epic itself is a more theologically-ambitious work than the Sumerian Eridu Genesis: it frames the flood as the gods' final solution to humanity's *noise* (a poetic euphemism for human over-population pressure on divine sleep), preceded by three lesser plagues (drought, famine, pestilence), each successively partial. The flood comes as the cosmic-final attempt at population-control; Enki secretly warns Atrahasis, who survives, and after the flood the divine order is restructured to permit human mortality, infant mortality, and reproductive limits — the *theodicy* dimension of the Akkadian recension is more developed than the Sumerian original.

## Historical evidence

- **The Akkadian Atrahasis Epic** ([[phase-1-006-atrahasis]]) — Old Babylonian tablets from Sippar and elsewhere (~-1800); the Lambert + Millard 1969 critical edition is standard.
- **Gilgamesh XI interpolation** — George 2003 argues the Standard Babylonian Gilgamesh Epic's flood-tablet (XI) is interpolated from the Atrahasis tradition, with the flood-hero renamed [[utnapishtim]].
- **Relationship to Ziusudra.** Atrahasis = Ziusudra in narrative function; the two names belong to two successive Mesopotamian literary traditions (Sumerian → Akkadian) telling the same story.

## Role in the tradition

Atrahasis sits at the **central position** in the Mesopotamian flood-tradition chain:

1. [[ziusudra]] (Sumerian, ~-2100 oral / -1600 ms) — original Eridu Genesis.
2. **Atrahasis (Akkadian, ~-1800)** — Old Babylonian theodicy-elaboration.
3. [[utnapishtim]] (Standard Babylonian, ~-1300) — Gilgamesh XI integration.
4. [[noah]] (Hebrew, J ~-950, P ~-550) — Israelite-Hebrew recension.

The *Atrahasis* recension is the literarily-most-developed of the three Mesopotamian versions and is the principal source for the *theological* features that distinguish Mesopotamian flood from later versions (the noise / over-population motive; the three preliminary plagues; the post-flood reproductive-limits etiology).

## Disputes

- **Identity with Ziusudra and Utnapishtim.** Scholarly consensus treats the three as the same legendary figure across three successive Mesopotamian recensions (George 2003; Lambert and Millard 1969).
- **Date of the Old Babylonian Atrahasis composition.** Most scholars place it ~-1800; tablet-witnesses range -1700 to -1600.
