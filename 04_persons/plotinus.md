# Plotinus

Founder of Neoplatonism. Born in Lycopolis in Egypt ~205; studied under Ammonius Saccas in Alexandria (~232–243); accompanied Emperor Gordian III's failed Persian expedition (intending to study Persian and Indian philosophy); after Gordian's assassination fled to Antioch and ultimately Rome (~245), where he taught for the next quarter-century until his death in 270. His pupil Porphyry collected and organized the 54 surviving treatises into the *Enneads* and prefaced them with the *Life of Plotinus* — one of our richest ancient philosophical biographies.

Plotinus's metaphysics articulates a triadic descent — the One → Nous → Soul → cosmos — that becomes the canonical framework of late-antique Platonism and shapes Christian apophatic theology via Augustine, Pseudo-Dionysius, and the Cappadocians. His Ennead II.9 "Against the Gnostics" is a polemical engagement with what we can now identify as specific Sethian Gnostic texts ([[phase-4-057-allogenes|Allogenes]], [[phase-4-056-zostrianos|Zostrianos]] — preserved at Nag Hammadi). [[porphyry|Porphyry]]'s *Vita Plotini* 16 records the documentary detail: Plotinus had his students refute these texts directly, Porphyry himself writing a forty-book refutation of *Zostrianos* and Amelius writing against *Allogenes*. This is the cleanest documented Christian-Gnostic / Platonist-philosophical confrontation in late antiquity (Turner 2001, Burns 2014). Plotinus reports four mystical unions (henōsis) with the One during his lifetime.
