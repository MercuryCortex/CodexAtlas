# Ali al-Hadi

## Identity
Son of [[muhammad-al-jawad]]; born in Medina ~828. Succeeded his father as **Tenth Imam** of the Twelver line in 835 (at age 6 or 7 — the second of the late-Twelver child-Imams). Lived in Medina until ~848, when the anti-Shi'i Caliph al-Mutawakkil (r. 847–861) ordered his forced relocation to the Abbasid military garrison city of Samarra (north of Baghdad), where he and his son [[hasan-al-askari]] lived effectively under house-arrest for the rest of their lives. Died in Samarra 868 (poisoned, per Shi'i tradition; the persecution under al-Mutawakkil was severe and the political circumstances make foul play plausible). Buried in Samarra; his shrine, alongside that of his son, is one of the four major Iraqi Shi'i pilgrimage centers (Najaf, Karbala, Kazimayn, Samarra) — the Samarra shrine famously bombed by Sunni-extremist militants in 2006, an event of major consequence for Iraqi Shi'i–Sunni relations.

## Historical evidence
Documented in Shi'i hadith collections and in the historiographical record of the al-Mutawakkil and al-Mu'tazz reigns. The forced relocation to Samarra and the conditions of surveillance are noted across sources.

## Role in the tradition

### The Samarra period and the *safīr* / *wakīl* network
Al-Hadi's forced residence in Samarra under continuous Abbasid surveillance meant that the Imam was, for practical purposes, *unreachable* by most of the dispersed Imami community. The *wukalā'* (deputy / agent) network — already developed under his predecessors — became the substantive operational structure of the community: deputies in Kufa, Qom, Baghdad, Khurasan, Yemen, etc. handled the collection of religious dues (*khums*), the resolution of legal questions, and the doctrinal-pastoral concerns of local Imami populations, with limited and mediated access to the Imam himself. This institutional pattern — the *wakil-network as the real Imami religion* — is what survives the eventual occultation and becomes the foundation of the post-occultation Twelver community: the post-941 *general deputyship* of the Imami *ʿulamāʾ* (jurists) is a continuous evolution from the late-9th-c. *wakāla* system. The path to [[event-iranian-revolution-1979]] *vilāyat-i faqīh* (guardianship of the jurist) doctrine begins, structurally, here.

### Doctrinal articulation
Al-Hadi's period sees the continued elaboration of Imami doctrine — esoteric (*bāṭin*) hermeneutics, eschatology (with increasing focus on *raj'a* — the return of the Imams at the end of days, and on the coming *Mahdī*), and ritual practice. The *Ziyarat al-Jami'a al-Kabira* (a major Shi'i pilgrimage-prayer attributed to al-Hadi) gives the fullest doctrinal statement of the *ʿiṣma* (infallibility) and cosmic role of the Imams in any single Shi'i text.

## Disputes
- **Cause of death.** Standard Shi'i tradition (poisoned by orders of Caliph al-Mu'tazz). The al-Mutawakkil and post-al-Mutawakkil Abbasid regimes were politically hostile to the Imams; the historical probability of foul play is real but not securely demonstrable for the specific death.

- **Authenticity of *Ziyarat al-Jami'a*.** The Twelver tradition holds it as authentically by al-Hadi; critical assessment is more cautious about specific phrasing, but the text reflects a late-9th-c. doctrinal articulation broadly consistent with the period.

## Cross-tradition connections
- ↔ [[muhammad-al-jawad]] (father), [[hasan-al-askari]] (son, Eleventh Imam) — the Twelver line under intensifying Abbasid pressure.
- ↔ [[tradition-shia-islam]] — Samarra period is the institutional bridge to the occultation era.
- ↔ The *wakāla* network → eventual *vilāyat-i faqīh* (Khomeini) — long-term institutional trajectory.
