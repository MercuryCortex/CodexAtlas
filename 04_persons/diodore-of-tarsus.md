# Diodore of Tarsus

## Identity
Antiochene presbyter and ascetic; bishop of Tarsus from 378. Founder (with Flavian) of the *asketerion* at Antioch — the influential school that trained John Chrysostom and Theodore of Mopsuestia. Champion of Nicene orthodoxy through the Arian conflicts; designated by Theodosius I (380) as one of the canonical bishops by whose communion catholic faith was to be measured in the East. Present at the Council of Constantinople (381).

## Historical evidence
Most of Diodore's enormous corpus was destroyed after his views were retroactively condemned along with Theodore at and after 553. Survivals: a substantial Greek catena on the Psalms (Hill 2005 translates Psalms 1–51), Syriac fragments, citations in opponents (Cyril of Alexandria's *Against Diodore and Theodore*, surviving only in fragments).

## Works
- *Commentary on the Psalms* (partially recovered from Greek catenae and Syriac).
- Genesis commentary (fragments).
- Anti-Apollinarian and anti-Eunomian dogmatic treatises (lost).
- An astronomical/calendrical work (lost; mentioned by later sources).

## Role in the tradition
Founder of the Antiochene exegetical tradition. Honored in his own lifetime as a Nicene champion; posthumously suspect after Theodore and Nestorius were condemned. Cyril of Alexandria wrote *Against Diodore and Theodore* — the title alone shows the line of suspicion. The Church of the East preserves him with honor.

## Connections — substrate and influence
- Founded the methodological commitment to *historia* (the historical-literal sense) against allegorical exegesis. O'Keefe 1996 shows how Antiochene *theoria* (the higher meaning) is still anchored to the literal — methodologically Aristotelian in contrast to Alexandrian Platonism.
- His ascetic school at Antioch is part of the broader 4th-c. monastic explosion that takes its impetus from Egypt ([[antony-the-great]], [[pachomius]]) and that he domesticates into an urban-clerical formation pattern.

## Disputes
- **Diodore's actual Christology.** Whether the *Nestorian* readings of Diodore later attached to him are fair to his original position is unrecoverable without the lost dogmatic works.
