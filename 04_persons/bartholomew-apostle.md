# Bartholomew the Apostle

## Identity
"Bar-Talmai" (Aramaic: "son of Talmai") is a patronymic rather than a personal name, suggesting the historical figure had a first name not recorded in the Synoptic apostle-lists. The traditional identification, going back at least to the 9th-century Byzantine commentators but already implied earlier, equates Bartholomew with Nathanael of Cana in the Fourth Gospel — based on (a) Bartholomew always being paired with Philip in the Synoptic lists, and (b) Nathanael appearing alongside Philip in Jn 1.

## Historical evidence
A name on all four Synoptic + Acts apostle-lists; nothing narrative attached. Pantaenus's late-2nd-century India mission, reported by Eusebius (*HE* 5.10), finds a Bartholomew tradition in "India" (= probably Yemen/South Arabia in ancient geography) — the earliest extra-NT mention of Bartholomew. Later traditions multiply: Armenia (where he is venerated as co-founder of the Armenian Apostolic Church with [[thaddaeus-jude-apostle]]), Mesopotamia, India proper, martyrdom by flaying alive (the visual icon of Michelangelo's Sistine *Last Judgment*) at Albanopolis (Armenia).

## Works
None. The *Gospel/Questions of Bartholomew* (4th–5th c., Greek/Coptic/Slavic) is pseudepigraphic, a post-resurrection dialogue between the risen Jesus and the apostle. The *Book of the Resurrection of Christ by Bartholomew* (Coptic) is an even later apocryphon.

## Role in the tradition
Founder-apostle of the **Armenian Apostolic Church** (with Thaddaeus-Jude). The flaying-martyrdom iconography is medieval. In Coptic tradition he is connected to a Christianized version of Egyptian iconography.

## Disputes
- **Identification with Nathanael.** Traditional but not made in the Gospels themselves; the silence is genuinely puzzling if true.
- **India vs. Armenia mission.** Both traditions claim him; "India" in ancient sources often means South Arabia or Ethiopia, complicating the data.
- **Martyrdom by flaying.** Late-medieval iconography; earlier sources have him crucified.
