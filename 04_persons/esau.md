# Esau

## Identity
Firstborn twin of Jacob, son of Isaac and Rebekah; eponymous ancestor of the Edomites who occupied the Seir highlands south of the Dead Sea. He sells his birthright to Jacob for a bowl of red stew (Gen 25:29-34 — the etymological pun on *adom* / red / Edom), is tricked out of his father's blessing by Jacob (Gen 27), and ultimately reconciles with Jacob at the Jabbok crossing (Gen 33). The Esau-figure encodes the historic Israel-Edom enmity in a sibling-rivalry frame.

## Historical evidence
The Edomite kingdom is historically secure (Iron-Age archaeology of Buseirah/Bozrah, Tawilan; Assyrian and Egyptian inscriptions). The Esau-figure as the eponymous Edomite ancestor is a literary projection of the historical Israel-Edom relationship onto patriarchal narrative.

## Role in the tradition
- **Hebrew Bible.** The Esau-Jacob narrative carries the load-bearing Israelite theology of divine election unhinged from primogeniture. Obadiah's entire short prophetic book is an anti-Edom oracle. Malachi 1:2-3 ("Jacob have I loved, but Esau have I hated") becomes the prooftext.
- **New Testament.** Romans 9:13 quotes Malachi 1:2-3 as the proof of God's sovereign election. Hebrews 11:20 (Isaac's blessing) and Hebrews 12:16 (Esau as "profane" / *bebēlos*, selling his birthright for a single meal) deploy him as a negative typological foil.
