# Huike (487–593)

Second patriarch of Chan Buddhism and disciple of [[bodhidharma]]. According to the Chan tradition, Huike received the transmission of the [[phase-4-103-lankavatara-sutra]] from Bodhidharma as the sole basis of Chan teaching — establishing the early Chan school's identity as "Laṅkāvatāra masters" (*Lengqie-shi* 楞伽師). The historicity of the Bodhidharma-Huike encounter is contested (Faure 1986 reconstructs it as largely legendary Chan self-narrative), but Huike's role as the second patriarch is a foundational element of Chan/Zen lineage mythology. The story of Huike cutting off his arm in the snow to demonstrate sincerity before Bodhidharma accepted him is among the most famous anecdotes in East Asian Buddhism.
