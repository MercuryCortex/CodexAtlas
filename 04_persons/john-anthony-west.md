# John Anthony West

## Identity
John Anthony West (1932–2018) was an American author and independent researcher best known for **popularizing heterodox theories about ancient Egypt** — above all the claim that the Great Sphinx is far older than the conventional 4th-Dynasty date. He brought the "symbolist" interpretation of Egyptologist R. A. Schwaller de Lubicz to a wide audience in *Serpent in the Sky* (1979) and championed the geologist [[robert-schoch|Robert Schoch's]] water-erosion hypothesis for re-dating the Sphinx.

> **Source-integrity note (T4).** West's central claims are **heterodox and rejected by mainstream Egyptology and most academic geologists**. This node is catalogued for completeness in the history of ideas and because West is named as a participant in the Atlas's Sphinx and Great Pyramid event nodes. The contested claims are flagged below; the mainstream consensus is carried alongside.

## The water-erosion / older-Sphinx hypothesis
West argued that the deep vertical weathering on the Sphinx enclosure walls was caused by prolonged **rainfall**, not wind-blown sand — which would push the monument's carving back to a wetter climatic period millennia before Khafre, possibly 7000+ BCE. He partnered with [[robert-schoch]], who presented the geological case at a 1992 Geological Society of America meeting and in a famous 1991 debate.

## The mainstream response
Egyptologists (Mark Lehner, Zahi Hawass) and geologists (James Harrell, K. Lal Gauri) rejected the redating, attributing the erosion to other mechanisms (salt weathering, haloclasty, exfoliation of the soft Member II limestone) and noting the overwhelming archaeological context tying the Sphinx to Khafre's 4th-Dynasty pyramid complex (~2500 BCE). The redating is **not accepted** in the field.

## Cross-tradition findings
- **Lost-civilization / forgotten-wisdom discourse.** West's work belongs to the modern "alternative Egyptology" current (Schwaller de Lubicz, later Hancock, Bauval) that posits a lost high civilization behind Egypt's monuments. The Atlas catalogues this as a documented **history-of-ideas phenomenon** — a recurring modern myth of antediluvian wisdom — explicitly tiered T3/T4 and balanced against the mainstream consensus. It is not a claim the Atlas endorses.
