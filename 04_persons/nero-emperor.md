# Nero

## Identity
Fifth Roman emperor (54–68 CE), last of the Julio-Claudians. Adopted by the emperor Claudius (his great-uncle and stepfather), elevated to the purple at age 16, deposed and suicided at 30. Famous for political and personal extravagance, the matricide of Agrippina the Younger, and — most consequentially for Christian memory — the **scapegoating of the Christian community for the Great Fire of Rome in July 64**, leading to the first known Roman persecution of Christians.

## Historical evidence
Strong: [[tacitus]] (*Annales* 15.44 — written ~+115/+120, fifty years after the events but using both archival and oral sources), [[suetonius]] (*Nero* 16.2), and indirect Christian sources (1 Clement, Eusebius). Tacitus's narrative — Nero needed someone to blame for the fire rumors, fastened on the unpopular Christians, executed many in cruel spectacles — is treated as substantially reliable by mainstream Roman-history scholarship.

## Works
None — though personally a poet and musician (the lyre-playing during the fire is a Suetonian and Dio Cassius elaboration).

## Role in the tradition
- **The First Persecutor**: Nero becomes the type of the demonic emperor in Christian memory.
- **Revelation's *Beast 666***: the mainstream critical interpretation reads the *Beast whose number is 666* (Rev 13:18) as **Neron Caesar** in Hebrew letter-numerology (NRWN QSR = 50+200+6+50+100+60+200 = 666), with the variant 616 in some manuscripts corresponding to the Latin *Nero Caesar* (without the final *n*). This identification is widely accepted.
- **Death of Peter and Paul**: traditional martyrdom during Nero's reign (~+64/+67) — historically plausible but not securely documented in surviving sources.
- **Nero redivivus**: 1st-c. expectation that Nero would return from the east — both pagan and Christian apocalyptic strands use the motif; the Sibylline Oracles 4–5 and Revelation both engage it.

## Disputes
- **Scale of the Neronian persecution**: Moss (2013) argues that subsequent Christian tradition exaggerated; Tacitus's account is reliable for Rome itself but the persecution did not extend through the Empire.
- **Date of Peter and Paul's deaths**: traditional ~+64/+67 fits the Nero-persecution frame but is not securely datable.
- **666 vs. 616 manuscript variants**: clinches the Nero-Caesar identification (the Latin transliteration *Nero* without final *n* gives 616; the Hebrew/Greek transliteration with final *n* gives 666).
