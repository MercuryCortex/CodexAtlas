# Abraham

## Identity
The founding patriarch of biblical Israel (Genesis 12–25) — called from Ur by YHWH, given the covenant promise of land and descendants, the father of Isaac and (through Hagar) Ishmael. The Abrahamic narrative is foundational for Judaism's self-understanding as a covenant-people; for Christianity as the prefiguration of faith ([[gentile-inclusion]], Rom 4 and Gal 3 trace gentile inclusion to Abraham's pre-circumcision righteousness by faith); for Islam (where Ibrāhīm is the *ḥanīf* monotheist before Judaism or Christianity, builder of the Ka'ba with Ishmael).

## Historical evidence
No extra-biblical attestation. The 19th–20th-c. "patriarchal age" reconstructions (Albright, de Vaux) tried to anchor Abraham in 2nd-millennium-BCE evidence (Mari, Nuzi customs); this is now largely abandoned. The contemporary mainstream view is that the patriarchal narratives were composed/redacted in the Iron Age (10th c. BCE forward) and project foundational legends back. Whether any historical kernel survives is unrecoverable.

## Role in the tradition
- Judaism: *Avraham Avinu* — "our father Abraham"; the founding covenant; the Akedah (binding of Isaac) is a paradigmatic event.
- Christianity: justification-by-faith proof-text (Paul); spiritual father of Gentile believers.
- Islam: *Ibrāhīm Khalīl Allāh* ("Friend of God"); the *millat Ibrāhīm* ("religion of Abraham") as the original *ḥanīf* monotheism; pilgrimage rites at the Ka'ba commemorate Abraham and Ishmael.

## Disputes
- Historicity (now largely accepted as legendary in critical scholarship).
- The Sarah / Hagar / Ishmael / Isaac inheritance question — fraught in Jewish-Muslim-Christian comparative exegesis.
- The Akedah's theological-ethical reading (Kierkegaard's *Fear and Trembling*; modern Jewish reception).
