# Khufu (Cheops)

## The pharaoh behind the largest monument

**Khufu** (~2589–2566 BCE) was the second pharaoh of Egypt's 4th Dynasty, son of **Sneferu** (builder of the Bent and Red pyramids), and the builder of the **[[great-pyramid-of-khufu|Great Pyramid of Giza]]** — the largest stone structure in the ancient world. He ruled for approximately 23 years.

The supreme paradox of Egyptian archaeology: the man responsible for the most impressive surviving monument from the ancient world is represented by only **one known portrait** — a **7.5 cm ivory figurine** found at Abydos in 1903 by Flinders Petrie. It is the smallest surviving depiction of any Egyptian pharaoh, and its identification as Khufu is secure only because the excavator found it with a cartouche. A pharaoh who built a monument 146 metres tall is known only through a figurine smaller than a hand.

## What is actually known

**Sources:** Almost nothing contemporary survives about Khufu's reign beyond:
- The **Great Pyramid** itself (including the Khufu cartouche in Campbell's Chamber, painted by workers)
- The **inventory stele** at Giza (New Kingdom; claims Khufu found the Sphinx already standing — chronologically impossible if it was his son's work; treated with skepticism)
- **Herodotus** (~450 BCE, *Histories* II): describes Khufu as a tyrant who closed the temples, forced 100,000 workers to build his pyramid in three-month shifts, and prostituted his own daughter to raise funds. Herodotus was writing 2,100 years after Khufu's death based on what Egyptian priests told tourists. The workforce description is plausible (rotating teams confirmed by archaeology); the other claims are probably propaganda.
- **The Papyri of Wadi el-Jarf** (discovered 2013): the world's oldest papyri (~2560 BCE), recording the work of a team delivering limestone from Tura to Giza under an official named Merer. The most direct documentary evidence of the pyramid's construction process.

**The workers' village (Heit el-Ghurab):** Mark Lehner's excavations revealed a permanent settlement of craftsmen with rations, medical care (evidence of bone surgeries), and honorable burial. **Not slave labor.** The workforce was organized as a royal enterprise with status and compensation.

## Khufu's family

- **Father:** Sneferu (4th Dynasty founder; the most prolific pyramid builder by volume)
- **Mother:** Hetepheres I (her intact tomb shaft — miraculously undiscovered by robbers — found at Giza 1925; contained gilded furniture and canopic jars, but the canopic jars were empty and no body was found)
- **Son:** **[[khafre|Khafre]]** (builder of the Second Pyramid and probable Sphinx commissioner)
- **Nephew:** **Hemiunu** (vizier; "Overseer of All Royal Works"; probable architect of the Great Pyramid)

## The construction mystery

How the Great Pyramid was built — specifically, how 2.3–2.6 million stones averaging 2.5 tonnes (with granite blocks up to 80 tonnes) were placed at heights up to 146 m — remains the most debated construction question in archaeology. Current leading hypotheses:
- **External ramp(s):** One or more straight or spiral ramps for hauling blocks — requires enormous ramp volume (possibly equal to the pyramid itself at upper stages)
- **Internal spiral ramp (Houdin hypothesis):** Straight external ramp for the lower 2/3; internal helical ramp (recently supported by microgravimetry data) for the upper portion
- **ScanPyramids Big Void (2017):** The muon tomography discovery of a large void (~30m+) above the Grand Gallery may be related to the internal ramp system or an undiscovered chamber

The Wadi el-Jarf papyri confirm that **Tura limestone was delivered by boat** along a canal to the Giza site — resolving the transport question for the casing stones, if not the internal granite.
