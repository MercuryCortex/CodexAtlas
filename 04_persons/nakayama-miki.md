# Nakayama Miki

**Nakayama Miki** (中山みき, 1798–1887) — venerated in Tenrikyo as *Oyasama* ("Beloved Parent") — is the founder of [[tradition-tenrikyo|Tenrikyo]] and author-scribe of its primary scripture, the [[phase-7-043-ofudesaki|Ofudesaki]]. A peasant woman from Yamato Province (modern Nara Prefecture), she reported in 1838 that *Oyagami* ("God the Parent") descended into her as a living divine shrine, commanding her to serve as the vessel through which God would reveal the path to the *Joyous Life*. Over the following decades she dispensed faith healings, composed 1,711 waka verses as divine dictation (Ofudesaki), and attracted thousands of followers despite persecution by the Japanese government.

Nakayama Miki is one of history's clearest cases of a woman founding a major enduring religion through direct divine revelation — alongside Ann Lee (Shakers), Mary Baker Eddy (Christian Science), and the Bahai prophetess Tahirih. All arose within roughly 60 years of each other (1770–1838) on different continents, all by the same pattern: female divine authorization overcoming patriarchal social structures.
