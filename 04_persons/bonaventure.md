# Bonaventure

## Identity
Bonaventure (1221–1274) was the supreme medieval Franciscan theologian and the contemporary scholastic counterpart and friend of Aquinas — both incepted as masters of theology at Paris in 1257; both died in 1274. Born Giovanni di Fidanza near Viterbo; entered the Franciscans young; studied at Paris under Alexander of Hales; appointed Minister General of the Franciscan order at age 36 (1257), holding the position for 17 years through the turbulent crisis between Spirituals and Conventuals. Created cardinal in 1273; died at the Council of Lyon, July 1274. The Franciscan tradition he shaped emphasizes Augustinian-Neoplatonic theology, illuminationist epistemology, and Christo-centric mystical theology against Aquinas's Aristotelianism.

## Works
*Sentences commentary* (Paris years); *Itinerarium Mentis in Deum* (foundational mystical-theological treatise, 1259); *Breviloquium* (theological textbook); *Lignum Vitae*; *Legenda Maior S. Francisci* (the official Bonaventurean biography of Francis, made authoritative and earlier biographies suppressed); homilies; *Collationes in Hexaemeron* (lectures on the six days of creation, lectured 1273, his final great work).

## Role in the tradition
Foundational for Franciscan theology; declared Doctor of the Church 1588. The Franciscan-Augustinian alternative to Thomistic Aristotelianism is institutionalized in Bonaventure and his successors (Duns Scotus, Ockham — though these last diverged substantially from Bonaventure's specific synthesis).
