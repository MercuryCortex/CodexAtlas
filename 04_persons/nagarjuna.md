# Nāgārjuna

## Identity
2nd–3rd-c. CE South Indian Buddhist philosopher, founder of the Madhyamaka (Middle Way) school — arguably the most influential single thinker in the development of Mahāyāna Buddhism after the Buddha himself. Nāgārjuna's central project: the systematic philosophical articulation of *śūnyatā* (emptiness) as the absence of *svabhāva* (inherent existence) in all phenomena, including emptiness itself. The *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* (MMK) is the foundational text, twenty-seven chapters of dialectical analysis demonstrating the incoherence of any account of phenomena that posits inherent existence — including time, motion, the self, causation, the Tathāgata, and nirvana itself.

## Historical evidence
Dated c. 150–250 CE on the basis of Chinese-translation chronology and internal references. The figure has substantial legendary accretion (longevity attribution; the *Suhṛllekha* addressed to a "king Satavahana"; the legend of his retrieving the *Prajñāpāramitā* sutras from the nāgas in their underwater realm); the philosophical works are nonetheless securely his.

## Works
- *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* — the foundational text.
- *Vigrahavyāvartanī* — defense against the objection that the emptiness-thesis is self-refuting.
- *Śūnyatāsaptati* — seventy verses on emptiness.
- *Ratnāvalī* — ethical-political-religious instruction to a king.
- *Suhṛllekha* — letter to a king; popular spiritual instruction.
- *Pratītyasamutpādakārikā*, *Yuktiṣaṣṭikā* — shorter philosophical works.

## Role in the tradition
- **Madhyamaka**: founder; the school continued through Āryadeva (his disciple), Buddhapālita and Bhāvaviveka (the *Prāsaṅgika*-*Svātantrika* dispute), Candrakīrti (definitive Prāsaṅgika exponent), and Śāntideva.
- **Tibetan Buddhism**: Madhyamaka becomes the dominant philosophical school; Tsongkhapa's 14th-c. systematization is monumental.
- **East Asia**: Sanlun school in China; profound influence on Chan/Zen via the *Heart Sutra* and *Diamond Sutra*.

## Disputes
- Authentic vs. attributed corpus (the so-called "second Nāgārjuna" — the tantric/alchemical Nāgārjuna of later legend — is generally distinguished).
- The interpretation of *śūnyatā*: nihilism (rejected by the school itself) vs. anti-foundationalist relational pluralism (Garfield) vs. apophatic realism.
