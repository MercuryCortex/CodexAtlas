# John the Evangelist

## Identity
The figure to whom the Fourth Gospel and the Johannine epistles are attributed. The traditional identification (from Irenaeus, ~+180) makes him **John son of Zebedee**, one of Jesus's twelve disciples, identical with the "Beloved Disciple" of the Gospel itself, who lived to old age in Ephesus and authored both the Gospel and Revelation. Modern critical scholarship considers this identification problematic.

## Historical evidence
The synoptic-Gospel John son of Zebedee is well-attested as one of the twelve disciples (inner circle: Peter, James, John). Whether *that* John authored the Fourth Gospel is the disputed question. Papias (~+125, preserved in Eusebius *HE* 3.39.4) distinguishes two Johns: **John the Apostle** and **John the Elder** — both at Ephesus. Bauckham (2006) builds his case on this Papian distinction: John the Elder, distinct from but contemporary with John son of Zebedee, may be the actual Beloved Disciple and author. Brown's classic reconstruction (1979) posits a Johannine school with a founding Beloved Disciple, generations of community development, and final redaction by ~+100/+110. The Gospel itself names the Beloved Disciple as bearing witness and writing (21:24), but does not name him.

For this vault: hold authorship as **disputed**; treat the Gospel as a Johannine-community product with a charismatic-tradition-bearer ancestor; the work itself is securely datable ~+90/+110.
