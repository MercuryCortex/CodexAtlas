# D.T. Suzuki

## Identity
Japanese scholar (1870–1966); the principal 20th-century vector by which Zen Buddhism entered Western intellectual and popular consciousness. Studied with Shaku Soyen at Engaku-ji; collaborated with Paul Carus at Open Court Publishing in La Salle, Illinois (1897–1909); subsequent academic career in Japan and frequent visits and lecture series in the US and UK. Influential on midcentury American intellectuals (Thomas Merton, Erich Fromm, Alan Watts, the Beats).

## Disputes
- **Wartime nationalism**: Brian Victoria's *Zen at War* (1997) and subsequent work document Suzuki's wartime writings expressing nationalist-militarist positions; the scholarly debate over the extent and interpretation of this material is active. Sharf 1995 *The Zen of Japanese Nationalism* is the foundational critical framing.
