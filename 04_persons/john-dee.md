# John Dee

## Identity
English mathematician, astronomer, astrologer, cartographer, Hermetic-Kabbalist magus, and adviser to Elizabeth I (1527–1609). Educated at Cambridge (St John's, then a founding fellow of Trinity at its 1546 founding); studied at Louvain (1547–1551) with Gemma Frisius and Gerardus Mercator, absorbing the Continental Agrippa-Trithemius synthesis; returned to England to become Elizabeth's unofficial scientific and astrological adviser, cartographer for Crown geographical-imperial projects (he coined "British Empire"), and possessor of the largest private library in England (~4000 volumes at Mortlake). Author of the *Monas Hieroglyphica* (1564), preface to Henry Billingsley's English Euclid (1570), and navigational treatises. From 1581 Dee turned increasingly to angelic communication, working with the medium Edward Kelley to record (in the Enochian language Kelley reported the angels dictating) a substantial visionary corpus. Their 1583–1589 continental tour took them to the Polish court of Stefan Báthory and the Bohemian court of Rudolf II at Prague — a period Yates (1972) identifies as central to the genesis of the Rosicrucian impulse. Dee returned to England impoverished; was named Warden of Christ's College, Manchester (1595–1605); died in poverty at Mortlake. His Mortlake library was looted during the continental absence; his papers were partly preserved by Elias Ashmole.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own substantial publications and manuscripts (diaries, library lists, the Enochian materials), royal and continental court records, contemporary references.

## Works
- *Monas Hieroglyphica* (1564).
- *Mathematicall Praeface* to Billingsley's Euclid (1570).
- *General and Rare Memorials Pertayning to the Perfect Arte of Navigation* (1577).
- The angelic-conversation diaries: *Mysteriorum Libri Quinque* (1581–1583), and the continental and post-continental conversation books (1583–1607).

## Role in the tradition
Dee is the principal English figure of Renaissance Hermetic-Kabbalist magic, a serious mathematician-natural philosopher (Clulee 1988 argues against the older art-vs.-science split), and via the Yates thesis the proximate source of the German Rosicrucian impulse. The Enochian angelic system has had a continuous esoteric afterlife in the Golden Dawn and modern occultism. His Mortlake library is one of the most-studied private libraries of the period.
