# Simon the Zealot

## Identity
One of the Twelve, distinguished by the epithet *zēlōtēs* (Luke/Acts) or its Aramaic equivalent *Qan'ana* (Mark/Matthew — both meaning "zealous/zealot"). The epithet likely identifies him with the **Jewish liberation movement** later crystallized as the Zealot party — though Josephus dates the formal Zealot movement to the 60s CE, "zealots" as a category of pious anti-Roman activists existed earlier (Phinehas-style zeal for the Torah). Whether Simon was a *political* zealot or merely a *religiously zealous* Jew is contested.

## Historical evidence
Name-only on apostle-lists; no narrative episode. The presence of a "Zealot" alongside [[matthew-apostle]] the tax-collector (a Roman collaborator) in the same disciple group is striking and frequently cited as evidence of the *diversity* of Jesus's followers.

## Works
None. Late legends place him in Egypt, Persia, or Britain; none have early attestation.

## Role in the tradition
A standard placeholder in the apostolic college. Brandon's controversial 1967 thesis — that Jesus himself led a politically Zealot-leaning movement — used Simon's epithet as one piece of evidence; Hengel and most subsequent scholarship reject Brandon's reconstruction.

## Disputes
- **Political vs. religious zealot.** The epithet's meaning is debated; the formal Zealot party post-dates Jesus's ministry.
- **Mission field.** Late traditions disagree (Persia, Egypt, Britain — none historically grounded).
