# Barnabas

## Identity
Joseph the Levite of Cyprus, nicknamed by the apostles **Barnabas** ("son of encouragement," per Lukan etymology Acts 4:36 — though the Aramaic etymology is debated). The pivot figure of the *Antiochene* mission and the *first missionary journey*. Paul's senior partner before the partnership broke over John Mark in Acts 15:36–41.

## Historical evidence
Strong (Acts + Pauline letters). Gal 2 confirms his presence at the Jerusalem Council and his subsequent troubled return to "Judaizing" at Antioch (Gal 2:13). 1 Cor 9:6 attests his ongoing missionary status. His Cypriot origin and Levite status fit early-Christian demographic patterns.

## Works
None genuine. The **Epistle of Barnabas** (~+100/+130) is a strongly anti-Jewish allegorical-exegetical treatise pseudonymously attributed to him; it was nearly canonical in the 4th c. (included in Codex Sinaiticus). Tertullian attributed Hebrews to him. The *Acts of Barnabas* is a late apocryphon.

## Role in the tradition
**Cypriot national apostle**: Barnabas is to Cyprus what Thomas is to India — the founding evangelist, traditionally martyred at Salamis ~+61. **Apostolic Fathers corpus**: the pseudonymous Epistle of Barnabas is one of its key documents. **Pauline biography**: the figure without whom Paul's Christian career might not have begun (Acts 9:27 — Barnabas sponsored Paul's introduction to the suspicious Jerusalem community).

## Disputes
- **Authorship of Hebrews.** Tertullian's attribution; not mainstream.
- **Authorship of the Epistle of Barnabas.** Universally pseudonymous in modern criticism.
- **Cause of the split with Paul.** Acts: dispute over John Mark. Galatians 2:13: Barnabas was "led astray" by the circumcision faction at Antioch. The two explanations may both be partial.
