# Paul of Samosata

## Identity
Bishop of Antioch ~+260, deposed by a Syrian synod in 268 for **Adoptionist (Dynamic Monarchian) Christology** — the position that Jesus was a *man indwelt by the divine Logos* rather than the eternal Logos enfleshed. Paul's case is enmeshed with the political situation: he held a paid civil post (*ducenarius*) under **Queen Zenobia of Palmyra** (~+267/+272), and remained in possession of the Antiochene cathedral until **Aurelian's reconquest of Antioch in 272** — at which point Aurelian (still pagan) was asked to adjudicate the ecclesiastical succession and ruled in favor of those holding communion with the bishop of Rome, becoming **the first imperial decision on a Christian ecclesiastical question**.

## Historical evidence
**Eusebius HE 7.27–30** preserves the *Synodical Letter* of 268 — extensive, polemical, but quoting Paul. Athanasius and the Cappadocians use "Samosatene" as a stock anti-Christological slur. **Millar 1971** unpacks the political-cultural context: Antioch under Zenobia, the importance of patronage, Aurelian's adjudication.

## Works
Lost. Some fragments in the Synodical Letter and Athanasius.

## Role in the tradition
- **Dynamic-monarchian / Adoptionist Christology**: Jesus as the Spirit-anointed man, the Logos as God's *power* dwelling in him — sharply distinguished from the Nicene "of the same substance" position 60 years later.
- **The Aurelian decision (272)**: the first imperial adjudication of a Christian ecclesiastical-doctrinal matter, foreshadowing Constantine.
- **Genealogy of Antiochene Christology**: Paul's distinguishing of the Logos from the man Jesus is read by some scholars as the deep root of the later Antiochene-school (Theodore of Mopsuestia, Nestorius) tradition — though the Antiochene-school theologians repudiate Paul.

## Disputes
- **Exact Christology**: was it Adoptionist (Jesus a mere man until baptismal indwelling) or merely Dyophysite-leaning (Logos and man as ontologically distinct)? The hostile sources do not allow a confident reconstruction.
- **Role of Zenobia**: whether Paul was theologically protected by the Palmyrene queen or merely a beneficiary of her patronage system is debated.
- **Genealogy**: whether his teacher [[lucian-of-antioch]] — and Lucian's student Arius — derive their subordinationist Christology from Paul is one of the most-debated lines in 4th-c. heresiology.
