# Ramanuja

## Identity
Rāmānujācārya (~1017–1137 traditional; some 1077–1157) was the founder of the Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta philosophical school and the architect of Śrī Vaiṣṇavism as a systematic theological tradition. Born Śrīperumbudūr near Chennai; broke with the Advaita teacher Yādavaprakāśa over interpretive matters; succeeded the recently deceased Yāmuna (an unmet teacher) as head of the Śrīraṅgam Vaiṣṇava sampradāya; exiled briefly to Hoysala Karnataka where he is credited with converting the king Bittideva to Vaiṣṇavism. The traditional 120-year lifespan is hagiographic but the historical kernel is well attested.

## Works
*Śrībhāṣya* (Brahmasūtra commentary — his major systematic work); *Gītā-Bhāṣya*; *Vedārtha-Saṅgraha* (summary of Vedāntic doctrine); *Vedānta-Dīpa*, *Vedānta-Sāra* (shorter Vedānta works); three *gadyas* — *Śaraṇāgati-gadya*, *Śrīraṅga-gadya*, *Vaikuṇṭha-gadya* (devotional surrender prose-poems).

## Role in the tradition
Foundational architect of theistic Vedānta. Śrī Vaiṣṇavism is one of the two largest contemporary Hindu communities (alongside Śaiva traditions). Influenced Madhva (as polemic target); influenced Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Chaitanya), Vallabha's Śuddhādvaita, and many later bhakti traditions.
