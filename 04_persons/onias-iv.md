# Onias IV

## Identity
Son of **[[onias-iii]]**, last legitimate Zadokite high priest of Jerusalem. After his father's murder (~170 BCE) and the subsequent Maccabean revolt and Hasmonean political consolidation, Onias IV fled to **Ptolemaic Egypt** and successfully petitioned Ptolemy VI Philometor (~162–150 BCE) for permission to build a Jewish temple at **Leontopolis** (Tell el-Yahudiyya) in the Nile delta's Heliopolis nome.

## Historical Evidence
Primarily attested in **Josephus** (*Antiquities* XII.387–388; XIII.62–73; *Jewish War* VII.423–436). Josephus gives two slightly different accounts, suggesting the information came through multiple sources. The temple's physical existence is confirmed archaeologically — excavations at Tell el-Yahudiyya have recovered relevant remains. Josephus quotes Onias IV's letter to Ptolemy VI, invoking Isaiah 19:19 ("In that day there will be an altar to the Lord in the midst of Egypt") as prophetic sanction for the Egyptian temple.

## The Temple of Leontopolis
Onias IV obtained land and permission, converted an existing Egyptian temple site, and built a Jewish temple modeled on — but smaller than — Jerusalem's Temple. The temple operated for **approximately 220 years** (~150 BCE to 73 CE), when the Romans ordered its closure following the First Jewish War, concerned that it could become a focus for further Jewish revolt.

**Why this is extraordinary:**
- The **only Jewish temple outside Jerusalem** to operate for an extended period in antiquity (the earlier Elephantine Temple, ~5th c. BCE, is the other significant case, and predates this)
- Staffed by **Zadokite priests** — the Oniad priestly line that had been displaced from Jerusalem
- Located near **Alexandria** — within the most intellectually productive Jewish diaspora community of antiquity, the milieu that produced the Septuagint, Philo, and the earliest proto-Hermetic synthesis
- The Oniad priests at Leontopolis and the Alexandrian Jewish intellectuals almost certainly knew each other; whether there was doctrinal or ritual cross-pollination is debated but the geographical and social proximity is undeniable

## Significance for the Vault
The Leontopolis temple represents the **Zadokite priestly tradition attempting to reproduce the Jerusalem cult in exile** — the same impulse, differently expressed, as the Qumran community's desert purity practice. Both are responses to the same crisis (Hasmonean illegitimacy). But where Qumran went inward and apocalyptic, Leontopolis went institutional and international.

The temple's proximity to Alexandria also makes it a possible bridge between the Zadokite-priestly tradition and the Alexandrian synthesis of Jewish, Greek, and Egyptian thought that eventually produced Hermeticism and Neoplatonism. The link is speculative but the physical proximity and temporal overlap are facts.
