# Antiochus IV Epiphanes

## Identity
Seleucid king (reigned 175–164 BCE), son of Antiochus III the Great. The pivotal villain of the Maccabean crisis: in 167 BCE he banned Jewish religious observance in Judaea (Sabbath, circumcision, dietary law), looted the Jerusalem Temple, and (most consequentially) erected an altar to Olympian Zeus in the Temple — the "abomination of desolation" of Daniel 11:31 — and ordered sacrifice of swine. The Maccabean revolt (167–164 BCE) under Judas Maccabeus and his brothers recovered the temple in 164 BCE; the rededication is celebrated annually as Hanukkah. Antiochus died on campaign in Persia in 164 BCE before learning of the temple's recovery.

## Historical evidence
Substantial: Seleucid official records (numismatic, Babylonian astronomical diaries); the books of 1 and 2 Maccabees (1st c. BCE Hellenistic-Jewish historiography); Polybius's Greek history; Daniel's apocalyptic figured-historiography (Dan 7–12 the bulk of which scholarly consensus dates to the Maccabean crisis).

## Role in the tradition
- Daniel's eschatological villain — the "little horn" of Dan 7, the "king of the north" of Dan 11; the four-kingdoms schema (see [[four-kingdoms-schema]]) culminates in him.
- 4 Maccabees develops the Maccabean martyrdoms (Eleazar, the mother and her seven sons) as paradigmatic [[righteous-suffering-vindication]] / [[martyrdom-theology]] / [[vicarious-atonement]] cases.
- Christian apocalyptic typology: Antiochus is read as a *type* of the eschatological Antichrist (cf. Rev 13 and the Beast tradition).

## Disputes
- Motivations: religious zealot, Hellenizing modernizer, or pragmatist responding to Jewish factional politics? Modern scholarship (Bickerman, Goldstein, Gruen) has worked through the question without complete resolution.
