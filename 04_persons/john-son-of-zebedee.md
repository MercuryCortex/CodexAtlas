# John, son of Zebedee

## Identity
John (Yochanan), younger son of Zebedee, brother of [[james-son-of-zebedee]], Galilean fisherman called by Jesus to be one of the Twelve. In the Synoptics he is part of the inner three with Peter and his brother. In Acts and Galatians he appears as one of the "pillars" (*styloi*) of the Jerusalem community (Gal 2:9). Distinct, in critical scholarship, from [[john-evangelist]] (the implied author of the Fourth Gospel) — though traditional Christianity has long identified the two.

## Historical evidence
The Galilean disciple John is securely attested in the Synoptics and Paul (Gal 2:9). The later traditions — exile to Patmos, residence at Ephesus, authorship of the Johannine literature — are 2nd-century-onward accretions that critical scholarship distributes among multiple Johns. **Papias of Hierapolis** (~+125, preserved in Eusebius *HE* 3.39) appears to distinguish two Johns: "John the apostle" and "John the Elder" (*ho presbyteros*). Modern scholarship is divided on whether the Fourth Gospel, the three Epistles, and Revelation derive from one, two, or three Johns. Brown (1979) reconstructs a "Johannine community" tradition centered on the *Beloved Disciple* whose identity remained anonymous and was *later* identified with John son of Zebedee.

## Works
None directly. The Johannine corpus is attached to him by 2nd-century tradition (Irenaeus, AH 3.1.1, *via Polycarp*). See [[john-evangelist]] for the corpus discussion.

## Role in the tradition
The most theologically elevated apostle in later tradition: identified as the Beloved Disciple, author of the high-Christology Gospel, eyewitness of the Apocalypse, "Theologian" par excellence in Greek tradition (one of the three figures called *ho theologos*). Polycarp claimed to have heard him at Ephesus (per Irenaeus); via Polycarp → Irenaeus → Western Christianity inherits a chain of memory.

## Disputes
- **Two-Johns problem.** Papias's "John the Elder" vs. "John the apostle" has been a fault-line since Dionysius of Alexandria (~+250, in Eusebius *HE* 7.25) used it to dissociate Revelation from the apostle. Modern critical scholarship (Bauckham, Hengel, Hill on the conservative side; Ehrman, Brown, Culpepper on the critical side) reaches no consensus.
- **Ephesus tradition.** Strongly attested from Irenaeus onward; not independently corroborated.
- **Identification with the Beloved Disciple.** Not made in the Gospel itself; first explicit in Irenaeus.
