# Lucian of Antioch

## Identity
A scholar-scribe of Antioch active in the late 3rd century, head of an Antiochene scriptural-school. Two distinct historical claims attach to him: (a) he produced a **critical recension of the Septuagint** (the "Lucianic / Antiochene text" — the form of the Greek OT that became dominant in the Byzantine world); (b) he was **the teacher of Arius** (and of [[eusebius-of-caesarea|Eusebius]] of Nicomedia, Maris of Chalcedon, Theognis of Nicaea, and other prominent 4th-c. subordinationists), making him the grandfather of the Arian controversy. Martyred at Nicomedia ~+312 under Diocletian-Galerius. Sharply to be distinguished from [[lucian-of-samosata]] (the 2nd-c. pagan satirist).

## Historical evidence
Eusebius preserves his martyrdom; Jerome (*De viris* 77) summarizes his scholarly work. The Lucianic recension is widely attested in patristic and Byzantine biblical citation traditions.

## Works
- **Septuagint recension**: a critical edition smoothing the Old Greek text, principally on stylistic and contextual grounds. Surviving manuscripts of the "Antiochene" Septuagint text-form reflect his work.
- **NT recension** (possible): the *Byzantine* or *Majority* text-form may stem from his work, though this is contested.

## Role in the tradition
- **The "Lucianist" school**: the group of his students became the principal carriers of subordinationist (eventually Arian) Christology in the early 4th century. Eusebius of Nicomedia — the Arian-leaning bishop closest to Constantine — was a "co-Lucianist" with Arius (per Arius's own letter, in Theodoret HE 1.5).
- **Septuagint textual critic**: Lucian's recension is one of the three principal patristic recensions of the LXX (with Origen's *Hexapla* and Hesychius's Alexandrian edition).
- **Martyr**: standardly venerated.

## Disputes
- **Status as Arius's teacher**: Arius himself affirms the connection (in his letter to Eusebius of Nicomedia); the historical link is therefore secure, though Lucian's *own* Christology is harder to reconstruct (was he himself subordinationist, or merely a teacher of theologians who became so?).
- **NT recension authorship**: contested; some scholars deny Lucian a clear role.
- **Schismatic status during life**: some sources suggest he was out of communion with the Antiochene episcopacy through the reigns of three bishops (until ~+285), then reconciled before martyrdom; details unclear.
