# Akbar the Great

**Akbar** (1542–1605) — the third and greatest Mughal emperor — ruled for 49 years (1556–1605) and transformed the Mughal state from a conquest empire into an administrative and cultural civilization. His religious policy was the most systematic experiment in royal syncretism in the premodern world: he invited Hindu, Jain, Zoroastrian, and Christian scholars to his court for theological debate, abolished the *jizya* (poll tax on non-Muslims), married Hindu Rajput queens, and around 1582 established the **Din-i-Ilahi** ("Divine Faith") — a syncretic court religion drawing from Islam, Hinduism, Jainism, and Zoroastrianism, centered on the emperor as its head. The Din-i-Ilahi remained a court curiosity with ~20 adherents and did not outlive Akbar.

His grandson's reign under [[aurangzeb-emperor]] reversed virtually all of his syncretic policies. The contrast between Akbar's inclusive synthesis and Aurangzeb's Sunni orthodoxy defines the full range of possibilities within [[tradition-islam-mughal]].
