# Elias Lönnrot

## Identity
Finnish physician, philologist, folklorist, and the compiler of the *Kalevala*. Lönnrot was born in Sammatti, Finland (then part of the Russian Empire); trained as a physician at the University of Turku; combined a country-doctor practice with intensive folklore-collection fieldwork in Karelia. Between 1828 and 1844 he made eleven major collecting expeditions, recording runo-poetry from Karelian and Finnish singers — most prolifically from Arhippa Perttunen and his son Miihkali. He compiled the *Old Kalevala* (1835) and the *New Kalevala* (1849); also compiled the lyric companion *Kanteletar* (1840); and produced the foundational Finnish-Swedish Dictionary (1866–80).

## Historical evidence
Extensively documented modern figure: Lönnrot's manuscripts at the Finnish Literature Society (SKS); his published works; his medical-society records; abundant scholarly biography.

## Works
- *Kalevala* (Old, 1835; New, 1849). The Finnish national epic.
- *Kanteletar* (1840). Lyric folk-poetry companion volume.
- *Suomi: Tutkimuksia isänmaallisessa tieteessä* (Finnish: Researches in Patriotic Science, ed., 1844 onward).
- *Suomalais-ruotsalainen sanakirja* (Finnish-Swedish Dictionary, 1866–80).
- Numerous published collections of folk-incantations and folk-tales.

## Role in the tradition
Lönnrot's compilation transformed Finnish-language cultural identity. The Kalevala was the central text of the *fennoman* movement (Finnish nationalism within the Russian Empire); shaped Finnish painting (Akseli Gallen-Kallela's *Kalevala* paintings), music (Sibelius's *Kullervo*, *Lemminkäinen Suite*, *Tapiola*, etc.), and ultimately the political path to Finnish independence (1917). Beyond Finland, the Kalevala influenced Longfellow's *Hiawatha* (1855, which copied the Kalevala meter) and Tolkien (whose Kullervo material in the *Silmarillion* derives directly from the Kalevala). Lönnrot is the principal recoverer of Finno-Karelian pre-Christian religious material.

## Disputes
- **Compilation method.** Lönnrot's editorial intervention — stitching together verses from different singers, different regions, and different traditional contexts into a single epic narrative — was acknowledged from the start but variably weighted. Critical re-evaluation from the 1960s onward (Hautala, Kuusi, Honko) clarified the extent of Lönnrot's literary shaping. Current consensus: the verses are predominantly genuine runo-material, the narrative coherence is Lönnrot's.
- **Comparability to Macpherson's Ossian.** Lönnrot is sometimes compared to Macpherson, who fabricated supposedly-ancient Gaelic epics; the comparison is misleading — Lönnrot collected genuine material extensively and was transparent about his compilatory method.
