# Valentinus

Christian Gnostic theologian, born in Phrebonis in the Egyptian Delta ~100, educated at Alexandria, moved to Rome ~136 where he taught publicly and (per Tertullian, *Adv. Val.* 4) was a candidate for the bishopric of Rome before being passed over — at which point he is said to have parted from the proto-orthodox communion. The school he founded (Ptolemaeus, Heracleon, Marcus, Theodotus, Florinus) was the most intellectually sophisticated branch of second-century Christianity; Valentinian materials make up a significant portion of the Nag Hammadi corpus (Gospel of Truth, Gospel of Philip, Treatise on the Resurrection, Tripartite Tractate).

Valentinus's own writings survive only in fragments preserved by Clement and Hippolytus, plus the disputed attribution of the Gospel of Truth. Even from the fragments his style emerges as poetic, philosophical, mystical — quite different from the satirical caricature Tertullian and Irenaeus made of his followers' mythology.
