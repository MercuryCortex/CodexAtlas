# Nichiren (1222–1282)

Japanese Buddhist monk and reformer who declared the [[phase-4-101-lotus-sutra]] the supreme and sole valid Buddhist text for the *Mappō* age (Latter Day of the Dharma). Reduced all Buddhist practice to the chant *Namu Myōhō Renge Kyō* (南無妙法蓮華経, "Homage to the Lotus Sūtra of the Wonderful Law"). His teaching gave rise to the Nichiren school and its modern successors (Nichiren Shū, Nichiren Shōshū, Sōka Gakkai). Known for fierce polemical attacks on other Buddhist schools — especially Pure Land, Zen, and Shingon — and on the Kamakura shogunate. Exiled twice. His single-formula devotional method is a structural parallel to Pure Land *nembutsu* and Islamic Shahāda: all three traditions independently discovered that the highest practice can be compressed to a single formula of commitment.
