# Michael (Archangel)

## Identity
Michael is the principal angelic warrior and protector-prince of the Second Temple Jewish, Christian, and Islamic traditions. His name (Hebrew *Mîkhāʾēl*) is a rhetorical question — "Who is like God?" — taken in early tradition as Michael's battle-cry against the rebellious angels. He stands in continuous tradition from the Book of Daniel (~-165) through 1 Enoch, Qumran, the New Testament (especially Revelation), patristic Christian angelology, Quranic angelology (as Mikha'il), and Mandaean tradition.

## Historical evidence
A *legendary / mythological* figure, not historical, but with documented continuity across **five major religious literatures**:

1. **Daniel 10–12** ([[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]]): Michael is one of the chief princes / Israel's angelic prince, fighting the princes of Persia and Greece — the first clearly individual archangel in the Hebrew Bible. Dan 12:1: "At that time Michael, the great prince, the protector of your people, shall arise."
2. **1 Enoch** ([[phase-3-004-1-enoch]]): Michael is one of the four (or seven) Watchers (in the *good* sense, holy ones) — he, Gabriel, Raphael, Uriel intercede before God on behalf of humanity victimized by the fallen Watchers; Michael binds Shemihazah and his accomplices.
3. **Qumran War Scroll** ([[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]]): Michael is the *Prince of Light* who leads the sons of light against the *Angel of Darkness* / Belial in the eschatological war.
4. **Jude 9 and Revelation 12** (NT): Jude 9 has Michael disputing with the devil over the body of Moses (drawing on the lost *Assumption of Moses*); Rev 12:7–9 has Michael lead the angels in heaven against the dragon, casting Satan to earth.
5. **Quran 2:98**: Mikha'il is named alongside Jibril (Gabriel) as a faithful angel of God; less elaborated than in Christian apocalyptic but acknowledged.
6. **Mandaean tradition**: Michael (and Gabriel) appear in elaborated Mandaean angelology.

## Role in the tradition
- **Defender of Israel / God's people.** From Daniel onward Michael's defining role is *protector* — of Israel, of the church, of the dying. In Christian funerary art and tradition Michael weighs souls at the judgment and bears them aloft.
- **Adversary of Satan.** The Revelation 12 imagery becomes definitive in Christian iconography: Michael with sword and scale, defeating the dragon.
- **Liturgical and cultic.** Michael has feast days in the Christian liturgical year (Western: 29 September, "Michaelmas"; Eastern: 8 November) and an extensive cult (the Mont-Saint-Michel, Castel Sant'Angelo).
- **Esoteric reception.** Medieval and modern angelology (Pseudo-Dionysius, Aquinas, Steiner) elaborate Michael as one of the seven (or nine) archangels.

## Disputes
- **Angelomorphic Christology.** Some early Christian traditions identified Christ-pre-incarnation with the Angel of the LORD / Michael (so Tertullian, occasionally). Mainstream Christology suppressed this; Gieschen (1998) documents the underlying angelomorphic Christological stream.
- **Watchers tradition relation.** Michael's role in 1 Enoch and Dead Sea Scrolls suggests deep involvement in the [[watchers-and-fallen-angels]] motif; whether his prominence is the *suppression* of fallen-Watcher etiology of evil (Michael as the antidote) or a *development* parallel to it is debated.
