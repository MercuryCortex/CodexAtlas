# Abu Bakr

Close companion of Muhammad and his father-in-law (father of [[aisha-bint-abi-bakr|ʿĀʾisha]]); first of the four *Rashidun* (Rightly Guided) caliphs after Muhammad's death (632–634). [[umar-ibn-al-khattab|ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb]] drove the selection of Abū Bakr at the [[event-death-of-muhammad-632|Saqīfa Banī Sāʿida meeting in 632]]. His brief caliphate suppressed the *Ridda* (apostasy) wars that erupted after Muhammad's death, beginning the Arab Muslim expansions out of the peninsula — completed under [[umar-ibn-al-khattab|ʿUmar's]] caliphate (634-644). Abū Bakr's caliphate also began the first major effort at Quranic canonical compilation under Zayd ibn Thābit. Sunni tradition holds Abu Bakr's caliphate as the legitimate succession; Shi'a tradition holds that [[ali-ibn-abi-talib|ʿAlī]] was Muhammad's designated successor and that Abu Bakr's elevation was usurpation — the foundational [[event-first-fitna-656-661|division of Islam]] that erupts as armed conflict ~24 years later.
