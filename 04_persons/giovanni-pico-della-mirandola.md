# Giovanni Pico della Mirandola

## Identity
Italian Renaissance philosopher, count of Mirandola and Concordia, founding figure of Christian Kabbalah, and author of the *Oration on the Dignity of Man* (1486). Born to a noble family of Mirandola, educated at Bologna, Ferrara, Padua, and Paris, fluent in Greek, Latin, Arabic, Hebrew, and Aramaic by his early twenties. At twenty-three he composed the *900 Conclusions* (1486) — a maximalist syncretist program drawing on Latin scholastic, Arabic philosophical, Greek (Plato, Aristotle, Neoplatonist), Hermetic, Chaldean, Pythagorean, and Kabbalistic sources — and announced a public disputation at Rome to defend them, with the *Oration* as the opening address. Innocent VIII forbade the disputation; a papal commission condemned thirteen of the Conclusions; Pico's defense (*Apologia*, 1487) was treated as defiance; the whole 900 was condemned by papal bull in August 1487; he fled to France, was briefly imprisoned at Vincennes, eventually rehabilitated by Alexander VI in 1493. He died in 1494 at age 31, possibly poisoned (1990s exhumation indicated arsenic).

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: papal commission records, his own substantial correspondence and writings, his nephew Gianfrancesco Pico's *Vita* (1496), Florentine humanist references. Recent forensic examination of his remains.

## Works
- *Conclusiones nongentae* (900 Theses, 1486).
- *Oratio* (later *De hominis dignitate*, written 1486, printed posthumously 1496).
- *Apologia* (1487).
- *Heptaplus* (1489).
- *De Ente et Uno* (1491).
- *Disputationes adversus astrologiam divinatricem* (1494, posthumous).

## Role in the tradition
Pico is the founder of Christian Kabbalah and the most consequential single figure in the construction of Renaissance esoteric synthesis. His extension of Ficino's *prisca theologia* to include Kabbalah, his programmatic claim that Kabbalah and magic demonstrate Christianity better than Aristotle, and his procuring of Latin Kabbalistic translations via Flavius Mithridates set the agenda for Reuchlin, Agrippa, Bruno, Kircher, and the entire Christian Kabbalist tradition through Knorr von Rosenroth.

## Disputes
- **Death by poisoning.** A 2008 exhumation indicated arsenic levels consistent with poisoning; political motives have been variously proposed.
- **Late piety.** Pico's late spiritual trajectory — under the influence of Girolamo Savonarola, whose movement he supported — has led some to read a Christian-piety turn; others read continuity.
