# Onias III

## Identity
Last widely recognized **Zadokite high priest** of the Jerusalem Temple. Onias III was the son of Simon II and a direct Zadokite descendant — arguably the last figure who could claim uncontested Aaronide-Zadokite legitimacy as high priest. He served under Seleucid overlordship and was a traditionalist opponent of Hellenizing reforms.

## Historical Evidence
Onias III is documented in both **2 Maccabees** (which presents him as a righteous martyr) and **Josephus's** *Jewish Antiquities* (Book XII). The crisis around him is one of the best-documented episodes of 2nd-century BCE Jewish history: Seleucid internal politics under **Antiochus IV Epiphanes** turned the high priesthood into a purchased office, and Onias III was the last casualty of the old order.

## The Crisis (175–170 BCE)
When Antiochus IV came to power in 175 BCE, Onias's brother **Jason** bribed Antiochus to be made high priest instead — a direct purchase of the sacred office. Jason introduced Hellenizing reforms (gymnasium, Greek citizenship for some Jerusalemites). Then in 172 BCE, **Menelaus** outbid Jason and purchased the office again — despite being from the tribe of Benjamin, not even Aaronide. This was an unprecedented desecration of the priestly succession. Onias III, now in Antioch seeking redress at the Seleucid court, was lured out of sanctuary at Daphne and murdered there in ~170 BCE, almost certainly on Menelaus's orders (2 Macc 4:33–38).

## Significance
Onias III's murder is the **end of the legitimately continuous Zadokite high priesthood** in Jerusalem — a 818-year chain broken by political corruption. His death catalyzed:
- The **Maccabean revolt** (167 BCE, under his ally-adjacent figure Mattathias and sons)
- The flight of his son **[[onias-iv]]** to Egypt, where Onias IV founded the Temple of Leontopolis
- The eventual formation of the **[[tradition-essenes|Essene community]]** at Qumran, which many scholars see as the Zadokite priestly remnant refusing to accept any subsequent non-Zadokite high priest
- The **2 Maccabees** narrative's framing of Onias as a martyred righteous man — a proto-martyr trope that feeds into later Jewish and early Christian martyrology

2 Maccabees 15:12 even depicts Onias as an intercessor in a vision — an early example of post-mortem priestly intercession that anticipates Christian sainthood theology.
