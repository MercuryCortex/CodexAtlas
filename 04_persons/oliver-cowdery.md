# Oliver Cowdery

## Identity
Oliver Cowdery (1806–1850) was the principal scribe for the dictation of the [[phase-7-001-book-of-mormon|Book of Mormon]] (1829), one of its **Three Witnesses**, and — with [[joseph-smith]] — a co-founder and the "Second Elder" of the early Latter Day Saint church. After Smith, he is the single most important figure in the production and earliest organization of [[tradition-mormonism|Mormonism]].

## Historical evidence
Documented through the Book of Mormon's own witness statements, early church records, contemporary newspapers, and Cowdery's own letters and later legal career. His name appears on the published Testimony of Three Witnesses bound with every edition of the Book of Mormon.

## Role in the tradition
Cowdery took down the bulk of the Book of Mormon text as Smith dictated it in 1829, an intensive collaboration of roughly three months. He and Smith reported a joint visionary experience in which they received the Aaronic priesthood. As one of the Three Witnesses he affirmed having seen the golden plates and the angel; notably, although he left the church in 1838 (excommunicated amid the Missouri conflicts), he never recanted his witness testimony, and he was rebaptized shortly before his death in 1850 — a fact frequently cited in Latter-day Saint apologetics.

## Cross-tradition findings
- **Revealed-scripture production by scribe-dictation.** The Smith–Cowdery dictation model — a charismatic seer "translating" a hidden sacred text to a scribe — is a 19th-century instance of a recurring pattern in scripture-formation: the revealed text mediated through an amanuensis (compare the traditional accounts of the Quran's transmission via scribes, or prophetic dictation in the Hebrew Bible, e.g. Jeremiah and Baruch). The Atlas tracks this as a structural feature of how new scriptural canons are produced, not as a borrowing.
