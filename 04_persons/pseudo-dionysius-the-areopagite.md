# Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite

## Identity
Anonymous Syrian (most likely) Christian theologian of the late 5th / early 6th century writing under the pseudonym of *Dionysius the Areopagite*, the Athenian convert of Paul named in Acts 17:34. His four treatises and ten letters constitute the *Corpus Areopagiticum* — the most thoroughgoing Christian appropriation of Neoplatonist (especially Proclean) metaphysics, often by direct verbatim borrowing from Proclus.

## Historical evidence
The corpus first surfaces in 533 at a colloquy in Constantinople, when Severus of Antioch's Monophysite delegation cited it as ancient apostolic authority; Hypatius of Ephesus immediately objected, suspecting forgery (Innocentius, *Liber adversus Eutychianos*). The corpus was nonetheless accepted, in part through the scholia of John of Scythopolis and the *Ambigua* of Maximus the Confessor that effectively domesticated it. The pseudepigraphic status was definitively proven in 1895 by Hugo Koch and (independently) Joseph Stiglmayr, who demonstrated extensive verbatim borrowings from Proclus's treatise *On the Evil Subsistences*.

## Works
- ***Divine Names*** — cataphatic theology of God's names from scripture.
- ***Mystical Theology*** — the short, foundational text of Christian apophasis: God beyond every name and every negation.
- ***Celestial Hierarchy*** — the nine ranks of angels; source of standard Christian angelology.
- ***Ecclesiastical Hierarchy*** — earthly counterpart: bishop / priest / deacon and the sacraments as initiatic mysteries.
- Ten Letters.

## Role in the tradition
The single most important Christian transmitter of Neoplatonism. Translated into Latin by Hilduin (~835) and (much better) by John Scotus Eriugena (~860); his apophatic theology became the spine of Eastern Christian mysticism (Maximus, Palamas) and Western mysticism (Eriugena, the Victorines, Aquinas as commentator, Bonaventure, the Rhineland mystics — Eckhart, Tauler, Suso — and via the *Cloud of Unknowing* into English mystical theology).

## Connections — substrate and influence
- **The decisive substrate edge.** Pseudo-Dionysius's metaphysics is, in significant part, [[proclus]]'s *Elements of Theology* and his commentaries on the *Parmenides* and the *Cratylus*, with the One renamed God and Hellenic theurgy renamed sacramental hierarchy. Koch and Stiglmayr 1895 demonstrated the textual dependence in detail; Rorem 1993, Louth 1989 and O'Rourke 1992 trace the philosophical structure. *This is the most direct Christian-pagan philosophical transmission documentable in the patristic age.*
- Behind Proclus stand [[iamblichus]] (theurgy, hierarchical mediation), [[plotinus]] (apophasis of the One, *Enneads* V.3, VI.9), and ultimately Plato's *Parmenides* (first hypothesis: the One that is not even one).
- His *Mystical Theology* "divine darkness" image picks up Gregory of Nyssa's reading of Moses entering the cloud on Sinai (*Life of Moses*) — a Christian recasting of Plotinian / Platonic apophasis.
- The *Celestial Hierarchy*'s nine ranks formalize what was an unsystematic NT and intertestamental angelology (1 Enoch, Pauline epistles) into a Neoplatonic hierarchical schema.
- The sacramental *theia mysteria* vocabulary picks up directly on Hellenistic mystery-cult language; the *Ecclesiastical Hierarchy* presents the sacraments as Christian theurgy.

## Disputes
- **Identity of the author.** Proposed candidates: Peter the Iberian (Honigmann 1952), a circle around Severus of Antioch, a disciple of Proclus's at Athens (Mazzucchi 2006 has argued for Damascius — controversial), an anonymous Monophysite Syrian (mainstream consensus). The geographical location (Syria) is most secure.
- **Date.** Earliest possible date: post-Proclus (d. 485). Latest: pre-533 colloquy. Most scholars settle on c. 500.
- **Theological orientation.** Whether crypto-Monophysite, crypto-Origenist, or "neutral" Chalcedonian is disputed.
