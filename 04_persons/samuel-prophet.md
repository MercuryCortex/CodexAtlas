# Samuel

## Identity
Last of the Israelite judges and first of the writing-prophets-in-narrative tradition; the kingmaker who, against his own theological reservations, anoints both Saul and David. His birth-story (Hannah's vow at Shiloh, 1 Sam 1) frames him as a Nazirite from infancy; his ministry binds the era of the Judges to the era of the Monarchy. His person is the pivot on which the Israelite political-theological transition turns.

## Historical evidence
No extrabiblical attestation; the figure is internal to the biblical-deuteronomistic narrative complex. The Samuel-narrative composition history is itself layered: the "ark narrative" (1 Sam 4-6 + 2 Sam 6), the "history of David's rise" (1 Sam 16 – 2 Sam 5), and the "succession narrative" (2 Sam 9-20 + 1 Kgs 1-2) are largely independent earlier source-units knit together by the Deuteronomistic Historian.

## Works
1-2 Samuel are not literally Samuel's writings — they post-date him by centuries and narrate his death mid-book (1 Sam 25). The traditional attribution is harmonized by classical Jewish tradition (Baba Bathra 14b) as Samuel writing the first part and the prophets Nathan and Gad completing it.

## Role in the tradition
In the New Testament Samuel anchors the prophetic chain: Peter's Acts 3:24 ("all the prophets who have spoken, from Samuel onward") and Stephen's Acts 13:20 locate him as the prophetic origin of the messianic expectation that culminates in Christ. In Hebrews 11:32 he is named in the great catalogue of faith.
