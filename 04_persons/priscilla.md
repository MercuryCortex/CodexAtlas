# Priscilla

## Identity
Jewish Christian woman who, with her husband [[aquila]], operated as Paul's most reliable co-worker pair across three cities (Rome, Corinth, Ephesus). The couple are leatherworkers / tentmakers like Paul (Acts 18:3), expelled from Rome by Claudius's edict (~+49 — the famous Suetonian "Chrestus" passage), met Paul at Corinth on his second missionary journey, traveled with him to Ephesus, and there *taught Apollos* the way of the Lord (Acts 18:26). Of the six NT mentions of the couple, four list **Priscilla first** — exceptional for the period and read by most scholars as indicating she was the senior partner in their teaching ministry.

## Historical evidence
Independently attested in Paul and Acts. The Claudius-expulsion (Acts 18:2) anchors them to a datable event in Roman history (Suetonius *Claudius* 25.4). The name-order convention provides indirect but strong evidence of her teaching authority.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
**Hebrews authorship hypothesis**: Adolf von Harnack (1900) proposed Priscilla as the author of Hebrews — explaining (a) the work's anonymity (a woman might suppress her name) and (b) its strong Hellenistic-Jewish exegetical character. The hypothesis is unproveable but periodically revived; it has never been mainstream but is taken seriously by some feminist scholars.

**Catholic and Orthodox tradition**: a major saint; the *Catacomb of Priscilla* in Rome (one of the earliest and most important Christian catacombs) bears her name.

## Disputes
- **Hebrews authorship.** Harnack's hypothesis is intriguing but unproveable.
- **Name-order significance.** Some scholars read the precedence as merely social-affectionate; the majority view connects it to teaching authority.
