# Shuʿayb

Quranic prophet sent to the people of **Madyan** (Midian) and to the people of the **Thicket** (*Aṣḥāb al-Ayka*). Shuʿayb's mission focuses on commercial ethics — fair weights and measures — making him unique among Quranic prophets in his emphasis on economic justice (Surah 7:85–93, 11:84–95, 26:176–191, 29:36–37).

## Quranic profile

- **Surah 7:85–93** — Shuʿayb's call to honest commerce; the people's rejection; divine punishment
- **Surah 11:84–95** — the most extended narrative; Shuʿayb's formal theological dialogue with Madyan
- **Surah 26:176–191** — "the people of the Thicket" version; parallel structure to the other Arabian-prophet narratives
- **Surah 29:36–37** — brief mention

## Jethro parallel (contested)

Islamic exegetical tradition (*tafsīr*), from Ṭabarī onward, frequently identifies Shuʿayb with the biblical **Jethro** (Moses's father-in-law, Exodus 3:1, 18:1–12), since Moses finds refuge with a Midianite priest and the Quran's Moses-in-Madyan narrative (Surah 28:22–28) places him with the daughters of an unnamed man in Madyan. The Quran itself does not make this identification explicit, and Wheeler 2002 treats it as an exegetical correlation rather than a textual assertion.

The prophet whose name designates a surah is Hūd (Surah 11) — Shuʿayb appears in that same surah in the sequence Hūd → Ṣāliḥ → Abraham/Lot → Shuʿayb → Moses, the Quran's typological chain of prophetic rejection.
