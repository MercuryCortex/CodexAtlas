# Nebuchadnezzar II

## Identity
King of the Neo-Babylonian (Chaldean) Empire 605–562 BCE; the conqueror of Jerusalem (capture 597 BCE; destruction of the First Temple 586 BCE); the deportation of the Judean elite to Babylon (the Babylonian Exile) is the formative trauma of biblical Israelite religion. His architectural projects (the Babylon city walls, Ishtar Gate, ziggurat Etemenanki — long associated with the "Tower of Babel" tradition) are well documented archaeologically and in his own building inscriptions.

## Historical evidence
Extremely well attested. The Babylonian Chronicles (Wiseman 1956), Nebuchadnezzar's own building inscriptions (Langdon 1912), the Lachish Ostraca (correspondence from the field commanders defending Judah during his 588-586 campaign), and the secondary corroboration in Josephus all triangulate his reign with precision unusual for an ancient Near Eastern figure.

## Role in the tradition
- **Hebrew Bible.** 2 Kings 24-25, Jeremiah, Lamentations, and Ezekiel all engage his campaigns and the Exile directly. He is YHWH's instrument of judgment on Judah in the prophetic theology (Jer 25:9 — "my servant Nebuchadnezzar").
- **Book of Daniel.** The first half of Daniel (chs. 1-4) is set at Nebuchadnezzar's court — Daniel and his three friends; the fiery furnace; the dream of the great image (Dan 2); the king's seven-year transformation into a beast (Dan 4). The Daniel-narrative's historical accuracy regarding Nebuchadnezzar is mixed (some details — e.g., the "Nebuchadnezzar's madness" — may be a transposition of the historically attested 10-year retreat to Tayma of his successor Nabonidus, supported by the Qumran *Prayer of Nabonidus* fragment).
