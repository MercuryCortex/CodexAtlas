# Cerinthus

## Identity
Late-first-century / early-second-century Christian teacher in Asia Minor whom Irenaeus (Adv. Haer. 1.26) describes as distinguishing the man Jesus from the heavenly Christ (Christ descended at baptism and departed before crucifixion), holding to Jewish observance, and teaching a demiurgic creator distinct from the supreme Father. Eusebius preserves the famous (probably legendary) story that the apostle John fled a bath-house upon seeing Cerinthus inside, fearing the building would collapse on "the enemy of the truth." No writings survive; reconstruction depends entirely on heresiological sources, which are critical and not always consistent.

## Historical evidence
- **Irenaeus** (*Adv. Haer.* 1.26; 3.3.4; 3.11.1) is the principal source. The 3.11.1 passage gives the famous claim that the Fourth Gospel was written *against* Cerinthus — making him one of the very few 1st-c. opponents named in early Christian heresiology with a specific text-genealogical claim attached.
- **Eusebius** (*HE* 3.28; 4.14) preserves Polycarp's report of the bath-house tradition and Caius's anti-Cerinthian polemic, providing independent attestation of Cerinthus's continuing 2nd-c. reputation.
- **Hippolytus** (*Refutatio* 7.33; 10.21) adds Egyptian-trained Jewish-Christian elements.
- **Epiphanius** (*Pan.* 28) links Cerinthus to the Ebionites and to circumcision-requiring Jewish-Christianity; the identification is contested but reflects a real proximity.

## Role in the tradition
- **Separation Christology.** The doctrine that the heavenly Christ descended on Jesus at baptism and departed before crucifixion is the earliest documented form of what later becomes the *separation* Christology found in Gnostic, Adoptionist, and Cerinthian sources. The Christology Irenaeus attacks in Cerinthus is the same Christology the Sethian *Apocryphon of John* and Valentinian sources presuppose.
- **Demiurgic theology, early.** A *creator-distinct-from-supreme-Father* in Cerinthus is one of the earliest known instances of a Christian-related two-power / demiurgic theology — preceding the developed Sethian and Valentinian systems, contemporary with [[saturninus]].
- **Carnal millennialism.** The expectation of a 1000-year earthly reign of Christ at Jerusalem with bodily pleasures (per Eusebius and Caius) places Cerinthus in continuity with intertestamental Jewish-apocalyptic millennialism — a substrate point that complicates the *Gnostic* labeling. Cerinthus's eschatology is *more* materially Jewish than Irenaeus is comfortable with.
- **Polemical anchor of the Johannine canon.** Irenaeus's claim that John's Gospel was written against Cerinthus has been variously evaluated by modern scholarship — most reject the direct anti-Cerinthian motivation, but the *perception* of Johannine-Cerinthian opposition is itself an important data point about 2nd-c. Asia Minor Christianity.

## Connections — substrate and influence
- **Jewish-Christian apocalyptic substrate.** Cerinthus's millennialism — a literal 1000-year reign with material pleasures at Jerusalem — picks up 4 Ezra and 2 Baruch traditions, and the millennialism of Papias of Hierapolis (Cerinthus's contemporary in Asia Minor). The proximity between Cerinthus and the Asian millennialist tradition that included Papias and the Asian *chiliast* current is a complication for the heresiologists, who must distinguish *good* millennialism from Cerinthian *bad* millennialism.
- **Egyptian-Jewish proto-Gnostic substrate, possibly.** Hippolytus (*Ref.* 7.33) reports that Cerinthus was educated in Egypt; if true, this connects him to the Alexandrian-Jewish proto-Gnostic milieu (Philo's wisdom-Logos theology; older Jewish-apocalyptic angelic-creator schemas). Pearson 1990, Myllykoski 2008.
- **The Adoptionist current.** Cerinthus's separation Christology is structurally the same move that Theodotion of Byzantium and the later Antiochene Adoptionists develop a generation later (*Ref.* 7.35) — Christ as a *power* descending upon a created human Jesus. The Cerinthian-Theodotion line is one substrate of later Christological controversies (Paul of Samosata, Photinus).
- **Heresiological reliability problem.** King 2003 and Myllykoski 2008 caution that Cerinthus is reconstructed *entirely* from hostile sources; the unified portrait Irenaeus offers may be a heresiological composite — Jewish-Christian millennialist + demiurgic dualist + separation-Christologist may be three separate currents the heresiologists *combined* under the name Cerinthus. The historical Cerinthus may have held some, but not all, of the positions attributed to him.

## Disputes
- **Whether the *Apocalypse of John* (Revelation) was written *by* or *attributed to* Cerinthus.** Caius of Rome (preserved in Eusebius *HE* 3.28) reports a tradition that Cerinthus wrote Revelation under John's name; the claim is universally rejected today but is an important data point about early 3rd-c. Roman anti-Johannine polemic. (The *Alogi* faction in Asia Minor likewise rejected the Fourth Gospel and Revelation as Cerinthian forgeries.)
- **Relation to the Ebionites.** Epiphanius identifies Cerinthians with Ebionites; modern scholarship treats this as conflation, while admitting real proximity in Jewish-Christian observance.
- **Historicity of the heresiological composite.** King 2003, Myllykoski 2008 caution against assuming the heresiological portrait reflects a single historical figure's actual teaching.
- **Egyptian education.** Hippolytus alone reports it; not independently corroborated.
