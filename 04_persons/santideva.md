# Śāntideva

## Identity
Śāntideva (~685-763) was an 8th-c. Mahāyāna monk at the Nālandā monastic university and the author of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* (*Way of the Bodhisattva* / *Entering the Conduct of the Bodhisattva*; see [[phase-5-049-bodhicaryavatara]]), the single most influential systematic articulation of the Mahāyāna [[bodhisattva-vow|bodhisattva-path]] in any tradition. He is also the compiler of the *Śikṣāsamuccaya* (*Compendium of Training*), an anthology of bodhisattva-path passages from earlier Mahāyāna sūtras. The traditional hagiography (Bu-ston, Tāranātha) presents him as a wandering yogi-monk dismissed by his Nālandā colleagues as "Bhusuku" (~"the eat-shit-sleep monk") until invited contemptuously to recite a sermon — at which point he ascended a throne and delivered the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* extemporaneously, ascending into the sky as the verses unfolded. The biographical details are hagiographic; the corpus is unambiguously his.

## Historical evidence
- ***Bodhicaryāvatāra*** corpus — securely his; surviving in Sanskrit (Vaidya 1960 critical ed.), Tibetan (Kangyur/Tengyur), and Chinese translations. **The most-quoted Mahāyāna ethical text in any tradition**; recited daily by Tibetan monastic students for ~1300 years.
- ***Śikṣāsamuccaya*** — also securely his; survives in Sanskrit + Tibetan.
- **Tibetan historiographical sources** (Bu-ston, Tāranātha, *Blue Annals*) preserve a consistent biographical-hagiographic tradition.
- **No surviving contemporary Indian biographical source** — the *Vita* tradition is medieval Tibetan.

## Works
- ***Bodhicaryāvatāra*** (~700-750) — 10 chapters, 913 Sanskrit verses on the bodhisattva-path: bodhicitta arising; vigilance; patience; effort; meditation; **wisdom (chapter 9 — Madhyamaka-Prāsaṅgika dialectic, one of the densest pieces of Indian Mahāyāna philosophical-poetic writing)**; dedication.
- ***Śikṣāsamuccaya*** — passage-compendium organizing Mahāyāna sūtra material into a bodhisattva-training framework.

## Role in the tradition
- **In Mahāyāna**: the foundational systematic-ethical work on the bodhisattva-path. The Dalai Lama has taught *Bodhicaryāvatāra* publicly more times than any other text. The "exchange of self and others" technique (Bca 8) is the foundation of modern *tonglen* practice.
- **In Tibetan Buddhism**: along with [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]] and [[asanga|Asaṅga]], Śāntideva is one of the three foundational Indian Mahāyāna systematizers whose works are canonical curricula in all four Tibetan schools.
- **In modern Western reception**: the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* is the most-translated and most-studied single Mahāyāna ethical text in 20th-21st-c. Western Buddhist scholarship — three competing major English translations (Padmakara 1997, Crosby & Skilton 1995, Wallace & Wallace 1997).

## Disputes
- **Dates**: ~685-763 is the Tibetan-tradition window; some scholars place him slightly earlier (~650-700). The Nālandā setting is uncontested.
- **Madhyamaka school affiliation**: Prāsaṅgika (Crosby-Skilton, modern Tibetan-Gelug reading) vs. some Svātantrika-leaning interpretations of chapter 9. The Padmakara translation follows the Tibetan Mipham-school reading.
- **Authenticity of attributed *Sūtra-samuccaya***: in some Tibetan transmissions ascribed to Nāgārjuna instead; not securely Śāntideva's.
