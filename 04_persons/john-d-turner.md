# John D. Turner

## Identity
American scholar of Gnosticism and Platonism (1938–2019); Cotner Professor of Religious Studies and Charles J. Mach University Professor of Classics and History, University of Nebraska–Lincoln. Trained at Duke. With Bernard Barc, Louis Painchaud, Wolf-Peter Funk, and Paul-Hubert Poirier, a principal architect of the Bibliothèque copte de Nag Hammadi (BCNH) critical-edition series at Université Laval — the French-language counterpart to the Brill Coptic Gnostic Library.

## Works
See texts-authored. *Sethian Gnosticism and the Platonic Tradition* (2001) is the foundational reconstruction of the Sethian Gnostic literary corpus (the "Platonizing" Sethian treatises: *Allogenes*, *Marsanes*, *Zostrianos*, *Three Steles of Seth*) and its philosophical interaction with Middle and Neoplatonic thought. Turner argued that several Sethian treatises are quoted/critiqued in [[plotinus]]'s school ([[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] II.9 *Against the Gnostics*; Porphyry, *Life of Plotinus* 16 names *Zostrianos* and *Allogenes* among Gnostic apocalypses read in Plotinus's circle).

## Role in the field
The principal late-20th- / early-21st-century scholar of the Sethian-Platonic interface. His thesis — that Sethian Gnostic texts and Plotinian Neoplatonism are in mutual contact and reciprocal critique — established the close historical proximity of Gnostic and Neoplatonic philosophical-religious culture in 3rd-century Egypt and helped re-frame Gnosticism as a *philosophical* phenomenon rather than a purely religious one. His categorization of Sethian literary types ("apocalypse," "dialogue," "treatise") is now standard.

## Connections
- Wires the ancient lineage [[plato]] → [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] (especially the *Parmenides*) → Middle Platonism → [[plotinus]] / [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] → Sethian Gnosticism ([[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]], [[phase-4-008-trimorphic-protennoia]], [[phase-4-006-hypostasis-of-the-archons]]) into modern academic Neoplatonism (Dillon, Gerson, Corrigan).
- The chain continues forward through the depth-psychology reception: Sethian Gnostic material influenced [[carl-gustav-jung]] ([[phase-7-018-aion]], [[phase-7-019-mysterium-coniunctionis]]) and the post-Jungian school (Hillman, [[james-hillman]]; [[james-hollis]]).
- Renaissance recovery: the Florentine-Platonist lineage of [[marsilio-ficino]] (translator of [[plotinus]]) and [[giovanni-pico-della-mirandola]] is the early-modern bridge that made this same Platonic-Gnostic complex available to the early modern West.

## Disputes
- **Direction of influence.** Whether Sethian Gnostic texts borrow from Platonism, Platonism borrows from Gnostic texts, or both draw on a shared Middle-Platonic milieu is debated. Turner argued for substantive bidirectional contact.
- **Categorization of Sethian texts.** Turner's typology has been refined but not displaced (cf. Rasimus 2009; [[david-brakke]] 2010).
