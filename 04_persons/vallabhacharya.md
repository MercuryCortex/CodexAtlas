# Vallabhacharya

## Identity
Telugu Brahmin theologian and devotional teacher (1479–1531), founder of the *Pushtimarg* (Path of Grace / Path of Nurture) — one of the principal medieval Krishna-bhakti sampradayas. Born in Champaran (in Mughal Bihar) to a Telugu family on pilgrimage; raised at Varanasi; debated his way across the major intellectual centers of late-15th-century India; established Krishna-as-Śrīnāthajī of Govardhan (Braj) as the primary devotional focus and codified the *Pushtimarg* as a householder bhakti path centered on *sevā* (loving service) of the Krishna-image. Married, fathered Vitthalnath (1515–1585), who systematized and expanded the sect after Vallabha's death.

## Works
- *Aṇubhāṣya* — his major systematic statement on Vedanta, a Brahmasutra commentary written from the *Śuddhādvaita* (Pure Non-dualism) position, with the world and souls not as illusion but as real manifestations of Brahman (= Krishna).
- *Subodhini* — extensive Bhagavata Purana commentary, incomplete.
- *Ṣoḍaśagrantha* — sixteen short doctrinal treatises that constitute the catechism of Pushtimarg.
- *Tattvārtha-dīpa-nibandha* — three-part treatise covering Pushtimarg theology, scriptural hermeneutics, and Bhagavata-summary.

## Role in the tradition
- **Pushtimarg founder**: the Pushtimarg is one of the four major Vaishnava sampradayas of medieval North India (alongside Ramanuja's Sri Vaishnavism, Madhva's Brahma sampradaya, and Nimbarka's Sanaka sampradaya — the standard *catuh-sampradāya* enumeration). The Pushtimarg's emphasis on lay-householder *sevā*, on aesthetic embellishment of the Krishna-image (Pichhvai painting tradition emerges here), on lay-merchant patronage especially in Gujarat, has shaped Western Indian (especially Gujarati and Rajasthani Marwari) Vaishnavism into the present.
- **Shuddhadvaita Vedanta**: the philosophical school he founded is one of the recognized medieval Vedantic alternatives to Shankara's Advaita, Ramanuja's Vishishtadvaita, and Madhva's Dvaita.
- **The Ashtachhap**: Vallabha's son Vitthalnath organized the *Ashtachhap* of eight Krishna poets — four from Vallabha's own circle (Surdas, Kumbhandas, Krishnadas, Paramanandadas) and four added by Vitthalnath (Govindswami, Chaturbhujdas, Chhitswami, Nandadas) — whose Braj poetry became the foundational liturgical corpus of Pushtimarg.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Sant-Sufi shared milieu**: Vallabhacharya's lifetime overlaps Kabir's late period (Kabir d. 1518) and precedes Surdas's mature work; the same Akbar-prelude North Indian devotional ferment that produced the Sant tradition and Sufi-Hindu cross-pollination produced Vallabha's Krishna-bhakti.
- **Pushtimarg in Gujarat & the Indian Ocean trade**: by the 17th–18th c. Pushtimarg merchant patronage networks integrated with the Indian Ocean trade diaspora (Surat, Diu, Zanzibar, Mumbai); the sect is one of the principal religious institutions of the Gujarati Marwari merchant diaspora globally, comparable in commercial-religious structure to the Sufi tariqa networks across the same maritime world.

## Disputes
- The traditional account of Vallabha's 84 *baithaks* (seats of teaching) across India and his debates with Smarta and other opponents at Vijayanagara is hagiographically shaped; the historical kernel is the establishment of the Govardhan Śrīnāthajī cult and the codification of the householder *sevā* path.
- The relationship between Vallabha and the contemporary Gaudiya tradition (Chaitanya, ~30 years younger) included documented points of contact at Vrindavan; the doctrinal differentiation between Pushtimarg and Gaudiya Vaishnavism took place under Vitthalnath and Jīva Gosvāmī.
