# Nicodemus

## Identity
A Pharisee and member of the Jerusalem ruling council (*archōn tōn Ioudaiōn*, Jn 3:1) who, in the Fourth Gospel, comes to Jesus by night, holds a celebrated dialogue on being *gennēthē anōthen* ("born again" / "born from above"), later argues for procedural fairness in the Sanhedrin, and finally helps with Jesus's burial. Entirely absent from the Synoptic tradition.

## Historical evidence
Single-source canonical attestation (John only). Bauckham (1996) has argued for identification with **Nakdimon ben Gurion** of rabbinic literature (Avot de-Rabbi Natan; bavli Gittin 56a), a famously wealthy Jerusalem aristocrat of the late Second-Temple period — making Nicodemus a securely-attested historical individual. The identification is plausible but not certain; the names are linguistically equivalent.

## Works
None. The 4th-c. *Gospel of Nicodemus / Acts of Pilate* is pseudonymous; it expands the Passion and *descensus ad inferos* narratives, attributing the testimony to Nicodemus as a Sanhedrin insider.

## Role in the tradition
- **Born-again Christianity**: Nicodemus's "born again" dialogue (Jn 3:3, 7) is foundational for evangelical and Pentecostal soteriological vocabulary.
- **Catholic devotion**: traditionally venerated as a saint and secret disciple.
- **Rabbinic tradition (if Bauckham right)**: Nakdimon as one of the three wealthiest men of Jerusalem before its fall in 70.

## Disputes
- **Identification with Nakdimon ben Gurion.** Bauckham's case is strong but not universally accepted.
- **Historicity beyond John.** Without Synoptic corroboration, his canonical portrait is single-source.
