Nicomachus of Gerasa (c. 60–120 CE) is the **critical transmission node** between original Pythagoreanism and the Latin medieval world. Without Nicomachus, the Pythagorean mathematical-sacred program would survive only in Platonic and Aristotelian summaries; with him, it enters the standard curriculum as the authoritative treatment of arithmetic and music theory for a thousand years.

## The Introduction to Arithmetic — Number as Divine Substance

The *Arithmetike Eisagoge* is not a mathematics textbook in the modern sense. It is a **philosophical treatise** in which the study of number is presented as the study of divine order. Nicomachus opens: "The ancients who practiced philosophy were very particular in investigating number, not for its practical utility but for its divine nature."

The classifications in the work are cosmological, not merely technical:
- **Perfect numbers** (equal to their factors' sum: 6, 28, 496) = divine harmony
- **Deficient numbers** (less than their factors' sum) = imperfection
- **Abundant numbers** (more than their factors' sum) = excess
- **Friendly numbers** (pairs where each equals the factors' sum of the other) = divine friendship

These are not mathematical curiosities. They are **theological categories** — ways in which numbers reflect divine attributes.

## The Manual of Harmonics — The 7-Fold Cosmos

The *Enchiridion Harmonikes* gives the fullest ancient statement of the Pythagorean **music-cosmos-alphabet** homology. The seven:
- 7 strings of the lyre (heptachord = the original Pythagorean instrument)
- 7 visible planets (Moon, Mercury, Venus, Sun, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn)
- 7 vowels of the Greek alphabet (α, ε, η, ι, ο, υ, ω)
- 7 modes/scales of Greek music

Each planet governs a vowel; each vowel is associated with a mode; each mode corresponds to a string. The cosmos is a musical instrument playing a continuous chord, and the alphabet's vowels are the notation.

This 7-fold homology is **cross-cultural**: the Indian system of 7 svaras (Sa, Ri, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, Ni) mapped to planetary bodies appears in medieval Indian music theory. Chinese music theory counts 7 primary tones. Islamic cosmological music theory via al-Kindi and al-Farabi maps planets to modes using an identical template. The question is whether this is independent convergence or transmission — but the convergence is real.

## The Transmission Chain

The decisive step: **Boethius** (c. 480–524 CE) translated and expanded Nicomachus's two major works into Latin as *De Institutione Arithmetica* and *De Institutione Musica*. These became the standard quadrivium textbooks in medieval European education — used without interruption from the 6th to the 13th century CE. Every educated European in this period learned Pythagorean arithmetic and music theory through the lens Nicomachus ground.
