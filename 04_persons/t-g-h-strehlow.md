# Theodor George Henry Strehlow

## Identity
Australian anthropologist and linguist, the principal 20th-c. scholar of Arrernte (Central Australian Aboriginal) religious tradition. T.G.H. Strehlow was the son of the German Lutheran missionary Carl Strehlow (see below); he was born and raised at Hermannsburg Mission in Central Australia, where he learned Western Arrernte as a first language alongside German and English. He pursued academic studies in classics and linguistics at the University of Adelaide; combined an academic career in Adelaide with intensive fieldwork in Arrernte country across decades; was entrusted by Arrernte senior custodians with restricted-sacred material on condition of safekeeping. His handling of that material — the disposition of his archive after his death — is the most contested element of his legacy.

## Historical evidence
Extensively documented modern figure; principal biography Hill 2002 (controversial within Strehlow scholarship for its critical stance); standard primary sources in his published works.

## Works
- *Aranda Traditions* (1947) — academic monograph on Arrernte religious-mythological tradition.
- *Songs of Central Australia* (1971) — magnum opus; ~800 pages; the largest published Arrernte-language song-corpus, with commentary on Arrernte religion. Published posthumously to many objections from Arrernte custodians regarding the release of restricted material.
- *Journey to Horseshoe Bend* (1969) — semi-autobiographical narrative of his father Carl Strehlow's death and his own early life.
- Various journal articles in linguistics and anthropology.

## Role in the tradition
Strehlow was, for several generations, the principal external academic authority on Central Australian Aboriginal religion. His material is the principal published corpus of Arrernte religious-mythological-linguistic material. Current Arrernte and Indigenous-Australian academic engagement has been mixed — gratitude for what Strehlow preserved combined with criticism of his terms of access, his disposition of the material, and his often-paternalistic interpretive framework.

The Strehlow Research Centre (Alice Springs, established 1991) holds his archive under access restrictions negotiated with Arrernte custodians. The disposition of restricted-sacred objects has been one of the major repatriation-and-restriction cases in Australian-museum / Indigenous-relations history.

## Disputes
- **Access ethics.** Whether Strehlow's terms of access to restricted Arrernte material were ethically sound by the standards of his own time, by contemporary standards, or by Arrernte custodial standards is intensively debated. Hill 2002 takes a sharply critical stance; some defenders argue Strehlow preserved what would otherwise have been lost.
- **Disposition of the archive.** Strehlow's plan to dispose of the archive (and the late-life sale of photographs of restricted ceremonies to a German magazine, 1978) caused controversy that has only partially been resolved through the Strehlow Research Centre's current custodianship.
