# Mary (Miriam of Nazareth)

## Identity
The mother of Jesus of Nazareth; in the canonical gospels she appears in: the infancy narratives of Matthew 1-2 and Luke 1-2 (the Annunciation, the Magnificat, the Nativity, the flight to Egypt or the presentation at the Temple); the Cana wedding (John 2); the brief Markan note of her standing outside with Jesus's brothers (Mark 3:31-35, par.); the crucifixion at the foot of the cross in John 19:25-27; and the upper-room gathering after the Ascension (Acts 1:14). Paul mentions her only obliquely as "born of a woman, born under the law" (Gal 4:4).

This node represents the *historical-biographical* figure of Mary, distinct from the developed deity-cult node [[mary-theotokos]] — the Theotokos cult is the post-Ephesus-431 cultic-iconographic crystallization of Marian veneration and follows a separate trajectory of its own.

## Historical evidence
Mary's historicity at the basic level (the mother of the historical Jesus) is essentially uncontested. Paul's Galatians 4:4 (~+54 CE) is the earliest written reference; the canonical-gospel material is layered with theological development. The non-canonical *Protoevangelium of James* (~+150) generates the developed Marian biographical tradition (Mary's parents Joachim and Anna, her temple-childhood, her virginal status as a vowed Temple maiden) that becomes load-bearing in later Orthodox and Catholic Mariology.

## Role in the tradition
- **New Testament.** Foundational, but textually thin: most of what later Christian piety means by "Mary" is post-canonical.
- **Mariology development.** Justin Martyr (Dial. 100; ~+155) develops the Eve / Mary typology. Irenaeus (Haer. 3.22.4; ~+180) systematizes it. The Council of Ephesus (431) formalizes *Theotokos* ("God-bearer") against Nestorian objection. Subsequent Marian dogmas (perpetual virginity; Immaculate Conception 1854; bodily Assumption 1950) are Catholic-tradition-specific developments.
- **Cross-tradition / MASSIVE-win edges.** The iconographic substrate of the nursing-Madonna (*Maria Lactans*) derives substantially from the Egyptian Isis-Lactans iconography that was widespread in Hellenistic Egypt and absorbed by Coptic Christianity in the 3rd-5th centuries — see [[isis-hellenistic]] and [[ethiopian-marian-devotion]] for the documentary thread. Witt 1971 and Frankfurter 1998 are the load-bearing scholarship.
- **Islamic tradition.** Maryam is honored in the Quran more than any other woman — Sura 19 is named for her; the Annunciation and the virgin birth of Isa are narrated at Quran 3:42-48 and 19:16-34. She is the only woman named by personal name in the Quran.
