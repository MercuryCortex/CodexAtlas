# Hildegard of Bingen

## Identity
Hildegard of Bingen (1098–1179) was the foremost female intellectual of medieval Latin Europe — Benedictine abbess of the Rupertsberg monastery (which she founded ~1150), visionary author, theologian, composer of liturgical music, herbalist, natural philosopher, correspondent of popes and emperors, and public preacher (one of the very few medieval women authorized to preach to mixed audiences). Her visionary works present a complex theology of cosmic harmony, divine wisdom (*Sapientia*) as a feminine cosmic principle, and the moral-spiritual order of creation; her musical-dramatic *Ordo Virtutum* is among the first liturgical dramas with music. Canonized and declared Doctor of the Church by Benedict XVI in 2012.

## Works
*Scivias* (visionary theology, three books, 26 visions); *Liber Vitae Meritorum*; *Liber Divinorum Operum*; *Physica* (~ 9 books on natural history — plants, animals, minerals); *Causae et Curae* (medicine); *Symphonia* (77 liturgical compositions); *Ordo Virtutum* (music drama); extensive correspondence with kings, popes, bishops; biographies of saints; a secret language *Lingua Ignota* with 1,011 invented words.

## Role in the tradition
The most influential medieval female visionary; the model of the *prophetissa* whose authority derives from direct divine revelation rather than ecclesiastical office. Her cosmic-feminine *Sapientia* theology and her divine-feminine imagery have made her foundational for modern Catholic feminist and ecotheological reflection.
