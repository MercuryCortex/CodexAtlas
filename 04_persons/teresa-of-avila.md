# Teresa of Ávila

## Identity
Spanish Carmelite nun, mystical-theological author, and institutional reformer (1515–1582). Born to a **converso** family (her paternal grandfather Juan Sánchez de Toledo had been reconciled by the Inquisition for crypto-Judaism in 1485 — a fact Teresa concealed; rediscovered by modern archival work). Entered the Carmelite Order at the Convent of the Incarnation, Ávila, in 1535. Underwent extended mystical-experiential development through the 1540s–1560s. In 1562 founded the first reformed **Discalced Carmelite** convent (St. Joseph's, Ávila), inaugurating the **Discalced Carmelite reform** in collaboration with [[john-of-the-cross|John of the Cross]] (from 1568 onward). Wrote her *Vida* (1565) at the order of her confessor; *Way of Perfection* (~1566); *Interior Castle* (1577) — her theological-mystical magnum opus; *Book of Foundations* (1573–1582). Beatified 1614, canonized 1622, named **Doctor of the Church** in 1970 (with Catherine of Siena — the first two women ever so named).

## Historical evidence
Exceptionally well-documented. Teresa's own letters (~470 surviving), her published works in multiple manuscript and printed witnesses, the Inquisitional examinations of her writings, the Carmelite and royal-Spanish documentation, and an extensive contemporary biographical tradition (Ribera 1590, Yepes 1606, Gracián's *Diálogos*) make Teresa's career one of the best-documented in 16th-c. religious history.

## Works
- ***Libro de la Vida*** (*Book of Her Life*, 1565) — spiritual autobiography written at the order of her confessor; published posthumously 1588 (Luis de León's editio princeps).
- ***Camino de Perfección*** (*Way of Perfection*, ~1566) — written for her nuns; practical-pastoral instruction.
- ***Castillo Interior / Las Moradas*** ([[phase-6-038-teresa-interior-castle|*Interior Castle*]], 1577) — the seven-mansions architecture of the soul's progress toward the central chamber where God dwells. **Teresa's theological-mystical masterwork.**
- ***Las Fundaciones*** (*Book of Foundations*, 1573–1582) — historical chronicle of the Discalced reform.
- ***Meditaciones sobre los Cantares*** — on Song of Songs.
- **Letters**, **Constitutions**, **Cuentas de conciencia** (spiritual accounts to confessors).

## Role in the tradition

- **Institutional reformer.** The **Discalced Carmelite Order** is Teresa's institutional creation. The reform — return to the primitive Carmelite Rule, strict enclosure, austerity — was substantially controversial in 1560s-70s Spain and required navigating Inquisitorial, Calced-Carmelite, and royal politics. By Teresa's death (1582) there were 17 Discalced female convents and 15 male houses.
- **Systematic mystical phenomenology.** Teresa's *Interior Castle* provides the most systematic spatial-architectural account of mystical-spiritual development in the Western Catholic tradition — seven concentric **mansions** (*moradas*) the soul traverses, with detailed phenomenological descriptions of each stage's experiences and dangers.
- **Distinction-system for mystical experience.** Teresa's distinctions among *locuciones* (interior speech), *visiones imaginarias* (imaginative visions), *visiones intelectuales* (intellectual visions), and *unión* / *éxtasis* (union, ecstasy) become diagnostic for Catholic spiritual direction in the 17th-21st c.
- **Doctor of the Church (1970).** Paul VI named Teresa and Catherine of Siena Doctors of the Church on 27 September and 4 October 1970 respectively — the **first women so designated** in the ~1,800-year history of the title.

## Disputes
- **Converso background and its theological significance.** Teresa concealed her Jewish-converso lineage; modern Jewish-Christian-historical scholarship (Egido, Tellechea, more recently Yovel) reads important aspects of her thought as influenced by the Jewish-mystical and converso intellectual heritage. The strong thesis (deep Kabbalah-influence) is contested; the weak thesis (some converso-cultural sensibility) is widely accepted.
- **How original is Teresa theologically?** Some 19th-20th-c. readings minimized her theological originality in favor of psychological-experiential interest. McGinn (*Presence of God* 6, 2017) and Ahlgren 1996 strongly defend her theological originality.
