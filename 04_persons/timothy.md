# Timothy

## Identity
Paul's most prominent younger co-worker and emissary. A native of Lystra in Lycaonia (south-central Asia Minor), with a Jewish mother (Eunice) and Greek father (Acts 16:1). Paul circumcised him on joining the mission "because of the Jews in those places" (Acts 16:3) — a striking concession given Paul's anti-circumcision theology, often invoked in Paul-was-flexible arguments. Co-sender of seven Pauline letters, the most frequently named individual associate.

## Historical evidence
Strong attestation: Paul's undisputed letters name him repeatedly as co-sender, courier, and personal emissary. Acts gives a detailed origin story (16:1–3). Both streams agree on his Lystran origin, his missionary partnership, and his pattern of being sent ahead to crisis-congregations (Thessalonica, Corinth). Heb 13:23 mentions his imprisonment and release — an interesting independent attestation.

## Works
None genuine. The Pastoral Epistles (1–2 Timothy, Titus) are addressed to him but mainstream critical scholarship considers them pseudonymous (early 2nd c.), composed in his name to provide apostolic authority for an emerging episcopal-presbyteral church order.

## Role in the tradition
**Bishop of Ephesus** in patristic tradition (Eusebius), martyred under Domitian or Nerva per legendary tradition. **Catholic and Orthodox liturgy**: a major saint. **Pauline biography**: the principal companion through the second and third missionary journeys.

## Disputes
- **Authorship of 1–2 Timothy.** Universally pseudonymous in mainstream critical scholarship; defended as genuinely Pauline by some conservative scholarship.
- **Date of death.** Late legendary tradition under Domitian (~+97) — not independently attested.
- **Circumcision in Acts 16.** Theological-historical puzzle given Paul's anti-circumcision polemics in Galatians; harmonized in various ways.
