# B.R. Ambedkar

**Bhimrao Ramji Ambedkar** (1891–1956) — jurist, economist, social reformer, and the principal architect of the Indian Constitution — was born into the *Mahar* caste, one of the communities classified as "untouchable" under the Hindu caste system. He overcame severe caste discrimination to earn doctorates from Columbia University (under John Dewey) and the London School of Economics, becoming one of the most credentialed intellectuals in South Asian history. He then devoted his career to the abolition of untouchability and the legal emancipation of the Dalit population.

Ambedkar is the primary author of the Constitution of India (1950), which explicitly bans untouchability and provides for affirmative action (reservations) for Scheduled Castes — the most sweeping constitutional anti-caste legislation in history. His intellectual legacy spans law, economics (*caste as the division of laborers*, not merely labor), Buddhist studies ([[phase-7-045-ambedkar-buddha-and-dhamma]]), and political philosophy.

On October 14, 1956 — six weeks before his death — Ambedkar converted to Buddhism at Nagpur alongside approximately 500,000 followers in the [[event-ambedkar-mass-conversion-1956|largest single-day mass religious conversion in recorded history]]. He founded [[tradition-navayana-buddhism]], a reconstruction of Buddhist doctrine that strips karma and rebirth (which he identified as brahminical interpolations used to naturalize caste) and refocuses Buddhist ethics on collective social liberation.

His *Annihilation of Caste* (1936 undelivered speech) remains the most radical theoretical critique of Hinduism from within South Asian thought — more structurally confrontational than anything Gandhi produced — and his debate with Gandhi over the Poona Pact (1932) is the foundational tension of modern Indian democratic politics.
