# Iain McGilchrist

## Identity
British psychiatrist and literary scholar (b. 1953); All Souls College, Oxford (Fellow 1975–1980); Bethlem Royal and Maudsley Hospital psychiatrist; trained originally in English literature before medicine. Now an independent scholar based on the Isle of Skye.

## Works
*The Master and His Emissary* (2009) and its expansion *The Matter With Things* (2021, two volumes, ~1,500 pp) develop a cultural-historical thesis: the two cerebral hemispheres attend to the world differently, the right hemisphere ("the Master") with broad, contextual, relational, embodied attention and the left ("the Emissary") with focused, abstracted, manipulative attention; Western culture since the Reformation/Enlightenment has progressively over-empowered the left-hemisphere mode at cost to the right; the resulting epistemology underlies modern reductionism, scientism, mechanical worldview, ecological crisis. *The Matter With Things* extends the argument to a metaphysics and a (post-Christian, panentheist-leaning) theology of nature.

## Role in the field
A widely-read but contested figure. The 2009 book has a substantial uptake in some humanities and theology circles (recent Anglican theology, contemplative-spirituality writers, Jordan Peterson's biblical lectures, [[jordan-peterson]]), and in continental philosophy of mind. The cognitive-neuroscience-discipline reception has been mixed-to-critical: specialist reviewers argue the hemispheric-asymmetry literature does not support the strong cultural-historical generalization, even if individual hemispheric-attention findings are well-established. McGilchrist's argument is in this sense **a serious academic synthesis applied controversially across a broad cultural front**; his book is Tier 2 in its core neuroscience and Tier 3 in its applied cultural-historical conclusions.

## Connections
- Continues the broad "Western civilization went wrong somewhere" lineage with strong overlap to: late Heidegger, Hannah Arendt, Lewis Mumford, [[carl-gustav-jung]], Owen Barfield (anthroposophy / [[tradition-anthroposophy]]).
- Used by [[jordan-peterson]] and Jonathan Pageau as a neuroscientific scaffold for re-traditionalist Christian (and broader perennialist) cultural critique.

## Disputes
- **Specialist-neuroscience pushback.** Multiple reviewers (Bishop 2010 in *Cortex*; later commentators) argue the hemispheric-asymmetry evidence base does not bear the cultural-historical weight McGilchrist places on it. The left-vs-right "pop-neuroscience" of the 1970s–80s is largely rejected by current neuroscientists; McGilchrist's version is more careful but specialist reviewers contend it remains overgeneralized.
- **Theological extension in *The Matter With Things*.** The 2021 book moves into metaphysics in ways that go well beyond the cognitive-neuroscience evidence; readers find this either a brave synthesis or a category-mistake.
