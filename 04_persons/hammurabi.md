# Hammurabi

## Identity
Hammurabi (~1810–1750 BCE) was the sixth king of the First Dynasty of Babylon and the most consequential ruler of the Old Babylonian period. He inherited a small regional kingdom around Babylon and, over a 42-year reign (1792–1750 BCE), expanded it into the dominant power of Mesopotamia through successful campaigns against Larsa (Rim-Sin I), Eshnunna, Assyria, and Mari. He is best known for the [[phase-1-007-code-of-hammurabi]] — the great diorite stele inscribed late in his reign with ~282 laws preceded by a theological prologue and followed by curse-epilogue. He is the author-figure (in royal voice) of the Code; whether he composed the contents personally or commissioned the work is moot — the Code is "by Hammurabi" in the constitutional sense.

## Historical evidence
Robust. Hammurabi is one of the best-documented Bronze Age kings: (1) the Code itself with its first-person royal voice; (2) the **Mari letters** — the royal archives of his contemporary Zimri-Lim of Mari, captured when Hammurabi sacked Mari (~1761 BCE), contain extensive correspondence to and about Hammurabi, providing detailed political-diplomatic evidence; (3) the date formulae of his 43 regnal years, each named for a major event; (4) numerous royal inscriptions and dedications; (5) economic and administrative texts mentioning him by name.

## Works
- The Code of Hammurabi (~1755–1750 BCE) — the law collection with prologue and epilogue.
- ~43 year-names (each a short narrative of a major event of the year, used as a dating system).
- Various royal inscriptions on buildings and temples.
- Letters and administrative orders (some preserved in the Mari archive and elsewhere).

## Role in the tradition
Hammurabi's name became synonymous with Babylonian kingship in later Mesopotamian memory. Copies of his Code were curated by scribal schools for over a millennium after his death; the stele itself was carried as war booty to Susa by the Elamites in the 12th century BCE and discovered in 1901. Modern reception has been intense — the Code is universally invoked as the paradigmatic ancient law-code (alongside the Mosaic and Solonic) and Hammurabi's image (handing or receiving the rod-and-ring from Shamash) is one of the most reproduced ancient Near Eastern art motifs.

## Disputes
- **Birth date.** ~1810 BCE is conventional but approximate; the Mesopotamian chronology of the Middle Bronze Age (Middle, Low, High, Ultra-Low chronologies) gives a range of about ±150 years for Hammurabi's absolute dates. The "Middle Chronology" (used here) puts his reign 1792–1750 BCE; the "Low Chronology" puts it ~1728–1686 BCE.
- **Authorship vs. compilation of the Code.** Whether the Code reflects a deliberate compositional act by Hammurabi or a court-scribal compilation under royal authority is unsettled.
