# Shenoute of Atripe

## Identity
Shenoute of Atripe (~347-465; reported lifespan ~118 years per Coptic tradition, almost certainly exaggerated — modern scholarship places his death ~465 but birth-date uncertain) was the third abbot of the White Monastery federation (Deir al-Anba Shenouda) near Sohag in Upper Egypt, succeeding his uncle Pjol. Under his ~83-year archimandritate he transformed the federation into the largest single monastic institution of late-antique Egypt — possibly 2,200+ monks + 1,800+ nuns across three linked houses — and produced the largest single body of native Coptic literature ever written. The Stephen Emmel reconstruction (2004) recovered his corpus into 9 *Canon* volumes (monastic rules + correspondence with the houses) and 8 *Discourse* volumes (sermons + polemics) from scattered manuscript fragments. He is the **single most important foundational figure of Coptic-language Christianity** as distinct from Greek-Christian Egypt: Pachomius and Antony founded Egyptian monasticism, but Shenoute made it speak Coptic.

## Historical evidence
- **~9 *Canon* volumes + 8 *Discourse* volumes** survive in Sahidic Coptic manuscript fragments — the largest native-Coptic literary corpus. Emmel 2004 is the magisterial reconstruction.
- **Besa's *Vita Shenoutii*** (Shenoute's disciple and successor, ~6th c.) — the foundational hagiographic source; preserved in Coptic, Arabic, Syriac, Ethiopic, and Greek translations (the multi-lingual translation history is itself evidence of his Mediterranean-wide importance).
- **The White Monastery itself** (Deir al-Anba Shenouda) still stands and has been continuously occupied; archaeological excavation is ongoing.
- **Late-antique anti-pagan-temple-destruction archaeology** in Upper Egypt (Hahn-Emmel-Gotter 2008) provides material context for Shenoute's iconoclastic activism.

## Works
- ***Canon* volumes (9)** — monastic rules + letters to/from the federation's three houses, especially the women's house. Krawiec 2002 is the foundational study of the women's-house material.
- ***Discourse* volumes (8)** — sermons + theological + anti-pagan + anti-Origenist + anti-Nestorian polemics.
- ***I am Amazed*** — major anti-Origenist + anti-pagan polemical sermon.
- The literary register is distinctively Coptic — not translated from Greek; original Sahidic prose of high stylistic ambition.

## Role in the tradition
- **In [[tradition-coptic-orthodox|Coptic Orthodoxy]]**: foundational Coptic-literary saint, the great archimandrite of the White Monastery, second only to Antony + Pachomius in monastic-foundational status. Feast day Epip 7 (~July 14).
- **In late-antique studies generally**: the leading case-study figure for late-antique violent Christianization of rural Egypt (temple-destruction; militant anti-pagan activism; physical assault on suspected heretics within the monastery — controversial in modern scholarship).
- **For Coptic-language history**: his corpus is **THE evidence-base for what high-style native Sahidic Coptic looked like**. Before Shenoute, Coptic literature is mostly Greek-translation; with Shenoute, original Coptic literary composition reaches sophisticated rhetorical complexity.
