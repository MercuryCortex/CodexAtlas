# Henri de Lubac

## Identity
French Jesuit theologian (1896–1991); one of the principal figures of the mid-20th-century Catholic *ressourcement* / *nouvelle théologie* — the return to Scripture and the Church Fathers as renewing sources for theology, against the regnant Neo-Scholastic manual-Thomism. Censured under Humani Generis (1950) and removed from teaching; rehabilitated under John XXIII; *peritus* at [[event-second-vatican-council-1962-1965]]; created cardinal by John Paul II 1983.

## Works
*Surnaturel* (1946) is his most controversial work — a patristic-medieval argument against the natura pura / extrinsicist account of grace and nature dominant in early-20th-c. Thomism. *Corpus Mysticum* (1944) traces the medieval semantic shift in which "mystical body" moved from designating the Eucharist to designating the Church. *Catholicisme* (1938) is the programmatic statement of his ecclesial-and-social theology. *Exégèse médiévale* (1959–1964) is the great history of medieval allegorical exegesis.

## Role in the tradition
With Jean Daniélou, [[hans-urs-von-balthasar]], Yves Congar, M.-D. Chenu, Karl Rahner, and Joseph Ratzinger ([[joseph-ratzinger-benedict-xvi]]), one of the architects of mid-20th-c. Catholic theological renewal that fed substantially into [[event-second-vatican-council-1962-1965]] and the long [[event-vatican-ii-aftermath-1965-present]].
