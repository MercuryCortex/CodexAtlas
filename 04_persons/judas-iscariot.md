# Judas Iscariot

## Identity
Judas (Yehuda) "Iscariot" — the epithet likely from *ish-Kerioth* ("man of Kerioth," a Judean village) — one of the Twelve, the disciple who delivered Jesus to the temple authorities, leading to his arrest and crucifixion. The four Gospels and Acts give convergent narrative (he handed Jesus over for money, kissed him to identify him in Gethsemane) but divergent details of his death (suicide-hanging in Matthew; falling-and-bursting in Acts; gruesome bodily corruption in Papias). The Sethian *Gospel of Judas* (Codex Tchacos, ~+150) radically inverts the canonical reading: Judas is Jesus's *most beloved* disciple, the only one given true gnosis, who *liberates* the inner Christ by handing over the body to be killed.

## Historical evidence
Multiply attested across all four Gospels and Paul (1 Cor 11:23 — *paredidoto*, "was handed over," may be the earliest stratum of the betrayal tradition). His historicity is essentially uncontested. The details (motive, mechanics, death) diverge across sources, and modern criticism has questioned the moral coherence of the narrative — Klassen 1996 argues for translating *paradidōmi* as "handed over" rather than "betrayed," softening the demonic-traitor reading.

## Works
None genuine. The 2nd-c. Sethian *Gospel of Judas* — recovered in the Tchacos Codex and published 2006 — pseudonymously presents Judas as the privileged hearer of Jesus's cosmological revelation; he alone understands the Pleroma; the other eleven worship a lesser god (the demiurge of the cosmos).

## Role in the tradition
**Mainstream Christian tradition**: archetypal traitor, demonized; in Dante the deepest pit of hell; in Western literature the type of treachery. **Cainite Gnostic tradition** (per Irenaeus *AH* 1.31.1): Judas revered as the agent who completed the cosmic plan. **Sethian Gnosticism** (Gospel of Judas): Judas is the favored disciple. **Modern critical scholarship**: a complex figure whose narrative trajectory shows clear theological shaping; the historical *fact* of betrayal-from-within is likely; the demonic framing is later development.

## Disputes
- **Etymology of "Iscariot."** "Man of Kerioth" is the leading proposal; alternatives include *sicarius* (assassin/dagger-man), *iškaryōta* (Aramaic "the liar"), and *issachar* (the tribal name). No consensus.
- **Manner of death.** Matthew's hanging vs. Acts' falling-and-bursting cannot be harmonized without strain; Papias's even more gruesome version adds further divergence.
- **Theological reading.** Cainite/Sethian re-evaluation in the 2nd c.; modern critical rehabilitation (Klassen 1996); the Gospel of Judas's publication in 2006 sparked another round.
- **Motive.** Greed (Mt), Satan-entry (Lk/Jn), or ideological (Brandon's Zealot-Judas hypothesis). The Gospels offer different answers; none is historically dispositive.
