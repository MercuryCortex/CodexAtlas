# Unas

## Identity
Unas (also spelled Wenis or Unis) was the last king of the 5th Dynasty of the Egyptian Old Kingdom, reigning approximately 30 years (~2375–2345 BCE). He built a comparatively modest pyramid at Saqqara, just south of the Step Pyramid complex of Djoser. His historical importance lies overwhelmingly in one fact: **his is the first pyramid known to have been inscribed with the [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]]** — the earliest large corpus of Egyptian religious literature. The inscriptions cover the interior walls of the burial chamber and antechamber in vertical columns of beautifully cut hieroglyphs, originally filled with blue pigment. After Unas, the 6th Dynasty pharaohs Teti, Pepi I, Merenre, and Pepi II continued the tradition; some queens' pyramids of the late Old Kingdom also bear the texts.

## Historical evidence
Robust. (1) His pyramid at Saqqara is preserved and excavated; (2) the inscribed Pyramid Texts within it; (3) his pyramid causeway with its surviving reliefs (the famine scenes of starving Bedouins, the unusual maritime scenes of obelisk transport); (4) the cult of Unas continued in the Old Kingdom and Middle Kingdom; (5) he appears in king lists. His reign-length is given variously by Manetho and the Turin Canon, with about 30 years a working consensus.

## Works
- No personally authored works — Unas did not compose the Pyramid Texts, which are anonymous priestly compositions probably much older than his reign in their oral form. He is, however, the **patron** under whose authority the texts were first committed to monumental writing in a royal mortuary context. This patronage transformed Egyptian religious literature.

## Role in the tradition
Unas's cult continued for centuries after his death — Egyptian Old Kingdom mortuary cults could persist for many generations. In the modern reception, Unas is famous principally for his pyramid's inscriptions. Gaston Maspero opened the pyramid in 1880 and was the first to identify the texts as religious literature. The "Pyramid of Unas" is one of the most-visited Old Kingdom monuments after the Giza complex, precisely because its interior is decorated where Giza's is austere.

## Disputes
- **Reign dates.** ~2375–2345 BCE is conventional; absolute Old Kingdom chronology has uncertainty of ~±50 years.
- **Did Unas commission the texts personally or did his priesthood?** The Pyramid Texts represent priestly compilation; whether Unas himself initiated the program of inscribing them or whether it emerged from his temple establishment is not recoverable.
- **Why Unas's pyramid first?** The decision to inscribe the texts in his burial chamber rather than in earlier pyramids is itself a historical puzzle; some scholars connect it with end-of-dynasty theological consolidation.
