# Géza Vermes

## Identity
Hungarian-born British scholar of Jewish history and religion (1924–2013). Born to Jewish parents who converted to Catholicism in 1931; survived the Holocaust (his parents did not); ordained Catholic priest 1950 (Notre Dame de Sion); left the priesthood and reverted to Judaism 1957; from 1965 at Oxford, where he held the inaugural Professorship of Jewish Studies (1989) and was a Fellow of the British Academy.

## Works
See texts-authored. *The Dead Sea Scrolls in English* (1962; revised through 7th edition 2011) was for decades the standard English-public access point to the non-biblical Qumran texts. *Jesus the Jew* (1973) inaugurated the major late-20th-century historical-Jesus shift toward Jewishness — re-locating Jesus firmly within charismatic Galilean Jewish piety rather than as a proto-Christian theological figure. *The Changing Faces of Jesus* (2000) tracks how successive New Testament authors recast Jesus.

## Role in the field
A foundational figure in the modern study of [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] and a principal architect of the "Jewish-Jesus" turn in historical-Jesus scholarship. His Penguin translation gave the Qumran scrolls a global public after the long [[event-dead-sea-scrolls-discovery]] aftermath; his 1973 *Jesus the Jew* set the agenda for the Third Quest. With Sanders, [[n-t-wright]], Meier, and Allison, Vermes shaped a generation of historical-Jesus work.

## Connections
- The decisive English-public access to [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] for half a century.
- "Jewish Jesus" shift: Vermes 1973 → Sanders → Meier → [[n-t-wright]] → Allison. Counters older Christological-from-Greek-philosophy readings of Jesus.
- Engagement with Christian-origins debates connects backward into [[phase-3-014-q-source]], [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]], and the apocalyptic-Jewish matrix of [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]].

## Disputes
- **"Hasid" or "charismatic" model for Jesus.** Vermes's model — Jesus as a Galilean *hasid* in the line of Hanina ben Dosa and Honi the Circle-Drawer — has been substantially absorbed but is critiqued for over-relying on later rabbinic sources for context.
- **Authorship and dating of individual Qumran scrolls.** Standard discipline-internal disputes; Vermes's translations follow a generally Essene-hypothesis consensus.
