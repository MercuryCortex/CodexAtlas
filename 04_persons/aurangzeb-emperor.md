# Aurangzeb

**Aurangzeb** (1618–1707) — Emperor Alamgir I — was the sixth Mughal emperor and the most controversial: the longest-reigning Mughal (49 years), the last to hold the empire together, and the one whose religious policies permanently alienated the Marathas, Rajputs, and Sikhs who would ultimately destroy Mughal power. He reversed the syncretic religious policies of his great-grandfather [[akbar-mughal]], reimposed the *jizya* (poll tax on non-Muslims), banned music at court, and ordered the demolition of Hindu temples. In 1675 he executed [[guru-gobind-singh-ji|Guru Tegh Bahadur]] (the 9th Sikh Guru), who according to Sikh tradition came to Delhi to protect the right of Kashmiri Brahmins to practice their religion. This execution transformed the Sikh community: Guru Tegh Bahadur's son [[guru-gobind-singh-ji]] responded by founding the Khalsa (1699), transforming Sikhs from a devotional community into a martial one.
