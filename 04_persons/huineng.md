# Huineng

## Identity
Huineng (638–713) is the Sixth Patriarch of Chan Buddhism in the standard lineage, founder of the Southern School of "sudden awakening" (*dunwu*), and the most influential figure in the formation of mature Chan / Zen. Born in Lingnan (modern Guangdong); legendary biography: illiterate woodcutter awakened by overhearing the *Diamond Sūtra*; received secret transmission of the patriarch's robe and bowl from the Fifth Patriarch Hongren over the head of the senior disciple Shenxiu via the famous "mind-mirror" verse contest. Modern scholarship: the historical Huineng existed; the biographical narrative as we have it is substantially constructed by his disciple Shenhui (~684–758) in his 730s public campaigns against the rival Northern School (associated with Shenxiu).

## Works
Attributed: [[phase-5-004-platform-sutra-huineng]]. The text actually emerges from a layered process — Huineng's teaching, Fahai's editing, Shenhui's polemical insertions, Song-era polishing.

## Role in the tradition
Foundational for all subsequent Chan / Zen / Seon / Thiền lineages. The Platform Sutra's sudden-awakening teaching is the doctrinal common ground; the lineage genealogies (Linji, Caodong / Sōtō, etc.) trace through Huineng. The traditional patriarchal lineage runs from the Indian First Patriarch (Mahākāśyapa, who received the wordless transmission from the Buddha) through the 28th Indian Patriarch [[bodhidharma]] (who, in the legendary biography, brought Chan to China in the early 6th c.) through the four Chinese Patriarchs (Huike, Sengcan, Daoxin, Hongren) to Huineng as the Sixth.
