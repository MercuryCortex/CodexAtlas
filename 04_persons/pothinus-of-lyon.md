# Pothinus of Lyon

## Identity
The first known **bishop of Lugdunum (Lyon)**, a transplanted Greek-speaking Christian community in southern Gaul (the city's bishops, presbyters, and elders were predominantly of Asia-Minor origin, reflecting the trade-routes that had brought Christianity from Asia Minor up the Rhone). Approximately 90 years old at the time of his arrest in the Lyon persecution of 177; he died of injuries in prison shortly after being beaten. Succeeded by his presbyter [[irenaeus-of-lyon|Irenaeus]] (who was absent on a mission to Rome at the time of the persecution, carrying a letter from the Lyon Christians about Montanism — preserved in part by Eusebius).

## Historical evidence
Single-source: the *Letter of the Churches of Lyons and Vienne* in **Eusebius HE 5.1**. The detailed contemporary narrative description (beating, prison death) is widely accepted.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
- **Founding bishop of Lyon**: anchors the Greek-Christian foundation of Gaulish Christianity.
- **Irenaeus's predecessor**: the apostolic-succession chain claimed by [[irenaeus-of-lyon]] runs through Pothinus → (Asia Minor link) → [[polycarp-of-smyrna|Polycarp]] → John.
- **Aged-martyr type**: along with [[polycarp-of-smyrna|Polycarp]], a paradigmatic "elder bishop martyr" — the kind of figure whose age and quietness amplifies the moral force of his witness.

## Disputes
- **Date of his arrival in Gaul**: probably mid-2nd c.; precise date unknown.
- **Cause of death**: dragged to court, beaten, died in prison within two days — the *Letter*'s account is detailed but partly literary.
