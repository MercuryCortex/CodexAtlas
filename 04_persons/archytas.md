# Archytas of Tarentum

## Identity
Greek Pythagorean philosopher, mathematician, and politician of Tarentum (c. -428 to -347 BCE) — the most important Pythagorean of the 4th century BCE. Archytas was elected *stratēgos* (general) of Tarentum seven consecutive years, in violation of the city's regular term-limit. He was a friend of Plato (probably the host of Plato's Sicilian journeys) and is referenced multiple times in Plato's *Letters*. Archytas's mathematical achievements include the first geometric solution to the "doubling-the-cube" problem (intersection of three solids of revolution), foundational work on mathematical harmonics (the arithmetic-geometric-harmonic mean and the theory of musical intervals), and probably the first systematic four-fold *mathēmata* curriculum (arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, music) that became Plato's quadrivium.

## Historical evidence
Substantial: ancient testimonia in Plato, Aristotle, Diogenes Laertius, Cicero, and the Pythagorean doxographic tradition. The authenticity of fragments has been carefully sifted by Huffman 2005, who treats four fragments as genuine.

## Works
- *On Mathematical Sciences* — programmatic work on the unity of the four mathematical disciplines.
- *Harmonics* — theory of musical intervals and the mathematical structure of pitch.
- *On the Universe* — fragmentary cosmological work.

## Role in the tradition
- The bridge-figure between 5th-c. early Pythagoreanism (Philolaus) and 4th-c. Plato and the Academy. The mathematization of cosmology and the unity of the mathematical sciences as a philosophical curriculum trace through Archytas.
- The later Pythagorean tradition (Iamblichus *Life of Pythagoras*, etc.) memorialized him as the model philosopher-king.

## MASSIVE WIN — The Quadrivium Origin

Archytas's *On Mathematical Sciences* contains the oldest known statement of the four mathematical disciplines as a unified philosophical program: arithmetic, geometry, astronomy, music. He opens: "Those who distinguish the sciences seem to me to have given us a fine example, and it is not at all strange that they have a correct understanding of the nature of each thing. For... they hand on to us a clear account of the speed of the stars and their rising and setting, and about geometry, arithmetic, and spherics, and, not least, about music."

This four-fold curriculum is the **origin of the medieval quadrivium** — the upper four disciplines of the seven liberal arts (arithmetic, geometry, music, astronomy). The chain: Archytas → Plato (*Republic* VII) → Aristotle → Hellenistic schools → Nicomachus → Boethius (*De Institutione Arithmetica*, *De Institutione Musica*) → medieval universities. Every student in a European university from the 9th to the 16th century followed a curriculum whose structure traces to Archytas.

## MASSIVE WIN — The Doubling-the-Cube Solution

Archytas produced the first known solution to the **Delian problem** — constructing a cube of double the volume of a given cube — using the intersection of three surfaces (a cylinder, a cone, and a torus). This requires genuinely three-dimensional reasoning and is the earliest documented purely geometric construction using solid geometry.

The mathematical achievement mattered less than the methodological precedent: Archytas showed that problems unsolvable in a plane become solvable in three dimensions. This prefigures the principle, central to modern mathematics, that embedding a problem in a richer space may dissolve it.

## Disputes

- Authenticity of attributed fragments; the *De Archyta* tradition contains many Hellenistic pseudonymous works. Carl Huffman (2005) accepts four fragments as genuine.
