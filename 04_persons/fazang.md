# Fazang (643–712)

Third patriarch and systematic philosopher of the Huayan (华严) school of Chinese Buddhism. Served as court philosopher to Empress Wu Zetian (r. 690-705), for whom he composed the famous demonstration of interpenetration using mirrors and a candle to illustrate the *shi-shi wu-ai* doctrine from the [[phase-4-102-avatamsaka-sutra]]. His *Treatise on the Golden Lion* (*Jīnshīzi zhāng*) is the foundational philosophical statement of Huayan's four *dharmadhātu* analysis. His systematization of Huayan philosophy profoundly shaped the Chan/Zen synthesis (Zongmi) and, through the Neo-Confucian appropriation of the *li*-concept, the entire trajectory of Chinese intellectual life through the Song dynasty.

## Key connections
- → [[phase-4-102-avatamsaka-sutra]] — *direct-influence*: the Avataṃsaka is the textual basis; Fazang's work is its philosophical apex
- → [[tradition-chan]] — *direct-influence*: Zongmi (Huayan + Chan synthesis) is Fazang's intellectual heir
- → [[tradition-neo-confucianism]] — *indirect-influence*: the Neo-Confucian *li* (principle) absorbs Huayan's *li-shi wu-ai* framework
- → [[zhu-xi]] — *indirect-influence*: Zhu Xi's li-qi synthesis is the secular heir of Huayan's li
