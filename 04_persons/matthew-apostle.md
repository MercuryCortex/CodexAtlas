# Matthew the Apostle

## Identity
A tax-collector (*telōnēs*) of Capernaum, called by Jesus from his customs booth. Mark and Luke call him **Levi son of Alphaeus**; Matthew alone gives the name "Matthew" — and Matthew's apostle-list (Mt 10:3) labels him "the tax collector," a self-marking unique among the apostle-lists. Critical scholarship distinguishes him sharply from [[matthew-evangelist]] (the anonymous author of the First Gospel, who is *not* the apostle).

## Historical evidence
The Levi/Matthew identification is internal to the Synoptic tradition: Matthew (the Gospel) appears to have *renamed* Levi to "Matthew" to harmonize with the apostle on the Twelve-list. Whether the underlying tradition is one historical tax-collector with two names, or two distinct individuals secondarily identified, is a longstanding crux. Beyond the call story, no narrative attaches to him.

**Papias's testimony** (~+125, in Eusebius *HE* 3.39) — "Matthew compiled the *logia* in the Hebrew dialect" — is the foundation of the traditional attribution of the Gospel of Matthew to the apostle. Mainstream critical scholarship reads Papias's *logia* as a sayings-collection (perhaps related to [[phase-3-014-q-source]]), *not* the canonical Greek Gospel of Matthew (which uses Mark as a source and is written in Greek, not Hebrew).

## Works
None. The traditional attribution of the canonical Gospel to him is critically rejected: the First Gospel is anonymous, written in Greek by a Jewish-Christian author of the 80s CE who relies on Mark and Q.

## Role in the tradition
The traditional author of the First Gospel — therefore enormously elevated in Christian piety despite the modest NT footprint of the historical apostle. Eusebius reports his missionary activity in "Ethiopia" or "Parthia" (sources disagree). His symbol in Christian iconography is the winged man / angel (from the four living creatures of Ezek 1 / Rev 4) because Matthew opens with Jesus's human genealogy.

## Disputes
- **Levi = Matthew?** Mark and Luke's "Levi son of Alphaeus" does not appear on any Synoptic apostle-list; the identification is Matthean.
- **Authorship of the First Gospel.** Universally attributed to him in tradition; universally denied to him in mainstream modern criticism — though conservative scholarship retains a modified version (Matthew compiled an Aramaic sayings-source incorporated into the Greek Gospel).
- **Mission field.** "Ethiopia" or "Parthia" — sources do not agree.
