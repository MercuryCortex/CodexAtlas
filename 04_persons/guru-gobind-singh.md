# Guru Gobind Singh

## Identity
Tenth and last human Sikh Guru (1666–1708); founder of the Khalsa; closer of the Sikh canon. Son of Guru Tegh Bahadur (executed by Aurangzeb 1675 for refusing to abandon his support of Hindu Kashmiri Pandits' religious freedom); succeeded as Guru at age 9 in 1675. On Vaisakhi day 1699 at Anandpur Sahib, instituted the Khalsa — the disciplined martial order of *Singhs* (lions) and *Kaurs* (princesses) marked by the *Five Ks* (kesh / uncut hair, kangha / comb, kara / iron bracelet, kachera / breeches, kirpan / sword) and committed to religious-political defense of the community. Fought multiple battles against Mughal and hill-rajah forces from Anandpur; lost his four sons in those wars (the two elder in battle; the two younger bricked alive at Sirhind on Wazir Khan's orders); composed the *Zafarnamah* (Letter of Victory) to Aurangzeb. Closed the Sikh canon in 1706 by adding Guru Tegh Bahadur's verses to the Adi Granth and declaring the Granth itself the eternal Guru after his death. Assassinated by Mughal-sponsored agents at Nanded in the Deccan 1708; on his deathbed declared the Granth as eleventh and eternal Guru — "Guru-Granth Granth-Guru."

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own substantial compositions (mainly in the *Dasam Granth*), Mughal records, Sikh hagiographical literature (the *Gur Bilas* genre), the autobiographical *Bachittar Natak* attributed to him.

## Works
Additions to and final redaction of the Adi Granth (the *Damdami Bir*, 1706). The *Dasam Granth* (the Tenth Book) — a substantial Punjabi-Braj-Sanskrit corpus attributed to him including *Jaap Sahib*, *Akal Ustat*, *Bachittar Natak*, *Chandi Charitra* (versified Devi narrative), *Chaupai Sahib*, *Zafarnamah*. The authorship of certain *Dasam Granth* sections is contested in Sikh-internal scholarship.

## Role in the tradition
Closing figure of the human Guruship and founder of the institutional form of modern Sikhism (the Khalsa). The 1699 institution of the Khalsa and the 1708 transition to the Granth-as-eternal-Guru together define the structural identity of Sikhism from then to now.

## Disputes
- ***Dasam Granth* authorship.** The composition's status — entirely by Gobind Singh? by his court poets? mixed? — is a long-running Sikh-internal debate. Modern scholarship varies; tradition holds Gobind Singh as principal author with some compositions by court poets.
