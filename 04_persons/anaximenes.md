# Anaximenes of Miletus

## Identity
Anaximenes (c. -586 to -526) was the third and youngest member of the **Milesian school** of Ionian natural philosophy — student of [[anaximander]] and grandstudent of [[thales]]. His distinctive contribution: where Thales proposed water and Anaximander proposed the *apeiron* as the cosmic *archê*, **Anaximenes proposed *aēr* (air / breath / mist)** as the single underlying principle, modified by **condensation and rarefaction** to produce the different observable substances. This is a major philosophical-methodological step: Anaximenes is the first to propose a **specific physical mechanism** (condensation-rarefaction) by which a single principle generates the apparent plurality of things. For the Codex Atlas mission Anaximenes is load-bearing because his *aēr*-cosmology is the substrate-text the [[phase-3-027-derveni-papyrus]] commentator (via Diogenes of Apollonia, an Anaximenean successor) uses to allegorize Orphic theogony — making Anaximenes part of the **Orphic-Hermetic-Christian breath-and-spirit theological lineage** running through Stoic *pneuma*, Pauline *pneuma hagion*, and patristic Holy-Spirit theology.

## Historical evidence
- **[[aristotle]]** *Metaphysics* 1.3 (984a5–7) — names Anaximenes among the early natural philosophers and reports his *aēr*-doctrine.
- **Theophrastus** (lost; preserved in later doxography) — the principal source for Anaximenes's condensation-rarefaction mechanism.
- **Hippolytus** *Refutation* 1.7 — biographical-doxographical entry.
- **Diogenes Laertius** *Lives* 2.3 — biographical entry.
- Aetius and Pseudo-Plutarch *Placita* — doxographical material.

A few verbatim fragments survive (DK B1, B2); they are scant compared to Anaximander's.

## Works
- ***On Nature*** (Περὶ Φύσεως, lost). Composed in the Ionian prose style established by Anaximander. Treats cosmology, meteorology, astronomy, and biology.

## Role in the tradition
1. **Air / *aēr* as *archê***. Where Thales proposed water and Anaximander the indefinite *apeiron*, Anaximenes returns to a **specific physical principle** but a more **subtle and pervasive** one — *aēr*, the air/mist/breath that permeates everything. **Critically: Anaximenes proposes a specific mechanism — condensation-rarefaction** — by which his single principle generates the observable plurality (rarefied air = fire; air condensed = wind, then cloud, then water, then earth, then stone). This is a **major methodological innovation** — proposing not just a unifying principle but the *transformative process* by which the unity becomes plurality.

2. **Pneumatic cosmology — the breath-and-spirit lineage**. Anaximenes's choice of *aēr* / *pneuma* (the same word in Greek can mean *breath*, *air*, *wind*, and later *spirit*) is the **earliest articulation in a long lineage**:
   - **Anaximenes (~-550)**: *aēr* as cosmic principle.
   - **Diogenes of Apollonia (~-440)**: Anaximenean *aēr* identified with intelligence (νοῦς).
   - **[[phase-3-027-derveni-papyrus]] commentator (~-400)**: deploys Diogenes-Anaximenean *aēr* to allegorize Orphic theogony.
   - **Stoic *pneuma* (~-300 onward)**: cosmic *pneuma* as living, intelligent, divine principle pervading all things. **Direct philosophical descendant of Anaximenean *aēr*.**
   - **Pauline *pneuma hagion***: Christian "Holy Spirit," translating Hebrew *ruach* but receiving Greek-Stoic *pneuma* connotations from Hellenistic-Jewish (Philo) and Greek philosophical context.
   - **Patristic Holy-Spirit theology**: Basil of Caesarea's *On the Holy Spirit* (~+375) explicitly engages Greek philosophical *pneuma* in defining the Christian Holy Spirit.

This is **one of the cleanest pre-Socratic-to-Christian theological lineages** running through 1000 years.

3. **Microcosm-macrocosm**. Anaximenes makes the **explicit analogy** between the human soul (as *aēr* / breath holding the body together) and the cosmic *aēr* holding the cosmos together (DK B2): **"As our soul, being air, holds us together, so do breath and air encompass the whole cosmos."** This is the **earliest extant articulation of microcosm-macrocosm**, a theological-philosophical structure that will run through Plato (*Timaeus*), Hermetic Corpus ("*as above, so below*"), and Renaissance Hermetic-Platonist [[microcosm-macrocosm]] theology.

## Disputes
- **Originality of condensation-rarefaction mechanism.** Whether Anaximenes was the first to propose this specific transformative mechanism, or it was implicit in earlier Milesian thought, is debated. The standard view credits him with the explicit formulation.
- **Was *aēr* divine?** Anaximenes (B2) seems to treat *aēr* as a living, intelligent, divine principle. Whether this is genuinely Anaximenean or a later doxographical projection by Stoic-sympathetic transmitters is debated. The structural parallel to Stoic *pneuma*-theology is striking enough that some scholars treat Anaximenean *aēr*-divinity as a back-projection from Stoicism; others (Graham 2003) defend the pre-Stoic divinity-of-*aēr* claim.
- **Influence on Diogenes of Apollonia.** Standard view: Diogenes (5th c. BCE) is an Anaximenean revivalist whose *aēr*-doctrine substantially mediates Anaximenes to later philosophy. This positions Anaximenes as the buried substrate of much later Greek pneumatic-cosmological thinking.
