# Rowan Williams

## Identity
Welsh Anglican theologian and church leader (b. 14 June 1950, Swansea). DPhil Oxford 1975 (on Lossky and Russian Orthodox theology). Successively: Lecturer at Mirfield and Cambridge; Lady Margaret Professor of Divinity, Oxford (1986–1992); Bishop of Monmouth (1992–2002); Archbishop of Wales (2000–2002); Archbishop of Canterbury (2002–2012; 104th); Master of Magdalene College, Cambridge (2013–2020); life peer (2013–). Active poet (Welsh and English).

## Works
See texts-authored. *Arius: Heresy and Tradition* (1987; revised 2001) is his major academic patristic monograph — a sympathetic reconstruction of Arius's theological project ([[event-council-of-nicaea-325]]) as a serious-but-misjudged late-antique attempt at Christian monotheism. *The Wound of Knowledge* (1979) is his New Testament-through-John-of-the-Cross history of Christian mystical and spiritual theology. *Dostoevsky* (2008) draws his Russian Orthodox theological reading into literary criticism. *Christ the Heart of Creation* (2018) is his constructive Chalcedonian-Christological statement.

## Role in the tradition
A leading 20th–21st-century Anglican theologian and one of the more academically substantial recent Archbishops of Canterbury. Theologically Anglo-Catholic with strong Orthodox-Russian influences (Lossky, Bulgakov, Florovsky). As Archbishop, navigated the Anglican Communion through the 2003–2012 crises over same-sex relationships and women bishops, attempting unity through "covenant" approaches; widely judged to have done more reconciliatory work than was generally noticed. Post-Canterbury, has had a major public-theological and literary career.

## Connections
- Continues the patristic-resourcement line ([[athanasius]], [[gregory-of-nyssa]], [[maximus-the-confessor]]) into contemporary Anglican theology.
- Russian Orthodox theology and the *Sergius Bulgakov / Vladimir Lossky / Georges Florovsky* lineage from Paris-school Russian theology forward.
- The continuing reception of [[event-council-of-nicaea-325]] in his Arius monograph.

## Disputes
- **Reading of Arius.** Williams's sympathetic reconstruction of Arius's theology is a major scholarly contribution but is debated by other patrologists (e.g., R.P.C. Hanson reads Arius differently).
- **Anglican Communion management** as Archbishop (2002–2012) was sharply criticized from progressive (Spong, Schori) and conservative (GAFCON) sides; assessment of his strategic choices remains contested.
