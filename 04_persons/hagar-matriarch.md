# Hagar

## Identity
Egyptian-born slave-handmaid of Sarah; surrogate mother (by Sarah's giving, Gen 16) of Abraham's first son Ishmael; the figure of the only biblical character explicitly to *name* God (Gen 16:13 — "You are *El-roi*, the God who sees me"). After Isaac's birth she and Ishmael are expelled to the wilderness (Gen 21), where the angel of God promises that Ishmael too will become a great nation. Her narrative is paralleled across J (Gen 16) and E (Gen 21) — one of the doublets that source-critical analysis isolates.

## Historical evidence
None extrabiblical. The Hagar-narrative is internal to the patriarchal complex; the dual-source structure (J + E) is one of the cleaner test cases for the Documentary Hypothesis.

## Role in the tradition
- **Hebrew Bible.** Hagar is the founding matriarch of the Ishmaelite line — by Genesis's own theology a recognized divine line (Gen 17:20, 21:13, 25:12-18) — but outside the covenant of Sarah-Isaac-Jacob.
- **New Testament — load-bearing allegory.** Paul's Galatians 4:21-31 reads Hagar (slave / Sinai / earthly Jerusalem / bondage) and Sarah (free / Jerusalem-above / promise) as the two covenants. This is the strongest Pauline supersessionist passage and one of the load-bearing texts of later Christian-Jewish polemic.
- **Islamic tradition.** Hajar is the mother of Isma'il and the foundational matriarch of the Arab line; her search for water at Marwah-and-Safa is commemorated in the Hajj rite of *sa'y*; the well of Zamzam is traditionally where the angel showed her water.
