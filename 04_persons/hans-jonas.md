# Hans Jonas

## Identity
German Jewish philosopher and historian of religion (1903–1993). Heidegger's doctoral student (with Bultmann co-advising); his 1928 dissertation became *Gnosis und spätantiker Geist* vol 1 (1934). Fled Germany 1933 to Palestine; served in the British Jewish Brigade WWII; postwar McGill (Montreal) and from 1955 the New School for Social Research, New York. His 1958 *Gnostic Religion* in English brought late-antique Gnosticism into general philosophical-religious discourse; his late work shifted to philosophy of biology (*Phenomenon of Life*) and environmental ethics (*Imperative of Responsibility*).

## Works
See texts-authored. *Gnostic Religion* (1958) is the most-cited; *Imperative of Responsibility* (1979) is the major ethics-of-technology work.

## Role in the tradition
Jonas's existentialist reframing of Gnosticism made it intellectually serious for two generations; the subsequent revision (Williams 1996, King 2003) operates against his framing. As a refugee-scholar paralleling Scholem (Hebrew University) and the Frankfurt School, Jonas is part of the mid-20th-century German Jewish intellectual diaspora that decisively shaped Anglophone humanities.
