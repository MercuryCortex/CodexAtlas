# Vasubandhu

## Identity
4th-c. CE North Indian Buddhist philosopher, half-brother of Asaṅga, author of the *Abhidharmakośa* (Treasury of Abhidharma) — the encyclopedic summa of Sarvāstivāda/Vaibhāṣika Abhidharma which has been the standard textbook of Buddhist scholastic-systematic thought from late antiquity to the present in every Mahāyāna tradition. Vasubandhu wrote the *Abhidharmakośa-kārikā* (the verse text) from a Sarvāstivāda perspective and then a substantial auto-commentary (the *bhāṣya*) that systematically critiques Sarvāstivāda from a Sautrāntika perspective. He later converted to Mahāyāna under his brother's influence and produced the foundational Yogācāra works — *Viṃśatikā* and *Triṃśikā* — which are the school's classical statements.

## Historical evidence
Documented via Chinese biographical sources (especially Paramārtha's *Life of Vasubandhu*, 6th c.) and via Tibetan tradition. The "two Vasubandhus" hypothesis (Frauwallner) — that the Abhidharma-Vasubandhu and the Yogācāra-Vasubandhu were different people — is debated in modern scholarship; the dominant current view returns to the traditional single-author position.

## Works
- *Abhidharmakośa* + *bhāṣya* — the foundational Abhidharma summa.
- *Viṃśatikā* (Twenty Verses on Mind-Only) + auto-commentary.
- *Triṃśikā* (Thirty Verses on Mind-Only) — completed by Sthiramati's commentary.
- *Trisvabhāva-nirdeśa* — the three natures of Yogācāra.
- *Karmasiddhi-prakaraṇa* — on the establishment of karmic action.

## Role in the tradition
- **Abhidharma**: the *Abhidharmakośa* is *the* standard text in Mahāyāna educational systems; Tibetan Gelug monastic curriculum gives it many years; East Asian Buddhism likewise.
- **Yogācāra/Cittamātra**: foundational; the *Triṃśikā* with Sthiramati's commentary is the basis of the school's later development.

## Disputes
- "One Vasubandhu or two?" (Frauwallner 1951).
- Authentic vs. attributed corpus.
