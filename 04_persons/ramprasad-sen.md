# Ramprasad Sen

## Identity
Bengali Shakta poet-saint, devotee of Kali, and the defining voice of the Bengali Shakta *padavali* (devotional-song) tradition (~1718–1775). Born to a Bengali Brahmin Vaidya family at Kumarhatta/Halisahar near Calcutta; traditional accounts report a youthful clerical employment in Calcutta, miraculous interventions by Kali, and patronage by Raja Krishnachandra of Nadia. His compositions — addressed primarily to Kali, with the goddess as wayward Mother and the devotee as suffering child — defined a new Bengali devotional register that fused Vaishnava bhakti's intimacy with Shakta tantric content. Ramprasad's songs (the *Shaktigiti*) and those of his successor Kamalakanta Bhattacharya are the foundational corpus of Bengali Kali-bhakti and were central to the religious world of 19th-century Bengal — Ramakrishna Paramahamsa sang them constantly, and they shape the modern Western image of Kali via the Vivekananda-Ramakrishna lineage.

## Historical evidence
Substantially hagiographical for the biography (the *Sadhak Ranjan* and later compilations); the songs are documented in 18th–19th-c. Bengali manuscripts and the major 19th-c. Bengali printed devotional anthologies.

## Works
The *Shaktigiti* corpus (Bengali Shakta hymns — Kali and Tara). *Kalikamangal* (Kalikirtan), in the Bengali *mangalkavya* tradition. *Krishna Kirtan* (Vaishnava lyrics — minor). *Bidya Sundara* (verse romance with religious-allegorical layer).

## Role in the tradition
Foundational figure of Bengali Shakta devotional poetry; principal channel by which Tantric Kali-worship was made accessible in personal-devotional rather than ritual-esoteric register. Ramakrishna's 19th-c. theology and the Vivekananda-Ramakrishna globalization of Bengal Vedanta-Shakta both presuppose Ramprasad's poetic-theological vocabulary.
