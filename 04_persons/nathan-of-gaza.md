# Nathan of Gaza

## Identity
Brilliant young Sephardic-Ashkenazi Talmudist-Kabbalist (1643–1680), the theological architect of the Sabbatean movement. Born in Jerusalem to an Ashkenazi family; trained in Gaza in halakhah and Lurianic Kabbalah. In 1665 (at age 22) he proclaimed Shabbatai Tzvi the Messiah and provided the Lurianic-Kabbalistic theological framework that sustained the movement. After Shabbatai's 1666 conversion, Nathan composed the *Iggeret Magen Avraham* and the *Drush ha-Tanninim* (Treatise on the Dragons) defending the apostasy as a redemptive descent of the messiah into the realm of the *kelippot*. Nathan was excommunicated multiple times by anti-Sabbatean rabbis; spent his later years traveling among Sabbatean communities (Salonica, Sofia, Belgrade, Adrianople, Italy); died at Skopje 1680.

## Historical evidence
Substantial corpus of Sabbatean writings (much preserved through Sabbatean and Donmeh communities, some through anti-Sabbatean heresiographies); contemporary correspondence on both sides of the controversy; Scholem's exhaustive reconstruction.

## Works
*Drush ha-Tanninim* (Treatise on the Dragons). *Drush ha-Menorah*. *Iggeret Magen Avraham* (defending the apostasy). *Zemir 'Aritzim* and other liturgical-theological compositions. Numerous letters and *drushim*.

## Role in the tradition
Nathan is the principal Sabbatean theologian; without him, Shabbatai is an unstable mystic, not a movement. Nathan's theological work — reading Shabbatai's biography through Lurianic categories — is one of the most radical and ingenious heterodox transformations of pre-modern Jewish theology, and a key reference point for the modern study of antinomian mysticism.
