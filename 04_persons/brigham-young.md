# Brigham Young

## Identity
Second President of the LDS Church (1847–1877); led the migration of Mormon refugees from Nauvoo, Illinois to the Great Salt Lake basin (1846–1847) after Joseph Smith's assassination; first territorial governor of Utah (1851–1857); president of the LDS Church through its institutional consolidation in the Mountain West.

## Role in the tradition
The institutional founder of the contemporary Utah-based LDS Church; the migration to Utah established Mormonism as a geographically rooted religious-cultural community.

## Disputes
- **Mountain Meadows Massacre (1857)**: degree of Young's foreknowledge / authorization of the massacre of an Arkansas emigrant party by Mormon militia and Paiute allies is debated.
- **Adam-God doctrine**: Young taught a controversial doctrine (Adam = God the Father in our world) that subsequent LDS leadership rejected and now considers heterodox; the historiography of *what exactly Young taught and when* is a Mormon-studies subject.
- **Racial priesthood ban**: Young instituted (and subsequent LDS leadership maintained until 1978) an exclusion of Black men from the priesthood; the historical record is well established and the LDS Church has formally repudiated the underlying racial theology (2013 "Race and the Priesthood" essay).
