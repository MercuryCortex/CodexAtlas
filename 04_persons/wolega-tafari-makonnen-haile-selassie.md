# Tafari Makonnen / Haile Selassie I

## Identity
Last emperor of Ethiopia (regnal name from coronation 1930: Haile Selassie I, "Power of the Trinity"). Born Tafari Makonnen 1892; regent and *Ras* from 1916; crowned Negus 1928 and *Negusä Nägäst* (King of Kings) 1930; resisted the Italian invasion 1935; in exile 1936–1941; restored after British liberation 1941; reigned until his overthrow by the Derg military council 1974; died (or was killed) 1975. He claimed Solomonic-dynasty descent — the line traced through Menelik I (son of Solomon and Sheba per the *Kebra Nagast*) down to the Solomonic emperors of Ethiopia, restored 1270 and continuing to Haile Selassie. The 1955 revised constitution explicitly stated that the throne descends from "the line of Menelik I, son of the Queen of Ethiopia, the Queen of Sheba, and King Solomon of Jerusalem."

## Historical evidence
Extensively documented modern figure: Ethiopian state records; international diplomatic records (League of Nations, UN); autobiography; biographies (Marcus 1987 most thorough).

## Works
- *My Life and Ethiopia's Progress* (autobiography, vol. 1, 1937; vol. 2, 1972).
- Public speeches and proclamations as emperor; notable: 1936 League of Nations speech against the Italian invasion ("It is us today; it will be you tomorrow").

## Role in the tradition
For Phase 8: Haile Selassie's principal religious-historical significance is *outside* Ethiopia, as the messianic figure of Rastafarianism. The Jamaican Rastafarian movement, emerging ~1930 (Leonard Howell, Joseph Hibbert, Archibald Dunkley, Robert Hinds), identified the new emperor Ras Tafari (crowned 1930) as the messianic Black King prophesied by Marcus Garvey ("Look to Africa for the crowning of a Black King") and as the returning Christ (drawing on Revelation 5:5's Lion of the Tribe of Judah, an Ethiopian imperial title; on Revelation 19:16's King of Kings and Lord of Lords, his titles). He visited Jamaica in 1966 — an event of central Rastafarian religious significance, "Grounation Day," April 21. Haile Selassie himself never publicly accepted the Rastafarian identification, reportedly stating during a 1967 interview that he was "a man and a mortal," but did not aggressively reject it either.

Within Ethiopia, his role was that of Christian Orthodox emperor and modernizing ruler; the famine of 1973 in Wollo, his perceived indifference, and military discontent led to his overthrow in September 1974.

## Disputes
- **Death (1975).** Officially natural causes; widely suspected to have been killed by the Derg; remains were recovered in 1992 from under a palace toilet and reinterred in 2000 at Holy Trinity Cathedral, Addis Ababa.
- **Solomonic descent.** Historically the Solomonic descent is a theological-political claim, not securely demonstrable. The Zagwe-Solomonic transition 1270 is a real political-historical change but the Davidic genealogy is mythologically-constituted.
- **Rastafarian theology.** Whether Haile Selassie is god / Christ / a divine king / a *symbol* of Black liberation is variously held across Rastafarian sects (Nyahbinghi, Bobo Ashanti, Twelve Tribes); within Ethiopia he is honored as emperor without the messianic-divine reading.
