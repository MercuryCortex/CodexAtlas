# Pherecydes of Syros

## Identity
Pherecydes of Syros (c. -580 to c. -520) is a **transitional figure** between mythological theogony and philosophical cosmology — sometimes called "the first writer of Greek prose theology" (the title goes to him *or* to [[anaximander]] depending on chronological priority and what counts as "prose"; the cases are close). Active on the Cycladic island of Syros in the mid-6th c. BCE, Pherecydes composed a **cosmogonic-theological prose work** variously titled *Heptamychos* ("Seven Recesses"), *Theology*, or *Pentemychos* — known principally from substantial quotations in Damascius's *De Principiis* (6th c. CE). His system features **three primordial deities** — *Zas* (Zeus), *Chthoniē* (Earth), and *Chronos* (Time) — who exist eternally and from whose interaction the cosmos emerges. He is also the **traditional teacher of [[pythagoras]]**, the originator of Greek **transmigration / soul-immortality doctrine** ([[reincarnation]]), and one of the first attested Greek figures with explicit **Near Eastern (Phoenician, Babylonian, Egyptian) intellectual borrowings**. For the Codex Atlas mission Pherecydes is a critical missing piece: he sits at the **junction** of pre-Socratic philosophy, Orphic-mystery theology, and Greek-Near-Eastern transmission.

## Historical evidence
- **Damascius** *De Principiis* — preserves the longest direct quotations from Pherecydes (~24+ lines of cosmogonic narrative).
- **Diogenes Laertius** *Lives* 1.116–122 — biographical entry, partly legendary.
- **Cicero** *Tusculan Disputations* 1.16.38 — attributes the immortality-of-souls doctrine to Pherecydes as Pythagoras's teacher.
- **Aristotle** *Metaphysics* 1.4 (1091b4–10) — treats Pherecydes among the "mixed" theologians who **also philosophize**.
- **Origen** *Contra Celsum* 6.42 — preserves cosmogonic detail (Cronus's battle with Ophioneus).
- **Plutarch** and **Maximus of Tyre** — additional fragmentary references.

The fragment-base is small but exceptionally rich — almost every surviving fragment is theologically important.

## Works
- ***Heptamychos*** / ***Pentemychos*** / ***Theology*** (lost; ~30 fragments). Composed c. -550 to -520, conventionally in **Ionian prose** (though some scholars argue for a partly poetic form). Treats the eternal pre-existence of three primordials (Zas, Chthoniē, Chronos), their cosmogonic interaction (Zas weaves a cloak depicting earth and ocean and gifts it to Chthoniē in cosmic-marriage), the cosmic-battle of Chronos with Ophioneus (the serpentine cosmic adversary), and the seven *mychoi* (recesses / chambers) of the underworld with souls passing through them.

## Role in the tradition
Pherecydes's foundational moves:

1. **Three eternal primordials.** Where Hesiod has Chaos as the first being "coming into being," Pherecydes has **Zas, Chthoniē, and Chronos as eternally pre-existent** — a major step toward philosophical theology (eternal first principles rather than chronological first things). This anticipates Anaximander's *apeiron* and Parmenides's eternal Being.

2. **Cosmogonic marriage (Zas-Chthoniē).** Zas weaves a cloak depicting the cosmos and gifts it to Chthoniē; this is one of the earliest articulations of **cosmic marriage as cosmogony** — a theological structure with parallels in Mesopotamian Apsu-Tiamat, Egyptian Geb-Nut, Indian Heaven-Earth Dyāvāprithivī, and Phoenician El-Asherah cosmogonies. West (1997) treats this as evidence of substantial Near Eastern transmission.

3. **Chronos-Ophioneus cosmic battle.** Time (Chronos) battles the cosmic serpent Ophioneus, who is cast down into Tartarus — structurally parallel to Mesopotamian Marduk-Tiamat ([[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]]) and Hittite Storm-god-Illuyanka ([[phase-1-030-illuyanka-myth]]) cosmic dragon-slaying. **The clearest single piece of Near Eastern cosmogonic transmission into pre-Socratic Greek thought**.

4. **Soul-immortality and transmigration**. Pherecydes is the **traditional teacher of [[pythagoras]]** and the **first attested Greek figure to teach soul-immortality and transmigration**. Cicero, Apollonius of Tyana, and others credit him with the doctrine that runs through Pythagoras → Empedocles → Plato → Plotinus → patristic engagement with [[reincarnation]] (rejected as heresy by orthodox Christianity but engaged in detail).

5. **Egyptian-Phoenician learning.** Ancient tradition (Apollonius of Tyana's *Letter to Pherecydes*, Diogenes Laertius) reports Pherecydes acquired his wisdom from **secret Phoenician books** and from **Egyptian priestly instruction**. Whether literal or legendary, the **fact of the tradition** places Pherecydes at the conventional juncture of Greek-Near-Eastern intellectual transmission — a juncture made plausible by his location on Syros (a Cycladic trade-hub island) and his lifetime overlap with intensive Greek-Phoenician-Egyptian contact (Naucratis founded ~-625; Greek mercenaries in Egypt under Psamtik I; Phoenician trading-settlements across the Aegean).

## Disputes
- **Prose or poetry?** Whether Pherecydes wrote in Ionian prose (the standard view) or in some intermediate prose-poetic form is debated. Schibli (1990) defends substantial prose.
- **Was he philosophical or mythological?** Aristotle's characterization (the "mixed" theologians who also philosophize) puts Pherecydes on the cusp — neither purely mythological like Hesiod nor purely philosophical like Anaximander. The transition itself is what's important.
- **Pythagoras-teacher tradition.** Whether Pherecydes literally taught Pythagoras is uncertain; the **tradition** is well-attested from at least Cicero onward. Schibli 1990 treats the teaching-relationship as plausible-but-unprovable.
- **Near Eastern transmission.** The cosmic-battle Chronos-Ophioneus parallel to Marduk-Tiamat and to Hittite Illuyanka is strong enough that direct transmission is widely accepted (West 1997; López-Ruiz 2010 *When the Gods Were Born*). Resistance to the transmission claim has weakened substantially in current scholarship.
