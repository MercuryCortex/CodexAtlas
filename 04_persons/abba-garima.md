# Abba Garima (Yəsḥaq)

## Identity
**Abba Garima** (royal birth-name *Yəsḥaq* / Isaac), traditionally one of the [[the-nine-saints]], is the eponymous monastic founder of the Abba Garima monastery near Adwa in highland Tigray (5 km east of Adwa town). By Ethiopian-hagiographic tradition he was the son of a Byzantine king (variously identified) and renounced his royal status to become a monk in Aksum; his foundation became one of the most important early Ethiopian monasteries. The monastery preserves the [[phase-4-080-garima-gospels]] — three Ge'ez illuminated Gospel codices whose radiocarbon ranges (330–540, 430–650, and 530–660 CE) place them in or very near Abba Garima's lifetime, making them *the oldest surviving illuminated Christian Gospel books in the world* and giving him eponymous association — possibly literal — with their production.

## Historical evidence

- **The Garima Gospels** themselves — three codices preserved continuously at Abba Garima monastery; radiocarbon-dated by the Capelli/Bausi 2013 study and art-historically analyzed by McKenzie and Watson 2016. The dating windows for the three codices straddle Abba Garima's traditional era and constitute the strongest material evidence for the monastery's antiquity and the early-Aksumite-Christian art tradition.
- **The continuous monastic occupation** of the Abba Garima site since at least the 6th century — confirmed by manuscript stratigraphy and the unbroken hagiographic-liturgical tradition centered there.
- **The Ethiopian *gadl* (hagiography) of Abba Garima** — surviving in Ge'ez manuscript; medieval-elaborate but with a defensible historical core: a Syriac-speaking Miaphysite refugee monk founded the monastery in the era of the Nine Saints, and the Garima Gospels were produced there.

## Works
The Garima Gospels are *traditionally* attributed to Abba Garima's own hand, with the further legend that he wrote one of them in a single day with divine miraculous assistance. Modern scholarship (McKenzie and Watson 2016) treats the codices as products of the monastery's scriptorium in the era of the Nine Saints rather than the autograph of any individual. The codices are extraordinary art-historical witnesses: they include the world's oldest surviving Christian portraits-of-the-evangelists (the seated-evangelist iconographic type that dominates Greek and Latin medieval Gospel-book illumination) and the *tempietto* / canon-table architectural frames that appear elsewhere only later.

## Role in the tradition
Abba Garima is one of the most honored of the Nine Saints. The monastery functions as a major pilgrimage site and manuscript repository. The Garima Gospels — kept in the monastery treasury and rarely displayed — are the church's foundational material artifact, materially anchoring its claim to apostolic-era continuity. The 2013 radiocarbon study materially elevated the Garima monastery's standing in global manuscript-studies scholarship, demonstrating that Aksumite Christianity produced illuminated codices contemporary with or earlier than the great Byzantine and Latin Gospel-book traditions (the Rabbula Gospels are 586 CE; the Book of Durrow is c. 680 CE; the Lindisfarne Gospels c. 715 CE; the Garima Gospels are decisively earlier or contemporary with all).

## Disputes
- **Dating.** The 2013 radiocarbon analysis established the codices as 4th–7th century CE, but the precise window for each codex (and which strata are original vs. restorations) remains debated. McKenzie and Watson 2016 give the consensus art-historical reading.
- **Whether the historical Abba Garima personally produced any of the codices.** Probably not — the codices likely emerged from the monastery scriptorium in the era of, or shortly after, his life. The traditional attribution is a hagiographic-eponymous gesture.
- **Origin of Abba Garima.** Various traditions; Byzantine-royal background is the most-recited but historically vague. The shared Syriac character with the rest of the Nine Saints is the strongest evidence for an Eastern-Roman provenance.
