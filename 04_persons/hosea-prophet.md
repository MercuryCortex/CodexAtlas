# Hosea

## Identity
Hosea son of Beeri is the only canonical prophet who was himself a native of the Northern Kingdom of Israel, prophesying there during its final, chaotic decades (the late reign of Jeroboam II through the dynastic instability of the 740s and 730s into the run-up to the Assyrian conquest of 722 BCE). His prophetic ministry is structured around the metaphor — or, in some readings, the lived enactment — of his marriage to Gomer, who is unfaithful: Israel is YHWH's unfaithful wife who has prostituted herself with the Baals.

## Historical evidence
Internal biographical detail in Hosea 1 and 3 (the marriage to Gomer, the named children Jezreel, Lo-Ruhama, Lo-Ammi); references to specific contemporary kings (Jeroboam, Menahem, Pekah, Hoshea); specific contemporary political crises (the Syro-Ephraimite war, the Assyrian advance). Consistent with the historical reconstruction of the period.

## Works
The book of Hosea — 14 chapters — mixing narrative biography (chs. 1–3) and prophetic oracles (chs. 4–14).

## Role in the tradition
Inaugurates the *marriage metaphor* for the YHWH-Israel relationship, which becomes a central biblical trope (Jeremiah, Ezekiel, Isaiah 40–66, Song of Songs read allegorically, the New Testament Church-as-bride). His attacks on Baal worship are the most extended in the Hebrew Bible; his theological vocabulary (*ḥesed*, "covenant loyalty") becomes central to Deuteronomic theology. Considered the northern prophetic source that flows into the Deuteronomic stream after 722.

## Disputes
- **The marriage to Gomer.** Whether biographical, allegorical, or symbolic-prophetic enactment.
- **Editorial Judahite layers.** Some Judah-favorable insertions (e.g., 1:7, 3:5) are widely identified as post-722 southern additions.
