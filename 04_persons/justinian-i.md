# Justinian I

Byzantine emperor 527–565. Closed the Platonic Academy at Athens in 529 (a symbolic terminus for institutional pagan philosophy in the eastern Mediterranean), promulgated the *Corpus Iuris Civilis* (which became the foundation of European law), rebuilt the Hagia Sophia after the Nika Riots (537), and presided over the Second Council of Constantinople (553) which condemned Origen and the "Three Chapters." His reign overlaps with the first appearance of the Pseudo-Dionysian corpus on the Monophysite side of the Christological disputes.

## Substrate-erasure consequences
- The 529 closure of the Platonic Academy (and the simultaneous closure of various pagan-philosophical schools across the empire) is the **institutional terminus of state-tolerated pagan philosophy** in the eastern Mediterranean. The last seven Athenian Platonists (Damascius, Simplicius, Priscian, Eulamius, Hermeias, Diogenes, Isidorus) emigrated to the Persian court of Khosrow I; some returned under a 532 treaty's protection clause. The institutional substrate is destroyed; the philosophical content survives via Christian Neoplatonism (Pseudo-Dionysius, Boethius) and Persian / later Islamic transmission (Damascius's late commentaries; the *Theology of Aristotle* / Plotinian fragments in Arabic).
- The 553 anti-Origenist anathemas (whether properly conciliar or pre-conciliar synodal — see [[event-council-of-constantinople-553]]) closed the open Christian transmission of the Origenist-Platonist tradition; the substrate survives in the apophatic-Dionysian register but the cosmological-Origenist register is suppressed. Ramelli 2013.
- The Justinianic project synthesizes **caesaropapism + Christian-imperial substrate-erasure** at maximum institutional intensity.
