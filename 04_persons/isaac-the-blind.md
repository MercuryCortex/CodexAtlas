# Isaac the Blind (Yitzhak Sagi-Nahor)

## Identity
Late-12th-/early-13th-c. Provençal Jewish mystic, son of Rabbi Abraham ben David of Posquières (the Ravad), often regarded as the *founding figure* of historical Kabbalah — the figure with whom Scholem identified the transition from the inchoate Bahir-tradition into a recognizably Kabbalistic mystical school. Isaac was blind from birth (hence the honorific "of much light" — *Sagi-Nahor* — by the well-known Jewish euphemistic-blessing convention); his teaching was transmitted orally to his disciples in Gerona (Ezra ben Solomon and Azriel of Gerona), from whom Kabbalah began to circulate in writing through the 13th-c. Geronese school.

## Historical evidence
Documented through references in the Geronese Kabbalistic literature (Ezra ben Solomon, Azriel, Nachmanides — the latter cites him as an authority); a *Commentary on Sefer Yetzirah* attributed to him survives; Scholem's *Origins of the Kabbalah* is the foundational scholarly reconstruction.

## Works
- *Commentary on Sefer Yetzirah* — only major surviving work, foundational to the kabbalistic interpretation of the *Sefer Yetzirah*.
- Letters and short texts preserved in Gerona-school manuscripts.

## Role in the tradition
- **Founder-figure of historical Kabbalah** (Scholem's framing): the orally-transmitted theosophy of the sefirot, the linguistic-creative theology, the Provençal-Gerona transmission chain.
- **Letter to Nachmanides and the Geronese**: a famous letter expresses Isaac's concern about written transmission of esoteric material — a transmission-anxiety the next generation overrode.

## Disputes
- Pre-Isaac sources of Provençal mysticism (the *Bahir*'s redaction; the *Iyyun* circle).
- The attribution of various Geronese texts (Isaac's vs. his disciples').
