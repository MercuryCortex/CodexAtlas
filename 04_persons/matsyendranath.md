# Matsyendranath

## Identity
The *ādi-guru* (founding teacher) of the Nāth sampradāya and the principal figure to whom medieval Hindu Tantric tradition attributes the public transmission of the Kaula (the secret yoginī-clan Tantric current) — placing him in the late 9th to 10th century in the eastern Indian region (Bengal, Kamarupa/Assam, or Nepal — sources differ). Per legend, born as Mīna (Fish) and recovered by Śiva who taught him Tantra in fish-shape — a transmission-myth that gives him his name (Lord of the Fish / Fish-Indra). Variously identified across traditions: in Hindu Kaula sources as the founder of the Yoginī Kaula; in Buddhist mahāsiddha lists as Mīnapā, one of the 84 *mahāsiddhas* of Indian Vajrayana; in Nepalese tradition as the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara incarnate (the syncretic Hindu-Buddhist Matsyendranath cult at Kathmandu's Bunga Dyah / Rato Macchindranath festival is one of the principal Hindu-Buddhist syncretic devotional cults of South Asia, with continuous ritual practice into the present). Guru of [[gorakhnath]] per the standard Nath account.

## Works
- ***Kaulajñānanirṇaya*** (Determination of Kaula Knowledge): a substantial Tantric text in 24 *paṭalas*; the earliest and most authoritative surviving work attributed to Matsyendra. Treats Kaula doctrine, ritual, the structure of the yogic body, mantras, and Yoginī worship. The colophon ascribes it to "Macchanda-pāda" / Matsyendra. Modern critical-philological study (Goudriaan and Schoterman, Sanderson) confirms it as a 10th–11th-c. composition genuinely in the Kaula stream Abhinavagupta inherits.
- Various other shorter Kaula and yogic texts attributed in Nath tradition are of uncertain date and authorship.

## Role in the tradition
- **Nath sampradaya founder**: Matsyendranath is the first of the *nava-nāthas* (Nine Naths) in the Nath lineage; his disciple Gorakhnath is the second and the principal organizer of the order. The Nath sampradaya — wandering yogis with characteristic earring-piercing (*kānphaṭa*), with Hatha Yoga as their core practical discipline, with a doctrine combining Kaula Tantric and Shaiva sources — was a major medieval Indian renouncer tradition with sub-continental reach.
- **Hatha Yoga origin**: Matsyendra and Gorakh together are the legendary originators of *Hatha Yoga* — the body-discipline (postures, breath-control, mudras, bandhas, internal alchemical ascent of *kuṇḍalinī*) that develops in the medieval Nath tradition and reaches its classical textual formulation in [[svatmarama|Svātmārāma]]'s 15th-c. *Haṭhayoga-Pradīpikā*. Modern global yoga (Krishnamacharya → Iyengar / Pattabhi Jois / Desikachar) descends, with major 19th–20th-c. transformations, from this Nath-Hatha lineage.
- **Public transmission of Kaula**: per Abhinavagupta's *Tantrāloka* (1.7), Matsyendra is the historical figure through whom the Kaula stream — until then a secret Yoginī-clan tradition — was made teachable to qualified disciples generally. The Kaula transmission is one of the central streams that Abhinavagupta's Trika synthesis absorbs.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Hindu-Buddhist syncretic identity (Hindu Nath / Buddhist mahāsiddha)**: Matsyendra's identification in the Buddhist mahāsiddha lists as *Mīnapā* makes him one of the clearest cases of a single named medieval Indian Tantric figure absorbed simultaneously into Hindu and Vajrayāna Buddhist hagiographies. The Buddhist Mīnapā is one of the 84 mahāsiddhas whose practices are foundational to Tibetan Kagyu and Sakya tantric lineages. Samuel 2008 and White 1996 examine the Hindu Tantra ↔ Vajrayāna intersection in detail.
- **Kathmandu Avalokiteśvara identification**: the Rato Matsyendranath / Bunga Dyah cult in Kathmandu Valley identifies Matsyendra with the Bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara — a Hindu-Buddhist composite deity with continuous ritual practice (the annual chariot festival is among the principal religious events of the Newar Buddhist-Hindu community).
- **Indian Tantra to Tibetan Vajrayāna transmission**: Matsyendra-Mīnapā exemplifies the Indian-Tantric → Tibetan-Vajrayāna transmission lineage — Sanderson 2009 argues that much medieval Tantric Buddhism is downstream of Śaiva Tantric originals; the structural-textual evidence Matsyendra-Mīnapā provides is one of the touchstone cases.

## Disputes
- **Historicity vs. compound-figure**: whether the historical Matsyendra is one person or a composite of several Bengali-Nepalese-Assamese Kaula founders is contested (White 1996 argues for substantive historicity; some skeptics argue for a layered tradition).
- **Date**: 9th c. (some), 10th c. (most consensus), 11th c. (revisionist) — the range is constrained by Abhinavagupta's citation (Tantrāloka, ~1000) as terminus ante quem.
- **Gorakh-Matsyendra dating relation**: the textual evidence suggests Matsyendra ~10th c. and Gorakh ~11th–12th c., though the traditional disciple-relation may collapse a longer transmission.
- **Sanderson's Śaiva → Buddhist directionality**: contested by Davidson 2002 and other Buddhist-studies scholars.
