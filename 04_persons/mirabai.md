# Mirabai

## Identity
Rajput princess and Krishna-devotee, one of the most beloved female saints of North Indian Hindu tradition (c. 1498–c. 1547). Born to the Rathore Rajput dynasty of Merta (Rajasthan); married to Bhojraj, the heir of the Sisodia Rajput dynasty of Mewar at Chittor (the marriage probably c. 1516); widowed early; her devotion to Krishna and her refusal of expected Rajput-court religious observance produced a sustained conflict with her in-laws, who (per tradition) attempted to poison and kill her multiple times. She left the Chittor court for Vrindavan, then (per the standard tradition) for Dwarka, where she is reported to have merged with the image of Krishna in the temple. Her songs — addressed to Krishna as Giridhar Gopal — express defiance of caste-and-rank constraint, ecstatic devotion as the female bride of the divine Beloved, and the *sant* ideal of bhakti as superior to ritual and learning.

## Historical evidence
Mixed: the historical Rajput princess is firmly documented in Mewar court records and contemporary references; the religious-poetic figure is more difficult to reconstruct, since the bulk of the popular "Mira corpus" is post-Mira accretion (Hawley 2005). The hagiographical biography (Priyadas, Nabhadas *Bhaktamala*) is substantially shaped by later devotional needs.

## Works
A large fluid corpus of *padas* / *bhajans*, no manuscript closer to her lifetime than the late 17th century. Scholarly attempts to identify the "earliest stratum" attributable to the historical Mira have been variously argued.

## Role in the tradition
Mirabai is the model of female Krishna-bhakti devotion and one of the central voices of the North Indian *bhakti sant* tradition. Her caste-and-gender-defiance has made her especially important in modern Indian feminist hagiography. Her absence from the Sikh Adi Granth (which incorporates her contemporaries [[kabir]], Ravidas, Namdev) is itself a notable feature of the early-modern bhakti landscape. Part of the broader [[event-bhakti-movement-c-700-1700|bhakti movement]] arc; her Krishna-bhakti is contemporary with [[chaitanya-mahaprabhu]]'s Gaudiya Krishna-bhakti, [[vallabhacharya]]'s Pushtimarg, and [[surdas]]'s Braj poetry.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Female mystic bridal poetry across traditions**: structurally cognate with [[andal]] (8th-c. Tamil Vaishnava), Mahādēviyakka (12th-c. Kannada Vīraśaiva, [[basava]]'s circle), Hadewijch (13th-c. Beguine), Mechthild of Magdeburg, Teresa of Avila (16th-c. Carmelite). Hawley & Wulff 1996 *Devī* treats Mira within this cross-tradition category.
- **Sufi-Vaishnava shared *viraha-firāq* idiom**: Mira's mode of female-soul yearning for the divine Beloved shares vocabulary and affective register with contemporaneous Indo-Persian Sufi poetry.
