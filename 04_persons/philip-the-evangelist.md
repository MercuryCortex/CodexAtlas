# Philip the Evangelist

## Identity
One of the **Seven Hellenist deacons** (with [[stephen-protomartyr]]) of Acts 6:5, who after Stephen's stoning and the consequent persecution-dispersal traveled to **Samaria** (Acts 8:5–13), preached and baptized — including [[simon-magus]] who was so impressed by the apostolic power that he attempted to purchase it. Subsequently directed by an angel to the Gaza road, where he encountered and baptized the Ethiopian court official of Queen Candace (Acts 8:26–40) — a programmatic moment for African Christian origins. Later resident at Caesarea Maritima with **four daughters, all prophetesses** (Acts 21:8–9).

## Historical evidence
Single-source (Acts), but reasonably detailed and plausible. Papias's later confusion of Philip-the-Evangelist with [[philip-apostle]] (placing the latter, with his daughters, at Hierapolis) suggests that some of the Philip-the-Apostle tradition is properly the Evangelist's. The **Ethiopian eunuch episode** has been variously historically read: as a literary template for cross-cultural Christianization, or as an authentic early encounter with the Aksumite/Meroitic court (the actual identity of "Candace" — a Meroitic queenly title — is well-attested).

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
**African Christian origins**: the Ethiopian-eunuch episode is the foundational story of African Christianity, central to Ethiopian Orthodox identity. **Samaritan mission**: first attested Christian penetration of Samaria — a politically and theologically charged frontier. **Women's prophecy**: his four prophetess daughters at Caesarea are explicit data for women's prophetic ministry in the apostolic age.

## Disputes
- **Conflation with the Apostle.** Papias's confusion is the source of much later muddle.
- **Historicity of the Ethiopian episode.** Theologically shaped but plausibly grounded.
