# Bernard-Raymond Fabré-Palaprat

## Identity
French physician (1773–1838), former Catholic seminarian, and **founder of the [[tradition-eglise-johannite|Église Johannite des Chrétiens Primitifs]]** (1804/1812) and the modern *Ordre du Temple* (Paris, 1804). Born in Cordes-sur-Ciel (Tarn) to a minor-aristocratic family; educated at the Jesuit college in Cahors; abandoned the priesthood for medicine; settled in Paris during the Revolution and Napoleonic period. **Founded the Order of the Temple in 1804 in Paris** with the support of approximately 200 French Masons and ex-Catholic clergy, anchoring his foundation on the **forged Larmenius Charter** (a document dated 1324 claiming continuous Templar-Grand-Master succession from [[jacques-de-molay|Molay]]'s named successor *Johannes Marcus Larmenius* through 22 Grand Masters to Fabré-Palaprat). The Larmenius Charter is **universally recognized as forgery** by modern critical scholarship (Barber 2006, Hanegraaff 2012, Collin 1985).

Fabré-Palaprat's distinctive contribution is the **integration of three contested traditions**:
1. **Templar-succession claim** (via the forged Larmenius Charter).
2. **Baptist-centered Christianity** — explicitly claiming the medieval Templars had carried a Mandaean-style John-the-Baptist tradition from Outremer back to Europe (one of the principal early-19th-c. articulations of the [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis|Templar-Gnostic transmission hypothesis]]).
3. **Western esoteric / Masonic / Hermetic integration** — Fabré-Palaprat's Paris circle overlapped substantially with continental Masonic-Templar-revival networks descending from [[karl-gotthelf-von-hund|von Hund]]'s Strict Templar Observance.

## Historical evidence
- Substantial 19th-c. French archival documentation of the Order of the Temple (1804–1838): institutional papers, member-lists, ritual texts.
- Fabré-Palaprat's published works (*Manuel des Chevaliers*, *Levitikon*, *Statuts*).
- Catholic-polemical literature targeting his organization.
- Modern critical scholarship — definitive on the Larmenius Charter as forgery.

## Works
- **Larmenius Charter (Charta Transmissionis)** — forged 1804; the foundational document.
- **Statuts de l'Ordre du Temple** (1804 onward) — institutional constitution.
- ***Manuel des Chevaliers de l'Ordre du Temple*** (1817; expanded 1825) — Order ritual and ceremony.
- ***Levitikon*** (Paris, 1831) — Johannite Church gospel-and-liturgy. **Baptist-centered Christology**, explicit anti-Petrine-Pauline polemic.

## Role in the tradition

- **Founder of modern neo-Templarism in its institutional-religious form.** Subsequent self-identified neo-Templar bodies — from 19th-c. continental revivals through the 20th-c. OSMTH-style organizations to the [[event-solar-temple-mass-suicides-1994-1997|Order of the Solar Temple]] — substantially descend (institutionally or ideologically) from the Fabré-Palaprat 1804 foundation.
- **Principal early-19th-c. articulator of the Mandaean-Johannite-Templar legendary chain.** This is one of the foundational ideational moves of modern Western esoteric Templarism: the claim that the medieval Templars, through Outremer contact, had inherited a Baptist-centered Eastern Christianity which they preserved against the institutional Pauline-Petrine Church.
- **The Larmenius Charter as a canonical 19th-c. esoteric forgery.** Alongside other 19th-20th-c. esoteric-tradition forgeries (the Theosophical *Mahatma Letters*, the Anastasius-of-Sinai forgeries, the 1960s *Dossiers Secrets* of the Priory of Sion), the Larmenius Charter is one of the classic productions of the genre.
- **Bridge to the 19th-c. occult Templar literature.** Fabré-Palaprat's circle in Paris overlapped institutionally with [[joseph-von-hammer-purgstall|Hammer-Purgstall]]'s Vienna milieu and (later) with [[eliphas-levi|Lévi]]'s Paris circles. The 1804–1830 Paris Templar-revival is the **immediate institutional context** for the post-1818 Hammer-Purgstall + post-1854 Lévi occult-Templar literature.

## Connections — Fabré-Palaprat at the institutional-and-ideational bridge

- **→ [[tradition-eglise-johannite]]** *founder*.
- **→ [[karl-gotthelf-von-hund]]** *predecessor*: Fabré-Palaprat's Templar-claim builds on the 18th-c. Masonic-Templar-revival tradition von Hund inaugurated.
- **→ [[event-wilhelmsbad-convent-1782]]** *post-event-continuation*: Fabré-Palaprat's 1804 system is a continental Templar-revivalist construction *after* and *despite* the 1782 Wilhelmsbad repudiation.
- **→ [[tradition-knights-templar]]** *claimed-but-unrecoverable-parent*.
- **→ [[tradition-mandaeism]]** *claimed-Eastern-precedent*: Fabré-Palaprat's Levitikon explicitly claims the Templars had inherited Mandaean Baptist-Christianity. **No documented Templar-Mandaean contact-and-transmission.**
- **→ [[joseph-von-hammer-purgstall]]** *contemporary-parallel*: the 1818 *Mysterium Baphometis* and Fabré-Palaprat's 1817/1825 *Manuel* are contemporary contributions to the Templar-Gnostic-Johannite construction.
- **→ [[eliphas-levi]]** *successor-tradition*: Lévi's 1854 Baphomet image and Templar-Gnostic readings build substantially on the Fabré-Palaprat-and-Hammer-Purgstall foundation.
- **→ [[templar-gnostic-transmission-hypothesis]]** *foundational-early-19th-c.-contributor*.

## Disputes
- **The Larmenius Charter as forgery.** Universally accepted by modern scholarship; Fabré-Palaprat's 1804 production is internally consistent only on the assumption that he or his immediate circle authored it.
- **Whether Fabré-Palaprat was personally sincere.** Modern scholarship is split: Hanegraaff 2012 treats him as opportunistic-and-ambitious; Collin 1985 is more generous about his religious sincerity. The honest position: agnostic on personal motives, definitive on historical-evidentiary status.
- **Napoleon's actual involvement.** Unverified by independent sources. Fabré-Palaprat's *Manuel* and supporters' literature claim 1808 attendance; no contemporary Napoleonic correspondence corroborates.
