# al-Sharīf al-Raḍī (~970–1015 CE)

Buyid-era Baghdad Shia scholar and poet; *naqib* (head) of the Alid (descendants-of-Ali) community of Baghdad; compiler of the [[phase-5-058-nahj-al-balagha|*Nahj al-Balāgha*]] ("The Peak of Eloquence") — the principal Shia liturgical-rhetorical compilation of sermons, letters, and aphorisms attributed to [[ali-ibn-abi-talib]] (the First Imam of Twelver Shia).

## The *Nahj al-Balāgha*

Compiled ~1009 CE during the Buyid (Shia-dynastic) control of Baghdad, the *Nahj al-Balāgha* collects 241 sermons + 79 letters + 489 aphorisms attributed to Ali. The work is for Shia tradition second only to the Quran in liturgical-spiritual authority; the authenticity of the attribution is debated by both Sunni + Shia + Western scholars (some contents are clearly historical-Ali-attributable; others reflect later Shia theological developments). Momen 1985 documents the work's central role in Twelver Shia piety.

The *Nahj al-Balāgha* is one of the principal carriers of the philosophical-mystical strand of Shia Islam (alongside Hadith collections like `al-kafi` by al-Kulayni). Its theological framework — divine transcendence + philosophical-rationalist articulation of monotheism + ethical-political reflection in the voice of Ali — would later influence Shia philosophical theology (Mulla Sadra, the Safavid + post-Safavid Imami school).

## Cross-tradition position

al-Radi sits at one node in the cross-tradition pattern of *compiler-anthologist as tradition-canonizer*:
- Compares structurally to Guru Arjan (compiler of the [[phase-6-016-guru-granth-sahib|Guru Granth Sahib]] in 1604)
- To Ibn Hisham (editor-canonizer of Ibn Ishaq's *Sira*)
- To the patristic-Christian compilers of the New Testament canon (across Athanasius's 367 Festal Letter establishing the Christian biblical canon)

In each case: a charismatic-originating figure's voice is preserved + canonized + transmitted by a later compiler whose institutional position makes the canonization stick.
