# Tsongkhapa

## Identity
Tsongkhapa Lobsang Drakpa (1357–1419) was the principal Tibetan Buddhist reformer of the late medieval period and the founder of the Gelug (Yellow Hat) school. Born in Amdo (northeastern Tibet); studied across all major Tibetan Buddhist schools (Kadampa, Sakya, Kagyu, Nyingma); produced an enormous systematic synthesis combining strict Madhyamaka philosophy (specifically the Prāsaṅgika reading of Candrakīrti), graded Mahāyāna path-teaching (the Kadampa *lam-rim* tradition deriving from Atiśa), and complete Vajrayāna tantric practice. Founded Ganden Monastery (1409) outside Lhasa as the seat of his new school. Tsongkhapa's reformist program emphasized monastic discipline, philosophical rigor, and the indispensability of grounded Madhyamaka prerequisite before Tantric practice — corrective against perceived laxity in other Tibetan schools.

## Works
*Lam-rim Chen-mo* (Great Treatise on the Stages of the Path, 1402); *Sngags-rim Chen-mo* (Great Treatise on Tantric Practice); *Drang-nges Legs-bshad Snying-po* (on the definitive vs. interpretable, on Madhyamaka); *Lam-rim Chung-ba*; numerous commentaries on Indian Madhyamaka and Pramāṇa texts; tantric commentaries.

## Role in the tradition
Founder of the Gelug school, the dominant school of contemporary Tibetan Buddhism. The Dalai Lama lineage (beginning with Gendün Drub, Tsongkhapa's direct disciple, ~1391–1474) is a Gelug-school institution. Tsongkhapa's philosophical school produced an enormous post-medieval Tibetan systematic corpus.
