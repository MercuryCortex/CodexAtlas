# Peter the Apostle

## Identity
Simon bar Yonah, called Peter (Greek *Petros*, Aramaic *Kepha* — both meaning "rock"), was a Galilean fisherman who became one of the inner circle of Jesus's twelve disciples and, after the resurrection appearances, a leader of the Jerusalem Christian community. His traditional martyrdom in Rome under Nero (~+64/+67) and the location of his tomb under the Vatican constitute the historical foundation of the papal claim of Roman primacy.

## Historical evidence
Strong attestation. Paul (Gal 1:18 reports a 15-day visit with Peter ~+37; Gal 2 reports both the Jerusalem Council with Peter and the Antioch confrontation) — primary, contemporary, eyewitness-level evidence of Peter as a real and prominent leader. All four Gospels (independently sourced) place him at the center of the disciple group. 1 Clement, Ignatius, Papias-via-Eusebius (Papias claims Mark wrote down Peter's preaching) attest his post-resurrection prominence. Archaeological investigation under St. Peter's Basilica (1939–68, under Pius XII) located 1st-century burial remains that the Vatican considers Peter's — historical certainty is unattainable but the tradition is early and consistent. For this vault, Peter matters as the *Jerusalem-Jewish-Christian* counterpart to Paul's Gentile-mission, and the legacy figure whose primacy claim grounds Catholic ecclesiology and the East-West schism over papal jurisdiction.
