# James, son of Alphaeus

## Identity
One of the Twelve, distinguished by his patronymic "son of Alphaeus" from [[james-son-of-zebedee]] and [[james-brother-of-jesus]]. The Synoptic apostle-lists name him but no episode is attached to him by name. Traditional Western harmonization (going back at least to Jerome) identifies him with **James the Less** (Mk 15:40, the brother of Joses, son of "the other Mary") and even with James the brother of Jesus — but this triple identification is critically rejected today; the three Jameses are best treated as distinct individuals.

## Historical evidence
Name-only attestation. The Alphaeus patronymic matches Mark's "Levi son of Alphaeus" (Mk 2:14 = [[matthew-apostle]]), raising the possibility that James and Matthew/Levi were brothers — but this is speculative.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
A liturgical placeholder in the apostolic college; iconographically subordinate. Some Eastern traditions assign him a Syrian or Persian mission; the legends are late and contradictory.

## Disputes
- **Triple identification with James the Less / James the Just.** Critically rejected by modern scholarship (Bauckham 2002) which treats the three Jameses as distinct.
- **Relationship to Matthew/Levi son of Alphaeus.** Plausible but unprovable.
