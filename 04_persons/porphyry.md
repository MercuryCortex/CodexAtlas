# Porphyry

Phoenician-born student and editor of Plotinus (234–~305). Edited the *Enneads* into the form we have, wrote the foundational *Life of Plotinus*, and produced his own substantial corpus: the *Isagoge* (introduction to Aristotelian logic, the standard textbook for nearly two millennia), the *Sententiae*, and the lost fifteen-book *Against the Christians* — burned under Theodosius II in 448 but partly reconstructable from Christian quotations. Porphyry's *Letter to Anebo* (lost) raising sceptical questions about Egyptian theurgy provoked Iamblichus' *De Mysteriis* in response.

## The Sethian confrontation
*Vita Plotini* 16 — Porphyry's own report — records that in Plotinus's Roman circle (~263–268) Christian and other heretical books were in circulation, "by Alexander the Libyan, Philocomus, Demostratus and Lydus," and "they presented as revelations of Zoroaster, Zostrianos, Nicotheos, Allogenes, Messos, and many others of the same kind." Plotinus assigned the refutations: he wrote *Enneads* II.9 "Against the Gnostics" himself; Amelius wrote forty books against the *Book of Zostrianos*; and Porphyry himself wrote against the *Book of Zoroaster* (and likely also against *Allogenes*; the exact text-distribution is debated). This is the *single most documented* Christian-Gnostic / Platonist-philosophical confrontation in late antiquity, and the principal evidence that Sethian-Gnostic theological literature ([[phase-4-056-zostrianos]], [[phase-4-057-allogenes]]) was operating in shared philosophical space with school-Neoplatonism. Turner 2001; Burns 2014 *Apocalypse of the Alien God*; Tardieu 1996.
