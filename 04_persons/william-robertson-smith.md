# William Robertson Smith

## Identity
Scottish Old Testament scholar and Semiticist (1846–1894). Free Church of Scotland minister; deposed from his Aberdeen chair in 1881 for "heresy" (he had introduced Wellhausen's documentary-hypothesis source criticism into the *Encyclopaedia Britannica* 9th-edition Bible articles). Moved to Cambridge; Adams Professor of Arabic from 1889. Mentor and Trinity College colleague of James Frazer; his *Religion of the Semites* (1889) is the immediate predecessor and provocateur of *The Golden Bough*.

## Historical evidence
Extensively documented through Aberdeen and Cambridge university records, his own writings, the Heresy Trial papers (Free Church of Scotland), and the Johnstone-edited reassessment volume (1995).

## Works
- *The Old Testament in the Jewish Church* (1881) — the work that triggered his heresy trial.
- *Kinship and Marriage in Early Arabia* (1885) — pioneering ethnographic study using pre-Islamic Arabic sources.
- *Lectures on the Religion of the Semites* (1889) — comparative theory of sacrifice as totemic communion-meal; foundational to Frazer's and Durkheim's later sacrifice-theory; influenced Freud's *Totem and Taboo* (1913).
- General editor (with Baynes) of *Encyclopaedia Britannica* 9th edition, the "scholar's edition" (1875–1889).

## Role in the tradition
Father of British comparative-religion fieldwork-respect: his work on pre-Islamic Arabia is based on Semitic-philological-ethnographic sources, not armchair comparativism. The 1881 heresy trial made him a cause célèbre of British biblical scholarship's confrontation with German higher criticism.
