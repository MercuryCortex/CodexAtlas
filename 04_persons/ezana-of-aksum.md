# Ezana of Aksum

## Identity
**Ezana** (Greek *Aizanes / Aezanas*) was king of [[aksum]] in the mid-4th century CE; conventional reign-dates c. 320–360. He is the first known monarch outside the Roman Empire to convert to Christianity and to officially Christianize a state — the conversion documented externally in [[athanasius]]'s *Apologia ad Constantium* 31 (357 CE) and on Ezana's own coinage and stelae. He was baptized by [[frumentius]] (consecrated bishop by Athanasius c. 328–335). Under Ezana, Aksum reached its territorial apex: he campaigned successfully against the Beja peoples to the north, against the Noba and Kasu (the surviving Meroitic Nubian state of Kush) c. 350 CE — an event commemorated on his stelae and effectively terminating the long-attested Kushite kingdom that had ruled in the Sudan from the 8th c. BCE.

## Historical evidence
Among the best-attested African monarchs of late antiquity:

- **Ezana's own inscriptions** — six surviving major stelae at Aksum, trilingual or bilingual in Greek, unvocalized Ge'ez, and vocalized Ge'ez. The earliest invoke the South-Arabian deity *Mahrem* (and the pre-Christian Aksumite pantheon Astar, Beher, Meder); the later inscriptions invoke "the Lord of Heaven who has set me as king" and a single Christian God ("Lord of all"). The transition is internally datable.
- **Aksumite coinage** — Ezana's earliest issues bear the South-Arabian-derived crescent-and-disk symbol of the moon-god *Almouqah / Almaqah*; mid-reign issues introduce the cross — placing Aksum among the earliest states to put a Christian cross on its coinage (under Constantine's sons after the Vetranio-Magnentius interval, ~350 CE).
- **Athanasius, *Apol. ad Const.* 31** (357) — Constantius II writes to "Aezanes and Saizanes" demanding Frumentius answer to George of Cappadocia. Saizanes is Ezana's brother (probably co-king or governor).
- **Rufinus *H.E.* 10.9–10** (~402) — narrative account from Aedesius's testimony, naming Ezana's mother and brother.
- **Aksumite material culture** — the giant stelae of Aksum (the largest, the *Great Stele*, ~33 m long, fell in antiquity; the *Obelisk of Aksum*, ~24 m, removed by Mussolini's troops 1937, returned 2005) belong to Ezana's reign or slightly before; they mark royal tombs.

## Works
The Ezana inscriptions (~mid-340s to mid-350s CE) are the primary corpus of his "works." They are critical-edited in Bernand, Drewes, and Schneider 1991 (Paris). Six major texts:

1. RIE 185 (vocalized Ge'ez) — Beja campaign; pre-Christian invocation.
2. RIE 270 (Greek) — Beja campaign.
3. RIE 271 (Greek) — campaign against Noba / Kasu (Meroë).
4. RIE 188/189 (vocalized Ge'ez) — campaign against Noba; *transitional* in religious formula.
5. RIE 190 (vocalized Ge'ez) — Christian-formula throughout; "Lord of all," "Lord of Heaven."
6. RIE 191 (unvocalized Ge'ez) — Christian-formula.

The introduction of *vocalized* Ge'ez script during Ezana's reign — placing vowel-marks on the consonantal South-Semitic skeleton, possibly under Greek-Christian influence — is itself an event in the history of writing in Ethiopia and a paleographic boundary for dating subsequent Ge'ez texts.

## Role in the tradition
For Ethiopian Orthodoxy, Ezana is the *Tewahedo Constantine* — the first Christian king, the political agent through whom the Christianization of Ethiopia was institutionalized. His baptism by Frumentius is the constitutive event of the church-state-people identity. Subsequent Ethiopian royal ideology, codified in the *[[phase-8-008-kebra-nagast]]*, retrojects the Christian kingship backward to Solomon-Menelik and forward through Ezana; Ezana functions in the historical-canonical narrative as the bridge between the legendary Solomonic founding and the historical Christian monarchy.

## Disputes
- **Exact reign-dates.** The Aksumite king list is approximate; conventional dates 320–360 derive from synchronisms with Athanasius and from numismatic stratigraphy (Munro-Hay 1991).
- **Whether Ezana converted personally or only inaugurated state Christianity.** Most scholars accept personal conversion (Rufinus's narrative, the coinage shift, the inscription-formula shift); skeptics argue the inscription-formula transition could reflect pragmatic-pluralist statecraft. Phillipson 2012 treats the personal conversion as the most economical reading.
- **Identity of Saizana.** Some have proposed Saizana / Ella Atsbeha is a distinct royal name borne in a later reign (the conventional *Ella Atsbeha* is identified with [[kaleb-of-aksum]] c. 525); the simpler reading takes Saizana as Ezana's brother and co-ruler as Constantius's letter suggests.
