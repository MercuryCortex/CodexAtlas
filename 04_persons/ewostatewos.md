# Ewostatewos of Sara'e

## Identity
**Ewostatewos** (1273–1352) was the founder of the second great monastic network of Solomonic-era Ethiopia (parallel to and sometimes in conflict with [[takla-haymanot]]'s Debre Libanos network). Born in the northern Ethiopian region of Sara'e, he became a fierce advocate for **Saturday-Sabbath observance** alongside Sunday — a position that put him in long conflict with the Coptic-appointed Abuna and the southern Shewan ecclesiastical establishment, which observed only Sunday in line with mainstream Christian practice. After protracted struggle, Ewostatewos went into voluntary exile, traveling through Egypt, Jerusalem, Cyprus, and ultimately Armenia, where he died in 1352. His followers (the "House of Ewostatewos") maintained the Sabbath-observance position in the northern monasteries (Debre Bizen, Debre Maryam Qoyetsa, and others); the issue eventually reached its institutional resolution under [[zara-yaqob-emperor]] at the 1450 Council of Mitmaq.

## Historical evidence
- **Gadla Ewostatewos** — Ge'ez hagiography, critical-edited by Turaiev 1906 (CSCO).
- **Royal-correspondence and church-council records** — the Ewostatewos-Sabbath controversy and its resolution at Mitmaq 1450 are documented in Ethiopian chronicle literature.
- **Monastic-foundation chronologies** — the spread of Ewostathian monasteries in northern Ethiopia in the 14th–15th centuries is documented archaeologically and through monastery archives.
- **External attestation** — Ewostatewos's travels through Egypt, Cyprus, and Armenia are referenced in Coptic and Armenian sources mentioning an Ethiopian monk in those communities in the early 14th century.

## Works
Not credited with surviving authored texts. His hagiography is post-mortem.

## Role in the tradition
Ewostatewos is the prototype of the *resistant* Ethiopian monastic — challenging the Coptic-appointed metropolitan and the southern establishment in defense of an Old-Testament-derived practice (Saturday-Sabbath, Leviticus 23 / Exodus 20). The Ewostathian-Takla-Haymanot split structured Ethiopian monasticism into two networks for over a century. Modern Ethiopian church practice observes *both* Saturday-Sabbath (*Sanbat*, "Sabbath") and Sunday (*Ihud*, "Lord's Day"), institutionalizing the Ewostathian victory at Mitmaq 1450 within a face-saving formula that retained the universality of Sunday.

The Sabbath-observance issue connects to the broader question of Old-Testament observance in Ethiopian Christianity — circumcision, dietary laws, Levitical priestly purity, the *tabot*. Whether these reflect a deep pre-Christian Jewish substrate (Ullendorff 1968) or medieval Old-Testament emulation (Rodinson 1964) is disputed; the Ewostathian Sabbath emphasis is part of the data on either side.

## Disputes
- **Origin of the Sabbath-observance practice.** Pre-existing in Ethiopian Christianity (Ewostatewos defends it) vs. Ewostathian innovation. Most scholarship sees Ewostatewos as the *systematizer* and *defender*, not the originator, of an older practice.
- **The 1450 Council of Mitmaq.** Whether this was a true ecclesiastical council or a Zar'a Ya'qob-orchestrated political settlement; both are partly true (Taddesse Tamrat 1972).
