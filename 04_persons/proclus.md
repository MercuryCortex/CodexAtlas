# Proclus Lycaeus (Proclus Diadochus)

## Biography

Born Constantinople 412 CE; educated in Lycia (his family origin) and Alexandria, then came to Athens to study first under Plutarch of Athens, then under Syrianus — whom he succeeded as scholarch (*diadochos*, "successor") of the Platonic Academy around 450 CE, a position he held until his death in 485. He is, after [[plotinus]], the most consequential philosopher produced by the Neoplatonic tradition. His epithet *Diadochus* ("the Successor") is the title of the head of the Platonic Academy — a lineage of institutional authority stretching back to Plato himself.

He was a committed pagan in a Christianizing empire. Around the 450s he was briefly exiled from Athens by Christian authorities for his active paganism — Marinus of Neapolis's *Life of Proclus* records this episode; he spent a year in Lycia before returning. This is important context: Proclus was not a nominal pagan but a practicing theurgist. He believed ritual (*theurgy*) — properly executed prayers, sacred objects, and divine names — could invoke the gods and achieve union with the One, not merely intellectual contemplation. This theurgical dimension, inherited from [[iamblichus]], entered Christian mysticism through [[pseudo-dionysius-the-areopagite]] as the sacramental hierarchy.

## Philosophy: The Systematizer of Neoplatonism

Proclus took [[plotinus]]'s visionary Neoplatonism and formalized it into rigorous logical chains. His *Elements of Theology* presents 211 numbered propositions with demonstrations in the style of Euclid's *Elements* — it is one of the most logically disciplined philosophical texts ever written. The core architecture: the **One** (beyond being, beyond thought, beyond all predication) overflows into **Intellect** (*Nous*) — the realm of pure thinking and pure being, where the Platonic forms live. Intellect overflows into **Soul** — the level of time and life and animation. Soul overflows into **Matter**. But crucially, each level participates in a triple dialectic of *mone* (remaining in source), *proodos* (procession/emanation outward), and *epistrophe* (return/conversion back to the source). Everything that proceeds from its cause remains in it, proceeds from it, and returns to it — simultaneously. The universe is always in the act of overflowing and always in the act of returning.

His *Platonic Theology* synthesized the entire Platonic corpus into a unified theological system. The *Parmenides* commentary reads the eight hypotheses of Plato's dialogue as a complete deduction of hypostatic structure from the One downward — it is the closest thing to a Proclean master-text.

## Transmission: The Hidden Engine of Medieval Thought — MASSIVE WIN

Proclus's works were directly plagiarized by [[pseudo-dionysius-the-areopagite]]: whole passages of the *Elements of Theology* appear nearly verbatim in Pseudo-Dionysius's *Divine Names*. Thomas Taylor documented this in 1792; Hugo Koch and Joseph Stiglmayr independently demonstrated it in 1895; E.R. Dodds confirmed and extended the analysis in his 1963 critical edition. The borrowings include not just ideas but phrasing — this is not influence but disguised quotation.

The consequence of that single act of philosophical plagiarism was incalculable:

- **→ [[pseudo-dionysius-the-areopagite]]**: Proclus's *mone-proodos-epistrophe* dialectic becomes the three-stage mystical ascent (purification, illumination, union); his apophatic logic of the One-beyond-being becomes Christian apophatic theology; his hierarchical mediation becomes the nine angelic orders.
- **→ [[john-scotus-eriugena]]** (~860 CE): translates Pseudo-Dionysius into Latin; Proclus becomes the hidden engine of Carolingian philosophy.
- **→ [[thomas-aquinas]]**: ~1,700 citations of "Dionysius" in the *Summa Theologiae*; Proclus silently shapes the most important systematic theology of medieval Christianity. When Aquinas (~1268) correctly identified the *Liber de Causis* as Proclus (using Moerbeke's fresh translation of the *Elements*), he realized the Arabic compilation he'd been reading as Aristotle was actually Proclus — but he never made the connection to "Dionysius."
- **→ [[meister-eckhart]]**: the *Gottheit* (Godhead) beyond God — "the desert of the Godhead," pure apophatic silence beyond all predication — is Proclus's One in Christian mystical dress.
- **→ [[dante-alighieri]]**: Beatrice explains the celestial spheres using the Pseudo-Dionysian hierarchy (see [[beatrice-portinari]]). Proclus is the unacknowledged upstream.
- **→ Milton**: the angelic hierarchy in *Paradise Lost* comes from this chain. See [[theme-rebel-against-the-divine]].

The *Liber de Causis* (*Book of Causes*) — a 9th-century Arabic compilation of Proclus, translated into Latin by Gerard of Cremona ~1167 — circulated in the medieval West as Aristotle's work for 200 years. A 5th-century Athenian pagan's philosophy was the most-read "Aristotle" of the early medieval period. Aquinas's 1268 correction did not diminish the influence — it only clarified attribution.

## Cross-Tradition Position

**Proclus henads → Kabbalistic sefirot (the Scholem thesis).** Proclus's *henads* — divine unities mediating between the transcendent One and lower hypostatic levels — are structurally parallel to the Kabbalistic sefirot. The channel: ibn Gabirol's *Fons Vitae* (~1050 CE, Andalusia) is a Neoplatonic-Jewish synthesis that entered Provençal Jewish intellectual culture and influenced Isaac the Blind's Kabbalistic circle (~1200). Scholem (1962) argued this is the documented transmission of the Proclean emanation scheme into Kabbalah. The Kabbalistic innovation: the abstract henads become mythological, personified, gendered divine figures — the emotionalization of the Proclean scheme. See [[sefirot-tree-of-life]], [[tradition-kabbalah]].

**Proclus → Islamic Neoplatonism.** The *Theology of Aristotle* — widely read in Islamic philosophy — is actually excerpts from [[plotinus]]'s *Enneads* IV–VI processed through the Proclean tradition. Through this channel Proclean Neoplatonism shaped Ibn Sina's *necessary being*, al-Farabi's emanation cosmology, and (via [[philo-of-alexandria]]'s parallel channel) eventually [[maimonides]]'s negative theology in the *Guide for the Perplexed*. A 5th-century Athenian pagan is a hidden substructure of scholastic Christianity, medieval Islamic philosophy, and Jewish rationalist theology simultaneously.

**Proclus → *Liber de Causis* → scholastic synthesis.** The Arabic *Liber de Causis* (9th c. Baghdad) excerpts and redacts the *Elements of Theology*. Translated into Latin ~1185, widely read as Aristotelian, it constituted a primary source for medieval cosmology until Aquinas corrected the attribution in 1268. Proclus's emanation scheme is structurally present in the metaphysics of Aquinas, Duns Scotus, and the entire medieval *esse* tradition. See [[tradition-neoplatonism]].
