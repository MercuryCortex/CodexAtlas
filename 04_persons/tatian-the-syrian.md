# Tatian the Syrian

## Identity
A Christian apologist of Mesopotamian (Assyrian/Syrian) origin who studied under [[justin-martyr]] at Rome in the 150s and 160s, and who after Justin's martyrdom in 165 returned east and developed a more radical, ascetic, and (per Irenaeus) "Encratite" Christianity that rejected marriage, meat, and wine. He is the author of two enormously important works: the **Address to the Greeks** (an extant apologetic) and the **Diatessaron** (a harmonization of the four Gospels into a single continuous narrative — the foundational Gospel text of Syriac Christianity for several centuries).

## Historical evidence
Both works are attested in their own day. The *Address* survives in Greek; the *Diatessaron*, while not surviving in any complete original-language exemplar, is reconstructible from a 4th-c. Syriac commentary by [[ephrem-the-syrian|Ephrem]] (Chester Beatty Codex), the 14th-c. Arabic translation of an earlier Syriac version, two Latin recensions (the Codex Fuldensis being most important), and Persian, Old Dutch, and Old High German recensions. Petersen 1994 is the standard reconstruction.

## Works
- **Address to the Greeks** (~+165/+175): a Christian apologetic to Hellenistic culture, sharply more aggressive than Justin's; argues for the antiquity and superiority of Hebrew/Christian wisdom over Greek philosophy and mythology.
- **Diatessaron** (~+170): merges the four canonical Gospels (and possibly some non-canonical material) into a continuous Jesus-narrative; the canonical text of Syriac Christianity for ~200 years (until Rabbula imposed the separated four Gospels in the 5th c.).

## Role in the tradition
- **Syriac Christianity's foundational Gospel text.** Until the 5th-c. transition to the separated four-gospel canon (Peshitta), the Diatessaron *was* the Gospel in the Syriac church.
- **Encratite movement**: Tatian as founder per the heresiologists. Whether he was a Gnostic-influenced thinker, a strict moralist, or simply a continuation of Justin's apologetic with greater asceticism is debated.
- **Apologetic tradition**: a key figure in the transition from Justin's accommodating Platonism to a more aggressively counter-Hellenistic Christianity.

## Disputes
- **Diatessaron's exact text.** Reconstruction is ongoing; the Dura-Europos parchment (3rd c. — a Greek fragment) was once considered Diatessaronic but is now contested.
- **Encratite origins.** Whether Tatian founded the movement or merely formalized an existing current is contested.
- **Whether the Diatessaron used a fifth, non-canonical source** (the *Gospel of the Hebrews* or similar). Some Syriac variants suggest possible non-canonical influence.
