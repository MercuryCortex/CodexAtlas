# Abraham Abulafia

## Identity
Avraham ben Shmuel Abulafia (1240–1291?) was the founder of the *Prophetic Kabbalah* — a stream of Kabbalistic mysticism focused on letter-permutation meditation (*ḥokhmat ha-tzeruf*) leading to ecstatic prophetic states, rather than on the theosophic sefirotic structure of the *Bahir-Zohar* tradition. Born in Zaragoza (Aragon); traveled widely (Israel, Greece, Italy, Sicily); famously attempted to visit Pope Nicholas III in 1280 to "discuss the truth of Jewish belief" — was scheduled for execution but the Pope died before the audience took place; spent later years in Sicily. Made messianic claims about himself (with mixed and disputed receptions); his works combine intense methodological detail about meditative practice with profound philosophical-theological learning (he was deeply read in Maimonides).

## Historical evidence
Substantial autobiographical material in his own works; persecution and ban by R. Shlomo ben Adret (Rashba, the leading Spanish halakhic authority of his generation) is well documented; date and place of death uncertain.

## Works
~26 surviving treatises in Hebrew. Principal: *Sefer ha-Ot* (autobiographical visionary work); *Or ha-Sekhel* (Light of the Intellect, on meditation method); *Sefer Hayyei ha-Olam ha-Ba*; commentaries on Maimonides' *Guide* and on the *Sefer Yetzirah*.

## Role in the tradition
The Prophetic Kabbalah stream is the principal alternative to Zoharic theosophic Kabbalah; Abulafia is its founder. His method (combinatorial letter-permutation meditation with breath-coordination and head-movement) bears structural similarity to other contemplative-meditation systems and has been compared to Sufi *dhikr* and Yoga. The Lurianic stream of Phase 6 partly recovers Abulafian elements; modern Idel-school scholarship has substantially elevated Abulafia's importance against the Scholem-tradition's foregrounding of Zoharic Kabbalah.
