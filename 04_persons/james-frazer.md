# Sir James George Frazer

## Identity
Scottish classicist and social anthropologist (1854–1941); fellow of Trinity College, Cambridge from 1879 until his death. Author of *The Golden Bough* (1890 → 12 vols 1906–1915 → 1-vol abridgment 1922), the most-circulated work of late-Victorian and early-20th-century comparative religion. Frazer never did fieldwork; he was an "armchair anthropologist" drawing on missionary, ethnographer, and colonial-administrator correspondence, classical-philological erudition, and folklore collections.

## Historical evidence
Extensively documented through Trinity College records, his correspondence (Trinity College Library archives), and Robert Ackerman's 1987 standard biography.

## Works
See texts-authored. *The Golden Bough* is the central work; the *Folk-Lore in the Old Testament* (1918) extended the comparative method to biblical material; the Pausanias and Ovid commentaries were major classical contributions.

## Role in the tradition
The most-read comparative-religion writer of his generation; popularized the "dying-and-rising god" category whose subsequent collapse under J.Z. Smith's critique is a foundational case in 20th-century method-criticism. His literary influence (Eliot, Joyce, Yeats, Lawrence, Graves, Campbell) outran his anthropological influence, which had been substantially superseded by Malinowski's fieldwork-based functionalism by Frazer's death.

## Disputes
- **Methodological adequacy of armchair comparativism.** Wittgenstein's *Remarks on Frazer's Golden Bough* (composed 1931, published 1967) is the canonical philosophical critique; Malinowski's functionalist program superseded Frazer's evolutionism methodologically.
- **Dying-rising god category.** See [[phase-7-007-golden-bough]] disputes section.
