# Mahāvīra

## Identity
Mahāvīra ("Great Hero"), the personal name Vardhamāna, is recognized in Jain tradition as the 24th (and last) *Tīrthaṅkara* (ford-maker, liberator) of the present cosmic age — and historically as the most important reformer/systematizer of the Jain tradition. A contemporary and rival of the Buddha, also from the kṣatriya class, also from the eastern Gangetic plain śramaṇa milieu, he taught a strict ascetic path emphasizing radical non-violence (*ahiṃsā*), non-possession (*aparigraha*), truthfulness, celibacy, and non-attachment. His attainment of *kevala-jñāna* (omniscience / liberation) and his subsequent teaching established the Jain monastic community.

## Historical evidence
Independent attestation in the Buddhist Pali canon (under the name Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta, "the Naked One of the Jñāta clan") — the Buddhists name him as a contemporary rival teacher with specific doctrines, which substantially confirms his historicity from outside Jain tradition. The Jain Āgamas preserve substantial biographical material (Kalpa Sūtra, Ācārāṅga). Aśokan period records and the Hāthīgumphā inscription of Khāravela (~-150) attest established Jain communities, retrospectively confirming the foundation.

## Works
Mahāvīra composed no text. His teachings were transmitted orally by his disciples (*gaṇadharas*); the surviving Jain Āgamas were finally redacted at the Vallabhī council in the 5th c. CE, with substantial later accretion. The Digambara branch rejects the Āgamic canon as unfaithful to the original teaching.

## Role in the tradition
Founder-figure (though tradition recognizes him as the 24th in a series, beginning with the legendary Ṛṣabhanātha). Exemplar of complete renunciation. The Jain ascetic path he established remains in continuous practice ~2,500 years later — Jainism is the oldest continuously practiced śramaṇa tradition (along with Buddhism).

## Disputes
- **Date.** Traditional Jain dating: -599 to -527. Some current scholarship favors a slightly later range (~-540 to -468), parallel to the redating of the Buddha. We carry the traditional dating in YAML; both are within Phase 2.
- **Founder vs. reformer.** Jain tradition recognizes a series of earlier Tīrthaṅkaras, most legendary; Pārśvanātha (the 23rd, traditional dates -872 to -772) may have a historical kernel and may represent an earlier Jain-like community. Mahāvīra is then a major reformer of an existing tradition, not its sole founder.
