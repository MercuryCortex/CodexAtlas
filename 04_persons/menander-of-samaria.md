# Menander of Samaria

## Identity
Samaritan religious teacher of the second half of the 1st century, named by Justin Martyr as the principal disciple and successor of [[simon-magus]], and as the founder of a school active in Antioch that taught its initiates would not die. The heresiologists place him at the head of a succession (Simon → Menander → [[saturninus]] / [[basilides]]) that they use to construct the "genealogy of all heresies."

## Historical evidence
- **Justin Martyr** (*1 Apology* 26, ~155 CE) is the strongest source. Justin is himself a Samaritan, writing within roughly two generations of Menander; he places Menander at Antioch and reports his magical and immortality-claiming teaching. The geographical specificity (Antioch) and the relative chronological proximity weigh in favor of historical Menander.
- **Irenaeus** (*Adv. Haer.* 1.23.5) depends on Justin and adds: Menander was Simon's disciple, taught his own baptism conferred immortality and incorruption, identified himself with the same *Standing Power* Simon had claimed.
- **Tertullian** (*De anima* 50; *De praescriptione* 46) and **Hippolytus** (*Ref.* 7.28) depend on Justin and Irenaeus.
- **Eusebius** (*HE* 3.26) repeats the heresiological succession.

What is reliably attested: Menander existed; he was a Samaritan magico-religious teacher; he was active at Antioch in the late 1st c.; his school survived into the 2nd c.; he was claimed as predecessor by Saturninus (per Irenaeus). What is less reliable: the precise content of his doctrine, the historicity of the *baptism-conferring-immortality* claim (a typical heresiological topos), and the genealogical chain through to all 2nd-c. Gnosticism.

## Works
No writings survive. The heresiological reports do not preserve any verbatim quotations.

## Role in the tradition
- **Bridge from Simonian Samaria to Antiochene proto-Gnosticism.** Menander's relocation to Antioch is one of the earliest documented movements of religiously-syncretic Samaritan-Hellenistic teaching into a major eastern Mediterranean Christian center. Antioch is, in the heresiological geography, where Simon's Samaritan school becomes Saturninus's Christian Gnosticism.
- **The "succession" topos.** Irenaeus's chain Simon → Menander → Saturninus / Basilides is partly a polemical construct (genealogy as heresiological strategy), partly a real master-disciple network. King 2003 argues the heresiological succession is mostly polemical organization; Pearson 1990 and Beyschlag 1974 hold that the basic chain has historical core, even if details are stylized.
- **Immortality-by-initiation.** The doctrine that Menander's baptism conferred immortality structurally anticipates the Sethian and Valentinian theologies of *gnōsis*-as-salvation; whether Menander really taught this or it is a heresiological caricature is contested.

## Connections — substrate and influence
- **Samaritan substrate.** The Sebuaean / Dosithean Samaritan currents (documented in Josephus, *Ant.* 18; in the *Memar Marqah*) provide the religious environment for the Simon-Menander succession. Samaria preserved an independent priestly-prophetic tradition that did not pass through Second-Temple Jerusalem, making it a distinctive substrate for syncretic religion. Bowman 1975 *The Samaritan Problem*.
- **Hellenistic-Egyptian magical and immortalization substrate.** Menander's reported baptismal-immortality claim parallels the Egyptian-Hermetic doctrine of *immortalization through gnōsis* (compare *Poimandres* 26; *Corpus Hermeticum* X). The Mediterranean-wide late-Hellenistic substrate of *initiation-conferring-divinization* is the broader environment Menander shares with the Mithraic mysteries, the Eleusinian mysteries, and Egyptian-Hermetic initiatic theology. Pearson 1990.
- **Antiochene Christianity.** Antioch was already, by Menander's lifetime, one of the principal centers of early Christian theological diversification (compare Ignatius's letters c. 110; the Antiochene Christian community had been founded c. 35–40 per Acts 11). Menander's school competed locally with Antiochene Christianity; the long-term result is the Antiochene proto-Gnostic milieu in which Saturninus and (per one tradition) Basilides develop.

## Disputes
- **Historicity of the doctrine.** That Menander existed is well-attested; that he taught precisely what Irenaeus says he taught is less secure. King 2003 reads the heresiological reports as polemical constructions; Beyschlag 1974 and Pearson 1990 hold that the broad theological-magical content is reliable.
- **Position in the Simonian succession.** Whether Menander is genuinely Simon's disciple or is retrospectively placed in Simon's line by the heresiologists is debated.
- **The Antiochene location.** Justin's testimony (Antioch) is strong but not unanimously accepted; some later heresiological reports place Menander elsewhere.
