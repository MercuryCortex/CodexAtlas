# Pope Urban II

## Identity
Born Odo / Otto of Lagery (Châtillon-sur-Marne, Champagne), Cluniac monk and prior, cardinal-bishop of Ostia, elected pope March 1088 in the midst of the Investiture Controversy and the Roman-Wibert (Clement III) antipope schism. Reigned 1088–1099. Continued the Gregorian Reform program of Gregory VII. Convoked the [[event-council-of-clermont-1095|Council of Clermont]] (November 1095) and, in its closing outdoor sermon, called Latin Christendom to a Holy-Land expedition — launching the [[event-first-crusade-1095-1099|First Crusade]] and effectively inventing the concept of crusading as a religiously-meritorious armed pilgrimage with attached plenary indulgence. Died 29 July 1099, two weeks after Jerusalem fell to the Crusaders, before news reached him.

## Historical evidence
Strong. Papal registers (preserved through the *Collectio Britannica* and other channels — Somerville-Kuttner 1996 is the standard analytical-edition study), chronicle attestation across the Latin and Greek worlds, and five later accounts of the Clermont sermon (none verbatim, all reconstructions written 1100–1110, each reflecting its author's perspective).

## Works
- **Five accounts of the Clermont sermon** — Fulcher of Chartres, Robert the Monk, Baldric of Dol, Guibert of Nogent, the anonymous of Reims. Each represents an attempt by an early-12th-century cleric to reconstruct what Urban *said* (and *meant*) on 27 November 1095. Common elements: liberation of the Holy Land, aid to Eastern Christians, plenary indulgence for participation, framing of crusading as armed pilgrimage. Divergent elements: emphasis on Jerusalem (some accounts) vs. emphasis on Byzantine aid (others), tone of anti-Muslim polemic, specific atrocity claims.
- **Papal bulls and letters** — primarily disciplinary, jurisdictional, and church-reform.

## Role in the tradition
- **Inventor of medieval crusading as a theological-juridical concept.** The plenary indulgence as motivation for armed pilgrimage is an Urbanian innovation.
- **Continuer of the Gregorian reform.** Urban's reign extends the Gregorian program (papal supremacy, clerical celibacy, anti-simony, anti-investiture) and the eventual papal victory in the Investiture Controversy.
- **Architect of post-1054 Latin–Byzantine *rapprochement*.** Urban's correspondence with Alexios I Komnenos (the Piacenza ambassadors of March 1095 are the immediate trigger for the Clermont call) was the first major attempt to repair the 1054 schism through joint military project. The project's actual *result* (after the 1204 Fourth Crusade) was the deepening of the schism.

## Disputes
- **What did Urban actually say at Clermont?** Genuinely uncertain. Riley-Smith 1986, Cole 1991, Asbridge 2004 reconstruct the core of the message; the rhetorical specifics are largely lost.
- **Was the Crusade primarily defensive (Byzantine aid) or offensive (Jerusalem liberation)?** Older scholarship: defensive shifting to offensive. Riley-Smith 1986: Jerusalem central from start.
