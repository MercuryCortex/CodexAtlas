# Martha of Bethany

## Identity
Sister of [[mary-of-bethany]] and [[lazarus-of-bethany]]; hostess of Jesus in Lk 10 (the active sister vs. contemplative Mary) and the speaker of the **Johannine confessional statement** at Jn 11:27: "Yes, Lord, I believe that you are the Christ, the Son of God, the one coming into the world." This is the Fourth Gospel's *equivalent* of Peter's Caesarea Philippi confession — placed on a woman's lips.

## Historical evidence
Multiply attested across Luke and John. The Lukan and Johannine portraits diverge in tone (Lukan Martha is faintly criticized for being "distracted with much serving"; Johannine Martha is the theologically articulate confessor) but agree on the basic familial and geographical setting.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
The "active vs. contemplative" reading of the Mary-Martha pair (Mary contemplative, Martha active) became foundational to medieval Western spirituality (Cassian, Aquinas, Eckhart). Feminist scholarship has recovered her Johannine confession as a key piece of evidence for women's theological authority in early Christianity.

## Disputes
- **Date of the Lazarus episode.** Johannine only; absent from Synoptics, which is striking given its dramatic significance.
- **Conflation with the anointing-woman.** Some traditions conflate the anointing of Bethany with Martha's hospitality.
