# Bernard of Clairvaux

## Identity
A French Cistercian abbot (1090–1153), founder of Clairvaux Abbey (1115), the most influential Latin churchman of his generation, principal voice of the reformist Cistercian movement, theological architect of the [[tradition-knights-templar|Knights Templar]] through the Latin Rule and *De Laude Novae Militiae*, and the principal preacher of the [[event-second-crusade-1147-1149|Second Crusade]]. A figure of immense political weight: settled the papal schism of 1130 in favor of Innocent II against Anacletus II, secured the condemnation of Peter Abelard at Sens (1141), advised five popes, and saw his pupil Eugenius III elected to the papacy (1145).

## Historical evidence
Massive — Bernard is among the best-documented twelfth-century individuals. His own *opera omnia* fill seven volumes in the Leclercq-Rochais critical edition (Sancti Bernardi Opera, 1957–1977). The *Vita Prima* hagiography (William of Saint-Thierry, Arnaud of Bonneval, Geoffrey of Auxerre, completed before 1180) is the principal contemporary biography, with the usual hagiographic caveats. Independent chronicle attestation across the medieval Latin West.

## Works
- ***De Laude Novae Militiae*** (~1129–1136) — [[phase-5-037-bernard-de-laude-novae-militiae]]. Bernard's manifesto for the new military-religious vocation; theological architecture of medieval crusading.
- ***De Diligendo Deo*** (*On Loving God*) — the four-stage soul-ascent treatise (love of self for self → love of God for self's sake → love of God for God's sake → love of self for God's sake).
- ***De Consideratione*** — the five-book directive to Pope Eugenius III on the papal office; major early text in the *speculum* (mirror-of-princes) genre as applied to the papacy. Notable for its anguished post-1149 reflection on the failure of the Second Crusade.
- ***Sermones super Cantica Canticorum*** — 86 sermons on the Song of Songs, the foundational text of medieval Latin bridal-soul mysticism. The work was interrupted by Bernard's death (he had reached Cant. 3:1).
- ***De Gradibus Humilitatis*** — twelve-step humility analysis.

## Role in the tradition
- **Cistercian movement.** Bernard's personality and example are the principal proximate cause of the Cistercian expansion from a single house (Cîteaux, founded 1098) to ~340 abbeys at his death. The Cistercian-Bernardine austere reform shapes Latin monasticism for two centuries.
- **Architect of crusading.** Through *De Laude Novae Militiae* and the [[event-council-of-troyes-1129|Council of Troyes Rule]], Bernard is the *theological* founder of the [[tradition-knights-templar|Templars]]. Through his 1146-1147 preaching tour, he is the principal preacher of the Second Crusade.
- **Foundational bridal mystic.** The *Sermones super Cantica* establish the medieval Latin tradition of the soul-as-bride / Christ-as-bridegroom mysticism developed subsequently by William of Saint-Thierry, the Victorines, Bonaventure, the Rhineland mystics, John of the Cross, and Teresa of Ávila. McGinn's *Presence of God* vol. 2 ch. 6 is the standard treatment.
- **Anti-heretical and anti-philosophical campaigner.** Bernard's targeting of Peter Abelard (Sens, 1141) and Gilbert of Poitiers (Reims, 1148) defined the boundaries of acceptable twelfth-century Latin theology — limiting Abelard's nominalist-style logic and Gilbert's Trinitarian distinctions.
- **Marian devotion.** Bernard's Marian sermons inaugurate a wave of intensified Latin Marian piety — the *Salve Regina* tradition, the Cistercian dedication of every abbey to the Virgin Mary.

## Connections — what threads through Bernard

- **→ [[hugues-de-payens]]** *direct-collaboration*: Hugues was Bernard's cousin or close associate (the kinship is documented but disputed in detail); Bernard's drafting of the Templar Rule at Troyes (1129) is the formative collaborative moment.
- **→ [[tradition-knights-templar]]** *founder*: the theological architecture is Bernardine.
- **→ [[event-second-crusade-1147-1149]]** *principal-preacher*: Bernard's preaching tour 1146-1147 (Vézelay, Speyer, Rhineland, Flanders) generates the crusade. The failure scars him.
- **→ Jewish-Christian relations in the Rhineland.** During the Second Crusade preaching Bernard *intervened* against Radulphus (the Cistercian rabble-rouser who was preaching crusade-cum-pogrom in the Rhineland) and against anti-Jewish massacre. The Hebrew chronicler Ephraim of Bonn explicitly credits Bernard with sparing Jewish lives. A complicated Bernardine record on Jews: theologically supersessionist, politically protective.
- **→ [[meister-eckhart]] / Rhineland mystics** *indirect-ancestor*: Bernardine bridal mysticism is one of the major streams feeding the Rhineland mystical tradition. Eckhart is not Bernardine in his apophatic Godhead-theology but Bernardine in his eros-language.
- **→ Marian devotion as a substrate for the [[isis-hellenistic|Isis-Mary]] continuity question.** Bernardine Marianism is the institutional Cistercian-era intensification of Latin Marian piety; whether the iconography is descending from Hellenistic Isis-Lactans (Witt 1971, Frankfurter 1998) is the older 0–600 CE question — but the *intensity* of Latin Marian devotion that Bernardine Cistercian piety institutionalizes is what made the iconographic continuity *possible at scale* in the medieval West.

## Disputes
- **Did Bernard write the Templar Rule himself or merely preside?** Bredero 1996, Barber 1994 ch. 1: Bernard did not draft every article but is the principal shaping voice. The Cistercian formal organization of the Rule's text is distinctive.
- **Was the Sens condemnation of Abelard procedurally fair?** Mews 2002, Clanchy 1997: probably not; Bernard pre-arranged the council's verdict.
- **Was Bernard *responsible* for the failure of the Second Crusade?** Bernard himself partly accepted responsibility in *De Consideratione* II.1. Phillips 2007 ch. 10 distributes the responsibility more broadly across strategic miscalculation.
