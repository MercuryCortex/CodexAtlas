# Madhva

## Identity
Madhvācārya / Ānandatīrtha (1238–1317) was the founder of the Dvaita (Dualist) Vedānta school and one of the three great medieval Vedāntic commentators (alongside Śaṅkara and Ramanuja). Born on the South Kanara coast of Karnataka; took saṃnyāsa young; debated his way to fame across South India; founded the Udupi Krishna temple (still the principal Mādhva pilgrimage center) and the eight Udupi *maṭhas*. He claimed to be the third incarnation of Vāyu — after Hanumān in the Rāmāyaṇa age and Bhīma in the Mahābhārata age.

## Works
37 treatises collected as the *Sarvamūla* corpus. Principal: *Brahmasūtra-Bhāṣya*; *Anuvyākhyāna* (expanded restatement); *Mahābhārata-Tātparya-Nirṇaya*; *Bhāgavata-Tātparya-Nirṇaya*; *Gītā-Bhāṣya*; many shorter philosophical and exegetical works.

## Role in the tradition
Founder of Dvaita Vedānta and architect of strict theistic dualism with the unique doctrine of three eternal soul-classes (including eternal damnation, a doctrine without close Indian parallel but with structural similarity to Calvinist double predestination). The Mādhva school's intellectual peak came under Vyāsatīrtha at Vijayanagara (~1500); the Haridāsa devotional poets brought Mādhva theology into Kannada-language popular religion.

## Disputes
Madhva's citations from allegedly "lost Vedic" texts have been philologically critiqued by Mesquita 2000 — whether he had access to lost recensions or was creatively constructing authorities.
