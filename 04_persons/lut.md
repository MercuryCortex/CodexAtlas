# Lūṭ

Quranic prophet who is a nephew of Abraham ([[abraham-patriarch]]), sent to the cities of Sodom (*Saddūm*). The Quran treats Lūṭ as a prophet-messenger in the same structural role as Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, and Shuʿayb — one sent to a people who rejected him and were consequently destroyed (Surah 7:80–84, 11:77–83, 15:61–77, 26:160–175, 27:54–58, 29:28–35, 54:33–39).

## Quranic profile

The Quranic Lūṭ narrative parallels Genesis 18–19 (the destruction of Sodom) but with significant differences: Lūṭ is explicitly a prophet with a prophetic mission, not merely Abraham's righteous nephew. The angel visitors come to him as divinely sent agents; the destruction is framed as divine punishment for a people who rejected God's messenger and committed gross wickedness (*fāḥisha*). The Quran's account mentions Lūṭ's wife as one who remained behind and was destroyed (Surah 11:81), consistent with Genesis 19:26 (Lot's wife turned to a pillar of salt), though the Quran gives no pillared-salt detail.

Lūṭ appears in the Quran's typological chain of rejected prophets in Surah 11 (alongside Nūḥ, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Shuʿayb, and Mūsā), making the surah a comprehensive typology of prophetic rejection-and-punishment.
