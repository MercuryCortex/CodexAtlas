# Onesimus

## Identity
A enslaved person belonging to Philemon (a Christian of Colossae or Laodicea), the subject of Paul's shortest extant undisputed letter. The letter to Philemon — composed during a Pauline imprisonment, likely at Rome ~+60 or possibly Ephesus earlier — pleads (without commanding) that Philemon receive Onesimus back "no longer as a slave but as more than a slave, a beloved brother" (Phlm 16).

## Historical evidence
Documented in Paul's letter (Phlm) and again in Col 4:9 ("Onesimus, our faithful and beloved brother, who is one of yourselves" — sending him back with Tychicus to Colossae). The historical Onesimus's specific situation has been debated: a runaway slave is the traditional reading; an enslaved person seeking arbitration with his owner via Paul (per Phlm scholarship since the 1990s) is now a strong alternative.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
**Ignatius's bishop of Ephesus**: John Knox (1959) proposed that the **Onesimus mentioned by Ignatius** as bishop of Ephesus ~+110 is the same person as the Pauline enslaved-person-now-brother. The chronology fits (Onesimus would be ~80) and the identification has been influential, though it cannot be proved. If correct, it would make Onesimus a singular early-Christian success story.

**Slavery debates**: Phlm has been a flashpoint of Christian abolitionist and pro-slavery debate for centuries — pro-slavery readings emphasizing that Paul did not *demand* manumission; abolitionist readings emphasizing the "no longer as a slave" undermining of the institution.

## Disputes
- **Runaway slave vs. arbitration-seeking.** Recent scholarship has questioned the runaway-slave reading.
- **Onesimus = bishop of Ephesus.** Knox's hypothesis: plausible but unprovable.
- **Did Paul ask for manumission or just kind treatment?** Phlm's diplomatic indirection makes this irreducibly ambiguous.
