# Mary of Bethany

## Identity
Sister of [[martha-of-bethany]] and [[lazarus-of-bethany]], one of the women named in the Gospels as part of Jesus's circle outside the Twelve. The Lukan portrait (Lk 10:38–42) shows her sitting *at Jesus's feet to learn* — the technical Jewish formula for a disciple receiving rabbinic instruction — making her an explicit female disciple. The Johannine portrait (Jn 11–12) places her at the center of the Lazarus-raising and the anointing-at-Bethany.

## Historical evidence
Multiply attested (Luke and John independently). The Synoptic anointing scene (Mk 14:3–9, Mt 26:6–13) is anonymous, leading Western tradition to conflate the Bethany anointing with the *Lukan sinful woman* (Lk 7:36–50) and with [[mary-magdalene]] — the same conflation that produced the medieval "Magdalene = prostitute" tradition. Modern critical scholarship treats the three women (Mary of Bethany, Mary Magdalene, the Lukan sinful woman) as distinct.

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
Western medieval tradition merged her into the composite Magdalene figure (so designated by Gregory the Great's homilies, 591 — see [[mary-magdalene]]). Modern critical and Greek/Eastern tradition retain her distinct identity. Feminist scholarship (Schüssler Fiorenza) reads her sitting-at-Jesus's-feet posture as authoritative claim to discipleship status.

## Disputes
- **Identity with the Anointing Woman of Mark/Matthew.** Plausible (same location, same act) but not made by the Synoptics.
- **Conflation with Mary Magdalene.** Gregorian Western tradition merged them; modern scholarship separates them.
