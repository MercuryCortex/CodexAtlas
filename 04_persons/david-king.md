# David

## Identity
Second king of the United Monarchy of Israel/Judah (~1010–970 BCE per traditional dating); founder of the Davidic dynasty seated in Jerusalem; the figure on whom messianic expectation — and the entire Christian Christology of Jesus as *huios Dauid* — is built. The biblical David is a composite: warrior-shepherd elevated above Saul (1 Samuel 16–31), king of Judah and then all Israel (2 Samuel 1–10), repentant adulterer of the Bathsheba-Uriah affair (2 Samuel 11–12), patriarch undone by his sons' rebellions (2 Samuel 13–20; 1 Kings 1–2), traditional author of the Psalter.

## Historical evidence
- **Tel Dan Stele** (Aramaic, c. -841/-840 BCE, Biran & Naveh 1993) is the earliest extrabiblical attestation of a *bytdwd* ("House of David"). Strong evidence that the Davidic dynasty was historically real by the 9th-c. BCE — though it doesn't necessarily verify the biblical narrative's details.
- **Mesha Stele** (Moabite, c. -840 BCE) probably contains a parallel reference (line 31; Lemaire 1994 reading).
- No contemporary 10th-c. BCE inscription names David himself; the historicity of the *figure* is highly debated (Finkelstein vs. Mazar; minimalist vs. maximalist Jerusalem archaeology).

## Works
- Psalter attribution: 73 of 150 Masoretic Psalms carry *l'David* superscriptions; these are pseudonymous in the strict critical sense — David is not the historical author of most or all of them, but the attribution itself becomes scripturally constitutive.

## Role in the tradition
David is the load-bearing Old-Testament figure for New-Testament Christology. The Matthean genealogy (Mt 1:1–17) opens with "Jesus Christ, son of David, son of Abraham." Paul (Rom 1:3) names Jesus as "descended from David according to the flesh." Peter's Pentecost sermon (Acts 2:25–36) reads Psalm 16 as Davidic prophecy of Jesus's resurrection. The "Son of David" title is densely deployed in Mark and Matthew. In Islamic tradition (Quran 4:163; 17:55), Dawud is a prophet who received the *Zabur* (Psalter).
