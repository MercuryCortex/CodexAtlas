# Andrew the Apostle

## Identity
Andrew, brother of Simon Peter, Galilean fisherman from Bethsaida, one of the original twelve disciples of Jesus and (in the Johannine tradition) a former disciple of [[john-the-baptist]] before transferring his allegiance to Jesus. The epithet *Protokletos* ("First-Called") is grounded in Jn 1:35–42, where Andrew hears the Baptist's testimony and then brings his brother Simon to Jesus.

## Historical evidence
Multiply attested in all four canonical Gospels and Acts. His historicity as a disciple of Jesus is not seriously disputed. Everything later — the missions to Scythia (Eusebius), to Byzantium/Constantinople (later Byzantine tradition), to Patras and crucifixion on the *crux decussata* (X-cross, late-medieval iconography) — is legendary accretion. The *Acts of Andrew* (~+200), known to Eusebius and Epiphanius and cited by the Manichaeans, presents Andrew as a wandering thaumaturge with strongly encratite (sexually renunciatory) teaching.

## Works
None genuine. The *Acts of Andrew* (encratite, possibly with Gnostic-leaning currents) is the principal apocryphon. A fragmentary *Gospel of Andrew* is listed in the 6th-century Gelasian Decree as condemned.

## Role in the tradition
Patron of Constantinople (translation of relics in 357 grounds the see's apostolic claim — important for Constantinople's parity-with-Rome arguments), Scotland (legendary), Russia (legendary, tied to the Scythia mission), Greece (Patras martyrdom site). In Byzantine ecclesiology, Andrew-as-*Protokletos* is mobilized to counter Petrine-primacy claims of Rome.

## Disputes
- **Historicity beyond the Gospel period.** No early independent attestation of his post-resurrection missions; the Scythia tradition appears in Eusebius (~+325) and may be earlier oral but is unverifiable.
- **Date and manner of martyrdom.** The X-cross is a late-medieval visual convention; earlier sources (the *Acts of Andrew*) simply say he was crucified.
