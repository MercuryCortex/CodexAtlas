# Enoch

## Identity
The seventh antediluvian patriarch (Gen 5:21–24), who "walked with God; then he was no more, because God took him" — a cryptic notice that grew into one of the richest figures of Second Temple Judaism. The Genesis text is brief; the *1 Enoch* corpus (Ge'ez Ethiopic, with Aramaic fragments at Qumran) develops Enoch into a heaven-tour-conducting visionary scribe, the recipient of cosmological-eschatological-meteorological-calendrical revelations, and ultimately (in 3 Enoch / *Sefer Hekhalot*) into the transformed angel Metatron, the "lesser YHWH" enthroned beside God's throne.

## Historical evidence
None as historical person; substantial as literary-traditional figure. The Aramaic Enochic texts from Qumran (4Q201–212) attest the antiquity (3rd c. BCE onward) of much of *1 Enoch*.

## Works (traditionally attributed)
- [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — the Book of the Watchers, Book of Parables, Astronomical Book, Book of Dreams, Epistle of Enoch.
- 2 Enoch (Slavonic) — later, possibly 1st c. CE.
- 3 Enoch / *Sefer Hekhalot* — late-antique Jewish mystical text; Enoch becomes Metatron.

## Role in the tradition
- Second Temple Judaism: Enoch is a major revelatory figure; his calendar (the 364-day solar calendar) is a sectarian-Qumran touchpoint.
- Christianity: Enoch is cited in Jude 14–15; *1 Enoch* is canonical Scripture in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church.
- Islam: identified by tradition with the Qur'anic prophet Idris (Qur'an 19:56–57, 21:85–86).
- Jewish mysticism: the Enoch-Metatron tradition is foundational for *Hekhalot* literature and for later Kabbalah; see [[metatron]].

## Disputes
- Dating and stratigraphy of *1 Enoch*'s constituent books.
- The "Enochic Judaism" hypothesis (Boccaccini): a distinct Second Temple stream rivaling proto-rabbinic Judaism.
