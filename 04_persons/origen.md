# Origen

Among the most prolific writers of antiquity (Jerome reports ~6,000 works; certainly hundreds of volumes). Born in Alexandria ~185 to a Christian family — his father Leonides was martyred under Septimius Severus 202. Head of the Alexandrian catechetical school after Clement; later relocated to Caesarea Maritima after conflict with Bishop Demetrius (~231) and was ordained a priest there. Tortured under the Decian persecution (250–251), died of the resulting injuries ~254.

Origen's *Peri Archōn* (On First Principles) is the first systematic Christian theology; his *Hexapla* is the foundational tool of Christian biblical scholarship; his *Contra Celsum* the most influential ancient Christian-pagan philosophical debate. Specific doctrines (pre-existence of souls; *apokatastasis* / universal restoration; subordination of the Son) were posthumously condemned at the Second Council of Constantinople (553); even so, Origen's exegetical method, ascetic ideal, and theological depth profoundly shaped subsequent Christianity (Cappadocian Fathers, Evagrius, eastern monasticism). Porphyry reports an "Origen" studied with Ammonius Saccas alongside Plotinus — whether this is the Christian Origen is contested.
