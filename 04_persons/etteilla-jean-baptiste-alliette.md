# Etteilla (Jean-Baptiste Alliette)

## Identity

**Jean-Baptiste Alliette** (Paris, 1738–1791), who wrote and traded under his own surname reversed — **Etteilla** — was a Parisian seed- and print-seller who became the first professional fortune-teller of the modern West. He is the person who turned card-reading from a parlour amusement into a paid practice with a literature, a method, a school and a product.

## What he actually did, in order

The order matters more than any single item in it, because it is what disproves the story he himself told.

**1770 — ordinary playing cards.** *Etteilla, ou manière de se récréer avec un jeu de cartes* teaches divination with a **32-card piquet pack**: no tarot, no Egypt, no Hebrew. French card divination begins here, with the cheapest deck in the country. See [[divination-cartomancy-playing-card]].

**1781 — the Egyptian frame arrives from elsewhere.** [[antoine-court-de-gebelin]] publishes *Le Monde primitif* vol. 8, asserting that the tarot is the Egyptian *Book of Thoth*, and [[comte-de-mellet]]'s essay in the same volume ties the trumps to the Hebrew alphabet. Etteilla is by now an established cartomancer of eleven years' standing.

**1783–1785 — tarot absorbed.** *Manière de se récréer avec le jeu de cartes nommées tarots* extends his method to the 78-card pack and adopts Court de Gébelin's Egyptian thesis wholesale. This is the first systematic divinatory treatment of tarot in print: fixed meanings for every card, **meanings for reversed cards**, and laying-out procedures.

**c. 1788–1789 — the first divination deck.** The ***Livre de Thot*** (the "Grand Etteilla"), engraved by Pierre-François Basan. Etteilla did not simply annotate the Marseille pack; he **redesigned it** — re-ordered the trumps, replaced imagery with pseudo-Egyptian scenes, added astrological attributions, and printed divinatory keywords directly onto the cards, upright and reversed. It is the first deck in history made for reading rather than playing.

He also founded a subscription society of interpreters of the *Livre de Thot* — the first organised body of tarot readers — and taught the method for fees until his death in 1791.

**The sequence is the finding.** Divinatory card-reading (1770) *precedes* the Egyptian origin-myth (1781) *precedes* divinatory tarot (1783). The practice did not descend from an ancient book; it was an existing French popular practice that acquired an ancient book as a credential, thirteen years in.

## Role in the tradition

- **Founder of the practice.** Every later divinatory tarot system — [[eliphas-levi]]'s, [[papus]]'s, the Golden Dawn's, [[arthur-edward-waite]]'s — inherits from Etteilla the premise that the cards answer questions, the apparatus of fixed per-card meanings, the reversal convention, and the spread.
- **Commercial as well as doctrinal.** He is also the origin of tarot as a *product*: a branded deck, a book to explain it, and a subscription school. The modern tarot market is structurally his invention.
- **Disowned by his heirs.** Lévi was contemptuous of Etteilla's scheme and replaced his correspondences entirely; Waite repeats the disdain. This is a recurring shape in the occult revival — each generation discards the previous one's system while keeping the premise that a system exists to be recovered.

## Disputes

- **The deck's date.** 1788 and 1789 are both given for the *Livre de Thot*; the publication ran over that boundary and the surviving evidence does not fix a single year. This vault writes it **c. 1788–1789**.
- **How much of the Egyptian frame is his.** Practitioner tradition sometimes credits Etteilla with the Egyptian origin-story itself, or claims he was taught it by an Italian named Alexis. Decker, Depaulis & Dummett (1996) find no support for either; the documented sequence has him taking the thesis from Court de Gébelin's 1781 essay.
- **"First cartomancer" as a superlative.** He is the first *documented* professional card-reader and the first to publish a system. Informal card-fortune-telling before 1770 is likely and largely unrecorded; the claim here is about the documented record, not about human behaviour.
- **The 1770 book's authorship.** A small number of practitioner sources dispute Alliette's authorship of the 1770 piquet manual. The critical literature accepts it.
