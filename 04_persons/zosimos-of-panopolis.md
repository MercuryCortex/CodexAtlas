# Zosimos of Panopolis

## Identity
A late-3rd / early-4th-century Greek-speaking alchemist active in Upper Egypt, born at Panopolis / Akhmim — the same Upper Egyptian region from which the [[event-nag-hammadi-discovery|Nag Hammadi]] Gnostic codices would later be buried. **The earliest extant Western alchemist whose writings have substantial direct preservation.** Author of a vast body of alchemical-philosophical writing — partly technical-procedural, partly visionary-symbolic — preserved in fragments in Byzantine alchemical compilations (esp. Marcianus graecus 299, the foundational ~10th-11th-c. Greek alchemical anthology), Syriac translations, and Arabic citations.

Zosimos is the **load-bearing transmission node** for Western alchemy in two directions simultaneously:
1. **Backward** into late-antique Greco-Egyptian Hermetic-Gnostic-philosophical milieu (his contemporary religious environment is shared with the Hermetic corpus, the Sethian and Valentinian Gnostic schools, and the Neoplatonic-magical literature).
2. **Forward** into the medieval Arabic alchemical tradition (cited by [[jabir-ibn-hayyan|Jabir]]) and the Byzantine Greek tradition that re-entered Latin Europe in the 12th-13th centuries through translation programs.

## Historical evidence
Documented through (a) the *Suda* lexicon (Byzantine, ~10th-c. encyclopedic entry); (b) extensive direct citation in the Byzantine alchemical Greek manuscript corpus (especially Marcianus graecus 299); (c) Syriac transmission (Cambridge Mm 6.29); (d) Arabic citation (Jabir, Razi). The Mertens 1995 Budé edition is the standard critical edition of the authentic Greek fragments.

## Works

- ***Cheirokmeta*** ("Things Done by Hand") — a vast technical-philosophical work organized by letters of the Greek alphabet, of which approximately 28 books are attested in Byzantine catalogues and substantial fragments survive.
- ***The Visions of Zosimos*** — the foundational visionary-alchemical literary text. Zosimos describes dream-encounters with a priest-figure who is dismembered, boiled, transformed in a bowl-shaped sacrificial apparatus, and reconstituted as a new being. **This is the proto-text of the Western tradition that reads alchemical-material operations as simultaneously psychic / spiritual transformations.** The visions explicitly equate the *priest's body* with the *materia prima* — making the alchemist's operation a sacrificial-redemptive *opus*.
- ***On the Letter Omega*** — discusses the cosmological significance of Ω (omega, the last Greek letter); Zosimos relates Ω to Kronos / Saturn / the seventh planetary sphere; mystical letter-allegory.
- **Letters to Theosebeia** — Zosimos addressed substantial correspondence to a female colleague / student named Theosebeia, in a Hermetic-teacher / Hermetic-student rhetorical form. The relationship documents the **gender-inclusive character of late-antique alchemical practice** (cf. [[maria-the-jewess]], who probably preceded Zosimos by a generation or two and is cited by him).

## Role in the tradition

- **The proto-spiritual-alchemist.** Earlier Greco-Egyptian alchemical authors (Pseudo-Democritus' *Physika kai Mystika*, ~1st c. CE; Maria the Jewess; the *Leiden Papyrus X* and *Stockholm Papyrus*, ~3rd c. CE, technical recipe collections) had given technical-craft alchemical instructions. Zosimos is **the first surviving extant Greek-language alchemist whose written work explicitly frames the *opus* as a religious-philosophical-soteriological undertaking** rather than only a craft. This framing becomes definitive for the entire subsequent Western tradition.
- **Bridge to Gnostic-Hermetic milieu.** Zosimos cites Hermes and Mary (Maria the Jewess) but also discusses *Adam* and the *plasma* (formed-being) — language showing engagement with Jewish-Gnostic anthropogony as well as Greek philosophy. His treatment of the [[demiurge-gnostic|demiurge]] is consistent with Sethian Gnostic positions.
- **Arabic transmission node.** Zosimos is one of the named alchemical *auctoritates* of medieval Arabic alchemy: Jabir, Razi, Ibn Umayl all cite him.
- **Latin-medieval reception.** Latin alchemy (12th-c. onward) recovers Zosimos indirectly through Arabic-Latin translation; he is not the most-cited authority for Latin alchemists (Hermes Trismegistus, Geber, Albertus Magnus, Pseudo-Lull occupy that space) but he is named.
- **Jungian reception (20th-c.).** [[carl-gustav-jung|Jung]]'s *Alchemical Studies* (1968 collected) treats Zosimos's *Visions* as the prototype of alchemy-as-individuation: the priest-dismemberment-and-reconstitution dream is read as the prototype of the psychological *transformatio* the alchemist projects onto matter. This Jungian reading is the dominant 20th-21st-c. channel for Zosimos's renewed visibility.

## Connections — Zosimos at the late-antique alchemy-mysticism interface

- **→ [[maria-the-jewess]]** *direct-citation*: Zosimos cites Maria as an alchemical predecessor. Maria, Cleopatra (the alchemist, not the Ptolemaic queen), and Zosimos's correspondent Theosebeia form the documented late-antique tradition of **women in Greco-Egyptian alchemy**.
- **→ [[phase-4-073-tabula-smaragdina|Tabula Smaragdina]]** *shared-milieu*: Zosimos predates the (Arabic-attested) Tabula by 400-500 years but operates in the same Greco-Egyptian Hermetic milieu from which the later Hermetic-alchemical corpus would emerge.
- **→ [[tradition-gnosticism]]** *shared-milieu*: Zosimos's Upper-Egyptian context, his engagement with Adam-anthropogony and *plasma* / *plasmatic-being* language, and his references to the *archons* / *eimarmene* (cosmic-rulership / fate) place him in conversation with the Sethian and Valentinian Gnostic schools active in his region and time.
- **→ [[tradition-hermeticism]]** *direct-engagement*: Zosimos cites Hermes (Trismegistus) repeatedly as an alchemical and philosophical authority. The Hermetic Greek philosophical corpus ([[phase-3-021-hermetic-corpus-earliest]], [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]]) is contemporaneous; Zosimos and the *Corpus Hermeticum* both belong to the late-antique Greek Hermetic intellectual world of Egypt.
- **→ [[jabir-ibn-hayyan]]** *cited-by*: Jabir cites Zosimos in the Arabic alchemical corpus.
- **→ [[phase-7-017-psychology-and-alchemy]]** *Jungian-reception*: Jung's reading of Zosimos's Visions as proto-individuation.

## Disputes
- **How much of the Marcianus 299 attributed-Zosimos corpus is authentically his?** Substantial; Mertens 1995 distinguishes the authentic core from later Byzantine attributions.
- **Did Zosimos consciously hold a Gnostic theology?** Probably not in any technical Sethian-or-Valentinian school sense, but he engages Gnostic-style anthropogonic motifs and operates in adjacent intellectual space.
- **Date of birth/death.** Late 3rd to early 4th c. CE is secure; specific years are reconstructive.
