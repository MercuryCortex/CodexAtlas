# Hiawatha (Ayenwatha)

Co-founder of the Haudenosaunee Confederacy alongside [[deganawidah-peacemaker]]. Because Deganawidah had a speech impediment, Hiawatha served as his orator and ambassador, carrying the message of the Great Law of Peace to each of the five nations. Hiawatha had himself suffered grievously from the cycle of Mourning War revenge — his daughters were killed by the Onondaga war chief Tadodaho — and his grief was combed away (literally — the "He Who Combs" etymology) by Deganawidah in an act that became the model for the Condolence Ceremony, the ritual through which new chiefs are installed and community grief is addressed. 

**Important disambiguation:** Longfellow's 1855 poem *"The Song of Hiawatha"* creates a fictional Ojibwe character also named Hiawatha, based on Henry Rowe Schoolcraft's conflation of Haudenosaunee Hiawatha with Ojibwe Nanabozho legends. The two figures are entirely unrelated. See [[tradition-haudenosaunee]] for the political theology context.
