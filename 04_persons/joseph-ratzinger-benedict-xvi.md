# Joseph Ratzinger / Pope Benedict XVI

## Identity
German Roman Catholic theologian (1927–2022); Pope from 2005 to his historically unusual 2013 resignation (the first papal resignation since Gregory XII in 1415). Patristic-Augustinian scholar; *peritus* at [[event-second-vatican-council-1962-1965]]; later Prefect of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith (1981–2005). Architect of the "hermeneutic of continuity" reading of Vatican II — the council as in-continuity rather than in-rupture with prior Catholic teaching.

## Works
*Introduction to Christianity* (1968) is his most-read theological work — a creedal-commentary engagement with modern doubt. *Jesus of Nazareth* (2007–2012, 3 vols) is his theological-historical Jesus trilogy written as Pope (in his personal-scholar voice rather than papal-magisterial voice). *Summorum Pontificum* (2007) expanded permission for the Tridentine Mass — reversed by [[pope-francis]]'s *Traditionis Custodes* (2021).

## Role in the tradition
A central figure in the long [[event-vatican-ii-aftermath-1965-present]]. Pioneered the "hermeneutic of continuity" interpretive school. Theologically Augustinian rather than Thomist; aligned with the *Communio* journal wing of *ressourcement* (with [[hans-urs-von-balthasar]] and [[henri-de-lubac]]) rather than the *Concilium* progressive wing (Küng, Schillebeeckx).
