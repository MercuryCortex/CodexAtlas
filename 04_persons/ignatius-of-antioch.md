# Ignatius of Antioch

## Identity
Bishop of Antioch in Syria, arrested under Trajan (probably ~+107/+108) and transported under guard across Asia Minor to Rome for execution. During the journey he composed seven letters to local churches and to [[polycarp-of-smyrna]] that survive (the *middle recension*) — among the most important documents of early-2nd-c. Christianity. He is the **first attested advocate of monoepiscopacy** ("Do nothing without the bishop" — Ign. *Smyrn.* 8.1) — i.e., the structural transition from college-of-presbyter-bishops to single-bishop-with-presbyters-and-deacons.

## Historical evidence
The seven letters of the middle recension are universally accepted (since Lightfoot 1885) as genuine. Polycarp's letter to the Philippians (Pol. *Phil.* 13) — written shortly after — confirms the journey, the correspondence, and the impending martyrdom. Eusebius and Origen consider Ignatius's letters authoritative.

A 19th-c. "Syriac short recension" controversy was decided in favor of the seven-letter middle recension. The thirteen-letter "long recension" is a 4th-c. expansion. The seven *Spurious* letters are universally rejected.

## Works
Seven authentic letters (~+108) addressing: (a) **threats to ecclesial unity** — schism, false teaching; (b) **threats from Docetism and Judaizing**; (c) **his impending martyrdom**, theologically as joyful imitation of Christ; (d) **monoepiscopacy** as the bulwark against heresy and schism. His letter *To the Romans* famously *begs the Romans not to intervene to save him* — "Let me be food for the wild beasts; through them I can reach God" (*Rom.* 4.1).

## Role in the tradition
- **Foundation of monoepiscopal church order**: the bishop-presbyter-deacon structure that became normative.
- **Anti-Docetist Christology**: insists on Jesus's real flesh, real birth, real suffering — against the proto-Gnostic / Docetist currents already strong in Antioch.
- **Theology of martyrdom**: martyrdom as eucharistic union with Christ.
- **Term *Christianismos* ("Christianity") used here for the first time** (*Magn.* 10, *Rom.* 3, *Phil.* 6) — earliest known instance.

## Disputes
- **Date.** Trajan's reign (98–117) is secure; the standard ~+107/+108 is approximate.
- **Authenticity** of various letter-recensions — settled in favor of the middle recension since Lightfoot.
- **Whether monoepiscopacy was already established in Antioch.** Ignatius assumes it; whether his picture is descriptive or aspirational is debated.
