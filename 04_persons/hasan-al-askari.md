# Hasan al-Askari

## Identity
Son of [[ali-al-hadi]]; born in Medina ~846; brought to Samarra with his father as a child. Succeeded his father as **Eleventh Imam** in 868 at age ~22. Lived under continued Abbasid surveillance in Samarra, with very limited access by the Imami community. Died in Samarra 874 at age ~28 (poisoned per Shi'i tradition; the historical record is suggestive). His death triggered the **succession crisis** that gave rise to the doctrine of the Twelfth Imam's *occultation* (*ghayba*) — see [[muhammad-al-mahdi]].

## Historical evidence
Documented in Shi'i hadith collections; less extensively in Sunni biographical sources. The crucial *historiographical-doctrinal* question — whether al-Askari had a son who succeeded him as Twelfth Imam — is the foundational issue of post-874 Twelver Shi'ism (see Disputes).

## Role in the tradition

### The succession crisis of 874
At al-Askari's death, the Imami community was thrown into an acute crisis. The Sunni-Abbasid sources of the late 9th c. report al-Askari as having died *without* a publicly-recognized son. Modarressi 1993 — the foundational critical study — documents that the early Imami community (the late 9th–early 10th c.) was deeply divided on the succession: at least **fourteen different sub-sects** appeared in the immediate post-874 period (some holding al-Askari's brother Ja'far was the rightful Imam; some holding al-Askari himself had been the Mahdi; some holding the Imamate had ended; some — eventually the majority — holding that al-Askari had had a secret son who had gone into *ghayba* / occultation).

The *eventual Twelver position* — that al-Askari had a son **Muhammad ibn al-Hasan** born ~869, hidden from public view from his father's death (or from age ~5) onward, who went into the *Minor Occultation* (communicating with the community through four successive *safīrs* — special deputies — from 874 to 941) and then the *Major Occultation* from 941 onward — consolidated only gradually over the following century. By the time of the Buyid period (10th–11th c.) and the great Imami hadith compilers (Kulayni d. 941; ibn Babawayh d. 991; al-Mufid d. 1022), the Twelver doctrine is firmly in place and the alternative sub-sects have largely faded.

This crystallization process is the focus of Modarressi's *Crisis and Consolidation* (1993) and Sachedina's *Islamic Messianism* (1981).

## Disputes

- **Did al-Askari have a son?** Sunni and non-Twelver sources of the late 9th c. report he did not. The Twelver position is that he had a son who was deliberately hidden from public view for reasons of safety against Abbasid persecution and that the lack of public recognition is precisely what *began* the occultation. This is the foundational historical-theological question of Twelver Shi'ism: the entire Twelver doctrine of the Twelfth Imam rests on the existence of this child. Critical historiography (Modarressi 1993; Hussain 1982 *The Occultation of the Twelfth Imam*) treats the question as genuinely open and emphasizes that the Twelver doctrine of the Twelfth Imam is a *consolidated reconstruction* of the Buyid period, not a pre-existing public certainty of 874.

- **The fourteen sub-sects.** Modarressi 1993 documents the immediate post-874 division of the community in considerable detail. The eventual Twelver consensus is not a simple persistence of an original truth but the outcome of a doctrinal-political consolidation process.

- ***Tafsir al-Imam al-Askari* authenticity.** A Qur'anic commentary preserved under al-Askari's name; widely treated as a later pseudonymous compilation rather than authentically by the Imam.

- **Cause of death.** Standard Shi'i tradition (poisoned by orders of Caliph al-Mu'tamid). Historical evidence suggestive, not securely demonstrable.

## Cross-tradition connections
- ↔ [[ali-al-hadi]] (father), [[muhammad-al-mahdi]] (son, per Twelver doctrine; Twelfth Imam in occultation) — the terminal link in the visible Twelver Imami line.
- ↔ [[tradition-shia-islam]] — the succession crisis at his death is the founding moment of post-Imam Twelver Islam.
- ↔ [[messianism]], [[apocalyptic-cosmic-warfare]] — the post-874 doctrinal consolidation produces the most fully-developed Islamic messianism.
