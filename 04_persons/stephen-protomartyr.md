# Stephen the Protomartyr

## Identity
A leading figure among the Greek-speaking ("Hellenist") faction of the Jerusalem Christian community, selected with six others as a *diakonos* to handle community administration (Acts 6:1–6). His public dispute with diaspora-Jewish synagogues led to charges of blasphemy against the Temple and the Torah, a Sanhedrin hearing, his long speech reviewing salvation history (Acts 7 — the *longest* speech in Acts), and his death by stoning outside Jerusalem ~+36 — **the first recorded Christian martyrdom**. Saul of Tarsus (the future [[paul-of-tarsus]]) is explicitly placed at the execution, holding the executioners' cloaks (Acts 7:58; 8:1) — making this a hinge moment in early Christian narrative.

## Historical evidence
Single-source (Lukan); Paul nowhere mentions Stephen by name in his letters, though he refers to having "persecuted the church of God" (Gal 1:13; 1 Cor 15:9). The Stephen-narrative likely preserves authentic memory of an early intra-Jewish-Christian conflict around Temple-critical theology — the Stephen speech (Acts 7) shows striking parallels to the Samaritan Pentateuch theology and to Qumran Temple-critique, suggesting an early Hellenist-Christian theological current that diverged sharply from the Temple-attached Jerusalem-disciple stream (Hill 1992).

## Works
None.

## Role in the tradition
**The Protomartyr**: the first and archetypal Christian martyr. December 26 feast (immediately after Christmas — the "first martyr after the Birth"). Patron of deacons. The Stephen-speech foreshadows the Pauline critique of Temple-Torah supersession.

**Hellenist origins**: Stephen and the Seven (Acts 6) constitute the **first attested Greek-speaking Christian leadership** in Jerusalem; their dispersal under the post-stoning persecution (Acts 8:1–4) is the engine of the Christian expansion to Antioch (where the term "Christian" was first used per Acts 11:26).

## Disputes
- **Date.** ~+33/+36 — depends on Pauline conversion chronology.
- **Authenticity of Acts 7 speech.** Mainstream critical view: substantially Lukan composition with possible historical kernel.
- **Theological position of the Hellenists.** Hill (1992) and others reconstruct it as distinct from the Twelve's position; the data are sparse.
