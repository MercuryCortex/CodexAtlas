# Mani

Founder of Manichaeism (216–277), born in southern Mesopotamia (modern Iraq) to an Elchasaite Jewish-Christian baptist family. Mani reports visions of his heavenly twin (syzygos) at ages 4, 12, and 24 — the third commissioning him to break with the Elchasaites and to found a universal world-religion that would absorb and supersede the partial revelations of Adam, Seth, Enoch, the Buddha, Zoroaster, and Jesus. Mani is the "seal of the prophets" of his own system. He traveled to India and back, eventually obtaining the favor of Sasanian king Shapur I (~242), to whom he dedicated the Šābuhragān.

Mani's career ended under Shapur's son Bahram I (~274–277), who imprisoned him at the instigation of the Zoroastrian Magi led by Kartir; Mani died in prison after twenty-six days of "audience" (*šemā'a*), and his body was mutilated and exhibited. The religion he founded spread from the Atlantic to the Pacific over the next millennium: Augustine was a Manichaean Hearer for nine years; Manichaean kingdoms existed in Central Asia; the Uighur Khaganate adopted Manichaeism as state religion in 763; Chinese Manichaean texts have been recovered from Dunhuang and persisted in some forms into the Ming dynasty. Mani's claim to be a *writing* prophet who personally produced canonical scriptures distinguished his movement from Christianity's reliance on disciples' accounts.
