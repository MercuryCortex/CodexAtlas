# Ur-Bau

## Identity
Ur-Bau ("Servant of [the goddess] Bau") was *ensi* of Lagash in the late Gutian period, conventionally dated ~-2164 to -2144 BCE. He is the predecessor of [[gudea-of-lagash]] in the Second Dynasty of Lagash sequence (Lagash II), and in the most widely-followed reconstruction is **Gudea's father-in-law** — Gudea married Ur-Bau's daughter Ningalla (Nin-alla) and inherited (or married into) the Lagash ensi-ship. Ur-Bau's reign overlapped with the declining phase of Gutian hegemony over southern Mesopotamia; whether he ruled as nominal Gutian vassal or as effective independent is debated. He initiated the program of temple-construction at Girsu — restorations of the Eninnu of Ningirsu and dedications to other Lagashite deities — that Gudea would extend on a far grander scale.

## Historical evidence
Solid for the period:
1. **Royal inscriptions** on votive objects, statues, and door-sockets (Edzard RIME 3/1).
2. **A diorite statue of Ur-Bau** (Statue 1 of the Lagash II corpus, now in the Louvre) — a seated figure in the style that would be perfected under Gudea, with extensive inscription on the lap.
3. **Brick stamps and foundation deposits** from his building works at Girsu.
4. **Lagash administrative records** of the late Gutian period naming him.

## Works
Royal inscriptions and the inscriptions on his statue and votive objects (Edzard 1997, RIME 3/1) — temple-restoration dedications and votive offerings to the deities of Lagash.

## Role in the tradition
Ur-Bau is the founder-figure of the Lagash II cultural project that culminated in Gudea. The diorite-statue tradition of Lagash II, the program of Eninnu rebuilding, and the cultivation of long-distance procurement for temple materials all begin under Ur-Bau and are extended under Gudea. In modern Assyriology and art history his statue is recognized as the immediate stylistic predecessor of the Gudea statues — important for understanding the development of third-millennium Mesopotamian sculpture.

## Disputes
- **Father-in-law relationship to Gudea**. The dominant view (Edzard 1997; Suter 2000) is that Gudea was Ur-Bau's son-in-law via Ningalla. A minority position holds that the relationship was non-familial or that Gudea was Ur-Bau's son.
- **Date**. Conventional ~-2164 to -2144 BCE; the chronological relationship to the Gutian dynasty's collapse and to Ur-Nammu's rise at Ur is debated.
- **Political status**. Independent ruler vs. Gutian vassal: unresolved.
