# Johannes Tauler

## Identity
14th-c. German Dominican preacher and mystic (1300–1361). **Pupil of [[meister-eckhart]]** at Cologne in the 1320s; with [[henry-suso|Suso]], the principal *post-Eckhart* Rhineland mystic. Based in Strasbourg from the 1330s; conducted pastoral preaching and mystical-spiritual direction during the **Black Death** (1348–1349) — an experience that shaped his preaching's preoccupation with suffering, equanimity, and *Gelassenheit* (releasement / letting-be). Associated with the *Gottesfreunde* (Friends of God) lay mystical network around Rulman Merswin and the Johannite Commandery at the Grünen Wörth, Strasbourg.

Tauler's enormous later-medieval and Reformation-era reception is partly due to **Martin Luther's** repeated 1516–1521 endorsement of his preaching — Luther treated Tauler as a "pre-Reformation" German evangelical voice and printed (or had printed) the **anonymous *Theologia Germanica*** under partial-Taulerian attribution in 1516 and 1518.

## Works
- **~80 vernacular German sermons** — most preached to nuns of Dominican convents along the Upper Rhine (Strasbourg, Basel, Cologne, Konstanz, Engelthal). The Vetter 1910 critical edition is the standard.

## Role in the tradition
- **Eckhart-popularizer.** Where Suso defended Eckhart's metaphysics theologically, Tauler popularized the central practical-spiritual content (*Gelassenheit*, *grundlosigkeit*, the divine birth in the soul) through accessible vernacular sermons.
- **Friends of God movement.** The *Gottesfreunde* was a lay-and-clerical mystical network that operated as a sort of devotional fellowship across confessional and class lines in the Upper Rhine 1330s–1380s. Tauler was its principal preacher-figure.
- **Pre-Reformation evangelical reception.** Luther's endorsement of Tauler made him a continuously-read German mystical author through the 16th-19th centuries.
