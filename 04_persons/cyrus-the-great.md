# Cyrus the Great

## Identity
Cyrus II, founder of the Achaemenid Persian Empire — the first world empire in the strict sense — and one of the most consequential figures of the Axial Age for *religious* history, despite not being a religious figure himself. Beginning as a vassal king of Anshan in the Median empire, he revolted (~559), defeated the Median king Astyages (~550), conquered Lydia (taking Sardis from King Croesus ~547), and entered Babylon as liberator in October 539 BCE without battle, ending the Neo-Babylonian Empire. His policy of religious tolerance and restoration of displaced peoples — most famously the Judahite exiles, who returned to Jerusalem by his decree (538/537 BCE) — set the precedent for Achaemenid imperial governance and inaugurated the Persian period (539–332 BCE) of Near Eastern history.

## Historical evidence
The Cyrus Cylinder (~539 BCE, Akkadian cuneiform, recovered 1879 in Babylon, now in the British Museum) — his own royal proclamation upon taking Babylon. The Nabonidus Chronicle (Babylonian) records the conquest. Greek historiography from Herodotus and Xenophon onward. Hebrew Bible references in Ezra, Nehemiah, 2 Chronicles, and most strikingly Second Isaiah, who hails Cyrus as YHWH's *māšîaḥ* ("anointed", Isa 45:1) — the only Gentile so titled — and as YHWH's instrument for the restoration.

## Works
None authored personally. The Cyrus Cylinder is a royal proclamation composed by Babylonian scribes in his name, in the Mesopotamian royal-inscription genre — explicitly framing Cyrus as legitimated by Marduk and restoring proper worship.

## Role in the tradition
- **Jewish tradition**: liberator and restorer; the Persian period under Cyrus and his successors (Darius, Xerxes, Artaxerxes) is the matrix in which the Hebrew Bible takes substantially its received shape, the Temple is rebuilt (~520–515 BCE), and the developing Judaism encounters Zoroastrian dualism, angelology, and eschatology. *This is the central inflection point of Phase 2-into-Phase 3.*
- **Greek tradition**: paradigmatic just king (Xenophon's *Cyropaedia*).
- **Iranian tradition**: legendary founder of Iranian civilization.
- **Modern**: the Cyrus Cylinder is sometimes presented (anachronistically) as "the first declaration of human rights"; the academic view is more measured (a royal proclamation in a standard genre, distinguished by its tolerance policy but not a rights document).

## Disputes
- **Religion.** Whether Cyrus was personally Zoroastrian, generically Iranian-polytheist, or pragmatically syncretic is debated; the Achaemenid imperial cult formally invokes Ahura Mazda from Darius onward.
- **Edict to the Jews.** The Ezra version is in Hebrew/Aramaic and may be a Judean retelling; the historical edict's exact wording is unknown but the *fact* of return permission is widely accepted.
