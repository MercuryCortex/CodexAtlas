# Ibn Rushd (Averroes)

## The Commentator

Averroes (1126–1198) is the philosopher who re-transmitted Aristotle to the medieval West — and in so doing, both created Scholasticism and threatened it. Latin medieval universities called him simply "the Commentator" (*al-Sharih*) — the Aristotle commentator, without further qualification. When Dante places him in the *Inferno*'s Noble Pagans (Canto IV), he shares the zone with Homer, Plato, and "the Philosopher" himself.

His commentaries on Aristotle — three levels: Short (paraphrase), Middle (exposition), Long (line-by-line analysis) — were the channel through which *Physics*, *Metaphysics*, *De Anima*, and the Nicomachean *Ethics* entered Latin Scholasticism. Without Averroes, Aquinas has different texts and writes a different *Summa*.

## MASSIVE WIN — The Aristotle Commentaries and Latin Scholasticism

The transmission chain:

```
Aristotle, Athens (-384 to -322)
     ↓ Arabic translation, House of Wisdom (c. 800–850 CE)
al-Farabi (950), Avicenna (1037) — Islamic Aristotelian synthesis
     ↓
Averroes, Córdoba (c. 1169–1195) — three-level commentaries on the entire corpus
     ↓ Latin translation, Toledo (c. 1230, Michael Scot translates from Arabic)
     ↓
Albertus Magnus, Paris/Cologne (c. 1250) — introduces Averroist Aristotle to Scholasticism
     ↓
Thomas Aquinas (c. 1265–1274) — Summa Theologiae: Christian-Aristotelian synthesis
     ↓
Duns Scotus, William of Ockham — Scholastic dissent from Thomism
     ↓
Renaissance Aristotelianism, Paduan school (16th c.)
```

The *Summa Theologiae* is impossible without Averroes. Every Thomist argument about substance, accident, form, matter, act, and potency uses Aristotelian vocabulary that entered Latin philosophy through Averroes's commentaries.

## The Controversy: Double Truth and the Unity of the Intellect

Two of Averroes's doctrines made him the most controversial philosopher of the 13th century:

**1. Eternity of the world**: Aristotle argued the world has no beginning in time; Averroes defended this against Islamic theologians. Both Islamic conservatives (al-Ghazali's *Incoherence of the Philosophers*) and Christian theologians rejected this. Aquinas argued against it; the 1277 Paris condemnation explicitly targeted it.

**2. Unity of the Active Intellect**: Averroes argued that the *active intellect* (the cognitive faculty that makes genuine understanding possible) is a single shared intellect for all humanity — not an individual faculty. This appeared to deny personal immortality (if there is no individual intellect, there is no individual soul to survive death). Latin Averroism developed this into the "double truth" doctrine (a proposition can be true in philosophy and false in theology simultaneously). Aquinas attacked this at length in *De Unitate Intellectus*.
