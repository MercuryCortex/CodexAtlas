# Melchizedek

## Identity
Melchizedek (Hebrew *malkī-ṣédeq* "my king is righteousness" or "king of righteousness") is a brief but disproportionately consequential figure in the Hebrew Bible — appearing in only two passages: **Genesis 14:18-20**, where he is "king of Salem and priest of God Most High" who blesses Abram after the battle of the kings and receives a tithe; and **Psalm 110:4**, where the addressed king is declared "a priest forever after the order of Melchizedek." From these two passages, Second-Temple Judaism, early Christianity, Gnosticism, and Mormonism each developed a substantial Melchizedek reception-tradition. The **Qumran 11Q13 (11QMelchizedek)** scroll (~-100) figures Melchizedek as an **eschatological priestly redeemer**, possibly identified with the archangel Michael. The **Letter to the Hebrews** (chapters 5-7) deploys him as the typological pattern for Christ's eternal, non-Levitical high priesthood. The **Nag Hammadi Melchizedek** (NHC IX,1) presents him as a heavenly priest-figure linked with Jesus Christ. The **Mormon Melchizedek Priesthood** (the higher of two LDS priesthood orders, since Joseph Smith's 1830s revelations) names a sacerdotal lineage. **Mandaean tradition** preserves him as well. He is the single most cross-tradition-receptive minor biblical figure.

## Historical evidence
The setting (Bronze-Age "Salem," Abram's lifetime ~-2000 by Hebrew chronology) is legendary; historicity in the Genesis-14 narrative sense is not defensible. **The reception-history is fully historically attested** from Psalm 110 forward — i.e., the Melchizedek figure as a *literary-religious construct* has documented continuous use from the late monarchy / exile period through the present in multiple traditions.

## Works
None — a frame-figure, not an author.

## Role in the tradition

- **In the Hebrew Bible**: a brief Genesis-14 figure + the eternal-priest-formula of Psalm 110:4. The latter becomes the load-bearing piece in subsequent reception.
- **In Qumran / Second-Temple Judaism**: **11QMelchizedek (11Q13)** — eschatological priestly figure who proclaims the Day of Atonement / Jubilee of liberation; identified by some scholars with Michael the archangel. **2 Enoch 70-72** (Slavonic Apocalypse, long recension) gives a remarkable birth-narrative: Melchizedek born miraculously to Sothonim, taken to paradise by Michael before the Flood.
- **In Philo of Alexandria** (*Legum Allegoriae* 3.79-82): allegorized as Logos / the right reason.
- **In Christianity**: the Letter to the Hebrews (chs. 5-7) is THE foundational Christian-Melchizedek text — Christ as "high priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek" (Heb 5:6, 6:20, 7:17, 21). The argument: Melchizedek's priesthood predates the Levitical priesthood (Abraham tithed to him before Levi was born), is non-genealogical ("without father, without mother, without genealogy" — Heb 7:3), and is therefore typologically superior — supplying the framework for Christ's non-Aaronic priesthood.
- **In Gnosticism**: *Melchizedek* (NHC IX,1) — a Sethian Christian-Gnostic treatise in which Melchizedek is a heavenly priestly figure linked to Jesus Christ.
- **In Mandaeism**: appears in the Right Ginza as a priestly figure.
- **In Mormonism**: the Melchizedek Priesthood (the higher of two LDS priesthood orders) named for him; Joseph Smith's revelations (D&C 107) construct an elaborate Melchizedek-Priesthood theology.

## Disputes
- **Identification of "Salem"**: Genesis 14 says "Salem"; the standard interpretation (Josephus *Antiquities* 1.180, *Jewish War* 6.438) is Jerusalem (Ps 76:2 calls Jerusalem "Salem"). A minority view places "Salem" near Shechem (citing John 3:23 "Aenon near Salim").
- **Melchizedek = Michael equation**: 11QMelchizedek's eschatological priestly redeemer is identified by Kobelski 1981 with Michael; this reading is contested but is a major Qumran-studies position.
- **Hebrews's source for the "without father, without mother, without genealogy" formula**: derives from the silence of Genesis 14 (no genealogy given) read typologically as positive — characteristic Second-Temple exegetical move.
- **Date of composition for the relevant strata**: Genesis 14 is a notoriously composite chapter (the "kings of the east" episode is sometimes treated as an independent insertion); Psalm 110 is variously dated to David's time (traditional) vs. post-exilic (some critical views). The reception-history is unambiguous from Qumran onward.
