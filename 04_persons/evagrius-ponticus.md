# Evagrius Ponticus

## Identity
Cappadocian. Born in Ibora in Pontus around 345 into a family connected to the Cappadocian Fathers (Basil and Gregory of Nyssa). Ordained reader by Basil, deacon by Gregory of Nazianzus. Brilliant career as the leading deacon and intellectual advocate in Constantinople (~380–82), serving Gregory of Nazianzus and then Nectarius. Fled the imperial capital around 382 — according to his disciple Palladius (*Lausiac History*), to escape a love affair with a senator's wife — and went first to Jerusalem (where Melania the Elder and Rufinus received him) and then to the Egyptian desert. Settled at Nitria, then in the inner desert at Kellia. Disciple of the Macarii (Macarius the Great and Macarius of Alexandria). Died at Kellia in 399. The intellectual systematizer of Egyptian desert spirituality.

## Historical evidence
His Greek corpus survives partly intact, partly only through Syriac translations (preserved by the Syrian Orthodox who did not enforce 553). Palladius's *Lausiac History* is a primary biographical source. Socrates Scholasticus and Sozomen mention him. After his posthumous condemnation in 553, his Greek manuscripts were systematically purged; survivals owe much to anonymous attribution (Nilus of Sinai) and to the Syriac translation tradition.

## Works
- ***Praktikos*** — the introductory work: the eight *logismoi* (thoughts / temptations) and how to fight them.
- ***Gnostikos*** — for those advanced past *praktike*.
- ***Kephalaia Gnostika*** (*Gnostic Chapters*) — six centuries of densely metaphysical chapters; the most controversial of his works. Preserved in Syriac in two versions: S1 (purged) and S2 (preserving the Origenist material on the *protoctisi*, pre-existence of souls, and *apokatastasis*). Antoine Guillaumont's 1958 publication of S2 (PO 28) was a major scholarly event.
- ***On Prayer*** (153 chapters) — transmitted under Nilus's name in the East, becoming a Philokalic classic; only in modern times restored to Evagrius.
- ***Antirrhetikos*** ("Talking Back") — counter-arguments against each of the eight *logismoi*, with scripture passages for each.
- Various letters and shorter works.

## Role in the tradition
The principal **theoretical systematizer** of Egyptian monastic practice. His eight-*logismoi* schema (gluttony, *porneia*, avarice, sadness, anger, *acedia*, vainglory, pride) is the source — via Cassian and Gregory the Great — of the medieval Seven Deadly Sins. His distinction *praktike* / *theoria* / *theologia* structures Greek-patristic-and-medieval-monastic spirituality. His *On Prayer* is foundational for Eastern hesychasm; Maximus the Confessor and Symeon the New Theologian build on him. Posthumously condemned at Constantinople II (553) for Origenism — but his ascetic-psychological work survives, often under attributed names, and is core to the Eastern *Philokalia*.

## Connections — substrate and influence
- **Evagrius is the most concentrated Origenist of the 4th c.** — *Kephalaia Gnostika* (S2) preserves the pre-existence of souls, the original henad of intellects, the fall into bodies, and the *apokatastasis* (universal restoration) in their most systematic form. Origen → Evagrius → (suppressed) is the direct line of Christian Platonist cosmology condemned in 553. Konstantinovsky 2009.
- Behind Origen stands Middle Platonism (Numenius, Philo) and Plotinus; the Evagrian psychology of *nous* / *psyche* / *body* is structurally Plotinian. The Christian-Platonist substrate is direct.
- His meditation technique (*nepsis*, watchfulness; *hesychia*, stillness; pure prayer beyond images and concepts) anticipates and feeds into the later Hesychast tradition. Stewart 1998, Bunge 1988.
- The eight-vices framework systematizes earlier Stoic catalogues of *pathē* (passions) and the Greek philosophical-medical psychology of the soul's disorders.

## Disputes
- **Authorship of certain works in the corpus.** Some have been over-attributed; some (including *On Prayer*) have been recovered for Evagrius from misattribution.
- **The 553 condemnations.** Whether the 15 anti-Origenist anathemas are properly conciliar (Daley 1991 argues they were a pre-conciliar synodal action by Justinian, not part of the council proper; Ramelli, *The Christian Doctrine of Apokatastasis*, 2013, develops this). Surface this carefully: the historical effect on Greek Origenist literature is what matters; the canonical status is contested.
- **Evagrius's actual theological position.** Casiday 2006 attempts a rehabilitation arguing he is more orthodox than the *Kephalaia* (S2) make him appear. Konstantinovsky 2009 holds the standard "yes he really was a radical Origenist" line.
