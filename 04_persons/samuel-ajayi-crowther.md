# Samuel Ajayi Crowther

## Identity
First African Anglican bishop; pioneering Yoruba-language linguist and Bible translator. Crowther was born ~1809 in the Yoruba town of Osogun; captured by slave traders in 1821; rescued from a Portuguese slave-ship by the British anti-slavery patrol; resettled in Freetown, Sierra Leone; educated at the Church Mission Society's school; baptized 1825; trained as a teacher and missionary; participated in the British Niger expeditions; was consecrated Bishop of the Niger in 1864 — the first African Anglican bishop. He completed the Yoruba Bible translation in 1884.

## Historical evidence
Extensively documented: Crowther's own journals (published); CMS records; British missionary and government correspondence; standard biographies (Page 1908; Walls 2002).

## Works
- *A Vocabulary of the Yoruba Language* (1843) — foundational Yoruba lexicography.
- *A Grammar of the Yoruba Language* (1852) — foundational Yoruba grammar.
- *Bible in Yoruba* (completed 1884) — the foundational translation that standardized Yoruba orthography and is still in use.
- Journals of the Niger expeditions; pastoral letters.

## Role in the tradition
Crowther's significance for Phase 8 is double:
1. *Yoruba-language linguistic standardization.* His vocabulary, grammar, and Bible translation effectively created written Yoruba; subsequent textualization of Yoruba religious tradition (including 20th-c. Ifá corpus textualization by Abimbola and others) depends on his orthographic foundation.
2. *Èṣù translation choice.* Crowther translated Greek *Satan / diabolos* with Yoruba *Èṣù*. This single translation decision — adopted by the Yoruba Bible's enormous reach — has shaped Christian-Yoruba religious vocabulary for 140 years and is a principal cause of the persistent (and theologically incorrect) identification of the orisha Èṣù with Satan. The choice was theologically motivated (Crowther understood the trickster-orisha as morally compromised), but the consequence has been a category error propagated for generations. See [[eshu]] discussion.

## Disputes
- **Èṣù-Satan translation.** Widely criticized by 20th-c. Yoruba scholars (Idowu, Abimbola, Pemberton): Èṣù is a morally complex orisha, not evil; the Satan-equation distorts Yoruba religion. Crowther's defenders argue he was translating into a Christian framework where Èṣù's morally ambivalent / disruptive character had no other Yoruba equivalent.
