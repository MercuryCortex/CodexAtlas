# Manuel I of Portugal (r. 1495–1521)

King of Portugal during the apex of the Portuguese maritime expansion. Took the throne 25 October 1495 after the death of his cousin João II; reigned for 26 years until his death 13 December 1521. His reign concentrates the Portuguese golden age: the [[event-vasco-da-gama-india-voyage-1497|Vasco da Gama India voyage]] (1497-1499), Cabral's discovery of Brazil (1500), the Casa da Índia, and the establishment of the Estado da Índia. The Manueline architectural style — distinctive maritime-Gothic ornamentation with rope, coral, armillary sphere, and Order-of-Christ-cross iconography — is named for him.

## Order of Christ Grand Master

Acquired the administratorship of the [[order-of-christ]] before his accession (as Duke of Beja) and held it as king. The Order's resources finance the maritime expansion under his reign; Portuguese ships of exploration fly the distinctive Order of Christ red cross. The Manueline architectural program at the Convento de Cristo at Tomar (the Manueline window, c. 1510) is the architectural-iconographic apex of the Order's institutional continuity.

## The 1496 forced conversion

The [[event-portuguese-forced-conversion-1496|1496 edict]] forcibly converted or expelled the Portuguese Jewish community (~70,000-100,000 persons) — a notable theological-political event in Sephardic Jewish history, undertaken partly to satisfy the dowry-condition of his marriage to Isabella of Aragon (daughter of Ferdinand and Isabella, who had expelled Iberian Jews in 1492). The forced conversion produced the *cristãos-novos* (New Christians) community whose Inquisition-era persecution is documented in Roth 1932 + Saraiva 1956. The Sephardic intellectual diaspora of the 16th c. partly stems from this event.

## Cross-tradition position

Manuel I sits at the intersection of three cross-tradition threads:

1. **Templar institutional survival** — as Order of Christ Grand Master, he is the late-15th/early-16th-c. articulation of the Portuguese Templar continuity
2. **Maritime empire as religious project** — the Estado da Índia + the *padroado real* (royal patronage of Catholic missions in Asia) make Portuguese imperial expansion a theologically-framed enterprise
3. **Forced-conversion theology** — the 1496 edict places him in the parallel cluster of Iberian forced-conversion politics alongside Ferdinand-Isabella's 1492 expulsion
