# Robert M. Schoch

## The water erosion hypothesis

**Robert M. Schoch** (b. 1959) is an associate professor of natural science at Boston University who in 1990–1992 analyzed the **[[sphinx-of-giza|Great Sphinx]]** enclosure walls and found weathering patterns he identified as characteristic of **precipitation and water runoff** — not wind and sand abrasion.

**The inference:** The Giza Plateau last received sufficient rainfall to produce this erosion profile during the African Humid Period (~11,000–5,000 BCE). The conventional Sphinx attribution to Khafre (~2558 BCE) falls after this humid period, suggesting the Sphinx body is older than the 4th Dynasty.

**What Schoch does NOT claim:** That the Sphinx was built by Atlanteans, that there are hidden chambers, or that the pyramids are older than conventionally dated. Those are additional claims made by others (West, Hancock) who adopted his geology within a broader alternative-history framework.

## Reception

Schoch presented at the **1992 Geological Society of America** annual meeting — a peer-reviewed venue. Fellow geologists acknowledged the erosion analysis was technically sound. Egyptologists challenged the climatic premise, proposing groundwater seepage and differential limestone strata as alternative explanations.

The enclosure weathering remains unexplained to every party's full satisfaction. Schoch's work is significant because it is the most technically credentialed geological challenge to conventional Sphinx chronology — made by a credentialed scientist at a professional venue, not by an amateur.

It illustrates the disciplinary boundary tension in Giza studies: geologists and Egyptologists use different evidentiary standards and reach different conclusions from the same evidence.
