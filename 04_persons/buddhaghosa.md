# Buddhaghosa

## Identity
Buddhaghosa ("Voice of the Buddha") was a 5th-c. CE Indian Buddhist scholar who traveled to Sri Lanka, took up residence at the Mahāvihāra monastery in Anurādhapura, and produced the systematic synthesis of Theravāda Buddhism that has remained authoritative for ~1500 years. His central work, the *Visuddhimagga* (*Path of Purification*), is the most influential single text of Theravāda Buddhism after the canon itself — a 23-chapter integrated synthesis of doctrine (Abhidhamma), meditation practice (*samatha* + *vipassanā*), and ethical training (*sīla* + *samādhi* + *paññā*) organized around the three trainings. Buddhaghosa is also the author of (or supervisor for) the *Aṭṭhakathā* commentaries on most of the Pali Tipiṭaka, the canonical commentaries that translated and supplemented older Sinhalese-language commentaries into Pali for international Theravāda use. His project effectively *re-canonized* the Pali tradition by giving it a standard commentarial apparatus accessible to non-Sinhalese Theravāda communities.

## Historical evidence
- **Mahāvaṃsa chapter 37** preserves a Pali biographical account: Buddhaghosa as an Indian brahmin convert, sent to Sri Lanka to translate the Sinhalese commentaries into Pali, who composed the *Visuddhimagga* as a test-piece to demonstrate his competence (the *thera*s of the Mahāvihāra are reported to have been so impressed they entrusted him with the full commentarial translation program).
- **His corpus**: ~16-20 attributed works survive, most plausibly his. The internal consistency of style, vocabulary, doctrinal positions, and cross-references across this corpus is the strongest evidence of single-authorial coordination (whether Buddhaghosa himself composed every line or supervised a Mahāvihāra editorial program is debated).
- **No extra-Theravāda corroboration**: there is no surviving Indian, Chinese, or Tibetan reference to Buddhaghosa from his own century; the Sri Lankan tradition is the only source. Modern scholarship accepts the broad historical core while treating the Mahāvaṃsa biographical details as partly hagiographic.

## Works
- **[[phase-4-076-visuddhimagga]]** — the *Path of Purification*, his magnum opus. The most systematic single Theravāda doctrinal-meditative manual.
- **Aṭṭhakathā** commentaries on:
  - Vinaya-piṭaka: *Samantapāsādikā*
  - Dīgha Nikāya: *Sumaṅgalavilāsinī*
  - Majjhima Nikāya: *Papañcasūdanī*
  - Saṃyutta Nikāya: *Sāratthappakāsinī*
  - Aṅguttara Nikāya: *Manorathapūraṇī*
  - Abhidhamma-piṭaka: *Atthasālinī* (on Dhammasaṅgaṇī); *Sammohavinodanī* (on Vibhaṅga); *Pañcappakaraṇaṭṭhakathā* (on the remaining five Abhidhamma books).
- Of these, the *Visuddhimagga* and the *Atthasālinī* are most securely his; the Sutta-piṭaka *Aṭṭhakathā*s may have been produced under his supervision rather than entirely composed by him.

## Role in the tradition
- **In Theravāda**: Buddhaghosa is the **second canonical authority** after the Buddha himself. The *Visuddhimagga* is the standard meditation manual taught across Sri Lanka, Burma, Thailand, Cambodia, and Laos to this day. His Aṭṭhakathā commentaries are the authoritative interpretive framework — to "study a sutta" in Theravāda traditionally means to study it together with Buddhaghosa's commentary.
- **In modern global vipassanā**: the 20th-c. Burmese vipassanā revival (Ledi Sayadaw, Mahāsi Sayadaw, U Ba Khin → S.N. Goenka) and the modern Insight Meditation Society (Goldstein, Salzberg, Kornfield) draw their meditation technique substantially from the *Visuddhimagga*'s chapters on satipaṭṭhāna, ānāpānasati, and the progressive stages of insight (*vipassanā-ñāṇa*). The *Visuddhimagga* is the principal classical Pali source behind contemporary MBSR and "secular mindfulness."
- **In Mahāyāna**: Buddhaghosa is treated as a Theravāda figure outside Mahāyāna canonical concern; the Theravāda commentarial apparatus is not used in Mahāyāna doctrinal training.

## Disputes
- **Birthplace**: the Mahāvaṃsa says India near Bodh Gaya; Burmese tradition claims him as a Mon-area Indian; modern scholarship is agnostic.
- **Composite vs. single-author corpus**: was Buddhaghosa a single scholar who composed all attributed works, or the figurehead of a Mahāvihāra editorial program? Most modern scholarship treats the *Visuddhimagga* + *Atthasālinī* + *Sammohavinodanī* as securely his; the Sutta-piṭaka commentaries as plausibly his with editorial assistance; the late-Abhidhamma commentaries (*Mūlaṭīkā* etc.) as possibly later.
- **Innovation vs. transmission**: Buddhaghosa's prefaces claim he is merely *translating* older Sinhalese commentaries (the *Sīhalaṭṭhakathā*) into Pali. Modern scholarship reads this as partly conventional modesty: he is also *systematizing* in distinctively new ways (the three-fold structure of the *Visuddhimagga*; the integrated cetasika analysis; the specific ordering of vipassanā-ñāṇas). The original Sinhalese commentaries are lost, so the precise mix of transmission vs. innovation is unrecoverable.
- **Relation to Buddhadatta and Dhammapāla**: contemporary or near-contemporary commentators whose work parallels his. The three are sometimes treated as a single "Buddhaghosa school."
