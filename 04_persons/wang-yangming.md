# Wang Yangming (王陽明)

Ming dynasty Neo-Confucian philosopher and military commander (1472–1529). Rejecting Zhu Xi's program of outward investigation of things (*gewu*) — reportedly spending seven days and nights meditating before bamboo plants to discern their *li* without result — Wang Yangming turned inward, proposing two radical doctrines:

**Liangzhi** (良知, "innate moral knowledge"): every person already possesses complete moral knowledge — the sage is not the one who has exhaustively studied external things, but the one who has cleared away obstacles to their innate knowing. This is innate Buddha-nature (*tathāgatagarbha*) re-stated in Confucian terms — structural identity so close that Wang Yangming was repeatedly accused of crypto-Buddhism.

**Zhixing heyi** (知行合一, "unity of knowledge and action"): genuine knowledge and genuine action are inseparable. To truly "know" that something is good and yet not do it proves the knowledge was not genuine. This radical epistemology has modern echoes in James's pragmatism and Dewey's instrumentalism.

Wang Yangming is also notable as a successful military general — one of the few Chinese philosophers who combined systematic philosophical innovation with battlefield command.
