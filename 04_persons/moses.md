# Moses

## Identity
The foundational prophet-lawgiver of biblical Israel, traditionally credited with the Torah's revelation at Sinai, the leading of the Exodus from Egypt, and the institution of Israelite cultic and judicial law. The biblical narrative spans Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, and Deuteronomy. In Christian tradition Moses is the prototype of Christ ([[new-moses-typology]]) and the giver of the Old Covenant superseded by the New. In Islam, Mūsā is one of the great pre-Islamic prophets and the most-mentioned figure in the Qur'an after only Allah.

## Historical evidence
There is no extra-biblical attestation of a historical Moses. Modern critical scholarship is divided: maximalists (Albright school, mid-20th-c.) defended a 13th-c. BCE Moses behind a redacted tradition; minimalists (Thompson, Davies) treat the figure as legendary-foundational with little or no historical kernel. The mainstream view since the late 20th century: *Mosaic* tradition is unrecoverable beyond the texts; the figure operates as Israel's foundational cultural memory regardless of historical reduction. Assmann's *Moses the Egyptian* (1997) explores the cultural-memory question through Egyptological-comparative reception.

## Works
Traditionally, the Pentateuch (Torah). Source-critical scholarship (Wellhausen, refined by Friedman and others) identifies J, E, D, P strata spanning the 10th–5th c. BCE; Moses' historical authorship is universally denied in critical scholarship.

## Role in the tradition
- Judaism: *Moshe Rabbenu* — "our teacher Moses" — the unsurpassed prophet (Deut 34:10).
- Christianity: prefiguration of Christ; the law-giver whose law is fulfilled and transformed.
- Islam: a major prophet who confronted Pharaoh, received tablets, and prepared the way for later prophets.
- Hermetic and Renaissance traditions: Moses as Egyptian-trained sage; *prisca theologia*; cf. Ficino, Pico.

## Disputes
- Historicity.
- Date (the "early date" 15th c. and "late date" 13th c. exodus theories).
- Egyptian-Mosaic religious continuities (Assmann; Freud).
