# Hongren

## Identity
Hongren (601–674 CE), the Fifth Patriarch of Chan Buddhism, successor to Daoxin and the master under whom the Chan school first achieved significant institutional scale. His monastery on East Mountain (Dongshan) in Huangmei, Hubei, attracted hundreds of monks and became the generative center of mature Chan. He transmitted the patriarchship — the *robe and bowl* symbolizing dharma-succession — in the famous secret midnight ceremony to the kitchen worker [[huineng]] over the objections of his head monk [[shenxiu]], as narrated in the [[phase-5-004-platform-sutra-huineng]].

Hongren's role is the critical pivot: under him, Chan became a distinct, institutionally recognizable Chinese Buddhist school. After his death, the school split into the Southern School (sudden awakening, [[huineng]]) and the Northern School (gradual cultivation, [[shenxiu]]). The Southern School prevailed.
