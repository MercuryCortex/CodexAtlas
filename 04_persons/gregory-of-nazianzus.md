# Gregory of Nazianzus

Cappadocian Father, briefly bishop of Constantinople (380–381) during the Council of Constantinople. The "Theologian" — a title reserved in Eastern Christianity for only three figures (John the Evangelist, Symeon the New Theologian, Gregory). His *Five Theological Orations* (delivered in Constantinople 380) gave the classical Greek-patristic statement of the Trinity that the Council of Constantinople ratified.

## Substrate connections
- His Trinitarian vocabulary deploys Aristotelian and Stoic substance-metaphysical terms (*ousia*, *hypostasis*, *physis*, *prosopon*) Christianized for the Trinitarian distinctions — the Cappadocian theology that 381 ratified is unintelligible without the Greek philosophical substrate.
- His foundational anti-Apollinarian principle — "what is not assumed is not healed" (*Letter to Cledonius* 101) — depends on a Greek-philosophical anthropology of soul-and-body that derives ultimately from Plato and Aristotle (the rational soul as the essential human).
- Trained in pagan rhetorical schools at Athens (with [[basil-of-caesarea]], and a young Julian who would later become "the Apostate"); his classical-rhetorical formation is the literary substrate of his Christian eloquence.
