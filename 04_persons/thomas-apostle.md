# Thomas the Apostle

## Identity
Thomas (Aramaic *Tāʾomā* "twin," Greek *Didymos* "twin" — both mean "twin," a redundancy suggesting the original personal name has been lost). In the Syriac tradition his full name is given as **Judas Thomas** or **Judas Didymus Thomas** — strongly suggesting his given name was Judas (Yehuda) and "Thomas" was an epithet. The Syriac and Thomasine literature (notably the *Acts of Thomas* and the *Book of Thomas the Contender*) explicitly identifies him as the **twin brother of Jesus** — a claim the Synoptic tradition obscures by translating the epithet.

## Historical evidence
A name on all four apostle-lists; substantial Johannine dramatic development (chs 11, 14, 20). The post-resurrection "doubting Thomas" scene (Jn 20:24–29) is the source of his epithet. The **Edessa tradition** that he sent his disciple Addai (Thaddaeus) to convert King Abgar V is preserved in Eusebius *HE* 1.13 and the Syriac *Doctrine of Addai* (~+400). The **Mylapore tradition** — that Thomas himself sailed to India, evangelized the Malabar coast, and was martyred near modern Chennai — is preserved in the Syriac *Acts of Thomas* (~+200) and remains the foundational story of the **Thomas Christians (Mar Thoma / Syro-Malabar)** of Kerala. Whether the latter reflects historical memory or hagiographic projection is contested; Roman-era trade between South India and the Mediterranean is well-documented, so the *possibility* is not implausible.

## Works
None genuine. The pseudepigraphic *Gospel of Thomas* (NHC II,2, Coptic translation of a Greek original ~+50/+140) is a sayings collection that bears his name programmatically — his role as Jesus's "twin" makes him the privileged hearer of esoteric teaching. The *Acts of Thomas* (Syriac, ~+200) is the major narrative; it includes the **Hymn of the Pearl**, one of the most beautiful texts in the Gnostic/Syriac corpus, allegorizing the soul's descent into matter and reascent.

## Role in the tradition
**Syriac Christianity**: the founding apostolic figure. The dyophysite/East-Syriac tradition (the Church of the East) traces its apostolic lineage through Thomas.

**Gnostic/Thomasine tradition**: the privileged disciple of the *hidden* sayings — the *Gospel of Thomas* presents him as Jesus's intimate; the very name Didymos (twin) suggests an esoteric identity with Christ.

**Thomas Christians of Kerala**: continuous community claiming origin from the Mylapore mission, present in India before the arrival of the Portuguese.

**Catholic and Protestant traditions**: the *doubting* Thomas is the iconic moralized figure — touchstone for discussions of faith and doubt.

## Disputes
- **Twin of Jesus?** The Syriac/Thomasine tradition reads the name literally; the canonical/Western tradition treats it as merely an epithet. This is the single most theologically loaded interpretive crux.
- **India mission.** Continuous communal memory in Kerala vs. critical-historical reservation. Most Indologists and church historians treat the Acts of Thomas as legend with possible kernel.
- **Authorship of the Gospel of Thomas.** Pseudonymous; the historical apostle has no plausible connection to the Coptic-preserved sayings collection (though the collection may preserve early independent Jesus-tradition).
