# Huldrych Zwingli

## Identity
Swiss Reformed pastor and theologian, the principal reformer of Zurich and the originator of the distinct Swiss-Reformed Protestant tradition (1484–1531). Born at Wildhaus in the Toggenburg; humanist education at Vienna and Basel; ordained Catholic priest 1506; military chaplain in the Italian wars; elected people's-priest of the Grossmünster in Zurich 1518. From 1519 onward Zwingli's preaching from the original Greek NT drove a programmatic reformation of Zurich's liturgy, ecclesiology, and civic religion — independently of Luther but in the same broad evangelical direction. Zwinglian theology developed a symbolic interpretation of the Eucharist (the bread and wine *signify* Christ rather than being his real presence) that became the principal Reformed Eucharistic position and the divisive issue at the 1529 Marburg Colloquy with Luther, which failed to produce reconciliation and split magisterial Protestantism into Lutheran and Reformed branches. Killed in battle at Kappel 1531 fighting for Protestant Zurich against the Catholic cantons; his successor Heinrich Bullinger consolidated the Zurich Reformed church.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own substantial works (Schuler & Schulthess edition 1828–1842; modern Corpus Reformatorum edition), Zurich civic records, contemporary correspondence.

## Works
*67 Articles* (1523). *Auslegen und Gründe der Schlussreden* (1523). *De Vera et Falsa Religione* (1525). *Fidei Ratio* (1530). *Christianae Fidei Brevis et Clara Expositio* (posthumous 1536).

## Role in the tradition
Zwingli founded the distinct Reformed branch of Protestantism (subsequently consolidated by Bullinger and theologically systematized by Calvin); the Zurich Reformed tradition was the third major path of magisterial Protestantism alongside Lutheran and (later) Anglican. The Lutheran-Reformed split on the Eucharist that emerged at Marburg in 1529 remains a permanent dividing line in Protestantism.
