# Mechthild of Magdeburg

## Identity
13th-c. Saxon Beguine mystic, author of ***Das fliessende Licht der Gottheit*** (*The Flowing Light of the Godhead*), the first major mystical-theological work in vernacular Middle Low German. Born c. 1207 to a noble family near Magdeburg. Joined the Beguine community in Magdeburg ~1230. Composed her book in stages from ~1250 onward at the encouragement of her Dominican confessor Heinrich of Halle. **Relocated to the Cistercian convent at Helfta** ~1270 (when Beguine institutional status was increasingly threatened) — Helfta under Abbess Gertrude of Hackeborn was the most intellectually-active female monastic community of 13th-c. Latin Europe, also home to **Gertrude the Great** and **Mechthild of Hackeborn** (the "Helfta trio").

## Works
- ***Das fliessende Licht der Gottheit*** — 7 books; lyric-prose mixture; bridal-erotic register intensifying Bernardine tradition. The *Flowing Light* uses the term **Gottheit** (Godhead) for the divine essence-beyond-distinction — a Middle Low German vocabulary item that becomes definitive in Eckhart and the Rhineland mystics a generation later.

## Role in the tradition
- **First major vernacular German mystical author.** Predates Eckhart by ~50 years.
- **Beguine vernacular mysticism.** Part of the broader 13th-c. Beguine wave ([[hadewijch-of-antwerp|Hadewijch]], [[marguerite-porete|Marguerite Porete]]).
- **Helfta nexus.** With Gertrude the Great and Mechthild of Hackeborn, one of the central figures of the Helfta community's mystical-theological production.
- **Direct lineage to Eckhart.** McGinn 1998 traces the *Gottheit*-vocabulary lineage Beguine → Mechthild → Eckhart with substantial confidence.
