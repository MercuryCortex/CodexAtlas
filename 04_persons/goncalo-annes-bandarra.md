# Gonçalo Annes Bandarra

## Identity

**Gonçalo Annes Bandarra** (c. **1500 – c. 1556**) was a **shoemaker (*sapateiro*) from Trancoso**, in the Beira region of north-central Portugal. The biographical record is thin: his profession is securely attested (the *Trovas* themselves and the 1541 Inquisition trial record both refer to him as a cobbler); his birth and death dates are approximate; his level of literacy and his sources of religious learning are debated. He is the **founding figure of [[tradition-sebastianism|Sebastianismo]]** — author of the *Trovas* (c. 1530s), a corpus of vernacular Portuguese prophetic verse drawing on Iberian Jewish-converso messianism, Iberian Christian apocalyptic, the Daniel-2 four-kingdoms schema, and (per modern scholarship: van den Besselaar 1976, Valdez 2010, Cohen 1998) the Calabrian abbot **Joachim of Fiore**'s tripartite-age schema.

He composed the *Trovas* between approximately 1530 and the early 1540s; was **tried by the Portuguese Inquisition in 1541** at Lisbon for *judaizante* (Judaizing) tendencies — the charge that his prophecies expressed Jewish-converso messianic content under Christian-allegorical cover. The trial outcome was relatively mild — he was sentenced to abjuration *de levi* (the lighter form) and to public penance, but **not** to imprisonment, exile, or burning. He returned to Trancoso and continued his cobbler's trade, dying probably c. 1556.

## The *Trovas*

See [[phase-6-040-bandarra-trovas]] for the document-level treatment. Briefly: the *Trovas* are vernacular Portuguese prophetic verse, typically in *redondilha* (octosyllabic) metres, organized around the recurrent figure of **"O Encoberto"** (The Hidden / Shrouded One) — a coming king who will restore Portugal's greatness, expel its enemies, and inaugurate a universal-Christian-messianic kingdom. The poetic vocabulary is heavily allegorical-typological, drawing on:
- **Daniel 2's four-kingdoms schema** (Babylon → Persia → Greece → Rome) extended to a fifth-and-final kingdom of universal-Christian-Portuguese sovereignty;
- **Joachimist tripartite-age** schema (Father / Son / Spirit);
- **Iberian Jewish-converso messianic** vocabulary (the *Mashiach ben David* expectation, refracted through Iberian-Catholic typology);
- **Portuguese imperial-eschatological** material (the Portuguese as a chosen-people-of-Iberian-Catholic-Reconquista, with global maritime-Christianizing mission).

The *Trovas* were composed *before* the birth of [[sebastian-i-portugal|Sebastian I]] (1554) but were **retrospectively reinterpreted after Sebastian's disappearance at [[event-battle-of-alcacer-quibir-1578|Alcácer Quibir]] in 1578** as predicting Sebastian himself — his reign, his disappearance, and his return. The match between Bandarra's *Encoberto* figure and the post-1578 messianic-expectation-of-Sebastian's-return is therefore *retrospective interpretive imposition*, not predictive correspondence — a fact that does not affect the tradition's purchase but does affect any honest historical reading of it.

## Inquisition trial (1541)

The Lisbon Inquisition tried Bandarra in 1541 for *judaizante* tendencies. The trial record (preserved in the Arquivo Nacional da Torre do Tombo, Lisbon) is one of the principal Portuguese-Inquisition early-period documents and provides the strongest contemporary attestation of Bandarra's identity, profession, and the *Trovas* circulation. The charges focused on:

- The vernacular prophetic-poetic mode (suspect because lay-controlled religious teaching evaded clerical supervision);
- The messianic-figure of the *Encoberto* (suspect because it could be read as the Jewish *Mashiach ben David* expectation, particularly given the substantial Portuguese-Jewish-converso audience for the *Trovas*);
- Possible converso-Jewish content in the *Trovas*' typology — a charge Bandarra denied;
- The 16th-c. dispute about *vernacular Bible reading* (which Bandarra is recorded as having practiced — he read Portuguese-language Bibles, suspect at this period).

The sentence — abjuration *de levi*, public penance, no imprisonment — was notable for its leniency. The historiographical interpretation (Cohen 1998, Valdez 2010): the Inquisitors recognized that Bandarra was personally a sincere Christian who happened to share a hermeneutic-typological vocabulary with the Cristão-Novo converso communities; punishing him heavily was politically counter-productive.

## Posthumous reception (1556–present)

1. **Post-mortem circulation (1556–1581).** The *Trovas* circulate in manuscript copies throughout Portugal and the Portuguese-Atlantic-imperial space.
2. **Index of Prohibited Books (1581).** Following the 1578 Alcácer Quibir catastrophe and the consequent 1580 Iberian Union, the Spanish-Habsburg-Portuguese Inquisition adds Bandarra's *Trovas* to the Index. They continue to circulate clandestinely in manuscript.
3. **First printed edition: Paris 1603** by **Dom João de Castro** (the émigré Portuguese pretender-claimant from the Coimbra-Castro family, not the earlier viceroy of India). João de Castro's Paris edition is the first printed *Trovas* text and the foundation of the modern textual tradition.
4. **First-wave Sebastianism (1578–1640):** four false Sebastians invoke the *Trovas* as predictive evidence (Hermit of Penamacor 1584; King of Ericeira 1585; Pastry-Maker of Madrigal 1594; Marco Túlio Catizone 1598–1603). The *Trovas* become the principal predictive text of the Sebastianist tradition.
5. **1640 Restoration:** the *Trovas* are reinterpreted to predict João IV's accession. Some Sebastianists transfer the *Encoberto* expectation onto the new Bragança king.
6. **Vieira's Fifth-Empire systematization (1640s–1690s):** Jesuit António Vieira's *História do Futuro* and *Clavis Prophetarum* explicitly use Bandarra as one of his three principal predictive sources (alongside Daniel and Isaiah). See [[tradition-sebastianism]] and [[fifth-empire-quinto-imperio]].
7. **18th–19th-c. cultural transmission:** the *Trovas* circulate in popular pamphlet editions, folk-prophetic readings, and rural-Sebastianist communities. Continue to be added to the Index in successive 17th-18th-c. revisions.
8. **Brazilian transplantation:** the Canudos messianic movement (Antônio Conselheiro, 1893–1897, in Bahia, Brazil) invokes Bandarra-Sebastianist material directly.
9. **20th-c. literary recovery:** Fernando Pessoa's *Mensagem* (1934) includes a specific poem titled **"Bandarra"** (in Section III, *O Encoberto*) — explicit Pessoan acknowledgment of Bandarra's foundational role. Pessoa's notes also engage Bandarra in his unpublished Sebastianist prose.
10. **20th-c. scholarly recovery:** Van den Besselaar 1976 (Brill) is the foundational modern philological monograph; Lima 1944 (Pimenta de Lima) is the standard critical edition; Cohen 1998 (Stanford UP) treats Bandarra's role in Vieira's project; Valdez 2010 (ICS Lisbon) is the principal contemporary Portuguese-academic synthesis; Gandra 1999 (*Joaquimismo e Esperança Sebástica*) develops the Bandarra-Joachimist linkage.

## Disputes

- **Was Bandarra a Cristão-Novo (New Christian / converso) himself?** Long-running historiographical question. The 1541 Inquisition charge of *judaizante* tendencies suggests at least the Inquisitors thought so. Van den Besselaar 1976 argues for converso origin or close converso milieu; Valdez 2010 is more cautious. The biographical record is insufficient to settle the question definitively, but the *Trovas*' hermeneutic vocabulary makes substantial converso-influence overwhelmingly likely whether or not Bandarra was himself a Cristão-Novo by descent.
- **How literate was Bandarra?** The *Trovas* are sophisticated, structured, scripturally and typologically informed work. Either Bandarra was substantially more learned than his cobbler's trade implies (educated in some monastic-school or family context), or the *Trovas* were composed in some collaborative-or-mediated context. Mainstream consensus: Bandarra was personally learned beyond his profession.
- **Did Bandarra "predict" Sebastian and Alcácer Quibir?** No — in the predictive-historical sense, no. The match is *retrospective interpretive imposition*. But the tradition does not depend on actual predictive correspondence; it depends on the symbolic-typological structure (the *Encoberto*, the fifth-and-final-empire, the cosmic-restoration) being available in vernacular Portuguese form when the 1578 trauma occurred.
