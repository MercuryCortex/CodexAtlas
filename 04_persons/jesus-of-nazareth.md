# Jesus of Nazareth

## Identity
Jesus of Nazareth (~-4 BCE to ~+30 CE) was a Galilean Jewish prophetic-charismatic figure whose teaching, execution by crucifixion under the Roman prefect Pontius Pilate, and reported post-mortem appearances to his followers generated the movement that became Christianity. Within four decades of his death, his followers produced the earliest extant writings (the Pauline epistles, ~+50/+58); within seven decades, the canonical Gospels; within three centuries, the imperially-sanctioned theology that would dominate Western religion.

## Historical evidence
Jesus's *historical existence* is supported by:
- **Pauline epistles** (~+50), Paul's claim to have met "James the Lord's brother" (Gal 1:19) — a personal contact with Jesus's biological brother by a man writing within 20 years of the crucifixion.
- **Josephus** (~+93): *Antiquities* 18.63–64 (Testimonium Flavianum — original Josephan core describing Jesus's career, with Christian interpolations added; the bracketing scholarly view is now near-consensus per Meier) and the undisputed *Antiquities* 20.200, where "James the brother of Jesus who was called Christ" is mentioned in passing.
- **Tacitus** (~+115): *Annals* 15.44 — Roman historian states "Christus" was executed under Pilate, used to explain Nero's scapegoating of Christians in +64.
- **Pliny the Younger** (~+112): *Epistles* 10.96 — Christians "sing a hymn to Christ as to a god."
- **Suetonius** (~+120): *Claudius* 25 — disturbances at Rome "at the instigation of Chrestus" (likely garbled reference to Christological disputes in Roman synagogues).
- **Multiple independent Christian streams** (Mark, Q, John, Pauline kerygma) — independent attestation in form-critical terms.

Mainstream historical-Jesus scholarship (Sanders, Meier, Ehrman, Dunn, Allison, Crossan) treats Jesus's existence as essentially certain — solidly within "likely-historical" or stronger. **Mythicist** positions (denying any historical Jesus — Doherty, Carrier, Price) are a vocal but small minority view, generally rejected by academic specialists across the theological spectrum (including secular scholars like Ehrman who reject most Christian theological claims).

The *content* of his life and teaching is reconstructed by criteria of authenticity (multiple attestation, dissimilarity, embarrassment, coherence, plausibility) and remains contested in detail.

## Works
None written. Sayings collections (Q) preserve material plausibly going back to him, with later editorial shaping.

## Role in the tradition
**Christianity**: the incarnate divine Logos and Son of God; resurrected Lord; the central object of worship.

**Judaism**: a Jewish teacher whose followers' subsequent claims about him are rejected; later rabbinic literature (b. Sanh. 43a, 107b) contains polemical traditions of disputed reliability.

**Islam**: ʿĪsā ibn Maryam is a major prophet, born of a virgin, not crucified (Q 4:157), to return at the end of time. The Qur'an honors Jesus while rejecting his divinity and the crucifixion.

**Gnostic Christianities**: a savior-revealer whose physical body and crucifixion are variously interpreted; for some Gnostics (Cerinthus, some Valentinians) the divine Christ-aeon descended on the human Jesus at his baptism and departed before crucifixion (a "separationist" Christology).

**Manichaeism**: Jesus is one of the prophets of Light; multiple "Jesus" figures in Manichaean cosmology.

**Mandaeans**: a false messiah who corrupted John the Baptist's teaching.

## Disputes
- **Historicity proper.** Mainstream scholarship: solid. Mythicists: dispute it. The mainstream is overwhelming.
- **Mainstream characterizations.** *Apocalyptic prophet* (Schweitzer, Sanders, Ehrman, Allison — currently the dominant position); *Cynic-sage / wisdom teacher* (Crossan, Mack — once influential, now waning); *charismatic Hasid* (Vermes); *Jewish reformer* (Sanders); *messianic claimant* (Wright). Most scholars converge on apocalyptic prophet of imminent Reign of God as the best-supported reconstruction.
- **Resurrection.** Theologians debate as theology; historians treat the *resurrection appearance experiences* of his followers as historically real (whatever their cause), distinguish these from the *empty tomb* (more debated), and the *bodily resurrection* (a theological claim beyond historical method).
- **Date of crucifixion.** +30 or +33 are the two main candidates (depending on chronological reconstruction of the gospels' Passover-vs.-Day-of-Preparation framing).
