# Elisha

## Identity
Successor of Elijah; Northern-Kingdom prophet (~849–800 BCE); the miracle-worker prophet of the Elisha cycle (2 Kgs 2 – 2 Kgs 13). Elijah's mantle falls on him after Elijah's ascent (2 Kgs 2:13), inaugurating a miracle-dense narrative cycle (Naaman's leprosy, raising the Shunammite's son, multiplied bread, floating axe) that probably preserves an originally independent oral-prophetic tradition before being knit into the Deuteronomistic History.

## Historical evidence
No direct extrabiblical attestation. The political backdrop — the late Omride dynasty, Jehu's coup (which Elisha foments, 2 Kgs 9), the Aramaean wars under Hazael (Tel Dan Stele) — is historically secure. The Elisha *figure* is probably historical, though the miracle-cycle is a developed prophetic-folkloric tradition with formal parallels to the Elijah cycle (raising-from-death, multiplied food, water-control).

## Role in the tradition
- **In the New Testament.** Jesus's sermon at Nazareth (Luke 4:27) invokes Elisha's cleansing of Naaman the Syrian as the precedent for the gospel reaching Gentiles; the Lukan reframing of Elisha as the gospel-to-Gentiles prophet is striking.
- **Miracle-typology source.** The Elisha cycle's raising-the-Shunammite's-son narrative (2 Kgs 4) is one of the structural pretexts for the synoptic raising-of-the-widow-of-Nain (Luke 7:11–17) and the raising of Jairus's daughter.
- **Islamic tradition.** Al-Yasa' is named as a prophet in Quran 6:86 and 38:48.
