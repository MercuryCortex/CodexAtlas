# Joshua ben Hananiah

## Identity
One of the **five principal disciples of [[yohanan-ben-zakkai]]** (with Eliezer, Yose, Shimon, and Elazar), of Levitical descent, generally Hillelite (mitigating) in halakhic tendency — frequently in dispute with the more stringent [[eliezer-ben-hyrcanus]]. A leading figure of the second Tannaitic generation at Yavneh. Rabbinic tradition records numerous dialogues between him and **Hadrian** (the *Caesar* of the narratives) — typically witty exchanges with the emperor on philosophical-religious matters; the historicity of specific dialogues is contested, but the *pattern* (rabbinic engagement with Hadrian) likely reflects a real period of Roman-rabbinic communication before the Bar Kokhba revolt.

## Historical evidence
Rabbinic only. Multiply attested across Mishnah, Tosefta, and both Talmuds. The Hadrian-dialogue traditions are best treated as conventional literary form with historical kernel.

## Works
None. Sayings and halakhic rulings preserved in Tannaitic corpus.

## Role in the tradition
- **Hillelite counterweight to Eliezer**: the second-generation disputational structure of rabbinic halakhah.
- **Hadrianic-period diplomatic interface**: rabbinic narratives place him in repeated audience with Hadrian, possibly historical.
- **Pre-Bar-Kokhba era**: his rabbinic generation is the one that *precedes* and partly *opposes* the Bar Kokhba revolt; the rabbinic tradition records ambivalence about messianic-military activism that distinguishes his generation from Akiva's.

## Disputes
- **Historicity of the Hadrian dialogues**: stylized; historical core probable.
- **Halakhic positions vs. later school-assignments**: difficult to reconstruct from layered sources.
