# Henry Steel Olcott

## Identity
American Civil War colonel, journalist, and lawyer (1832–1907); co-founder (with H.P. Blavatsky and W.Q. Judge) of the Theosophical Society (1875); first President of the Society. Took *pansil* (the Five Precepts of refuge) with Sumangala Thera in Galle, Ceylon, May 1880 — among the first formally Buddhist Americans/Europeans. His *Buddhist Catechism* (1881) became a standard Theravada Buddhist teaching text in Sri Lanka and beyond.

## Role in the tradition
Olcott's organizational work institutionalized Theosophy. His Buddhist work in Ceylon contributed significantly to the late-19th-century Sinhalese Buddhist revival (Anagarika Dharmapala was Olcott's protégé) and to the broader "Buddhist modernism" / "Protestant Buddhism" of the 20th century.
