# Jacob Baradaeus

## Identity
Jacob Baradaeus (~500-578; the surname *Burdoʿono* means "the man in the ragged horse-blanket," from his beggar's disguise) was a Miaphysite Syriac monk consecrated bishop of Edessa in 542 by the exiled Patriarch Theodosius of Alexandria under the political cover of the Ghassanid Arab king al-Harith ibn Jabala. Operating underground for the next 36 years across Syria, Mesopotamia, Anatolia, Egypt, and the Aegean islands — disguised as a beggar to evade Justinian's anti-Miaphysite police — **he is reported to have consecrated ~30 bishops and ~100,000 priests, single-handedly preserving the Miaphysite hierarchical structure that became the [[tradition-syriac-orthodox|Syriac Orthodox Church]].** Without Jacob Baradaeus the Syriac Orthodox Church would have died out in the Justinianic persecution; with him it survived in an independent episcopal lineage that has continued unbroken to the present. The pejorative Chalcedonian label "Jacobite" for Syriac Miaphysites derives from his name.

## Historical evidence
The principal contemporary source is **John of Ephesus**'s *Lives of the Eastern Saints* (composed ~566-588), which devotes substantial chapters to Jacob and is sympathetic / hagiographic. Bar Hebraeus's *Chronicon Ecclesiasticum* (13th c.) preserves the Syriac Orthodox tradition. Procopius's hostile mention in the *Anecdota* / *Secret History* confirms Jacob's activity from the Chalcedonian-imperial side. The figures of "30 bishops + 100,000 priests" are John of Ephesus's report; the numbers are likely inflated but the underground-consecration program is uncontested.

## Works
None — he was a hierarchical organizer, not a theologian. His historical role is the institutional preservation of the Syriac Orthodox episcopate.

## Role in the tradition
- **Eponym of "Jacobite"**: the Chalcedonian-imperial pejorative for Syriac Orthodox Miaphysites was "the Jacobites" — i.e., the followers of Jacob Baradaeus's underground hierarchy. The Syriac Orthodox themselves resist the label (preferring "Syriac Orthodox" or "Suryāyē") but it became the standard external designation for ~1400 years.
- **Foundational hierarchical founder of [[tradition-syriac-orthodox|Syriac Orthodox Church]]**: every Syriac Orthodox bishop ordained today traces apostolic succession through Jacob Baradaeus's consecrations.
- **Saint in the Oriental Orthodox calendar** — commemorated 30 July (Syriac Orthodox).
