# Athanasius Kircher

## Identity
German Jesuit polymath of the Roman Counter-Reformation; based at the Collegio Romano from 1635 until his death; author of ~40 published Latin works on subjects ranging from Egyptian hieroglyphs and Coptic, to music, magnetism, vulcanology, Chinese religion, biblical chronology, and the Tower of Babel (1602–1680). Kircher's Egyptological work — *Prodromus Coptus*, *Lingua Aegyptiaca Restituta*, *Obeliscus Pamphilius*, *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654) — established Coptic as the modern descendant of ancient Egyptian (a correct identification used by Champollion 1822), but proposed a symbolic-philosophical decipherment of hieroglyphs that was demolished by Champollion. Kircher's Hermetic-Kabbalist universal-history project preserved the Renaissance prisca-theologia framework even after Casaubon's 1614 redating of the Hermetica. His Roman museum (the Museum Kircherianum at the Collegio Romano) was one of the great cabinets of curiosities of the 17th century.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own enormous published corpus, the Jesuit archives, the surviving collections of the Museum Kircherianum (dispersed; substantial holdings in the Museo Nazionale Romano), extensive correspondence.

## Works
Egyptological: *Prodromus Coptus* (1636), *Lingua Aegyptiaca Restituta* (1643), *Obeliscus Pamphilius* (1650), *Oedipus Aegyptiacus* (1652–1654), *Obelisci Aegyptiaci Nuper Erecti Interpretatio* (1666). Other major works: *Ars Magnesia* (1631), *Musurgia Universalis* (1650), *Mundus Subterraneus* (1665), *China Illustrata* (1667), *Turris Babel* (1679), *Arca Noe* (1675).

## Role in the tradition
Kircher is the last great Renaissance universal-history-and-prisca-theologia synthesist; his Counter-Reformation Catholic version of the project parallels Fludd's Protestant English version. The post-Champollion dismissal of his Egyptological program has been moderated by recent scholarship (Findlen, Stolzenberg) which restores Kircher's seriousness as a 17th-century philologist.
