# Praxeas

## Identity
A Christian teacher attacked in Tertullian's ***Adversus Praxean*** (~+213) for two simultaneous offenses: (a) **Modalist Monarchian Christology** — the position that "Father, Son, and Holy Spirit" are merely modes or names of one undifferentiated God, with the consequence that *the Father suffered on the cross* (Patripassianism); (b) **opposition to Montanism** — persuading the bishop of Rome (probably Zephyrinus or his predecessor Victor) to withdraw recognition of the New Prophecy movement to which Tertullian had converted. Tertullian's famous quip: "He did two works of the devil at Rome: he drove out prophecy and brought in heresy; he put the Paraclete to flight and crucified the Father."

## Historical evidence
**Tertullian alone**. No other early source mentions a "Praxeas." Some scholars have proposed that "Praxeas" is a Tertullian-coined nickname (Greek *praxeas* ~ "busybody" or "active") for someone known by another name — possibly **Callistus** of Rome, or possibly **Epigonus** or **Sabellius** (see [[sabellius]] and [[noetus-of-smyrna]]). The fact that Hippolytus's contemporary Roman anti-modalist polemic targets Noetus and Callistus, not anyone named Praxeas, strengthens the nickname hypothesis.

## Works
None survives independently. Tertullian quotes him at points (probably from a now-lost treatise).

## Role in the tradition
- **Foundational document of Western trinitarian theology**: Tertullian's *Adversus Praxean* is the work in which he coins much of the Latin trinitarian vocabulary — *Trinitas* itself (as a noun), *substantia*, *persona*, the "three persons, one substance" formula — language that becomes constitutive for the 4th-c. Nicene-Constantinopolitan settlement.
- **Anti-Montanist political action**: Praxeas's role in turning Rome against Montanism is read by Tertullian as a critical moment in proto-orthodox consolidation.

## Disputes
- **Real person or pseudonym for Callistus / Noetus / another**: unresolved.
- **Modalist Christology genealogy**: how closely connected to [[noetus-of-smyrna]] and [[sabellius]] is contested.
- **Date of the treatise**: ~+213 standard but possibly later.
