# Daniel

## Identity
Protagonist of the Book of Daniel — the figure presented (in the narrative frame) as a Judean exile rising to high office under Nebuchadnezzar, Belshazzar, Darius the Mede, and Cyrus; the visionary recipient of the apocalyptic dream-sequences (Dan 7-12) that define the genre. The Book of Daniel is widely treated by critical scholarship as a Maccabean-era (~-167 to -164) pseudonymous composition projecting its own crisis (the Antiochus IV Epiphanes persecution) into a vaticinium-ex-eventu prophecy spoken by an exilic-period figure.

## Historical evidence
The figure as the Book describes him is internal to the literary frame; no extrabiblical attestation of *this* Daniel. The much older Ugaritic "Dan'el" (in the Aqhat epic, 14th-c. BCE) is unrelated — but Ezekiel 14:14, 14:20, and 28:3 mention a "Daniel" or "Dan'el" alongside Noah and Job as paradigms of righteousness, which probably reflects the *older* legendary figure, not the Book-of-Daniel character.

## Role in the tradition
- **Hebrew Bible / canon location.** Daniel is in the *Writings* (Ketuvim) in the Tanakh — NOT among the Prophets — reflecting its late composition; the Christian Old Testament places it among the Major Prophets.
- **New Testament.** Mark 13:14 / Matthew 24:15 cite Daniel's "abomination of desolation" (Dan 9:27, 11:31, 12:11) as the eschatological sign. The "Son of Man" figure (Dan 7:13-14) is the single most important Old-Testament-prophetic source-text for the Synoptic Christology of Jesus as the Son of Man; Mark 14:62 and parallels deploy Dan 7:13 explicitly.
- **Islamic tradition.** Daniyal is not in the Quran but appears in early *hadith* and the Islamic prophetic legend collections (Tha'labi, Kisa'i).
