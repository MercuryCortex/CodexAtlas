# Nefertiti

## Identity
Nefertiti (c. 1370–1330 BCE) was the principal royal wife of the pharaoh [[akhenaten]] and a central theological figure in the Amarna revolution. Her name means "**the beautiful one has come**." She is among the most famous individuals from ancient Egypt and her limestone and plaster-coated portrait bust (Berlin, Neues Museum, Inv. ÄM 21300) — discovered in 1912 by Ludwig Borchardt in the sculptor Thutmose's workshop at Tell el-Amarna — is one of the most reproduced works of art in human history. Importantly, Egypt regards the bust as stolen cultural heritage and has repeatedly (and so far unsuccessfully) sought its repatriation from Germany since 1924.

## Theological role
Amarna relief art consistently depicts Nefertiti and [[akhenaten]] as a **paired theological dyad** making offerings to [[aten]] — not as consort-in-the-background but as a co-participant in the new solar cult. This is theologically significant: several Amarna reliefs show Nefertiti *alone* (without Akhenaten) performing Aten worship, suggesting she held genuine independent religious authority within the Aten cult. The [[phase-1-011-great-hymn-to-aten]] (composed during Akhenaten's reign and linked to him by many scholars) refers to the royal pair as the earthly reflection of Aten's creative power. In Amarna theology, the king and queen together represent the creative principle — a dyadic divine manifestation that echoes the cosmic generative pair in Egyptian theology generally.

## Fate and identity after Akhenaten
Her fate following Akhenaten's death (c. 1336 BCE) is one of Egyptology's most contested questions:
- **Nefertiti-as-Neferneferuaten hypothesis (Dodson 2009; supported by several cartouche readings):** A short-reigning pharaoh named *Neferneferuaten* appears in the archaeological record between Akhenaten's death and Tutankhamun's accession. Some Egyptologists argue that Nefertiti ruled under this name — making her one of ancient Egypt's rare regnant female pharaohs alongside Hatshepsut.
- **Smenkhkare hypothesis:** Others read Neferneferuaten as a male co-regent Smenkhkare (possibly Akhenaten's brother or son).
- **Mummy identification:** The "Elder Lady" mummy in KV35 (found beside Tiye, Amenhotep III's wife) has been tentatively linked to Nefertiti by Hawass et al. 2010 DNA analysis; the identification remains contested.

## Connections
- **[[akhenaten]]** — consort and theological co-participant in the Aten cult; Amarna art presents them as an inseparable dyadic pair
- **[[aten]]** — the solar deity of the Amarna revolution; Nefertiti and Akhenaten function jointly as Aten's earthly vessels
- **[[phase-1-011-great-hymn-to-aten]]** — attested in: the hymn's theological context requires the royal pair
- **[[phase-1-012-amarna-letters]]** — Nefertiti is attested in diplomatic correspondence of the Amarna period
