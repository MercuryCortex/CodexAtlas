# Shinran (1173–1263)

Japanese Pure Land Buddhist reformer and founder of Jōdo Shinshū (True Pure Land school). Disciple of Hōnen. Shinran radicalized Pure Land soteriology to a position structurally indistinguishable from Lutheran *sola fide* / *sola gratia*: salvation is entirely the Other Power (*tariki*) of Amitābha's Original Vow; the practitioner's sincere entrusting (*shinjin*) is itself a gift from Amitābha, not a human achievement. Even a single sincere nembutsu is sufficient; multiple nembutsu practice, if motivated by anxiety about one's own merit, contradicts the spirit of *tariki*.

**The Luther parallel**: Shinran was working in 13th-century Japan; [[martin-luther]] was working in 16th-century Germany. No historical connection. The structural convergence is complete:
- Amitābha's Original Vow (the divine saving act) ↔ Christ's atoning sacrifice
- *Shinjin* / sincere entrusting ↔ *fides* / faith
- *Tariki* (Other Power alone) ↔ *sola gratia* / *sola fide*
- Shinran's debate with the *jiriki* tradition ↔ Luther's debate with the works/merit tradition

His major work, the *Kyōgyōshinshō* (Teaching, Practice, Faith, Realization, 1224), is the systematic statement of Jōdo Shinshū soteriology.
