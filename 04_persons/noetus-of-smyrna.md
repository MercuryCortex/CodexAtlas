# Noetus of Smyrna

## Identity
A second-century Christian of Smyrna who taught the early form of **Modalist Monarchianism** — the position that the Father himself, being identical with the Son, was born, suffered, and died (the *Patripassianist* corollary). His disciples Epigonus and Cleomenes brought the teaching to Rome ~+200, where it provoked the trinitarian disputes that *Adversus Praxean* and *Refutatio* engage. The proto-Hippolytan ***Contra Noetum*** is the principal anti-Noetian polemic.

## Historical evidence
Hippolytus (or proto-Hippolytan author) of *Contra Noetum* and *Refutatio*. Epiphanius derives from Hippolytus. No contemporary non-hostile source.

## Works
None survives.

## Role in the tradition
- **Earliest named exponent of Modalist Monarchianism**: prior to [[sabellius]] and [[praxeas]] in chronological order, though all three positions are sometimes lumped.
- **Smyrnaean Christianity**: an internal sectarian crisis in the church of [[polycarp-of-smyrna|Polycarp]]'s former diocese, a generation later.
- **Catalyst for the Roman trinitarian crisis**: Noetus's disciples brought the teaching to Rome, precipitating the disputes that Hippolytus and Tertullian engage.

## Disputes
- **Authorship of *Contra Noetum***: traditionally Hippolytus; some scholars assign to a proto-Hippolytan school.
- **Whether the Noetian-Sabellian-Praxean trio represent distinct schools or stages of one position**: the heresiologists distinguish them, but modern reconstruction treats them as variants of a single broad current.
