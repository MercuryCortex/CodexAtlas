# Gennadios II Scholarios

George Scholarios (c. 1400-c. 1473), known as **Gennadios II** after taking monastic vows. The leading anti-Union Greek theologian of the late Byzantine period and the **first Patriarch of Constantinople under Ottoman rule** — appointed in January 1454 by [[mehmed-ii|Sultan Mehmed II]] in a deliberate political-religious gesture that gave the Greek-Christian population an autocephalous patriarch independent of Latin influence (a reversal of the 1439 Union of Florence — [[event-council-of-florence-1438-1439]] — that had been so divisive in Constantinopolitan Greek Christianity). Originally a participant at the Council of Florence under his secular name (and reportedly a moderate pro-Union voice there), Scholarios converted definitively to the anti-Union position after returning to Constantinople. His scholarly output included a Greek commentary on Thomas Aquinas (transmitted Latin scholasticism into Greek Orthodox theology — a rare and important channel) and the formal Christian-Muslim dialogue document composed at Mehmed II's request explaining Christian doctrine to a Muslim audience. The Gennadios-Mehmed appointment is one of the foundational moments of the Ottoman *millet* system of governance for non-Muslim populations.
