# Gabriel (Archangel)

## Identity
One of the seven (or in Jewish tradition four) chief angels of the post-exilic Jewish angelology; the angel who interprets visions to Daniel (Dan 8:16, 9:21), announces the births of John the Baptist and Jesus to Zechariah and Mary in Luke 1, and — most consequentially — delivers the Qur'anic revelations to Muhammad in Islamic tradition (Q 2:97). The name in Hebrew means "God-is-my-strength" or "warrior-of-God."

## Historical evidence
Mythological figure with continuous Jewish-Christian-Islamic tradition. The figure first emerges named in the late Second Temple period; the Watcher/angel apparatus of *1 Enoch* and Daniel provides the matrix.

## Role in the tradition
- Judaism: one of the four archangels (with Michael, Raphael, Uriel) of the apocalyptic tradition; appears as interpreter of visions; in later Kabbalah, associated with the sefirah Yesod or with the West.
- Christianity: the angel of Annunciation (Lk 1:26–38); generates extensive iconographic tradition.
- Islam: *Jibrīl* — the *rūḥ al-amīn* ("trustworthy spirit") who delivers the Qur'an over 23 years (610–632 CE); appears to Muhammad in the cave of Hira at the Night of Power (*Laylat al-Qadr*). The hadith of Gabriel (*Ḥadīth Jibrīl*) is a foundational pedagogical text on islām, imān, and iḥsān.

## Disputes
- The Christian-Muslim comparative theological problem: is the Gabriel of the Bible the same angel as the Jibrīl of the Qur'an? The traditions treat them as identical; comparative theology approaches the question variously.
