# April D. DeConick

## Identity
American historian of religion (b. 1958); Isla Carroll and Percy E. Turner Professor of Biblical Studies at Rice University; chair of Rice's Department of Religion 2011–2019. Editor of the Gnostica book series (Routledge) and longtime editor of *Gnosis: Journal of Gnostic Studies* (Brill). Trained at the University of Michigan under Jarl Fossum and David Noel Freedman.

## Works
See texts-authored. *The Thirteenth Apostle* (2007) is her most publicly known book: a sharp Coptic-philological rebuttal of the National Geographic Society team's 2006 reading of the Gospel of Judas, arguing that Judas in the original Coptic is a *demon* (Greek *daimon* rendered into Coptic) rather than the redeemed insider the National Geographic translation made him out to be. Her *Original Gospel of Thomas* (2007) defends a "rolling corpus" model of Thomas's composition. *The Gnostic New Age* (2016) extends ancient Gnosticism's reception forward into modern Theosophy, Jungianism, and contemporary spirituality.

## Role in the field
A principal active scholar of Coptic Gnostic texts post-Pagels/King generation. Her critique of the 2006 Judas reading is now reflected in the revised critical edition (Kasser, Wurst et al. 2008) and accepted by most subsequent scholarship. Her work explicitly bridges ancient Gnostic studies and the modern reception of "Gnosticism" (Theosophy, Jung, [[helena-blavatsky]], [[carl-gustav-jung]]) — making her a key figure for the lineage this vault traces.

## Disputes
- **Gospel of Judas reading.** The 2006 National Geographic translation team (Meyer, Wurst, Kasser, Gaudard) initially read Judas favorably; DeConick (2007) and Painchaud argued strongly otherwise. The dispute is now substantially resolved in favor of the DeConick/Painchaud reading.
- **"Rolling corpus" model for Thomas.** Her stratigraphic model is contested by scholars who prefer a more unified composition (e.g., Patterson, Plisch).

## Connections
- Continues the Coptic Gnostic-studies lineage from [[elaine-pagels]], [[karen-king]], [[hans-jonas]] and now into the [[tradition-new-age]] reception (her 2016 book).
