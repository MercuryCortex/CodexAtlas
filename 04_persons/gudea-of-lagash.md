# Gudea

## Identity
Gudea (r. ~-2144 to -2124 BCE) was the most famous *ensi* (city-ruler) of the Second Dynasty of Lagash, the period between the collapse of the Akkadian Empire and the rise of the Third Dynasty of Ur. As Lagash navigated the political vacuum of the late Gutian period, Gudea presided over an extraordinary cultural flourishing centered on the city-state's principal religious project: the rebuilding of the **Eninnu** ("House of Fifty") temple of [[nin-girsu]] at Girsu. The two **Gudea Cylinders** ([[phase-1-018-gudea-cylinders]]), Cylinder A (~840 lines) and Cylinder B (~430 lines), are jointly the longest extant Sumerian literary text — a temple-construction hymn that records (in extraordinary detail) Gudea's divine commission, the temple's plan, the procurement of materials from across Western Asia (cedar from Amanus, diorite from Magan, copper from Kimash, gold from Ḫaḫḫum, lapis lazuli from Afghanistan), the construction itself, the gods' installation, and the dedication festivals. Beyond the Cylinders, Gudea is uniquely well-represented in **statuary**: over thirty diorite statues of him (mostly seated, hands clasped in prayer, with extensive inscriptions on the laps and backs) have survived from his temple complexes — the largest surviving sculptural corpus of any third-millennium Sumerian ruler. The Gudea statues are among the principal art-historical monuments of ancient Mesopotamian sculpture.

## Historical evidence
Extraordinary:
1. **The Gudea Cylinders** — the principal literary monument, presumed contemporary with Gudea or shortly after.
2. **The Gudea statues** — at least thirty, primarily diorite, with extensive inscriptions naming him, his patron deities, and his works.
3. **Votive inscriptions** on door-sockets, foundation tablets, mace-heads, and bowls.
4. **Brick stamps** from the Eninnu construction.
5. **Cylinder seals** of his officials.

He is one of the best-documented Sumerian rulers of any period — and one of the few whose visual likeness is preserved on a substantial scale.

## Works
- **The Gudea Cylinders A and B** ([[phase-1-018-gudea-cylinders]]) — temple-construction hymn.
- **The Gudea Statue inscriptions** — Edzard 1997 catalogs Statues A through Z (and beyond). The inscriptions are in royal first-person voice, dedicating each statue and commemorating his works.
- **Votive inscriptions** on the door-sockets, foundation-pegs, and dedicatory objects of the Eninnu, the Etarsirsir of [[bau-sumerian]], the Esirara of [[nanshe-sumerian]], and other Lagashite sanctuaries.

## Role in the tradition
In Sumerian historical memory Gudea is the paradigmatic *pious builder-king* — a counter-image to the *hubristic conqueror* type embodied by [[naram-sin]]. Unlike Naram-Sin, Gudea does not claim divinity in his lifetime; he depicts himself with humility, hands clasped in prayer, consulting the gods at every step. The Eninnu construction is presented as commissioned by Ningirsu in a dream, interpreted by Nanshe, executed by Gudea, and consecrated by the divine council. This *theological exemplarity* secured Gudea's place in the Old Babylonian scribal-school curriculum, and his statues secured his place in 19th-century-onward European museum collections — many are now in the Louvre (recovered by Ernest de Sarzec from Telloh, ancient Girsu, beginning in 1877).

The art-historical importance of the Gudea statues is hard to overstate: they are the principal evidence for the development of three-dimensional sculpture in ancient Mesopotamia and demonstrate technical mastery in working diorite (one of the hardest stones used in ancient sculpture) — material that had to be transported from Magan (modern Oman) by sea, as Gudea's own inscriptions record.

## Disputes
- **Date**. ~-2144 to -2124 BCE per the conventional chronology; the precise relationship to the Gutian rulers and to Ur-Nammu's rise at Ur is debated.
- **Independence from Gutian rule**. Whether Gudea ruled as nominal Gutian vassal or as effective independent is unresolved; he does not name a Gutian overlord in his inscriptions.
- **Number and identification of his statues**. The total surviving Gudea statues is a moving target; new fragments are occasionally identified. Edzard 1997 catalogs the corpus as known then.
