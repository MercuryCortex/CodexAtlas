# António Vieira (1608–1697)

## Life
Born in Lisbon (1608), raised in Brazil from age 6, Vieira joined the Jesuit novitiate in Salvador de Bahia (1623). He returned to Portugal in 1641 as a court preacher to the newly-restored King João IV (House of Bragança, post-Iberian-Union). His brilliance as a preacher, diplomat, and writer made him the most prominent intellectual figure of 17th-century Portuguese letters. He spent periods in Brazil (as missionary to indigenous peoples and African slaves), in Rome (as a diplomatic agent), and in Portugal — repeatedly caught between political-religious factions.

**Inquisition trial (1663–1667):** Vieira was arrested by the Portuguese Inquisition primarily for his prophetic writings identifying Portugal as the seat of the Fifth World Empire and articulating a theology of Sebastianist return that the Inquisition considered heterodox. He was condemned and prohibited from preaching. He appealed to Rome; Pope Clement X issued a brief protecting his writings (1675). He returned to Brazil in 1681 and died in Bahia in 1697.

## The Fifth Empire theology
Vieira's contribution to [[fifth-empire-quinto-imperio]] is the decisive scholastic systematization. His main prophetic works:

**"História do Futuro" (1664, incomplete):** A providentialist history of Portugal arguing from Daniel's four-kingdom vision that Portugal is destined to establish the Fifth and final empire — a universal Christian temporal-spiritual monarchy. The text was incomplete when the Inquisition arrested him.

**"Clavis Prophetarum" (composed 1663–1697; published posthumously):** "The Key of the Prophets" — Vieira's attempt to demonstrate from scripture and classical prophecy that the Fifth Empire was imminent and that Portugal was its vehicle. He uses Daniel 2, Isaiah, Jeremiah, and the Sibylline Oracles alongside Bandarra's vernacular *Trovas* and astronomical cycles. This is the most systematic theological text in the Sebastianist canon.

## The Joachimist transmission
Vieira's prophetic periodization of history derives directly from **Joachim of Fiore** (1135–1202), the Calabrian Cistercian abbot whose three-age schema (Age of the Father / Son / Spirit) was one of the most influential heretical theological frameworks of medieval Europe. Vieira inherits Joachim's periodization, adapts it to five ages matching Daniel's five kingdoms, and inserts Portugal as the agent of the fifth and final age. The transmission path: Joachim (12th c. Italy) → Franciscan Spirituals (13th–14th c.) → Portuguese Franciscans and Augustinians (15th–16th c.) → Bandarra (vernacular appropriation, 1530s) → Vieira (systematic scholastic articulation, 1660s–1690s). This is **documented transmission**, not independent invention (Cantel 1960; Cohen 1998).

## Brazil and the prophetic-political dimension
Vieira preached against the enslavement of indigenous Brazilians — he argued from the same providentialist theology that the Indians were potential subjects of the Fifth Empire and therefore could not be permanently enslaved. This made him enemies among the Portuguese settler class. His sermons on behalf of indigenous Brazilians are among the most politically consequential texts of 17th-century Iberian Catholicism.

Fernando Pessoa called Vieira "the most perfect Portuguese prose writer" and "the Emperor of the Portuguese Language" — elevating him alongside Camões, Cesário Verde, and Antero de Quental as one of the four pillars of Portuguese literary genius.
