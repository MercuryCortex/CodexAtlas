# Al-Tabari

## Identity
Muḥammad ibn Jarīr al-Ṭabarī (839–923) was a polymath Sunni scholar of late-Abbasid Baghdad: author of the foundational *Tafsīr* (Qur'anic commentary, *Jāmiʿ al-bayān*) and of the standard early Islamic universal history *Taʾrīkh al-Rusul wa-l-Mulūk* (History of Prophets and Kings, from creation through ~915 CE). Born in Āmul, Ṭabaristan (south of the Caspian); independently wealthy from family lands; traveled extensively across the Islamic world (Mecca, Medina, Iraq, Syria, Egypt) before settling in Baghdad to write. Founded a short-lived legal school (the Jarīrī, extinct ~1000) of his own.

## Historical evidence
Well-documented through contemporary biographical dictionaries (Ibn al-Nadīm's *Fihrist* ~988) and his own substantial corpus.

## Works
*Jāmiʿ al-Bayān* (Tafsīr, ~883–923, 30 vols modern); *Taʾrīkh* (universal history, 40 vols modern English); shorter legal works; lost works in jurisprudence and hadith-criticism.

## Role in the tradition
Foundational; the two great works are the *imām* of Sunni tafsīr and the most-cited early Islamic historical source. The *Taʾrīkh* preserves earlier sources (especially the lost works of Ibn Isḥāq, al-Wāqidī, al-Madāʾinī) without which we would know very little of early Islamic history. Modern Western Qur'an criticism (Wansbrough 1977) uses Ṭabarī as a principal data point.
