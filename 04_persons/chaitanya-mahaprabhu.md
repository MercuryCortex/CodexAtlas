# Chaitanya Mahaprabhu

## Identity
Bengali Brahmin Krishna-devotee and ecstatic mystic, regarded by his followers as the combined Radha-Krishna avatara, and the founder of Gaudiya Vaishnavism (1486–1534). Born Vishvambhara Misra at Navadvipa (Bengal); known as Nimai in his youth; a brilliant Sanskrit pandit. Underwent religious transformation ~1508 during a pilgrimage to Gaya; took sannyasa 1510 at age 24 (as Krishna Chaitanya); thereafter lived as a wandering devotee. Traveled extensively in South India and Vrindavan (~1510–1515) before settling at Puri in Orissa under the patronage of King Prataparudra of the Gajapati dynasty (1515–1534). The Six Goswamis of Vrindavan — Rupa, Sanatana, Jiva, Gopala Bhatta, Raghunatha Bhatta, Raghunatha Dasa — were dispatched to Vrindavan to rediscover the holy sites of Krishna's childhood and to systematize the theology of the new movement. Chaitanya himself emphasized *nama-sankirtana* (congregational chanting of the divine names "Hare Krishna") and the ecstatic *madhurya-rasa* devotion in which the devotee identifies with Radha's love for Krishna. Died at Puri 1534 under circumstances obscure to the tradition (possibly entered the Jagannath temple and merged with the deity, per devotional account).

## Historical evidence
Multiple hagiographical biographies: Murari Gupta's *Sri Krishna Chaitanya Charitamrita* (~1535, the earliest), Vrindavana Dasa Thakura's *Chaitanya Bhagavata* (~1545), Locana Dasa's *Chaitanya Mangala*, and the canonical Krishnadasa Kaviraja *Chaitanya Charitamrita* (c. 1612–1615). The Orissan Gajapati and Bengali sources document the historical figure firmly; the theological-mystical figure is hagiographically shaped.

## Works
Only the eight verses of the *Shikshashtakam* are firmly attributed; tradition reports that Chaitanya was deliberately reticent about writing, devoting himself to ecstatic devotional practice and to commissioning his learned disciples (the Six Goswamis) to produce the textual-theological corpus.

## Role in the tradition
Founder of Gaudiya Vaishnavism, the Krishna-bhakti tradition that has had the broadest modern global reach (through ISKCON, founded 1966). His theological signature — *achintya-bheda-abheda* (inconceivable simultaneous identity and difference, structurally cognate to [[nimbarka]]'s *Dvaitādvaita*), the supremacy of *madhurya-rasa*, the identification of Krishna of Vrindavan as supreme — was systematized by his disciples. Part of the broader [[event-bhakti-movement-c-700-1700|bhakti movement]] arc; contemporary with [[vallabhacharya]]'s Pushtimarg, [[mirabai]]'s Rajasthani Krishna-bhakti, and [[surdas]]'s Braj poetry.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Vrindavan as the principal late-medieval Krishna-bhakti pilgrimage center**: the Six Goswamis of Chaitanya (Rūpa, Sanātana, Jīva, Gopāla Bhatta, Raghunātha Bhatta, Raghunātha Dāsa) and the parallel Vrindavan-area institutional development by Nimbarka and Vallabha sampradāyas together transformed the Vrindavan region into the principal Krishna-bhakti pilgrimage landscape — a process broadly comparable to the medieval Catholic establishment of major Marian pilgrimage centers (Lourdes, Czestochowa, Guadalupe).
- **ISKCON global diaspora**: A.C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada's 1965 arrival in New York and 1966 founding of ISKCON made Gaudiya Vaishnavism the most globally-visible Hindu tradition in the late-20th-c. Western world.
