# Blandina

## Identity
An enslaved Christian young woman of the Christian community of Lugdunum (Lyon, Gaul), one of the principal martyr-figures of the **Lyon and Vienne persecution of 177** under [[marcus-aurelius-emperor|Marcus Aurelius]]. The *Letter of the Churches of Lyons and Vienne* — preserved in Eusebius HE 5.1 — gives one of the most graphic and theologically charged martyrdom narratives in early Christian literature. Blandina is depicted as a frail enslaved woman whose feared inability to endure torture became, through divine empowerment, an astonishing public testimony — repeatedly tortured, tossed by a bull, *forced to watch the martyrdom of her companions* before her own — until she was finally killed by the sword.

## Historical evidence
**Eusebius HE 5.1** preserves the contemporary *Letter of the Churches of Lyons and Vienne*, addressed to the brethren in Asia and Phrygia (Lyon's Christian community had originated as Asia-Minor immigrants, hence the letter east). The detail and dramatic structure mark it as semi-literary; the core historicity is widely accepted.

## Works
None — but as the subject of one of the most-quoted martyrdom narratives, her presence in Christian memory is enormous.

## Role in the tradition
- **Martyr of the marginalized**: an enslaved woman whose *gender-and-status* make her the perfect counter-imperial witness. The narrator explicitly emphasizes the irony: a "small and weak and despised" body manifesting divine power.
- **Lyon martyrdoms as foundational Western Latin martyrology**: the *Letter* is among the earliest and most influential Western martyrologies, predating the *Passio Perpetuae* by 25 years.
- **Christ-typological martyrdom**: Blandina's hanging on a stake (per the narrative) explicitly evokes the crucifixion.

## Disputes
- **Number of martyrs in the Lyon persecution**: 48 named in later tradition; the *Letter* names ~10 principals.
- **Date precisely**: ~+177 standard; Eusebius's chronology is approximate.
