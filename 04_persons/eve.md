# Eve

## Identity
The first woman of the Genesis 2–3 narrative — formed from Adam's rib, named *Ḥawwāh* ("mother of all living," Gen 3:20), partner in the eating of the forbidden fruit, mother of Cain, Abel, and Seth. The Pauline reading (1 Tim 2:13–14) makes Eve the *first sinner*, theologically grounding gendered hierarchy; the patristic readings (Tertullian's "you are the devil's gateway") radicalize the move. Gnostic re-readings systematically *invert* the Pauline-orthodox reading: in the *Hypostasis of the Archons* (NHC II,4) and *On the Origin of the World* (NHC II,5), Eve is the *bringer of gnosis*, accompanied by Sophia/Zoe (Life), and the serpent is the instructor; the wicked archons rape Eve's bodily form but fail to seize her spiritual self.

## Historical evidence
None; the figure is mytho-narrative.

## Role in the tradition
- Orthodox Christian: Eve as the "second Adam" Mary's prefiguration-by-contrast (Justin Martyr, Irenaeus): "Eve unbound through her disobedience; Mary unbound through her obedience."
- Gnostic: Eve as wisdom-bearer; the serpent as instructor. See [[phase-4-006-hypostasis-of-the-archons]], [[phase-4-007-on-the-origin-of-the-world]].
- Islamic: *Ḥawwā* is named in tradition but rarely in the Qur'an directly; the Qur'an's fall narrative (e.g., Sura 7) does not single her out as cause of the fall.
- Jewish: rabbinic and kabbalistic developments (Eve and Lilith pairings; the Zoharic feminine).
- Modern: feminist hermeneutics (Trible's *God and the Rhetoric of Sexuality*) re-reads Gen 2–3 against the patriarchal-traditional gloss.

## Disputes
- Theological: original sin, gendered hierarchy, the fall.
- Gnostic vs. orthodox readings — the locus classicus of [[atonement-reinterpreted]]-style inversions.
