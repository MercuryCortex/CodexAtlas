# Joseph Caiaphas

## Identity
High priest of the Second Temple from c. 18 to 36 CE — an exceptionally long tenure for a position the Roman procurators usually rotated frequently. Son-in-law of Annas; the high priest who presides over the Sanhedrin trial of Jesus in the synoptic and Johannine passion narratives (Matt 26:57-68, par.; John 11:49-52, 18:13-14, 18:24, 18:28). John 11:50 attributes to him the unintentionally prophetic remark that "it is expedient that one man die for the people."

## Historical evidence
Caiaphas is one of the most-evidenced gospel-secondary figures. Josephus names him as high priest in *Antiquities* 18.2.2 (appointment under Valerius Gratus) and 18.4.3 (removal under Vitellius). The **Caiaphas Ossuary** (discovered 1990 in a south-Jerusalem burial cave; Greenhut and Reich 1992) bears the Aramaic inscription *Yosef bar Qayyafa* and almost certainly contained the high priest's remains; this is one of the very few first-century-CE Jerusalem ossuaries with a direct historical-figure attribution.

## Role in the tradition
The gospel passion narratives feature him as the primary Jewish-establishment antagonist of Jesus. The historical accuracy of the trial scene as the gospels present it is heavily debated (Sanders 1985; Brown 1994; Bond 2004 monograph on Caiaphas specifically) — was there a formal Sanhedrin trial, or only a preliminary hearing? Were the charges blasphemy, sedition, or both? Caiaphas's actual role in the decision to hand Jesus to Pilate is well-attested in outline but uncertain in detail.
