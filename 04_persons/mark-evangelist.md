# Mark the Evangelist

## Identity
"Mark" is the traditional name attached to the author of the earliest canonical Gospel. The tradition (Papias, ~+125, preserved by Eusebius) identifies him with **John Mark**, the New Testament figure who appears in Acts as a Jerusalem disciple from a prominent family, a companion of Paul and Barnabas, and finally an associate of Peter. Coptic tradition further makes him founder of the Alexandrian church.

## Historical evidence
The Papias tradition (Mark as Peter's interpreter who wrote down Peter's preaching at Rome) is our earliest external attestation of the Gospel's authorship — but the Gospel itself is *anonymous*. Bauckham (2006) defends the Papias tradition as substantially reliable; mainstream critical scholarship (Marcus, Hooker, Schnelle) is more skeptical, treating Papias's claim as a 2nd-century attribution to a New-Testament-attested figure rather than firm historical knowledge. The figure of John Mark himself (Acts 12:12 etc.) is reasonably well-attested as a real person; whether *that* John Mark is *our* Mark the Evangelist is the open question. For this vault we hold the authorship as **disputed** while noting the Gospel itself is securely datable to ~+65/+75 regardless.
