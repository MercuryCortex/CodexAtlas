# Zakariyyā

Quranic priest and father of **Yaḥyā** (John the Baptist). Zakariyyā appears in the Quran's nativity cluster — Surah 3 (Āl ʿImrān) and Surah 19 (Maryam) — as the aging priest who asks God for a son despite his wife's barrenness and his own old age. God grants the prayer; the birth of Yaḥyā is announced as a miracle (Surah 3:37–41, 19:2–15).

## Quranic profile

- **Surah 3:37–41** — Zakariyyā is guardian of Mary (*Maryam*) in the Temple; sees miraculous food provided to her; asks God for an heir; receives the annunciation of Yaḥyā
- **Surah 19:2–15** — the most extended account; Zakariyyā's private prayer to God; his awe at being old and grey; the announcement; he requests a sign and is told he will be unable to speak for three days

The Quranic Zakariyyā narrative is closely parallel to the Lukan annunciation narrative (Luke 1:5–25, 57–80), and Reynolds 2018 documents the extensive shared material with Protevangelium of James (the apocryphal infancy Gospel), suggesting the Quran is engaging with the same cluster of late-antique nativity traditions.

## Distinction from Zechariah the prophet

This node is distinct from the Hebrew prophet Zechariah (author of the book of Zechariah, c. 520 BCE). Zakariyyā the Quranic priest belongs to the 1st-century CE nativity tradition, not the 6th-century BCE prophetic tradition. They share a name but are different figures in the tradition-history.
