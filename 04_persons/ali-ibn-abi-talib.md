# Ali ibn Abi Talib

## Identity
Cousin, ward, and son-in-law of the Prophet Muhammad (husband of [[muhammad-ibn-abdullah]]'s daughter Fatima al-Zahra; father of [[hasan-ibn-ali]] and [[husayn-ibn-ali]]). Born in the Kaaba (per pious tradition) in Mecca ~600; among the very first converts to Islam (in Shi'a tradition the very first male convert after Muhammad himself, age ~10 at the call); accompanied Muhammad through the Meccan persecution, the Hegira (622), and the Medinan campaigns. Fourth of the *Rashidun* (Rightly Guided) caliphs (656–661), ruling from Kufa. Assassinated in the Kufa mosque by the Kharijite ibn Muljam in Ramadan 40 AH (January 661). For Sunni Islam, the last of the four Rightly Guided caliphs; for Shi'i Islam, **the First Imam**, divinely designated by Muhammad as his successor at Ghadir Khumm (March 632, on Muhammad's return from the Farewell Pilgrimage), whose rightful authority was usurped at Saqifa (June 632) by the choice of [[abu-bakr]].

## Historical evidence
Documented across the full early-Islamic textual record: Qur'anic passages whose referent is contested between Sunni and Shi'i exegesis (especially Q 5:55, "Your wali is only God, His Messenger, and those who believe — those who establish prayer and give zakat while bowing"); the Sira tradition (Ibn Ishaq via Ibn Hisham); the early historiographical works ([[al-tabari]]'s *Ta'rikh*, Baladhuri's *Ansab al-Ashraf*, Ya'qubi); the Sunni hadith corpus ([[phase-5-007-sahih-al-bukhari]] et al.); the Shi'i hadith corpus (Kulayni's *al-Kafi*, ibn Babawayh, al-Tusi); the *Nahj al-Balagha* compiled by al-Sharif al-Radi ~1009 (a major source for Ali's reported sermons and letters, though scholarly assessment of the authentic stratum vs. later attribution varies — see Disputes). The historicity of Ali as a major political-military figure in the founding generation of Islam is not in serious doubt.

## Works
- ***Nahj al-Balagha*** ("The Peak of Eloquence") — compilation of sermons, letters, and aphorisms attributed to Ali, assembled by the Twelver Shi'i scholar al-Sharif al-Radi (d. 1015). A foundational text of Shi'i religious-political prose, treasured also by literarily-attuned Sunni readers. Authenticity of individual items varies; the compilation represents the high tradition of Ali's reported speech, mediated through ~350 years of transmission.
- ***Du'a Kumayl*** — supplicatory prayer attributed to Ali, taught (per Shi'i tradition) to his companion Kumayl ibn Ziyad; a central piece of Shi'i devotional liturgy, recited on Thursday nights.

## Role in the tradition
The structural fault-line of the entire Sunni–Shi'i division turns on the legitimacy of the succession that bypassed Ali. The Sunni view: the *Rashidun* (Abu Bakr, [[uthman|Umar]] (note: existing slug TBD), Uthman, Ali) succeed in order through consensus (*shura*) of the community; all four are legitimate, Ali included; the Sunni–Shi'i division is regrettable. The Shi'i view: Muhammad explicitly designated Ali at Ghadir Khumm; the Saqifa election was illegitimate; the three preceding caliphs were usurpers; only Ali's caliphate, and the line of Imams through his sons by Fatima, carries divinely-authorized authority.

In Shi'i theology, Ali is the First Imam — possessor of the *walaya* (spiritual-political authority) and *isma* (sinlessness / infallibility) that mark the divinely-designated successor of Muhammad. Mature Shi'i devotional theology (Amir-Moezzi 1994) attributes to Ali an esoteric (*batin*) hermeneutic of the Qur'an, transmitted only through the Imams — Ali as the source of esoteric Islamic knowledge. This esoteric-imam-as-knowledge-source doctrine has structural parallels to Gnostic-hermetic esotericism (Henry Corbin's life work) — contested but influential.

For Sufism, Ali is a key figure in most Sufi *silsila* (chains of transmission), which trace spiritual authority from Muhammad through Ali to subsequent masters; only the Naqshbandi order (notably Sunni-Sufi) traces primarily through [[abu-bakr]]. For 12-Imam Shi'i Islam, Ali is the cosmic-historical center; for Alawi Shi'ism (Syria, Turkey), Ali approaches divinity in some doctrinal strands.

## Disputes
- **Ghadir Khumm.** The hadith is well-attested in both Sunni and Shi'i sources; the interpretive question — whether Muhammad's words ("whoever I am his *mawla*, Ali is his *mawla*") constitute formal succession-designation, formal acknowledgment of Ali's status without designation, or something else — is the foundational Sunni–Shi'i hermeneutic dispute. Madelung 1997 takes the Shi'i historical claim seriously: the textual evidence does favor an early intent toward Ali's succession; the political outcome of Saqifa was a coup of sorts. This is a relatively unusual concession by a non-Shi'i historian.

- ***Nahj al-Balagha* authenticity.** Twelver Shi'i tradition treats the compilation as substantively the speech of Ali. Some Sunni critics (medieval and modern) have suggested al-Sharif al-Radi himself authored or heavily redacted portions. Critical assessment (Djebli, EI2 "Nahdj al-Balagha") finds a discernible early stratum with later expansion; the high literary register reflects later Arabic prose conventions in places. Authenticity-per-item is the right frame, not blanket attribution.

- **The Kharijite secession.** The 657 Battle of Siffin against Mu'awiya's Syrian forces ended in arbitration after the celebrated raising of Qur'an pages on Syrian lances; the Kharijites broke with Ali on the grounds that submitting the matter to human arbitration was a usurpation of God's judgment. This sub-tradition produced the assassins of Ali (661) and the long-running Kharijite-jurisprudential tradition. The Sunni–Shi'i–Kharijite triangulation is the earliest doctrinal structure of Islamic politics.

## Cross-tradition connections
- ↔ [[husayn-ibn-ali]] — son; the line through Ali → Fatima → Hasan & Husayn is the genealogical-charismatic spine of all Shi'i Islam.
- ↔ [[muhammad-ibn-abdullah]] — father-in-law, cousin, ward; the closest male relative of Muhammad in the generation of his sons-in-law.
- ↔ [[event-karbala-680]] — the catastrophe-event that turns Ali's bypassed line into the foundational martyr-line of Shi'i Islam.
- ↔ [[abu-bakr]] / [[uthman]] — the proximate Rashidun-caliph competitors in the succession question.
- ↔ [[al-hallaj]], [[rumi]], [[ibn-arabi]] — the Sufi tradition that locates spiritual authority in *silsila* descending through Ali (Naqshbandi excepted).
- ↔ Esoteric / [[gnosis-as-salvation]] — Amir-Moezzi's argument that early Shi'ism transmits a *batin* (esoteric) hermeneutic comparable in religious-structural position to Gnostic-hermetic esotericism. Corbin (1971–1972) is the influential, contested expositor of this comparative reading.
