# Symeon the New Theologian

## Identity
Symeon the New Theologian (949–1022) — granted the rare title "Theologian" (after John the Evangelist and Gregory of Nazianzus) — was the first Byzantine writer to develop a fully articulated theology of explicit vision-of-light at the heart of monastic experience. Abbot of the monastery of St. Mamas in Constantinople ~980–1009; deposed and exiled by ecclesiastical authorities for the publicly provocative claim that direct experience of God was the proper goal of monastic life and that the experienced monk could authoritatively interpret Scripture and teach. The theology of vision-of-light (the uncreated divine light of Christ's Tabor Transfiguration as experientially available to advanced practitioners) is the foundational experiential warrant for later Hesychast practice and for the Palamite essence/energies distinction.

## Works
*Catechetical Discourses* (sermons preached to his monks); *Hymns of Divine Love* (~58 mystical poems); *Theological, Gnostic and Practical Chapters*; Ethical Discourses; Letters.

## Role in the tradition
Bridge between earlier Byzantine monastic spirituality (Macarian Homilies, the Sinai tradition of Climacus) and the high Hesychast tradition (~14th c. Palamas). Indispensable to the Eastern Orthodox theological self-understanding as a tradition of experiential-mystical theology grounded in direct vision rather than primarily scholastic argumentation.
