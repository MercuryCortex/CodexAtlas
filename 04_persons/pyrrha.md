# Pyrrha

## Identity
**Pyrrha** (Greek *Πύρρα*) is the wife of [[deucalion]] and the female co-survivor of the Greek flood. She is the daughter of **Epimetheus** and **Pandora** — making her, by Greek mythological genealogy, the daughter of the first-created woman (Pandora) and one of the Titans of forethought-and-afterthought (Epimetheus, "after-thinker," the foolish brother of Prometheus).

The Deucalion-Pyrrha pairing genealogically integrates the two foundational Greek anthropogonic traditions: **Prometheus's lineage** (Deucalion as son of Prometheus the forethought-Titan and friend-of-humanity) + **Pandora's lineage** (Pyrrha as daughter of Pandora the first woman and Epimetheus the after-thought-Titan). After the flood, Deucalion and Pyrrha repopulate humanity by throwing stones (the *bones of their mother*, Earth) over their shoulders — Deucalion's stones becoming men, Pyrrha's becoming women.

Legendary; no contemporary attestation.

## Role in the tradition

Pyrrha is the Greek flood-tradition's female-survivor figure. The Greek pairing of flood-hero with named *wife* is distinctive — Mesopotamian Ziusudra/Atrahasis/Utnapishtim's wife is unnamed in the cuneiform sources; Noah's wife is unnamed in Genesis (named only in later rabbinic and Christian tradition as Naamah or Norea); Manu's wife is unnamed in the early Vedic flood-narrative. Pyrrha's named-and-acting role in the Greek tradition reflects a distinctively Greek emphasis on the female-survivor as co-actor in the post-flood human-restoration.

## Disputes

- **Etymology.** "Pyrrha" relates to *pyrros* "red, fiery"; possible folk-etymology connections to red clay (the material from which post-flood humans are created in some traditions) or to a hypothesized red-haired ethnic-mythological substrate.
- **Genealogical position.** As Epimetheus + Pandora's daughter, Pyrrha is integrated into the Greek anthropogonic genealogies in a specific way that her Mesopotamian and Hebrew counterparts are not.
