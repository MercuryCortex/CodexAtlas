# Ahmose I

## Identity

Ahmose I (r. c. 1550–1525 BCE) was the founder of the 18th Dynasty and the Egyptian New Kingdom — the pharaoh who completed the expulsion of the Hyksos 15th Dynasty, reunified Upper and Lower Egypt under Theban rule, and inaugurated Egypt's most powerful imperial period. He was the son of Seqenenre Tao II, whose mummy shows multiple battle wounds (axe blows to the skull) consistent with combat — possibly the battle that began the final war against the Hyksos.

## Historical evidence

Ahmose I is exceptionally well-documented for the early New Kingdom. Primary sources include: (1) the **Kamose Stelae** (his elder brother's campaign; Luxor Museum), which provide the immediate pre-Ahmose military context; (2) the **autobiography of Ahmose son of Ebana**, inscribed in his tomb at El-Kab — a first-person military account of the siege of Avaris and subsequent operations in Canaan (trans. Lichtheim 1976); (3) royal inscriptions from Ahmose I's reign; (4) the **Ahmose Tempest Stele** (Karnak), a text describing catastrophic storms — some scholars connect this to the Thera/Santorini volcanic eruption (c. 1620–1540 BCE, dates disputed), though the volcanic-connection thesis is contested.

The autobiography of Ahmose son of Ebana describes fighting at Avaris both on land and from ships on the Delta waterways, receiving "gold of valor" decorations for taking enemy captives, and the pursuit of the Hyksos to the Canaanite fortress city of Sharuhen (in the southern Negev), where Ahmose besieged for three years.

## Role in the tradition

Ahmose I is Egypt's memory of national liberation from foreign domination. His 18th Dynasty successors — Thutmose I, II, III, Hatshepsut, Amenhotep II, III, and ultimately Akhenaten — built on his expulsion of the Hyksos to project Egyptian imperial power into Canaan. The Egyptian administrative apparatus that dominates Canaan in the Amarna Letters (~1350 BCE) is the direct institutional descendant of Ahmose's reunification. The New Kingdom Egypt that provides the historical backdrop for the Moses tradition is the world Ahmose I created.
