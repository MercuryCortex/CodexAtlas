Isaac Newton (1643–1727) — English mathematician, physicist, and theologian; formulated the laws of motion and universal gravitation in the *Principia Mathematica* (1687), systematizing [[kepler]]'s empirical laws under a unified force-based framework. Newton spent as much time on alchemical and biblical-prophetic research as on physics — his unpublished manuscripts (now at the Keynes Collection, Cambridge) include millions of words on alchemy and biblical chronology. His heterodox theology (anti-Trinitarian Arianism) and alchemical practice situate him at the intersection of the scientific revolution and the hermetic tradition. See [[kepler]] for his direct mathematical predecessor; [[mathematics-house-of-wisdom]] for the Arabic algebraic tools that reached him via the European mathematical tradition he inherited.
