# Isaac Newton

## The Hidden Newton — Theologian and Alchemist

The Isaac Newton known to popular culture — the discoverer of gravity, the mathematician who invented calculus — is a selective portrait. The actual Newton spent **more time on theology, alchemy, and biblical prophecy than on physics and mathematics combined**. His unpublished papers, now distributed between the Keynes Collection (King's College, Cambridge), the Yahuda Manuscripts (Jewish National Library, Jerusalem), and the Newton Project (Oxford), contain approximately **1.4 million words of theological writing** — manuscripts on biblical prophecy, church history, the symbolism of Solomon's Temple, chronology of ancient kingdoms, and anti-Trinitarian theology.

Newton was not a deist who happened to do science. He was a deeply committed heterodox Christian who believed his scientific and theological projects were aspects of the same investigation: reading "God's cryptogram" in both the Book of Nature and the Book of Scripture.

## Anti-Trinitarianism — The Secret Heresy

Newton's most dangerous secret: he believed the doctrine of the Trinity was a corruption of original Christianity, introduced fraudulently at the Council of Nicaea (325 CE) by Athanasius. He held that Christ was a divine being but subordinate to God the Father — the Arian position condemned as heresy.

He kept this view secret throughout his life because it was potentially a criminal offense (the Blasphemy Act of 1697 made denial of the Trinity punishable by loss of office and legal incapacity). His *Irenicum* manuscript, circulated privately, argued that primitive Christianity was unitarian. He could not have remained Lucasian Professor of Mathematics or Master of the Royal Mint had his theological views been public.

Newton saw himself as a **restorer of true primitive religion** — the same program as the prisca theologia, applied to Christianity.

## Alchemy — Not a Hobby

Newton's alchemical work was not a youthful phase or eccentric sideshow. It continued throughout his scientific career, and may be structurally related to his physics. Key points:

- He wrote approximately **1 million words on alchemy** — transcriptions of alchemical texts, experimental records, theoretical synthesis
- He studied the full corpus of Western alchemical literature: Flamel, George Starkey ("Eirenaeus Philalethes"), Michael Maier, the *Rosarium Philosophorum*
- He conducted hands-on experiments in a laboratory at Trinity College, Cambridge, attempting transmutation and the discovery of the *menstruum universale* (a solvent that would dissolve any substance)
- His concept of gravitational "force" — action at a distance without mechanical contact — was attacked by Leibniz as an "occult quality" because it required a non-mechanical active principle, which is precisely what alchemical "vegetable spirit" or Paracelsian *quinta essentia* provided

The philosopher John Maynard Keynes, who acquired Newton's alchemical manuscripts at auction in 1936, described Newton as "not the first of the age of reason" but "the last of the magicians."

## Solomon's Temple — Sacred Geometry as Cosmological Cipher

Newton's *The Chronology of Ancient Kingdoms Amended* and extensive unpublished manuscripts treat Solomon's Temple as a cosmological model encoding heliocentric geometry. He:

- Reconstructed the Temple's dimensions from the biblical text (1 Kings, Ezekiel), treating cubit measurements as precise astronomical data
- Argued the Temple's proportional system encoded the distances of the planetary spheres from the Sun
- Placed the eternal fire (*ner tamid*) in the center of the Temple as the structural analogue of the Sun in the heliocentric solar system
- Argued that the Temple's architecture, like Stonehenge and other ancient monuments, preserved heliocentrism before Ptolemy's geocentric corruption

This program connects Newton directly to the prisca theologia tradition: ancient sacred architecture encodes divine-mathematical truth that later ages have corrupted and that the true philosopher must recover.

## The Bible as Scientific Source

Newton's *Observations upon the Prophecies of Daniel and the Apocalypse of St. John* treated the prophetic books not as spiritual literature but as **coded historical-astronomical information**. He:

- Calculated dates for prophetic events using astronomical retrocomputation (stellar precession, eclipse cycles)
- Identified the "little horn" of Daniel with specific historical empires using his own chronological framework
- Treated Revelation's symbolic numbers as cryptographic encodings of real historical sequences

He also did extensive **chronological reconstruction** using astronomical data — computing the date of the Argonauts' voyage, the fall of Troy, and the Exodus from Egypt by working backward from stellar positions described in ancient sources. His *Chronology of Ancient Kingdoms Amended* revised the received ancient chronology substantially.

## MASSIVE WIN — Newton's Principia as Sacred Mathematics

In unpublished scholia to the *Principia* (reconstructed from Gregory's notes, 1694), Newton argued that the ancient Pythagoreans had **already known the inverse-square law of gravity** and encoded it in the music-of-the-spheres doctrine:

> "The Ancients knew that the planets... moved in orbits around the sun as center... They knew that the sesquialteral ratio obtained between periods and distances... [and] Pythagoras symbolized this by the harmonics of the spheres."

The "sesquialteral ratio" (3:2) is Kepler's third law: T² ∝ r³. Newton claims Pythagoras had it. If Newton is right, the chain is: Pythagorean harmonia sphairon (c. 530 BCE) → encoded as musical ratios → lost/corrupted by geocentrism → recovered by Kepler (1619) → systematized by Newton (1687).

In Newton's self-understanding, the *Principia Mathematica* was not a discovery but a **restoration** — the recovery of divine knowledge that the ancients had possessed and transmitted in sacred form.
