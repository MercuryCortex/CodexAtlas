# Al-Biruni

## Identity
Abu Rayhan al-Biruni (973–1048) was a Khwarazmian polymath working in the Ghaznavid court who produced the most thorough account of Indian philosophy, mathematics, and religion ever written by a non-Indian scholar before the modern era. His *Kitāb al-Hind* (Book of India, ~1017–1030 CE), composed after he spent several years in the Punjab learning Sanskrit, is a systematic comparative study of Indian thought — including extended treatment of Shaiva, Vaishnava, and Vedantic metaphysics — that made the substance of Indian non-dual philosophy available in the Arabic intellectual world for the first time.

## Significance as transmission node
Al-Biruni is the critical figure in the hypothesis that **Kashmir Shaivism's consciousness metaphysics reached Islamic philosophy** through Arabic translation. He:
- Translated multiple Sanskrit texts into Arabic, including the *Yoga Sutras* and the *Sāmkhya-kārikā*
- Provided the first Arabic-language account of Shaiva metaphysics, including the non-dual consciousness doctrine
- Explicitly compared Indian metaphysics with Greek philosophy (Neoplatonism, Stoicism) in a comparative framework
- Was read by subsequent Islamic philosophers working in the Persian intellectual world

The transmission hypothesis runs: al-Biruni's *Kitāb al-Hind* → Persian philosophical readers including possibly Suhrawardi (1154–1191) → Suhrawardi's Illuminationist philosophy → Ibn Arabi's circle. No scholar has confirmed a direct citation chain, but al-Biruni is the most plausible Arabic conduit for Shaiva non-dual metaphysics into Islamic philosophy.

## Cross-tradition position
- **← Kashmir Shaivism / Vedanta**: first Arabic transmitter
- **→ Islamic philosophy (Suhrawardi, possibly)**: the *Kitāb al-Hind* was the primary Arabic source on Indian metaphysics for centuries
- **Comparative method**: al-Biruni's explicit cross-tradition comparisons (Indian/Greek) anticipate modern comparative religion by 900 years
