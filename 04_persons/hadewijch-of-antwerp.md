# Hadewijch of Antwerp

## Identity
13th-c. Brabantine Beguine mystic, author of one of the foundational corpora of Middle Dutch vernacular mystical literature. Personal biography poorly attested — her dates (c. 1210–1260) and even her existence as a single author have been periodically debated, but the consensus position (Mommaers 1989, McGinn 1998) treats her as a historical Brabantine Beguine of substantial education, probably from an aristocratic background, active as the leader of a Beguine community somewhere in the Brabantine ecclesiastical-cultural sphere (Antwerp / Brussels area).

## Works
- **Letters (*Brieven*)** — 31 letters of spiritual direction to other Beguines and unnamed correspondents.
- **Visions (*Visioenen*)** — 14 visionary narratives, structured with theological commentary.
- **Strophic Poems (*Strophische Gedichten*)** — 45 lyric poems in *minne*-mystical register. Hadewijch adapts the secular courtly-love verse form of contemporary Brabantine trouvère poetry to mystical-divine content; her *Lady Love* (*Minne*) is the divine Beloved.
- **Mengeldichten** — additional poems; the later items (17-29) are by a separate author ("Hadewijch II" or "Pseudo-Hadewijch") and are sometimes Eckhart-influenced.

## Role in the tradition
- **Foundational figure of Middle Dutch literature.** Hadewijch's verse is one of the earliest substantial bodies of literary Middle Dutch.
- **Brabantine Beguine mysticism.** With Beatrice of Nazareth (1200–1268, author of *Seven Ways of Holy Love*), Hadewijch is the central figure of 13th-c. Brabantine vernacular mysticism. This tradition feeds directly into 14th-c. Flemish mysticism (Ruusbroec).
- **Minne-mysticism.** Hadewijch's adaptation of secular courtly-love forms to mystical content is paradigmatic for the broader Beguine register and influences Eckhart and the Rhineland mystics.

## Disputes
- **Historical existence as single author.** The composite-author hypothesis has been periodically revived but the Mommaers-McGinn consensus position treats Hadewijch as a single historical Brabantine Beguine.
- **Identification with the historical "Hadewijch of Antwerp" recorded in some Brabantine sources.** Probable but not certain.
