# Johannes Reuchlin

## Identity
German humanist, jurist, Greek and Hebrew philologist, and systematizer of Christian Kabbalah (1455–1522). Studied at Freiburg, Paris, Basel, Orléans, and Poitiers; held various legal-administrative posts in the service of the Counts of Württemberg. Reuchlin learned Hebrew from the imperial physician Jacob ben Jehiel Loans (1492) and from Obadiah Sforno; his Hebrew grammar *De Rudimentis Hebraicis* (1506) was the first by a non-Jewish author and enabled Christian Hebrew study. His two Kabbalistic dialogues — *De Verbo Mirifico* (1494) and *De Arte Cabalistica* (1517) — systematize Pico's earlier programmatic Christian Kabbalah into a sustained scholarly account. The "Reuchlin Affair" (1510–1520), in which the converso Johann Pfefferkorn and the Cologne Dominicans demanded the burning of all Jewish books in the Empire and Reuchlin defended Jewish literature, became the central German humanist cause célèbre of the pre-Reformation period.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: extensive correspondence, official records of the Reuchlin Affair (papal, imperial, university), the satirical *Letters of Obscure Men* (1515–1517) produced in his defense, contemporary humanist references.

## Works
- *De Verbo Mirifico* (1494).
- *De Rudimentis Hebraicis* (1506).
- *Augenspiegel* (1511).
- *De Arte Cabalistica* (1517).

## Role in the tradition
Reuchlin is the founder of Christian Hebrew scholarship and the principal systematizer of Christian Kabbalah after Pico. His Christological reading of the Tetragrammaton (YHShVH = Yeshua) became the standard Christian-Kabbalist trope. The Reuchlin Affair galvanized German humanism on the eve of the Reformation; many of Reuchlin's defenders went on to support Luther.
