# Lucian of Samosata

## Identity
A second-century **Syrian-Greek satirist** writing in Atticist Greek, one of the most read and influential authors of the Second Sophistic. To be sharply distinguished from [[lucian-of-antioch]] (the late-3rd-c. Christian scribe). Lucian's work is principally satirical-comic, but two passages bear directly on Christianity:

(1) ***The Death of Peregrinus*** (~+165) — the satirical biography of a wandering Cynic philosopher who at one point joined a Christian community in Palestine, was imprisoned and supported by the Christians, became a sort of itinerant prophet among them, and was eventually denounced. Lucian's portrait of Christians is sociologically rich: they are credulous, generous to a fault, regard their teachers as gods, and "have a brotherhood organized to a man." The earliest sustained pagan ethnographic description of Christian community life.

(2) ***Alexander the False Prophet*** — mentions Christians in passing as opponents of Alexander's oracle alongside Epicureans.

## Historical evidence
Lucian's works survive in extensive Byzantine manuscript tradition. The *Peregrinus* dating and its Christian-community-of-Palestine setting are accepted.

## Works
~80 prose pieces in dialogue, satirical narrative, parody, and rhetorical-essay forms.

## Role in the tradition
- **Second pagan ethnography of Christianity** (after Pliny): independent perspective, mid-2nd-c., from an outsider sympathetic to none of the players. The *Peregrinus* portrait is invaluable — Christians as a tight-knit, mutually supporting, gullibly generous, prophet-following community.
- **The Peregrinus passage on Jesus**: "the man who had been crucified in Palestine for having introduced this new cult into the world" — independent pagan reference to Jesus's crucifixion, parallel to Tacitus.
- **Influence on later European satire**: Erasmus, Voltaire, Lucian as the model of philosophical satire.

## Disputes
- **Lucian's reliability on Christian practice**: probably reasonable, though shaped by genre conventions of philosophical satire.
- **Historical Peregrinus**: a real Cynic philosopher whose self-immolation at Olympia in 165 was contemporary and witnessed; the Christian-phase is plausibly historical.
