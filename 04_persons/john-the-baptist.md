# John the Baptist

## Identity
John the Baptist (Yochanan ben Zechariah) was a Jewish apocalyptic prophet active in the late 20s CE in the Judean wilderness and Jordan valley, summoning Israel to repentance through a water-immersion ritual (*baptisma*) and proclaiming imminent eschatological judgment. He was executed by Herod Antipas at the fortress of Machaerus.

## Historical evidence
**One of the most securely attested figures of early-1st-century Judea.** Three streams of evidence:
1. **Josephus** (*Antiquities* 18.116–119), writing in the 90s CE, gives an independent account: John was a "good man" who exhorted Jews to virtue, baptized them, attracted large crowds, and was killed by Antipas because Herod feared his political influence. This is *independent* of Christian sources — Josephus does not connect John to Jesus or to Christianity.
2. **Q and all four Gospels** report his ministry, his baptism of Jesus, his arrest and execution.
3. **The Mandaeans** of southern Iraq / Iran preserve a continuous religious tradition centered on John as their principal prophet — calling Jesus a false messiah. This independent transmission stream is unique among early-1st-century figures.

The two-source overlap (Josephus + Christian) and the Mandaean tradition together make John's historicity *more* solid than that of Jesus by some metrics (Josephus's mention is undisputed, while *Antiquities* 18.63–64 on Jesus, the *Testimonium Flavianum*, is partially interpolated).

## Works
None directly attributed; his preaching is preserved only in Christian sources (likely with theological framing).

## Role in the tradition
For Christianity: the **Forerunner**, divinely sent to "prepare the way" (Mk 1:2–3 quoting Isa 40:3); his baptism of Jesus is a foundational christological event. Christian theology gradually subordinated John to Jesus, though the New Testament preserves evidence of a continuing "John-the-Baptist movement" parallel to Christianity (Acts 18:25; 19:1–7 — disciples of John who don't know about the Holy Spirit).

For Mandaeism: John is *the* prophet; Jesus is a false messiah who corrupted John's teaching. The Mandaean *Ginza Rabba* and *Sidra d-Yahya* (Book of John) preserve a strikingly anti-Christian polemical tradition rooted in a parallel reception of John.

For Islam: Yahya ibn Zakariyya is a major prophet, mentioned in the Qur'an (Surah 3, 6, 19, 21).

## Disputes
- **Connection to Qumran.** John's wilderness setting, ascetic diet, baptism-of-repentance, and use of Isa 40:3 echo Qumran sectarian patterns. Some scholars hypothesize he was raised at or near Qumran; others see only *shared milieu* of Judean apocalyptic-sectarian Judaism. The shared use of Isa 40:3 is striking (1QS 8:13–14 uses it as the community's foundational text).
- **Mandaean origins.** Whether the Mandaeans descend directly from John-the-Baptist disciples (their own claim) or are a later Jewish-Christian-gnostic syncretism that retrofitted John as ancestor — contested.
- **Reason for execution.** Josephus: political (Antipas feared a revolt). Gospels: John publicly denounced Antipas's marriage to Herodias. Both may be true; Josephus's framing is more historically illuminating.
