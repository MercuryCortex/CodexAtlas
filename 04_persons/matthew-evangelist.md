# Matthew the Evangelist

## Identity
The traditional author of the Gospel of Matthew, identified by Christian tradition with the New Testament figure called "Matthew" — one of Jesus's twelve disciples, who is described as a Galilean tax-collector (*telōnēs*) called by Jesus from his booth. The Synoptic Gospels identify this Matthew with Levi son of Alphaeus (Mk 2:14 / Lk 5:27 = Mt 9:9).

## Historical evidence
**Papias** (~+125): "Matthew compiled the *logia* (sayings/oracles) in the Hebrew dialect (*Hebraïdi dialektō*), and each interpreted them as he was able." This is the earliest external attestation — but the surviving Greek Gospel of Matthew is *not* a translation from a Semitic original (it uses Greek wordplay, quotes the LXX directly, and depends on the Greek Mark). Modern scholarship interprets Papias's *logia*-reference variously: (a) a lost Aramaic sayings-collection now incorporated into the Greek Gospel; (b) an early 2nd-century misidentification; (c) Papias was speaking of testimony-collections of OT prophecies. The Greek Gospel itself is anonymous and clearly composed by a Greek-writing scribe in or near Antioch (~+80/+95). Whether the apostle Matthew stands behind any layer of the tradition is contested; mainstream critical scholarship treats the Gospel as a *Matthean community* product with the apostolic attribution a later tradition. The historical Matthew/Levi the tax-collector is real (multiple-attestation), but his connection to the Greek Gospel that bears his name is disputed.
