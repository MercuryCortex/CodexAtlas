# Suetonius

## Identity
Roman biographer, equestrian, and briefly imperial secretary (*ab epistulis*) under Hadrian. His ***De vita Caesarum / Twelve Caesars*** (~+121) is the principal Latin biography-collection of the early Empire. Three passages bear on Christianity:

(1) ***Claudius* 25.4** — "Since the Jews constantly made disturbances at the instigation of *Chrestus*, he [Claudius] expelled them from Rome." This is the standard datum behind the Roman Jewish expulsion (~+49) and connects to Acts 18:2 (Aquila and Priscilla being among those expelled). Whether "Chrestus" was a confused reference to Christ (the most common interpretation), an unrelated Jewish agitator, or both, is debated.

(2) ***Nero* 16.2** — "Punishments were inflicted on the Christians, a class of men given to a new and mischievous superstition."

(3) ***Domitian* 12.2** — references Jews fined or harassed who "lived a Jewish life without acknowledging that they were Jews" — sometimes read as evidence for Domitianic persecution affecting Christians.

## Historical evidence
*Twelve Caesars* is extant and stable in the manuscript tradition. The Chrestus passage in Claudius 25 is the most debated; mainstream view: probable reference to Christ-related Jewish-Christian unrest in 1st-c. Roman synagogues. The Nero passage is taken as independent corroboration of Tacitus's account.

## Works
- *De vita Caesarum* (12 biographies, complete)
- *De viris illustribus* (biographies of literary figures — partially preserved)

## Role in the tradition
- **Claudius's expulsion of Jews (49)**: anchors Pauline-mission chronology (when [[aquila]] and [[priscilla]] meet Paul at Corinth, Acts 18:2).
- **Christ-as-political-agitator perception**: the Chrestus passage, if it refers to Christ, indicates Roman aristocratic perception of Christianity as politically disruptive — even if the historical Christ predates Claudius by ~20 years (Suetonius's grammar is *present participle*, "at the instigation of," implying a contemporary).
- **Nero passage**: brief but independent.

## Disputes
- **"Chrestus" identification**: probably (mainstream) but not certainly Christ.
- **Reliability of his procedures**: Suetonius is a gossip-prone biographer; specifics need corroboration.
