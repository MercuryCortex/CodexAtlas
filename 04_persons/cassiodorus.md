# Cassiodorus

## Identity
Roman senatorial aristocrat in Ostrogothic Italy. Held the highest civil offices under Theodoric and his successors — quaestor, magister officiorum, praetorian prefect — and edited the official state correspondence as the *Variae*. After the Byzantine reconquest of Italy under Justinian wrecked the late-Roman senatorial world, Cassiodorus retired (~554) to his ancestral estate at Squillace in Calabria, where he founded the monastery of Vivarium with a *scriptorium* devoted to copying and preserving Christian and pagan classical literature. He lived to be approximately 100.

## Historical evidence
The *Variae* are themselves a primary archive of Ostrogothic-Italian administration. The *Institutiones* details Vivarium's library and curriculum. Various medieval manuscripts of classical and patristic texts trace back, via Bobbio and other monasteries, to Vivarium exemplars.

## Works
- ***Variae*** (12 books) — selected official letters / edicts drafted for Theodoric and successors. A primary source for Ostrogothic Italy.
- ***Institutiones divinarum et saecularium litterarum*** — the curriculum and reading list for Vivarium, in two books (divine letters: scripture and patristics; secular letters: the seven liberal arts).
- ***Expositio Psalmorum*** — major commentary on the Psalms, dependent on Augustine but extensive in its own right.
- ***Historia Ecclesiastica Tripartita*** — Latin condensation of the Greek church historians Socrates, Sozomen, Theodoret (made under Cassiodorus's direction by Epiphanius); the main source of post-Eusebius church history known to the Latin West for a millennium.
- ***De anima*** — short Christian-Platonist treatise on the soul.

## Role in the tradition
Pivotal figure of late-antique-to-medieval cultural transmission. The Vivarium project preserves Latin classical and patristic learning by integrating manuscript copying into monastic discipline — a model that Benedictine monasticism (without Cassiodorus's specific institution, which did not long survive) eventually generalized across Europe. Most of what the early Middle Ages read of classical Latin (and translated Greek) literature reaches them through Vivarium-pattern monastic scriptoria.

## Connections — substrate and influence
- The *Institutiones* book II is the bridge by which the late-antique liberal-arts curriculum (*trivium* and *quadrivium*) is handed forward to the medieval Latin West — preserving substantial chunks of pagan classical learning (rhetoric, dialectic, arithmetic, music, geometry, astronomy) inside a Christian monastic syllabus.
- His *Expositio Psalmorum* is methodologically Augustinian — Christian Platonist reading of scripture.
- The *Historia Ecclesiastica Tripartita* transmits Eusebian church-historical method (an event-by-event documentary history of the church-imperial alliance) into the medieval West.
- Vivarium is one of the major channels through which **Greek philosophical and patristic literature reaches Latin Christendom** in translation — Cassiodorus commissioned Latin translations of Josephus, the Greek church historians, and various Greek scientific works.

## Disputes
- **Date of birth.** 485 or slightly later; 490 is also defensible.
- **Survival of Vivarium.** How long the monastery lasted after Cassiodorus's death is unclear; the dispersal of its library is the more important historical question.
