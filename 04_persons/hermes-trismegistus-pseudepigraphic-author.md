# Hermes Trismegistus (pseudepigraphic author)

## Identity
The **legendary / pseudonymous author** of the Hermetic corpus — a body of Greek and Latin philosophical, theological, alchemical, astrological, magical, and medical texts authored by various anonymous late-Hellenistic and Roman-Egyptian writers (~300 BCE – 800 CE), all attributed to a single ancient sage **Hermes Trismegistus** (*Hermes the Thrice-Greatest*) identified through *interpretatio* with both the Greek Hermes and the Egyptian Thoth.

**This entry is distinct from the deity-node [[hermes-trismegistus]]**: the deity-node treats Hermes as a *worshipped* / *invoked* figure within Hermetic cult and religiosity; **this person-node treats Hermes as the *pseudepigraphic author* angle** — the figure under whose name a vast and historically-consequential textual tradition was composed, transmitted, and received.

## Historical evidence (and its limits)
- **No historical Hermes Trismegistus existed** as a single individual. The figure is a Greek-Egyptian syncretic construction (Hermes ↔ Thoth identification) developing from the early Hellenistic period onward.
- The figure functions in the tradition as a **constructed ancient sage** — an antediluvian or near-antediluvian Egyptian priest-king-philosopher whose teachings represent primordial wisdom.
- The *actual* authors of the Hermetic corpus are anonymous late-Hellenistic-to-Roman-Egyptian writers (likely Greek-speaking, with substantial Egyptian-cultural engagement, possibly some Jewish-Hellenistic and proto-Christian elements in selected texts).

## Works (pseudonymously attributed)
- **Philosophical Hermetica** — the *Corpus Hermeticum* (17 Greek tractates; CH I = *Poimandres*; CH XIII = *Discourse on Rebirth*), the *Asclepius* (Latin), the *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth* (Coptic, Nag Hammadi VI.6), the Stobaean fragments, the *Kore Kosmou*.
- **Technical Hermetica** — astrological (the *Hermippus*, *Liber Hermetis*), medical, alchemical (including [[phase-4-073-tabula-smaragdina|Tabula Smaragdina]], the *Kyranides*, alchemical fragments).
- **Magical Hermetica** — passages in the Greek Magical Papyri (PGM).

## Role in the tradition

- **Pivotal figure of the Renaissance *prisca theologia*.** From [[phase-6-001-ficino-pimander|Ficino's 1463 Latin translation]] onward, Hermes Trismegistus is treated as **the original ancient sage** — the foundational figure of the *ancient theology* preserved from primordial revelation, harmonious with biblical truth. Pico della Mirandola, Bruno, Patrizi, Kircher, Fludd all build on this framing.
- **[[event-casaubon-redates-hermetica-1614|Casaubon's 1614 redating]]** dismantles the *prisca theologia* framework: Isaac Casaubon's philological analysis of the *Corpus Hermeticum* demonstrates that the Greek texts are post-Christian (~2nd-3rd c. CE) Greek pseudepigrapha, not antediluvian Egyptian wisdom. **The Hermetic tradition does not collapse after 1614** — but it has to relocate its self-understanding from "ancient pre-Christian wisdom" to "perennial wisdom independent of textual antiquity."
- **Continuous reception 1614–present.** Post-Casaubon Hermeticism (Boehme, Andreae, Khunrath, Maier, Newton's alchemy, Mary Anne Atwood, Theosophy, Jungian alchemy, modern Western esotericism) operates with full knowledge of Casaubon's redating but treats the *content* of the Hermetic tradition as valuable regardless of its actual authorship.

## Disputes
- **Egyptian or Greek origin of the figure?** Greek-receptive of Egyptian; the *Hermes-Thoth* identification is documentable from Ptolemaic Alexandrian context. Fowden 1986.
- **Single composite Hermetic corpus or distinct strands?** Distinct strands — the philosophical-theological and the technical Hermetica may have different authorial milieus. Fowden 1986, Copenhaver 1992.
- **Did the *prisca theologia* survive Casaubon's 1614 redating?** Substantially yes, in modified form. Yates 1964 ch. 21–23 is the foundational treatment of post-Casaubon Hermeticism.
