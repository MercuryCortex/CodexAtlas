# Shaikh Nuruddin Wali (Nund Rishi)

## Identity
Shaikh Nuruddin Wali (1377–1439), known as Nund Rishi ("the saint who is a *ṛṣi*"), is the founder of the Kashmiri Rishi Order — the distinctive Sufi tradition of Kashmir that synthesizes Islamic mysticism with the indigenous Kashmir Shaivism mediated through Lal Ded. He is the patron saint of Kashmir and is revered by both Hindus and Muslims. His *Shrukhs* (oral Kashmiri verse) are described by scholars as "almost identical" in form and doctrinal content to Lal Ded's *Vakhs* — making this one of the most documented cases of Kashmir Shaivism → Sufi transmission in history.

## The Lal Ded connection
Traditional sources record that Nund Rishi was spiritually initiated by Lal Ded at birth — a hagiographic claim that encodes a real doctrinal inheritance: his poetry continues her vocabulary of non-dual recognition, inner temple, and rejection of external religious mediation. The transmission represents the survival of Trika Pratyabhijñā doctrine inside an Islamic mystical framework, 300 years after Abhinavagupta's systematic formulation.

## The Rishi synthesis
The "Rishi" model — a figure who combines Islamic Sufi practice with Hindu ascetic (*ṛṣi*) forms (vegetarianism, forest retreat, non-violence toward animals) — is a uniquely Kashmiri religious synthesis. It has no exact parallel in the broader Islamic world, making it a genuine local tradition shaped by the Shaiva substrate.

## Cross-tradition position
- **← Lal Ded (direct inheritance, Tier-1)**: Kashmiri Shaivism recognition doctrine in vernacular form
- **← Ibn Arabi's Akbarian school (indirect)**: Mir Sayyid Ali Hamadani (d.1385) brought Akbarian Sufism to Kashmir one generation before Nund Rishi, creating an environment of Shaivism + Akbarian non-dualism
- The combination of Lal Ded's Pratyabhijñā and Hamadani's *wahdat al-wujūd* in the same cultural moment makes the Rishi Order a documented node where Kashmir Shaivism and Ibn Arabi's tradition meet
