# Pope Leo I (the Great)

## Identity
Roman aristocrat, deacon, then bishop of Rome 440–461. The architect of the doctrine of papal primacy in its developed Latin form: that the bishop of Rome holds, through Peter (cf. Matthew 16:18), the *plenitudo potestatis* over the universal Church. His *Tome to Flavian* (Letter 28, 449) was acclaimed at the Council of Chalcedon (451) with the cry "Peter has spoken through Leo!" — the high-water mark of papal authority in the patristic age. He met Attila the Hun in 452 and is traditionally credited with persuading him to spare Rome; he met Geiseric the Vandal in 455 and limited the sack of Rome.

## Historical evidence
96 sermons and 143 letters survive, all in Latin, in continuous transmission. The acta of the Robber Council (449) and Chalcedon (451) are primary sources for his theological intervention.

## Works
- ***Tome to Flavian*** (Letter 28, 449) — the foundational Latin Christological text: two distinct natures (divine and human) united in one *persona* in Christ. The Chalcedonian Definition's structure is Leonine.
- ***Sermons*** (96 surviving) — preached at Rome over the liturgical year; defining for Latin liturgical theology.
- ***Letters*** (143 surviving) — administrative and doctrinal, including the *Second Tome* (Letter 165 to Emperor Leo I of Constantinople, 458).

## Role in the tradition
Defining figure of Latin Catholic ecclesiology. The "Petrine" theology of the Roman see, while older in origin, takes systematic Latin form in Leo's sermons and letters. He is one of only three popes called "the Great" (with Gregory I and the modern, unofficial designation for John Paul II).

## Connections — substrate and influence
- His two-natures Christology is the Latin parallel to Cyril's Greek formulation but with more clean separation (cf. the Cyrillian *mia physis*). Both feed into the Chalcedonian compromise; the Eastern non-Chalcedonians reject Leo's Tome.
- Roman political vocabulary — *auctoritas*, *plenitudo potestatis*, *vicarius* — is repurposed for ecclesiastical primacy. The Leonine papacy is partly a Christian translation of Roman imperial ideology onto the Roman see (Green 2008: 195–238).
- His Marian sermons (Sermons 21–30) help establish Roman Marian devotion that resonates with — and is repeatedly compared to — older Mediterranean Mother-Goddess piety (Isis, Cybele, Magna Mater). Direct cultic continuity is contested (Limberis 1994 reads Constantinople; Roberts 1996 reads Rome) but the Mediterranean substrate of Marian devotion is well-attested.

## Disputes
- **Authenticity of certain sermons.** A few of the 96 attributed sermons are now considered dubious.
- **Historicity of the Attila meeting (452).** Prosper of Aquitaine attests it; the political-mythical accretion is later (Raphael's famous fresco shows Peter and Paul descending from heaven to back Leo — purely later legend).
