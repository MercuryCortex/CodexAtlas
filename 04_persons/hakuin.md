# Hakuin Ekaku

## Identity
Major 18th-century Japanese Rinzai Zen master, reformer of the Rinzai *kōan*-curriculum, voluminous writer and prolific Zen painter and calligrapher. Hakuin systematized the Rinzai post-kenshō curriculum, devising the schema of progressively penetrating kōan sequences leading from initial *kenshō* (seeing-into-nature) through deeper realization to the eventual *bodhisattva* concern with helping others. Modern Rinzai Zen worldwide is substantially Hakuin's lineage; almost all modern Rinzai roshis trace their lineage through him. His "What is the sound of one hand?" (*sekishu no onjō*) is one of the most famous kōans in the world. He is also celebrated as one of Japan's great painters in the Zen *zenga* tradition.

## Historical evidence
Substantial: his own autobiographical works (*Wild Ivy*, *Yasenkanna*), Tokugawa-period Rinzai records, his extensive surviving paintings, and the continuous lineage transmission.

## Works
- *Orategama* — Hakuin's letters to lay disciples; doctrinal compendium.
- *Yasenkanna* (*Idle Talk on a Night Boat*) — autobiographical-pedagogical work; describes the *naikan* internal-energy practice he developed to recover from severe meditation-induced illness.
- *Sokkō-roku Kaien-fusetsu* — formal commentary on the *Recorded Sayings of Xutang Zhiyu* (Sokkō); the major Rinzai philosophical-pedagogical work.
- "Song of Zazen" (*Zazen Wasan*) — short popular verse summary, widely chanted in Rinzai temples.

## Role in the tradition
Hakuin is *the* major modern shaper of Rinzai Zen — his kōan-curriculum, his pedagogical methods, his lay-instruction emphasis, his recovery of vital practice in a Tokugawa Rinzai he diagnosed as decadent.
