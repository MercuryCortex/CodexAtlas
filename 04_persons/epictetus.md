# Epictetus

## Identity
Stoic philosopher, born a slave in Phrygia c. 55 CE, brought to Rome, eventually freed and established as a teacher; expelled with other philosophers under Domitian (89 CE), settled in Nicopolis (northwest Greece) where he ran a school until his death c. 135. Epictetus wrote nothing himself; what we have are the *Discourses* and the shorter *Enchiridion* (Handbook), recorded by his student Arrian. The classical formulation of practical Stoicism centered on the *dichotomy of control* — *some things are up to us, some are not* (*Enchiridion* 1) — and *prohairesis* (rational moral purpose) as the locus of human freedom.

## Historical evidence
Documented through Arrian's records, Marcus Aurelius's references in the *Meditations*, and the doxographic tradition.

## Works
- *Discourses* (originally 8 books; 4 survive).
- *Enchiridion* (Handbook) — a programmatic summary.
- *Fragments* preserved in Stobaeus and others.

## Role in the tradition
Marcus Aurelius's *Meditations* repeatedly quote and engage Epictetus; the Roman Stoa runs through him. Christian-monastic reception (the *Enchiridion* was Christianized as *Encheiridion Aviator* and circulated in monastic settings). The Enlightenment and modern Stoa-revival (Pierre Hadot, William Irvine) take Epictetus as the practical exemplar.
