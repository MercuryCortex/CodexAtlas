Philolaus is the **first Pythagorean to commit the tradition to writing**, breaking the oral-secret rule of the early community after the anti-Pythagorean uprising at Croton (c. 450 BCE) scattered its members. His *Peri Physeos* fragments are the earliest authentic written record of Pythagorean cosmology and number theory. Key doctrines: the cosmos revolves around a **Central Fire** (not the earth) at its center — the first heliocentric-adjacent cosmology in Greek philosophy (the sun merely reflects the Central Fire's light); the **Counter-Earth** (Antichthon) as a 10th body balancing the heavens to make the total a perfect Pythagorean decad; **limiters and unlimiteds** as the two fundamental principles from which all things are composed, united by **harmonia** (proportion/number). [[plato]]'s *Phaedo* reportedly uses Philolaus's material (Socrates mentions Philolaus living in Thebes). [[pythagoras]] is the tradition-founder; Philolaus is the tradition's first author.
