# Joseph (father of Jesus)

## Identity
The legal father of Jesus per the canonical infancy narratives (Mt 1–2; Lk 1–2). Matthew presents him as a *tzaddik* (righteous man) of Davidic descent who initially intended to divorce Mary quietly upon learning of her pregnancy, but who, instructed by an angel in a dream, took her as wife and named the child Jesus. Luke gives an independent infancy narrative also stressing Davidic descent (Lk 2:4). The Gospel of Mark, our earliest, does not mention Joseph by name (Mk 6:3 calls Jesus "the son of Mary" — striking matronymic).

## Historical evidence
Indirect: the Matthean and Lukan infancy narratives are theologically shaped and frequently divergent, with strong infancy-tradition development between Mark (no Joseph) and Matthew/Luke (extensive Joseph material). Critical scholarship considers Joseph's historical existence likely but his specific narrative material (dreams, flight to Egypt, return) heavily theologized. He is presumed dead by the time of Jesus's ministry (he is never mentioned during Jesus's adult activity).

## Works
None. Two apocryphal traditions develop him: (a) the *Protoevangelium of James* (mid-2nd c.) makes him an elderly widower with adult sons from a previous marriage — a literary maneuver to preserve the perpetual virginity of Mary; (b) the *History of Joseph the Carpenter* (4th–7th c.) gives an extensive deathbed narrative.

## Role in the tradition
**Catholic and Orthodox tradition**: elevated saint, patron of workers, the universal Church. The Christmas Nativity tableau is unimaginable without him.

**Critical historical reconstruction**: a Galilean Jewish artisan (*tektōn* — carpenter or general builder), of presumed Davidic descent (the Davidic genealogies of Mt 1 and Lk 3 differ irreconcilably), legal father of Jesus.

## Disputes
- **Davidic descent.** The two NT genealogies (Mt 1 traces through Solomon; Lk 3 through Nathan) cannot be harmonized without strain.
- **Brothers and sisters of Jesus.** Were Jesus's siblings (Mk 6:3) (a) sons/daughters of Joseph and Mary [Helvidian view, modern Protestant default]; (b) children of Joseph from a prior marriage [Epiphanian, Orthodox]; (c) cousins [Hieronymian, Catholic]? This is the famous "brothers of Jesus" problem.
- **Date and circumstances of death.** Not recorded canonically; presumed died before Jesus's ministry.
