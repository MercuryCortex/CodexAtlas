# Birger A. Pearson

## Identity
American historian of religion (b. 1934); Professor Emeritus of Religious Studies at the University of California, Santa Barbara. Trained at Harvard (PhD 1968, under Krister Stendahl and Helmut Koester, in the same generation as [[elaine-pagels]]). Editor of multiple volumes in the *Nag Hammadi Studies* / *Coptic Gnostic Library* series (Brill), including the critical editions of Nag Hammadi Codices IX and X.

## Works
See texts-authored. *Gnosticism, Judaism, and Egyptian Christianity* (1990) is a landmark collection arguing that several Sethian Gnostic texts (especially the *Apocalypse of Adam*, [[phase-4-007-on-the-origin-of-the-world]], [[phase-4-006-hypostasis-of-the-archons]], [[phase-4-008-trimorphic-protennoia]]) preserve Jewish-pre-Christian roots — that Gnosticism has substantial Jewish origins independent of Christianity. *Ancient Gnosticism* (2007) is the textbook synthesis.

## Role in the field
Foundational figure in the post-1945 Coptic Gnostic Library project (Robinson, Pearson, Wisse, Layton, Turner, Pagels). His "Jewish origins" thesis is one of the major late-20th-century scholarly positions on Gnostic origins, debated alongside the older "Christian heretical" thesis (Harnack), the "Iranian/Zoroastrian" thesis (Bousset, Reitzenstein, [[hans-jonas]]), and the more recent "Platonizing" thesis ([[john-d-turner]]).

## Disputes
- **Origins of Sethian Gnosticism.** Pearson (Jewish/pre-Christian) vs. Turner (Platonic-philosophical) vs. earlier hypotheses. The dispute is unresolved; texts likely have multiple substrates.

## Connections
- Co-architect with [[john-d-turner]] of the Brill Nag Hammadi critical-edition project that made the Coptic library scholarly-accessible after [[event-nag-hammadi-discovery]].
- Sets the stage for [[david-brakke]]'s 2010 historiographical re-framing of "Gnostic" categories.
