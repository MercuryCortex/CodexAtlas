# Gemistos Plethon

## Identity

**George Gemistos Plethon** (c. 1355–1452) is one of the most consequential and least-known figures in the history of Western thought — **the direct transmission link between Byzantine Neoplatonism and the Italian Renaissance**. He was a Byzantine philosopher who had abandoned Christianity for a thoroughgoing return to Hellenic Neoplatonism (he privately worshipped the Olympian gods using Proclus's *Chaldean Oracles*-based rituals), and who, at the **Council of Florence (1439)**, sparked in Cosimo de' Medici the enthusiasm that led directly to Marsilio Ficino's Platonic Academy and the entire Hermetic Renaissance project.

His name "Plethon" is a Hellenized form of his actual name "Pletho" — itself a play on "Plato" — signaling his explicit identification with Plato as the master he was reviving.

## The Transmission Link

Plethon is the **critical node** between two eras:
- **He inherits**: the Byzantine Neoplatonic tradition (Proclus → Olympiodorus → the Byzantine Platonic commentators) — a living, if embattled, tradition of Hellenic philosophy preserved in Constantinople's intellectual culture
- **He transmits**: to Cosimo de' Medici and the Italian humanists the idea that Platonic philosophy was not merely an academic heritage but a living religious-philosophical practice

Without Plethon's presentation at Florence in 1439, there is no Platonic Academy, no Ficino, and no Hermetic Renaissance as we know it.

## At the Council of Florence (1439)

Plethon traveled to Florence as part of the Byzantine delegation to the **Council of Ferrara-Florence** (the attempt to reunite Eastern and Western Christendom before the Ottoman threat). He was 83-84 years old. While the official council agenda was ecclesiastical reunion, Plethon used his time in Florence to present Platonic philosophy to the Italian humanist court.

His lectures on the differences between Plato and Aristotle (summarized in his text *De Differentiis*) electrified the Florentine intellectual world. Cosimo de' Medici, then the effective ruler of Florence, was so captivated that — according to the tradition recorded in later sources — he conceived the idea of founding a Platonic Academy on the spot.

**The direct chain:**
- Plethon lectures at Florence → 1439
- Cosimo founds the Platonic Academy, installs Ficino as its head → c. 1459
- Greek manuscripts of the *Corpus Hermeticum* arrive in Florence from Macedonia → 1462
- Cosimo orders Ficino to stop translating Plato and translate Hermes first → 1463
- Ficino's Latin *Pimander* published → 1471

## The Pagan Neoplatonist

Plethon's intellectual system was extraordinary and, for his time, dangerous. In his *Nomoi* (Book of Laws) — a text his student Bessarion burned after his death to protect his reputation — Plethon proposed replacing Christianity with a reformed Hellenic polytheism based on Platonic theology:
- The Olympian gods as manifestations of the Neoplatonic divine hierarchy (from the One down through Nous to the gods)
- Zoroaster as the founding sage of the *prisca theologia* chain (predating Moses and matching Hermes as primordial revealers)
- A liturgical calendar of Platonic-pagan festivals
- The goal of **henosis** (union with the divine) through philosophical contemplation

This makes Plethon the only serious case in the medieval/Renaissance period of a philosopher who was not merely influenced by Neoplatonism but **actually tried to practice ancient Greek religion** as a living system.

## The Zoraster Connection

Plethon believed he had access to authentic Zoroastrian teaching via the *Chaldean Oracles* (late antique texts attributed to Julian the Chaldean) — and placed Zoroaster at the head of his *prisca theologia* chain:

**Plethon's chain**: Zoroaster → Pythagoras → Plato → Neoplatonic tradition → the universal religion

This differs from Ficino's chain (which puts Hermes Trismegistus first) but uses the same logical structure. Plethon was likely the source from which Ficino first encountered the idea of the *prisca theologia* as a transmission chain.

## Significance

Plethon matters for the vault as:
- The **direct human transmission link** between Byzantine Neoplatonism and Italian Renaissance Hermeticism
- The proof that the Neoplatonic tradition was never fully extinguished — it survived in Byzantine Constantinople and Mystras until the Turkish conquest (1453) forced its carriers westward
- The demonstration that the *prisca theologia* idea (all wisdom traditions share a single primordial revelation) was being articulated in a serious philosophical framework before Ficino systematized it
- The **last figure in the living Hellenic philosophical tradition** before it becomes the "ancient wisdom" of Renaissance scholarship rather than a practiced discipline
