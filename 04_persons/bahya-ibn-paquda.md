# Bahya ibn Paquda

## Identity
Baḥyā ben Yosef ibn Paqūdā (~1040–1120) was an Andalusian Jewish philosopher and pietist of Zaragoza, author of the *Ḥovot ha-Levavot* (Duties of the Heart) — the most-read medieval Jewish ethical-pietistic work. The book argues that Jewish observance has two complementary dimensions: the *duties of the limbs* (external ritual and ethical acts, the domain of standard halakhic literature) and the *duties of the heart* (interior dispositions — *kavvanah*, gratitude, love, fear, trust in God, repentance, self-examination, contemplation) — and concentrates on the latter, neglected dimension. The work draws heavily on Sufi ethical-spiritual literature (Bahya cites unnamed Muslim sources he calls *ḥakhmei ha-umot*, "the sages of the nations") and on Neoplatonist apparatus.

## Historical evidence
Limited biographical detail; chiefly known through the *Ḥovot ha-Levavot* itself.

## Works
*Ḥovot ha-Levavot* (Judeo-Arabic original ~1080; Hebrew translation Yehudah ibn Tibbon 1180); a smaller liturgical-poetic corpus.

## Role in the tradition
The *Ḥovot ha-Levavot* became the standard Jewish ethical-pietistic work of the medieval period and beyond, continuously studied in yeshivot down to the present. It is one of the principal vectors for the integration of Sufi ethical-spiritual sensibilities into mainstream Jewish ethical literature.
