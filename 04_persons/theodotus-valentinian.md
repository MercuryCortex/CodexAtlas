# Theodotus the Valentinian

## Identity
A second-century **Eastern-school Valentinian** teacher whose lectures or notes were preserved in part by [[clement-of-alexandria]] in the collection known as the ***Excerpta ex Theodoto*** — a set of 86 numbered passages, mostly Theodotus's positions interspersed with Clement's annotations. The Eastern Valentinian school (Hippolytus's distinction, *Ref.* 6.35) taught a *pneumatic* (spiritual) Christ from conception, in distinction from the Italian school's psychic-becoming-pneumatic Christology. Theodotus is our principal direct witness to the Eastern school.

## Historical evidence
Clement's *Excerpta* is the only substantial source. The compilation is uneven — some excerpts are direct Theodotus, others Clement's commentary, others apparently from other Eastern Valentinians — and the textual demarcation has been a major task of editors (Casey, Sagnard).

## Works
None survives independently. The *Excerpta ex Theodoto* preserves his views on pleromatology, the divine syzygies, the descent of Sophia, the formation of Christ's body, the rite of *apolutrōsis* ("redemption" — a Valentinian sacrament of release from the archons), and the eschatological reintegration of pneumatic souls into the Pleroma.

## Role in the tradition
- **Direct Eastern-Valentinian witness**: where Heracleon represents the Italian school, Theodotus represents the Eastern.
- **Sacramental theology**: the *Excerpta* are our best evidence for Valentinian rites — bridal-chamber language, *apolutrōsis*, the seal.
- **Clement's interlocutor**: Clement's engagement is *closer* to dialogue than to Irenaeus's polemics; the *Excerpta* are critical (probably) but the lower-temperature treatment makes them more reliable as evidence for Theodotus's actual views.

## Disputes
- **What in the *Excerpta* is Theodotus and what is other Eastern Valentinians or Clement himself.** Editorial reconstruction is the principal scholarly task.
- **Location.** Alexandria probable but not certain.
- **Date.** ~+160/+185; the Clement composition-date constrains the *terminus ad quem*.
