# Cosimo de' Medici (the Elder)

## Identity
Florentine banker, political leader, and patron of the Florentine Renaissance (1389–1464). Head of the Medici bank from 1429; effective ruler of Florence from 1434 until his death. Cosimo's cultural patronage transformed Florence and established the model of princely-mercantile cultural sponsorship that would shape European patronage for centuries: he funded the rebuilding of San Lorenzo, commissioned Donatello's sculpture and Brunelleschi's architecture, supported the manuscript-acquisition program that built one of the great Renaissance libraries (subsequently the core of the Medicea Laurenziana), and — most consequentially for this vault — patronized Marsilio Ficino. Cosimo provided Ficino with a villa at Careggi outside Florence as the seat of the informal Platonic Academy; commissioned the complete Latin Plato; and (per Ficino's own account) interrupted that Plato translation to demand the Hermetica be translated first when his agent Leonardo da Pistoia brought a Greek manuscript of the *Corpus Hermeticum* from Macedonia to Florence in 1460. The 1463 *Pimander* translation — the founding act of Renaissance Hermeticism — was completed under Cosimo's specific patronage in the year of his death.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: Medici bank records, Florentine civic and ecclesiastical records, contemporary references, his own surviving correspondence and library inventories.

## Works
None. As patron rather than author.

## Role in the tradition
Cosimo is the indispensable patronal figure for Renaissance Hermeticism. The specific intervention — ordering Ficino to translate Hermes before Plato — is the historical hinge at which Phase 4 Hermetic content begins its Phase 6 retrieval.
