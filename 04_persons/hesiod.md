# Hesiod

## Identity
Hesiod is the second great Archaic Greek poet (after, or just after, Homer), a Boeotian rural smallholder who composed the Theogony (systematic genealogy of the gods) and Works and Days (didactic agricultural-ethical poem addressed to his brother Perses). Unlike Homer, Hesiod *names himself* in his poems (Theog. 22 — "the Muses on Helicon taught Hesiod beautiful song while he was tending sheep") and supplies autobiographical material: father an emigrant from Cyme in Aeolia who settled in Ascra; quarrel with brother Perses over inheritance.

## Historical evidence
Internal self-naming and autobiographical material make Hesiod's historicity considerably better-attested than Homer's. Herodotus 2.53 treats him as roughly contemporary with Homer, slightly later. No external inscription or document; the texts are the evidence.

## Works
- Authored: Theogony (~1,022 lines), Works and Days (~828 lines).
- Disputed: the Shield of Heracles (largely rejected as Hesiodic), the Catalogue of Women / *Ehoiai* (partly preserved, of mixed authorship).
- Lost: various fragments under the "Hesiodic" name in ancient catalogues.

## Role in the tradition
Pair with Homer as the foundational poets who, per Herodotus, "made the Greeks' theogony, gave the gods their epithets, distinguished their honors and skills, and indicated their forms." Hesiod's systematization of the divine genealogy is the principal *systematic* theological text of pre-philosophical Greece. The Five Ages myth (gold, silver, bronze, heroic, iron) became a Western archetype. Hesiod is the first European poet who *thinks* — Pre-Socratic philosophy in part proceeds from extending and critiquing his cosmogony.

## Disputes
- **Date.** Most scholars: ~700–680 BCE, slightly later than the Iliad.
- **Authorship of the Catalogue of Women, Shield, etc.** Disputed; current consensus rejects most of the broader "Hesiodic" corpus as later.
- **Historicity of the Mount Helicon vision.** Generally accepted as a literary persona-making device, perhaps reflecting genuine local cult of the Muses.
