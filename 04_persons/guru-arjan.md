# Guru Arjan

## Identity
Fifth Sikh Guru, compiler of the Adi Granth (1604), and the first Sikh martyr (1563–1606). Son of Guru Ram Das; succeeded as Guru in 1581. Under his leadership Amritsar was developed as the central Sikh sacred city; the Harmandir Sahib (Golden Temple) was built and the Adi Granth — the founding compilation of Sikh scripture — was prepared by Arjan and the scribe Bhai Gurdas at Ramsar near Amritsar and installed in the Harmandir Sahib on 16 August 1604. The compilation drew on the earlier Goindval Pothis prepared under the third Guru Amar Das, the compositions of Guru Nanak, Angad, Amar Das, Ram Das, and Arjan himself, and *bhagat bani* of fifteen Hindu and Muslim sant-poets including Kabir, Ravidas, Namdev, and Sheikh Farid. Arjan was arrested by Mughal Emperor Jahangir in 1606 on charges related to political-religious activity (Jahangir's *Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri* memoirs are the principal source) and tortured to death at Lahore in late May 1606 — the founding martyrdom of the Sikh tradition, which thereafter increasingly developed a martial dimension. His son Guru Hargobind initiated the principle of *miri-piri* (temporal and spiritual sovereignty) in direct response.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: the 1604 compilation itself (the Kartarpur Bir), Bhai Gurdas's *Vars* (contemporary praise compositions), Mughal records including Jahangir's memoirs, Sikh hagiographical literature.

## Works
*Sukhmani Sahib* (the "Pearl of Peace" — a long meditational composition that is one of the most-recited Sikh devotional texts), *Bavan Akhri*, parts of *Anand Sahib*, hundreds of *shabads* in multiple *rāg*s. His compositions are the largest single Guru-block in the Adi Granth.

## Role in the tradition
The compiler of the founding Sikh scripture and the first Sikh martyr. The 1604 Adi Granth made Sikhism a textual religion with a fixed canonical scriptural foundation, while Arjan's 1606 martyrdom oriented the community toward the militant-protective stance that Guru Gobind Singh would consolidate.
