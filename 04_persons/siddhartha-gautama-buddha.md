# Siddhārtha Gautama (the Buddha)

## Identity
Siddhārtha Gautama, known to his tradition as the Buddha ("Awakened One") or Śākyamuni ("Sage of the Śākyas"), was a north Indian ascetic teacher who founded what became the Buddhist tradition. Born into the Śākya clan in or near Lumbini (in modern southern Nepal), he renounced his aristocratic life, sought liberation through ascetic practice with various śramaṇa teachers, abandoned extreme asceticism, achieved awakening under the bodhi tree at Bodh Gaya, taught for ~45 years across Magadha and Kosala, and died at Kuśinagara at age ~80.

## Historical evidence
The Buddha's historicity is well-supported by multiple lines of evidence: (a) the personal voice in the earliest suttas (Aṭṭhakavagga, Pārāyanavagga); (b) parallel testimony in Jain sources that mention him as a contemporary rival teacher; (c) the [[phase-3-030-asokan-edicts|Aśokan Lumbinī pillar]] (~-249 BCE) commemorating his birthplace by [[asoka-maurya|Aśoka]] in person, ~150 years after his death — **independent epigraphic confirmation**; (d) the consistent geographic and political details (named contemporary kings Bimbisāra and Ajātaśatru, specific cities and clans) that fit the recoverable history of the period.

## Works
The Buddha did not personally compose any text. His teachings were memorized and transmitted orally by his disciples and the monastic community for ~400 years before being written down. The Pali Tipiṭaka (Suttapiṭaka, Vinayapiṭaka, Abhidhammapiṭaka) and the Chinese Āgamas preserve overlapping early material. The Aṭṭhakavagga and Pārāyanavagga of the Sutta Nipāta are widely regarded as the oldest stratum closest to his actual teaching style and content.

## Role in the tradition
Founder, exemplar, and ultimate authority. Later Buddhist traditions vary on his metaphysical status: in early Theravāda he is a fully awakened human; in developed Mahāyāna he is one of many buddhas, with cosmic and trans-historical aspects; in some traditions he becomes a quasi-eternal cosmic principle.

## Disputes
- **Dates.** Traditional Theravāda: ~-563 to -483. "Corrected long chronology" (Bechert et al. 1991–1997): ~-480 to -400. Most current scholarship favors the later dating.
- **Reliability of biographical detail.** Birthplace (Lumbini) attested by Aśoka, broadly accepted. Detailed life-stories (four sights, palace abandonment, etc.) are partly hagiographic.
- **Relationship to the Upaniṣads.** Whether the Buddha's *anattā* doctrine engages a developed Upaniṣadic *ātman* doctrine (Gombrich) or a non-Vedic śramaṇa milieu (Bronkhorst) is debated.
