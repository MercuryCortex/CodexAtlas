# Shah Wali Allah of Delhi

## Identity
Mughal-Indian Sunni theologian, jurist, hadith scholar, and Naqshbandi-Sufi reformer (1703–1762); the principal intellectual figure of 18th-century Indian Sunni Islam and the source of the Deobandi reform tradition. Born at Delhi to Shah 'Abd al-Rahim, founder of the Madrasa-i Rahimiyya. Studied with his father; performed Hajj 1731–1732 and studied with leading Medinese hadith scholars (Abu Tahir al-Kurani); returned to Delhi to inherit and transform the family madrasa. His reform program combined: (1) re-grounding Sunni jurisprudence in hadith and in rational anthropological-sociological analysis of the law's rationales (*asrār al-shari'a*); (2) preserving Sufi metaphysics with a sophisticated mediation of Ibn 'Arabi's *waḥdat al-wujūd* and Ahmad Sirhindi's *waḥdat al-shuhūd*; (3) producing the first Persian Quran translation by a Sunni Indian author (allowing direct Quranic access for the Persianate Indian Muslim public); and (4) responding intellectually to the political collapse of the Mughal order with religious-reform proposals. His four sons (especially Shah 'Abd al-'Aziz) continued the school; the Deoband seminary (founded 1867 by his intellectual descendants) is the principal modern institutional vehicle of his tradition.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own substantial Persian and Arabic corpus, contemporary references, the Naqshbandi-mujaddidi tradition's textual transmission, and continuous Madrasa-i Rahimiyya → Deobandi documentation.

## Works
*Hujjat Allah al-Baligha* (Arabic). *Fuyuz al-Haramayn* (Persian — visions during his Medinese stay). *Tafhimat-i Ilahiyya* (Persian — divine inspirations). *Sata'at* and *Lama'at* (Persian — short Sufi treatises). *Al-Budur al-Bazigha*. *Izalat al-Khafa' an Khilafat al-Khulafa'*. *Al-Insaf fi Bayan Sabab al-Ikhtilaf*. Persian Quran translation (*Fath al-Rahman*).

## Role in the tradition
The single most influential figure in 18th-century Indian Sunni Islam. The Deobandi movement (1867+), modern Indian-Pakistani Islamic reformist thought (Sayyid Ahmad Khan, Muhammad Iqbal, Mawdudi), and contemporary South Asian Sunni religious-political discourse all descend from his school.
