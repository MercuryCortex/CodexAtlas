# William Matthew Flinders Petrie

## The man who measured the pyramids

**Flinders Petrie** (1853–1942) is the most important figure in the history of pyramid scholarship and one of the founders of modern scientific archaeology. His 1883 survey of Giza — *The Pyramids and Temples of Gizeh* — established the baseline measurements that all subsequent pyramid research references and demolished the "Pyramid Inch" pseudo-metrology that had dominated popular pyramid scholarship for decades.

Petrie learned surveying from his father and arrived in Egypt in 1880 with the primary goal of precisely measuring the Great Pyramid to test **Charles Piazzi Smyth**'s claims. He spent 1880–1882 at Giza measuring every accessible surface with instruments of his own design, achieving accuracy to fractions of an inch at distances of hundreds of feet.

## The debunking of the Pyramid Inch

**John Taylor** (1859) and **Charles Piazzi Smyth** (Astronomer Royal of Scotland; 1864–1867) had claimed that the Great Pyramid's dimensions encoded:
- A unit called the "Pyramid Inch" (1/500,000,000 of the Earth's polar axis) which, multiplied by 25, gave the "Sacred Cubit"
- The value of π in the ratio of the pyramid's perimeter to height
- The distance from the Earth to the Sun encoded in the height (in Pyramid Inches × 10⁹)
- The precession of the equinoxes in the Grand Gallery's length

Piazzi Smyth's measurements had been taken from ambiguous features (the edge of broken socket stones; the presumed original height of the apex) with instruments of uncertain accuracy.

**Petrie's finding:** When he precisely measured the pyramid's base, he found that Piazzi Smyth had been **wrong by several inches** in key measurements — enough to invalidate the claimed mathematical encodings. Petrie wrote in his field notes: "It is useless to state the exact result obtained by Piazzi Smyth, as all his determinations rest on a false principle... the 'Pyramid Inch' is a thing of the imagination."

This did not end the Pyramid Inch tradition — it continues today in alternative-history literature — but Petrie's measurements are the scientific benchmark, and his refutation has never been answered with counter-measurements.

## His broader contributions

Petrie excavated over 40 sites in Egypt and Palestine over a 45-year career. He is credited with:
- **Sequence dating** (seriation): using pottery styles to establish relative chronology before radiocarbon dating — a methodology that transformed the field
- **Systematic documentation**: publishing full field reports with drawings, photographs, and precise measurements rather than simply extracting objects
- **Identification of pre-Dynastic Egypt**: his excavations at Naqada (1894–1895) revealed the pre-Dynastic Naqadian culture, extending Egyptian civilization's documented history by 1,500+ years

He established the **British School of Archaeology in Egypt** (1905) and trained a generation of archaeologists. His personal collection forms the core of the **Petrie Museum** at University College London.

## On alternative interpretations

Petrie's measured response to pyramid mysticism is a model of scientific engagement: he didn't simply dismiss the claims — he measured against them and showed precisely where they failed. His attitude toward the Great Pyramid as a monument was one of profound respect for its builders' achievements on purely human engineering grounds: "The mere fact that it exists shows us what men can do with their hands and brains."
