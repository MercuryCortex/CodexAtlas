# Utpaladeva

## Identity
Kashmir Shaiva philosopher and poet (~900–950); founder of the *Pratyabhijñā* ("Recognition") school of non-dualist Tantric Shaivism, and direct grandteacher of [[abhinavagupta]] (Utpaladeva → Lakṣmaṇagupta → Abhinavagupta). Pupil of Somānanda (whose *Śivadṛṣṭi* is the proto-Pratyabhijñā text); transformed his teacher's polemical Śaiva idealism into a rigorous philosophical system. Composed the four-part *Īśvarapratyabhijñā-kārikā* (the verse-text), the *vṛtti* (his own short commentary), and (lost) a *vivṛti* (long commentary) — Abhinavagupta's *Īśvara-Pratyabhijñā-Vimarśinī* and *Īśvara-Pratyabhijñā-Vivṛti-Vimarśinī* are the surviving commentarial elaboration. Also composed the *Śivastotrāvalī* — one of the great poetic-devotional collections of Kashmir Shaivism.

## Works
- ***Īśvarapratyabhijñā-kārikā*** (Stanzas on the Recognition of the Lord): ~190 verses in 4 books. The foundational text of Pratyabhijñā philosophy. Argues that liberation is the *recognition* (*pratyabhijñā*) that one's individual self is in fact the supreme Śiva-consciousness — the cognitive-mystical event of realization, not the cultivation of new states.
- ***Īśvarapratyabhijñā-vṛtti***: Utpaladeva's own short auto-commentary.
- ***Śivastotrāvalī*** (Garland of Hymns to Śiva): ~20 hymns; intensely personal devotional poetry from within the non-dualist framework. The devotional-philosophical coherence is itself instructive — Utpaladeva loves Śiva *and* knows Śiva is himself.
- ***Ajaḍapramātṛ-siddhi***, ***Sambandhasiddhi***, ***Īśvara-siddhi***: three short philosophical *siddhi*-treatises arguing for, respectively, the non-inert nature of the cognizing subject, the structure of relation, and the existence of God.

## Role in the tradition
- **Pratyabhijñā philosophy founder**: the school's name comes from his *Īśvarapratyabhijñā-kārikā*. Pratyabhijñā became the principal philosophical wing of non-dualist Kashmir Shaivism, distinct from the more ritual-oriented Trika and Krama wings but doctrinally compatible. Abhinavagupta synthesizes the three.
- **Grandteacher of Abhinavagupta**: the lineage Somānanda → Utpaladeva → Lakṣmaṇagupta → Abhinavagupta is the central Kashmir Shaiva philosophical pedigree.
- **Cognitive theory**: Utpaladeva's analysis of consciousness as inherently self-luminous (*svaprakāśa*) and constitutively reflexive (*ahaṃ-vimarśa* — "I-recognition") is one of the most sophisticated Indian theories of consciousness, with renewed contemporary attention from philosophers of mind (Ram-Prasad, Watson, Ratié).

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Buddhist Pramāṇa engagement**: the *Īśvarapratyabhijñā-kārikā* is in constant dialogue with the Buddhist epistemological school (Dignāga, Dharmakīrti, Dharmottara). Utpaladeva accepts much of the Buddhist analytic apparatus (perception, inference, sensation-modes) but argues that the Buddhist denial of a substantial self mistakes the cognitive structure of awareness. Torella 1994 and Ratié 2011 trace the Buddhist-Śaiva intellectual exchange in detail — one of the most intellectually generative cross-tradition philosophical debates of medieval India.
- **Cross-tradition non-dualism comparative**: Utpaladeva's *saṃvit-advaita* (consciousness-non-dualism) stands alongside Shankara's Vedāntic Brahman-non-dualism, the Buddhist Yogācāra "consciousness-only" position (Vasubandhu, Asaṅga), the Plotinian Neoplatonic *henōsis* with the One, and certain forms of Sufi *waḥdat al-wujūd* (especially [[ibn-arabi]]'s) as five different medieval-Eurasian articulations of the position that ultimate reality is a single, self-aware, immaterial unity. The structural parallels are striking; the philosophical-historical question of whether there is any traceable transmission (most likely: Buddhist Yogācāra ↔ Pratyabhijñā within South Asia; Pratyabhijñā / Vedānta substantively independent of late-antique Mediterranean) is largely settled in favor of independent development within South Asia and substantive independence from the Mediterranean.
- **Continuous transmission to the 20th c.**: the Pratyabhijñā philosophical lineage attenuated after the Mughal period but survived in narrow Kashmiri pandit lineages, culminating in the 20th-c. teacher Swami Lakshman Joo (1907–1991), through whom modern Western scholars (Sanderson, Dyczkowski, Muller-Ortega, Bailly) recovered the tradition.

## Disputes
- **Dates**: ~900–950 is the standard placement based on Abhinavagupta's chronology and citation patterns; uncertainty remains.
- **The lost *Vivṛti***: Utpaladeva's long auto-commentary on the *Īśvarapratyabhijñā-kārikā* survives only in fragments recoverable from Abhinavagupta's commentary on it; recent textual work (Torella, Ratié) has substantially reconstructed it.
