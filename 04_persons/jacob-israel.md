# Jacob / Israel

## Identity
Second son of Isaac and Rebekah, twin of Esau; the patriarch from whom the twelve tribes descend and to whom Yahweh gives the renamed *Yisrael* ("strives with God") after the night-wrestling at Penuel (Gen 32:22-32). His Jacob-cycle (Gen 25-35) is one of the densest narrative complexes in J/E, including the stolen-blessing of Isaac (Gen 27), the ladder-vision at Bethel (Gen 28), the marriages to Leah and Rachel (Gen 29), and the reunion with Esau (Gen 33). The Joseph cycle (Gen 37-50) extends his story to Egypt.

## Historical evidence
None extrabiblical for the *figure*. As the eponymous ancestor of the twelve-tribe federation, "Jacob/Israel" is plausibly a late retrojection of national identity onto a patriarchal narrative-structure. The merneptah Stele (~-1207) attests "Israel" as a people in Canaan by the late 13th-c. BCE — providing a terminus ante quem for the people-name but not for the patriarch-figure.

## Role in the tradition
- **Hebrew Bible.** Jacob is the eponymous origin of the twelve tribes; the post-Penuel name change is a load-bearing identity-naming moment.
- **New Testament.** Paul's Romans 9:6-13 deploys Jacob-vs-Esau as a paradigm of divine election unhinged from human merit ("Jacob have I loved, Esau have I hated"). Matthew's genealogy (Mt 1:2) names him third after Abraham. Hebrews 11:9, 11:20-21 catalogues his faith. John 4:5-6 sets the Jesus-Samaritan-woman dialogue at "Jacob's well." Stephen (Acts 7) recounts his descent into Egypt.
- **Islamic tradition.** Ya'qub is a prophet (Quran 19:49) and patriarch of the Israelites.
