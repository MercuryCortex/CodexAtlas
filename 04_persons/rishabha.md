# Ṛṣabhanātha — First Tīrthaṅkara

## Identity
Ṛṣabhanātha ("Lord Bull"), also Ādinātha ("First Lord"), is the first *Tīrthaṅkara* (ford-maker / liberated teacher) of the current cosmic half-cycle (*avasarpiṇī*) in Jain cosmology. Within Jain tradition he is a cosmic figure of vast antiquity — his temporal location is billions of years in the past. As the tradition's culture-hero, Ṛṣabha is credited with teaching humanity the foundational arts: agriculture, cooking, crafts, and writing, at a time when humans were newly requiring food and shelter after the golden age of wish-fulfilling trees ended. After ruling as king for a vast span, he renounced his kingdom to become the first *muni* (monk), attained *kevala-jñāna* (omniscience), and established the four-fold Jain *saṅgha* (community of monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen).

## Cross-tradition position

**Bhāgavata Purāṇa: Ṛṣabha as Viṣṇu avatar (the great cross-tradition win)**
Book V of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa gives an extended account of a king Ṛṣabha who renounced his kingdom, wandered naked, practiced extreme austerities, and taught a path of inner liberation detached from social roles. The Purāṇic text explicitly identifies him as a *bhāgavata* (devotee of Viṣṇu / form of the Lord) — effectively absorbing the first Tīrthaṅkara into the Vaiṣṇava avatar tradition. This is a well-documented case of a Jain founding figure being re-narrated as a Hindu avatar — a case of inter-tradition absorption rather than synthesis. The Jain tradition identifies the Purāṇic Ṛṣabha as straightforwardly derived from the earlier Jain figure; Hindu scholars have interpreted him as a genuine Vaiṣṇava saint misidentified with the Jain tradition.

**Pārśvanātha and the historical question**
The 23rd Tīrthaṅkara Pārśvanātha (traditional dates ~-872 to -772) is often argued to be the earliest Jain figure with a possible historical kernel, suggesting a pre-Mahāvīra Jain-like community existed. In this reading, Ṛṣabha is the mythological backstory Mahāvīra's community developed to assert primordial antiquity over the rival Buddhist tradition (and over Vedic religion). The structure is analogous to how Buddhist tradition extended the Buddha's lineage back through previous Buddhas.

**Culture-hero archetype**
The pattern of a royal renouncer who founds civilization then abandons it to pursue liberation recurs: Manu (Hindu), Yao and Shun (Chinese), and other culture-heroes establish society and then transcend it. What is distinctive in the Jain formulation is the absolute equation of the greatest civilizational achievement (giving humanity the arts of life) with the greatest spiritual achievement (complete renunciation of all of it).
