# N.T. Wright

## Identity
English New Testament scholar and Anglican bishop (b. 1948). DPhil Oxford 1981 (on Paul's *Letter to the Romans*). Successively: Chaplain at Worcester College Oxford; Lecturer at McGill; Dean of Lichfield; Canon Theologian at Westminster Abbey; Bishop of Durham (2003–2010); Research Professor of New Testament and Early Christianity at St Andrews (2010–2019); Senior Research Fellow at Wycliffe Hall, Oxford (2019–). Among the most widely-read living English-language New Testament scholars.

## Works
See texts-authored. His major scholarly project is the *Christian Origins and the Question of God* series (COQG), planned in six volumes: vol. 1 *The NT and the People of God* (1992) — Second Temple Judaism and historical method; vol. 2 *Jesus and the Victory of God* (1996) — the historical Jesus; vol. 3 *The Resurrection of the Son of God* (2003) — the bodily-resurrection question; vol. 4 *Paul and the Faithfulness of God* (2013, 2 vols, ~1,700 pp); vol. 5 *Paul and the Powers* (announced for 2025); vol. 6 projected on John and gospels. *Surprised by Hope* (2008) is his popular eschatology book. The *New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition* (NRSVue, 2021) Anglican-NT involvement.

## Role in the field
Wright is one of the principal late-20th-/early-21st-century "Third Quest" historical-Jesus scholars. His thesis: Jesus is understood within Second Temple Judaism as an apocalyptic prophet announcing the long-awaited *return-from-exile* of YHWH and inaugurating Israel's God's kingship. His Pauline work is a major contributor to the "new perspective on Paul" (with E.P. Sanders, James D.G. Dunn) — Paul understood within rather than against Second Temple Judaism, *justification* as covenant membership rather than individual psychic comfort.

## Connections
- "New perspective on Paul": Sanders 1977 → Dunn → Wright; engages directly with [[paul-of-tarsus]] / [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] and Second Temple Jewish context ([[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]], [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]]).
- "Third Quest" historical Jesus alongside [[bart-ehrman]], Sanders, Crossan, Allison, [[geza-vermes]] — markedly different conclusions from Ehrman or Crossan on resurrection and continuity-with-Judaism.
- Popular bridge for contemporary Anglican and broader evangelical readers to academic NT scholarship.

## Disputes
- **Resurrection methodology.** Wright argues the bodily-resurrection claim is the historically most plausible explanation for the rise of early-Christian belief; Ehrman, Crossan, Lüdemann reject this on methodological-naturalism grounds.
- **"Apocalyptic" vs. "social-prophet" Jesus.** Wright's apocalyptic-kingdom Jesus differs from Crossan's Cynic-peasant Jesus and Borg's social-prophet Jesus.
- **New perspective on Paul.** Sharply contested by "old perspective" Reformed and Lutheran NT scholars (Carson, Piper, Schreiner, Westerholm).
