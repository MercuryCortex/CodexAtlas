# Jan van Ruusbroec

## Identity
14th-c. Brabantine vernacular mystical theologian (1293–1381). **Augustinian canon** at the forest hermitage of Groenendaal (southeast of Brussels), which he co-founded in 1343 with Frank van Coudenberg and Hendrik van Brakel after years as chaplain of St. Gudula in Brussels. **The most theologically-systematic of the late-medieval Western vernacular mystics**, working in Middle Dutch / Flemish. Direct predecessor of Gerard Groote and the *Devotio Moderna* (Modern Devotion) movement that would shape late-medieval and Reformation-era European spirituality. Long-lived (88 years); his late-career works are mature theological systems.

## Works
- ***Die geestelike brulocht*** (*The Spiritual Espousals*, ~1335) — his masterwork; three-part treatment of the active, interior, and contemplative spiritual life.
- ***Vanden blinkenden steen*** (*The Sparkling Stone*, ~1338).
- ***Boecsken der verclaringhe*** (*Little Book of Clarification*, ~1366) — his defense of his Trinitarian language.
- ***Spieghel der eeuwigher salicheit***, ***Vanden geesteliken tabernakel*** — additional major treatises.
- 10-volume bilingual Middle Dutch-Latin critical edition (Mommaers et al. 1981–2006, CCCM 101–110).

## Role in the tradition

- **Brabantine vernacular mystical tradition.** Ruusbroec inherits and theologically systematizes the Beguine ([[hadewijch-of-antwerp|Hadewijch]], Beatrice of Nazareth) tradition into a sustained theological corpus.
- **Trinitarian-dynamic mystical theology.** Ruusbroec's most original contribution: a *dynamic* Trinitarian theology in which the divine Persons eternally proceed out-and-return-in in dynamic *enjoyment* (*ghebruken*) and *operation* (*werken*) — the soul's mystical participation is in this dynamic Trinitarian life. Jean Gerson critiqued this in 1399 as pantheistic; Ruusbroec had already defended it as orthodox in his *Little Book of Clarification* (1366).
- **Devotio Moderna predecessor.** Gerard Groote visited Ruusbroec at Groenendaal ~1378 and was substantially influenced. The Modern Devotion movement (Brethren of the Common Life, etc.) descends in part from Ruusbroec's pastoral-mystical example.
- **Reception.** Substantial post-medieval reception — particularly in 19th-20th-c. Dutch and German Catholic theological revival (Maeterlinck's 1891 translation; Pieter van Mierlo's editorial work; Mommaers and the Ruusbroec Institute).
