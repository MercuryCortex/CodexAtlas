# Shenxiu

## Identity
Shenxiu (606–706 CE), head monk under the Fifth Patriarch [[hongren]] and founder of the Northern School of Chan Buddhism. In the famous verse contest recorded in the [[phase-5-004-platform-sutra-huineng]], Shenxiu wrote: *"The body is like the Bodhi tree / The mind is like a clear mirror stand / At all times we must strive to polish it / And must not let the dust collect."* The Fifth Patriarch [[hongren]] publicly praised the verse but secretly judged it insufficient — a representation of gradual cultivation (*jianwu*), not sudden awakening (*dunwu*).

Shenxiu's verse represents the Northern School's position: the mind-mirror must be continuously polished; awakening requires sustained practice. After [[hongren]]'s death, Shenxiu became the most prominent Chan teacher of his era, patronized by Empress Wu Zetian and both Tang emperors Zhongzong and Ruizong. His rival [[huineng]]'s disciple [[shenhui]] launched a successful public campaign c. 730 CE to discredit the Northern School and establish the Southern School's sudden-awakening position as orthodox Chan. The Platform Sutra's contest narrative is largely a product of Shenhui's polemical program.
