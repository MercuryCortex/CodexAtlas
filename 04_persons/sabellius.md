# Sabellius

## Identity
A Libyan-Christian teacher active at **Rome ~+215/+220** under Pope Zephyrinus and then Callistus; condemned by Callistus probably ~+220. Sabellius's Christology — the most systematically articulated form of **Modalist Monarchianism** — held that the one God is one *hypostasis* with three successive operative modes (*prosōpa*): *Father* (creating), *Son* (redeeming), *Spirit* (sanctifying) — not three persons but three names or roles of one undivided God. Sabellianism became the **byword for Modalism** in subsequent trinitarian debate, often used loosely to denounce any insufficient three-distinctions theology.

## Historical evidence
**Hippolytus's *Refutatio*** (~+220) is the principal contemporary source — Hippolytus and his community had broken with Callistus partly over the latter's perceived softness toward Sabellius. By the mid-3rd c. *Sabellianism* is a heresiological category used to attack Dionysius of Alexandria's loose subordinationism and later positions.

## Works
None survives. Sabellius's specific formulations are reconstructable only from his opponents.

## Role in the tradition
- **The named representative of Modalism**: the position that "Father, Son, and Spirit" are *modes* of God rather than distinct persons is *Sabellianism* in subsequent theological vocabulary.
- **Catalyst for trinitarian articulation**: the proto-orthodox response to Sabellius and to [[praxeas]] developed the *three persons, one substance* formula that Nicaea presupposes.
- **Persistence as theological accusation**: through the 4th-c. Arian controversies, "Sabellian" remained a stock charge — used by Arians against Nicenes who declined sufficient subordinationist concessions.

## Disputes
- **Sabellius's actual position**: his opponents may have caricatured. Some modern scholarship (Le Boulluec) emphasizes the rhetorical construction of "Sabellianism."
- **Relation to Noetus and Praxeas**: probably a developed form of the same modalist current; precise dependence unclear.
- **Date of his condemnation**: ~+217/+220.
