# Emanuel Swedenborg

## Identity
Swedish natural philosopher and visionary theologian (1688–1772); first a major 18th-c. scientist (mining engineer to the Swedish Crown, anatomist, cosmologist with original contributions on the brain and on the nebular hypothesis of the solar system) and after 1745 a full-time theological-visionary author. Born Emanuel Swedberg, son of Lutheran Bishop Jesper Swedberg of Skara; ennobled 1719 (as Swedenborg) by Queen Ulrika Eleonora; member of the Swedish House of Nobles; spent his life in Sweden, the Netherlands, and England. The 1745 London visionary experience (Swedenborg reported being visited by Christ in a tavern and given a commission to reveal the spiritual sense of scripture) initiated his 27-year theological career. Composed ~20 substantial Latin theological works, most published anonymously in his lifetime in Amsterdam and London. The Church of the New Jerusalem (Swedenborgian) was founded by his English readers in 1787; American congregations followed; Swedenborgian influence on the early American Transcendentalists (Emerson, Henry James Sr.), on William Blake (engaged and partly rebelled against), on Balzac (*Séraphîta*), Borges, D.T. Suzuki, and modern Spiritualism is substantial.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own enormous published Latin corpus, the Swedish parliamentary and scientific records, his correspondence, contemporary references (Kant's polemical *Träume eines Geistersehers* of 1766 attacks Swedenborg in detail), the 1769 Swedish heresy investigation (decided in his favor).

## Works
- Pre-1745 scientific: *Principia Rerum Naturalium* (1734), *Oeconomia Regni Animalis* (1740–1741), *Regnum Animale* (1744–1745).
- Theological: *Arcana Coelestia* (12 vols. 1749–1756), *De Coelo et Eius Mirabilibus et de Inferno* (1758), *De Nova Hierosolyma et Eius Doctrina Coelesti* (1758), *De Equo Albo* (1758), *De Telluribus in Mundo Nostro Solari* (1758), *Apocalypsis Explicata* (manuscript), *Apocalypsis Revelata* (1766), *De Amore Conjugiali* (1768), *Vera Christiana Religio* (1771).

## Role in the tradition
Swedenborg is the principal 18th-century figure of Christian visionary theosophy and a major precursor of modern Spiritualism, the American Transcendentalist absorption of esoteric Christianity, and the broader 19th-century esoteric current.
