# Joseph

## Identity
Eleventh son of Jacob, firstborn of Rachel; the protagonist of the Joseph-cycle (Gen 37-50) which carries the Israelite-patriarchal narrative from Canaan into Egypt and so sets up the Exodus. The narrative-block is widely treated as a self-contained novella distinct in style and structure from the rest of Genesis (Coats 1976; Schmid 2010). Sold into slavery in Egypt by his brothers, Joseph rises to become Pharaoh's vizier, reconciles with his family, and brings the seventy-souls of Israel to settle in Goshen.

## Historical evidence
None extrabiblical. The Joseph-narrative reflects accurate-enough late-second-millennium-BCE Egyptian background (titles, customs, royal court protocol — Redford 1970 catalogued the parallels) for a long time used to defend an early dating, but the consensus position now treats the narrative as a Persian-period or late-monarchic literary construction with deliberate archaizing detail.

## Role in the tradition
- **Hebrew Bible.** Joseph is the structural bridge from patriarchs to Exodus; the eponymous father of the half-tribes Ephraim and Manasseh.
- **New Testament.** Hebrews 11:21-22 names him in the faith catalogue. Stephen's speech (Acts 7:9-16) recounts the cycle as backstory to the Exodus narrative the speech sets up.
- **Islamic tradition.** Yusuf is a prophet (Quran 12 — the longest sustained narrative in the Quran is *Surat Yusuf*, devoted to him). The Islamic Yusuf-narrative parallels Genesis 37-50 closely while reframing the moral-theological emphasis.
