# Pāṇini

The Sanskrit grammarian who codified [[language-sanskrit-classical|Classical Sanskrit]] in the *Aṣṭādhyāyī* — c. 4,000 sutras of algorithmic grammatical rules that fix the language at the end of the late-Vedic period (c. -500 to -400). Pāṇini's work is one of the most precise and complete grammatical descriptions of any natural language produced before the 20th century, and his metalinguistic + rule-ordering machinery has been a subject of sustained interest in modern formal linguistics + computer science (Cardona 1976; Bhate & Kak 1993).

## The *Aṣṭādhyāyī*

The *Aṣṭādhyāyī* ("eight chapters") describes Sanskrit phonology, morphology, syntax, and (limited) semantics in ~4,000 sutras of extreme compression. Pāṇini uses:

- An auxiliary metalanguage of technical-term abbreviations (the *pratyāhāra* system condenses phoneme classes; *anubandha* "marker" suffixes flag rule-applicability)
- A rule-ordering convention (later rules override earlier where they conflict; *paribhāṣā* meta-rules govern this resolution)
- A pre-segmented morphological base (*dhātu* "verbal root" + *pratyaya* "suffix") that essentially anticipates the lexeme-morph structure of modern morphological theory

The compression is so extreme that the *Aṣṭādhyāyī* requires commentaries to read: Kātyāyana's *Vārttikas* (c. -3rd c.), Patañjali's *Mahābhāṣya* (c. -150), and a long subsequent commentarial tradition.

## Why this matters for the Atlas

Pāṇini is the *codification fix-point* that makes Sanskrit the **transregional cosmopolitan language** of South + Southeast Asia (Pollock 2006). Without the *Aṣṭādhyāyī*, Sanskrit would have continued to evolve into vernaculars (as Latin did into Romance) and the unified transregional literary community would not have been possible. The fix-point is what enables a 5th-century Kashmiri + a 10th-century Tamil + a 15th-century Javanese to all write the *same* language.

This is structurally cognate with what later happens to other liturgical languages — Quranic Arabic fixed at the moment of revelation, Hebrew fixed at the Tiberian Masoretic redaction, Church Slavonic fixed at the Cyril-Methodius translation — but Pāṇini is by far the earliest and most explicit case.
