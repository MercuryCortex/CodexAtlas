# Mark Singleton

## Identity
British scholar of modern yoga (b. 1971); PhD Cambridge 2007 on yoga's modern history. Senior Research Fellow at SOAS in the Hatha Yoga Project (Mallinson PI). *Yoga Body* (2010) is the foundational scholarly demonstration that "modern postural yoga" (the asana-heavy practice now globally exported as "yoga") is a 20th-century construction in dialogue with European physical-culture (gymnastics, body-building, Scandinavian and German naturism, military calisthenics) and Indian nationalist body-discipline movements — not a direct continuation of pre-modern Indian yoga, which was overwhelmingly a meditation-and-pranayama tradition with comparatively limited asana inventory.

## Role in the tradition
Definitively reframes the modern-yoga discussion. The popular Western "yoga" inherited from B.K.S. Iyengar, Krishnamacharya, and Pattabhi Jois turns out to be substantially mid-20th-c. South Indian work in conversation with global physical-culture rather than a transparent inheritance from the Yoga Sutras or hatha-yoga classical literature.

## Connections
- Continues the historiography of [[tradition-hindu-modernism]] alongside [[swami-vivekananda]] and [[sri-aurobindo]] — Singleton documents the *body* dimension of the same modernizing-Hinduism movement.
- Implications for how the modern-Western reception of Indian traditions is read: parallel to the [[helena-blavatsky]] / [[tradition-theosophy]] mediation of Indian thought (which Singleton also discusses).
