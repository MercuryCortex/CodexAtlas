# Gilgamesh (historical king of Uruk)

## Identity
Gilgamesh was — most probably — a real Early Dynastic king of Uruk who reigned in the Early Dynastic II or early ED III period, conventionally placed in the range ~-2700 to -2500 BCE. He is distinguishable from but inseparable from his literary doppelgänger, the protagonist of the [[phase-1-004-gilgamesh-old-babylonian]] and later Standard Babylonian *Gilgamesh* — the historical king became the seed-figure for the most extensive literary epic of the Mesopotamian tradition. The Sumerian King List ([[phase-1-015-sumerian-king-list]]) places him as the fifth king of the First Dynasty of Uruk, attributes him a reign of 126 years (mythologically inflated), and names his father as Lugalbanda (also mythologized in the *Lugalbanda* compositions) and his mother as the goddess Ninsun. The historical-Gilgamesh tradition centers on his role as a builder — the walls of Uruk and the Tummal at Nippur — and his rivalry with King Aga of Kish.

## Historical evidence
The historicity of Gilgamesh is debated but most current Assyriology (George 2003 vol. 1; Klein 1976) treats him as **likely historical**, on the following converging grounds:
1. **The Tummal Chronicle** (Sollberger 1962) — an early historical text — names Gilgamesh as the builder of the Tummal sanctuary at Nippur, alongside other rulers (Enmebaragesi, Mes-anepada) who are independently attested by contemporary inscriptions. The text presents Gilgamesh as one ruler in a sequence of credible historical figures.
2. **The Sumerian King List** entry, while inflated chronologically (126 years), is consistent with the ED placement.
3. **Archaeologically** the great wall of Uruk (the "Eanna wall") is dated to the ED II period; the Gilgamesh tradition's attribution to him fits the archaeological window.
4. **The *Gilgamesh and Aga* composition** — a short Sumerian epic — depicts a politically plausible conflict between Uruk (Gilgamesh) and Kish (Aga, son of Enmebaragesi who is independently inscriptionally attested). The historical Aga / Enmebaragesi anchor makes the Gilgamesh figure in the same text more credible.
5. *No* contemporary inscription naming Gilgamesh himself has been recovered, however. The absence of a primary-source inscription is the strongest argument against full historicity; this is why "likely-historical" rather than "documented" is the appropriate label.

**Distinguish carefully from the literary Gilgamesh.** The 126-year reign, the two-thirds-divine parentage, the friendship with Enkidu, the journey to Utnapishtim — these are mythologized accretions on a historical seed. The historical king (if real) was a city-king of Uruk in the ED II/III period whose actual reign-length, biography, and exploits are essentially unknown.

## Works
None — the historical Gilgamesh authored no text we possess. The Sumerian "Gilgamesh compositions" (*Gilgamesh and Aga*, *Gilgamesh and Huwawa*, *Gilgamesh and the Bull of Heaven*, *Gilgamesh, Enkidu and the Netherworld*, *The Death of Gilgamesh*) — surviving in Old Babylonian school copies — are literary works about him, presumably with Ur III court origins. The Old Babylonian and later Standard Babylonian Akkadian *Gilgamesh* epics ([[phase-1-004-gilgamesh-old-babylonian]]) are later literary syntheses.

## Role in the tradition
After his death he was deified (his name appears in Ur III god lists as a netherworld deity, "Gilgamesh judge of the underworld") — a posthumous deification parallel to that of [[shulgi]] and several other kings. By the Old Babylonian period he is principally a literary-mythological figure; the historical king is largely subsumed into the epic protagonist. The 19th-century rediscovery of the Standard Babylonian epic (George Smith, 1872) catapulted the literary Gilgamesh into the modern Western literary canon. The historical king has remained primarily a specialist-Assyriological subject.

## Disputes
- **Historicity itself**. Likely-historical is the current majority view (George 2003); a minority (e.g., older Kramer; sometimes Civil) treats Gilgamesh as a purely literary figure with no historical core.
- **Date**. ED II (~-2700) vs. early ED III (~-2500); the conventional range covers both.
- **Father Lugalbanda**. Lugalbanda is named as Gilgamesh's father in the King List and the *Lugalbanda* compositions, but Lugalbanda is more thoroughly mythologized than Gilgamesh and his historicity is more doubtful.
