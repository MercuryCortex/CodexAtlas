# Gorakhnath

## Identity
Gorakṣanātha / Gorakhnath (~1100–1200, traditional dates contested) is the founder of the Nath Sampradaya / Nath Yoga tradition and the principal historical figure associated with the emergence of classical *Haṭha Yoga* — the system of postural, breath-control, and energetic practices that became, much transformed, the foundation of modern global yoga. Origins variously placed in Punjab, Nepal, Bengal; his teacher Matsyendranāth and a network of legendary "nine Naths" (*nava-nāthas*) form the foundational lineage of medieval North Indian yogi-asceticism. The historicity of Gorakhnath himself is widely accepted; specific biographical details are largely legendary.

## Works
Traditional attribution: *Gorakṣa-Śataka*, *Gorakṣa-Saṃhitā*, foundational Hatha Yoga texts. The actual textual history is layered and substantially later — Mallinson and Singleton's 2017 critical work substantially complicates simple attributions.

## Role in the tradition
Foundational for the medieval Nath Sampradaya, which influenced medieval Indian devotional and Tantric movements broadly; for the development of Hatha Yoga (the [[phase-5-035-hatha-yoga-pradipika|Haṭhayoga-Pradīpikā]] of Svātmārāma names Gorakh as foundational); and through Hatha Yoga (heavily transformed in the late 19th c. and 20th c. by Krishnamacharya, Iyengar, and others) for modern global yoga. Disciple of [[matsyendranath]] per the standard Nath account.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Hindu-Buddhist transmission via the Nath / mahāsiddha tradition**: like his teacher [[matsyendranath|Matsyendra]]-Mīnapā, Gorakhnath appears in Buddhist mahāsiddha lists (Gorakṣa is one of the 84 mahāsiddhas in the standard list); the figure crosses the Hindu Nath / Buddhist Vajrayāna boundary. Tibetan Kagyu and Sakya tantric lineages cite him.
- **Sufi-Yogic *Amṛtakuṇḍa* tradition**: medieval Indo-Persian translations of Nath-tradition Yogic texts circulated in Sufi circles in medieval North India; Ernst 2003, 2016 has documented this explicit Sufi-Yogic cross-pollination.
