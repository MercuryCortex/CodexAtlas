# Khafre (Chephren)

## The pharaoh and the Sphinx question

**Khafre** (~2558–2532 BCE) was the fourth pharaoh of Egypt's 4th Dynasty, son or half-son of **[[khufu|Khufu]]** (the relationship is disputed; there may be an intervening pharaoh, Djedefre). He built the **[[pyramid-of-khafre-giza|Second Pyramid at Giza]]** and, by conventional attribution, commissioned the **[[sphinx-of-giza|Great Sphinx]]**.

The Sphinx attribution is one of the most contested questions in Egyptology:

**Evidence for Khafre:**
- The Sphinx faces east along the causeway axis of Khafre's pyramid complex
- The **Valley Temple** (immediately adjacent to the Sphinx) uses identical limestone blocks and was clearly the same construction project
- The **diorite statue of Khafre** (Cairo Museum) — found in the Valley Temple — shows facial features scholars compare to the Sphinx's damaged face
- A partial cartouche on the Dream Stele between the Sphinx's paws may read "Khafre"

**Evidence against or complicating:**
- **Robert Schoch's water erosion hypothesis** (1992): the Sphinx enclosure's vertical erosion pattern suggests rainfall weathering rather than wind/sand — implying pre-4th Dynasty construction (see [[sphinx-of-giza]])
- The **Inventory Stele** (New Kingdom) claims Khufu found the Sphinx already existing — but this stele dates ~2,000 years after Khufu and is considered a later composition, not contemporary evidence
- The Sphinx's **body is disproportionately small** relative to its head — suggesting the head was recarved, possibly from an older lion or animal figure

## The diorite statue: the finest royal portrait

Khafre's survival in the archaeological record is substantially better than his father's (the 7.5 cm ivory figurine of Khufu): **23 diorite statues** were found in the Valley Temple, of which one is nearly complete and is considered the finest example of Old Kingdom royal portraiture. The image shows Khafre enthroned, with the **Horus falcon** spreading its wings protectively behind the king's head — a perfect embodiment of the theological formula "*Horus-in-the-Horizon*" (*Hor-em-akhet*), the name later applied to the Sphinx itself.

## Succession and the 4th Dynasty

The 4th Dynasty represents the apex of pyramid-building:
- Sneferu → Khufu → Djedefre (?) → Khafre → Menkaure → Shepseskaf

After Shepseskaf, the 4th Dynasty ended and the 5th Dynasty rulers built smaller, less ambitious pyramids but introduced the **Pyramid Texts** (first inscribed under Unas, ~2375 BCE) — suggesting the theological program shifted from architectural embodiment to textual inscription.
