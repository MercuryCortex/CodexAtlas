# Ammonius Saccas

## Identity

**Ammonius Saccas** (c. 175–242 CE, Alexandria) is the **founding teacher** of what becomes Neoplatonism — and the single most important figure in the history of cross-tradition philosophical synthesis for which we have no surviving writings. His significance is entirely visible through his students' works: he was the teacher of **[[plotinus]]** (founder of Neoplatonism) AND **[[origen]]** (the greatest Christian theologian of the 2nd–3rd centuries), meaning the entire subsequent traditions of both Neoplatonic philosophy and Alexandrian Christian theology flow from the same Alexandrian teacher.

His nickname *Saccas* ("sack-bearer" in Greek) suggests he worked as a porter before becoming a philosopher. His epithet *Theodidaktos* ("God-taught") indicates the reverence his students held him in.

## The Fork Point: Plotinus and Origen

The fact that both **[[plotinus]]** and **[[origen]]** studied under Ammonius Saccas — the former becoming the founder of Neoplatonism, the latter the founder of systematic Christian theology — makes Ammonius the **fork-point** of two traditions that would profoundly shape Western civilization:

- **Plotinus's line**: Ammonius → Plotinus → [[porphyry]] → [[iamblichus]] → [[proclus]] → [[pseudo-dionysius]] → all of Western Christian mysticism
- **Origen's line**: Ammonius → Origen → Clement → Cappadocian Fathers (Basil, Gregory of Nyssa, Gregory Nazianzus) → the entire Greek theological tradition → Eastern Orthodoxy

Both lines converge in the **Byzantine theological synthesis** and ultimately in **Thomas Aquinas** — who draws on both Plotinus (via pseudo-Dionysius) and Origen (via Jerome's transmission).

## The Alexandrian Context

Ammonius taught in **[[event-school-of-alexandria-300bce-415ce|Alexandria]]** at the height of its intellectual florescence. The city was simultaneously:
- The center of Egyptian priestly theological scholarship (the temples of Serapis and Isis)
- The center of Jewish Platonism ([[philo-of-alexandria]] had died c. 50 CE)
- The center of Christian theological development (the catechetical school of Alexandria)
- The hub of Platonic philosophical education (direct heir of the Academy tradition)

Ammonius stood at the intersection of all four streams. Whether he personally drew on Egyptian temple theology is disputed, but his student [[plotinus]] demonstrably did (Plotinus lived in Alexandria for 11 years and knew the Egyptian philosophical tradition well).

## Surviving Evidence

Ammonius wrote nothing, or nothing survived. We know him entirely through:
1. **Porphyry's *Life of Plotinus*** (the primary source): records that Plotinus came to Alexandria at age 28 (c. 232 CE), heard Ammonius speak once, and immediately said "this is the man I was looking for" — then studied under him for 11 years
2. **Eusebius of Caesarea** — records that Origen studied under Ammonius before his conversion to Christianity (disputed by some scholars who argue there were two men named Ammonius)
3. **Two titles** attributed to him: *On the Harmony of Moses and Jesus* and *A Commentary on Plato's Timaeus* — neither surviving

The *On the Harmony of Moses and Jesus* title is extraordinary: it suggests Ammonius explicitly attempted a synthesis of Egyptian-rooted Jewish Scripture (via Moses) with Greek philosophical cosmology (via Plato's Timaeus) — the precise synthesis that his students Plotinus and Origen each pursued in their distinct ways.

## Significance

Ammonius Saccas matters for the vault as the **missing common node** between Neoplatonism and Christian theology — the teacher whose synthesis each student then developed in a different direction. The fact that Neoplatonic philosophy and Christian theology share a common Alexandrian teacher helps explain why their conceptual vocabularies are so deeply intertwined — they are literally sibling traditions from the same parent.
