# Ptolemaeus (Valentinian)

## Identity
Mid-2nd-c. CE Valentinian Gnostic teacher of the Italian (western) Valentinian school, the principal student-system that Irenaeus targeted in *Against Heresies*. Ptolemaeus's *Letter to Flora* — preserved by Epiphanius (*Panarion* 33.3–7) — is one of the very few surviving direct compositions from a Valentinian teacher's hand. The letter expounds the Valentinian threefold reading of the Mosaic Law (the law of God / the law of Moses / the law of the elders), proposing a graduated hermeneutic that locates the law's true divine portion alongside imperfect Mosaic and human accretions. Ptolemaeus also developed an exegesis of the Prologue of John (Jn 1:1–18) that mapped its terms onto the Valentinian Pleroma; preserved in Irenaeus *AH* I.8.5.

## Historical evidence
The *Letter to Flora* in Epiphanius's quotation; Irenaeus's exposition and refutation of Ptolemaean systematic theology in *AH* I.1–8; possible identification with the *Ptolemaeus the martyr* mentioned by Justin Martyr (*2 Apology* 2) — accepted by some scholars, disputed by others.

## Works
- *Letter to Flora* — hermeneutical-systematic letter to a non-Valentinian Christian woman.
- Exegesis of John 1:1–18 (preserved in Irenaeus).

## Role in the tradition
- The principal Valentinian teacher Irenaeus knows by name and engages systematically.
- The Italian/Western Valentinian school is distinguished from the Eastern (Anatolian) school by a particular Christological position (Italian: psychic Christ; Eastern: pneumatic Christ).

## Disputes
- Identification with Justin's martyr.
- The extent and originality of Ptolemaeus's contribution vs. his transmission of Valentinian school positions.
