# Isidore of Seville

## Identity
Visigothic Iberian bishop and encyclopedist. Born to a Hispano-Roman aristocratic family in Cartagena around 560; succeeded his elder brother Leander as bishop of Seville around 600; presided at the influential Fourth Council of Toledo (633). Died in Seville in 636. His ***Etymologiae*** — a 20-book encyclopedia of essentially all classical learning, organized by the "etymologies" of words — became the most-copied secular reference work of the medieval Latin West.

## Historical evidence
The *Etymologiae* survives in roughly 1,000 medieval manuscripts (Reynolds, *Texts and Transmission*, 1983). Contemporary sources include Braulio of Zaragoza's *Renotatio librorum Isidori* (a list of Isidore's works, the principal bibliographic guide).

## Works
- ***Etymologiae*** (also *Origines*) — 20 books covering grammar, rhetoric, logic, mathematics, music, astronomy, medicine, law, the Church, languages, races, animals, the cosmos, agriculture, war, games, ships, buildings, vestments, and household goods. The structuring principle: explain each thing by tracing the (often fantastical) etymology of its name.
- ***Sententiae*** (3 books) — handbook of Christian doctrine and morals.
- ***De natura rerum*** — natural-philosophical compendium.
- ***Chronica Maiora*** — world chronicle.
- ***Historia Gothorum*** — history of the Goths in Spain.
- ***De viris illustribus*** — catalog of ecclesiastical writers (continuing Jerome and Gennadius).

## Role in the tradition
For the early Middle Ages, "what Isidore says" is what is known of the classical world. The *Etymologiae*'s combination of (often garbled) classical learning with Christian frame became standard. Aquinas cites him frequently; Dante puts him in the Heaven of the Sun (*Paradiso* X.131). His *Sententiae* shaped early-medieval moral theology.

## Connections — substrate and influence
- The *Etymologiae* is a deliberate **conservation operation** preserving Roman classical learning inside a Christian framework. Pliny the Elder, Solinus, Servius, Lactantius, Augustine, Cassiodorus and Pliny-like Latin grammarians all feed in. Henderson 2007; Barney et al. 2006: 11–24.
- His etymological method — finding meaning in name-origins — descends from Plato's *Cratylus* and the Stoic etymological tradition (Stoic Chrysippus argued language reflects reality); Isidore Christianizes it for biblical and ecclesiastical vocabulary.
- His preservation of Hellenistic and Roman scientific and natural-philosophical material gives the medieval West an (incomplete and partly garbled) picture of antique learning until the 12th-c. translation movements supplied better Greek and Arabic sources.

## Disputes
- **Authorship of the *Etymologiae*.** The work was unfinished at Isidore's death and edited / arranged by Braulio of Zaragoza; how much editorial intervention shaped the final form is debated.
- **Isidore's role at Toledo IV and the Visigothic-church relationship.** Wood 2012 reads Isidore as more politically engaged with the Visigothic monarchy than the older historiography.
