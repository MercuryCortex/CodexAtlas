# Sigmund Freud (1856–1939)

Austrian neurologist and founder of psychoanalysis. Developed the structural model of the psyche (id, ego, superego) and the topographical model (unconscious, preconscious, conscious). His theory of the unconscious — a depth layer beneath conscious awareness that carries the traces of all past experience, repressed drives, and infantile wishes, and that drives surface behavior through mechanisms inaccessible to introspection — is the most influential secular-psychological doctrine of the 20th century.

**Cross-tradition parallel — ālayavijñāna**: Freud's unconscious is structurally parallel to the Yogācāra *ālayavijñāna* (storehouse consciousness) in the [[phase-4-103-lankavatara-sutra]]: both posit a depth-layer below conscious experience that carries the traces of all past actions/events, that operates without the subject's awareness, and that drives surface experience. [[carl-gustav-jung]] explicitly made this parallel (CW 11, foreword to Suzuki's Zen anthology), extending it via the collective unconscious. The Laṅkāvatāra predates Freud by ~1,500 years.

## Key connections
- → [[carl-gustav-jung]] — *professional successor*: Jung trained under Freud before diverging; both developed depth-psychology frameworks with Buddhist structural parallels
