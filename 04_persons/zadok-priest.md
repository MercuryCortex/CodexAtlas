# Zadok

## Identity
High priest under **David** and **Solomon**, co-serving initially alongside Abiathar (of the Elide line). When Solomon's succession was contested between Adonijah and Solomon, Zadok sided with Solomon — anointing him king at the Gihon spring (1 Kings 1:38–45). Solomon subsequently dismissed Abiathar (1 Kings 2:26–27) and appointed Zadok as sole high priest. From this point forward, the **Zadokite line** — Zadok's biological descendants — held the Jerusalem high priesthood exclusively until the Hasmonean usurpation of 152 BCE, a span of approximately 818 years.

## Historical Evidence
Zadok appears with sufficient consistency across source strata (court narratives, Succession Narrative, later DH editing) to be considered probably historical, though the genealogy assigned to him varies between texts (1 Chr 6:8 traces him to Eleazar son of Aaron; Ezek 44:15 designates "the sons of Zadok" as the legitimate priests without specifying that genealogy). The name *Tsadoq* means "righteous" — possibly a theophoric claim embedded in the name itself.

## Role in the Tradition
Zadok is the **founding pivot** of the most consequential institutional-genealogical chain in Western religious history. His family's monopoly on the Jerusalem high priesthood:
- Defined Israelite/Jewish priestly legitimacy for eight centuries
- Was encoded by Ezekiel as the *condition* of any restored Temple (Ezekiel 40–48)
- Became the standard by which the Hasmonean usurpation was judged illegitimate
- Drove the Essene withdrawal to Qumran (the Dead Sea Scrolls community identifies itself as the "Sons of Zadok")
- Gave the Sadducees their name (*Tsadduqim* — "Zadokites")

The Sadducees are literally named after Zadok. The Essenes define themselves by loyalty to Zadok's line. The Pharisees emerge as an alternative to the Zadokite priestly monopoly. **Every major Jewish sect of the Second Temple period is a response — conformist, protest, or alternative — to the Zadokite institution Zadok founded.**
