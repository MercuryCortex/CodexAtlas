# Montanus

## Identity
A Phrygian Christian who, beginning ~+156/+172 (the date is the foundational dispute of Montanist studies — Eusebius gives a confusing 156 elsewhere implied 172), began prophesying in **ecstatic trance** with the claim that the *Paraclete* (the Holy Spirit promised in John 14–16) was now speaking through him. Together with the prophetesses **[[priscilla-and-maximilla|Priscilla and Maximilla]]** he founded the movement known to its critics as **Montanism** and to its adherents as the **New Prophecy** (*Hē Kainē Prophēteia*). The movement was characterized by: (a) continuing ecstatic prophetic revelation; (b) heightened ascetic-eschatological expectation; (c) belief that the New Jerusalem would descend at Pepuza in Phrygia; (d) full ministerial participation by women.

## Historical evidence
Hostile sources only — though abundant. The principal pre-Eusebian source is the "Anonymous" anti-Montanist preserved in **Eusebius HE 5.16–18**; Eusebius adds material from **Apollonius** (~+200) and others. **Tertullian** is the most important sympathetic source — he converted to Montanism in his later years and his late works (*De ieiunio*, *De pudicitia*, *De fuga*, etc.) defend the New Prophecy. **Tabbernee's** epigraphic collection (1997) gives the inscriptional and material evidence — Montanist communities in Phrygia survived into the 6th c.

## Works
None survives. Short prophetic utterances preserved in Epiphanius: "I am the Lord God Almighty dwelling in a man... I am the Father, and I am the Son, and I am the Paraclete" (*Pan.* 48.11) — though the authenticity of even these short fragments is debated.

## Role in the tradition
- **The first major *internal* Christian schism over the question of continuing prophecy** — pitting the institutional bishops against revelatory charismatics.
- **Women's ecclesial authority**: Montanism is the most explicit 2nd-c. movement preserving women's prophetic and likely sacramental authority. The proto-orthodox suppression of Montanism was simultaneously the suppression of women's ministry (Trevett 1996).
- **Tertullian's conversion** lent the movement intellectual respectability and indicates its serious theological character — not the rustic enthusiasm of the anti-Montanist polemic.

## Disputes
- **Date of origin.** Eusebius gives ~+156 in one place and is read as implying ~+172 in another; mainstream scholarship splits between these dates.
- **Whether Montanism was *theologically* heretical** or merely *institutionally* unacceptable. Modern scholarship inclines toward the latter — Montanist doctrine is broadly orthodox; what was rejected was its claim to continuing revelatory authority.
- **The "I am the Father, and the Son, and the Paraclete" fragment**: a sabellian-modalist-sounding utterance, but possibly the Spirit speaking *through* Montanus in the prophetic-ventriloquism sense, not Montanus claiming divinity.
