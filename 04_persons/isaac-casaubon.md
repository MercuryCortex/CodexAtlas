# Isaac Casaubon

## Identity
Genevan-born French (later English) classical philologist (1559–1614), one of the greatest scholars of the late Renaissance. Educated at Geneva (his father a Calvinist refugee from Bordeaux); professor of Greek at Geneva (1582–1596) and then at Montpellier; royal librarian to Henri IV of France (1600); after Henri IV's assassination 1610, moved to England under James I's patronage; died at London 1614. His critical editions and commentaries on Strabo, Theophrastus, Polybius, Suetonius, Athenaeus, Persius, and Aulus Gellius set new standards for classical philology. His *De rebus sacris et ecclesiasticis exercitationes* (1614), written as part of a polemical reply to Cardinal Baronius's Catholic *Annales Ecclesiastici*, included a long demonstration (Exercitatio I, sections X–XX) that the Hermetic texts were not pre-Mosaic compositions of Hermes Trismegistus but Hellenistic Greek pseudepigrapha of the early Christian era — the most consequential single act of Renaissance philology, demolishing the foundation of the Renaissance Hermetic project.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own published corpus, extensive learned correspondence (the *Epistolae* edited by Almeloveen 1709), state and university records.

## Works
- *De rebus sacris et ecclesiasticis exercitationes XVI* (1614).
- Critical editions and commentaries of Strabo (1587), Theophrastus *Characters* (1592), Athenaeus (1597), Suetonius (1595), Persius (1605), Polybius (1609), and many others.

## Role in the tradition
Casaubon's 1614 redating of the Hermetica is the philological turning-point at which Renaissance Hermeticism's claim to pre-Mosaic primordial authority collapsed. The Hermetic texts continued to be read but as Hellenistic philosophical-religious documents rather than as primordial revelation; the Yates thesis treats this as the demarcation event between Renaissance Hermeticism and modern critical study. Casaubon is also a foundational figure in the development of historical-critical philological method.
