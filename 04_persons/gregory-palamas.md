# Gregory Palamas

## Identity
Gregory Palamas (1296–1359) was the principal defender of the Athonite Hesychast monastic tradition against the philosophical critique of Barlaam the Calabrian and the architect of the *essence/energies distinction* that became the defining doctrinal contribution of late-Byzantine and modern Eastern Orthodox theology. From a Constantinopolitan aristocratic family; entered Athonite monasticism young; defended the Hesychast practice (silent prayer with somatic disciplines, the Jesus Prayer, vision of uncreated light) doctrinally in the *Triads*. Archbishop of Thessalonica from 1347; was captured by Turks for a year during a Byzantine civil-war embassy. Hesychast Councils of 1341, 1347, 1351 ratified Palamism as official Orthodox doctrine. Canonized 1368; the Second Sunday of Lent is annually dedicated to him in the Orthodox calendar.

## Works
*Triads in Defense of the Holy Hesychasts* (~1338–1341); *150 Chapters* (synthetic theological summary); *Theophanes*; homilies; polemical works against successive critics.

## Role in the tradition
Foundational; Palamism is the defining mature Eastern Orthodox doctrine of God. Recovered for modern Orthodox theology by the Russian Paris school (Florovsky, Lossky, Meyendorff). The Catholic-Orthodox dialogue over the essence/energies distinction continues.
