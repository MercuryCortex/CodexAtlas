# Musa al-Kazim

## Identity
Son of [[jafar-al-sadiq]] by an enslaved Berber woman named Hamida. Born in al-Abwa' (between Mecca and Medina) ~745. Designated **Seventh Imam** of the Twelver Imami line by his father after the pre-death of the elder son Isma'il (whose alternative succession founds [[tradition-ismailism]] — see [[jafar-al-sadiq]] for the divergence). Led the Imami community from 765 (the death of his father al-Sadiq) until his own death in Baghdad in 799 — 34 years, the second-longest tenure of any of the Twelve Imams. His epithet *al-Kāẓim* — "the Restrainer of Anger" — points to his quietist comportment under intense Abbasid pressure. Imprisoned for years in Basra and then Baghdad under [[harun-al-rashid|Harun al-Rashid]]; died in prison 799, almost certainly poisoned (the Shi'i tradition's account is here in unusually firm agreement with the historiographical record — Harun's regime had concrete political reasons to remove al-Kazim, whose Medinan prestige and Imami partisans made him a perceived threat).

## Historical evidence
Documented across the Sunni and Shi'i sources of the late 8th c. The imprisonment and death in Baghdad are well-attested in both traditions. His shrine in Baghdad (at Kazimayn — also containing his grandson [[muhammad-al-jawad]]) becomes one of the major Iraqi Shi'i pilgrimage sites alongside Najaf (Ali) and Karbala (Husayn). The Shi'i hadith corpus preserves an extensive attributed corpus of legal-theological dicta.

## Role in the tradition
Al-Kazim's tenure is the period in which the Imami line consolidates as **politically marginal but doctrinally cohesive** — quietism under Abbasid hostility, organization of a network of *wukala'* (agents / deputies) collecting religious dues (the *khums*) on behalf of the Imam across the dispersed Imami community, and continued teaching activity. The institutional pattern is set: Abbasid caliphal hostility, Imam in Medina or in Abbasid prison, dispersed community managed through a deputy-network. This pattern would intensify under his successors and reach its terminus in the Minor Occultation of the Twelfth Imam, when the wakil-network *becomes* the institutional Shi'i community in the absence of a present Imam.

### The Waqifi sub-sect
Some Imami partisans, after al-Kazim's death, refused to accept his death and held that he was the Mahdi who had gone into occultation. This **Waqifiyya** ("those who stop" — at the Seventh Imam) sub-sect persisted for some centuries and represents the first major application of the *occultation* idea within Twelver-line Shi'ism — a foreshadowing of the later Twelver doctrine of the Twelfth Imam's occultation. The Waqifi line eventually dies out; the mainstream Twelver position is that the imamate proceeded to al-Kazim's son [[ali-al-rida]].

## Disputes
- **Cause of death.** Standard tradition (poisoning by orders of Harun al-Rashid) is, in this case, historically well-grounded — both Sunni and Shi'i sources concur on the political circumstances and the suspicious nature of the death in prison.

- **Authenticity of the Waqifi imamate-tradition.** Whether the Waqifi position was a serious early option or a marginal aberration is debated. Modarressi 1993 reads it as a serious early-Twelver dispute that the eventual Twelver doctrine had to overcome.

## Cross-tradition connections
- ↔ [[jafar-al-sadiq]] (father), [[ali-al-rida]] (son, Eighth Imam) — the Twelver line through the Ismaili-divergence.
- ↔ [[tradition-shia-islam]] — definitive of the quietism-under-pressure pattern.
- ↔ [[tradition-ismailism]] — the alternative succession (through Isma'il rather than al-Kazim) that defines the Sevener tradition.
- ↔ Abbasid caliphate — the most concrete instance of caliphal persecution of an Imam in the Twelver line after Husayn.
