# Martin Luther

## Identity
German Augustinian friar, professor of theology at Wittenberg, and founding theologian of the Protestant Reformation (1483–1546). Born to a copper-mining family at Eisleben; entered the Augustinian Order 1505 after his famous thunderstorm vow; ordained priest 1507; doctorate in theology 1512 and Wittenberg professorship the same year. Composed the 95 Theses 1517 protesting the Tetzel indulgence-campaign; through 1517–1521 his theological position hardened and the rupture with Rome became irreversible (the Leipzig Disputation 1519 forced him to reject papal infallibility; the Diet of Worms 1521 produced the *Hier stehe ich* moment and the imperial Edict against him). Sheltered by Elector Frederick the Wise at the Wartburg 1521–1522 where he produced the German NT translation in eleven weeks. Returned to Wittenberg 1522; married Katharina von Bora 1525; the remaining 21 years of his life were spent organizing Lutheran ecclesiastical, liturgical, catechetical, and educational structures, polemicizing against Catholics, the Radical Reformation, and (in his final, deeply troubling years) against Jews.

## Historical evidence
Densely documented: his own enormous corpus (Weimar Ausgabe ~120 vols.), imperial-ecclesiastical-electoral records, contemporary correspondence and pamphlet response.

## Works
*95 Theses* (1517). Three Reformation treatises of 1520: *To the Christian Nobility of the German Nation*, *The Babylonian Captivity of the Church*, *On the Freedom of a Christian*. *De Servo Arbitrio* (1525). German Bible translation (NT 1522, complete 1534). *Large Catechism* and *Small Catechism* (1529). *Smalcald Articles* (1537). Hundreds of sermons, lectures, table-talk, letters.

## Role in the tradition
The single most consequential figure in the history of Western Christianity since Augustine. His doctrines of justification by faith alone (*sola fide*), scripture alone (*sola scriptura*), and the priesthood of all believers reshaped European Christianity and indirectly European political-cultural history.

## Disputes
- **Late anti-Jewish writings.** Luther's 1543 *On the Jews and Their Lies* is one of the most virulent anti-Jewish polemics in Christian history; its influence on subsequent German Christian antisemitism is documented and grave.
- **Religious vs. social radicalism.** The 1524–1525 Peasants' War tested Luther's stance; his *Against the Murderous and Thieving Hordes* (May 1525) broke with the radicals and committed the Lutheran Reformation to the territorial-princely path.
