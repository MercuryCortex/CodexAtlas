# Numenius of Apamea

## Identity
Numenius of Apamea (~+130/+200) was a Middle Platonist philosopher of Syrian origin, often classified as Neo-Pythagorean for his stated commitment to the unity of Platonic and Pythagorean wisdom. He was one of the most theologically and religiously cosmopolitan philosophers of the Antonine and Severan periods, treating Plato as fundamentally a Pythagorean and Pythagoras as fundamentally a transmitter of an ancient sacred wisdom shared with the Brahmans, the Egyptians, the Magi, and the Jews. Numenius is the most important *immediate* philosophical precursor to Plotinus: his three-Gods doctrine (the First God / Father, the Second God / Demiurge, the Third God / cosmos), his sharp matter-spirit dualism, and his sophisticated engagement with the *Timaeus* directly shape the Plotinian hypostatic structure (the One, Nous, Soul). The ancient testimony that Plotinus was accused of plagiarizing Numenius (Porphyry *Life of Plotinus* 17) is hostile but reflects the real proximity of the two thinkers. Numenius's work survives only in fragments preserved by Eusebius of Caesarea, Origen, Porphyry, and Proclus.

## Historical evidence
- **Eusebius's *Praeparatio Evangelica*** (~+313, books IX, XI, XV) preserves the longest verbatim quotations from Numenius — including the famous fragments from *On the Good* and *On the Defection of the Academics from Plato*. Eusebius found Numenius useful because Numenius spoke favorably of the Jews and their philosophy ("What is Plato but Moses speaking Attic Greek?" — fr. 8 des Places).
- **Origen *Contra Celsum*** (~+248) cites Numenius on the Jewish prophets and engages his three-Gods doctrine; this is direct evidence that Christian intellectuals of the mid-3rd century knew and respected Numenius.
- **Porphyry's *Life of Plotinus*** reports that Plotinus's pupil **Amelius** assembled Numenius's complete works (a major library) and wrote a 100-book commentary on him; this commentary is lost but its existence shows the magnitude of Numenius's influence in early Neoplatonist Rome.
- **Proclus *Commentary on the Timaeus*** preserves additional fragments and shows the continuing 5th-century Neoplatonist engagement.

## Works
All survive in fragments. The major works:
- **On the Good (Peri t'Agathou)** — Numenius's most-cited work. Develops the three-Gods doctrine; the First God is *autoagathon* (the Good-itself, pure intellective being), the Second God is the demiurge who looks toward the First and orders the cosmos, the Third God is the cosmos itself (or, in another reading, the lower soul-aspect of the demiurge). The First-Second God distinction is the philosophical seedbed of the Plotinian One-Nous distinction.
- **On the Defection of the Academics from Plato (Peri tēs tōn Akadēmaikōn pros Platōna Diastaseōs)** — polemical history of the Platonic Academy from Plato to Antiochus of Ascalon; argues that the New Academy (Arcesilaus, Carneades) betrayed Plato's genuine doctrine. Eusebius preserves long extracts in *Praep. Evang.* XIV.4–9.
- **On the Secret Doctrines in Plato (Peri tōn para Platōni Aporrhētōn)** — argues that Plato concealed his most important doctrines in symbolic / mythological form to protect them from the vulgar.
- **On Number (Peri Arithmōn)** — Neo-Pythagorean number-metaphysics; fragments via Iamblichus.
- **On Place (Peri Topou)** — physics / cosmology.

## Role in the tradition

Numenius is the **load-bearing bridge between Middle Platonism and the Plotinian / Neoplatonic synthesis**, and a key figure for understanding the cross-pollination of Platonism with Gnosticism, Hermeticism, and Jewish-Christian theology in the 2nd century CE.

### Toward Plotinus
The three-Gods doctrine and the matter-spirit dualism in Numenius are the immediate intellectual context Plotinus inherits. Plotinus's One / Nous / Soul triad is a refinement (with the crucial Plotinian moves of strict simplicity of the One and the abolition of cosmic dualism). The accusation of plagiarism is exaggerated but reflects the deep continuity.

### Numenius and the Gnostic / Hermetic stream
Numenius's interests overlap substantially with concerns visible in the Gnostic and Hermetic literature of his century:
- **Two-or-three-Gods structure.** The Numenian First-God / Second-God distinction parallels (without being identical to) the Gnostic First Father / demiurge distinction.
- **Sharp matter-spirit dualism.** Numenius is the most strongly anti-cosmic of the Middle Platonists; he sees matter as the principle of evil. This brings him close to the Gnostic anti-cosmic position.
- **Cross-cultural syncretic interest.** Numenius's enthusiasm for Brahman, Egyptian, Magian, and especially Jewish wisdom places him in the same intellectual culture that produced the Corpus Hermeticum and the philosophical Gnostic texts (Allogenes, Zostrianos, Marsanes).
- **Direct contact?** Whether Numenius knew Gnostic texts directly is debated; Plotinus's *Ennead* II.9 "Against the Gnostics" explicitly engages Sethian Gnostic texts (Allogenes, Zostrianos) and the philosophical Gnostic milieu — and Numenius was the figure whose work Plotinus was being accused of plagiarizing in the same period.

### Christian reception
Origen, Eusebius, and (via Eusebius) the Cappadocians knew Numenius. His friendliness to the Hebrew Bible ("What is Plato but Moses speaking Attic?") made him an unusually welcome pagan philosopher in patristic citation. Some scholars (Frede 1987) argue that Numenius's three-Gods doctrine influenced early Christian Trinitarian thinking; others (Edwards 1989, 2002) read the resemblance as structural-parallel rather than direct influence.

## Disputes
- **Pythagorean or Platonist?** Numenius is often called "Neo-Pythagorean" but his own framing is *both* — Plato is interpreted as Pythagoras's transmitter, so the distinction collapses for him. Modern scholarship (Dillon 1977; Tarrant 2020) places him within Middle Platonism with Neo-Pythagorean inflection.
- **Second God: one or two?** Some fragments suggest the Second God is itself dual (productive-creator and produced-cosmos aspects); others read this as the Second God / Third God distinction. The reconstruction is contested.
- **Influence on Plotinus.** Whether Plotinus's debt to Numenius is substantial (so Dodds, Frede) or moderate-and-mediated (so Dillon) is debated. The ancient plagiarism-accusation is rhetorical exaggeration but reflects real proximity.
- **Numenius and Gnosticism.** Whether Numenius directly engaged Gnostic texts, was peripherally aware of them, or simply moved in the same Middle-Platonist anti-cosmic intellectual current as some Gnostic authors is debated.
