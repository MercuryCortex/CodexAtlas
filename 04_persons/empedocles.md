# Empedocles of Acragas

## Identity
Empedocles (c. -495 to -435) was a **pre-Socratic Greek philosopher, poet, physician, and ritualist** from the Greek-Sicilian city of Acragas (modern Agrigento). He is the **originator of the four-element cosmology** (earth, water, air, fire) — which dominates Greek and Latin natural philosophy through Aristotle and persists into early-modern alchemy and medicine — and a major exponent of **the transmigration of souls** ([[reincarnation]]). His two philosophical poems, *On Nature* (Περὶ Φύσεως) and *Purifications* (Καθαρμοί), survive in fragmentary form, vastly expanded by the **Strasbourg Papyrus** (published 1999), which doubled the known corpus. For the Codex Atlas's mission Empedocles is doubly load-bearing: (1) his **four-element cosmology** is the *Stoic-medieval-Christian* substrate of all natural-philosophical cosmology before chemistry; (2) his **transmigration doctrine** is a key piece in the Greek-Pythagorean-Orphic transmigration tradition that feeds into [[plato|Plato]]'s *Phaedrus*, *Republic* X, and *Phaedo*, and that has scholarly-parallel resonance with Indian *saṃsāra* doctrines via possible Greek-Indian contact in the Persian and Hellenistic worlds.

## Historical evidence
Empedocles is documented in: (1) substantial fragments of his own works preserved by quotation in [[aristotle]], [[plotinus]], Theophrastus, Simplicius, Plutarch, and many others; (2) the **Strasbourg Papyrus** (P. Strasbourg gr. Inv. 1665–1666, recovered 1904, published 1999) — about 70 new lines of *On Nature*; (3) extensive biographical-doxographical material in **Diogenes Laertius** *Lives* 8.51–77 (~3rd c. CE) — full of legendary material (Empedocles healing the plague at Selinus, halting the etesian winds, throwing himself into Mount Etna to prove his divinity) but with a historical kernel.

## Works
- ***On Nature*** (Περὶ Φύσεως) — philosophical-cosmological poem in dactylic hexameter; ~5,000 lines in antiquity, perhaps 1,500–2,000 lines extant in fragments + Strasbourg. Treats the four elements, the cosmic forces of Love (Philia) and Strife (Neikos), and cosmogonic cycles.
- ***Purifications*** (Καθαρμοί) — religious-ritual poem on transmigration of souls, expiation for sin, and the cycle of divine fall and return. Whether *On Nature* and *Purifications* are **one work or two** is a major scholarly question (see Disputes below).

## Role in the tradition
Five distinct strands of Empedoclean influence:

1. **The four-element cosmology.** Empedocles is the **originator** of the four-element theory (earth, water, air, fire) — adopted and modified by [[aristotle]], canonized in Aristotelian-Stoic-medieval natural philosophy, and persisting as the dominant cosmological framework of Western thought until the 17th–18th c. chemical revolution. **All medieval Christian natural philosophy** — Augustine's discussions of the elements, Aquinas's natural-philosophical treatises, the entire medieval university *quadrivium* — operates within an Empedoclean-Aristotelian four-element frame.

2. **Love-and-Strife cosmology.** The cosmic forces of Philia (Love, drawing elements together) and Neikos (Strife, separating them) drive Empedocles's cosmogonic cycle. This **two-force cosmology** is structurally parallel to later cosmic-dualist systems (Zoroastrian, Manichaean, Gnostic) and is a major source for Hellenistic and Christian-Platonist conceptions of cosmic Eros and cosmic disruption. Cited by [[plato|Plato]] *Symposium*; [[plotinus]] *Enneads*; and the Christian-Platonist tradition.

3. **Transmigration / reincarnation.** Empedocles in *Purifications* describes his own past lives: **"I have already been a boy and a girl, a bush and a bird and a mute fish from the sea"** (DK B117). This is one of the most explicit early-Greek statements of [[reincarnation]] — alongside [[pythagoras]] and the Orphic tradition — and feeds into [[plato]]'s *Phaedrus* / *Republic* X / *Phaedo* eschatology. The Greek transmigration tradition is the principal channel by which reincarnation doctrines enter Hellenistic philosophy and from there contact (under contested historical conditions) Hellenistic Buddhism and Indian Vedantic thought; the structural parallels between Empedoclean and Indian transmigration are striking and are widely discussed (West 1971; Long 1948–49; McEvilley 2002).

4. **Ritual purification.** *Purifications* prescribes vegetarianism, abstention from blood-sacrifice, and personal ascetic-ritual discipline as the means of breaking the cycle of fallen incarnation. This is structurally close to Pythagorean and Orphic mystery-cult discipline and influences early-Christian ascetic regimes (vegetarianism in some early-Christian movements, Manichaean food-codes, etc.).

5. **Wonder-worker self-presentation.** Empedocles famously presents himself as a divine being walking among mortals: **"Friends, I declare to you — among you I walk no longer as a mortal man, but as an immortal god, garlanded, honored among all, fitting the holy fillets and the flowing wreaths"** (DK B112). This **divine self-attribution** is one of the most striking pre-Christian self-deification statements in Greek philosophy and is read in late antiquity as a wonder-worker precedent (Apollonius of Tyana, etc.).

## Disputes
- **One poem or two?** Whether *On Nature* and *Purifications* are one continuous poem or two distinct works has been disputed since antiquity. The Strasbourg Papyrus (1999) showed that the two contain integrated material (a Strasbourg fragment connects what Diogenes Laertius classified as both poems), which has pushed many scholars toward the one-poem view (Inwood 2001; Janko 2004 — strongly one-poem); others maintain the distinction (Wright 1981; Trépanier 2004). Currently active scholarly question.
- **Death of Empedocles.** The famous Etna leap (Empedocles throwing himself into the volcano to prove his divinity; only his bronze sandal is recovered) is legendary; he most likely died in the Peloponnese in exile. Diogenes Laertius preserves multiple competing death-stories.
- **Indian parallels.** McEvilley 2002 *The Shape of Ancient Thought* argues for substantial Indian-Greek philosophical contact in the pre-Socratic period, with Empedocles a major case. The argument is contested but the structural parallels are striking enough that the question remains live.
- **Strasbourg Papyrus interpretation.** The 1999 publication continues to generate substantial scholarly literature — the new fragments are still being absorbed into the philosophical interpretation of Empedocles.
