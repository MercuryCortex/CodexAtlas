# Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai (Rashbi)

## Identity
Tannaitic rabbi of the mid-2nd-c. CE Galilee, student of Rabbi Akiva, a prominent figure of the post-Bar-Kokhba generation. Tradition narrates that he and his son Rabbi Eleazar hid for 12 (or 13) years in a cave at Peki'in to escape Roman persecution after Shim'on cursed Roman rule; in the cave he received heavenly revelation. The historical Shim'on is moderately attested in the Talmud (the Mishnah cites him frequently); the *legendary* Shim'on is the traditional author and revealer of the *Zohar* — a tradition that modern critical scholarship since Scholem treats as a 13th-c. pseudepigraphic attribution by Moses de León rather than as historical authorship.

## Historical evidence
- Tannaitic: substantial Mishnaic and Talmudic citations attest the historical rabbi.
- Zoharic: the *Zohar*'s authorship by Rashbi is the universal traditional position; the critical-scholarly consensus since Scholem 1941 (re-articulated by Liebes, Idel, etc.) attributes most of the Zoharic corpus to Moses de León (c. 1240–1305), Castile.

## Role in the tradition
- **Tannaitic source**: a significant Mishnaic authority; the "Halakha is according to Rabbi Shim'on" pattern.
- **Lag BaOmer**: the 33rd day of the Omer count is the traditional date of his death (yahrzeit); a major popular Jewish-mystical festival, especially at Meron where his traditional tomb is located. The annual gathering at Meron is one of the largest Orthodox-mystical celebrations in Israel.
- **Zoharic literature**: the central narrative voice; the *Idrot* (Great and Small Assemblies) and the bulk of the body of the *Zohar* are Rashbi-attributed teachings to his circle.

## Disputes
- Authorship of the Zohar (the long-running traditional-vs.-critical debate).
- Historicity of cave-revelation narrative.
