# ʿĀʾisha bint Abī Bakr

## Identity
**ʿĀʾisha bint Abī Bakr al-Ṣiddīq (~614–678 CE)** — known by the honorific *Umm al-Muʾminīn* (Mother of the Believers) and by Muhammad's affectionate epithet *al-Ḥumayrāʾ* (the Reddish-One) — is the **third wife of Muhammad** (after Khadīja and Sawda), the **daughter of [[abu-bakr|Abū Bakr]]** (the first Sunni Rashidun caliph), and **one of the two foundational hadith transmitters of the early Sunni hadith corpus** (~2,210 hadith attributed to her, behind only Abū Hurayra in volume). She married Muhammad in Medina shortly after the Hegira (per traditional sources, at age 9; per modern critical scholarship, the age is contested) and was widowed at Muhammad's death in 632 ([[event-death-of-muhammad-632]]). She lived as Umm al-Muʾminīn in Medina for the next 46 years (632-678), becoming the **principal female hadith authority of the early Muslim community** and a major political-religious figure. In 656, she **led the rebel anti-ʿAlī faction at the Battle of the Camel** (the first major engagement of the [[event-first-fitna-656-661|First Fitna]]) — riding in a camel-litter at the center of the battle line. The defeated faction was spared by ʿAlī; ʿĀʾisha was returned to Medina with full honor and lived quietly until her death in 678. The **ambivalence of ʿĀʾisha's reception in Shīʿī tradition** stems entirely from the Battle of the Camel — Sunni tradition venerates her unreservedly; Shīʿī tradition is critical of her opposition to ʿAlī while acknowledging her hadith-transmitter status. The Sunni-Shīʿī divergence on ʿĀʾisha is one of the **principal foundational-period sectarian fault lines**.

## Historical evidence
Documented extensively. ʿĀʾisha appears in **massive volume in the Sunni hadith corpus** (Bukhārī, Muslim, the Sunan collections), in the early Sira (Ibn Ishaq), and in early historical-biographical literature (Ṭabarī, Ibn Saʿd). Independent Shīʿī sources (al-Kāfī, etc.) also treat her extensively. Modern critical scholarship (Spellberg 1994; Watt 1956) has secure documentary access to her life and role.

## Works
- **No written works.** ʿĀʾisha is foundational for the hadith *tradition* (as transmitter), not for written texts. Her role is as primary-source narrator of Muhammad's biographical and doctrinal Sunna.

## Role in the tradition
- **Foundational hadith transmitter.** ~2,210 hadith attributed to ʿĀʾisha across the canonical Sunni collections. Her hadith concern especially: Muhammad's personal life and household practices, the revelation-occasions of Quranic passages (*asbāb al-nuzūl*), Muhammad's prayer practice, his physical description and personal habits, and his theological clarifications on matters arising in the Medinan period. ʿĀʾisha's hadith are central to **Sunni hadith methodology**: she is a canonical isnād (chain-of-transmission) authority whose narrations are presumptively *ṣaḥīḥ* (sound) absent specific counter-evidence.
- **The *ḥadīth al-ifk* (Slander of ʿĀʾisha) incident, 627.** ʿĀʾisha was lost from a Muslim caravan returning from a raid and rejoined the caravan the next morning escorted by a young man (Ṣafwān ibn al-Muʿaṭṭal); rumors of impropriety circulated; Muhammad eventually received Quranic revelation (Quran 24:11-20) **vindicating ʿĀʾisha** and establishing the *hadd*-penalty for false accusation of *zinā* (adultery) at 80 lashes. The *ḥadīth al-ifk* is the principal narrative-context for the Quranic legal penalty of *qadhf* (false accusation) and is foundational for Islamic law of testimony and reputation.
- **Battle of the Camel, 656.** ʿĀʾisha was a principal figurehead of the post-Uthmān anti-ʿAlī faction. After the assassination of [[uthman|ʿUthmān]] in 656, ʿĀʾisha joined with Ṭalḥa and al-Zubayr in opposing ʿAlī's caliphate (claim: ʿAlī had not pursued ʿUthmān's killers vigorously enough). The faction marched on Basra; the resulting **Battle of the Camel (656)** — named for the camel ʿĀʾisha rode in a litter at the center of the army — was the first major engagement of the First Fitna. ʿAlī's forces won; ʿĀʾisha's litter was disabled by archers killing the camels of her bodyguard; she was returned to Medina with honor. Her political role ended after the Battle of the Camel; she lived in Medina until her death in 678.
- **Sunni-Shīʿī divergence figure.** Sunni tradition venerates ʿĀʾisha unreservedly as Umm al-Muʾminīn, hadith authority, and beloved wife of Muhammad. Shīʿī tradition, while acknowledging her formal status, is **critical of her opposition to ʿAlī at the Battle of the Camel** and is correspondingly more reserved in citation of her hadith. The intra-Sunni / Shīʿī divergence on ʿĀʾisha is foundational; modern intra-Muslim polemic still re-fights the Battle of the Camel along sectarian lines.

## Disputes
- **ʿĀʾisha's age at marriage.** Traditional Sunni hadith-tradition (Bukhārī, citing ʿĀʾisha herself): age 6 at marriage-contract, age 9 at consummation. **Modern critical scholarship** is divided: some scholars (Spellberg 1994) accept the traditional figures as historical with the caveat that pre-pubertal marriage was culturally distinct in 7th-c. Hijazi context; others (modern reform-minded scholars) argue the figures are textually anomalous and may reflect later-tradition reconstruction. The traditional figures are well-attested in the hadith corpus; their interpretation is a major site of modern intra-Muslim and Muslim-Western dispute.
- **Authenticity of attributed hadith.** Some Shīʿī and modern-critical scholarship questions the authenticity of the highest-volume ʿĀʾisha narrations; Sunni hadith-tradition treats most as canonical *ṣaḥīḥ*.
- **Battle of the Camel motivations.** Sunni-traditional view: ʿĀʾisha was motivated by legitimate grievance over ʿAlī's slow response to ʿUthmān's killers. Shīʿī-traditional view: ʿĀʾisha's opposition to ʿAlī was politically opportunistic. Modern critical view: complex; the political opposition has real grievance-content with personal-history overlay.
