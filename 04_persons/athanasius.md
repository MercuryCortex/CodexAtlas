# Athanasius of Alexandria

## Identity
Bishop of Alexandria from 328 until his death in 373 — with five exiles totaling some seventeen years for his uncompromising opposition to Arianism. Defender of the Nicene *homoousios* (consubstantiality of Father and Son) through the long fourth-century Trinitarian controversies, and architect of the Christological framework that has dominated catholic Christianity ever since.

## Historical evidence
Documented in his own extensive corpus (*Orations against the Arians*, festal letters, apologetic works), in Eusebius and the later church historians (Socrates, Sozomen, Theodoret), and in the *Apophthegmata Patrum*. His role at Nicaea (325) as deacon and assistant to Bishop Alexander of Alexandria is firmly attested.

## Works
- *On the Incarnation* (with its paired *Contra Gentes*) — the foundational text of the doctrine of *theosis*: "He became human that we might become divine."
- *Orations against the Arians* (3 books) — sustained polemic against the Arian subordinationist Christology.
- *Letter to Serapion* — extended the Nicene *homoousios* to the Holy Spirit, anticipating the 381 Constantinopolitan settlement.
- *Vita Antonii* (~360) — the founding text of monastic hagiography; translated into Latin within decades and disseminated across the Christian world.
- Festal Letter 39 (367) — the earliest surviving document listing exactly the 27 New Testament books that constitute the current canon.

## Role in the tradition
Called the "pillar of orthodoxy" by Gregory of Nazianzus. His *Vita Antonii* shaped Western monasticism via Augustine (*Confessions* VIII.6 cites it as instrumental in his conversion). His Christology was vindicated definitively at Constantinople 381 and remained the touchstone for both East and West.

## Connections — substrate and influence
- Athanasius's [[logos-cosmic-reason]] Christology directly inherits Philo's [[logos-philonic]] (the *Logos* as God's instrumental reason in creation) — Anatolios 1998 traces the dependence explicitly; Philo's Alexandrian-Jewish Logos doctrine is the proximate Hellenistic-Jewish substrate of *De Incarnatione*'s claim that the Word "frames the universe."
- Behind Philo stands Stoic [[logos-cosmic-reason]] (Chrysippus, Cleanthes) and ultimately [[heraclitus]]'s cosmic *logos*. The Athanasian Christ-Logos is, structurally, the Stoic-Heraclitean *logos* with a personal name — Hanson 1988 and Pelikan, *Christianity and Classical Culture* (1993) trace the lineage.
- His *Vita Antonii* canonized [[antony-the-great]]'s Egyptian desert spirituality, which itself sits in continuity with older Egyptian ascetic-priestly traditions documented since at least the New Kingdom (Brakke 1995: 1–22).
- Theological vocabulary: *ousia*, *hypostasis*, *physis* — all Aristotelian and Stoic substance-metaphysical terms repurposed for Christian Trinitarian discourse.

## Disputes
- The authenticity of certain late letters and the precise dating of *Contra Gentes* / *De Incarnatione* (Anatolios 1998 favors a date c. 328–335 against the older 318 dating).
