# Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen)

## The First Experimental Scientist

Ibn al-Haytham (965–1040 CE) is the first recorded scientist to systematically use controlled experiment to refute a theory — the founding act of the experimental scientific method. His *Kitāb al-manāẓir* (*Book of Optics*, 7 volumes, c. 1021 CE) disproved the Greek "emission theory" of vision (the Pythagorean and Euclidean claim that eyes emit rays to perceive objects), established that light travels from objects to the eye, and introduced the camera obscura as an experimental demonstration device.

His method: pose a theory, design a controlled experiment that would distinguish between the theory and its alternative, observe the result, revise the theory. This is not Greek philosophical argument or Islamic theological reasoning — it is the experimental method that Francis Bacon claimed to invent in 1620, six centuries after al-Haytham practiced it.

## MASSIVE WIN — Intromission Theory and the Experimental Method

The debate: does vision work by emission (eyes send out rays) or by intromission (objects send in images)? Greek tradition was divided: Euclid and Ptolemy assumed emission (geometric optics works either way); Aristotle assumed intromission but without rigorous analysis.

Al-Haytham's experiment: if emission theory is correct, looking at a bright light should cause pain (the eye's rays would be reflected back). If intromission theory is correct, the pain comes from the bright object's intensity, not the eye's rays. He designed experiments using the camera obscura and systematic observation to distinguish the two theories — and concluded in favor of intromission. The argument was not philosophical but empirical: data, not authority.

## MASSIVE WIN — The Transmission Chain to Modern Optics

```
Ibn al-Haytham, Cairo (c. 1021 CE)
     ↓ Latin translation (c. 1250 CE, "De Aspectibus" / "Perspectiva")
Roger Bacon (c. 1265), Witelo (c. 1270), John Peckham (c. 1280)
     ↓
Kepler, Dioptrice (1611) — theory of retinal image formation
     ↓
Descartes, Dioptrique (1637) — laws of refraction
     ↓
Newton, Opticks (1704) — dispersion of white light
     ↓
Modern optics, photography, telescopy, spectroscopy
```

Every photograph, every telescope observation, every optical fiber cable is downstream of ibn al-Haytham's 11th-century experiments in Cairo.
