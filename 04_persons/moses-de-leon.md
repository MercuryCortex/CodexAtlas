# Moses de León

## Identity
Moshe ben Shem-Tov de León (~1240–1305) was a Castilian Kabbalist, the principal author of the *Sefer ha-Zohar*, the foundational classical text of Kabbalah. Born in León, Castile; spent his early adulthood in Guadalajara (where he produced or began the Zoharic corpus in the 1280s); moved later to Ávila. He distributed Zohar manuscripts to wealthy patrons in the 1280s and 1290s, claiming to have transcribed them from an ancient manuscript of R. Shimon bar Yochai. After his death in 1305, his widow reportedly confessed to Yitzhak ben Shmuel of Akko that her husband had composed the work himself (the confession is reported in Yitzhak's surviving notebook).

## Historical evidence
Documented through his own surviving works (under his name), the Yitzhak ben Shmuel of Akko notebook, and references in contemporary Castilian Jewish documents. Modern Scholem-school philological analysis of the Zohar (since 1941) decisively establishes Moses de León as principal author through the artificiality of the Zohar's "Aramaic" (post-Talmudic neologisms and grammatical patterns).

## Works
Apart from the Zohar (which he never claimed in his own name): *Sefer ha-Rimon*, *Mishkan ha-Edut*, *Sheqel ha-Qodesh*, and several shorter Hebrew Kabbalistic treatises. These signed works substantially overlap doctrinally with the Zohar and provide the philological evidence for Zoharic authorship.

## Role in the tradition
Author of the most consequential text of Jewish mysticism. Tradition continued to attribute the Zohar to R. Shimon bar Yochai for centuries; the medieval critic Elia del Medigo (1496) was the first to publicly question this; modern academic scholarship (Scholem 1941) has fully established Moses de León's authorship.

## Disputes
Whether Moses de León worked alone (Scholem) or with a Castilian Kabbalist circle (Liebes 1993). Some material strata in the Zohar may derive from older inherited materials.
