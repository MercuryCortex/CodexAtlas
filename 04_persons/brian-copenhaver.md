# Brian P. Copenhaver

## Identity
American historian of Renaissance and early-modern philosophy; Distinguished Professor of Philosophy and History at UCLA. Translator of the standard modern English *Hermetica* (Cambridge UP 1992) — the canonical reference for any English-language reader of the Greek Corpus Hermeticum and the Latin Asclepius. Author of monographs on Pico della Mirandola, Renaissance magic, and the Western philosophical tradition.

## Historical evidence
Living scholar; UCLA faculty page; Cambridge UP and Oxford UP catalogs document his books. Cited 5+ times across the Codex Atlas Hermetic node-cluster as the standard English source.

## Works
- *Hermetica: The Greek Corpus Hermeticum and the Latin Asclepius* (Cambridge UP 1992) — the standard English.
- *Renaissance Philosophy* (with Charles Schmitt; Oxford UP 1992) — the textbook.
- *Magic in Western Culture: From Antiquity to the Enlightenment* (Cambridge UP 2015).
- *Magic and the Dignity of Man: Pico della Mirandola and His Oration in Modern Memory* (Harvard UP 2019).

## Role in the tradition
The standard English-language access point to the Hermetica. Where Fowden gave us the Egyptian-historical context and Mahé gave us the Coptic-Armenian recovery, Copenhaver gave us the readable English translation that put Hermetic studies on the standard graduate-school syllabus.

## Disputes
The notorious gap in Copenhaver's *Hermetica* is the **omission of the Stobaean fragments** (Kore Kosmou and the other 28 excerpts preserved in Stobaeus's *Anthologion*). Copenhaver explained in his preface that he restricted himself to the Greek Corpus and the Latin Asclepius for textual-critical reasons; the Stobaean fragments would have required a separate volume. The consequence is that English-language scholarly access to the Stobaean Hermetica remains weaker than to the Corpus — a real gap in modern Hermetic studies.
