# Lucius Annaeus Seneca

## Identity
Roman Stoic philosopher, tragedian, tutor of the emperor Nero, and one of the wealthiest men in 1st-c. Rome. Born in Cordoba (Spain) c. 4 BCE; exiled to Corsica under Claudius; recalled to Rome to tutor the young Nero; effectively co-administered the early Neronian principate with Burrus; fell from favor and was forced to commit suicide on Nero's orders in 65 CE. His Stoic prose works — especially the 124 surviving *Epistulae Morales* — are the most influential Latin Stoic corpus in the Western tradition.

## Historical evidence
Substantial. Tacitus *Annals* XIII–XV gives a major narrative; Suetonius's *Nero* and Cassius Dio also cover the period; Seneca's own letters and treatises are voluminous self-testimony.

## Works
- *Epistulae Morales* (124 letters) — the most-read ancient Stoic text in modern times.
- *De Brevitate Vitae*, *De Tranquillitate Animi*, *De Providentia*, *De Vita Beata* — popular philosophical dialogues.
- *Naturales Quaestiones* — Stoic natural philosophy.
- Tragedies (*Medea*, *Thyestes*, *Hercules Furens*, etc.) — Senecan tragedy is foundational for Renaissance drama.

## Role in the tradition
- Christian reception: Tertullian called Seneca "almost one of us"; an apocryphal correspondence with Paul circulated in late antiquity; medieval Christian humanists read him as proto-Christian moralist.
- Renaissance: foundational for *Neo-Stoicism* (Justus Lipsius, 16th c.); important for Montaigne.
- Modern: continuing relevance as practical ethical guide.

## Disputes
- The philosophical-rhetorical question: does Seneca innovate philosophically, or is he a popularizing rhetor recycling Greek Stoic doctrine?
- The political question: complicity with Neronian tyranny vs. attempted moderation.
