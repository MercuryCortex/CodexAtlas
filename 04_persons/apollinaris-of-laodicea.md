# Apollinaris of Laodicea

## Identity
Bishop of Laodicea in Syria from around 360. Famous Christian intellectual of the 4th c. (his father, Apollinaris the Elder, was a grammarian at Laodicea; both father and son composed Christian classical-style poetry — the *Christ in Hymns* etc. — in response to Julian the Apostate's 362 ban on Christian teachers using pagan texts). Strong anti-Arian; close friend of Athanasius and Basil of Caesarea in earlier life. His Christology — that in Christ the divine Logos *replaced* the rational human soul — was found problematic from around 374 onward; condemned at synods at Rome (377), Antioch (379), and definitively at Constantinople (381). Continued to gather disciples; died around 390. The "Apollinarian" Christology was the principal target of Gregory of Nyssa's *Antirrheticus* and Gregory of Nazianzus's *Epistles 101–102 to Cledonius*.

## Historical evidence
Apollinaris's own works survive almost exclusively through:
1. Quotations by opponents (especially the two Gregories, Basil's later correspondence).
2. The "Apollinarian forgeries" — Apollinarian dogmatic texts circulated under orthodox names to evade post-381 censorship. Detected by Caspari (1879) and Lietzmann (1904), who identified pseudo-Athanasian, pseudo-Gregorian, and pseudo-Julian texts as Apollinarian.

## Works
- ***On the Incarnation of God the Word*** (fragments) — the principal statement of his Christology.
- ***On the Union*** (fragments).
- The "Apollinarian forgeries" — including the influential **pseudo-Athanasian formula *mia physis tou theou logou sesarkomene*** ("one incarnate nature of the divine Word"). This formula, which Cyril of Alexandria would later use believing it Athanasian, was actually Apollinarian — placing a piece of Apollinarian Christology at the very foundation of the later 5th-c. Cyrillian / Miaphysite Christological tradition. **A central irony of patristic history.**

## Role in the tradition
Condemned at Constantinople 381 (which fixed the formula "perfect God and perfect man, with a rational soul"). His Christology — divine Logos *plus* fleshly body, no human rational soul — was rejected because the *unassumed is unhealed* (Gregory of Nazianzus's principle: what Christ does not assume, he does not save). Indirectly his **vocabulary** (*mia physis*, single subject) was preserved through the Apollinarian forgeries' attribution to Athanasius, and feeds via Cyril into the great Christological controversies of the 5th c.

## Connections — substrate and influence
- His Christology has been read (Lietzmann, Spoerl) as drawing on **Stoic** *krasis* / "blending" metaphysics — the divine and the fleshly mixed in the way Stoic spirit (*pneuma*) permeates matter. The substrate is Stoic physics applied to Christology.
- The replacement-of-soul model has a structural relation to certain forms of pagan *theios anēr* / divine-man theology, where a god possesses or replaces a human's higher faculties — a substrate motif in Hellenistic religion (oracular possession, mystery-cult union).
- **The Apollinarian forgeries are the most consequential pseudepigraphic operation in patristic Christology**: the *mia physis* formula attributed to Athanasius reaches Cyril of Alexandria as authentic Athanasian doctrine, becomes the keystone of Cyrillian Christology, and is therefore foundational for both Chalcedonian and Miaphysite traditions. Lietzmann 1904, Hanson 1988: 633–650.

## Disputes
- **Date of his death.** 390 is approximate; sources are unclear.
- **Scope of the forgeries.** Lietzmann's identifications are mostly accepted; some borderline cases remain debated.
