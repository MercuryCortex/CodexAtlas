# Hūd

Quranic prophet sent to the people of **ʿĀd**, a powerful ancient Arabian people known for their monumental construction (Surah 89:6–8 refers to "ʿĀd of the pillars"). The ʿĀd refused Hūd's call to monotheism and were destroyed by a devastating wind (Surah 7:65–72, 11:50–60, 26:123–140, 46:21–26, 89:6–8).

## Quranic profile

Hūd appears in four extended narrative passages:
- **Surah 7:65–72** — Hūd sent to ʿĀd; they reject him; divine punishment by wind
- **Surah 11:50–60** — longest account; Hūd's theological argument with the ʿĀd
- **Surah 26:123–140** — abbreviated but structurally parallel to the Ṣāliḥ and Shuʿayb narratives in the same surah
- **Surah 46:21–26** — framed as Hūd's warning before the "barren wind"

The **name of Surah 11** (Hūd) indicates his canonical prominence. Surah 11 juxtaposes the punishment-narratives of Noah, Hūd, Ṣāliḥ, Abraham/Lot, Shuʿayb, and Moses as a typological chain of prophetic rejection.

## Purely Quranic figure

Hūd has no counterpart in the Hebrew Bible or New Testament. He belongs to the Quran's distinctive category of **Arabian prophets** (alongside Ṣāliḥ and Shuʿayb) — figures within an indigenous Arabian prophetic tradition that the Quran presents as parallel to but independent of the Mosaic and Jesuanic lines. Wheeler 2002 (ch. 4) discusses the scholarly debate over whether these Arabian prophets draw on pre-Islamic South Arabian religious memory or are Quranic theological constructions.

The ʿĀd people are also mentioned in pre-Islamic Arabian poetry and South Arabian inscriptions, suggesting a pre-Quranic legendary tradition that the Quran transforms into a prophetic-punishment narrative.
