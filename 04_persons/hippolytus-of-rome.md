# Hippolytus of Rome

## Identity
A leading Roman presbyter, theologian, and **heresiologist** of the early 3rd century, author of the ***Refutation of All Heresies*** (a 10-book systematic anti-heretical work, the principal source for many Gnostic and Modalist positions). Schismatic at Rome from ~+217 — opposed Pope Callistus on disciplinary, theological, and Christological grounds — and the first known **antipope** (claimed the Roman episcopal title himself). Exiled with Pope Pontianus to Sardinia ~+235 under Maximinus Thrax, where both died. Their bodies were returned to Rome and the schism healed posthumously.

A major scholarly problem (the **"Hippolytus problem"** — Cerrato 2002, Brent 1995) is whether the corpus attached to "Hippolytus" is the work of one Roman presbyter or several distinct authors (a Roman Hippolytus, an Eastern Hippolytus, possibly a school). The traditional single-author view has been substantially shaken since the 1990s.

## Historical evidence
The works are extant in various manuscript traditions. Eusebius and Jerome supply the biographical sketch. A 3rd-c. statue (now in the Vatican Library) labeled with Hippolytus's *Easter calculation* anchors the chronology.

## Works
- ***Refutation of All Heresies / Philosophoumena*** — the principal source for many 2nd-c. heretical positions, especially Sethian Gnosticism (book 5), Valentinianism (book 6), Basilideanism (book 7), and the Modalist controversies (book 9). Rediscovered in 1842 (book 1 had been known earlier as Origen's *Philosophoumena*).
- ***Contra Noetum*** — anti-Modalist polemic.
- ***Apostolic Tradition*** — church-order text (baptismal liturgy, ordination prayers, Eucharist, ascetic discipline) of immense liturgical-historical importance. Attribution to Hippolytus is contested by Bradshaw et al. (2002), who suggest a multi-stage compilation.
- ***Commentary on Daniel*** (~+205) — the **earliest extant Christian biblical commentary**.
- ***De Christo et Antichristo*** — apocalyptic-eschatological work.

## Role in the tradition
- **Foundational heresiology**: *Refutation* is one of the principal sources for reconstructing 2nd-c. Gnostic systems.
- **Earliest extant biblical commentary** (Daniel).
- **Liturgical history**: the *Apostolic Tradition* is the central document for early-3rd-c. Roman Christian liturgy.
- **First antipope**: a striking institutional precedent.
- **Christological subordinationism**: Hippolytus's "two-economies" Logos theology (Logos as God's Word, distinct from the Father since the foundation of creation) influenced Origen and the subsequent Eastern subordinationist tradition.

## Disputes
- **The Hippolytus problem**: one Roman author or multiple? Mainstream is moving toward at least two — a Roman Hippolytus author of *Refutation* and *Contra Noetum*, an Eastern Hippolytus author of the *Commentary on Daniel* and *De Christo et Antichristo*. The traditional fusion may be Eusebian.
- **Authorship of the *Apostolic Tradition***: contested; even traditionally-Hippolytan attribution is doubted by Bradshaw 2002.
- **Position vs. Callistus**: theologically Hippolytus was more conservative on Christology, more rigorist on penance.
