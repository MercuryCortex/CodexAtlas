# Tulsidas (Goswami Tulsidas)

## Identity
North Indian Hindu bhakti poet-saint, devotee of Rama, and author of the *Ramcharitmanas* (1532–1623). Born to a Saryupareen Brahmin family (traditional account); lost both parents young per tradition; became a wandering ascetic and student under Narharidas (or Narayandas) of the Ramanandi tradition; finally settled at Banaras (Varanasi) where he composed most of his works. Begun the *Ramcharitmanas* at Ayodhya on Ram Navami 1574 and completed at Banaras 1576 per his own internal dating. Tradition reports interactions with the Mughal court of Akbar (historicity uncertain) and with Mughal officials (firmer); the legend that he turned aside Mughal harassment by miracle is among many later accretions to his hagiography. Tulsidas's lifelong project was making Rama-bhakti accessible in vernacular Hindi-Awadhi — a project of pan-North-Indian devotional consolidation that succeeded so thoroughly that the *Ramcharitmanas* remains the central religious text of Hindi-speaking Vaishnavas.

## Historical evidence
Documented through his own works' internal references, hagiographical literature (especially Priyadas's *Bhaktirasabodhini*, c. 1712, in the Nabhadas *Bhaktamala* tradition), Banaras inscriptional and architectural traces, and continuous Ramanandi tradition.

## Works
*Ramcharitmanas* (1574–1576). *Vinaya Patrika* (devotional petitions). *Kavitavali* (Brajbhasha verses on Rama's life). *Hanuman Chalisa* (40 verses on Hanuman — among the most widely-recited Hindu devotional texts). *Dohavali* (collection of *doha* couplets). *Geetavali* (Rama lyrics). *Krishna Gitavali* (Krishna lyrics — minor compared to his Rama corpus). *Janaki Mangal*, *Parvati Mangal*, *Barvai Ramayan*.

## Role in the tradition
The founder of the North Indian Hindi vernacular Rama-bhakti devotional tradition. The *Ramcharitmanas* is the most-read religious text of the Hindi-speaking Hindu world; Tulsidas is one of the founding figures of modern Hindi literature; his devotional theology shaped pan-Indian religious imagination including Gandhi's *Ram-rajya* and modern Hindu political vocabulary. His late *Vinaya Patrika* ([[phase-6-030-vinaya-patrika]]) is among the most personally confessional pre-modern Indian devotional texts. Part of the broader [[event-bhakti-movement-c-700-1700|bhakti movement]] arc.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Akbar-period Hindu-Muslim devotional milieu**: Tulsidas's late career (1580s–1623) is the height of the Akbar-period *sulh-i kull* atmosphere; the documented (though hagiographically inflated) interactions with Mughal officials are part of the broader Hindu-Sufi devotional cross-fertilization context.
- **Modern Hindu nationalist political vocabulary**: Gandhi's *Ram-rajya* (Rule of Rama as political ideal) and the Hindutva movement's invocation of Rāma have Tulsidas's *Ramcharitmanas* as the principal popular religious-textual source. The political invocation has been variously assessed — pluralist (Gandhi) and nationalist-exclusivist (Hindutva) — within a shared textual tradition.
