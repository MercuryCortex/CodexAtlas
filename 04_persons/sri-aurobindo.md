# Sri Aurobindo

## Identity
Bengali nationalist-revolutionary-turned-yogi (1872–1950); educated at Cambridge (King's College); active in Indian independence agitation 1906–1910; arrested for the Alipore Bomb Case (1908); after his 1909 acquittal moved to French Pondicherry (1910) and remained there for the rest of his life as a yogic-philosophical teacher. Author of *The Life Divine*, *Synthesis of Yoga*, *Essays on the Gita*, and the epic poem *Savitri*. Collaborator with Mirra Alfassa ("the Mother," 1878–1973), who led the Sri Aurobindo Ashram after his death.

## Role in the tradition
Founder of Integral Yoga; the philosophical-systematic register of 20th-century Hindu modernism, complementary to [[swami-vivekananda]]'s missional-popular register and [[paramahansa-yogananda]]'s American-popular register.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Hindu modernism's intellectual lineage**: Aurobindo's project — recovering the Vedic and Upaniṣadic sources as substantively philosophical and contemporarily live, and integrating them with Western evolutionary metaphysics (Bergson, Hegel, Whitehead-adjacent positions) — is the philosophical capstone of the 19th-c. Hindu reform tradition begun by [[ram-mohan-roy]], systematized by [[dayananda-saraswati]] and [[swami-vivekananda]], and given its most metaphysically ambitious articulation by Aurobindo. *The Life Divine* is the most-cited 20th-c. Hindu metaphysical work in English.
- **Western reception**: Aurobindo's *Life Divine* and *Synthesis of Yoga* have been substantially read by 20th–21st-c. integral-spirituality and consciousness-studies movements (Ken Wilber's Integral Theory explicitly builds on Aurobindo; the Auroville community founded 1968 institutionalizes the Aurobindonian-Mother project).
