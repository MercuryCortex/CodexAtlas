# Vidyaranya

## Identity
14th-century Advaita Vedānta philosopher, monastic head of the Sringeri *maṭha* (the southern of the four Daśanāmi *maṭhas* traditionally founded by Shankara), and political-ideological architect of the early Vijayanagara empire. Born Mādhava (the regnal-administrative name *Mādhavācārya* is the secular form; *Vidyāraṇya* is his post-renunciation monastic name); brother of the great Vedic commentator Sāyaṇa (~1315–1387). Together the brothers were the principal intellectual figures of the Sangama dynasty Vijayanagara court (founded 1336): Sāyaṇa's vast Vedic commentary corpus (*Vedārthaprakāśa*) and Mādhava-Vidyāraṇya's Advaita and *Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha* projects together constitute the most ambitious systematizing intellectual enterprise of medieval Hinduism.

## Works
- ***Pañcadaśī*** (Fifteen-Chapter Treatise): the cardinal medieval textbook of Advaita Vedānta, organized in three sections of five chapters each (Viveka, Dīpa, Ānanda — Discrimination, Illumination, Bliss). The standard manual through which Advaita has been taught for the past six centuries.
- ***Jīvanmukti-viveka*** (Discrimination of the Living-Liberated): the cardinal Advaita treatment of *jīvanmukti* — the doctrine that liberation can be achieved in this life, before death. Central for the Advaita-yogic synthesis.
- ***Vivaraṇa-prameya-saṅgraha***: systematization of the Vivaraṇa-school Advaita interpretation (the post-Padmapāda lineage of Advaita commentary).
- ***Anubhūti-prakāśa***: poetic-meditative work.
- ***Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha*** (Compendium of All Philosophical Systems): the most-studied medieval Indian doxographical survey of philosophical schools — Cārvāka, Buddhism, Jain, Rāmānuja's Viśiṣṭādvaita, Pāṇini's grammar-school, Sāṃkhya, Yoga, Mīmāṃsā, Advaita. Treats the systems as a hierarchical progression culminating in Advaita. Authorship is traditionally Mādhava; some modern scholarship attributes to his brother Sāyaṇa or to a school production.

## Role in the tradition
- **Advaita systematization**: Vidyāraṇya is the principal medieval systematizer of Advaita Vedanta between Shankara (~800) and the early modern revival. The *Pañcadaśī* and *Jīvanmukti-viveka* are the standard manuals through which Advaita has been transmitted in pedagogy.
- **Sringeri matha**: Vidyāraṇya is by tradition the 12th *jagadguru* of the Sringeri *Śāradā-pīṭham*. The Sringeri matha became, under his and successor patronage, the institutional nerve-center of Smarta-Advaita Hindu orthodoxy in South India.
- **Vijayanagara empire's political theology**: tradition (firm in some sources, semi-legendary in others) credits Vidyāraṇya with the foundational counsel that established the Vijayanagara empire in 1336 — the Sangama brothers Harihara and Bukka, raised in Vijayanagara to oppose the Tughluq Sultanate's pressure on the southern Deccan, are said to have received Vidyāraṇya's blessing and ideological framing for the new empire. Vijayanagara presented itself as a Hindu state defending dharma against the Delhi Sultanate / Bahmani; Vidyāraṇya's intellectual program — Sāyaṇa's Vedic commentaries, his own Advaita systematization, the *Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha*'s doxographical assertion of Hindu philosophical comprehensiveness — gave the new state an explicit ideological coherence.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Hindu state-religion crystallization under Islamic political pressure**: the Vijayanagara project of which Vidyāraṇya was the principal intellectual architect is one of the clearest pre-modern South Asian cases of "Hinduism" as a self-consciously consolidated category constructed in response to political-religious external pressure (the Delhi Sultanate, the Bahmani Sultanate). Pollock 1993, Ali 2004, and Stein 1989 trace this institutional-ideological consolidation; modern Hindutva ideologues (Savarkar onward — see [[v-d-savarkar]] and [[phase-7-029-hindutva]]) have drawn explicit lineage from this Vijayanagara moment.
- **Comparative imperial religious systematization**: parallel in structure to Byzantine Christological consolidation under Justinian, to Mughal *sulh-i kull* under Akbar 250 years later, to Tibetan dGe lugs imperial systematization under the Fifth Dalai Lama — pre-modern states deploying religious-philosophical systematization as state-coherence work.
- **Doxography as cross-tradition genre**: the *Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha*'s genre of "compendium of all philosophical positions" has structural parallels in Jain doxographies (Haribhadra's *Ṣaḍ-darśana-samuccaya*, 8th c.), in Buddhist Tibetan *grub mtha'* (siddhānta) literature, and in late-antique Greek doxographical surveys (Diogenes Laertius). Each tradition's doxography is partisan — Vidyāraṇya's culminates in Advaita as Haribhadra's in Jainism — but the doxographical impulse is cross-cultural.

## Disputes
- **Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha authorship**: Mādhava-Vidyāraṇya or his brother Sāyaṇa or a school product.
- **Founding of Vijayanagara**: how much of the "Vidyāraṇya as kingmaker" tradition is historical and how much is later Sringeri matha self-presentation is debated; the institutional fact of the Sringeri-Vijayanagara alliance is firm.
- **Identity-pair "Vidyāraṇya / Mādhava"**: some 19th–20th-c. Indologists distinguished Mādhavācārya and Vidyāraṇya as two different people; the modern consensus is that they are one figure under two names (pre-renunciation Mādhava → post-renunciation Vidyāraṇya).
