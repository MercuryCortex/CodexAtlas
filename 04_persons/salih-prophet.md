# Ṣāliḥ

Quranic prophet sent to the people of **Thamūd**, another ancient Arabian people. The defining episode of Ṣāliḥ's mission is the she-camel (*nāqa*) miracle: God provides the Thamūd with a sacred camel as a sign; when they hamstring and kill it, the divine punishment follows (Surah 7:73–79, 11:61–68, 26:141–159, 27:45–53, 54:23–31, 91:11–15).

## Quranic profile

Ṣāliḥ appears in six Quranic surahs. The Thamūd punishment narrative is one of the Quran's most frequently repeated punishment stories, rivaled only by the Pharaoh narrative. Key passages:
- **Surah 7:73–79** — Ṣāliḥ sent; the camel-sign; hamstringing; three-day warning; earthquake
- **Surah 11:61–68** — most extended; Ṣāliḥ's theological confrontation with his people
- **Surah 91:11–15** — the briefest account, focusing on the cosmic weight of the camel's killing

## Archaeological correlation

The Nabataean/pre-Islamic site of **Ḥijr** (modern Madāʾin Ṣāliḥ in Saudi Arabia, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site) is traditionally identified with the Thamūd's territory. The Thamūd are also attested in Assyrian annals (~8th c. BCE), Ptolemy's *Geography*, and pre-Islamic Arabian inscriptions, indicating a historical population that the Quran's punishment narrative transforms into a theological type.

## Arabian prophet category

Like Hūd ([[hud-prophet]]), Ṣāliḥ is a purely Quranic figure with no Hebrew Bible or New Testament parallel. The two together constitute the Quran's claim that God sent prophets to the Arabian peoples before Islam, making the Arabs (like the Israelites) recipients of a prior prophetic tradition that they failed to heed.
