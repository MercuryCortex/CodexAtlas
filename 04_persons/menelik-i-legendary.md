# Menelik I (legendary founder of the Solomonic dynasty)

## Identity
**Menelik I** is, by Ethiopian tradition codified in the *[[phase-8-008-kebra-nagast]]*, the son of King Solomon of Israel and Queen [[makeda-queen-of-sheba]] of Aksum, and the founder of the Ethiopian **Solomonic dynasty**. The narrative core: Makeda visits Solomon in Jerusalem (1 Kings 10 / 2 Chr 9 substrate); Solomon contrives that she share his bed; she returns to Aksum and gives birth to Menelik (alternatively *Bayna Lehkem* / *Ebna Hakim*, "son of the wise one"). As a young man Menelik travels to Jerusalem to meet his father; Solomon educates him and acknowledges him as firstborn; Menelik returns to Aksum, accompanied (in the *Kebra Nagast* narrative) by Azariah son of Zadok the high priest and other Israelites, who *secretly remove the Ark of the Covenant* from the First Temple and bring it to Aksum, where it has remained ever since (in Ethiopian tradition) — guarded in the Chapel of the Tablet at the Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion. Menelik becomes the first Solomonic king of Ethiopia; his descendants reign continuously to 1974.

Modern Ethiopian iconography depicts him as a young African prince in Solomonic regalia.

## Historical evidence
Menelik I is a **purely legendary** figure: no contemporary or near-contemporary attestation. The *Kebra Nagast* tradition crystallizes in surviving form in the 14th c. (1314–1322 colophon), drawing on much older Jewish-Christian-Islamic Solomon-Sheba narrative substrate; whether any historical Aksumite king bore a name retroactively transferred to "Menelik" cannot be recovered.

The historical Ethiopian monarchs named *Menelik*:
- **Menelik II** (r. 1889–1913) — the modernizing emperor who decisively defeated the Italian invasion at the Battle of Adwa (1896) and established the modern Ethiopian state. He is *historical*. (He should have his own person node — see vault TODO.)
- **Menelik I** (legendary) — the figure of this node, foundational dynastic ancestor.

## Role in the tradition
Menelik I is the *constitutive ancestor* of the Solomonic dynasty. The 1955 Ethiopian constitution under Haile Selassie made the Solomonic-Menelik descent claim formal and legal: "The Imperial Dignity shall remain perpetually attached to the line of Haile Selassie I, descendant of King Sahle Selassie, whose line descends without interruption from the dynasty of Menelik I, son of the Queen of Ethiopia, the Queen of Sheba, and King Solomon of Jerusalem" (Article 2). The dynastic claim is theologically grounded — Christ is of David's line; the Solomonic dynasty is of David's line through Solomon and Menelik; the Ethiopian emperor is thus a Davidic-messianic figure.

For Rastafarianism (see [[tradition-rastafari]]), the Menelik-Solomonic line authenticates Haile Selassie's messianic role — Menelik functions in Rastafarian theology as the *ancestor of the messianic king*.

The Ark-translation aspect of the Menelik narrative is the load-bearing claim: it *re-locates* Israelite sanctity from Jerusalem to Aksum, transferring election from Israel to Ethiopia. This is a theologically supersessionist move structurally analogous to (but distinct from) Christian-supersessionist readings of Israelite election — Christianity replaces Israel; in the Kebra Nagast logic, Ethiopia replaces Israel as the *physical* custodian of sacred kingship and the Ark.

## Disputes
- **Historicity.** Legendary; no defensible historical kernel.
- **Date of the Kebra Nagast narrative core.** The 1322 colophon dates the *redaction* of the present Ge'ez form; the underlying narrative complex (Solomon-Sheba elaborated with an Ethiopian son) circulated in Near Eastern Jewish-Christian-Islamic material long before 1322 (Targum Sheni; Quran Surah 27; Coptic and Syriac legends). Munro-Hay 2006 surveys the textual history.
- **Authenticity of the Ark of the Covenant claim.** The Ethiopian church holds the original Ark resides at Aksum. The chapel is closed to investigation. The vast majority of secular scholars treat this as theological claim, not historical fact.
