# Ramakrishna Paramahamsa

## Identity
Bengali Hindu mystic (1836–1886); priest at the Dakshineswar Kali Temple (north of Kolkata) from 1855; reportedly engaged in *sadhana* (spiritual practice) in multiple Hindu traditions (Tantric, Vaishnava, Vedantic) and in Islam (briefly) and Christianity (briefly), reporting realization in each — the experiential basis of the "all religions are paths to the same goal" position later systematized by his disciple Vivekananda. Practiced and taught a passionate devotion to the Divine Mother (Kali).

## Role in the tradition
Guru of Vivekananda; co-spiritual-source (with [[sarada-devi]]) of the lineage that produced the [[event-ramakrishna-mission-founded-1897|Ramakrishna Mission (founded 1897)]] and the broader Hindu-modernist universalist position. The *Gospel of Sri Ramakrishna* (1942) is the principal record. Also a documented bridge figure to the Brahmo Samaj — visited multiple times by [[keshub-chandra-sen]] from 1875 onward; Sen's *Paramahamser Ukti* (1878) was the first published account of Ramakrishna in English.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Proto-comparative-religion experimental praxis**: Ramakrishna's reported *sādhana* in multiple traditions (Hindu Tantric Kālī-worship, Vaishnava Krishna-bhakti, Advaitic non-dualism via the wandering monk Totāpurī, Christianity via reading the Gospels and meditating on Jesus, Islam via Sufi instruction from a Muslim teacher) is among the most fully-realized 19th-c. cases of experiential multi-tradition religious praxis. The systematized doctrinal position "all religions are paths to the same goal" that Vivekananda subsequently exported was rooted in Ramakrishna's lived experimental practice.
- **Bengali Śākta Tantric substrate beneath Hindu modernism**: the Ramakrishna Mission's public face is Vivekananda's universalist Vedanta; the internal lineage's affective-ritual core is Bengali Śākta Tantric Kālī-worship. Kripal 1995 and McDermott 2003 trace the Tantric substrate.
