# Dioscorus I of Alexandria

## Identity
Dioscorus I (~380-454) was the 25th Patriarch of Alexandria (444-451), Cyril of Alexandria's archdeacon and chosen successor, and the central deposed figure of the [[event-council-of-chalcedon-451|Council of Chalcedon (451)]]. To the Chalcedonian tradition (Roman Catholic + Eastern Orthodox) he is the heresiarch of monophysite ultra-Cyrillianism who illegitimately deposed Flavian of Constantinople at the 449 Second Council of Ephesus (the "Latrocinium" / "Robber Council" per Pope Leo I) and was justly deposed at Chalcedon. To the Oriental Orthodox tradition ([[tradition-coptic-orthodox|Coptic]], [[tradition-syriac-orthodox|Syriac]], [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo|Ethiopian]], [[tradition-armenian-apostolic|Armenian]]) he is **St. Dioscorus the Great** — the unjustly deposed defender of Cyrillian Christology against the Antiochene-leaning two-natures formula imposed by Chalcedon under imperial pressure. The two assessments split the Christian world in 451 and have never been formally reconciled, though modern Oriental Orthodox / Chalcedonian dialogue (1971 Vienna, 1989-90 Anba Bishoy + Chambésy agreed statements) has substantially narrowed the gap by acknowledging that the historical Christological dispute was largely terminological.

## Historical evidence
- **Acts of Chalcedon 451** (Price & Gaddis 2005 critical translation) — the most extensive primary source for his deposition: read, charges against him, and the procedural drama. Even hostile sources confirm his theological commitments and the substantive facts of the procedural disputes.
- **Letters preserved in Greek + Syriac + Coptic florilegia** — selections from his correspondence including the encyclical against Theodoret of Cyrrhus and his exilic letters from Gangra.
- **Severus of Antioch's writings (early 6th c.)** preserve substantial quotations and theological assessment from the Miaphysite side.
- **Zacharias of Mytilene's *Ecclesiastical History*** (~6th c.) — Miaphysite ecclesiastical history with detailed Dioscorus narrative.
- **Coptic *Synaxarium* entries** for his death-day (Tobi 7 / January 15 Gregorian) preserve hagiographic tradition.
- **Egyptian + Syriac + Ethiopian + Armenian liturgical commemorations** preserve continuous Oriental Orthodox memory.

## Works
Surviving works are mainly **letters** — to the Egyptian church, to Theodoret of Cyrrhus, to the empress Pulcheria, to bishops across the Oriental Christian world; an encyclical against Theodoret; exilic letters from Gangra (after his post-Chalcedon banishment). The Chalcedon acts preserve his speeches during 451 nearly verbatim.

## Role in the tradition
- **In Chalcedonian Christianity (Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Anglican, Protestant)**: heresiarch; the central architect of the 449 "Robber Council"; deposed at Chalcedon 451 for procedural abuses + Christological deviation (not always for heresy in the strict sense — the actual conciliar charges at Chalcedon were procedural, not Christological).
- **In Oriental Orthodox Christianity (Coptic, Syriac, Ethiopian, Armenian, Malankara)**: St. Dioscorus the Great. Co-foundational figure of Oriental Orthodoxy alongside Cyril of Alexandria and (in different ways) [[severus-of-antioch|Severus]] and [[jacob-baradaeus|Jacob Baradaeus]]. His exile and death at Gangra is read as martyric witness. Liturgically commemorated on Tobi 7 (Coptic) / Hedar 7 (Ethiopian Tewahedo).
- **In modern ecumenical dialogue**: the 1989-1990 Oriental Orthodox / Eastern Orthodox agreed-statements at Chambésy acknowledged that the Christological dispute at Chalcedon was substantially terminological (the Miaphysite *mia physis* and the Chalcedonian *en duo physesin* both rejecting actual Eutychian monophysitism), but the rehabilitation of Dioscorus as a canonical saint of the entire church remains contested.

## Disputes
- **Procedural vs. doctrinal deposition at Chalcedon**: did Chalcedon depose him for heresy or for procedural abuses (irregular re-deposition of Flavian at Ephesus II)? The acts show the formal charges were procedural; the underlying doctrinal hostility was real but not the technical basis of deposition. Frend 1972 + Price-Gaddis 2005 + Davis 2014 examine this in detail.
- **Was he a "Monophysite" (one-nature Eutychian) or a "Miaphysite" (one-incarnate-Word Cyrillian)?** Oriental Orthodox tradition + modern scholarship: clearly Miaphysite, not Eutychian. The "monophysite" label is the Chalcedonian polemical reduction. See [[miaphysite-christology]].
- **The "Robber Council" charge**: Leo I's pejorative for Ephesus II 449. Davis 2014 argues that the procedural irregularities at Ephesus II were genuine but the imperial-driven character of Chalcedon 451 was equally problematic; both councils operated under heavy imperial pressure.
