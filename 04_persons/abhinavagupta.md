# Abhinavagupta

## Identity
Abhinavagupta (~950–1016) was the supreme systematic theologian of non-dualist Tantric Shaivism (Trika) and simultaneously the foundational theorist of classical Sanskrit aesthetics (*rasa* theory). A Kashmiri Brahmin from a family of Tantric initiates; son of Narasiṃhagupta and Vimalakalā; studied with ~15 named teachers across all major Śaiva, Krama, and Trika streams; never married; lived as a Tantric polymath. The *Tantrāloka* is the monumental synthesis of non-dualist Tantric thought; the *Abhinavabhāratī* and *Locana* are foundational works in Sanskrit literary theory.

## Works
*Tantrāloka* (~37 chapters, ~5800 verses, his magnum opus); *Tantrasāra* (prose summary); *Parātrīśikā-vivaraṇa* (commentary on a Trika scripture); *Abhinavabhāratī* (commentary on Bharata's *Nāṭyaśāstra* — the principal classical treatise on dramatic and aesthetic theory); *Locana* (commentary on Ānandavardhana's *Dhvanyāloka*, foundational text of *dhvani* poetic theory); *Īśvara-Pratyabhijñā-Vimarśinī* (commentary on Utpaladeva); ~40+ works total.

## Role in the tradition
Foundational for two distinct major intellectual traditions: (1) non-dualist Tantric Shaivism (where the *Tantrāloka* remains the central systematic statement, in continuous transmission to Lakshman Joo in the 20th c.); (2) Sanskrit aesthetic theory (where the *rasa-camatkāra* / *rasa as aesthetic-relish* doctrine of the *Abhinavabhāratī* and *Locana* defines classical Sanskrit aesthetics). The structural connection between the two — that aesthetic experience is a flash of non-dual recognition — is itself a major contribution. Grand-disciple of [[utpaladeva]] (Somānanda → Utpaladeva → Lakṣmaṇagupta → Abhinavagupta). Commentator on [[bharatamuni]]'s [[phase-3-024-natyashastra|Nāṭyaśāstra]] (the *Abhinavabhāratī*) and on Vasugupta's [[phase-5-033-shiva-sutras|Śiva Sūtras]] tradition.

## Cross-tradition connections
- **Śaiva → Vajrayāna Buddhist transmission (Sanderson thesis)**: Sanderson 2009 argues that much medieval Tantric Buddhism is downstream of Śaiva Tantric originals; Abhinavagupta's circle is the institutional context.
- **Rasa-aesthetics as cross-tradition aesthetic-theological category**: Abhinavagupta's identification of aesthetic experience as structurally cognate with non-dual recognition has cross-tradition parallels with Sufi *samāʿ* (the ecstatic-vehicle reading of sacred music), with medieval Christian aesthetic theology (Pseudo-Dionysius and beauty as theophany), and with German Romantic philosophical aesthetics (Schopenhauer's will-less-knowing reading of aesthetic experience).
