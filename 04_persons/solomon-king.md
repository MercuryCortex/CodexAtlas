# Solomon

## Identity
Son of David and Bathsheba; third king of the United Monarchy (~970–931 BCE per traditional dating); biblical builder of the First Temple in Jerusalem; canonical pseudonymous author of the wisdom literature (Proverbs, Ecclesiastes / Qohelet, Song of Songs, plus the Hellenistic-Jewish *Wisdom of Solomon* and *Psalms of Solomon*). His reign is described in 1 Kings 1–11 (paralleled in 2 Chronicles 1–9). Tradition magnifies him as the paradigmatic wise king and as the canonical sponsor of Israelite wisdom literature.

## Historical evidence
The Solomonic kingdom as biblically described — international wealth, an extensive bureaucracy, monumental architecture across Hazor / Megiddo / Gezer (1 Kgs 9:15) — is heavily debated. Finkelstein's "low chronology" reassigns the gate complexes traditionally dated to Solomon down a century, leaving thin direct archaeological evidence of a 10th-c. imperial Jerusalem. Mazar and the Jerusalem maximalists defend the traditional dating. The Solomonic *figure* is more secure than the Solomonic *empire* — but both are contested.

## Works
- **Proverbs** carries his name in 1:1; the collection is a multi-author wisdom anthology with the Solomon-attribution layered atop pre-existing Egyptian (e.g., Amenemope) and Israelite wisdom material.
- **Ecclesiastes / Qohelet** opens "the son of David, king in Jerusalem" — pseudonymous Solomonic frame around a post-exilic skeptical wisdom voice.
- **Song of Songs** carries the Solomonic superscription; the collection is independent.
- **[[phase-3-012-wisdom-of-solomon]]** (1st-c. BCE/CE Alexandrian Greek) is pseudonymous in Solomon's voice.
- **Psalms of Solomon** (1st-c. BCE Pharisaic-Jewish Greek pseudepigraphon).

## Role in the tradition
Solomon's reign is the climax of the Deuteronomistic Historian's united-monarchy theology; his late-life apostasy (1 Kgs 11) is the etiology of the kingdom's division. The Solomonic temple becomes the architectural model for the Second Temple and an iconographic template for every later Christian and Jewish sacred-space tradition. In Christian tradition, Jesus's "greater than Solomon" (Mt 12:42, Lk 11:31) deploys him as a wisdom-foil. In Islamic tradition Sulayman is a prophet-king with control over the jinn (Quran 27, 34, 38). In Ethiopian tradition Solomon's union with Makeda the Queen of Sheba produces Menelik I — the foundational myth of the Solomonic dynasty preserved in the [[phase-8-008-kebra-nagast|*Kebra Nagast*]].
