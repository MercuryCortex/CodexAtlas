# Thaddaeus / Jude the Apostle

## Identity
The Synoptic apostle-lists are not consistent here: Mark and Matthew list **Thaddaeus** (or "Lebbaeus called Thaddaeus" in some manuscripts), while Luke and Acts list **Judas of James** in the same slot. Traditional Christian harmonization treats this as one person with multiple names. In the Syriac tradition his name appears as **Addai**, the apostle who founded the church at Edessa under King Abgar V (per Eusebius and the *Doctrine of Addai*).

## Historical evidence
Name-only in canonical Gospels apart from the brief Johannine question at the Last Supper. The **Abgar correspondence** (Eusebius *HE* 1.13: an exchange of letters between Jesus and King Abgar V of Edessa) is the foundation of the Syriac Addai tradition; the letters are textually preserved but historically considered apocryphal (Eusebius accepts them as authentic). Edessa was certainly Christianized early, and the Addai tradition may preserve real memory of an apostolic-era mission, but the specific Thaddaeus = Addai identification is contested.

## Works
**Epistle of Jude** (canonical, ~+65/+90). The author identifies himself as "Jude, brother of James" — most likely [[james-brother-of-jesus]] rather than James the apostle, making the author *the brother of Jesus* rather than the apostle. The Epistle of Jude is one of the earliest extant Christian uses of [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] (Jude 14–15 quotes 1 Enoch 1:9 as authoritative prophecy), a critical datum for the Enochic-Christian connection.

## Role in the tradition
**Armenian Apostolic Church**: with [[bartholomew-apostle]], founding apostle. **Syriac tradition**: founder of the Edessan church through the Abgar legend. **Catholic devotional tradition**: patron of "lost causes" — a late-medieval development.

## Disputes
- **One apostle or two?** Mark/Matthew (Thaddaeus) vs. Luke/Acts (Judas of James) — modern critical scholarship is divided.
- **Authorship of Jude.** The author identifies as "brother of James"; most critical scholarship considers him the brother of Jesus, not the apostle.
- **Addai = Thaddaeus.** Eusebian identification accepted in Syriac tradition but historically uncertain.
