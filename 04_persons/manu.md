# Manu (Vaivasvata Manu)

## Identity
**Manu** (Sanskrit *Manu*, "man" / "thinker"; from the same Indo-European root as Germanic *Mannus* and English *man*) is the Vedic-Hindu **first man** and **flood-survivor**. The earliest substantial Vedic flood-narrative attestation — **Shatapatha Brahmana 1.8.1** (~-700 BCE) — narrates:

> Manu, washing his hands in the river, catches a small fish. The fish asks Manu to protect it, predicting it will save him in return. Manu rears the fish in a pot, then a tank, then a pond, then the sea (the fish grows progressively larger). The fish then warns Manu of an impending flood, instructs him to build a great ship and to tether it to the fish's horn. The flood comes; the fish (revealed to be a manifestation of [[matsya-avatar]] / Vishnu) tows Manu's ship to a northern mountain. Manu survives, sacrifices, and from his sacrifice his daughter Iḍā emerges, and from their union humanity descends.

The Vedic narrative is **structurally cognate** with the Mesopotamian / Hebrew / Greek flood-traditions but with distinctive features:
- The savior is the **fish-avatar of God** (Matsya = Vishnu) rather than a separate deity warning a human hero.
- The post-flood repopulation is via a daughter (Iḍā) born from the *sacrifice* rather than from surviving paired humans.
- Manu is *both* the first man (anthropogonic founder) *and* the flood-survivor (post-flood ancestor) — a doubled foundational role.

## Historical evidence

- **Shatapatha Brahmana 1.8.1** (~-700 BCE) — the earliest surviving Vedic narrative.
- **Mahabharata *Vana Parva* 187** (~-300 BCE to +400 CE composition) — elaborated recension where the fish-Matsya identification is more explicit.
- **[[phase-4-065-bhagavata-purana]] 8.24** (~-9th–10th c. CE) — the most-elaborated Vaishnava-Puranic recension.
- **Manusmriti / *Laws of Manu*** (~-200 BCE to +200 CE) — the legal-codification text attributed to Manu; opens with Manu naming himself as Vaivasvata.

Manu is **legendary / mythological** — no contemporary attestation; the Vedic-Hindu flood-tradition is mythological-canonical.

## Works

- **Manusmriti / *Laws of Manu*** — the foundational Hindu legal-social-religious treatise; composed ~-200 BCE to +200 CE, traditionally attributed to Manu. Establishes the *varna-dharma* social-religious order, life-stage (*ashrama*) regulations, and broad Hindu jurisprudence. Standard English: Doniger and Smith 1991 (Penguin Classics).

## Role in the tradition

Manu is the Vedic-Hindu **flood-survivor + first-man + ancestor of humanity + legal-founder** in a single integrated mythological-canonical figure. The cosmic-time framework of the *Manvantara* (each cosmic age — *manvantara* — is governed by a Manu; the current age is the seventh, that of Vaivasvata Manu) extends the figure to a broader cosmological function.

In the Vaishnava-Hindu reception (Bhagavata Purana), Manu's rescue by [[matsya-avatar]] (Vishnu's fish-avatar) integrates the flood-narrative into the *dashavatara* (ten avatars of Vishnu) framework, with Matsya as the first avatar.

## Disputes

- **Vedic flood vs. Mesopotamian-Hebrew flood — independent or transmitted?** The principal comparative-religion question:
  - **Witzel 2012** (*Origins of the World's Mythologies*) argues for a deep "Laurasian" mythological substrate from which both the Mesopotamian and the Vedic flood-traditions descend — independent-but-cognate-from-shared-deep-stratum.
  - **Frazer 1918** (*Folklore in the Old Testament*) — comparative-religion approach treating all flood-narratives as parallel-form expressions of a universal mytheme.
  - **Smith 1990** (*Drudgery Divine*) — methodological caution against strong comparative-religion claims of transmission.
  - **Sayce 1887** (early-comparative) argued for direct Mesopotamian → Vedic transmission via Persian intermediation; mostly rejected today.
- **Date of the Shatapatha Brahmana flood-narrative.** Conventional dating ~-700 BCE; some scholars push earlier or later.
