# Ptolemy II Philadelphus

## Identity
Ptolemy II Philadelphus (c. -308 to -246; "Philadelphus" = "sibling-lover," referring to his marriage to his full sister Arsinoe II — a Pharaonic Egyptian dynastic practice formally adopted by the Ptolemies and shocking by Greek norms) was the second Ptolemaic king of Egypt. Under his reign the institutions established by his father [[ptolemy-i-soter]] reached their mature form: the **Library of Alexandria** under Demetrius of Phalerum became the largest collection of books in the ancient world; the **Pharos lighthouse** was completed (-247); and two projects of immense long-term importance for the Codex Atlas's mission were carried out under his patronage — **the Greek translation of the Hebrew Bible (the [[phase-3-006-septuagint|Septuagint]])** and **[[manetho]]'s *Aegyptiaca*** ([[phase-3-025-manetho-aegyptiaca]]). Ptolemy II is the principal patron-figure of mature Alexandrian intellectual production.

## Historical evidence
Ptolemy II is documented in extensive papyrological evidence from Egypt (the Zenon archive, Ptolemaic-administrative papyri); his coinage; multiple ancient narrative accounts (Theocritus *Idyll* 17 *Encomium of Ptolemy* is a major contemporary poetic source; Diodorus; Plutarch); and Egyptian-language stelae from his Pharaonic role. The Letter of Aristeas (pseudonymous, ~2nd c. BCE) is the principal narrative source for the LXX project, dramatized but reflecting institutional reality.

## Works
None authored. Ptolemy II is a **patron-figure** rather than an author.

## Role in the tradition
1. **Patron of the [[phase-3-006-septuagint|Septuagint]] translation.** Under Ptolemy II's reign (and traditionally at his commission), seventy or seventy-two Jewish scholars from Jerusalem translated the Torah from Hebrew to Greek for inclusion in the Library of Alexandria. The Letter of Aristeas (~2nd c. BCE) narrativizes this; the institutional reality is well-documented. **The LXX is the single most consequential biblical text of late antiquity** — read by Hellenistic Jews, used by every New Testament writer, the principal Old Testament of early Christianity, and the basis for the Latin Vetus Latina before Jerome's Hebrew-based Vulgate. **This is a MASSIVE-win cross-tradition transmission edge** — Hebrew scripture translated into Greek under Ptolemaic Greek-Egyptian institutional patronage, generating the Greek-language Bible that becomes the text of Christianity.

2. **Patron of [[manetho]]'s *Aegyptiaca*.** Manetho's history of Egypt for Greek readers, traditionally completed under Ptolemy II, is the institutional bridge between Egyptian priestly tradition and Greek-language scholarship. The work establishes the 30-dynasty framework still used by Egyptology.

3. **Library of Alexandria's mature institutional form.** Under Demetrius of Phalerum's directorship (under both Ptolemy I and Ptolemy II) the Library acquires its famous mass acquisition policy: every ship docking at Alexandria has its scrolls confiscated for copying; the Library acquires (and keeps) the official Athenian state copies of Aeschylus, Sophocles, and Euripides. The mature Library is the principal physical site of Hellenistic intellectual production.

4. **Sibling-marriage and Pharaonic legitimation.** Ptolemy II's marriage to his sister Arsinoe II in -279 — and the subsequent deification of the sibling-couple as the *Theoi Adelphoi* — was a Pharaonic-Egyptian dynastic move (Egyptian royal sibling marriage was traditional and theologically sanctioned by the Osiris-Isis sibling-marriage model). The Greek world found it shocking; Egyptian religious culture supported it. The move marks one of the clearest cases of Ptolemaic Greek-Macedonian rulers **adopting Egyptian-religious norms** rather than imposing Greek ones — important institutional evidence for the depth of the Greek-Egyptian fusion.

5. **Continuation of the Serapis cult.** The Serapis cult instituted under Ptolemy I matured under Ptolemy II with major temple construction and cult-spread; Isis-Serapis-Harpocrates as the Hellenistic-Egyptian divine family becomes a Ptolemaic state-icon.

## Disputes
- **Date of LXX translation.** Letter of Aristeas claims Ptolemy II's reign for the translation of the Torah (Pentateuch); modern scholarship accepts the Pentateuch's Greek translation in the early-to-mid 3rd c. BCE (Ptolemy II era) but treats the other books as translated piecemeal over the following centuries (some not until 1st c. BCE or even later). The Aristeas narrative is dramatized; the institutional context is historical (Honigman 2003).
- **Date of Manetho's *Aegyptiaca*.** Could be late Ptolemy I or early Ptolemy II; the institutional pattern points to Ptolemy II as the more probable patron.
- **Library size claims.** Ancient sources give the Library figures of 200,000 to 700,000 scrolls; modern estimates are uncertain but the Library was clearly the largest book collection of antiquity.
