# Mes-anepada

## Identity
Mes-anepada was the founder of the First Dynasty of Ur, conventionally dated ~-2563 to -2524 BCE in the Early Dynastic III period. He is the earliest king of Ur attested by contemporary inscriptions. A famous lapis-lazuli bead inscribed with his name was excavated at Mari (Tell Hariri) by André Parrot in 1952, attesting either a diplomatic gift or commercial reach as far up the Euphrates as Mari — a remarkable testament to the inter-regional connections of mid-third-millennium Sumer. He is also named in the Sumerian King List ([[phase-1-015-sumerian-king-list]]) as the first king of the First Dynasty of Ur (after Kish "smote with weapons" and kingship was carried to Uruk and then to Ur), with a reign of 80 years, and in the Tummal Chronicle as one of the kings who built the Tummal sanctuary at Nippur. He is the inscriptional-historical anchor for the Royal Cemetery of Ur excavations (Woolley 1922–1934), though no royal tomb has been definitively identified as his.

## Historical evidence
1. **The Mari lapis bead** — a votive lapis bead inscribed "Mes-anepada, son of Meskalamdug (or Mes-kalam-dug), king of Ur" was excavated at Mari in 1952. This single object provides contemporary primary-source evidence of his historical existence and his far reach.
2. **Royal inscriptions** from Ur itself (Frayne RIME 1) — bricks and votive objects.
3. **The Sumerian King List** — entry as first king of First Dynasty of Ur, 80-year reign.
4. **The Tummal Chronicle** — names him as builder.

These together make him **documented** rather than merely likely-historical.

## Works
None — beyond votive inscriptions.

## Role in the tradition
Mes-anepada anchors the founding of the First Dynasty of Ur, the dynasty whose royal tombs Leonard Woolley would excavate ~4500 years later — yielding the spectacular grave goods (the Standard of Ur, the gold helmet of Meskalamdug, the lyres, the so-called "death pits") that revolutionized 20th-century understanding of Sumerian material culture. The relationship between Mes-anepada and Meskalamdug (whose grave-goods are inscribed with his name) is unresolved: Meskalamdug may be his father (as the Mari bead's patronymic suggests) or a separate ruler. The First Dynasty of Ur is, on the archaeological evidence, one of the great wealth-and-craftsmanship centers of the mid-third millennium.

## Disputes
- **Relationship to Meskalamdug**. The Mari bead reads "Mes-anepada, son of Meskalamdug, king of Kish." Whether Meskalamdug was an earlier king of Ur or held the Kish title in some honorific sense is debated.
- **Date**. Conventional ~-2563 to -2524 BCE; the Middle Chronology gives this range, lower chronologies push later.
- **Tomb identification**. Whether any of Woolley's Royal Cemetery tombs are Mes-anepada's is unresolved.
