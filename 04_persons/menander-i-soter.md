# Menander I Sōter

## Identity
Menander I Sōter ("Menander the Saviour"; Pali *Milinda*; Kharoṣṭhī *Menadra*) was the most successful Indo-Greek king, reigning ~-165 to -130 over Bactria, Arachosia, Gandhāra, and Punjab from his capital at Sākala (Sialkot). Greek by descent, Bactrian by upbringing, ruling a polyglot population of Greeks, Iranians, and Indo-Aryans, he is uniquely the only Hellenistic king whose conversion to a non-Greek religion (Buddhism) is independently attested in both Greek and Pali sources. The Pali *Milindapañha* (~-100 to +100 in core, redacted later) preserves him as the philosophical interlocutor of the Buddhist monk [[nagasena]] in the highest-fidelity Greek-Buddhist contact document in existence; Plutarch records a posthumous Greek tradition of his subjects fighting over his ashes and erecting *stūpa*-style monuments to him in their cities — structurally parallel to the parinirvāṇa-relics distribution of the Buddha himself.

## Historical evidence
- **Coinage**: >200 distinct coin types in his name; bilingual Greek-Kharoṣṭhī legends ("BASILEŌS SŌTĒROS MENANDROU" on the obverse / "MAHARAJASA TRATARASA MENADRASA" — "of the Great King the Saviour Menander" — on the reverse, on a single coin). His coinage circulated across the largest geographical range of any Indo-Greek king, an indirect index of his political reach. The "wheel-symbol" reverse (the *cakra* / dharma-wheel) on some of his coin types is sometimes read as Buddhist iconography but is also a Greek wheel-of-Tyche and the reading is contested.
- **Greek literary**: Strabo 11.11.1 ("Menander, who extended his power across the Hypanis"); Plutarch *Moralia* 821D-E describes his posthumous death-cult ("When Menander, who had reigned in Bactria with mildness, died in camp...cities erected monuments to him in all their cities"); Pompeius Trogus / Justin *Epitome* 41.6 records his rule.
- **Pali/Buddhist**: [[phase-3-029-milindapanha]] — the dialogue with Nāgasena. The Pali version is in the Theravāda Khuddaka Nikāya (Burmese and Thai recensions). A Chinese parallel survives in two translations (*Nāgasena-bhikṣu-sūtra*, T 1670 A and B). The earliest stratum is generally dated to ~-100 to +100, post-Menander.
- **Inscriptional**: a Bajaur reliquary inscription (Karoshthi, ~2nd c. BCE) mentions a "King Menander" — variously identified with Menander I or a later Menander II.

## Works
None directly. The Milindapañha attributes philosophical questions to him; the Plutarch death-tradition implies he was remembered as a just ruler. He is a frame-figure of a major Buddhist text, not its author.

## Role in the tradition
- **In Pali Buddhism**: Milinda is the paradigm of a Greek-philosophical interlocutor brought to Buddhist conviction by Nāgasena's reasoning. The genre of the Milindapañha — Socratic-style dialectic, with philosophical question-and-answer, similes, and refutations — is itself a Hellenistic-Buddhist hybrid genre. The Pali tradition records that Menander eventually renounced his throne to become a Buddhist monk under Nāgasena, though this monastic-conversion ending is not in all recensions and is generally regarded as later embellishment.
- **In Greco-Buddhist art history**: Menander's coinage represents the most extensive surviving visual record of Indo-Greek kingship; his "wheel" reverse types are foundational for the iconographic question of how the *cakra*/Tyche-wheel transitioned into the Buddhist *dharma-cakra*.
- **In Indian religious memory**: Menander is the single named Greek king in extant Pali tradition. His existence is foundational evidence that Greco-Buddhism was not a scholarly construct but an attested social reality.

## Disputes
- **Was he actually a Buddhist?** The Milindapañha says yes (Pali); Plutarch's account is religiously neutral ("with mildness"); his coinage uses Greek-pantheon types alongside the wheel-reverse. The conservative reading (Tarn 1938): Menander was a Greek polytheist who showed favor to Buddhists; the Pali tradition partly reads back. The maximalist reading (Pali tradition; Halkias 2014): Menander was a Greek-philosophical convert whose conversion the Pali tradition preserves with substantial fidelity. **The Plutarch death-cult description is the strongest single piece of evidence for his Buddhist alignment**: the distribution of ashes to multiple cities and erection of monuments is the *stūpa*-cult pattern of post-parinirvāṇa Buddha-veneration, transposed onto a king.
- **Identity of "Menander I" vs. other Menanders**: numismatists distinguish Menander I (Sōter, c.-165 to -130) from Menander II (Dikaios, c.-90), and there is also a "Menander Kallinikos" attested only on coins. The Milindapañha-figure is generally Menander I.
- **Date of his reign**: -165 to -130 is Bopearachchi's chronology (1991, *Monnaies gréco-bactriennes et indo-grecques*); older datings ranged -155 to -130 (Tarn 1938) and other variants.
- **Capital location**: Sākala = modern Sialkot, Pakistan, is the standard identification, supported by Ptolemy *Geography* 7.1.46.
