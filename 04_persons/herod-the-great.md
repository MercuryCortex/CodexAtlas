# Herod the Great

## Identity
Herod I, called "the Great," ruled Roman Judea as client-king from -37 to -4 BCE. Of Idumean (Edomite) origin on his father's side (his father Antipater had been a key political operator for the late Hasmoneans under John Hyrcanus II), Herod combined Idumean ancestry with Nabataean ancestry on his mother's side, a Hasmonean wife (Mariamne — whom he later executed), Roman military and political training, and a ruthless political instinct that allowed him to navigate four successive Roman patrons (Antony, Octavian/Augustus, Agrippa, etc.) over nearly four decades.

## Historical evidence
**Among the best-documented Jewish rulers of antiquity**, primarily through Josephus's detailed biographical treatment in *Antiquities* 14–17 and *War* 1. The biographical narrative goes back to Nicolaus of Damascus, Herod's Greek court historian, whose lost universal history is Josephus's main source for the Herodian period — meaning Josephus is presenting an *originally pro-Herodian* tradition with his own editorial coloration. Archaeological evidence is extensive: Herod's building program is among the most spectacular of the ancient world — Caesarea Maritima's harbor, Masada's two palaces, the Herodium fortress-tomb, Sebaste (Samaria's rebuilding), the renovation and massive expansion of the Jerusalem Temple (begun -20, completed under his successors).

For this vault, Herod matters as: (a) the figure who built the **Second Temple in its final and most spectacular form** — the Temple Jesus and the disciples knew, the Temple destroyed in +70; (b) **founder of the Herodian dynasty** that ruled Roman Judea (variously) for over a century; (c) the **Matthean infancy-narrative villain** (the Slaughter of the Innocents in Mt 2 — not attested in Josephus or other extra-biblical sources, and probably literarily modeled on Pharaoh's slaughter of Hebrew infants in Exodus); (d) the figure whose troubled succession created the political conditions under which the major Jewish sects (Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, eventually the Zealots and the Jesus-movement) operated.

Herod's relationship with **the Temple** was ambivalent: he funded and built it grandiosely, but he also installed a golden eagle on its gate (a Roman-loyalty symbol that pious Jews violently tore down), placed his own appointees as high priests (breaking with hereditary succession), and was widely regarded as not properly Jewish (his Idumean ancestry being a stigma). His final years featured paranoid executions of family members — Mariamne, sons Alexander and Aristobulus, son Antipater — that Augustus famously remarked upon: "I would rather be Herod's pig than his son" (a pun in Greek on *hys* / *huios* — pig / son — and a reference to Herod's nominal Jewish observance preventing pig-slaughter).
