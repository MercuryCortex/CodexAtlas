# Muhammad Iqbal

**Muhammad Iqbal** (1877–1938) — philosopher, poet, and jurist — is the intellectual architect of the Pakistan idea and the most important Islamic modernist thinker of the 20th century in South Asia. Born in Sialkot (Punjab), he studied in Lahore, Cambridge, and Munich (where he received his doctorate on Persian metaphysics). He wrote poetry in both Urdu and Persian and philosophical prose in English; his philosophical output and poetic output are a unified project on the nature of selfhood, Islam, and history.

His central concept — **khudi** (خودی, selfhood, ego) — holds that the supreme value of Islamic civilization is the dynamic, creative, self-realizing individual personality, in direct contrast to the Vedantic dissolution of self into universal ground (*ātman=Brahman*) and against the Sufi tradition of *fanāʾ* (self-annihilation in God). His [[phase-7-046-iqbal-reconstruction-religious-thought|*Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam*]] (1930) synthesizes Bergson's *élan vital*, Nietzsche's will to power, and Hegelian dialectics with Quranic theology — the most systematic attempt to make Islam philosophically "modern" without secularizing it.

His 1930 Allahabad Address proposed a Muslim-majority state in northwestern India as a "consolidated Muslim community in the north-west of India" — the first formal articulation of what became Pakistan. He died nine years before Partition and never saw the state his philosophy helped create.
