# Ptolemy (Valentinian)

## Identity
Ptolemy (Greek *Ptolemaios*; sometimes Latinized *Ptolemaeus*) was the principal disciple of [[valentinus]] in the Italian (Western) branch of the Valentinian school in mid-2nd-century Rome (~140–170 CE), and one of the most important Gnostic theologians whose work survives in any substantial form. His extant writings — most importantly the *Letter to Flora*, preserved in Epiphanius's anti-heretical work, and fragments of a commentary on the Gospel of John, preserved in Irenaeus's *Against Heresies* I.8.5 — are among the most theologically sophisticated witnesses to the Valentinian system, and the *Letter to Flora* is one of the very few surviving Christian texts from the 2nd century addressed to a woman in her own name.

The *Letter to Flora* is a remarkable text in its own right: it instructs the woman Flora on the proper way to understand the Mosaic Torah, distinguishing three layers (commandments from God himself, supplements from Moses, and additions from "the elders" of the Jewish people), and within the divinely-originating layer further distinguishing the *pure law*, the *law mixed with injustice* (which the Saviour cancelled), and the *symbolic law* (which the Saviour transposed from sensible to spiritual). The framework is a careful theological reading of the Old Testament from a Christian Gnostic standpoint, neither rejecting it (Marcion) nor accepting it whole (catholic Christianity). Thomassen 2006 reads it as theologically central to understanding the Valentinian engagement with Jewish scripture.

The Johannine-commentary fragment (in [[phase-4-023-irenaeus-against-heresies]]) is the earliest extant Christian commentary on the Gospel of John (predating Heracleon's full commentary, which is also Valentinian, slightly later). It reads the Johannine prologue (Jn 1:1–18) systematically into the Valentinian *Pleroma* of aeons — *Logos*, *Zoe*, *Anthrōpos*, *Ekklēsia* etc. — demonstrating the Valentinian exegetical technique of reading canonical scripture through the Valentinian theological grid.

## Historical evidence
Ptolemy is documented primarily through hostile witnesses (Irenaeus c. 180; Tertullian c. 200; Epiphanius c. 375), but Irenaeus is exceptionally accurate as a heresiologist — modern Valentinian scholarship has confirmed his reports against Nag Hammadi Valentinian materials. The dating of Ptolemy's career to the middle-to-late 2nd century in Rome is secure. A separate Ptolemy mentioned by Justin Martyr (*2 Apology* 2) as martyred in Rome under Antoninus Pius is sometimes identified with the Valentinian Ptolemy by some scholars (Lampe), though the identification is contested.

## Role in the tradition
Ptolemy systematized Valentinus's teaching into the canonical Italian-Valentinian doctrinal scheme — the thirty aeons of the *Pleroma*, the fall of Sophia and her *Achamoth*-emanation, the demiurge as psychic-not-evil agent, the three-class division of humans (pneumatic / psychic / hylic), the salvation history culminating in restoration of the spirituals to the *Pleroma*. This is the version Irenaeus engages in *Adv. Haer.* I.1–8 and to which most catholic anti-Gnostic polemic responds. Heracleon and other named Italian Valentinians work in dialogue with Ptolemy's systematization.
