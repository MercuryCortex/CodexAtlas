# Rabbi Ishmael ben Elisha

## Identity
Rabbi Ishmael ben Elisha was a leading **Tanna** (rabbinic sage of the Mishnaic period, early 2nd century CE) and, in the later mystical literature, one of the principal pseudepigraphic protagonists of [[tradition-merkavah-mysticism|Merkavah mysticism]]. As a halakhist he is famous for the **Thirteen Hermeneutical Rules** (*middot*) for interpreting Torah and for the exegetical school ("the school of R. Ishmael") that produced the *Mekhilta*. In the [[phase-5-013-hekhalot-literature|Hekhalot literature]] he appears as a visionary who ascends through the heavenly palaces (*hekhalot*) to behold the divine Chariot-Throne (*merkavah*).

## Historical evidence
The historical Ishmael is attested across the Mishnah, Tosefta, and Tannaitic midrash as a contemporary and sometime disputant of [[rabbi-akiva]]. The mystical Ishmael of the Hekhalot texts is a later literary construction (the texts themselves are composite, redacted in the talmudic and post-talmudic period) that attaches visionary ascent traditions to his prestigious name.

## Context
The Merkavah/Hekhalot corpus describes the disciplined ascent of the *yored merkavah* ("descender to the Chariot") through seven heavenly palaces guarded by angelic gatekeepers, culminating in a vision of God enthroned — an esoteric elaboration of Ezekiel 1 and Isaiah 6. R. Ishmael and [[rabbi-akiva]] are its chief heroes; the related Sar-Torah ("Prince of Torah") material promises miraculous mastery of Torah through theurgic practice.

## Cross-tradition findings
- **Heavenly ascent and the throne-vision.** The Hekhalot ascent through guarded celestial gates to a throne-vision is structurally akin to ascent motifs across late-antique religious literature — Gnostic ascents through the archontic spheres, apocalyptic heavenly tours (1 Enoch, the Apocalypse of Abraham), and Hermetic ascent of the soul. Scholarship treats these as a shared late-antique repertoire (Halperin 1988; Scholem 1941), and the Atlas wires them as parallels rather than dependencies.
