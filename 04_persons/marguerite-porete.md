# Marguerite Porete

## Identity
A Beguine mystic from Hainaut (Low Countries / Northern France), born ~1250, **burned at the stake at the Place de Grève, Paris, on 1 June 1310** for refusing to recant or remove from circulation her vernacular Old French mystical treatise [[phase-5-040-mirror-of-simple-souls|*Le Mirouer des simples ames*]] (*The Mirror of Simple Souls*). **The first person executed by the medieval Inquisition for the contents of a written book in a vernacular European language.** Her doctrinal positions — particularly her language of the soul's *annihilation* and *becoming God* in apophatic-mystical union — were generalized as part of the bull ***Ad nostrum*** at the [[event-council-of-vienne-1311-1312|Council of Vienne]] (1311–1312), condemning eight propositions of the "Heresy of the Free Spirit" — **at the same Council that suppressed the [[tradition-knights-templar|Templars]]**.

The synchronicity is structurally important: Pope [[pope-clement-v|Clement V]], King [[philip-iv-of-france|Philip IV]], and the Dominican Inquisitor of France Guillaume de Paris were simultaneously prosecuting Marguerite (1306–1310) and the [[event-trial-of-templars-1307-1314|Templars]] (1307–1314), and the **Vienne Council generalized the heresy categories of both prosecutions**. The medieval institutional apparatus that ended the Templars also ended Marguerite Porete and the Beguine mystical tradition's institutional space.

## Historical evidence
Strong documentation:
- Marguerite's own book in multiple medieval manuscript witnesses (Old French Chantilly ms.; Middle English Methley translation Cambridge Pembroke ms.; Latin translation; Italian translation). The Chantilly manuscript is the principal critical witness, edited by Guarnieri 1986.
- **Inquisitorial dossier of William of Paris** — survives in part in the Continuator of Guillaume de Nangis. Documents the trial process 1308–1310.
- **Chronicle attestation** of the public burning, 1 June 1310 (Continuator of Guillaume de Nangis; *Grandes Chroniques de France*).
- **Indirect attestation** through *Ad nostrum* at Vienne (1311–1312) — the eight Free Spirit propositions are substantially Marguerite-derived in formulation.

## Works
- ***Le Mirouer des simples ames*** ([[phase-5-040-mirror-of-simple-souls]]) — vernacular Old French mystical-allegorical dialogue, ~1290–1300. **The work she refused to retract or remove from circulation, and for which she was burned.**

## Role in the tradition

- **Vernacular mystical author of the highest theological caliber.** Marguerite writes Old French (Picard dialect) at a moment when *Latin* was the language of theological authority — like contemporary Beguines and Beguards, she insists that high mystical theology can be done in the vernacular. Her *Mirror* is structurally a chivalric-courtly allegory (the Soul, Love, Reason as personified speakers) in which the Soul progresses through seven stages culminating in *annihilation* and *becoming-God-through-love*.
- **Radical apophatic theology.** The Soul's final stage is *un-being* — *néant* — in which all created intermediation (including the institutional Church) is transcended. This formulation, taken doctrinally, is condemnable as *quietism* or *antinomianism* in 14th-c. categories. Marguerite herself would have understood it as the radical-mystical fulfillment of Bernardine bridal-mysticism; her judges read it as institutional-disregarding heresy.
- **Anticipator of Eckhart and the Rhineland mystics.** Marguerite's *annihilation* language and her language of the "soul which is God by virtue of love" (*l'ame qui est Dieu par condition d'amour*) anticipates [[meister-eckhart]]'s **Godhead-language** by a generation. Eckhart was in Paris (likely 1311 / 1312, *concurrently with Marguerite's posthumous condemnation*) and almost certainly knew her work. McGinn 1998 ch. 9 is the load-bearing scholarly treatment.
- **Foundational for Beguine mysticism.** With [[mechthild-of-magdeburg|Mechthild of Magdeburg]] and [[hadewijch-of-antwerp|Hadewijch]], Marguerite is part of the Beguine vernacular mystical tradition of the 13th-14th c. Low Countries / Rhineland.

## Connections — Marguerite at the Vienne nexus

- **→ [[phase-5-040-mirror-of-simple-souls]]** *direct-author*.
- **→ [[event-council-of-vienne-1311-1312]]** *condemnation-event*: *Ad nostrum* generalizes her positions as Free-Spirit heresy. **This is the same council that suppressed the [[tradition-knights-templar|Templars]] via *Vox in excelso*.**
- **→ [[event-trial-of-templars-1307-1314]]** *synchronous-prosecution*: the Dominican Inquisitor of France Guillaume de Paris prosecuted both Marguerite (1308–1310) and the Templars (1307–1314).
- **→ [[meister-eckhart]]** *direct-influence*: Eckhart's "Godhead" / *Gottheit* language and his vernacular mystical sermons descend from the Beguine vernacular mystical tradition Marguerite was central to. McGinn 1998 ch. 9.
- **→ [[mechthild-of-magdeburg]]** *parallel-tradition*: contemporary Beguine vernacular mystic.
- **→ [[bernard-of-clairvaux]]** *predecessor*: Bernardine bridal-mysticism is the broader theological framework Marguerite radicalizes.
- **→ [[apophatic-mysticism]]** *paradigmatic-instance*.
- **→ [[bridal-mysticism]]** *radical-extension*.

## Disputes
- **Was Marguerite's *Mirror* actually heretical?** Modern Catholic scholarship (especially after the 2001 vatican-archival rediscovery of her manuscripts and the broader 20th-c. recovery of vernacular medieval mysticism) is broadly more sympathetic. Hollywood 1995, Babinsky 1993, McGinn 1998 read her as within the bounds of late-medieval apophatic mysticism, not as a doctrinal Free-Spirit antinomian. Lerner 1972 had already argued that the "Free Spirit" as a *movement* was substantially inquisitorial construct.
- **Did Eckhart know Marguerite's work?** Substantial circumstantial evidence (Paris c. 1311; Dominican network; conceptual continuity); not yet a smoking-gun citation but strongly likely. McGinn 1998.
- **Why was Marguerite the *first* vernacular-book burning?** The combination of (a) her refusal to retract, (b) her insistence on re-circulating her book after the first condemnation, and (c) the political-inquisitorial alignment of 1308-1310 (the same Inquisition was preparing the Templar case). Lerner 1972, Hollywood 1995.
