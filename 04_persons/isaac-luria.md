# Isaac Luria (ha-Ari)

## Identity
Sephardic Kabbalist and the most influential single figure in post-classical Jewish mysticism (1534–1572). Born in Jerusalem to an Ashkenazi father; raised in Cairo by his maternal uncle after his father's death; lived ~15 years in Egypt immersed in Kabbalistic study and Zoharic exegesis (~1555–1570). Arrived in Safed 1570, briefly studied with Moses Cordovero, and within months had emerged as the new theological center of the Safed circle. Taught orally to a select group of disciples (the *gurei ha-Ari*, "cubs of the Lion," including Chaim Vital, Israel Sarug, and Joseph ibn Tabul) for less than two years before dying in the 1572 epidemic. Luria himself wrote almost nothing; the Lurianic system survives entirely through his disciples' written records, most authoritatively Chaim Vital's *Etz Chayyim* and *Shemonah She'arim*. The Lurianic doctrines of *tzimtzum* (divine contraction), *shevirat ha-kelim* (breaking of the vessels), and *tikkun* (cosmic restoration) became the dominant Kabbalistic theology for the next four centuries and remain so today.

## Historical evidence
Vital's diaries and writings; Safed community records; the testimonies of multiple disciples; later hagiographical literature (the *Shivchei ha-Ari*, 17th-18th c.). Documentation of Luria's life is dense compared with most pre-modern mystics but less so than for his student Vital.

## Works
None directly authored beyond a few short poems and Sabbath hymns (including *Azamer be-shevahin*) and a brief commentary on Sifra di-Tzeniuta in the Zohar. The corpus is fundamentally his students' transmission.

## Role in the tradition
The most influential single figure in post-Zoharic Kabbalah. Sabbatean theology (Nathan of Gaza), Hasidic theology (Baal Shem Tov's circle), Christian-Kabbalist reception (Knorr von Rosenroth), and modern Jewish theological appropriations of *tikkun olam* all descend from his teaching.

## Disputes
- **Manuscript transmission.** Three principal Lurianic redactions diverge (Vital, Sarug, ibn Tabul); reconstructing the historical Luria is genuinely difficult. Yosef Avivi's three-volume Hebrew study is the modern critical effort.
- **Was he literate in writing?** Vital reports Luria's own statement that he could not write because the source was too abundant for the pen; modern scholarship takes this seriously.
