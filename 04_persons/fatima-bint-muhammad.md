# Fāṭima bint Muḥammad

**Fāṭima al-Zahrāʾ** (c. 605–632 CE) — daughter of the Prophet Muhammad and his wife Khadija, wife of [[ali-ibn-abi-talib]], mother of Hasan and [[husayn-ibn-ali]] — is the central female figure of Islamic devotion and the linchpin of Shia sacred genealogy. All descendants of the Prophet (*sayyids*) trace their lineage through Fatima, making her the biological bottleneck through which the entire Prophetic lineage passes.

In Shia theology, Fatima is one of the **Five People of the Mantle** (*Ahl al-Kisa*: Muhammad, Ali, Fatima, Hasan, Husayn) — the five figures sheltered under the Prophet's cloak in a foundational Shia hadith — and one of the **Fourteen Infallibles** (*Maʿṣūmūn*). She is venerated as *al-Zahrāʾ* ("the Radiant One") and *al-Batūl* ("the Pure One"). Her death, 75–95 days after Muhammad (632 CE), is mourned by Shia Muslims as among the first injustices done to the Prophet's family. **Cross-tradition parallel**: Fatima's status in Shia Islam — grief-stricken intercessor, perfect woman, theological fulcrum of a tradition — is structurally parallel to Mary's role in Catholic and Orthodox Christianity, independently arrived at within two generations of Muhammad's death.
