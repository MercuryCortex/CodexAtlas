# Sarah

## Identity
Wife and half-sister of Abraham; mother of Isaac in old age; first of the three biblical matriarchs (Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel-and-Leah). Her narrative is interleaved with Abraham's throughout Gen 11-23. Renamed from Sarai to Sarah at Gen 17:15 in the same theophany in which Abram is renamed Abraham. Her gift of Hagar as a surrogate to Abraham (Gen 16), the birth of Isaac in old age (Gen 21), and her death at Hebron (Gen 23) are the load-bearing episodes.

## Historical evidence
None extrabiblical. The Sarah-figure is internal to the patriarchal narrative complex.

## Role in the tradition
- **Hebrew Bible.** Sarah is the first matriarch and the source of the "free-woman" lineage that produces Isaac and so Jacob/Israel and the twelve tribes — over against the Hagar-Ishmael lineage.
- **New Testament.** Romans 4:19 and 9:9 invoke Sarah as the paradigm of barren-then-fruitful divine promise. Galatians 4:21-31 allegorizes Sarah and Hagar as two covenants — Sinai-bondage vs. Jerusalem-above-free. Hebrews 11:11 names her in the faith catalogue. 1 Peter 3:6 cites her obedience-to-Abraham as a model.
- **Islamic tradition.** Sara is the wife of Ibrahim and the mother of Ishaq, though she is not named in the Quran itself.
