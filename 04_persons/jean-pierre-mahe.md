# Jean-Pierre Mahé

## Identity
French Coptologist and Armenologist; Director of Studies at the École pratique des hautes études (5e Section: Religious Sciences); member of the Académie des inscriptions et belles-lettres. Career-defining work: the editio princeps of the **Armenian Definitions of Hermes Trismegistus to Asclepius** (1982) and the critical edition of the **Coptic Hermetica from Nag Hammadi VI** (1978). Without Mahé, two of the four extant Hermetic textual transmissions (Coptic and Armenian, alongside Greek and Latin) would lack a critical edition.

## Historical evidence
Documented academic career; EPHE faculty page; Académie des inscriptions et belles-lettres membership; voluminous publications in Coptic and Armenian studies. Cited 5+ times across the Codex Atlas Hermetic node-cluster.

## Works
- *Hermès en Haute-Égypte* (Presses de l'Université Laval, vol. 1: 1978; vol. 2: 1982). The two-volume foundational study.
  - Vol. 1: *Les textes hermétiques de Nag Hammadi et leurs parallèles grecs et latins* (NHC VI Hermetica + reconstruction of the Greek Vorlagen).
  - Vol. 2: *Le fragment du Discours parfait et les Définitions hermétiques arméniennes* (Armenian Definitions editio princeps).
- (with C. Salaman et al.) *The Way of Hermes* (Duckworth 2000). English translation of the Definitions.
- (with Annie Mahé) *Histoire des chrétiens d'Arménie* (Fayard 1990).
- Numerous articles on Coptic Christian and Hermetic literature, Armenian patristics, and Caucasian Christianity.

## Role in the tradition
Mahé extended the Hermetic corpus by two entire transmission channels. Before Mahé, the working scholarly text was the Greek Corpus + the Latin Asclepius (Nock-Festugière 1945–54, then Copenhaver 1992 in English). After Mahé, the working corpus also includes the Coptic Hermetica from Nag Hammadi (NHC VI,6 + VI,7 + VI,8) and the Armenian Definitions. He also contributed the methodologically-decisive **triangulation method** for reconstructing lost Greek Hermetic Vorlagen from multilingual daughter-witnesses (most paradigmatically applied to the Hermetic Prayer of Thanksgiving, attested simultaneously in Coptic NHC VI,7, Latin Asclepius §41, and Greek Papyrus Mimaut PGM III.591–611).
