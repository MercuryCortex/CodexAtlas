# Aaron

## Identity
Elder brother of Moses; founding figure of the Israelite priesthood; in the Priestly (P) source the architect of the Tabernacle cult and the first high priest. The figure is centrally important in P (Leviticus, parts of Exodus and Numbers) and absent or marginal in the older J / E strata, indicating that the developed Aaronide priesthood is a late-monarchic / exilic theological construct projected back into the Mosaic period.

## Historical evidence
None extrabiblical. Aaron's name is plausibly Egyptian in form (parallel to Moses, Phinehas, Hophni — all Egyptian-derivable names retained within Levite priestly lines), which a number of scholars (Albright, Cross) take as a faint historical-memory anchor for a Levite-Egyptian connection. The *figure* as the biblical text presents him, however, is a P-redactional construction.

## Role in the tradition
- **Cultic founder.** The Aaronide priesthood (Lev 8-9) is the legitimating institution of the Second Temple priestly establishment.
- **In the Hebrew Bible.** Aaron's failures (the Golden Calf, Ex 32; the rebellion at Meribah, Num 20:1-13) are dampened in the priestly tradition and amplified in the Deuteronomistic / prophetic critique.
- **In the New Testament.** Hebrews 5-7, 9 deploys Aaron as the typological foil to the Melchizedek-Christ priesthood: the Aaronide priesthood is provisional, fleshly, and superseded by Christ's eternal Melchizedekian priesthood.
- **In Islamic tradition.** Harun is a prophet (Quran 7:142, 19:53, 20:30-32), Moses's brother and supporter.
