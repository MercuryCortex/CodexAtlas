# Miriam

## Identity
Sister of Moses and Aaron; the third member of the Mosaic-Aaronide triad in the Priestly tradition. Named as a prophet (*nəbîʾâ*, Ex 15:20). The "Song of Miriam" (Ex 15:21 — "Sing to YHWH, for he has triumphed gloriously / horse and rider he has thrown into the sea") is widely treated as one of the oldest poetic strata of the Hebrew Bible (Cross & Freedman 1955; Albright); some scholars argue that the long Song of the Sea (Ex 15:1-18) is in fact a later expansion of the original Miriamic couplet.

## Historical evidence
None extrabiblical; the figure is internal to the Mosaic-narrative complex. The persistence of female prophetic memory in the canonical text — even as later redaction sidelines or punishes her (Num 12 — Miriam alone is punished for the joint Miriam-and-Aaron challenge to Moses) — is treated by feminist biblical scholarship (Trible, Frymer-Kensky) as a marker of an earlier tradition partially suppressed.

## Role in the tradition
- **Hebrew Bible.** Miriam appears at the Red-Sea song (Ex 15), the wilderness leprosy episode (Num 12 — punished with *tzaraat* for challenging Moses), and the brief notice of her death at Kadesh (Num 20:1). Micah 6:4 names her with Moses and Aaron as a trio of YHWH's leaders out of Egypt.
- **Feminist reception.** Miriam has become a load-bearing figure in modern Jewish and Christian feminist liturgical and theological reflection — Miriam's cup at Passover (parallel to Elijah's), feminist haggadot, etc.
