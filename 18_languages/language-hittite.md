# Hittite (Neši-li — the oldest attested Indo-European language)

## What it is
Hittite is the language of the Bronze Age empire centered on Hattusa in central Anatolia (c. 1650–1180 BCE), recovered from tens of thousands of cuneiform tablets. It is the **oldest attested Indo-European language** and the chief witness to the most archaic, first-splitting branch of the family — the Anatolian. Its 1915 decipherment by Bedřich Hrozný (who recognized that *watar* meant "water" and *ezzatteni* "you eat") was a landmark of historical linguistics.

## Context
- **Where and when.** The Hittite royal archives at Hattusa, written in borrowed Mesopotamian cuneiform; the empire fell in the Late Bronze Age collapse.
- **The pantheon.** "The thousand gods of Hatti," blending native Anatolian deities with the imported Hurrian gods (Teshub, Kumarbi).
- **Decipherment.** Hrozný's 1915 demonstration that Hittite was Indo-European reshaped the family tree and confirmed Saussure's predicted "laryngeals."

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **Anatolia as the bridge from Near Eastern myth to Hesiod — and the key to Indo-European itself.** Two things make Hittite pivotal. First, the Hurro-Hittite **Kumarbi Cycle** — in which the storm-god Teshub deposes and castrates his divine predecessor — is the closest and best-documented parallel to **Hesiod's succession myth** (Ouranos castrated by Kronos, Kronos overthrown by Zeus), giving a concrete channel by which Near Eastern theogony reached Greece through Anatolia (West, *The East Face of Helicon*). Second, as the oldest and most archaic Indo-European language, Hittite confirmed the **laryngeal theory** and forced the reconstruction of Proto-Indo-European — the hypothetical ancestor of languages from Sanskrit to English — to be rebuilt around it. A dead Bronze Age tongue thus illuminates both the roots of Greek religion and the deep history of half the world's languages (Hoffner & Melchert 2008; West 1997; Hoffner 1998).
