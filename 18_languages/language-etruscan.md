# Etruscan

The non-Indo-European language of pre-Roman central Italy — the language of the civilization that taught Rome divination, gave Latin its alphabet, and whose longest surviving text reached us by being torn into strips and wrapped around an Egyptian mummy. Etruscan can be *read aloud* with confidence (the script is a transparent Greek adaptation) but only partially *understood*: roughly 250 words are securely known, and the language has no demonstrated relatives beyond the tiny Tyrsenian group (Raetic, Lemnian).

## What survives

The corpus is ~9,000-13,000 inscriptions — overwhelmingly funerary formulas — plus a handful of longer ritual texts:

- **The Liber Linteus Zagrabiensis** ("Zagreb linen book") — the only surviving linen book of antiquity and the longest Etruscan text: a ritual calendar prescribing dated offerings to named gods. It survived because the linen was cut into strips in Ptolemaic Egypt to wrap a mummy, where the dry climate preserved what Italy lost. A ritual book of one dead religion preserved inside the funerary practice of another.
- **The Piacenza Liver** — a bronze model of a sheep's liver, its surface divided into the regions of the sky with each region's deity inscribed ([[tinia|Tinia]] among them). It is the teaching instrument of **haruspicy** ([[haruspicy-etruscan-roman]]), the liver-divination Rome institutionalized.
- **The Pyrgi gold tablets** — the Etruscan-Phoenician bilingual treated under Cross-tradition findings below.

The religious literature Rome actually used — the *libri haruspicini*, *fulgurales*, *rituales* of the revealed Tagetic tradition — survives only in Latin citations. Rome kept the Etruscan ritual science and let the Etruscan language die: by the 1st c. CE the emperor Claudius, who compiled a 20-book Etruscan history (lost), was studying a closed corpus.

## Cross-tradition findings

- **The Pyrgi bilingual (c. -500).** Thefarie Velianas, ruler of Caere, dedicates a shrine at Pyrgi to Etruscan [[uni-etruscan|Uni]] explicitly identified with Phoenician [[astarte-canaanite|Astarte]] — in parallel Etruscan and Punic texts on gold sheets. It is the cleanest dated artifact of west-Mediterranean religious fusion, and a primary control case for how deity-identification works under contact (see [[language-phoenician]]).
- **The alphabet relay.** Etruscan is a pure transmission node in script history: it received the Euboean Greek alphabet c. -700 ([[alphabet-greek-vowel-revolution]] → [[alphabet-etruscan]]) and passed it to Latin ([[alphabet-latin]]) — so every Latin-alphabet text, including this one, descends through Etruscan hands. The North-Italic hypothesis runs the runic row ([[alphabet-elder-futhark]]) through the same Etruscan orbit.
- **The word "person" is Etruscan.** Latin *persona* (mask, role, then person) is generally derived from Etruscan *phersu*, the masked figure labeled in the Tomb of the Augurs paintings (Bonfante & Bonfante 2002). The conceptual vocabulary of personhood in Western theology — the three *personae* of the Trinity — carries an Etruscan theatrical-funerary word at its root.
- **Divination as the surviving organ.** Rome absorbed the *disciplina Etrusca* whole — haruspices remained consulted state functionaries into late antiquity (a haruspex named Spurinna warned Caesar of the Ides). The pattern — conqueror keeps the conquered's divination science as a technical specialty — parallels Mesopotamian extispicy's survival across imperial successions; see [[haruspicy-etruscan-roman]].
