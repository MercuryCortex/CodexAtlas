# Classical Sanskrit

The Pāṇini-codified successor register of [[sanskrit-vedic|Vedic Sanskrit]] and the *cosmopolitan language* of South and Southeast Asia for ~1500 years (Pollock 2006). Classical Sanskrit is one of the most thoroughly codified languages in world history: [[panini-grammarian|Pāṇini]]'s *Aṣṭādhyāyī* (c. -500 to -400) sets out the language in ~4,000 algorithmic sutras with such precision that it has been credited as an antecedent of modern generative grammar (the Backus-Naur form for context-free grammars is structurally analogous; Bhate & Kak 1993 and others have explored the formal-language angle).

## The Pāṇinian fix-point

After Pāṇini, Classical Sanskrit *stops evolving*. The grammatical norm is the *Aṣṭādhyāyī*; any future speaker / writer who deviates is in error against the norm. The language is preserved in this fixed state for the next 2,500 years — making Classical Sanskrit roughly comparable to Latin in its post-Cicero stability, but more rigorously codified.

This is what makes Sanskrit a *transmission medium*: a 5th-century-CE author in Kashmir + a 10th-century-CE author in Tamil Nadu + a 15th-century-CE author in Java + a modern-day pandit in Varanasi all write in the *same* language. Sheldon Pollock's *Language of the Gods* (2006) calls this the **Sanskrit cosmopolis** — a transregional, transtemporal, religiously and politically diverse community of literate elites unified by a shared language and literary practice.

## What Classical Sanskrit carries

| Domain | Examples |
|---|---|
| Epic + Puranic | [[phase-3-095-mahabharata|Mahābhārata]], [[ramayana]], the eighteen *Mahāpurāṇas* |
| Philosophical | Brahma Sutras, Sankara's Bhasyas, Madhva, Ramanuja, the six darśanas + their commentary chains |
| Court poetry (*kāvya*) | Kalidasa, Bhāravi, Māgha, Bāṇa, Jayadeva |
| Drama | Kalidasa's *Shakuntala* + *Mālavikāgnimitra*; Bhāsa; Śūdraka's *Mṛcchakaṭika* |
| Scientific + technical | Mathematics + astronomy ([[astronomy-aryabhatiya]], Brahmagupta, Bhaskara, [[astronomy-surya-siddhanta]]), grammar (Patañjali's *Mahābhāṣya*), medicine (Suśruta + Caraka), Kāmaśāstra (Vatsyayana's *Kāmasūtra*), Arthaśāstra (Kauṭilya) |
| Buddhist Sanskrit | Lotus Sutra, Lalitavistara, Mahayana sutras, tantric corpora |
| Jaina Sanskrit | Jaina canonical commentaries |
