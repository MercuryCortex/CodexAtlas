# Egyptian (Hieroglyphic / Hieratic phases)

The language of pharaonic Egypt from c. -3200 to -650 BCE — the single branch of the Afroasiatic language family that has a continuous textual record longer than any other ancient language. Old Egyptian → Middle Egyptian → Late Egyptian are three successive registers of the same language, all encoded by [[alphabet-medu-netjer|hieroglyphs]] for monumental + religious use and by the cursive hieratic script for administrative + everyday writing.

## Periodization

Loprieno 1995 + Allen 2010 establish the standard four-phase chronology of the hieroglyphic-encoded language (excluding the later Demotic + Coptic continuations, which have their own nodes):

1. **Old Egyptian (-2600 to -2000)** — the language of the Pyramid Texts and Old Kingdom royal inscriptions; preserves the most archaic verbal morphology.
2. **Middle Egyptian (-2000 to -1300)** — the classical literary register, codified during the Middle Kingdom and frozen as the *language of monumental + religious writing* for the next 2,500 years. Just as Latin remained the European literary-religious language long after vernacular Romance languages emerged, Middle Egyptian persisted as a *learned register* throughout the New Kingdom, Third Intermediate Period, Saite Period, even into Ptolemaic + Roman times.
3. **Late Egyptian (-1300 to -700)** — the vernacular of the New Kingdom + Third Intermediate Period; the language of the [[phase-1-011-great-hymn-to-aten|Amarna hymns]], the Late Egyptian Miscellanies, and most non-monumental New Kingdom texts.
4. **Demotic (-650 to +450)** — see [[language-demotic-egyptian]].

The Coptic phase (200 CE → present liturgical use) is at [[language-coptic]].

## The hieroglyphic system

The hieroglyphic script (Egyptian *mdw nṯr*, "words of the god") is a mixed logo-phonetic system: signs can function as logograms (representing whole words), as phonograms (representing 1, 2, or 3 consonants), and as classifiers (indicating semantic category). About 24 uniliteral signs form a *de facto* consonantal alphabet within the larger inventory; the script was never reduced to alphabet alone (cf. the [proto-Sinaitic alphabet], which appears to have abstracted exactly this principle).

Champollion's decipherment (1822, using the [[event-rosetta-stone-decipherment-1822|Rosetta Stone]] trilingual) was made possible by Coptic — the modern-era afterlife of the same language — providing a phonetic skeleton against which the hieroglyphic signs could be aligned.

## Religious-cultural significance

Egyptian is the linguistic medium of the longest continuous religious tradition in human history. The Pyramid Texts (c. -2400) are the world's oldest substantial religious corpus; the [[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead|Book of the Dead]] is the most-copied funerary corpus of antiquity (some 2,000 papyri survive). The theological vocabulary — *kꜣ* (ka, life-force), *bꜣ* (ba, animating soul), *ꜣḫ* (akh, glorified spirit), *ḏt* / *nḥḥ* (the two eternities, linear + cyclical) — is one of the most elaborated technical theological lexicons in the ancient world.

Egyptian theological vocabulary + iconography transmit downstream into:
- **Hellenistic Mediterranean** through the Ptolemaic-court interface — Hermetism, alchemy, the Isis cult, the magical papyri (PGM), and the Coptic-language Gnostic library at Nag Hammadi.
- **Hebrew Bible** through the wisdom-literature parallel between *Instruction of Amenemope* and Proverbs 22:17-23:11 (Williams 1971; Emerton 2001), as well as scattered Egyptianisms in Genesis-Exodus geography and Joseph-cycle court vocabulary (Hoch 1994).
- **Christian theology + iconography** through the Isis-Horus → Madonna-Christ transition documented at Coptic-Christian Egypt (Witt 1971; Frankfurter 1998).
