# Sogdian (the Silk Road's lingua franca)

The Eastern Middle Iranian language of Sogdiana — [[place-samarkand|Samarkand]], Bukhara, Penjikent — and of the merchant diaspora that ran the overland [[silk-road-overland|Silk Road]] between Persia and China. Sogdian is the purest case in the Atlas of a **carrier language**: its surviving literature is almost entirely *other peoples' religions in Sogdian translation* — Buddhist, Manichaean, and Christian corpora moving east in Sogdian caravans — while the Sogdians' own cult is known mostly from art, onomastics, and ossuaries.

## A merchant language with three scriptural corpora

Between the 4th and 10th centuries, Sogdian colonies stretched from Merv to the Chinese capitals ([[sogdian-trading-network]]). What survives of the language, almost all from the desert-preserved east (Dunhuang, Turfan):

- **The Ancient Letters** (c. 313) — merchant mail abandoned in a watchtower: trade in musk and silver, a stranded wife's fury, the sack of cities. The oldest substantial Sogdian, and entirely secular.
- **Buddhist Sogdian** — sutra translations made largely *from Chinese* at Dunhuang and Turfan ([[dunhuang-manuscripts]], [[phase-5-002b-diamond-sutra|the Diamond Sutra's]] milieu).
- **Manichaean Sogdian** — the Turfan hymns, parables, and cosmological texts; after the fall of the Uyghur steppe empire's state Manichaeism, Sogdian-language Manichaica are among the religion's best witnesses anywhere ([[tradition-manichaeism]], [[mani]]).
- **Christian Sogdian** — the Bulayïq monastery library: Psalter, lectionaries, and martyrologies of [[tradition-church-of-the-east|the Church of the East]] in East-Syriac script — Christianity's furthest sustained eastward literary outpost before the modern era.

The home religion underneath all three was a Sogdian Zoroastrianism unbound by the Sasanian canon — an Iranian polytheism whose great goddess Nana (of Mesopotamian descent) presided over Penjikent. The conquering religions wrote; the home cult painted.

## Death and one survivor

The Arab conquest of Sogdiana (completed c. 722, the Mugh archive recording its last resistance) began the slide to New Persian and Turkic; by c. 1000 Sogdian was dead as a written language. One mountain dialect survives: **Yaghnobi**, still spoken in Tajikistan — the living remnant of the Silk Road's lingua franca.

## Cross-tradition findings

- **One language, three missionary canons.** Sogdian is the clearest documented case of a single language simultaneously carrying three competing scriptural religions eastward (Buddhism, Manichaeism, East-Syriac Christianity), each in its own script — the same merchants' language, confessionalized three ways (de la Vaissière 2005).
- **The Aramaic scribal machine's longest reach.** The Sogdian script continues the Achaemenid Aramaic chancellery system — heterograms included — and was passed to the Uyghurs, whose script the Mongols took ([[alphabet-mongolian]]) and the Manchus after them. An Achaemenid bureaucratic technology was still writing imperial decrees in Beijing in 1900; the chain runs through Sogdian (see [[alphabet-sogdian]], [[language-aramaic-imperial]]).
- **Buddhism relayed through Chinese.** Buddhist Sogdian translates Chinese versions, not Indic originals — evidence that by the 7th c. the eastward Buddhist transmission had reversed polarity, with China re-exporting Buddhism into Central Asia along the same road that first delivered it (Yoshida 2009).
- **Sister of the Zoroastrian canon language.** Sogdian and [[language-middle-persian-pahlavi|Middle Persian]] are the eastern and western wings of Middle Iranian: one carried the Zoroastrian canon inside the Sasanian state; the other carried everyone else's canons outside it.
