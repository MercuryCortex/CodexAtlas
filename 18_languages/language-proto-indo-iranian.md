# Proto-Indo-Iranian

The reconstructed common ancestor of the Indo-Iranian (or *Aryan* in the narrow technical sense) branch of Indo-European — direct parent of [[sanskrit-vedic|Vedic Sanskrit]] and [[language-old-avestan|Old Avestan]]. PIIr is the linguistic-cultural unity that immediately precedes the split of the Indo-Aryan and Iranian branches.

## Where + when

Linguistic + archaeological convergence locates PIIr in the **Sintashta-Andronovo cultural horizon** (Anthony 2007 ch. 16) — southern Urals + northern Central Asia steppe, c. -2100 to -1500. The Sintashta culture is also where the *spoked-wheel chariot* appears in the archaeological record, and the inherited Indo-Iranian vocabulary for chariotry (*ratha*-, *yuga*-, *cakra*-) is among the most secure parts of the PIIr reconstruction — establishing the PIIr horizon as a chariot-warrior society.

From there, two major southward migrations:

- **Indo-Aryan branch** → through the Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex (BMAC) → northwestern Indian subcontinent (c. -1700 onwards). The Rigveda is composed in this branch shortly after arrival (c. -1500 to -1200).
- **Iranian branch** → onto the Iranian plateau (c. -1500 onwards). The Old Avestan Gāthās are composed in this branch by c. -1200 to -1000.

## What PIIr provides

PIIr supplies the *shared religious-ritual vocabulary* that Vedic and Iranian religion both inherit:

| PIIr root | Vedic Sanskrit | Old Avestan | Shared meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| *yaj-* | *yajña* | *yasna* | "sacrifice, worship" |
| *sauma-* | *soma* | *haoma* | "the pressed sacred drink" |
| *daiu̯a-* | *deva* "god" | *daēva* "demon" (inverted) | (PIIr "deity") |
| *asura-* | *asura* (drifts toward "demon") | *ahura* "Lord" (Mazdā) | (PIIr "lord") |
| *r̥ta-* | *r̥ta* | *aṣ̌a* | "cosmic-moral order" |
| *manas-* | *manas* | *manah-* | "mind" |
| *ātar-* | *agni* (different root) but cf. *atharvan* "fire-priest" | *ātar* | "fire" |
| *bʰaga-* | *bhaga* "share, lord" | *baga* "deity" | "share / dispenser" |
| *agʰni-* | *agni* | (cf. *azi-* "fire") | "fire" |

The systematic correspondence is dense enough that the *Indo-Iranian shared substrate* — Vedic religion + Zoroastrianism as cousin descendents of one ancestral religion — is one of the firmest cross-tradition reconstructions in the entire study of religion.
