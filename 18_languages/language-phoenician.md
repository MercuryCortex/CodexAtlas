# Phoenician (and Punic)

The Canaanite language of the Iron-Age coastal cities — Byblos, Tyre, Sidon — and of their Mediterranean colonial network, above all Carthage. Phoenician is the language in which the 22-letter [[alphabet-phoenician|alphabet]] was standardized and exported; the script that writes the Torah, the Gospels, and the Quran descends from the letters these merchants carried. The language itself, sister to [[hebrew-biblical|Biblical Hebrew]], preserves the cultic world of [[baal|Baal]], [[astarte-canaanite|Astarte]], and [[melqart|Melqart]] that the Hebrew Bible polemicizes against — in the polemicized party's own words.

## What Phoenician is

Phoenician is a Canaanite dialect of Northwest Semitic — the same group as Biblical Hebrew, Moabite, Ammonite, and Edomite. A literate Israelite of the monarchic period and a Tyrian merchant could have understood one another with little effort; the languages are that close. What separates Phoenician is sociology, not structure: it is the dialect of the mercantile port cities that survived the Late Bronze Age collapse, kept the Canaanite scribal tradition alive, standardized the 22-letter consonantal script (the [[alphabet-phoenician|Ahiram sarcophagus]] at [[place-byblos|Byblos]], c. -1050, is the conventional starting point), and spread both language and script across the Mediterranean from [[place-tyre|Tyre]] and [[place-sidon|Sidon]] to Cyprus, Anatolia, North Africa, Sardinia, Sicily, and Spain.

**Punic** is the Carthaginian continuation. After Rome destroyed Carthage (-146), **Neo-Punic** continued as a spoken and inscribed language of North Africa for centuries — Augustine of Hippo, in the 4th-5th c. CE, repeatedly cites spoken Punic in his Numidian congregation and calls it a kindred witness to Hebrew. A Canaanite language was still being spoken among North African Christians six centuries after Carthage fell.

## The corpus

Phoenician left no surviving literature — no epic, no mythology in its own voice (the loss is severe: Philo of Byblos's Greek *Phoenician History* claims to translate a native cosmogony by Sanchuniathon). What survives is epigraphy, and the epigraphy is religiously dense:

- **Royal funerary inscriptions** — Ahiram (Byblos), Tabnit and Eshmunazar II (Sidon) — curse formulas protecting the tomb, naming Astarte's temples and priesthoods.
- **The Karatepe bilingual** (c. -700) — the longest Phoenician text, paired with Hieroglyphic Luwian on an Anatolian citadel gate; the key that unlocked Luwian decipherment.
- **The Pyrgi gold tablets** (c. -500) — a Punic-Etruscan bilingual from an Etruscan sanctuary in Italy, dedicating a shrine to Astarte identified with Etruscan [[uni-etruscan|Uni]].
- **The Marseilles and Carthage tariffs** — Punic temple price-lists assigning fees for each class of sacrifice, the closest extra-biblical analogue to the sacrificial taxonomy of Leviticus.

## Cross-tradition findings

- **The Hebrew Bible's sister dialect.** Phoenician royal and cultic inscriptions are the nearest contemporary relatives of monarchic-period Hebrew. The shared Canaanite literary koine — divine epithets, poetic parallelism, curse formulas — is foundational evidence that Israelite literature emerges from the Canaanite literary world it argues with (Cross 1973). The Baal the Hebrew prophets denounce speaks, in these inscriptions, a language nearly identical to theirs.
- **The Pyrgi contact.** The Pyrgi tablets document Phoenician religion physically installed inside an Etruscan sanctuary, with the dedication drawn up bilingually and the goddesses explicitly identified (Astarte = Uni). It is one of the cleanest dated artifacts of west-Mediterranean religious syncretism — see [[language-etruscan]] for the Etruscan side.
- **The script export is the headline transmission.** The language's deepest consequence is its vehicle: every alphabet of the Western and Islamic scriptural worlds — Greek, Latin, Hebrew square script, Aramaic, Syriac, Arabic — descends from the Phoenician abjad. The full descent narrative lives on [[alphabet-phoenician]]; the Aramaic-route continuation lives on [[language-aramaic-imperial]] and [[alphabet-aramaic]].
- **Sacrificial vocabulary across the Canaanite-Hebrew line.** The Punic tariffs share technical sacrificial terms with Leviticus (e.g. *kll* whole-offering ~ Hebrew *kalil*; *zbḥ* sacrifice ~ Hebrew *zebaḥ*), showing a common Canaanite sacrificial institution behind both cults (Pardee; Hackett 2008).
