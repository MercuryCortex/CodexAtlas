# Younger Avestan

The Eastern Old Iranian language of the bulk of the Avestan corpus — chronologically c. -900 to -300, succeeding the [[language-old-avestan|Old (Gathic) Avestan]] of Zarathustra's prophetic core. Younger Avestan is the language of the *Yashts* (hymns to individual yazata-divinities), the *Vendidad* (anti-demonic ritual code), the *Visperad*, and most of the surrounding Yasna liturgical material. It is also the largest single body of preserved ancient Iranian textual material.

## What Younger Avestan is

Linguistically, Younger Avestan represents the next stage of Avestan after the archaic Gathic register: the verbal morphology simplifies, certain archaic noun forms drop out, the syntax becomes somewhat more analytic. But the differences are differences within a single language family — Younger Avestan and Old/Gathic Avestan are continuous in a way that Old + Middle Persian, separated by a similar timespan, are not.

The corpus is enormous compared to Old Avestan: where Old Avestan is essentially the Gathas of Zarathustra + the Yasna Haptanghaiti + four short mantras (maybe 30 pages of text), Younger Avestan supplies hundreds of pages of liturgical, hymnic, mythological, and ritual-legal material — virtually all of what survives of the Sasanian-era Great Avesta in its 21 Nasks (most of which is lost, but most of *what survives* is Younger Avestan).

## The four principal corpora

1. **The Yasna** — the daily liturgical text; Old Avestan Gathic material is embedded inside a Younger Avestan frame.
2. **The Yashts** — 22 hymns to individual *yazata*-divinities. The Yashts to [[mithra-zoroastrian|Mithra]], [[anahita-zoroastrian|Anāhitā]], Tishtrya (Sirius), Verethraghna (Bahrām), and Vāyu are the longest and most mythologically substantive. The Yashts preserve pre-Zoroastrian Iranian theology that Zarathustra's reform did not eliminate; the Mithra Yasht in particular is one of the principal documents of Indo-Iranian religious-comparative method.
3. **The Vendidad** — *Vīdēvdāt*, "Law against the demons." 22 chapters of anti-demonic ritual + purity code. Vendidad 2 contains the cosmogonic Yima narrative; Vendidad 19 contains the Chinvat-bridge eschatological journey. This is the bulk of the Zoroastrian ritual-legal tradition's first-millennium-BCE attestation.
4. **The Visperad** + the **Khorde Avesta** — additional liturgical material.

## The pre-Zoroastrian substrate

One of the most remarkable features of Younger Avestan literature is its preservation of pre-Zoroastrian Iranian religion under Zoroastrian theological reframing. The Mithra Yasht is addressed to a god who Zarathustra's Gathic theology should have demoted — the Younger Avesta instead elevates him as one of Ahura Mazdā's principal yazatas. Similarly Anāhitā (the waters/fertility/warrior goddess), Verethraghna (the divine warrior who corresponds to Vedic Indra-Vṛtrahan), and Tishtrya (the star Sirius, brought into Zoroastrian theology as a yazata) all preserve Iranian-religion material that pre-dates Zarathustra's reform.

This is the load-bearing evidence that Zarathustra's reform was *theological* (re-organizing the divine hierarchy under Ahura Mazdā) rather than *cultic-eliminationist* (Boyce 1975 ch. 6). The priestly tradition retained much of the inherited Iranian-religion material; it slotted it inside the Zoroastrian theological frame as the yazatas (created divine beings subordinate to Ahura Mazdā).

## Transmission and oral preservation

The Younger Avestan corpus was transmitted orally in priestly memorization for ~1,500 years before the Sasanian period committed it to writing using the purpose-built [[alphabet-avestan|Avestan alphabet]]. The standard view (Boyce 1975; Skjærvø 2009) is that the corpus was composed orally in Eastern Iran during the late 2nd through early 1st millennium BCE and carried west by priests moving with the Achaemenid imperial expansion. Modern Parsi + Iranian Zoroastrian liturgy continues to recite Younger Avestan material as the daily liturgical core.
