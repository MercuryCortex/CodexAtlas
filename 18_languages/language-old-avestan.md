# Old Avestan (Gathic Avestan)

The oldest attested Iranian language and the language of [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra|Zarathustra's Gāθās]] — the founding hymns of [[tradition-persian-zoroastrian|Zoroastrianism]]. Old Avestan is sister to [[sanskrit-vedic|Vedic Sanskrit]] and, with it, one of the two principal witnesses to Proto-Indo-Iranian.

## The Indo-Iranian sisterhood

Old Avestan and Vedic Sanskrit are so close that the morphological + vocabulary correspondence is essentially mechanical. A Vedic priest and an Old-Iranian priest, around -1200, would have understood each other's liturgical language with effort but without translators — they spoke late-stage dialects of what had been a unified Indo-Iranian ritual-theological language only a few centuries earlier.

| Sanskrit | Old Avestan | gloss |
|---|---|---|
| *yajña* | *yasna* | "sacrifice / worship" |
| *soma* | *haoma* | "the pressed sacred drink" |
| *deva* | *daēva* | "deity" / "demon" (**polemic inversion**) |
| *asura* | *ahura* | "lord" (**polemic inversion**) |
| *r̥ta* | *aṣ̌a* | "cosmic-moral order" |
| *agni* | *ātar* | "fire" (different roots but same liturgical centrality) |
| *manas* | *manah-* | "mind" |
| *sapta sindhavaḥ* | *hapta həṇdu* | "seven rivers" (the same geographic memory) |

The shared *liturgical-technical* vocabulary — not just everyday words — establishes that Vedic and Iranian religion descend from a *common Indo-Iranian ritual-theological system*, not just a common language. See [[soma]] and [[haoma]] for the substance side, and [[asura-deva-inversion]] for the polemic-inversion theme.

## The deva/daēva inversion (the load-bearing edge)

The Indo-Iranian word *daiva-* originally meant "deity" (cognate with Latin *deus*, Greek *Zeus*, and Sanskrit *deva*). In Zarathustra's reform, the *daēvas* are reclassified as false gods / demons / objects of polemic — and the parallel term *ahura* (Sanskrit *asura*, which in later Vedic comes to mean "anti-god / demon") is elevated to designate the supreme deity, *Ahura Mazdā* ("Wise Lord").

The inversion is *symmetrical*: Vedic *deva* is good and *asura* drifts toward evil; Avestan *daēva* is evil and *ahura* is good. Two cousin traditions perform a mirror-image polemic against each other's chief theological vocabulary. This is one of the cleanest documented cases in world religious history of a *cross-tradition polemic split* preserved at the level of language itself.

## Transmission downstream

Old Avestan was the ritual-liturgical language of Iranian religion for the next ~2,500 years. The corpus was transmitted *orally* in priestly memorization for ~1,500 years before being committed to writing in the Sasanian period (5th-6th c. CE) using the purpose-built [[alphabet-avestan|Avestan alphabet]] — derived from Pahlavi but with extra signs added to capture the precise phonetic detail the priests had memorized.

Old Avestan → Young Avestan (later Yasna books + the rest of the Avesta) → Middle Persian (Pahlavi) for the post-Achaemenid commentarial tradition (*Zand*) → Modern Persian for the present-day Iranian + Parsi community's liturgical-cultural inheritance.
