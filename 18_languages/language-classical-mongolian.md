# Classical Mongolian

The written language of the Mongols — born as the chancellery language of [[event-mongol-conquests-1206-1294|the world's largest land empire]], matured as the canonical language of Mongolian Buddhism. Its two monuments mark the two poles: the *Secret History of the Mongols* (13th c.), the steppe epic of Chinggis Khan under the eternal sky-god Tengri; and the Mongolian Kanjur-Tanjur (17th-18th c.), 333 volumes of the Buddhist canon translated from [[language-classical-tibetan|Tibetan]] — the largest translation project in Inner Asian history.

## From imperial chancellery to canonical language

Mongol writing begins as an act of imperial appropriation: c. 1204, Chinggis Khan ordered the captured Uyghur scribe Tatatonga to adapt the Uyghur vertical script for Mongol ([[alphabet-mongolian]]). The earliest dated monument (Stele of Yisüngge, c. 1224-25) is already imperial self-commemoration. **Middle Mongol** — the empire's language — survives in the *Secret History* (preserved, remarkably, in Ming Chinese-character transcription), in chancellery documents from Iran to Korea, and in the multi-script Yuan record.

**Classical Mongolian** proper crystallizes with the Buddhist conversion of the 16th-17th centuries: the 1578 alliance of Altan Khan with Sonam Gyatso (the meeting that coined the title *Dalai Lama*), Ligdan Khan's Kanjur translation bureau (1628-29), and the Qing xylograph editions (1718-20 Kanjur; 1742-49 Tanjur). The translation projects fixed the orthography and built a calqued dharma-terminology — making the written language itself an artifact of the Tibetan-Mongolian religious alliance ([[tradition-tibetan-buddhism]]).

## Tengri beneath the dharma

The *Secret History* opens with a wolf and a doe born "by the destiny of Heaven above" (*deger-e tngri*): the native theology is the **Tengri sky-mandate**, which legitimated the Chinggisid claim to world rule — the steppe's functional analogue to the Chinese Mandate of Heaven its khans confronted. The 17th-c. chronicles (Altan Tobchi, Erdeni-yin Tobchi) then perform a documented theological fusion: the Chinggisid lineage is rewritten as a dharma-king (*cakravartin*) genealogy descending from the Buddhist kings of India and Tibet — Tengri's mandate re-clothed in Buddhist universal kingship.

## Cross-tradition findings

- **A canon imported whole.** Mongolian Buddhism received its entire scriptural canon by organized state translation from Tibetan within roughly a century — one of the cleanest documented cases of complete canon-transfer between languages, with the receiving literary language reshaped by the transfer (Kara 2005). Parallel in shape to the Tibetan reception from Sanskrit it continues.
- **The Aramaic script lineage crosses the steppe.** Vertical Mongolian writing descends Aramaic → [[alphabet-sogdian|Sogdian]] → Old Uyghur → Mongolian (→ Manchu): Achaemenid chancellery technology carried by Silk-Road merchants into the scripts of two Chinese dynasties' imperial archives. The language node for the carrier stage is [[language-sogdian]].
- **Dual-mandate theology.** The Chinggisid Tengri-mandate and its later Buddhist cakravartin re-framing form a documented two-stage political theology — sky-god election fused with dharma-kingship — that the chronicles perform openly, line by line (de Rachewiltz 2004; Kara 2005).
