# Vedic Sanskrit

The oldest attested Indo-Aryan language and the principal liturgical / scholarly medium of the [[tradition-vedic|Vedic]] religious-cultural complex (c. -1500 to -500 BCE). Vedic Sanskrit is one of the three principal witnesses (alongside Hittite and Mycenaean Greek) used to reconstruct the Proto-Indo-European language and culture — making it not only a religious vehicle but also a foundational evidentiary basis for the entire comparative Indo-European program.

## Stratification

Standard scholarship (Witzel 1995, Jamison & Brereton 2014) recognizes three internal periods:

1. **Mantra-language stratum** (c. -1500 to -1100) — the oldest Rigvedic hymns (especially Maṇḍalas 2-7); shows the most archaic phonology + morphology
2. **Saṃhitā-prose stratum** (c. -1100 to -800) — later Rigvedic + Yajurveda + Samaveda + Atharvaveda; transitional features
3. **Brāhmaṇa-Upaniṣadic stratum** (c. -800 to -500) — the explanatory ritual prose ([[phase-2-006-brahmanas-aranyakas]], the early [[phase-2-012-brihadaranyaka-upanishad]] and [[phase-2-013-chandogya-upanishad]]); already shows Middle-Indo-Aryan features intruding

Around -500 to -400, [[panini-grammarian|Pāṇini]] codified the successor register as [[language-sanskrit-classical|Classical Sanskrit]] in his *Aṣṭādhyāyī* — fixing the language as a scholarly standard for the next ~2,500 years.

## The Indo-Iranian unity

Vedic Sanskrit's closest living relative is [[language-old-avestan|Old Avestan]] — the language of [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra|Zarathustra's Gāthās]]. The two languages are so morphologically and lexically close that whole Rigvedic verses can be transposed into Avestan with regular sound-change rules:

| Sanskrit | Avestan | gloss |
|---|---|---|
| *sapta sindhavaḥ* | *hapta həṇdu* | "seven rivers" |
| *yajña* | *yasna* | "sacrifice / worship" |
| *soma* | *haoma* | "the pressed sacred drink" |
| *deva* | *daēva* | "deity" / "demon" (the famous polemic inversion — see [[asura-deva-inversion]]) |
| *asura* | *ahura* | "lord" (the parallel inversion in the other direction) |

The Sanskrit/Avestan polemic inversion of *deva* and *asura* is one of the cleanest documented cases of cross-tradition vocabulary divergence-by-religious-polemic in world religious history. See [[language-old-avestan]] for the Iranian side.

## As transmission medium

Vedic Sanskrit's significance for the broader graph isn't only that it is the language of the Vedas — it is also a *transmission medium* that carries cross-tradition material across two further long arcs:

- **Vedic → Buddhist Pali / Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit → East Asian Buddhist literature.** The vast Buddhist textual corpus is a descendant register; key Vedic ritual + philosophical vocabulary persists into Mahayana technical language. See [[language-sanskrit-classical]] for the post-Panini standard and [[phase-5-004-platform-sutra-huineng]] et al. for the East Asian terminus.
- **Vedic comparative linguistics → 18th–19th century European Indo-European studies → Grimm's Law, Verner's Law, the Neogrammarian school.** William Jones's 1786 address to the Royal Asiatic Society of Bengal, identifying Sanskrit's kinship with Greek + Latin, was the founding moment of historical comparative linguistics. The Indo-European hypothesis itself — and via it the entire modern field — descends from Vedic Sanskrit as its empirical seed.
