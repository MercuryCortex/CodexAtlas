# Classical Armenian (Grabar)

The language of the first Christian kingdom — and of an alphabet created, in a documented act c. 405 CE, specifically so that a people could read scripture in its own tongue. Classical Armenian (*grabar*, "the written") is an independent branch of Indo-European whose entire written existence begins with Bible translation, whose Golden Age is a translation program, and whose lasting gift to cross-tradition scholarship is preservation: a series of Greek religious works now survive only in Armenian.

## Born literate, born scriptural

Armenia converted at the top c. 301 CE (King Trdat III under Gregory the Illuminator — state Christianity a generation before Constantine's patronage), but for a century Armenian Christians worshipped in Greek and Syriac. Mesrop **Mashtots**, with Catholicos Sahak and royal sponsorship, created the 36-letter alphabet c. 405 ([[alphabet-armenian]]); his pupil Koriwn's *Life of Mashtots* is the earliest surviving biography of a script-inventor. The Bible translation completed by c. 435 — worked first from [[language-syriac|Syriac]], then revised against [[koine-greek|Greek]] exemplars after the Council of Ephesus — earned the medieval epithet "Queen of Translations." A language with no prior written form opens its record with a complete scriptural canon and a Golden Age of original theological prose (Eznik, Agathangelos) inside one generation.

After rejecting the Council of Chalcedon ([[event-council-of-chalcedon-451]] — formally repudiated at Dvin, 506/554), the Armenian Apostolic Church became a self-standing miaphysite communion, with Grabar as its liturgical language to the present ([[tradition-armenian-apostolic]]).

## Cross-tradition findings

- **A preservation vault for Greek religious literature.** Because the Hellenizing School translated with calque-like fidelity, works lost in Greek survive in Armenian: several Philo of Alexandria treatises, Eusebius's *Chronicle*, Irenaeus's *Demonstration of the Apostolic Preaching* — and the [[phase-4-077-definitions-hermes-asclepius-armenian|Definitions of Hermes Trismegistus to Asclepius]], whose 6th-c. Armenian version is a witness to Hermetic literature older than much of the Greek manuscript tradition. The language functions as an archive of another tradition's lost library.
- **The Iranian stratum beneath the Christian vocabulary.** Armenian's core religious lexicon is largely Parthian-Iranian on loan (*den* "religion," *hreshtak* "angel," *dzhokhk* "hell"), documenting centuries of Zoroastrian-Armenian symbiosis — the very world Eznik's *Refutation* (a primary source for Sasanian Zurvanism) then argues against ([[language-middle-persian-pahlavi]], [[tradition-zoroastrianism]]). The misclassification of Armenian as Iranian, corrected by Hübschmann (1875), is a founding episode of comparative linguistics.
- **The alphabet-for-scripture pattern.** Armenian opens the Caucasian chapter of the cross-tradition pattern in which conversion + script-creation + Bible translation arrive as one package: Mashtots is credited by the sources with involvement in the Georgian ([[language-old-georgian]]) and Caucasian Albanian alphabets as well — three scriptural alphabets radiating from one missionary-philological project. Compare [[language-old-church-slavonic]] (Cyril-Methodius), [[language-geez]], and Gothic.
