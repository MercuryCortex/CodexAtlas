# Ugaritic (the language of Ras Shamra)

## What it is
Ugaritic is the Northwest Semitic language of the Late Bronze Age city of Ugarit (modern Ras Shamra, on the Syrian coast), known from clay tablets recovered there since 1929. Written in a unique 30-sign cuneiform **alphabet** — one of the earliest alphabets, and the source of the oldest attested alphabetic letter-order — it preserves the richest body of Canaanite mythology and religion we possess, in a language so close to biblical Hebrew that it transformed the modern understanding of the Bible.

## Context
- **Where and when.** The royal archives of Ugarit, c. 1400–1185 BCE, until the city's destruction in the Late Bronze Age collapse.
- **The script.** A cuneiform-shaped abjad of 30 letters — an independent alphabetic experiment alongside Proto-Sinaitic/Phoenician.
- **Discovery.** The 1929 finds at Ras Shamra opened a window onto Canaanite religion that had previously been visible only through the hostile lens of the Hebrew Bible.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The language that rewrote biblical studies.** Before 1929, Canaanite religion was known almost entirely from the polemics of the Hebrew Bible. The Ugaritic tablets gave it its own voice — and revealed how much of the Bible is Canaanite in bone. The high god **El**, the **divine council**, the storm-god **Baʿal**, the goddess **Asherah/Athirat**, the chaos-sea **Yam** and the seven-headed **Lotan** (biblical Leviathan), and even the parallelistic verse-form of Hebrew poetry all stand illuminated by Ugaritic. Psalms, Job, and the prophets read differently once Ugaritic is known; the Baal Cycle is the nearest thing we have to the mythological matrix from which Israelite monotheism emerged by sharp reaction (Cross 1973; Smith & Pitard 2009; Bordreuil & Pardee 2009).
