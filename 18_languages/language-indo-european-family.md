# Indo-European Language Family

The largest and best-studied language family in the world, containing the native languages of roughly half the global population. The IE family includes the principal classical languages of the European, Near Eastern, and South Asian worlds (Hittite, Mycenaean Greek, [[koine-greek|Koine Greek]], Latin, [[sanskrit-vedic|Vedic Sanskrit]], [[language-old-avestan|Old Avestan]], Old Persian, Old Irish, Old Norse, Old English, Old Church Slavonic, Classical Armenian, Tocharian A/B), and almost all modern languages of Europe, the northern half of South Asia, and the Iranian plateau.

## Why this lens matters for the Atlas

The Atlas's whole methodology is *cross-tradition transmission* — and Indo-European is the single largest cross-tradition vector in world religious history.

- The [[sanskrit-vedic]] / [[language-old-avestan]] sisterhood and the [[asura-deva-inversion]] are not abstract linguistic facts — they are the *load-bearing evidence* for the entire Indo-Iranian shared religious substrate (Vedic + Zoroastrian) from which Hindu, Buddhist (via Sanskrit), and Mithraic / Manichaean (via Iranian) traditions descend.
- Greek + Latin + Sanskrit + Avestan trifunctionality (sovereign / warrior / fertility classes) — the Dumézilian hypothesis — works *because* these languages preserve cognate vocabulary for the three classes.
- Christian Latin liturgy + Vedic Sanskrit ritual + Iranian Avestan recitation share a common *liturgical-language* posture (sacred-language-distinct-from-vernacular) traceable to the inherited IE priestly-language pattern that Watkins 1995 reconstructs.
- Modern Indo-European comparative philology — Jones / Bopp / Grimm / Saussure / Watkins / Mallory — is the *methodology* that proves these connections rigorously and so underwrites every transmission-bucket edge that runs across these traditions in the graph.

## The branches and their classical witnesses

| Branch | Earliest attested | Significance for Atlas |
|---|---|---|
| Anatolian | Hittite tablets, c. -1700 | Earliest attested IE; preserves PIE laryngeal phonology and an archaic case system. |
| Indo-Iranian | Vedic Sanskrit ~-1500, Old Avestan ~-1200 | The Indo-Iranian unity is the single most fertile cross-tradition vector — see above. |
| Hellenic | Mycenaean Greek (Linear B) ~-1400 | Carrier of Hellenistic philosophy + Septuagint + early Christianity (via [[koine-greek]]). |
| Italic | Latin from -7th c. | Liturgical language of medieval Latin Christianity; carrier of Roman + Renaissance + scientific vocabulary. |
| Celtic | Old Irish from -4th c. (Ogham); Continental Gaulish | Druidic religion; the *Táin* + Welsh *Mabinogion* preserve archaic IE motifs. |
| Germanic | Gothic from 4th c. CE (Ulfilas Bible) | Old Norse mythology corpus; English as the modern lingua franca. |
| Balto-Slavic | Old Church Slavonic from 9th c. CE | Carrier of Byzantine Christianity into Slavic Europe; Slavic-pagan substrate. |
| Armenian | 5th c. CE (Mesrop Mashtots's alphabet) | Earliest Christian-state liturgical language; archaic IE vocabulary. |
| Albanian | 15th c. CE | Late attestation; archaic features useful for PIE reconstruction. |
| Tocharian | 6th-8th c. CE manuscripts (Tarim Basin) | Buddhist texts in an IE language at the Silk Road; striking evidence of IE eastern spread. |
