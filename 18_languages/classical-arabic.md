# Classical Arabic

The fixed literary register of Arabic that emerges with the Qur'an (610-632 CE), is codified by the early-Islamic Basran + Kufan grammatical schools (8th c.), and serves as the trans-regional + trans-temporal high register of the Islamic world for the next ~1400 years. Like [[language-sanskrit-classical|Classical Sanskrit]] and [[language-latin-classical|Classical Latin]], Classical Arabic is a *cosmopolitan literary language* — codified by reference to a fix-point text (here the Qur'an), preserved across a vast geography (Andalusia to Bengal at the maximum extent), and the medium for religion + scholarship + literature for the entire premodern Islamic world.

## The Qur'an as linguistic fix-point

Like Sanskrit-by-Pāṇini, Classical Arabic is a *codified* language — but where Pāṇini's *Aṣṭādhyāyī* is a grammar that *describes* a register, the Qur'an is a *text* that *exemplifies* a register. The fix-point is documentary, not metalinguistic. The 8th-century Basran + Kufan grammatical schools (Sibawayh, al-Khalil ibn Ahmad) then describe + codify the language by reference to the Qur'an + the pre-Islamic poetry corpus + a small body of Bedouin oral testimony. From then on, *al-fuṣḥā* is the language of the Qur'an, of Sibawayh's *Kitāb*, of the pre-Islamic *Mu'allaqāt* — and any post-codification author writes by the standards of those texts.

## The Greco-Arabic translation movement (the load-bearing cross-tradition role)

The single most consequential cross-tradition role of Classical Arabic is as the *receiving and transmitting language* of the Greco-Arabic translation movement of 9th-10th-century Baghdad (Gutas 1998). Under Abbasid patronage at the *Bayt al-Ḥikma* (House of Wisdom) and adjacent institutions, an enormous corpus of Greek philosophical, mathematical, medical, and astronomical work was systematically translated into Arabic via Syriac intermediaries — by Hunayn ibn Ishaq + his school + many others.

The output: nearly all of Aristotle's surviving corpus; Plato's principal dialogues; Hippocrates + Galen; Euclid + Archimedes + Apollonius; Ptolemy's *Almagest* + *Geographia* + *Tetrabiblos*; Plotinus (in the Arabic *Theologia Aristotelis* compilation); Galen's voluminous medical writings. These translations then became the substrate for ~500 years of original Islamic philosophical, scientific, and medical work — al-Kindi, al-Farabi, Ibn Sina, al-Razi, Ibn Rushd, al-Tusi, Ibn al-Haytham — which then itself transmitted back into Latin Christian Europe via the 12th-13th c. Toledo + Sicily translation movements (Gerard of Cremona, Michael Scot, Hermann of Carinthia).

This is the single largest documented cross-tradition scholarly-textual transmission in world history. Arabic is its load-bearing medium.
