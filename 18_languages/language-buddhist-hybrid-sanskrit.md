# Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit

The literary register of the Mahāyāna sutra corpus — a hybrid of Middle Indo-Aryan (Prakrit-like) morphology + vocabulary with Sanskritized phonology + spelling. Franklin Edgerton's *Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit Grammar and Dictionary* (1953) defined and catalogued the register; it remains the standard reference 70+ years later. Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit is the language of the Lotus Sutra, the Heart Sutra, the Diamond Sutra, the Avataṃsaka, the Vimalakīrti, the Pure Land sutras — the canonical Mahāyāna corpus that becomes the source for East Asian + Tibetan Buddhism.

## What Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit is

Edgerton 1953 argued that the Mahāyāna sutra corpus emerged from an originally Middle-Indo-Aryan (Prakrit-like) Buddhist canonical tradition that progressively *retro-Sanskritized* itself as Sanskrit became the prestige literary language of the Indian world (c. -200 to +200). The result is a register with three diagnostic layers:

1. **Phonology + spelling** — heavily Sanskritized (consonant clusters restored, long vowels reinstated, etc.).
2. **Morphology + syntax** — partially Sanskritized, with persistent Middle-Indo-Aryan irregularities (especially in verbal forms, certain noun-stem treatments, and the *gāthā*-verse register).
3. **Vocabulary** — mixed; Sanskrit lexemes alongside Middle-Indo-Aryan loanwords + neologisms.

The result is not "bad Sanskrit" (as 19th-c. philologists initially supposed) but a distinct literary register — a *Mischsprache* in Edgerton's term — preserving an earlier Middle-Indo-Aryan layer under a Sanskritized surface.

## The Lotus Sutra and the canonical Mahāyāna corpus

Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit is the linguistic medium of one of the most influential literary corpora in world history. The Lotus Sutra alone (composed c. 100-200 CE in this register) becomes the foundational text of:

- Tiantai Buddhism in China
- Tendai Buddhism in Japan (whence Pure Land, Zen, Nichiren branch)
- Nichiren Buddhism (Japan; the daimoku invocation *Namu-Myōhō-Renge-Kyō* is the title of the Lotus Sutra)

The Prajñāpāramitā corpus (Heart Sutra, Diamond Sutra, the 8,000-line + 25,000-line + 100,000-line Perfection-of-Wisdom sutras) supplies the *śūnyatā* (emptiness) doctrine that becomes the core philosophical inheritance of all subsequent Mahāyāna + Vajrayāna Buddhism. The Avataṃsaka Sutra supplies the Huayan / Hwaeom / Kegon school's cosmology. The Vimalakīrti supplies the lay-bodhisattva paradigm. The Pure Land sutras supply the Amitābha-rebirth path. Together this BHS-corpus is the doctrinal source-text for the entire eastern half of the Buddhist world.

## The Pollock-Cosmopolis context

Pollock 2006 (*The Language of the Gods in the World of Men*) argues that c. -200 to +1000 CE the Sanskrit-speaking + Sanskrit-writing world constituted a *cosmopolis* — a transregional prestige-language order spanning the Indian subcontinent + Southeast Asia. Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit is one of the most striking case studies of this dynamic: a vernacular religious-canonical tradition (originally Middle Indo-Aryan, parallel to Pali) reshapes itself toward the prestige literary register without fully assimilating, leaving the hybrid traces that Edgerton catalogued.

## Transmission to Chinese, Tibetan, Mongolian

The BHS corpus reaches East Asia through a translation-history of remarkable scale:

- **Chinese** — Lokakṣema (2nd c.), An Shigao (2nd c.), Dharmarakṣa (3rd c.), Kumārajīva (4th-5th c.), Paramārtha (6th c.), Xuanzang (7th c.), Yijing (7th-8th c.), Amoghavajra (8th c.). The resulting Chinese Buddhist canon (Taishō Tripiṭaka, edited 1924-1934) contains thousands of texts.
- **Tibetan** — Thonmi Sambhota (7th c.), Vairocana (8th c.), Rinchen Zangpo (10th c.). The Mahāvyutpatti standardization commission c. 820 CE established the highly-literal Tibetan translation register; the resulting Kangyur + Tengyur canon (final redaction 13th-14th c.) preserves many BHS texts whose Sanskrit originals are now lost.
- **Mongolian / Manchu / Tangut** — further chains via Tibetan + Chinese.
