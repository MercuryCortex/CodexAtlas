# Classical Latin

The Italic-branch Indo-European language of Roman Republic + Empire, codified literarily in the Ciceronian + Augustan + Silver-Age period (-100 to +200) and preserved as the principal scholarly + ecclesiastical language of Latin Christianity + medieval European learning for the next ~1500 years.

## Cosmopolitan-language parallel with Sanskrit

Pollock 2006's *The Language of the Gods in the World of Men* draws an extended parallel between Latin and [[language-sanskrit-classical|Classical Sanskrit]] — the two great cosmopolitan literary languages of the pre-modern world. Both:

- Function as transregional high registers above continuum of vernaculars
- Codify a fixed literary norm (Ciceronian Latin / Pāṇinian Sanskrit) that scholars across centuries can write in identically
- Serve religious + scholarly + administrative + literary functions
- Yield gradually to vernacular literatures in the medieval-to-early-modern period (Italian / French / Spanish / etc. for Latin; Tamil + Bengali + Hindi + Marathi etc. for Sanskrit)
