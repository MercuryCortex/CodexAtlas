# Classical Mandaic

## What it is
Classical Mandaic is the Eastern Late Aramaic language of the Mandaeans — the only Gnostic religion to survive continuously from antiquity. A sister-register of [[language-babylonian-aramaic|Jewish Babylonian Aramaic]] and Syriac, it is the liturgical and scriptural language of the entire Mandaean canon (the Ginza Rabba, the Mandaean Book of John, and the Qolasta prayerbook), written in a unique 24-letter Mandaic alphabet that the Mandaeans regard as itself sacred. It survives as a living language (Neo-Mandaic) among a few thousand speakers in Iraq, Iran, and a global diaspora.

## Context
- **Religious milieu.** Mandaeism — a baptismal (masbuta) Gnostic faith of the southern Mesopotamian marshes, revering a line of true prophets (Adam, Seth, Noah, Shem) crowned by **John the Baptist**, while rejecting Jesus, Abraham, and Moses as false.
- **Phonology and script.** Distinguished from Syriac and Jewish Babylonian Aramaic by characteristic sound-shifts; uniquely among Aramaic scripts, Mandaic writes vowels as full letters.
- **Transmission.** Preserved by a hereditary priesthood under Sasanian, Abbasid, and modern pressures; the community has been gravely endangered since the 2003 Iraq War.

## MASSIVE WIN — cross-tradition edges
- **The last living Gnostics — and the only religion that makes John the Baptist its central prophet.** Mandaeism is the sole Gnostic tradition that has survived unbroken from late antiquity into the present, and Mandaic is the language that carried it. Uniquely in the religious world, the Mandaeans exalt **John the Baptist** (Yahya Yuhana) above Jesus — treating the Baptist, not Jesus, as the true teacher — which has made their scriptures a key (and contested) source in the study of John, of first-century baptismal sects, and of the origins of Gnosticism in the Jordan-valley milieu shared with early Christianity (Macuch 1965; Häberl 2009; Buckley, *The Mandaeans*, 2002).
