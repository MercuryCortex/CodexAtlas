# Koine Greek

The "common dialect" — the supra-regional Greek that emerged from the blending of Attic + Ionic with elements of other dialects in the wake of Alexander's conquests (-323 onwards) and that served as the *lingua franca* of the eastern Mediterranean, the Near East, and Egypt for roughly a millennium.

## Why this matters for the Atlas

Koine Greek is one of the most consequential transmission media in the history of religion. Three load-bearing roles:

### 1. The Septuagint — the first cross-cultural translation of a sacred corpus

The [[phase-3-006-septuagint|Septuagint]] (c. -250 to -150) is the Koine Greek translation of the Hebrew Bible, produced by Hellenistic Jewish scholars in Alexandria. It is the *first* documented translation of a major sacred-textual corpus from one language into another, and it carried Hebrew Bible content + theology to the entire Greek-speaking world for the next ~600 years. When the New Testament writers cite "the Scriptures", they almost always cite the Septuagint form, not the underlying Hebrew. Christian theology is in significant part Koine-Greek theology of the Septuagint text. See [[event-nag-hammadi-discovery]] for the 1945 recovery of the Coptic Gnostic library whose Greek originals were composed in this language.

### 2. The New Testament — the founding texts of Christianity

The entire 27-book New Testament corpus was composed in Koine (mid-1st to early-2nd c. CE). Its theological vocabulary is built from Koine words — *logos*, *pneuma*, *sōtēria*, *charis*, *agápē*, *kyrios*, *christos*, *evangelion*, *ekklēsia*, *baptisma*, *eucharistia*. Every doctrinal debate of the early-Christian centuries (Nicaea, Constantinople, Ephesus, Chalcedon) is conducted in Koine and depends on Koine semantics (*homoousios* vs *homoiousios*; *physis* vs *hypostasis* vs *prosōpon*).

### 3. The carrier of Hellenistic philosophy + Hermetism + Gnosticism

The [[corpus-hermeticum|Corpus Hermeticum]] (2nd-3rd c. CE), the Gnostic Greek-original texts behind the Coptic [[event-nag-hammadi-discovery|Nag Hammadi Library]], Philo of Alexandria's Middle Platonist commentaries on the Bible, the Neoplatonist tradition (Plotinus's *Enneads* are in Koine), and the Hellenistic-Jewish + Christian + Gnostic literary cross-pollination of Alexandria are *all* in Koine.

## Cognacy in the Indo-European frame

Koine is a daughter of the Hellenic branch of [[language-indo-european-family|Indo-European]] — making it cognate with [[sanskrit-vedic]], [[language-old-avestan]], Latin, the Celtic + Germanic + Slavic branches, etc. The shared inherited vocabulary and morphology (cf. Greek *patēr* / Sanskrit *pitr̥* / Latin *pater* / English *father*; Greek *theós* / Latin *deus* / Sanskrit *deva*) is one of the load-bearing evidence-classes for the entire IE hypothesis. Watkins 1995 reconstructs inherited PIE poetic formulae preserved in both Greek and Vedic (the *kléos áphthiton* / *śrávas ákṣitam* "imperishable fame" formula; the chaoskampf serpent-slaying line) — see [[chaoskampf]].
