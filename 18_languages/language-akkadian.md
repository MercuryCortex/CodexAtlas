# Akkadian

The East Semitic language of Mesopotamia from approximately -2500 to 100 CE — named for Sargon of Akkad's royal city, spoken in two principal regional varieties (Babylonian in the south, Assyrian in the north), and the medium of one of the deepest continuous literary + scientific + religious traditions in human history. The single most extensively documented ancient Semitic language by sheer volume of preserved text.

## What Akkadian is

Akkadian is the only well-attested branch of East Semitic (Eblaite, its sister, is documented only in the 3rd-millennium archives of Tell Mardikh). It diverges from the other Semitic branches (Northwest Semitic — Hebrew, Aramaic, Ugaritic; South Semitic — Arabic, Ethiopic, Old South Arabian) early in the prehistory of the family. Its East Semitic signature features include the *parras* / *iprus* / *iparras* verbal pattern (rather than the West-Semitic prefix + suffix conjugations), the *u*-vowel marker of the indicative, and the loss of the Proto-Semitic ʿ + ḥ + ġ + ḫ phonemes (variously merged or lost).

The internal periodization runs:

- **Old Akkadian (-2500 to -2000)** — Sargonic + Ur-III period.
- **Old Babylonian (-2000 to -1500)** — the classical literary phase; Hammurabi's Code, the Old Babylonian Gilgamesh.
- **Old Assyrian (-2000 to -1500)** — Anatolian trading-colony archives (Kanesh).
- **Middle Babylonian + Middle Assyrian (-1500 to -1000)** — Kassite Babylon, Middle Assyrian kingdom.
- **Neo-Babylonian + Neo-Assyrian (-1000 to -600)** — Neo-Assyrian empire, Neo-Babylonian (Chaldean) empire.
- **Late Babylonian (-600 to ~100 CE)** — Achaemenid + Seleucid + Parthian-period scholarly survival.

## The cuneiform script

Akkadian inherited the [[alphabet-cuneiform-sacred|cuneiform script]] from [[language-sumerian|Sumerian]] c. -2500 and adapted it to Semitic phonology. The script is logo-syllabic: signs can stand for whole words (logograms, mostly inherited Sumerian word-signs read with Akkadian phonetic values) or for syllables (CV, VC, CVC). A literate Akkadian scribe controlled roughly 600 active signs out of an inherited inventory of several thousand. The script's polyvalence + its preservation of Sumerian logograms made Akkadian-cuneiform reading a serious scholarly discipline — and the institutional training of scribes preserved Sumerian as a learned language inside Akkadian-speaking Mesopotamia for over two millennia after Sumerian's vernacular extinction.

## The Bronze-Age lingua franca

In the Late Bronze Age (c. -1500 to -1200) Akkadian served as the diplomatic *lingua franca* of the entire Near East, used between Egyptian, Hittite, Mitanni, Babylonian, Assyrian, Cypriot, and Levantine courts. The Amarna Letters (c. -1360 to -1330) — Egyptian-Pharaonic diplomatic correspondence with vassals + foreign powers — are written in regional varieties of Akkadian, with substrate features showing the writers' native Canaanite or Hurrian languages. No other ancient language served as such a broad-based intercultural diplomatic medium.

## The literary + cosmological canon

Akkadian preserves a literary canon of remarkable scope:

- **The Epic of Gilgamesh** — composed in Old Babylonian (c. -1800) and revised into the canonical 12-tablet Standard Babylonian recension (c. -1200 to -1000). Tablet XI's flood narrative is the proximate Mesopotamian source for the Genesis flood story.
- **Enuma Elish** — the Babylonian creation epic, c. -1100 in its final form. Theogonic + cosmogonic; the Marduk-Tiamat combat narrative is one of the load-bearing Near Eastern chaoskampf instances.
- **Atrahasis** — the Old Babylonian flood-and-creation poem (c. -1700), narratively a more complete cosmogony than the surviving Enuma Elish.
- **Descent of Ishtar** — the goddess's underworld descent + return, with strong parallels to the Sumerian Inanna's Descent.
- **Erra and Ishum**, **Ludlul bēl nēmeqi** (the "Babylonian Job"), **Counsels of Wisdom** — additional poetic + theodicy traditions.
- **The omen + astronomical + medical + lexical series** — the scholarly canon of *Enuma Anu Enlil* (astronomy + celestial omens, ~70 tablets), *Šumma izbu* (birth omens), the *MUL.APIN* astronomical compendium, *Diagnostic Handbook* of medicine.
