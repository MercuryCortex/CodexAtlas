# Sumerian

The earliest substantially written language in human history — spoken in southern Mesopotamia from at least the late 4th millennium BCE, the inventor of the cuneiform writing system, the language of the world's first cities (Uruk, Eridu, Ur, Nippur, Lagash), and a language isolate with no demonstrable genetic relationship to any other language family. After its vernacular extinction around -2000 it survived as a learned + liturgical language inside Akkadian-speaking Mesopotamia for roughly another two millennia.

## What Sumerian is

Sumerian is a *language isolate*. Despite over a century of attempts to relate it to almost every conceivable language family — Dravidian, Munda, Uralic, Caucasian, Sino-Tibetan, Bantu, Basque, even Hungarian — no proposal has gained acceptance. It is typologically distinctive: agglutinative + ergative-absolutive in case-marking, with a complex finite-verb system involving chains of prefixes that mark TAM, mood, dimensional case (the so-called "split ergative + prefix chain" structure that has no close analogue in the known languages of Eurasia).

Two principal registers are attested:

- **Eme-ĝir** (literally "native tongue") — the standard literary + administrative dialect.
- **Eme-sal** (literally "fine tongue") — a sociolectal variety associated with women's speech and with the goddess-laments + temple-liturgy of female priestesses (the *gala* / *kalû* priest's repertoire was sung in Eme-sal).

## The script — world's first writing

Sumerian-speaking Uruk (the city of Gilgamesh, in the late-4th-millennium archaeological layers Uruk-IV + Uruk-III, c. -3300 to -3000) is the documented birthplace of writing — the proto-cuneiform tablet-archives of administrative accounting that gradually evolve into the full logo-syllabic [[alphabet-cuneiform-sacred|cuneiform script]]. Every other cuneiform-using language in history (Akkadian, Eblaite, Hurrian, Hittite, Urartian, Elamite, Old Persian, Ugaritic) inherits the script from Sumerian — directly or via Akkadian intermediation. Sumerian-cuneiform is one of the two principal independent inventions of writing (along with Chinese oracle-bone script; Egyptian and Mesoamerican are the other contested-independent candidates).

## The death of vernacular Sumerian + the rise of "learned Sumerian"

Vernacular Sumerian becomes extinct by approximately -1900 — but Sumerian *as a language* does not die. Instead it transitions into what Piotr Michalowski calls the "learned phase" — a classical liturgical + scholarly language preserved by the Akkadian-speaking scribal tradition. The Sumerian-Akkadian Mesopotamian scribal curriculum (the *eduba'a*, "tablet-house") taught Sumerian for the next two millennia through:

- **Bilingual lexical lists** — the *Urra=hubullu* thematic encyclopedia (24 tablets, ~10,000 entries), the *Erimhuš*, *Antagal*, *Diri* series.
- **Bilingual literary texts** — Sumerian original with Akkadian interlinear translation.
- **Bilingual royal inscriptions** — many Akkadian kings (including the last Babylonian Nabonidus, 6th c. BCE) composed inscriptions in Sumerian-Akkadian bilingual format.
- **Liturgical Sumerian** — the great hymns to Enlil, Inanna, Nanna remained sung in Sumerian by trained temple personnel until the cuneiform tradition itself died c. 75 CE.

The structural parallel is medieval Latin: a classical language preserved by educated specialists in liturgy + scholarship long after vernacular extinction. But Sumerian's afterlife was twice as long as Latin's so far.

## The cosmological + literary canon

Sumerian preserves one of the deepest cosmogonic + literary traditions of antiquity:

- **The Sumerian creation myths** — Enki + Ninhursag, Enki + Ninmah, Enki + the World Order; the universe-organizing role of Enki/Ea.
- **Inanna's Descent to the Underworld** — the foundational dying-and-rising-goddess narrative (foreground for Ishtar's Descent + the Demeter-Persephone parallel + downstream parallels to Greek + Levantine cycles).
- **The Sumerian Gilgamesh tales** — five Sumerian poems about Bilgames (Sumerian form of Gilgamesh) that are the direct narrative ancestors of the Akkadian epic.
- **The Sumerian King List** — the antediluvian-and-postdiluvian dynastic catalogue; the antediluvian rulers reigning tens of thousands of years are the proximate ancestor of the long-lived antediluvian patriarchs of Genesis 5.
- **The Hymns of Enheduanna** — Sargon of Akkad's daughter, high priestess of Nanna at Ur, the world's earliest named author (-2300), composing massive hymns to Inanna in Sumerian.
- **The City Laments** — the Lamentation over the Destruction of Ur, the Lamentation over Sumer and Ur, the Lamentation over Nippur — the foundational ancient Near Eastern lament-for-fallen-city genre, ancestor of the Hebrew book of Lamentations.
