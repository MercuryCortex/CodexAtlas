# Biblical Hebrew

The Northwest Semitic Canaanite language of the Hebrew Bible — the linguistic medium of the Torah, the Prophets, and the Writings, of the documentary-hypothesis sources (J, E, D, P), and of the Iron-Age + Persian-period Israelite + Judahite religious + literary culture. One of the most intensively studied languages in human history because its corpus is the founding scriptural canon of three world religions.

## What Biblical Hebrew is

Biblical Hebrew belongs to the Canaanite sub-branch of Northwest Semitic, alongside Phoenician, Moabite, Edomite, and Ammonite. The diagnostic Canaanite isogloss is the **Canaanite Shift**: Proto-Northwest-Semitic long-*ā* shifts to long-*ō*, so PNWS *šalāmu* "peace" → Hebrew *šālōm* / Phoenician *šālōm*, while Aramaic preserves *šalām*. In the early monarchic period (c. -1000 to -800), Biblical Hebrew + Phoenician + Moabite are mutually intelligible regional varieties of essentially one language — the Mesha Stele (Moabite, -840) reads almost like a Hebrew text in another script.

Internal diachrony divides Biblical Hebrew into three layers:

- **Archaic Biblical Hebrew (ABH)** — fragments in Genesis 49 (Jacob's blessing), Exodus 15 (Song of the Sea), Judges 5 (Song of Deborah), Deuteronomy 32-33 (Song of Moses). Preserves older morphological + lexical features (yqtl preterite, archaic suffixes). Dated c. -1200 to -1000.
- **Standard Biblical Hebrew (SBH)** — the prose register of the Pentateuchal narrative sources, Joshua-Kings, the early prophets. The monarchic-period literary norm, c. -1000 to -500.
- **Late Biblical Hebrew (LBH)** — Chronicles, Ezra-Nehemiah, Esther, Ecclesiastes, Daniel (Hebrew portions). Post-Exilic, heavily Aramaicized in vocabulary + syntax. C. -500 to -200.

## The script + the Masoretic vocalization

Biblical Hebrew was written first in the Paleo-Hebrew script (a regional variant of the proto-Canaanite alphabet, used c. -1000 to -300, surviving longer in priestly + numismatic contexts). After the Babylonian Exile (-586) Jews adopted the Aramaic Square Script — what becomes the standard [[alphabet-hebrew-aleph-bet|Hebrew alphabet]]. The square script was a *consonantal* writing system; the vocalization of the Biblical-Hebrew text was preserved orally by the scribal tradition for roughly a millennium, then committed to writing by the [[alphabet-masoretes|Tiberian Masoretes]] (c. 700-1000 CE) in the elaborate pointing system that yields the modern printed Masoretic Text.

## The cross-tradition role

Biblical Hebrew's load-bearing cross-tradition function is as the *source language* of the Hebrew Bible — and hence of every translation, citation, commentary, and theological elaboration in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam.

The [[phase-3-006-septuagint|Septuagint]] carries the Hebrew Bible into [[koine-greek|Koine Greek]] (c. -250 to -150) — and thereby into Christianity. The [[phase-4-108-peshitta|Peshitta]] carries it into [[language-syriac|Syriac]] (2nd-3rd c. CE) — and thereby into Eastern + Indian + Chinese Christianity. The Vulgate of [[jerome]] carries it into [[language-latin-ecclesiastical|Late/Ecclesiastical Latin]] (late 4th c. CE) — and thereby into Latin Christendom. Aramaic Targums (Onkelos, Jonathan, Pseudo-Jonathan) carry it into [[language-aramaic-imperial|Aramaic]] for the Jewish synagogue audience. The Qur'an, while in Classical Arabic and explicitly not a translation, presupposes and re-narrates substantial Hebrew-Bible content (Adam, Noah, Abraham, Joseph, Moses, David, Solomon, Jonah). Through the Septuagint → Vulgate → vernacular Bible translations, Hebrew-Bible vocabulary (*amen*, *hallelujah*, *shibboleth*, *cherub*, *seraph*, *messiah*, *jubilee*, *sabbath*) enters every European + Christianized language on Earth.
