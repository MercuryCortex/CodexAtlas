# Old Chinese (the oracle-bone and bronze-inscription stage)

The earliest attested stage of Chinese — the language of the Shang oracle-bone divination archive (c. -1250), of the Western Zhou bronze inscriptions, and of the oldest received literature (the [[phase-2-032-shijing-book-of-songs|Shijing]] odes and the [[phase-1-026-yijing|Zhouyi]] core). Distinct from [[language-classical-chinese]], which is the later *literary fixation* of this language: Old Chinese is the stage at which Chinese was still a spoken vernacular being written down, and the stage at which Chinese writing was born — inside a divination cult.

## Writing born in divination

The first Chinese writing is not literature, law, or accounting: it is ~150,000 fragments of inscribed turtle plastron and ox scapula from the Shang royal cult center at Anyang ([[place-china-shang]]), recording divinations addressed through royal ancestors to the high god Di / [[shangdi|Shangdi]] — about harvest, war, childbirth, sacrifice, dreams ([[scapulimancy-oracle-bones]]). The diviner cracked the bone with heat and read the fissures; the inscription records the charge, sometimes the king's reading, occasionally the verification. Chinese script and Chinese state religion are, at their origin, the same institution (Keightley 1978). The script node [[alphabet-chinese-oracle-bones]] carries the writing-system story; this node carries the language.

The Western Zhou continuation moves the same language into cast bronze: ritual vessels inscribed for ancestral-temple use, including the proclamations that articulate the **Mandate of Heaven** (*tiānmìng*) — the theological doctrine legitimating the Zhou conquest that became the permanent grammar of Chinese political theology.

## The rhymes that unlocked the reconstruction

The Shijing's temple hymns and odes rhymed when composed (c. -1000 to -600); by the medieval period many rhymes had broken. That breakage — systematically analyzed from the Qing philologists to Baxter & Sagart (2014) — is the primary evidence for reconstructing Old Chinese sound. The sacred corpus is thus also the linguistic fossil record: as with Vedic Sanskrit and the Gathas, the liturgical text's conservatism preserves the language-state of its composition.

## Cross-tradition findings

- **Divination archive as proto-scripture.** The oracle-bone corpus is the Chinese member of the cross-tradition pattern in which a divinatory record-keeping practice precedes and seeds scripture: the Zhouyi ([[phase-1-026-yijing]]) literally canonizes a divination manual, which the Confucian tradition then surrounds with cosmological commentary. Compare Mesopotamian omen-compendia growing into canonical series — parallel institutional shape, no contact.
- **Sino-Tibetan kinship.** Old Chinese and the ancestor of [[language-classical-tibetan|Classical Tibetan]] descend from Proto-Sino-Tibetan (Baxter & Sagart 2014) — the genetic bridge between the two great scriptural languages of East and Inner Asia, millennia before Buddhist translation connected their literatures.
- **Temple-complex origin of writing.** With [[language-sumerian|Sumerian]], Old Chinese is the cleanest case of writing emerging inside a temple-administrative-divinatory institution rather than for trade or narrative — a structural parallel across the two independent script-inventions at opposite ends of Eurasia.
- **Di / Shangdi's afterlife.** The oracle-bone high god [[shangdi|Shangdi]] becomes the classical texts' supreme deity and — two and a half millennia later — the term chosen by Jesuit and Protestant translators for the biblical God in Chinese, making the Shang diviners' addressee the name of God in millions of Chinese Bibles.
