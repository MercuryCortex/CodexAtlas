# Ge'ez (Classical Ethiopic)

The South-Semitic language of the [[aksum|Aksumite kingdom]] (1st-7th c. CE) and the literary + liturgical language of Ethiopian + Eritrean Christianity for the next ~1700 years. Ge'ez ceased to be a vernacular by c. 1000-1300 CE — superseded in everyday speech by Tigrinya + Amharic + the other Ethiopian Semitic descendants — but was preserved by the [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo|Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church]] as its liturgical + scholarly language continuously to the present.

## Why this lens matters for the Atlas

Ge'ez is the principal language of one of the world's most distinctive religious-literary inheritances:

1. **The Ge'ez biblical canon** ([[phase-4-082-ethiopic-biblical-canon]]) is the *broadest* Christian canonical scripture in the world — 81 books including texts (1 Enoch, Jubilees) that no other Christian church preserves canonically.
2. **The Ge'ez 1 Enoch** ([[phase-4-081-mashafa-henok-geez-1-enoch]]) is the *only complete witness* to the Book of Enoch. The Aramaic original is fragmentary (Dead Sea Scrolls); the Greek translation is fragmentary; the Ge'ez translation (made from the Greek in the Aksumite period, c. 4th-6th c. CE) is the only place we have the complete text. Without Ethiopian Christian liturgical preservation, much of 1 Enoch would be lost.
3. **The Kebra Nagast** ("Glory of the Kings") is the Ethiopian national epic that frames the Solomonic dynasty + the Aksumite-Israelite continuity narrative.

## Position in the comparative-liturgical-language frame

Ge'ez sits with [[language-old-avestan|Old Avestan]] + [[language-latin-classical|Classical Latin]] + [[language-sanskrit-classical|Classical Sanskrit]] + Church Slavonic + [[classical-arabic|Classical Arabic]] + Mishnaic + Biblical Hebrew as one of the world's *liturgical-language* cases — natural languages that became fixed by religious-institutional reverence beyond the vernacular extinction of their underlying registers + were preserved by trained priest-scholar communities across many centuries. The Ge'ez case is distinguished by:

- The very long oral-only stratum in the Aksumite + early Ethiopian period
- The breadth of the preserved literary canon (biblical + extra-biblical apocalyptic + national-epic + legal)
- The continuous unbroken transmission from the Aksumite period to the present
