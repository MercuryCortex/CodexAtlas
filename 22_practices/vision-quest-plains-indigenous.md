# Vision Quest — Plains Indigenous Solo Initiatory Retreat

The Plains-Indigenous practice of a **solo retreat (typically 1-4 days) to a remote sacred location for fasting + waterless abstention + prayer + waiting for a transformative vision** that conveys identity, guidance, song, name, or medicine. The canonical ethnographic case is the **Lakota Hanblecheya** ("Crying for a Vision"), documented by Black Elk in his teaching to John Neihardt (1932) + Joseph Epes Brown (1953) and given its scholarly-critical treatment in DeMallie 1984 *The Sixth Grandfather*. Structurally cognate practices are found across most North American Indigenous nations under various names: Crow *akhpitiisshe-iiwe*, Anishinaabe *giigwishimowin*, Cheyenne *vehoe-isi*, Blackfoot *nato-osi*.

## The Lakota Hanblecheya

The canonical Sioux form proceeds:

1. **Preparation** — the seeker approaches an elder/medicine person and requests guidance. The medicine person evaluates readiness, prescribes the location + duration, and convenes the preparatory rites.
2. **Sweat-lodge purification** — the seeker enters the [[sweat-lodge-inipi-lakota|inipi]] sweat-lodge for several rounds of intense steam + prayer before going out to the quest site.
3. **The fast** — the seeker is taken to the remote butte/hilltop, sealed within a circular sacred space marked with *čhaŋlí wapȟaȟta* (prayer-ties: small tobacco-pouches tied to four sticks at the cardinal directions). The seeker remains within the circle for the prescribed duration — typically 4 days + 4 nights — without food or water, holding the sacred pipe + sage, praying continuously + lamenting (the literal meaning of *hanblecheya* is "crying for a vision").
4. **The vision** — if the vision comes, it typically takes the form of an encounter with an animal-spirit, a thunderbird, the *wakȟáŋ* (mystery-powers) of the four directions, or one of the Seven Grandfathers. The vision may convey a name, a song, a medicine-bundle pattern, or a vocational identity.
5. **Return + interpretation** — the seeker returns to the sweat-lodge for a closing inipi + recounts the vision to the medicine person, who blesses + interprets + frames it within the tradition's repertoire of shared visionary patterns.

## Black Elk's Hanblecheya at age 9

The canonical recorded vision is **Black Elk's Great Vision at age 9** (1872), recounted by Black Elk to Neihardt in 1931 + to Brown in 1947-1948. In the vision, Black Elk was carried by horses representing the four directions into the cloud-tipi of the Six Grandfathers (the spirits of the four cardinal directions + Earth + Sky); each Grandfather gave him an object + a teaching; he was shown the sacred tree at the center of the world; he was shown the destruction + flowering of his people across time. The vision-content has become a canonical reference text for Plains-Indigenous + comparative-religion scholarship. The DeMallie 1984 critical edition of the original Neihardt transcripts disentangles Black Elk's narration from Neihardt's literary additions in the popularized 1932 *Black Elk Speaks*.

## Inheritance + transformation

The vision-quest is one of the **Seven Sacred Rites** transmitted to the Oglala Lakota by the White Buffalo Calf Woman (in the foundational origin-narrative of Lakota religion). The seven rites:

1. *Inipi* (sweat-lodge)
2. *Hanblecheya* (vision quest)
3. *Wiwáŋyaŋg Wachípi* (sun dance)
4. *Hunkápi* (making-relatives ceremony)
5. *Iśnáthi Awíchalowaŋpi* (girls' coming-of-age)
6. *Tȟápa Waŋkáyeyapi* (throwing of the ball)
7. *Naǧí Glúhapi* (keeping of the soul)

The vision-quest is preceded + followed by the inipi (#1) and is part of the broader ritual ecology of Lakota religion.

## MASSIVE-WIN — cross-tradition shamanic-initiation cluster

Vision-quest is the canonical North-American-Plains member of the **global shamanic-initiation cluster** (Eliade 1964):

- **Siberian shamanic recruitment** via initiatory-illness — the apprentice is "called" by the spirits through a near-death illness; recovery is the confirmation of shamanic vocation
- **Korean *sinbyeong*** — divine-illness preceding shamanic vocation; similar pattern to Siberian
- **Mongolian *udgan*** and ***böö*** recruitment — similar dream/illness-based call
- **Amazonian apprentice-shaman initiations** — multi-year apprenticeship with a master often involving plant-medicine (ayahuasca, yopo) ordeals
- **Australian Aboriginal initiation** ordeals — the Wirrarika or Bilbiringa quest sequences
- **African divinatory-priest initiation** — Yoruba *babalawo* initiation, Sangoma *ukuthwasa* initiatory-illness
- **Vision-quest** — the Plains-Indigenous solo-fast initiation

The structural pattern across all members: **solo or supervised threshold-experience → animal-spirit or transcendent encounter → identity-transformation → vocational-confirmation**. The Plains form is distinctive in its (a) explicitly solo character, (b) waterless fasting as the threshold mechanism, (c) youth-initiatory and adult-transitional dual function, and (d) integration into the broader Seven Sacred Rites ceremonial complex.

## Modern reception + appropriation concerns

The Lakota vision-quest has been one of the most-appropriated Indigenous practices in the 20th-21st-c. New Age + neopagan + experiential-retreat industries. Plastic-shaman appropriations + paid commercial vision-quests led to the 1993 *Declaration of War Against Exploiters of Lakota Spirituality* signed by Lakota elders (the resolution of the Wopila Tiospaye gathering). The contemporary Lakota community asserts that the Hanblecheya is a tradition-specific rite that requires authentic transmission through Lakota medicine-elders + tribal participation; commercial appropriations are explicitly rejected.
