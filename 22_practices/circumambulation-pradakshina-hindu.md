# Pradakṣiṇā — Hindu Clockwise Circumambulation

The Hindu practice of **clockwise (right-side-keeping) circumambulation** of a sacred object — deity image, temple, sacred fire, sacred tree, sacred mountain, or sacred person. The Sanskrit *pradakṣiṇā* (प्रदक्षिणा) is built from *pra-* ("forward") + *dakṣiṇa* ("right side, southern"); the term itself names the cardinal feature: the sacred is kept on the practitioner's **right side** throughout the circuit. The direction encodes the apparent daily westward path of the sun (east → south → west → north → east) as cosmological macrocosm; the practitioner walking pradakṣiṇā around a sacred center re-enacts the solar circuit at micro-scale.

## Vedic kernel — agni-pradakṣiṇā

The original Vedic form is **agni-pradakṣiṇā**: circling the sacred fire (*agni*) clockwise. The Ṛgveda + Yajurveda + Brāhmaṇa literature codify it as the central rubric of Vedic sacrifice — the priest moves around the fire keeping it on his right, the hotṛ priest stations himself at prescribed cardinal positions, the rite unfolds as a cosmological enactment. The most-preserved Vedic agni-pradakṣiṇā in contemporary practice is the **saptapadī** (seven-step) marriage-rite: bride + groom circle the marriage fire seven times, each circuit invoking one of the seven oaths of householder life. The fire is the witness (*sākṣin*); the seven circuits make the marriage ritually valid.

From this Vedic kernel the practice radiated outward across the entire Hindu sacred-object field as Hindu religion developed temple-cult + image-worship after the post-Vedic Bhakti turn.

## Architectural inscription — the pradakṣiṇā-patha

Hindu temple architecture inscribes the practice into the building. The **pradakṣiṇā-patha** (circumambulation-path) is the corridor around the inner-sanctum (*garbha-gṛha*, "womb-chamber") containing the central deity-image. The Āgama + Tantra temple-construction texts (Mayamata, Mānasāra) prescribe its dimensions + the placement of subsidiary shrines around it. The pilgrim entering a temple first does darśana (seeing the deity) through the open garbha-gṛha doorway, then performs pradakṣiṇā around the sanctum — typically 1, 3, 7, 21, or 108 circuits depending on the deity + the occasion. The bodily practice and the architecture mutually presuppose each other.

## Sacred-mountain scaling — giri-pradakṣiṇā

The pradakṣiṇā principle scales upward from image → temple → mountain. The major Hindu mountain-circumambulations (*giri-pradakṣiṇā* or *parikrama*):

- **Arunachala** (Tiruvannamalai, Tamil Nadu) — 14-km circuit around the mountain sacred to Śiva-as-fire (*Aruṇa* = "red dawn-fire"). Performed each full-moon by tens of thousands of pilgrims; canonized in modern times by Ramana Maharshi (1879-1950) as a walking-meditation contemplative practice.
- **Govardhan** (Mathura region) — 21-km parikrama of the hill that Kṛṣṇa lifted to shelter the cowherds from Indra's storm. Central to Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava pilgrimage; many devotees perform it as daṇḍavat-parikrama (body-prostration), taking weeks.
- **Mount Kailash** (Tibet) — the cross-tradition sacred mountain ([mount-kailash-place]). For Hindus, the abode of Śiva + Pārvatī (Liṅga Purāṇa, Skanda Purāṇa). The 52-km kora is shared with Buddhists + Jains + Bonpos — the densest cross-tradition sacred-mountain convergence site in Asia.
- **Narmada-parikrama** — the river-circumambulation: a 3-year-3-month-3-day walking circuit of the entire Narmada River, ~2,600 km on each bank. The most extreme parikrama-discipline still living.

## MASSIVE-WIN — the Hindu-Buddhist transmission chain

Pradakṣiṇā is the **ancestor** of one of the cleanest cross-tradition direct-borrowing chains in comparative religion:

- **Hindu pradakṣiṇā** (Vedic origin, -1500 onward) — the parent
- **Buddhist pradakṣiṇa** (3rd c. BCE onward, Aśokan stūpa-cult) — inherited wholesale into Indian Buddhism; the stūpa is built with circumambulation-platform as architectural fact
- **Tibetan [[kora-tibetan-circumambulation|kora]]** (7th-8th c. CE onward) — direct Indian-Buddhist inheritance northward via Vajrayāna transmission; the Tibetan *skor ba* is a semantic calque of *pradakṣiṇā*
- **East Asian Buddhist circumambulation** — preserved at Borobudur, the great Theravāda + Mahāyāna stūpa-cults, Chinese pagoda circumambulation, Japanese henro Shikoku-pilgrimage circuit

Independent of this transmission chain, the **Islamic [tawaf-kaaba-circumambulation|ṭawāf]** at the Kaaba is the other ancient living right-side-keeping circumambulation tradition — same direction, same somatic discipline, no proven historical contact (Eck 1981; Peters 1994 *The Hajj*). The structural convergence of two independently-developed traditions on identical body-mechanics speaks to the macrocosmic-solar logic of the right-side-keeping rule.

## Modern reception

The Eck 1981 *Darśan* analysis — pradakṣiṇā as the bodily complement of darśana (the deity-seeing) — has shaped the modern academic understanding of Hindu visual + somatic piety. The Arunachala full-moon parikrama draws international meditators following Ramana Maharshi's teaching; the Narmada-parikrama is documented in Amritlal Vegad's modern travelogue + several scholarly studies. Pradakṣiṇā remains a daily devotional practice for hundreds of millions of contemporary Hindus.
