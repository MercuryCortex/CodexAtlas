# Qìgōng — Energy Cultivation

A Chinese family of body-cultivation practices coordinating movement, breath, and attention for the cultivation, circulation, and refinement of *qì* (氣). The classical pre-modern roots are **dǎo yǐn** (導引, guided stretching/movement) and **tǔ nà** (吐納, breath-discipline), attested in the Mawangdui silk manuscripts (~168 BCE). The term *qìgōng* itself is a **1950s PRC-state coinage** (Liu Guizhen, 1953) consolidating these earlier traditions plus Buddhist + Daoist meditation breath-practices under one secularized medical-and-health-cultivation umbrella. The decisive scholarly history is David Palmer's *Qigong Fever* (2007).

## How it works

Qigong is an umbrella term spanning a wide range of practice forms:

- **Standing post (zhàn zhuāng)** — held standing postures (typically the *wú jí* posture, the Three-Circle posture, the Embracing-the-Tree posture) for extended periods (5-60 minutes), cultivating root, structural integrity, and qi-sensation
- **Moving forms** — the canonical short-sequence sets:
  - *Bā Duàn Jǐn* / Eight Pieces of Brocade — 8-movement health-cultivation set, attributed to Yue Fei (12th c.), in widespread modern public-park practice
  - *Wǔ Qín Xì* / Five Animal Frolics — bear, tiger, deer, monkey, crane animal-imitation movements, attributed to Hua Tuo (~3rd c.)
  - *Liù Zì Jué* / Six Healing Sounds — six breath-and-vocalization combinations correlated with the Five Phases
- **Seated breathing meditations** — coordinated breath + visualization + microcosmic-orbit (*xiǎo zhōu tiān / 小周天*) qi-circulation along the governing (du) and conception (ren) vessels
- **Medical qigong** — therapeutic exercises prescribed within TCM for specific conditions; significant clinical research literature on hypertension, cancer-supportive care, fibromyalgia, etc.
- **Nèi dān** (Internal Alchemy) — the esoteric Daoist root tradition cultivating the "inner elixir" through three-treasures (*jīng-qì-shén*: essence-energy-spirit) refinement; the underlying religious-mystical framework that 1950s PRC-era qigong secularizes

## Historical layers

Qigong as a unified concept is a modern 20th-c. construction. The historical layers it consolidates:

1. **Pre-Han / Han daoyin + tu na** (~3rd c. BCE - 3rd c. CE) — the Mawangdui *Dǎoyǐn tú* (~168 BCE) is the earliest illustrated record: 44 figures performing stretching/movement postures, some named ("Bear Hang", "Bird Stretching"). The *Zhuāngzǐ* "Knowledge Wanders North" chapter contains a famous reference to *dǎoyǐn* practitioners as "bear-hangs and bird-stretches"
2. **Daoist longevity tradition** (Han onward) — Ge Hong's *Bàopǔzǐ* (~320 CE) systematizes Daoist longevity-cultivation. The Tang-Song nèidān tradition (internal alchemy) develops the esoteric framework
3. **Buddhist + Daoist medieval transmission** — Chan / Tiantai Buddhist breathing meditations; Quanzhen Daoist neidan
4. **Late-imperial popular religious sects** — many qigong-precursor practices circulated in syncretic White Lotus / Yiguandao / heterodox-sectarian milieus
5. **Republican-era reframing (1912-1949)** — body-cultivation practices recoded for national-strengthening + modern medicine
6. **PRC institutionalization (1949-1979)** — Liu Guizhen's *Qigong Therapy* (1957); Beidaihe Qigong Sanatorium; secularized state-medical framework
7. **Qigong Fever (1979-1999)** — explosive 1980s-90s grassroots qigong-master movement; tens of millions of practitioners; the Falun Gong / Li Hongzhi crisis of 1999; massive state suppression
8. **Post-1999 reorientation** — state-sanctioned "health qigong" (健身氣功 / Jiànshēn Qìgōng) of standardized non-esoteric forms; banned or marginalized esoteric / heterodox forms

The 20th-c. trajectory documented in Palmer 2007 is a case study in the modern state-religion-medicine-body politics of contemplative practice.

## Cross-tradition position

Qigong sits at the intersection of Chinese medicine, Daoist religion, and Chinese martial arts. The cross-tradition convergences:

- **[[tai-chi-quan-internal-martial|Tài Jí Quán]]** — qigong's sibling internal-arts practice; same qi-cultivation framework, more martial / formal-sequence focus
- **[[yoga-asana-postural|Yoga āsana]] + [[pranayama-yogic-breath|prāṇāyāma]]** — Indic embodied-discipline cluster; structurally + theoretically cognate (nāḍī / prāṇa parallels meridians / qì). Joseph Needham's *Science and Civilisation in China* Vol 5 part V documents the Hindu-Chinese body-discipline connections; the historical question of whether there was direct transmission (Buddhist intermediaries) remains contested but the structural-typological cognation is well-established
- **Tibetan Buddhist *lu jong / trul khor*** — yogic-body movements in the Tibetan Vajrayāna tradition; Indic origin transmitted through Tibet
- **Modern Western somatic-movement traditions** — Feldenkrais, Alexander Technique, Body-Mind Centering — secularized cross-cultural inheritors

## MASSIVE-WIN — terminological-construction case study

Qigong is one of the cleanest documented cases of a **modern terminologically-unified construction of a religious-practice tradition**. The word "qìgōng" did not exist as a unified category before 1953. The umbrella terminology was deliberately created by Liu Guizhen and the PRC health-state to consolidate multiple pre-existing traditions (dǎoyǐn, tǔnà, nèidān, Buddhist + Daoist meditation breath-practices, popular sectarian body-cultivation) under one secularized medical framework that could be institutionalized within socialist-state health policy. This is structurally parallel to:

- The 20th-c. construction of "Hinduism" as a unified religion-category (Brian Pennington's *Was Hinduism Invented?* 2005)
- The 20th-c. construction of "modern postural yoga" (Singleton 2010; see [[yoga-asana-postural]])
- The 20th-c. construction of "Buddhist mindfulness / MBSR" (Jeff Wilson, *Mindful America*, 2014; see [[vipassana-insight-meditation]])

Modernity does not just transmit pre-modern religious practices; it actively constructs the categories under which they become globally legible. Qigong is the cleanest Chinese case of this pattern.
