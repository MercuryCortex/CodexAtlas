# Prāṇāyāma — Yogic Breath Discipline

The principal breath-discipline practice of Hindu yogic tradition: controlled regulation of breath via specific patterns of inhalation (*pūraka*), retention (*kumbhaka*), exhalation (*recaka*), and after-exhalation pause. Patañjali (~2nd c. BCE - 4th c. CE) systematizes pranayama as the 4th of the 8 limbs (*ashtanga*) of yoga in *Yoga Sutras* 2.49-53; later Hatha Yoga tradition (Svatmarama, ~15th c. *Hatha Yoga Pradipika*; Gheranda, ~17th c. *Gheranda Samhita*) elaborates the technique-catalogue. One of the principal cross-tradition contemplative-physical practices.

## How it works

The practitioner sits in stable meditation posture (typically lotus / half-lotus / kneeling-seiza / chair-sitting with erect spine) and performs one of several specific breath-patterns:

- **Anuloma viloma / nadi shodhana** — alternate-nostril breathing (left-inhale + right-exhale + right-inhale + left-exhale); for nadi (subtle-channel) purification
- **Bhastrika** — bellows breath (rapid forceful inhale-exhale); for prana-activation
- **Kapalabhati** — skull-shining (forceful exhale + passive inhale); for purification + activation
- **Ujjayi** — victorious breath (constricted-glottis audible breathing); used in modern Ashtanga yoga as accompanying-breath throughout asana practice
- **Bhramari** — humming-bee breath; calming + nervous-system-regulating
- **Sitali / Sitkari** — cooling breath through rolled-tongue or teeth
- **Surya / Chandra bhedana** — single-nostril breathing for sun-activation / moon-activation
- **Kumbhaka** — breath retention (after-inhale OR after-exhale); the most-advanced practice; the Patanjali Yoga Sutras' principal pranayama specification

The transformative claim: controlled breath regulates *prana* (the subtle life-energy), purifies the *nadis* (subtle channels), prepares the mind for concentration (*dharana*) and meditation (*dhyana*) and samadhi (the 6th-7th-8th limbs of ashtanga yoga). The practice is preparatory to deeper contemplation + simultaneously transformative in its own right.

## Patanjali's framework

*Yoga Sutras* 2.49-53 establishes pranayama as the 4th limb of ashtanga (eight-limb) yoga:

> *2.49: "tasmin sati svāsa-prasvāsayor gati-vicchedaḥ prāṇāyāmaḥ"* — "When [stable seat = asana] is attained, *pranayama* is the cessation of the movement of inhalation and exhalation"

The Patanjali specification is relatively simple — primarily about *kumbhaka* (breath-retention) and the cessation of normal breath-flow. The elaborate technique-catalogue of named breath-patterns (anuloma viloma, kapalabhati, etc.) emerges in the Hatha Yoga tradition ~1,500 years later. The Patanjali pranayama is a contemplative-preparatory breath-discipline; Hatha Yoga pranayama is an elaborated technical-physiological tradition.

## MASSIVE-WIN — cross-tradition breath-discipline convergence

Pranayama is the most-elaborated cross-tradition breath-discipline tradition, but cross-tradition cognates exist:

- **Buddhist anapanasati** ("mindfulness of breath") — Theravada [[vipassana-insight-meditation]] uses breath as primary meditation object; structurally distinct (anapanasati = OBSERVATION of natural breath; pranayama = CONTROL of breath)
- **Daoist qigong + neidan breathing** — Chinese internal-alchemy breath-practices; structurally + theoretically cognate (qi-circulation parallels prana-circulation); Joseph Needham documented the Chinese parallels
- **Sufi breath-coordination** with [[dhikr-sufi-remembrance|dhikr]] — Naqshbandi + Mawlawi orders coordinate breath with divine-name recitation
- **Hesychast breath-coordination** with [[hesychasm-jesus-prayer|Jesus Prayer]] — same breath-coordination structure in Eastern Christian contemplative-recitative practice
- **Modern Western breathwork** (Wim Hof method, holotropic breathwork [Grof], rebirthing) — secular adaptations of breath-discipline; Western 20th-c. interest is partially Hindu-derived (especially via Krishnamacharya lineage)

The cross-tradition pattern: **conscious breath-regulation as a contemplative-physiological discipline that transforms consciousness + bodily state**. The convergence across Hindu + Buddhist + Daoist + Christian + Islamic + modern-secular contexts is one of the strongest cross-tradition contemplative-practice convergences. The Hindu Patañjali + Hatha Yoga tradition is the most-elaborated formal systematization.

## Modern global diffusion

The Krishnamacharya lineage (Iyengar + Pattabhi Jois + Desikachar + others) globalized pranayama (alongside asana) through 20th-c. teaching. B.K.S. Iyengar's *Light on Pranayama* (1981) is the canonical modern systematic exposition. Modern global yoga practice typically includes pranayama as a major component alongside asana, especially in Hatha-derived modern yoga lineages.
