# Zazen — Zen/Chan Seated Meditation

The principal contemplative practice of Chan / Zen Buddhism — extended seated meditation in specific posture, executed in two principal modes: **shikantaza** ("just sitting", open-awareness, Soto-Dōgen tradition) and **koan introspection** (paradoxical-phrase investigation, Rinzai-Hakuin tradition). Traces lineage from the legendary Bodhidharma (~5-6th c.) through the Southern School / Huineng codification (7-8th c.; see [[shenhui]] for the polemical establishment) into Japanese Soto + Rinzai (13th c.) and globally via 20th-c. Western Zen diffusion.

## How it works

The practitioner sits in cross-legged posture (full lotus / half-lotus / Burmese / kneeling-seiza), hands in cosmic-mudra, spine erect, eyes half-open. Then:

**Shikantaza (Soto)**: open awareness without object. The practitioner does not concentrate on the breath, does not investigate a koan, does not analyze sensations — simply sits with awareness alert + relaxed. The practice IS the realization (Dōgen's *practice-enlightenment*, *shushō ittō*): zazen is not preparation for awakening; zazen is the form awakening takes when expressed as seated meditation.

**Koan introspection (Rinzai)**: the practitioner is given a koan (a paradoxical phrase or case from Chan literature — *"What is the sound of one hand clapping?"* / *"What is your original face before your parents were born?"* / Zhaozhou's *"Mu"*) and sustains continuous attention on the koan until intellectual-conceptual problem-solving dissolves into a sudden insight (*kenshō* / *satori*) of the koan's non-conceptual answer. Hakuin's 18th-c. systematic curriculum organized hundreds of koans into a progressive training sequence.

## The Bodhidharma + Huineng lineage

Chan traces its lineage to **Bodhidharma** (~5-6th c.) — the legendary South Indian monk who brought "Buddha's mind-transmission" to China. The famous Bodhidharma narratives include 9-year wall-gazing at Shaolin Monastery + the cutting-off-of-eyelids (to prevent sleep during meditation) + the meeting with Emperor Wu of Liang. Historicity of Bodhidharma is contested; the lineage-narrative function is central to Chan self-understanding regardless.

The historical-doctrinal Chan crystallization happens 7-8th century with the Southern School / Northern School division. [[shenhui]] (670-762)'s polemic established the Southern School / Huineng / sudden-enlightenment framework as orthodox Chan (see the [[shenhui|Shenhui essay-stub]] in 04_persons/ for the polemical-construction history). The *Platform Sutra* (see [[phase-5-004-platform-sutra-huineng]]) codifies the Southern School position.

## The Japanese transmission + global diffusion

Chan crossed into Japan via Eisai (Rinzai, ~1200) + Dōgen (Soto, 1227 return from China). Dōgen's *Shōbōgenzō* (Treasury of the True Dharma Eye) is one of the principal texts of world Buddhist philosophy + the foundational text of shikantaza practice.

The 20th-c. Western diffusion happens via:
- **D.T. Suzuki** (1870-1966) — academic-philosophical Western introduction
- **Shunryu Suzuki** (1904-1971, unrelated to DT) — San Francisco Zen Center founder; *Zen Mind, Beginner's Mind* (1970) is the canonical Western Zen-introductory text
- **Philip Kapleau** (1912-2004) — Rinzai-trained American teacher; *Three Pillars of Zen* (1965)
- Multiple monastic-lineage transmissions established Western Zen centers from the 1960s onward

The Western Zen diffusion is one of the principal cross-cultural religious-practice transmissions of the 20th century, parallel in scope to the [[vipassana-insight-meditation|vipassana MBSR diffusion]] but with different cultural-religious framing (Zen as Japanese-aesthetic + philosophical-influence on Western art / poetry / counterculture vs. vipassana as medical-therapeutic).

## Cross-tradition position

Zazen anchors the Chan/Zen pole of the cross-tradition meditation cluster (see [[vipassana-insight-meditation]] for the Theravada pole). Cross-tradition structural relations:

- **vs Vipassana**: same Buddhist soil but Mahayana doctrinal framing + open-awareness shikantaza method vs Theravada observational-categorical method. The Mahayana / Theravada methodological split is sustained in their respective meditation methods
- **vs Tibetan Vajrayana meditation** (deity yoga, mahamudra, dzogchen): Tibetan adds elaborate visualization + deity-identification practices that Zen explicitly rejects (Zen tradition's iconoclastic stance: *"if you meet the Buddha on the road, kill him"*)
- **vs Christian contemplation** (Centering Prayer, Cistercian contemplation, Eckhart's *Gelassenheit*): structurally cognate with shikantaza's open-awareness emphasis; Merton + Catholic Zen interlocutors (Thomas Merton + Robert Kennedy) have documented the convergence
- **vs Sufi dhikr / hesychast Jesus Prayer**: the recitative cluster contrasts with Zen's silent-shikantaza pole; both are documented contemplative-practice approaches with distinct cognitive modes
