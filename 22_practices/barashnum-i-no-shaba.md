# Barashnum-i No-Shaba (the Nine-Night Ritual Purification)

The **Barashnum** is the **principal major-purification rite of Zoroastrianism** — a **nine-day, nine-pit ritual ablution** prescribed in extensive detail in **Vendidad chapters 8 and 9**. The rite is undertaken by **priests as part of their full ordination** and by **laity after major pollution-events** (corpse-contact, post-childbirth, post-menstruation, conversion-initiation). The practice is one of the most-elaborately-codified single ritual-purification systems in the comparative-religion record.

## The ritual structure

The Barashnum is performed in a **specially-prepared purification-ground** (the *barashnumgāh*): a rectangular area, ritually-cordoned-off, containing **nine pits arranged in a row**. The practitioner moves successively through the pits over nine days, undergoing **graduated ablutions**:

- **Pits 1-3**: ablution with **consecrated cow-urine** (*gōmēz* / nirang) — the strongest purifier in the Zoroastrian purity-system. Cow-urine is the cosmic-asha-aligned bodily-substance that drives out the cosmic-druj that has invaded the polluted-body.
- **Pits 4-6**: ablution with **sand**.
- **Pits 7-9**: ablution with **water**.

Each pit-transition is accompanied by recitation of the **Yatha Ahu Vairyo + Ashem Vohu** prayer-formulas. Between the third and seventh days the practitioner is in **complete isolation** in a separate enclosure.

## Theological grounding

Zoroastrian ritual-pollution is theologically conceived as **the soul's alignment-with cosmic-druj** (the cosmic-Lie principle, [[doctrine-druj-falsehood]]). Pollution is not merely physical — it is **cosmic-ethical mis-alignment**. The Barashnum reverses the alignment + **restores the soul to cosmic-asha** (cosmic-Truth alignment). The cow-urine is not a quaint hygienic measure but a **theologically-charged asha-aligned substance** chosen for its cosmic-druj-expelling efficacy in the Zoroastrian framework.

(Choksy 1989 *Purity and Pollution in Zoroastrianism* is the principal modern academic treatment.)

## Cross-tradition: the comparative ritual-purification corpus

The Barashnum is comparable to (though more elaborated than):

- **Vedic + Hindu *snāna* + Brahmanical-purity-codes** of the Dharma-sutras (the PII-cognate purity-tradition; West 2007 ch. 7).
- **Hebrew Levitical purification system** (Leviticus 11-15, 17-22): water + ash + blood, with specific day-counts for different pollution-categories.
- **Islamic *ghusl* + *wudu*** purifications: water-ablution before prayer.
- **Christian baptism + various Christian post-baptismal purification practices**.

The cross-tradition comparison is treated in Mary Douglas's classic *Purity and Danger* (1966), with the Zoroastrian system as one of the principal case-studies.

## Modern continuity

The full nine-day Barashnum is increasingly rare in the modern period because of its extensive ritual-infrastructure requirements (the barashnumgāh + ordained-priest officiation + the 9-day commitment). However, the rite continues to be performed in both the Iranian (Yazd) + Parsi (Mumbai-Udvada) Zoroastrian communities, particularly for **priestly ordination** + **post-corpse-bearer purification** (the nasa-salars after their work at the dakhma towers of silence).
