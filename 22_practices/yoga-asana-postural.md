# Yoga Āsana — Postural Practice

The physical-postural limb of yoga: held bodily postures (*āsanas*) coordinated with breath and attention. Patañjali (~2nd c. BCE - 4th c. CE) gives āsana its minimal classical definition — *Yoga Sūtras* 2.46: *sthira-sukham āsanam* ("posture is steady and comfortable") — but with no postural catalogue and no elaboration beyond seated meditation-seats. The elaborated postural tradition emerges in **Haṭha Yoga** (~11-15th c.; *Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā*, *Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā*) and is dramatically expanded in the **20th-century Mysore synthesis** under T. Krishnamacharya, whose disciples (Iyengar, Pattabhi Jois, Desikachar, Indra Devi) globalized postural yoga across the second half of the 20th century into one of the most-practiced contemplative-physical disciplines on Earth.

## What Patañjali actually says about āsana

The full Patañjali specification is three sūtras:

- **2.46**: *sthira-sukham āsanam* — "posture is steady and comfortable"
- **2.47**: *prayatna-śaithilya-ananta-samāpattibhyām* — "[posture is mastered] by relaxation of effort and absorption in the infinite"
- **2.48**: *tato dvandvānabhighātaḥ* — "thereupon there is no assault from the pairs-of-opposites [heat/cold, hunger/thirst]"

That is the entire classical-Patañjali doctrine of āsana: a steady seat for meditation, with no postural catalogue. The 200+-pose modern repertoire is NOT in Patañjali. Mark Singleton's *Yoga Body* (2010) is the decisive scholarly demonstration that the modern postural tradition is a 20th-century construction synthesizing premodern haṭha postures, European physical-culture exercise systems (Swedish Ling gymnastics, Eugen Sandow bodybuilding, British military calisthenics imported to colonial Indian schools), and Krishnamacharya's creative Mysore-Palace synthesis.

## Haṭha Yoga elaboration

The first significant postural catalogue appears in the Haṭha Yoga corpus (~11-15th c.):

- *Haṭha Yoga Pradīpikā* (Svātmārāma, ~15th c.) — 15 named āsanas, primarily seated/reclining
- *Gheraṇḍa Saṃhitā* (~17th c.) — 32 named āsanas
- *Śiva Saṃhitā* (~15-17th c.) — 84 āsanas mentioned (4 described)

Haṭha postures are predominantly seated/reclining/inverted meditation-supporting postures plus a small number of forceful bodily-disciplining postures (paścimottānāsana, mayūrāsana, kukkuṭāsana). The standing-pose-heavy repertoire of modern yoga (warrior, triangle, sun salutation sequences) is largely absent from the premodern corpus.

## The Krishnamacharya synthesis (Mysore Palace, 1920s-1950s)

T. Krishnamacharya (1888-1989), under the patronage of the Maharaja of Mysore, taught a vigorous athletic yoga in the Mysore Palace gymnasium to teenage boys (including the future Pattabhi Jois and B.K.S. Iyengar) in the 1930s-40s. His synthesis combined:

- Premodern Sanskrit haṭha sources (which Krishnamacharya could read in original)
- The British-Indian gymnastic-physical-culture milieu (Ling gymnastics, Sandow exercises, Indian wrestling)
- Innovative vinyāsa-sequencing tying postures together with breath-coordinated transitions

Krishnamacharya's four principal disciples globalized the system in distinctive forms:

- **B.K.S. Iyengar** — *Light on Yoga* (1966), Pune institute; alignment-precision method with props; the canonical modern reference text
- **K. Pattabhi Jois** — Ashtanga Vinyāsa Yoga; codified primary/intermediate/advanced series taught at Mysore from the 1970s
- **T.K.V. Desikachar** — Viniyoga; therapeutic individualized method
- **Indra Devi** — first Western woman taught by Krishnamacharya; introduced postural yoga to Hollywood (1947) and Soviet Union (1960)

## MASSIVE-WIN — the 20th-century globalization

Postural yoga's diffusion from a small Mysore-Palace circle of practitioners in the 1930s to >300 million global practitioners by the 2020s is one of the largest cross-cultural religious-practice diffusions in human history, comparable in scale to the modern Buddhist [[vipassana-insight-meditation|vipassana / MBSR]] diffusion and the modern Western [[zazen-zen-meditation|Zen]] diffusion — but larger than either by an order of magnitude. The diffusion is a hybrid religious-secular transmission: yoga in the modern Western practice context is typically framed as fitness/wellness with optional spiritual/meditative content rather than as a Hindu religious practice, but its theological/philosophical roots in Patañjali, the [[tradition-tantra|Tantric]] body-as-microcosm framework, and Hindu cosmological metaphysics remain visible.

The HAF (Hindu American Foundation) "Take Back Yoga" campaign (2008-) and Andrea Jain's *Selling Yoga* (2014) have generated significant scholarly + popular debate over the secularization-vs-Hinduization question for modern postural yoga.

## Cross-tradition position

Yoga āsana anchors the Hindu pole of cross-tradition **embodied-contemplative-discipline** practices. Cross-tradition cognates:

- **Daoist [[qigong-energy-cultivation|qigong]] + [[tai-chi-quan-internal-martial|tai chi]]** — internal-energy cultivation through slow coordinated movement; structurally + theoretically cognate (nāḍī / prāṇa parallels meridians / qi)
- **Tibetan Buddhist *lu jong* / *trul khor*** — yogic-body movement practices in the Tibetan tradition; share Indic-tantric ancestry with Hindu haṭha
- **Christian monastic prostration cycles** — see [[prostration-cross-tradition]]
- **Sufi Mawlawi sema (whirling)** — embodied dhikr; analogous breath-movement-attention coordination

The embodied-contemplative cluster (yoga + qigong + tai chi + tantric movement + ritual prostration + dance) is one of the cleanest cross-tradition convergences in religious practice.
