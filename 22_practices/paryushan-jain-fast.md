# Paryūṣaṇa — Annual Jain Fast

The most important annual Jain religious observance: an 8-day (Śvetāmbara, *Aṣṭāhnika*) or 10-day (Digambara, *Daśalakṣaṇa*) intensified-ascetic festival of fasting, scripture-reading, intensified meditation + introspection, formal confession (*pratikramaṇa*), and culminating mutual forgiveness-rite. Held during the monsoon retreat (Bhādrapada month, August-September), Paryūṣaṇa is the principal lay-community Jain ritual observance and one of the most-elaborated annual fasting practices in any religious tradition globally.

## How it works

The festival structures intensified versions of standard Jain religious practices into a continuous 8-10 day observance:

**Daily structure**:
- **Pratikramaṇa** (formal confession + repentance liturgy) performed morning and evening — extended versions during Paryūṣaṇa beyond the standard daily/weekly observance
- **Sāmāyika** (48-minute equanimity meditation) practiced multiple times daily
- **Pratyākhyāna** (renunciation vows) — daily formal renunciation of specific foods, activities, possessions
- **Pūjā** at the temple — extended ritual worship
- **Pravacana** (religious discourse) — monastic teaching attended daily by lay community
- **Kalpa Sūtra reading** (Śvetāmbara) — canonical text divided into 8 portions read over the 8 days, ending with the *Bhadrabāhu Kalpa* sermon on Mahāvīra's life

**Fasting levels** — laity choose a level of fasting appropriate to their capacity:
- **Upavāsa** — complete water-only fast (varying number of days through the festival; some practice 8 consecutive days; some practice all 8 days plus extra days; the most-rigorous lay practice)
- **Chauvihar** — no food + no water (from sunset to sunrise OR for an entire day)
- **Ekāsana** — one single-sitting meal per day
- **Biyāsana** — two single-sitting meals per day
- **Āyambil** — bland/tasteless food only (no spices, oil, ghee, salt, sweetness, dairy)

The fasting program is calibrated to individual capacity but the underlying principle is intensified withdrawal of attention from food-engagement to free attention for religious practice. Extreme fasters may sustain water-only fasts for the full festival; the practice of *Aṭṭhāī* (8-day fast) is widely undertaken by committed laity.

**Culminating rituals**:
- **Saṃvatsarī Pratikramaṇa** (Śvetāmbara) — the annual confession on the final day; an extended pratikramaṇa liturgy lasting several hours covering the year's accumulated transgressions
- **Kṣamāpanā** (forgiveness-rite) — the practitioner formally seeks forgiveness from every other person and indeed from all sentient beings, using the formula **Micchāmi Dukkaḍaṃ** ("may my misdeeds become inconsequential" or "I seek forgiveness for any harm I have done") exchanged in person, by telephone, by letter, and now extensively by text/email/social-media in the modern diaspora. The forgiveness-exchange is not merely interpersonal but cosmically ambitious — including all *jīvas* (souls) of all classes (humans, animals, plants, microorganisms) toward whom any harm might have been done

## The Daśalakṣaṇa (Digambara) variant

The Digambara sect observes *Daśalakṣaṇa Parva* — the "Ten Religious Virtues" festival — for 10 days following the Śvetāmbara Paryūṣaṇa. Each day is dedicated to one of the ten core Jain religious virtues drawn from the *Tattvārtha Sūtra*:

1. **Uttama Kṣamā** — supreme forbearance/forgiveness
2. **Uttama Mārdava** — supreme humility
3. **Uttama Ārjava** — supreme straightforwardness
4. **Uttama Śauca** — supreme purity
5. **Uttama Satya** — supreme truthfulness
6. **Uttama Saṃyama** — supreme self-restraint
7. **Uttama Tapas** — supreme austerity
8. **Uttama Tyāga** — supreme renunciation
9. **Uttama Ākiñcanya** — supreme non-possessiveness
10. **Uttama Brahmacarya** — supreme celibacy

Each day's observance contemplates that day's virtue through scripture-reading, sermon, meditation, and vow-renewal. The Daśalakṣaṇa is the Digambara equivalent of the Śvetāmbara Paryūṣaṇa, sharing the same underlying purpose (intensified annual religious observance during the monsoon retreat) but expressed through the Ten-Virtues framework rather than the Kalpa Sūtra reading.

## Position in the Jain calendar + theology

Paryūṣaṇa falls during the four-month *cāturmāsa* monsoon retreat when Jain monastic mendicants suspend their normal wandering and reside in a fixed location, providing intensive teaching to the lay community. The retreat is itself a substantial annual religious observance for monastics; Paryūṣaṇa is the intensified peak of the retreat for the lay community.

The theological framework: Jain doctrine treats every action as generating *karma* (subtle material substance) that adheres to the soul (*jīva*) and prevents liberation (*mokṣa*). The path to liberation requires both *saṃvara* (stopping new karma-influx) and *nirjarā* (burning off accumulated karma through austerity). Paryūṣaṇa is the year's most-intensified *saṃvara* + *nirjarā* practice, combining fasting (the principal austerity), confession + forgiveness (eliminating karma-generating residue), and intensified meditation/study.

## MASSIVE-WIN — the Micchāmi Dukkaḍaṃ forgiveness-rite

The Kṣamāpanā / Saṃvatsarī forgiveness-rite is one of the most distinctive religious practices in the cross-tradition cluster. The structural pattern:

1. **Annual temporal location** — performed once per year on a specific day
2. **Mutual + universal scope** — sought from and offered to all persons (and all sentient beings)
3. **Specific verbal formula** — *Micchāmi Dukkaḍaṃ*
4. **Concrete interpersonal performance** — sought in person from every person the practitioner has interacted with that year

The closest cross-tradition cognate is the **Jewish Yom Kippur** annual atonement, particularly the practice of seeking forgiveness from any person one has wronged in the preceding year before the day itself (since Yom Kippur atones only for sins against God, not sins against persons — those require direct interpersonal reconciliation). The structural similarity (annual; mutual; verbal formula; interpersonal performance) is striking; the historical connection (if any) is undocumented and the two traditions are believed independent. The Jain practice extends the scope beyond humans to all sentient beings (consistent with the broader Jain *ahiṃsā* framework), making it the most-ambitiously-universal forgiveness-rite in any documented religious tradition.

## Cross-tradition position

Paryūṣaṇa anchors the Jain pole of the cross-tradition cluster of **intensified-annual-fasting + introspection festivals**:

- **Islamic [[ihram-pilgrimage-state|iḥrām]] + Ramadan** — annual intensified consecration + month-long daylight fasting; structurally cognate as annual intensified-ascetic observance
- **Christian Lent** — 40-day pre-Easter fasting + introspection
- **Jewish Yom Kippur + High Holy Days** — annual atonement + introspection cluster
- **Hindu Navarātri** — 9-day Goddess-festival with fasting + scripture-reading
- **Buddhist vassa** (rains retreat) + uposatha days — monastic + lay intensified-observance pattern; structurally cognate as monsoon-retreat-anchored intensified practice

The pattern: an annually-recurring intensified religious observance involving food-discipline + scripture-engagement + introspection + (often) community reconciliation. Paryūṣaṇa is among the most-rigorously-fasting of these (extended water-only fasts exceeding what most other traditions undertake) and the most-universally-scoped in its forgiveness-rite.
