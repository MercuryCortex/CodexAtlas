# Mayan Calendrical System

A composite calendrical system of remarkable mathematical sophistication, combining multiple interlocking cycles: the **260-day Tzolkin** (ritual / deity-cycle), the **365-day Haab** (solar approximation), the **18,980-day Calendar Round** (Tzolkin + Haab coincidence cycle, ~52 solar years), and the **1,872,000-day Long Count** (~5,125 solar years; the current cycle ended on 21 December 2012 = 13.0.0.0.0 in Long Count notation).

## MASSIVE-WIN — independent invention of zero + positional notation

The Mayan calendar runs on the **vigesimal (base-20) positional numeral system with a dedicated zero glyph**. The Maya independently invented zero as a positional placeholder — one of only three ancient civilizations to do so (alongside Mesopotamian + Indian developments). The zero glyph (a stylized shell) appears in Long Count inscriptions from the 1st century BCE onward, predating Indian zero (~5th c. CE) and contemporaneous with the latest stage of Mesopotamian-Babylonian placeholder-zero usage.

The Long Count arithmetic — kin (day) × 20 = winal × 18 = tun (~year) × 20 = katun × 20 = baktun — uses base-20 throughout except at the winal-to-tun step (× 18 instead of × 20, to make tun ~360 days, an approximate-solar-year). This is the cleanest case of *deliberate-mathematical-design* in ancient calendrics: the Maya know that 18 × 20 = 360 ≈ 365.25; the base-20 system breaks for this step specifically to keep the higher cycles solar-anchored.

## The four interlocking cycles

1. **Tzolkin (260 days)**: 13 numbers × 20 named day-signs. Pure ritual cycle with NO astronomical reference; the 260-day length may derive from human gestation period or from 9 lunar months or from Venus-cycle subdivision (scholarly hypotheses vary). Each Tzolkin day has divinatory significance + deity association.

2. **Haab (365 days)**: 18 named months of 20 days + 5 unlucky **Wayeb** days at year-end. The 365-day length approximates the solar year but is NOT corrected via intercalation — the Haab drifts ~1 day per 4 years against astronomical reality. The Maya knew of this drift (they tracked it astronomically in the Dresden Codex) but kept the 365-day ritual structure.

3. **Calendar Round (18,980 days, ~52 Haab years)**: every Tzolkin/Haab combination recurs after 52 solar years. Within the Calendar Round, every day has a unique Tzolkin-Haab name (e.g., 4 Ahau 8 Cumku is the Long Count epoch + recurring Calendar Round position).

4. **Long Count (1,872,000 days, ~5,125 solar years)**: linear count from the epoch (11 August 3114 BCE Goodman-Martínez-Thompson correlation). Long Count dates anchor every Tzolkin/Haab combination to absolute time. The famous "2012 Maya prophecy" is the completion of the 13th Baktun in the current Long Count cycle (21 December 2012 = 13.0.0.0.0).

## Cross-tradition position

The Mayan calendrical system sits at three distinctive cross-tradition junctures:

1. **Independent calendrical invention** — like Yi Jing + Ifá divination (see [[yi-jing-divination]] + [[ifa-odu-divination]]), Mayan calendrics are an independent emergence with no documented contact with Old World calendrical traditions. The mathematical sophistication is independent invention.

2. **The 260-day cycle as a singular feature** — no other major world calendar has a 260-day ritual cycle. The closest cross-cultural parallels are 9-lunar-month gestation associations + the broader pattern of "ritual cycles with non-solar period" found elsewhere (e.g., Tibetan astrological cycles, Aztec Tonalpohualli which is structurally identical to Tzolkin).

3. **Mesoamerican cross-tradition spread** — the Aztec, Mixtec, Zapotec, Olmec, Toltec, and other Mesoamerican civilizations share variants of the Tzolkin (= Aztec Tonalpohualli) + Haab (= Aztec Xiuhpohualli) + Calendar Round structure. The Long Count is more distinctively Maya (especially Classic-period). This cross-Mesoamerican family is the second documented calendrical-tradition cluster (alongside the Mediterranean-lunisolar family).
