# Chinese Sexagenary Calendar (Stem-Branch 60-Cycle + Lunisolar)

A composite lunisolar calendar combining the **60-year sexagenary cycle** (10 Heavenly Stems × 12 Earthly Branches) with a Metonic-style 19-year lunar-solar reconciliation cycle + 24 solar-term subdivisions (*jieqi*). One of the oldest continuously-operating calendrical systems in human history with documented Shang-dynasty oracle-bone attestation from ~-1500.

## The sexagenary cycle

The defining structural feature: **10 Heavenly Stems (天干) × 12 Earthly Branches (地支) = 60 unique combinations**, cycling continuously without fixed epoch. Each year, month, day, and hour has a sexagenary designation; the 60-year cycle is the most prominent. The current year (2026 CE) is *Bing-Wu* 丙午 in the sexagenary cycle.

The 60-year cycle's mathematical foundation: stems cycle in 10s, branches cycle in 12s; lcm(10, 12) = 60. The system is documented in Shang oracle-bone inscriptions from -1500 onward (the same oracle-bone corpus that includes [[scapulimancy-oracle-bones|scapulimancy]] divinatory inscriptions) and has cycled continuously for 3,500+ years — the longest unbroken documented calendrical cycle in world history.

## The 24 solar terms (*jieqi* 节气)

The Chinese calendar's distinctive solar component: **24 evenly-spaced solar-term divisions of the year** marking 15° increments of ecliptic position. These include the solstices, equinoxes, and 20 intermediate divisions named for seasonal-agricultural phenomena (*Lichun* "start of spring", *Yushui* "rainwater", *Jingzhe* "awakening of insects", etc.). The jieqi are astronomically defined (by sun's ecliptic longitude) NOT by date — so their calendar-day-date varies year to year. The system provides solar-anchored agricultural-seasonal precision within a primarily-lunar calendar structure.

The jieqi are one of the most-sophisticated cross-tradition examples of *solar-term subdivision within a lunisolar framework*. The 15° ecliptic-increment regularity reflects detailed Chinese astronomical observation; some terms (especially the solstices + equinoxes) have provenance back to the Shang or Western Zhou periods.

## The Metonic-style intercalation

Like the [[hebrew-calendar]] and Babylonian lunisolar predecessors, the Chinese calendar uses a **19-year Metonic-style cycle with 7 leap months in 19 years** to keep the lunar months aligned with solar seasons. The leap month's POSITION is determined by the relationship between lunar months and solar terms: a leap month is added between two regular months that fall in a year-section without enough jieqi solar-term anchors.

The independent emergence of Metonic 19-year cycles in three separate cultural-mathematical traditions — Babylonian (~-500), Chinese (Zhou + Han period systematization), and Greek (Meton of Athens, ~-432) — is itself a cross-tradition convergence finding. The mathematical observation (235 lunar months ≈ 19 solar years to high precision) is astronomically true and was independently discovered by multiple traditions.

## Cross-tradition position

The Chinese sexagenary calendar sits at multiple cross-tradition junctures:

1. **East Asian calendrical family** — Korean + Japanese + Vietnamese + Mongolian calendars are derived from or strongly influenced by Chinese sexagenary structure. Japanese era-naming (era-name + sexagenary year) persists into the modern period
2. **Tibetan adaptation** — Tibetan calendar is sexagenary-influenced but incorporates Kalachakra-astrological elements; modern Tibetan calendar reform is documented under Phugpa + Tsurphu schools
3. **Sexagenary divination** — the sexagenary cycle underlies Chinese astrological + divinatory systems (Bazi / Four Pillars of Destiny; see also [[yi-jing-divination]] for the related binary-divinatory framework). The same 60-cycle that runs the calendar runs the personal-fate astrology
4. **Independent Metonic convergence** — Chinese 19-year intercalation + Babylonian 19-year cycle + Greek Meton 19-year cycle = cross-tradition convergence on the same astronomical-mathematical fact
