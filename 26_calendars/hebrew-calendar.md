# Hebrew Calendar (Lunisolar with Metonic Cycle)

The lunisolar calendar of Jewish liturgy + civil life, attested in biblical texts from ~-800 and codified mathematically in the 4th c. CE (traditionally by Hillel II in 359 CE). The system reconciles **lunar months** (29-30 days each, anchored to the new moon / *molad*) with **solar seasons** (the agricultural-festival cycle must keep Pesach in spring per Exodus 12:2) via the **19-year Metonic cycle**: 235 lunar months ≈ 19 solar years; the calendar adds a leap month (*Adar II*) in 7 of every 19 years to keep them aligned.

## The structure

- **12 months in a common year** = 353-355 days; **13 months in a leap year** = 383-385 days
- **Months alternate ~30 / 29 days**; the precise lengths of Cheshvan + Kislev vary per year-rule to ensure festival-day-of-week constraints
- **Years begin civilly at Tishrei** (autumn — Rosh Hashanah is Tishrei 1) but ritually at Nisan (spring — per Exodus 12:2)
- **Anno Mundi epoch**: -3761 per Seder Olam Rabbah + Maimonides; current year is 5786 AM (2025-26 CE)

## Astronomical sophistication

The Hebrew calendar is one of the most-astronomically-sophisticated traditional calendars:
- The lunar months track the synodic lunar period (~29.530589 days) with high precision via the *molad* calculation
- The Metonic 19-year cycle is mathematically near-perfect: 19 solar years × 365.2422 = 6939.6 days; 235 lunar months × 29.530589 = 6939.69 days. Drift is ~2 hours per 19-year cycle
- Hillel II's 359 CE calculated calendar replaced the older observational system (where the Sanhedrin sighted the new moon visually) due to the Christianization of Roman power + the destruction of the Sanhedrin authority

## Cross-tradition position

The Hebrew calendar sits at the foundational point of multiple cross-tradition calendar lineages:

1. **Babylonian lunisolar substrate** — the Hebrew calendar's structure (lunar months + intercalation to keep festivals in season) inherits from the Babylonian calendar that the Jewish exilic community engaged with during the Babylonian Exile (-586 to -538). The month-names (Nisan, Iyyar, Sivan, etc.) are Akkadian/Babylonian loan-words adopted during the exile, replacing earlier Hebrew month-names (Aviv, Ziv, Ethanim, Bul)
2. **Christian-liturgical inheritance** — the Easter computation depends on the Jewish Pesach calendar (per Acts 12:3-4 + the patristic Quartodeciman controversy); the Christian liturgical year's spring-festival anchoring inherits from the Hebrew framework
3. **Islamic-Hijri contrast** — the Islamic Hijri calendar (see [[islamic-hijri-calendar]]) is pure-lunar (no intercalation, drifts through the solar year); the Hebrew is lunisolar (locked to seasons). The contrast is structurally illuminating: same Semitic-religious matrix produces opposite calendrical solutions
