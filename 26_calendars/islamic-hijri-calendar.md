# Islamic Hijri Calendar (Pure Lunar)

The lunar calendar of Islamic religious + civil life, established by Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab in 638 CE with the Hegira event of 622 CE as its epoch (Anno Hegirae, AH). The defining structural feature: **purely lunar with no intercalation to seasons**. The Hijri year of ~354 days is ~10-11 days shorter than the solar year, so the calendar systematically drifts through the seasons over a 33-year cycle. Ramadan can fall in any season.

## The structural decision

The Quran (9:36-37) explicitly prohibits *nasīʾ* — the pre-Islamic Arabian intercalation practice that had kept lunar months seasonally anchored. Muhammad's farewell sermon (632 CE) confirmed the prohibition: *"time has come back to its original state which it had when God created the Heavens and the Earth — the year is twelve months, four of them being sacred"*. The non-intercalation is therefore *theologically motivated*, not merely structurally simple.

The result: the Islamic calendar is **the only major living world religious calendar that is purely lunar without seasonal correction**. Ramadan in 2025 falls in late February to late March; Ramadan in 2035 will fall in November-December. Over a 33-year cycle, every Muslim experiences Ramadan in every season.

## Cross-tradition contrast

The Hijri calendar's pure-lunar structure is most-illuminating in contrast with its predecessor + competitor traditions:

- **Pre-Islamic Arabian calendar** — was lunisolar (with intercalation per Babylonian-Hellenistic model). Muhammad's prohibition of *nasīʾ* deliberately broke from this
- **[[hebrew-calendar]]** — same Semitic-religious matrix, but lunisolar (locked to seasons via Metonic cycle). The structural contrast with Hijri is the cleanest cross-tradition calendar comparison available
- **[[gregorian-calendar]]** — pure solar (with leap-year correction); Hijri is its pure-lunar mirror
- **Bahá'í Badíʿ calendar** (see [[bahai-badi-calendar]]) — the Bahá'í tradition that emerges from 19th-c. Iranian-Islamic context reverses Hijri's design by adopting a solar-anchored 19×19 month structure with intercalation

## The theological motivation

The non-intercalation decision is **theologically deliberate**: it ensures that:
- Religious obligations (Ramadan fasting, Hajj pilgrimage) are experienced in every season, generating universal cross-seasonal religious-experience equity
- The calendar is decoupled from solar / agricultural / civil seasons — emphasizing religious primacy over agricultural / political utility
- The lunar observation is universally accessible (every Muslim can observe the new moon) — democratizing calendrical authority versus elite-astronomical-priesthood models

This makes Hijri one of the most-distinctive religious calendars in world history: it deliberately rejects the seasonal-anchoring that almost every other major religious calendar (Hebrew, Christian, Hindu, Chinese, Iranian, Mayan) maintains.

## Modern variants

Modern Islamic-state usage varies:
- **Saudi Arabia + Gulf states**: Hijri is the official civil calendar
- **Most other Muslim countries**: Gregorian for civil + Hijri for religious
- **Tabular Hijri vs Sighted Hijri**: scholarly calculated tables vs visual moon-sighting create occasional 1-day disputes at month-beginning. Modern committees in different countries adopt different rules, occasionally producing Ramadan-start-date disagreements
