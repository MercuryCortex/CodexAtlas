# Jubilee and Sabbatical Year (shmita and yovel)

## Identity

The **sabbatical year** (Hebrew *shmita*, שמיטה, "release") and the **jubilee** (*yovel*, יובל, the ram's-horn year) are the Israelite extension of the Sabbath principle from the *week* to the *year*. Where the weekly Shabbat rests every seventh day, the shmita rests every seventh **year** — the land lies fallow and debts are remitted — and the jubilee falls after **seven sabbaticals**, in the fiftieth year, when ancestral land reverts to its original owners and Israelite bond-servants go free. This is not a day-reckoning calendar in the sense of the [[hebrew-calendar|Hebrew lunisolar calendar]] that carries it; it is a **supra-annual cycle-of-years** — a septennial and quinquagenary count overlaid on the ordinary calendar, whose content is agricultural rest, debt-cancellation, manumission, and land-restitution rather than the phases of the moon.

The governing theology is stated in a single verse: **"the land shall not be sold in perpetuity, for the land is mine; for you are strangers and sojourners with me"** (Leviticus 25:23). Because the land belongs to YHWH, Israelite families hold it only as tenants; the sabbatical and jubilee are the periodic re-assertion of that divine ownership, and a built-in structural brake on the two engines of ancient impoverishment — permanent debt and permanent loss of the family plot. The jubilee proclamation, *"proclaim liberty (deror) throughout the land unto all the inhabitants thereof"* (Leviticus 25:10), is the verse cast on the American Liberty Bell.

## In the Torah law-codes

The legislation is layered across the Torah's three main law-codes, and the layering is itself the evidence for its development:

- **The Covenant Code (Exodus, oldest stratum).** *Exodus 21:2* releases the Hebrew debt-slave in the seventh year of service; *Exodus 23:10-11* commands the seventh-year **fallow**, letting the poor and the wild animals eat what the untilled land yields. Here the seventh-year release is still tied to the individual's term of service, not a nationally synchronised year.
- **Deuteronomy (7th c. BCE).** *Deuteronomy 15* generalises the seventh year into a fixed, nationwide *shmitat kesafim* — a **remission of debts** ("every creditor shall release that which he hath lent") — and repeats the seventh-year manumission (Deut 15:12), now with the humane rider that the freed servant not be sent away empty-handed.
- **The Holiness Code (Leviticus 25, exilic/post-exilic Priestly-Holiness material).** Here the institution reaches its fully systematised form: a *shabbat* of the land every seventh year (Lev 25:2-7), and then, counted as **"seven sabbaths of years, seven times seven years" (Lev 25:8)**, the **jubilee** in the fiftieth year, announced by the *yovel* (ram's-horn) blast on the Day of Atonement. In the jubilee, sold land returns to the ancestral family (Lev 25:13-28) — so that a "sale" of land is really a lease of its harvests, priced by the number of years left until the next jubilee — and Israelites who fell into bond-service are freed (Lev 25:39-43).

In the Second Temple period the **shmita was a real, observed institution**: sabbatical-year fallows are noted at moments of crisis (e.g. the food shortage during a sabbatical year at the siege recorded in 1 Maccabees 6:49, 53; Josephus repeatedly dates events to sabbatical years). To keep credit from drying up as the seventh year approached, **Hillel the Elder** instituted the *prosbul*, a legal device transferring private debts to the court so they escaped the shmita remission (Mishnah *Sheviit* 10:3-4). The shmita is still kept in the modern State of Israel, negotiated through halakhic work-arounds such as the *heter mechira* (a nominal sale of farmland to a non-Jew).

## Cross-tradition significance

**The within-tradition core — a fractal of the Sabbath.** The sabbatical and jubilee are the clearest case in the biblical corpus of a single principle scaled across time: the seventh day of creation on which God rested and which he *sanctified* (Genesis 2:2-3) becomes the weekly [[shabbat-jewish-sabbath|Shabbat]], then the seventh-year land-sabbath (Lev 25:2 uses the very word *shabbat* of the land), then the "sabbath of sabbaths" of the forty-ninth/fiftieth year. This is **structural extension within one tradition**, not cross-tradition convergence — the septenary rhythm is deliberately re-applied at each scale by the same priestly theology.

**The ancient Near Eastern background — release edicts (transmission vs convergence).** The jubilee's operative word, **deror** ("release, liberty," Lev 25:10), is the West-Semitic **cognate of Akkadian *(an)durarum***, the technical term in Mesopotamian royal edicts for the remission of debts and the freeing of debt-slaves. Old Babylonian and later kings — Hammurabi (see [[phase-1-007-code-of-hammurabi]]) and his successors, and most fully the *misharum* ("equity/justice") edicts such as the **Edict of Ammi-saduqa** — periodically proclaimed a "clean slate" that cancelled consumer debts, released those enslaved for debt, and returned forfeited land (Weinfeld 1995). The parallel is close and the *deror ~ andurarum* lexical link is secure. What is debated is **what follows from it**. The linguistic cognate does not by itself prove that Leviticus 25 *borrows* the Mesopotamian institution; both may draw on a common ancient Near Eastern vocabulary and social-justice impulse. The **distinctive Israelite move**, on which scholars agree, is transformative regardless of the direction of influence: the Mesopotamian release was a **sporadic act of royal grace**, proclaimed at a king's accession or in a fiscal emergency and dependent on his will; the biblical jubilee **fixes the release into a recurring calendrical cycle**, binding on the whole society, grounded not in a king but in the divine ownership of the land. Milgrom reads Leviticus 25 as a priestly-**utopian** systematisation of that older, irregular practice.

**The reception afterlife.** The jubilee's language of *deror* was taken up prophetically in **Isaiah 61:1-2** (see [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]]) — "to proclaim liberty to the captives... the year of the LORD's favour" — and that text is the one Jesus reads and applies to himself in **Luke 4:18-19** (see [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]]), so that the "acceptable year of the Lord" becomes an image of eschatological release. In the Second Temple period the **Book of Jubilees** ([[phase-3-009-jubilees]]) made the 49-year jubilee-period the master unit of a whole sacred chronology. In the modern era the same institution supplied the name and rationale of the **Jubilee 2000** campaign for the cancellation of poor-country debt — an unusually direct case of a biblical calendrical law re-entering economic politics three millennia after its composition.

## Disputes

- **Forty-nine or fifty years?** Leviticus 25:8-10 counts *seven weeks of years* (49) and then hallows *the fiftieth year* as the jubilee. Whether the jubilee is a genuine additional (fiftieth) year — which would break the sabbatical rhythm and force two consecutive fallow years (the 49th sabbatical plus the 50th jubilee) — or whether the "fiftieth" is the 49th reckoned inclusively so that jubilees recur every 49 years, is a classic unresolved problem (North 1954; Milgrom 2001). The agricultural difficulty of two successive fallow years is the main argument for a 49-year cycle.
- **Was the jubilee ever practised?** The **shmita** is well attested as an observed institution in the Second Temple period. The **jubilee** is much more doubtful: there is little to no positive evidence that the fiftieth-year land-return and mass manumission were ever actually carried out, and many scholars read Leviticus 25 as a priestly **ideal or programme** — possibly composed in or after the exile as a blueprint for a restored society — rather than a description of a functioning institution (North 1954; Milgrom 2001).
- **The count's start-point.** Because no continuous historical jubilee reckoning survives, when the sabbatical/jubilee count began (and how the rabbinic sabbatical-year cycle used today relates to any pre-exilic count) is reconstructed rather than documented, and different chronologies are defended.
- **Direction of the ANE link.** As above: the *deror ~ andurarum* cognate is not in dispute; whether Leviticus 25 reflects **borrowing** from Mesopotamian release-edicts or **independent convergence** on a shared West-Semitic institution is genuinely open.
