# Babylonian Calendar

## Identity

The **Babylonian calendar** is the lunisolar calendar of Mesopotamia: twelve lunar months
begun at the first sighting of the crescent moon, kept in step with the solar year by
inserting a thirteenth month seven times in every nineteen years. It is the single most
consequential calendar in history for downstream transmission — its month-names, its
19-year intercalation cycle, and its zodiac all pass westward into the Hebrew, Greek, and
ultimately the Christian computistical traditions.

## On the branch — the calendar as cosmogony realized

In Babylonian theology the calendar is not a convention but the *visible order Marduk
imposes on the chaos-deep*. In **[[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]]** Tablet V, immediately after
defeating **[[tiamat]]** and splitting her body to make heaven and earth, Marduk "fixed the
stations" of the stars, set the moon to "mark the days," and defined the month and the year.
The calendar is thus the **temporal face of the cosmogony**: the same act that orders space
(sky/earth) orders time (month/year). The **[[astronomy-mul-apin]]** compendium later
records the working rules — the path of the Moon (the future zodiac), the intercalation
scheme, the schematic 360-day year — all computed in **[[mathematics-sexagesimal-babylonian]]**.
The New Year, when this order is annually renewed, is the **Akitu** festival, at which the
Enuma Elish itself was recited aloud.

## Transmission

- **The 19-year "Metonic" cycle** is Babylonian first. Meton of Athens (c. -432) and later
  Greek astronomy inherit it; through Hellenistic Judaism it becomes the backbone of the
  fixed Hebrew calendar and, via the computus, of the Christian calculation of Easter.
- **The month-names** (Nisannu, Ayyaru, Simanu, …, Addaru) are borrowed wholesale into the
  Hebrew calendar (Nisan, Iyyar, Sivan, …, Adar) during and after the Babylonian Exile — the
  names in the modern Jewish calendar are Babylonian.
- **The zodiac**: the twelve equal ecliptic signs are a Babylonian creation of the mid-first
  millennium BCE, abstracted from the month-constellations of the calendar-year.
