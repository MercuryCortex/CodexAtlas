# Tibetan Phugpa Calendar (Kālacakra-Based Lunisolar)

The Buddhist lunisolar calendar of Tibet, computed by the **Phugpa-lugs** (Phug-school) or **Tsurphu-lugs** (Tsurphu-school) algorithmic traditions derived from the **Kālacakra Tantra** introduced from India in 1027 CE. Structurally a hybrid of (a) Indian Kālacakra astronomical-tantric inheritance, (b) Chinese sexagenary 60-year cycle adopted independently of the Chinese calendar's lunisolar structure, and (c) Tibetan-specific computational refinements developed at Ganden + Tsurphu monasteries from the 14th century onward.

## The Kālacakra inheritance

The **Kālacakra Tantra** ("Wheel of Time Tantra") is a Vajrayāna Buddhist text whose **outer wheel** (*phyi'i 'khor-lo / lo-khor*) section provides comprehensive astronomical-calendrical instructions. Brought to Tibet from India c. 1027 CE (the traditional year-1 of the *rab-byung* sexagenary system), the text's calendrical algorithms became the foundation of all Tibetan time-reckoning.

Distinctive Kālacakra inheritances preserved in the Tibetan calendar:
- **27 nakshatra lunar mansions** (a-shvini through revati — same as the Indian astronomical-astrological system)
- **Metonic-style 19-year lunar-solar reconciliation** with leap months
- **Tithi (lunar-day) system** with doubling + omission rules to keep day-count aligned with astronomical lunar position
- **Five-planet sidereal-position computation** for astrological + ritual-timing purposes

## The 60-year rab-byung cycle

The Tibetan **rab-byung** ("prosperous-rising") 60-year cycle is structurally identical to the Chinese sexagenary cycle (5 elements × 12 animals = 60 unique combinations) but operates on a DIFFERENT epoch — beginning in 1027 CE rather than at any Chinese reference point. Each rab-byung year carries an element + animal name (2026 = Fire-Horse, year 40 of Rab-byung 17).

This is a cross-tradition convergence finding: the **Chinese sexagenary structure was adopted by Tibetan Buddhism but anchored to a Buddhist religious-historical epoch (the Kālacakra Tantra arrival)** rather than to any Chinese reference. The same sexagenary mathematical structure thus appears in TWO independent religious-historical traditions with different epoch-anchoring choices.

## Phugpa vs Tsurphu schools

The famous calendrical feature of Tibetan Buddhism: **two computational schools** produce slightly different dates in some years:

- **Phugpa-lugs** — founded at Ganden monastery in the 14th c., refined by Phugpa Lhundrub Gyatso (1308-1389); the dominant Gelug-school computational tradition; used in most Tibetan calendar-almanacs (*lo-tho*) today
- **Tsurphu-lugs** — the Karma-Kagyu school's rival computational tradition based at Tsurphu monastery; uses slightly different intercalation rules + tithi-day calculations
- **Date divergences** — Losar (Tibetan New Year) can fall 1-2 days or even 1 month apart between Phugpa and Tsurphu calculations; this is theologically/institutionally accepted rather than treated as a problem to resolve

This **persistent institutional pluralism in calendrical computation** is unusual in cross-tradition comparison — most religious calendars eventually settle on a single canonical rule. The Tibetan Buddhist tolerance of computational variation reflects the underlying Vajrayāna theological position that **multiple coherent algorithmic approaches to the same astronomical reality are all valid manifestations of the Kālacakra wisdom**.

## Tibetan Buddhist liturgical year

The Tibetan calendar structures Vajrayāna Buddhist ritual life through:

- **Losar (Tibetan New Year)** — 1st day of 1st month; the major civil-religious festival
- **Monlam Chenmo (Great Prayer Festival)** — instituted by [[tsongkhapa]] at Lhasa in 1409 as a 2-week Mahayana prayer assembly running through Losar
- **Saga Dawa (15th day of 4th month)** — Buddha's birth + enlightenment + parinirvana commemorated on the SAME day, per the Theravada-Mahayana convention that all three events occurred on the same lunar day
- **Lhabab Duchen** — Buddha's descent from Tushita heaven
- **Gaden Ngamcho** — Tsongkhapa parinirvana anniversary; Gelug-school butter-lamp festival

## Cross-tradition position

The Tibetan Phugpa calendar sits at multiple cross-tradition junctures:

1. **Indian Vajrayāna inheritance** — preserves Kālacakra Tantra calendrical computation that was largely lost in India after the 13th-c. Muslim destruction of north-Indian Buddhist monasteries
2. **Chinese sexagenary adaptation** — adopted the 60-year cycle structure but anchored it to a Buddhist epoch (1027 Kālacakra introduction), independent of Chinese reference
3. **Bhutanese + Mongolian + Buryat reach** — the Tibetan calendar is also the calendar of Bhutan, Mongolia (after Gelug-Mongol patronage from the 16th c.), and Russian-Buryat Buddhism
4. **Modern persistence** — continues to function as the Tibetan-diaspora ritual calendar; the Dalai Lama's office in Dharamsala publishes annual Phugpa-lugs almanacs
