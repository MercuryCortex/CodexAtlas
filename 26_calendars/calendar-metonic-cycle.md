# Metonic Cycle (19 Years ≈ 235 Lunar Months)

## Identity

The **Metonic cycle** is the discovery that **nineteen solar years contain very nearly two hundred thirty-five lunar months** — the small-integer coincidence on which almost every lunisolar calendar in the Western and Near-Eastern world fixes its intercalation. Nineteen tropical years run to about 6939.60 days; 235 synodic months run to about 6939.69 days; the two agree to within roughly **two hours**. Because 235 = (12 × 19) + 7, the rule that follows is exact and memorable: keep twelve lunar months in most years, but **insert a thirteenth month in seven years out of every nineteen**, and a lunar month-count will stay locked to the sun almost indefinitely.

It is named for **Meton of Athens**, who with Euctemon observed the summer solstice at Athens in **-432** (27 June, Julian) and published the cycle as the basis of a reformed Athenian calendar and star-almanac (*parapegma*). But the cycle is not, in origin, Greek alone: the same 7-in-19 intercalation scheme was already fixed in **Babylonian** astronomical practice under the Achaemenid Persians, earlier than Meton — so the "Metonic" cycle is better understood as a Babylonian achievement that Greece named. In the Atlas it is placed in the calendar lens as the **diagnostic object of the lunisolar type**: the specific mechanism that the [[hebrew-calendar|Hebrew]], [[babylonian-calendar|Babylonian]], and (through the computus) [[liturgical-year-christian|Christian]] calendars all share, and against which the independently-computed lunisolar calendars of India, China, and Tibet are measured.

## The cycle in practice

The reconciliation problem the cycle solves is the master fact of the whole calendar lens: the synodic month (~29.53 days) and the tropical year (~365.24 days) are **incommensurable** — twelve lunations fall about eleven days short of a solar year, so a purely lunar year drifts backward through the seasons. The Metonic solution is to let the eleven-day annual shortfall accumulate and pay it off with a whole intercalary month roughly every three years, on a fixed nineteen-year ledger:

- **In Babylon** the scheme became the standard fixed calendar of the Achaemenid and Seleucid periods: seven years in nineteen carry a second Addaru (or, in the older placement, a second Ululu), keeping the New Year month Nisannu near the spring equinox. The working rules descend from the astronomy compiled in **[[astronomy-mul-apin]]**.
- **In the fixed Hebrew calendar** the seven leap years fall at cycle-positions **3, 6, 8, 11, 14, 17, 19** (the mnemonic *GUChADZaT*), each adding a second Adar (Adar II) so that Pesach stays in spring (Exodus 12:2). The calculated calendar traditionally credited to **Hillel II** (359 CE) is built on this cycle.
- **In the Christian computus** the position of a given year within the nineteen-year cycle is the **Golden Number** (*numerus aureus*, 1–19), from which the date of the Paschal full moon — and hence Easter — is read. The cycle entered the Latin West through **Dionysius Exiguus** (525 CE) and was systematized by **Bede** (*De Temporum Ratione*, 725 CE).
- **The Greek refinement.** Because the cycle is about two hours too long per nineteen years, **Callippus of Cyzicus** (c. -330) proposed the **Callippic cycle**: four Metonic cycles less one day (76 years = 940 months = 27,759 days), yielding a better mean-month value that Hipparchus and Ptolemy used for astronomical reckoning.

The cycle even survives as a physical object: the **[[astronomy-antikythera-mechanism|Antikythera mechanism]]** (c. -150 to -100) carries the Metonic cycle as a five-turn spiral dial of 235 lunar-month cells, with subsidiary dials for the derived Callippic and Saros cycles — the earliest surviving machine that gear-encodes the 19-year reconciliation.

## Cross-tradition significance (transmission vs convergence)

The Metonic cycle is one of the cleanest case-studies in the whole Atlas of the distinction between a **traceable lineage** and an **independent rediscovery**, because it is both at once depending on which branch one follows.

**Securely documented transmission.** The line **Babylon → Greece → Judaism → Christendom** is genuine history, not resemblance. The fixed 7-in-19 scheme is attested in Babylonian cuneiform intercalation practice before Meton (Britton 2007; Stern 2012); the fixed [[hebrew-calendar|Hebrew calendar]]'s intercalation descends from that Babylonian-derived tradition rather than from an independent Jewish computation (Stern 2001); and the Christian **computus** carries the same nineteen-year cycle forward as the Golden Number, through Alexandrian Easter-tables, Dionysius Exiguus, and Bede into the medieval Latin West (Richards 1998). One astronomical fact, handed down through four calendrical cultures.

**Independent convergence.** The Chinese lunisolar calendar reached the **identical** 19-year / 7-intercalary-month ratio — the *zhāng* (章) cycle — by its own observational route, with no contact with the Babylonian–Greek tradition (Martzloff 2016). Here the shared period is not borrowing but two civilizations independently finding the best small-integer solution to the same sky. The [[hindu-panchang-calendar|Hindu]] and [[tibetan-phugpa-calendar|Tibetan]] lunisolar calendars, by contrast, decline the fixed cycle altogether and compute intercalation from their own planetary astronomy — the same *type*, a different *mechanism*. The honest lesson the cycle teaches is exactly the comparative hub's: **lunisolar recurrence across cultures is mostly convergence on a real constraint; only a few specific lineages (this one among them) are actual transmission.**

## Disputes

- **Did Meton learn the cycle from Babylon, or rediscover it?** That the fixed 7-in-19 scheme was in Babylonian use before -432 is not seriously contested; what is debated is the channel to Athens. Neugebauer (1975) treated Meton's cycle as substantially an independent Greek result grounded in his own solstice observation, while Stern (2012) and others emphasize the Babylonian priority of the *fixed* scheme and the plausibility of eastern astronomical contact. The safe statement: **the Babylonian priority of the regularized cycle is established; the mechanism of its arrival in Greece is not.**
- **When did Babylonian intercalation become fully regular?** The transition from *ad hoc* royal-decree intercalation to a rigid 19-year rule is placed somewhere in the Achaemenid period, but the exact date at which the scheme became strictly fixed (rather than conventionally regular) is contested in the cuneiform record (Britton 2007) — hence the -499-to-380 window used here rather than a single year.
- **Its identity as a "calendar."** The Metonic cycle is a reckoning *rule*, not a stand-alone calendar with months and an epoch; it is typed here as a calendar-system node because it is the shared intercalation engine of the lunisolar calendars in this lens and the natural comparative slot for the 19-year cycle, distinct from the [[babylonian-calendar|Babylonian calendar]] that first regularized it.
