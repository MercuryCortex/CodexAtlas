# Egyptian Civil Calendar (365-day Solar) + Sothic Cycle

The Egyptian civil calendar — **365 days exactly, organized as 12 months × 30 days + 5 epagomenal "days outside the year"** — is the first solar calendar in world history, attested from Egyptian Old Kingdom (~-2800) and continuously operating until the Roman / Coptic reforms.

## MASSIVE-WIN — the first solar calendar

The Egyptian civil calendar is one of the most-consequential calendrical inventions in human history. Three structural-historical points:

1. **First documented 365-day civil year** — every other Mediterranean / Near Eastern major calendar of the same period (Mesopotamian, Babylonian, Hebrew) is lunisolar with seasonal intercalation. The Egyptian decision to use a 365-day exact solar year — without any leap-year correction — is unique among Bronze Age calendars
2. **Source of the 365-day structure that propagates** to Julian + Gregorian via the Roman adoption (Caesar's 46 BCE reform incorporated the 365-day Egyptian structure + added the 4-year leap-day correction the Egyptians had refused)
3. **Independent of the lunar calendar in everyday use** — Egyptian astronomers maintained separate observational-lunar calendars for ritual + temple purposes; the civil 365-day was the *administrative-bureaucratic* calendar. The duality reflects Egyptian comfort with parallel calendar systems for different domains

## The Sothic cycle

The defining astronomical feature: the **Sothic cycle of 1,460 years** (1,461 × 365 = 1,461 × 365; the 365-day civil year drifts through all seasons; the heliacal rising of Sirius / Sothis returns to its calendrical position every 1,460 years).

Egyptian civilization had observed from at least the Old Kingdom that the Nile flood (the agricultural event of supreme importance) correlated annually with the heliacal rising of Sirius (the brightest star, observable just before dawn in late summer). The 365-day civil year was originally aligned so that Wepet Renpet (New Year, civil 1st month 1st day) coincided with Sothic-rising + Nile-flood. Over centuries the civil year drifted through the seasons; the Sothic cycle is the period for the alignment to recur.

The Sothic cycle is one of the **principal anchor-points of ancient Mediterranean chronology**: Sothic-rising observations from specific reigns can be cross-referenced against the 1,460-year cycle to date Egyptian + (via cross-references) Mesopotamian + Hittite + Levantine + Greek chronologies. Hornung-Krauss-Warburton 2006 is the canonical chronological-reconstruction work.

## Why no leap-year correction?

The Egyptians knew the civil year drifted; they declined to correct it. The 238 BCE Canopus Decree (Ptolemy III) attempted to introduce a 6th epagomenal day every 4 years (the structural precursor of the Roman leap-day). The reform FAILED — Egyptian priesthoods + bureaucratic conservatism rejected it. The Egyptian civil calendar retained its 365-day structure until the Roman conquest.

Hypotheses for the no-leap-year decision:
- **Ritual-administrative regularity**: the 365-day exact structure made bureaucratic-fiscal calculations straightforward (taxes, regnal dates, mortuary contracts)
- **Religious continuity**: certain festival positions (especially the Osirian + Sokar mortuary cycles) had become culturally fixed at specific civil dates; correcting the calendar would disrupt these
- **Sothic-cycle theology**: the 1,460-year cycle was understood + accepted as the larger-scale anchor; the in-cycle drift was tolerated

## Cross-tradition position

The Egyptian civil calendar is the foundational point of multiple cross-tradition calendar lineages:

1. **Julian + Gregorian via Roman adoption** — Caesar's 46 BCE Julian reform took the Egyptian 365-day structure + added the 4-year leap-day correction. Gregorian (1582) refines the leap-year rule further. The 365-day-year structure of the modern global civil calendar traces directly to Egyptian invention
2. **Coptic calendar** — direct surviving Egyptian-civil descendant. The Coptic calendar (still used by the Coptic Orthodox Church + Ethiopian Tewahedo Church) retains the 12-month × 30-day + 5 epagomenal structure with Julian-style leap-year correction added
3. **Ethiopian Ge'ez calendar** — Coptic-derived; 13 months (12 × 30 + Pagumē 5-or-6 days); 7-8 year offset from Gregorian. See `ethiopian-geez-calendar.md` for the Ethiopian articulation
