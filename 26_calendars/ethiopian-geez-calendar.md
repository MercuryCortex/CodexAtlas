# Ethiopian Ge'ez Calendar (13-month Coptic-derived)

The official civil + religious calendar of Ethiopia and Eritrea, used continuously since the 4th-century Aksumite Christianization. The calendar is a direct descendant of the Egyptian-Coptic calendrical tradition (introduced via [[frumentius]]'s 4th-c. Aksumite mission from Alexandria) and preserves the **13-month structure** (12 × 30-day months + a 5-or-6-day Pagumē intercalary period) inherited from the [[egyptian-civil-calendar|Egyptian civil calendar]] with Julian-style leap-year correction added.

## Distinctive features

The Ethiopian calendar has three internationally-distinctive features:

1. **13 months instead of 12** — the 12 regular 30-day months + the *Pagumē* (5 or 6 days; the term derives from Coptic "additional"). "Ethiopia is the only country with 13 months of sunshine" is the common tourist-board tagline + structurally accurate

2. **~7-8 year offset from Gregorian** — Ethiopian Anno Mundi reckoning places Christ's birth at AM 5500 (Coptic reckons it at AM ~5492; Ethiopian computation differs slightly). The current Ethiopian year is 2018 (Sep 11, 2025 - Sep 10, 2026 Gregorian). Ethiopians celebrated their millennium in September 2007 Gregorian = AM 2000 Ethiopian

3. **Julian-style leap-year rule** — the Ethiopian calendar uses Julian's "every 4 years exactly" rule, NOT the Gregorian "century-exception" rule. So Ethiopian Mäskäräm 1 falls on Sep 11 in pre-Gregorian-leap years and Sep 12 in Gregorian-leap years — a 1-day drift over the 400-year Gregorian century-correction cycle

## Cross-tradition position

The Ethiopian Ge'ez calendar sits at multiple cross-tradition junctures:

1. **Egyptian-Coptic calendrical descent** — direct lineage from the [[egyptian-civil-calendar|Egyptian civil calendar]] via Coptic mediation (~4th c. CE Frumentius mission). The 13-month + 30-day-month structure + Pagumē intercalary preserves the Egyptian template that the Roman / Julian / Gregorian reforms replaced everywhere else
2. **First non-Roman Christian state calendar** — the Aksumite kingdom (see [[aksum]]) was the first Christian state outside the Roman Empire; its calendrical-liturgical institution is the first non-Roman Christian liturgical-calendar with its own continuous tradition
3. **Continuous Aksumite-Tewahedo-liturgical tradition** — the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church has used this calendar continuously since the 4th c. The 13-month structure makes Ethiopian + Eritrean liturgical-time mathematically distinct from every other Christian tradition
4. **Africa's longest-continuously-operating calendar** — the Ethiopian calendar is the most-continuously-used African-civilizational calendar with documented textual tradition

## Festival-cycle distinctness

The Ethiopian festival cycle differs from both Western Christian and Eastern Orthodox patterns:

- **Christmas (Gənna)**: Jan 7 (Gregorian) = Taḫśaś 29 (Ethiopian) — same date as Russian + Serbian + Coptic Orthodox Christmas
- **Epiphany / Timkat**: Jan 19 = Ṭərr 11 — the central Ethiopian Orthodox festival, dwarfing Christmas in scale; reenacts Christ's baptism with massive river-procession liturgies
- **Mäsqäl**: Sep 27 = Mäskäräm 17 — the Finding-of-the-Cross festival commemorating Empress Helena's 326 CE discovery of the True Cross; distinctively prominent Ethiopian observance
- **Ənkutatash**: Sep 11 = Mäskäräm 1 — Ethiopian New Year + Queen of Sheba return-from-Jerusalem-with-Solomon's-gifts tradition (see [[makeda-queen-of-sheba]] + [[solomonic-genealogy]] for the cross-tradition Solomonic dynastic claim that anchors Ethiopian-Imperial identity)
