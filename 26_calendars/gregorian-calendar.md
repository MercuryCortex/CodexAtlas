# Gregorian Calendar (1582 reform)

The current global civil calendar — a solar calendar with the leap-year rule "divisible by 4 except centuries-not-divisible-by-400" — promulgated by Pope Gregory XIII via the bull *Inter Gravissimas* on 24 February 1582. Replaces the `julian-calendar` (Caesar 46 BCE) which had accumulated ~10 days of drift relative to the astronomical solar year by 1582.

## The reform

The Julian calendar's 365.25-day average year was 11 minutes 14 seconds longer than the astronomical tropical year (365.2422 days). Over the 16 centuries between Caesar's 46 BCE reform and Gregory's 1582 reform, the calendar had drifted ~10 days behind the actual seasons. The reform did two things:

1. **Dropped 10 days**: Thursday 4 October 1582 was followed by Friday 15 October 1582. The skip-10-days correction realigned the spring equinox to its 4th-c. Council of Nicaea date (~March 21), critical for computing Easter
2. **Refined leap-year rule**: Julian had a leap year every 4 years exactly (100 leap years per 400 years). Gregorian: 97 leap years per 400 years (excludes century-years not divisible by 400). New average year = 365.2425 days, accurate to ~26 seconds vs astronomical year

The reform was authored mathematically by Aloysius Lilius + refined by the Jesuit astronomer Christopher Clavius (who later corresponded with Galileo on telescopic observations).

## Adoption timeline

Catholic countries adopted immediately or within a year (Spain, Portugal, Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, much of Italy in 1582; France December 1582). Protestant + Orthodox countries resisted for political-religious reasons:

- **Protestant Germany**: 1700 (Protestant states); Sweden took a botched approach over 1700-1753
- **Britain + colonies (including American colonies)**: 1752 — the famous "11 days skipped" (Wednesday Sept 2 → Thursday Sept 14, 1752); some popular protest ("Give us back our 11 days!"), but this rioting may be mythologized
- **Russia**: 1918 (post-Bolshevik) — the "October Revolution" of November 1917 reflects Russia still on Julian
- **Greece**: 1923 (the last European country)
- **Eastern Orthodox liturgical use**: most Orthodox churches still use Julian for liturgical purposes (Russian Orthodox + Serbian Orthodox + Coptic Orthodox use Julian-derived liturgical calendars; Greek Orthodox adopted "Revised Julian" in 1923 which matches Gregorian until 2800)

## The Anno Domini epoch

The Gregorian calendar inherits its **AD/CE epoch** from Dionysius Exiguus (525 CE), who established the convention of counting years from the Incarnation of Christ. His date for the Incarnation is widely thought to be ~4-6 years too late (Herod the Great died in 4 BCE; Jesus must have been born before Herod's death per the Matthean infancy narrative). The epoch is therefore *historically inaccurate* but has become the dominant global civil-time convention regardless.

## Cross-tradition position

The Gregorian calendar's reach as the **global civil calendar** is a cross-tradition fact: every country uses it for civil + diplomatic + economic purposes regardless of religious calendrical traditions in use. China + Japan + Korea + the Islamic world + India + every other major civilization uses Gregorian dates for civil purposes while maintaining traditional calendars for religious-festival purposes. This makes Gregorian the only world calendar with *near-universal civil adoption*, and the only major calendar whose origin is a specific dated papal-religious decree.

The reform's mathematical achievement (the 400-year leap rule producing 365.2425-day average accurate to 26 seconds vs astronomical) is one of the cleanest cases of pre-modern computational accuracy in cross-tradition calendar history.
