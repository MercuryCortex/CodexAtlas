# Hindu Panchang Calendar (Lunisolar with Regional Variants)

A family of lunisolar calendars — collectively termed *Panchang* (पञ्चाङ्ग, "five-limbed" for the five Panchang components: tithi + vara + nakshatra + yoga + karana) — used across the Indian subcontinent in dozens of regional variants. The shared structural framework: lunar months + tithi-based festivals + solar Sankranti markers + Metonic-style intercalation + the cosmological *yuga* framework at the long scale.

## The five Panchang components

Every Hindu calendrical day is described by five astronomical parameters (the *Pancha-anga* = "five limbs"):

1. **Tithi** — the lunar day, defined as 1/30 of the synodic lunar month (12° of moon-sun angular separation). Tithis are the basis for nearly all Hindu festival dating. There are 30 tithis in a lunar month, numbered 1-15 in shukla-paksha (waxing) + 1-15 in krishna-paksha (waning)

2. **Vara** — the weekday (Ravivara = Sunday + Somvara = Monday + ... = same names as Greco-Roman planetary weekday names; the cross-tradition convergence is documented)

3. **Nakshatra** — the lunar mansion: 27 (sometimes 28) named star-clusters dividing the ecliptic. The moon transits one nakshatra per ~1 day. Foundational to Jyotisha (Hindu astrology) + festival auspiciousness

4. **Yoga** — the sun-moon longitudinal-sum division: 27 named yogas, each 13°20' of combined-longitude. Astrological-divinatory significance

5. **Karana** — half-tithi: 11 fixed-karana names + 4 rotating-karana names; 60 karanas per lunar month

The Panchang is the **most-elaborated calendrical computation in any traditional calendrical system** — far more detailed than the Hebrew or Chinese lunisolar systems. Every day in Hindu liturgical-life is described by all five parameters simultaneously.

## The yuga cosmic-scale framework

The Hindu calendar embeds within a **cosmological time-scale of extraordinary scope**:

- **Kali Yuga**: 432,000 years (current age, began 3102 BCE traditionally)
- **Dvapara Yuga**: 864,000 years (prior age)
- **Treta Yuga**: 1,296,000 years
- **Krita (Satya) Yuga**: 1,728,000 years
- **Mahayuga**: 4,320,000 years (full Krita-Treta-Dvapara-Kali cycle)
- **Kalpa**: 1,000 Mahayugas = 4.32 billion years (one cosmic day of Brahma)
- **Brahma's lifetime**: 100 Brahma-years = ~311 trillion human years

This yuga framework provides the deepest cosmological time-scale in any traditional calendrical system. Modern cosmology's age-of-universe (~13.8 billion years) sits within a Brahma-lifetime; the conceptual scale is genuinely cosmic.

## Regional variants

The Hindu calendar exists in dozens of regional / sectarian / community-specific variants:

- **Vikram Samvat**: most common North Indian; epoch 57 BCE; mostly purnimanta (full-moon-to-full-moon months)
- **Shaka Samvat**: Indian National Calendar; epoch 78 CE; basis of modern civil-state Hindu calendar
- **Tamil calendar**: solar Sankranti-based; epoch varies; New Year at Solar Aries-entry (Vaisakhi, April 13-14)
- **Bengali Pôñjika**: solar-anchored; Bengali New Year (Pohela Boishakh) at solar Aries-entry
- **Malayalam Kollam Era**: epoch 825 CE; Kerala-specific
- **Telugu Ugadi**: lunar New Year at Chaitra shukla pratipada
- **Maharashtrian Gudi Padwa**: same as Telugu Ugadi
- **Jain calendar (Vira Nirvana Samvat)**: epoch -527 (Mahavira's nirvana); Jain-community-specific
- **Sikh Nanakshahi**: epoch 1469 CE (Guru Nanak's birth); recent (1999) reform

## Cross-tradition position

The Hindu calendar sits at multiple cross-tradition junctures:

1. **Lunisolar family** with Babylonian / Hebrew / Chinese — independent emergence of Metonic-style intercalation in three separate cultural-astronomical traditions
2. **Yuga cosmological-time as Indian distinctive** — no other major traditional calendar provides comparable cosmological-time-scale depth (Mayan Long Count is the closest parallel at ~5,125 years; Hindu yuga at trillions of years dwarfs it)
3. **Weekday-name cross-tradition convergence** — Hindu vara names (Ravivara = Sun + Somvara = Moon + Mangalavara = Mars + Budhavara = Mercury + Brihaspativara = Jupiter + Shukravara = Venus + Shanivara = Saturn) correspond exactly to Greco-Roman planetary-weekday names. Documented transmission via Hellenistic astrological contact (~+200 onward; Yavanajataka)
4. **Mathematical-astronomical sophistication** — Aryabhata's 476 CE *Aryabhatiya* contains heliocentric-adjacent insights, irrational-number-correct π approximation, accurate planetary periods; the Indian astronomical tradition is one of the most-mathematically-advanced pre-modern traditions
