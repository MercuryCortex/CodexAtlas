# Soul Immortality

The soul (psychē, ātman, nepheš in some traditions, ruh) survives bodily death intrinsically — by its nature — rather than being preserved by a divine miracle, resurrected from death, or absorbed. Distinguished from (a) bodily resurrection (the dead body is restored — Jewish/Christian/Islamic mainstream); (b) ancestor-shade survival (the dead persist as diminished shades in an underworld — Homeric Greek, pre-exilic Israelite); (c) absorption (the individual soul dissolves into a higher unity — strong Advaita Vedānta).

## Manifestations
- **Pre-Homeric & Homeric Greek:** [[phase-2-008-homeric-epics]] — the *psychē* is a thin shade that departs at death to a bleak Hades. Not "immortal" in any robust sense — barely existent. The classical position evolves *away* from this.
- **Pythagorean and Orphic:** Metempsychosis — the soul transmigrates across bodies; soul existed before birth, will exist after death, cycles through many incarnations until liberation. Earliest clear Greek doctrine of robust soul-survival.
- **Plato:** [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — *Phaedo* presents four arguments for the immortality of the soul (cyclical, recollection, affinity, form of life). *Phaedrus* myth of the charioteer; *Republic* 10 Myth of Er; *Timaeus* — the soul is "akin to" the eternal Forms. The doctrine becomes Platonic orthodoxy. Among the most influential sources for later Western philosophical and theological reflection on the soul.
- **Vedic / Upanishadic:** [[phase-2-012-brihadaranyaka-upanishad]] — *ātman* is brahman; the soul's true nature is identical with ultimate reality. Survival is presupposed; liberation (*mokṣa*) is from the cycle of rebirths, not survival per se. Distinct from Platonic in not making individuation a feature of the surviving soul.
- **Hellenistic Judaism:** [[phase-3-012-wisdom-of-solomon]] 3:1: "the souls of the righteous are in the hand of God"; Wisdom marries Platonic soul-immortality to biblical theology. [[phase-3-019-4-maccabees]] develops martyrological soul-immortality (the seven martyred sons go to the patriarchs).
- **Philo:** Soul-immortality as Platonic doctrine integrated with Mosaic Law.
- **Christian dispute:** Pauline and Synoptic Jesus tradition emphasize bodily *resurrection*, not Platonic soul-immortality. Patristic synthesis fuses both (Augustine: soul-immortality between death and resurrection). The 20th-c. critique (Cullmann 1958, "Immortality of the Soul or Resurrection of the Dead?") sharpened the distinction.
- **Plotinian Neoplatonism:** [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — the rational soul is undescended and eternal; embodiment is partial. The most metaphysically robust version of Greek soul-immortality.

## Disputes
- **Hebrew Bible.** Whether ANY Hebrew Bible texts teach robust soul-immortality (vs. shadowy Sheol-existence) is contested. Most current scholarship: the explicit doctrine first appears in Hellenistic Jewish texts under Greek influence (Wisdom, 4 Macc).
- **Plato-Pythagorean priority.** Whether Plato got soul-doctrine from Pythagoreans or developed it more or less independently — old debate; current consensus accepts substantial Pythagorean inheritance plus Platonic development.
