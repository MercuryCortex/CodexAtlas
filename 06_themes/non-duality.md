# Non-duality

The collapse of the subject-object distinction, or of any ultimate two-ness (self/world, knower/known, creature/creator). Distinct from monism (which is an *ontological* claim about how-many-substances) and from pantheism (which identifies God with the world). Non-duality is typically a *soteriological-experiential* claim: the apparent duality is *grasped through* in realization, after which it is seen never to have been real. The motif appears independently across at least four major traditions (Advaita, Mahāyāna, Sufism, Christian Neoplatonism) and the convergence is itself a major comparative-religion question.

## Manifestations
- **Vedic / Upanishadic seed:** [[phase-2-012-brihadaranyaka-upanishad]], [[phase-2-013-chandogya-upanishad]] — *tat tvam asi* ("that thou art"); *ayam ātmā brahma* ("this self is Brahman"). The earliest formulation of identity between innermost self and ultimate reality.
- **Advaita Vedānta:** [[phase-5-005-shankara-brahma-sutra-bhasya]] — Śaṅkara's systematic non-dualism: Brahman alone is real; the apparent world is *māyā*; liberation is recognition of one's identity with Brahman. The locus classicus.
- **Kashmir Shaivism / Tantric non-dualism:** [[phase-5-006-vijnana-bhairava-tantra]], [[phase-5-014-abhinavagupta-tantraloka]] — Śiva-Śakti non-duality; everything is the play of consciousness recognizing itself.
- **Mahāyāna *śūnyatā*:** [[phase-5-002-heart-sutra]], [[phase-5-004-platform-sutra-huineng]] — emptiness as the dependent-arising of all phenomena; "form is emptiness, emptiness is form." Not metaphysical monism (everything is one substance) but the dismantling of substantial selfhood as such; related to non-duality but not identical (Loy 1988 maps the differences).
- **Daoist:** [[phase-2-014-daodejing]] — the *Dao* before naming, before the ten-thousand-things; the sage acts non-actingly (*wu wei*) by not standing apart from the natural flow.
- **Neoplatonic One:** [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — the One is beyond all multiplicity, including the subject-object structure; mystical union (*henosis*) is the dropping of the perceiver-perceived distinction. The Greek philosophical analogue of Eastern non-dualism (Loy explicitly compares).
- **Sufi *wahdat al-wujūd*:** [[phase-5-021-ibn-arabi-fusus-al-hikam]] — Ibn ʿArabī's "Unity of Being": only God truly exists; creatures are *theophanies*, modulations of the one Being. Hugely controversial within Sunni orthodoxy.
- **Christian Neoplatonist mystics:** [[phase-5-008-eriugena-periphyseon]], [[phase-5-028-meister-eckhart-sermons]] — Eriugena's *Periphyseon* makes God both creator and creation; Eckhart's *Gottheit*-beyond-God dissolves the distinction between divine ground and human ground ("the eye with which I see God is the same eye with which God sees me"). Both condemned by orthodoxy.
- **Hesychast counter:** [[phase-5-030-palamas-triads]] — Eastern Orthodox theology defends mystical union *while* preserving creature-Creator distinction via the essence/energies distinction; an explicit theological resistance to non-dualism within a tradition otherwise sympathetic to deification.

## Disputes
- **Are Asian and Western non-dualisms the same experience?** Forman (1990, 1999) argues for a "pure consciousness event" cross-culturally; the constructivist response (Katz 1978, Proudfoot) insists experience is always tradition-shaped and the appearance of cross-cultural sameness is a modern theosophical construction.
- **Non-dualism vs. śūnyatā.** Loy 1988 — Madhyamaka emptiness is *not* a positive non-dual reality but a methodical dismantling of all positive metaphysical claims; lumping it with Vedānta is a Neo-Vedantic conflation.
- **Perennialist reading.** Schuon, Coomaraswamy, Huxley made non-duality the convergence point of all "esoteric" mysticism; this is the [[perennial-philosophy]] claim, criticized by Sedgwick and Hanegraaff as a 20th-century construction, not a discovery.
