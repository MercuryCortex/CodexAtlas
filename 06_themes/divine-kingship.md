# Divine Kingship

The king is identified with, descended from, chosen by, or made the earthly representative of the gods. Spectrum: from pharaonic god-king (Egypt) to god-appointed-king (Mesopotamia, later Israel) to divinized-after-death (Roman imperial cult).

## Variants
- **Strong (Egypt):** Pharaoh IS Horus in life, becomes Osiris in death. See [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]].
- **Mediated (Mesopotamia):** Kings serve gods; some claim divine sonship (Naram-Sin) but most rule "by command of" Anu/Enlil/Marduk. See [[phase-1-007-code-of-hammurabi]] prologue.
- **Covenantal (Israelite):** King anointed by Yahweh; sonship language (Ps 2:7) used metaphorically.
- **Imperial (Rome):** Augustus and successors divinized; cult enforced.

## Frankfort's thesis
Henri Frankfort (1948) drew the now-classic distinction between Egyptian "the king is a god" and Mesopotamian "the king is the chosen of the gods." Subsequent scholarship complicates this binary but the framing remains foundational.
