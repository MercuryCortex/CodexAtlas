# The Numinous (Mysterium Tremendum)

Rudolf Otto's category (*Das Heilige*, 1917) for the *non-rational, non-ethical* dimension of religious experience — the "wholly other" (*ganz andere*) confronted as *mysterium tremendum et fascinans*: an awful, fascinating, terrifying mystery that simultaneously repels (tremendum) and attracts (fascinans). Otto coined "numinous" (from Latin *numen*) to name what is left of religion when ethical-rational content is bracketed. The category was foundational for 20th-century phenomenology of religion (Eliade, van der Leeuw, Wach) and for [[hierophany]] (Eliade's term for the manifestation of the sacred).

- [[phase-7-011-idea-of-the-holy]] — Otto's monograph; chapters on *mysterium tremendum* (the awe-element), *fascinans* (the attraction-element), and *augustum* (the worth/weight element).
- [[phase-7-022-sacred-and-profane]] — Eliade's development of Otto: the *sacred* manifests in *hierophanies*, breaking into profane space-time and ordering it. Eliade explicitly cites Otto as the starting point.

The category remains contested. Critics (Proudfoot, Wayne Proudfoot 1985 *Religious Experience*) argued that Otto's "numinous" is itself a culturally-shaped construction — there is no pre-conceptual numinous experience independent of the interpretive frameworks the subject already holds. Defenders insist on the cross-cultural recurrence of *mysterium tremendum* phenomenology.
