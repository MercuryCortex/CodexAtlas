# Cosmopolitanism (Hellenistic)

The Stoic-Cynic doctrine that the wise person is *kosmopolitēs* — "citizen of the cosmos" — bound by natural-rational ties to all rational beings, rather than only to the city of birth. Diogenes the Cynic when asked his city replied "the world." Zeno's *Republic* envisioned a single ideal city without political borders, currency, or temples; later Stoa (Chrysippus, Cicero, Seneca, Marcus Aurelius) maintained the doctrine in less radical form. The doctrine fed the Hellenistic-Jewish universalizing impulse (Philo's *citizen of the great city of the world*) and the Pauline "neither Jew nor Greek" framing.

- [[phase-3-005-stoic-foundational-texts]] — the locus classicus; Cicero *De Officiis*, Seneca *Epistles*, Marcus Aurelius *Meditations* IV.4 ("if intelligence is common to us all, then so is the reason... we are fellow citizens").
- [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]] — Philo's adoption: the wise person belongs to the *megalopolis*, the great city of the world; Mosaic Law is the law of the cosmos.
- [[phase-3-019-4-maccabees]] — Stoic-Hellenistic philosophical-rhetorical framing of Torah-faithful martyrs as paragons of *autocrateia* in service of universal reason.

The doctrine has modern reception in Kant's *Toward Perpetual Peace*, in 20th-century human-rights discourse, and in contemporary global-ethics theory (Appiah 2006 *Cosmopolitanism*).
