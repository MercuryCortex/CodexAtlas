# Ḥanīf monotheism

## Definition
*Ḥanīf* (Arabic *ḥanīf*, حنيف; plural *ḥunafāʾ*) is the **Quranic term for a pre-Islamic Arabian monotheist following the religion of Abraham (*millat Ibrāhīm*)** — neither Jewish nor Christian, but worshipping the one God in the Abrahamic-monotheist tradition that the Quran self-identifies as recovering. The term is doubly load-bearing: (1) it is the Quran's preferred **self-designation of Abraham** (Quran 2:135, 3:67, 3:95, 6:79, 6:161, 16:120-123, 22:31 — Abraham as "*ḥanīf muslim*, not of the polytheists"); (2) it is the Islamic-tradition designation for **late-antique pre-Islamic Arabian individuals** said to have rejected polytheism in favor of Abrahamic-style monotheism on their own initiative, before or contemporary with Muhammad — the *ḥunafāʾ*: Waraqa ibn Nawfal (Khadija's Christian cousin), Zayd ibn ʿAmr ibn Nufayl (rejected Meccan idolatry, traveled in search of the true religion of Abraham), Quss ibn Saʿida al-Iyadi (legendary Christian-Arab bishop and *kāhin* preacher remembered by Muhammad), Khalid ibn Sinan (legendary Banu ʿAbs prophet). The *ḥanīf*-concept is the **theological hinge** by which the Quran positions itself as a recovery of original Abrahamic monotheism, neither novel (since Abraham was *ḥanīf*) nor derivative-from-Judaism-or-Christianity (since Abraham preceded Moses and Jesus).

## Earliest attestations
- **Quran (~610-632 CE)** — the principal textual attestation. The *ḥanīf* term appears 12 times in the Quran. Key passages:
  - **2:135** "They say, 'Be Jews or Christians and you will be guided.' Say: 'No — rather, the religion of Abraham, *ḥanīfan*, and he was not of the polytheists.'"
  - **3:67** "Abraham was neither a Jew nor a Christian, but he was a *ḥanīf muslim*, and he was not of the polytheists."
  - **3:95** "Say: 'God has spoken the truth. Follow the religion of Abraham, *ḥanīfan*, and he was not of the polytheists.'"
  - **6:161** "Say: 'My Lord has guided me to a straight path, a right religion, the religion of Abraham, *ḥanīfan*, and he was not of the polytheists.'"
  - **16:120-123** Abraham described as "*ummah qānit lillāh ḥanīfan*" (a community, devoted to God, *ḥanīf*).
  - **22:31** "*ḥunafāʾa lillāh ghayra mushrikīna bihi*" ("*ḥunafāʾ* to God, not associating partners with him").
- **Pre-Islamic Hijazi memory** (Islamic-tradition transmission, ~7th-9th c. CE) — Ibn Ishaq's *Sirat Rasul Allah* and subsequent Islamic-tradition material name the named *ḥunafāʾ*: Waraqa ibn Nawfal (who recognizes Muhammad's first revelation as authentic prophecy per the canonical Sira-narrative), Zayd ibn ʿAmr ibn Nufayl (Muhammad's contemporary, rejected idolatry, died before the public revelation), Quss ibn Saʿida, Khalid ibn Sinan, and others.
- **Syriac and Greek external attestation** (problematic) — the Syriac cognate *ḥanpā* (plural *ḥanpē*) means "pagan / Gentile" — the *opposite* polarity of the Quranic Arabic. The semantic inversion (Syriac *ḥanpā* "pagan" → Quranic *ḥanīf* "Abrahamic monotheist") is one of the principal source-critical puzzles of the term. Various theories: (a) Arabic semantic specialization preserved a more original "Abrahamic-pre-Mosaic-monotheist" sense lost in Syriac; (b) the Quran deliberately reclaimed a Syriac-Christian polemical term and inverted its valence; (c) the two terms are not actually cognate. No consensus. (Hoyland 2001; Crone 2010.)

## Variants
- **Quranic *ḥanīf***. Abraham's religion; the Quran's self-positioning frame.
- **Islamic-tradition *ḥunafāʾ***. The named individuals (Waraqa, Zayd ibn ʿAmr, Quss, Khalid ibn Sinan) — Islamic-tradition memory of pre-Islamic Arabian monotheists.
- **Donner's "Believer-movement" reading** (Donner 2010). The early Islamic *muʾminūn* "Believers" community originally encompassed *ḥanīf* + sympathetic Jews and Christians under a broad late-antique Arabian monotheist umbrella; the narrowing to "Muslims" specifically is later, post-conquest.
- **Crone-Cook 1977 *Hagarism* revisionist reading** (Tier 4). The *ḥanīf*-tradition is substantially a later Islamic-tradition construction projecting back a pre-Islamic Abrahamic monotheism that did not historically exist in the way Islamic tradition remembers. Tier 4 revisionist; later modified by Crone herself.
- **Mainstream contemporary view** (Hoyland 2001, Donner 2010). Late-antique Arabia had a genuine emergent monotheist milieu including Christian-Arab tribes (Ghassanids, Lakhmids, Najran), Jewish-Arab tribes (Yathrib Jews, Himyar's 4th-c. conversion to Jewish-style monotheism — *Rahmanan*), and unaffiliated Abrahamic-monotheist individuals — the *ḥunafāʾ*-tradition has a real historical core in this milieu, even if the specific named individuals' biographies are partly Islamic-tradition reconstruction.

## Connections claimed
- **Christianity → emergence of Islam**: Waraqa ibn Nawfal, Khadija's cousin, is described in Ibn Ishaq's Sira as a Christian *ḥanīf* who recognizes Muhammad's first revelation as authentic prophecy — the canonical Sira-narrative of the cousin-Christian validating the new prophecy is a load-bearing **Christian-Arab → Muhammad transmission edge** in the Islamic-tradition memory itself.
- **Judaism → emergence of Islam**: Himyar's 4th-c. conversion to Jewish-style monotheism ("Rahmanan") + the Hijazi Jewish tribes (Banu Qaynuqa / Nadir / Qurayza at Medina; Banu Naḍir at Khaybar) provides the Jewish-monotheist substrate. The Quranic *milla* (community)-language and the *qibla* originally toward Jerusalem reflect this.
- **Aksumite-Ethiopian Christianity → emergence of Islam**: Quran 105 (Year of the Elephant) remembers Abreha's Aksumite invasion attempt c. 570; first Muslim hijra to Aksum c. 615; Najran-Christian massacre by Dhū Nuwās 523 (Bowersock 2013). The Christian-Aksumite Arabian milieu is part of the late-antique frame. ([[event-kaleb-aksum-himyar-invasion-525]], wired by `opus-ethiopian-1`.)
- **Direct-borrowing claim**: The Quran's positioning as Abrahamic-pre-Mosaic-pre-Jesuanic monotheism (Abraham as *ḥanīf muslim*, predating Judaism and Christianity) is the **theological frame** that lets Islam claim primary continuity with Abrahamic religion while rejecting Jewish and Christian developments as corruptions. This frame is original to the Quran; it has no exact pre-Islamic parallel.
- **Independent-development claim**: Hawting 1999 and the Crone-Cook revisionism emphasize that much of the *ḥanīf*-tradition material is later Islamic-tradition reconstruction; the named *ḥunafāʾ* biographies are partly retrospective construction. Donner 2010 and Hoyland 2001 push back: the late-antique Arabian monotheist milieu is historically real; the *ḥanīf*-tradition has a real core.

## Disputes
- **Etymology of *ḥanīf*** — Arabic-internal (Northwest-Semitic *ḥ-n-p* "incline, turn, lean toward") vs. Syriac-loanword (*ḥanpā* "pagan"). The semantic inversion if Syriac-loaned is the principal puzzle. (Hoyland 2001; de Blois 2002 "Naṣrānī and ḥanīf" *BSOAS*.)
- **Historicity of the named *ḥunafāʾ*** (Waraqa, Zayd ibn ʿAmr, Quss, Khalid ibn Sinan). The Islamic-tradition narratives are later reconstructive; the historical individuals are partly recoverable for some (Waraqa, Zayd ibn ʿAmr) and largely legendary for others (Quss, Khalid ibn Sinan).
- **Donner's "Believers" thesis** — was early Islam a narrowly Muslim movement or a broader late-antique monotheist coalition? (Donner 2010; debated.)
- **Crone-Cook *Hagarism***. Tier 4 revisionist; substantially modified by Crone's later work; cite balanced.
