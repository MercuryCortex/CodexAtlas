# Shamanism hypothesis (in prehistoric ritual)

## Definition
A family of interpretations — primarily associated with David Lewis-Williams and Jean Clottes — that read Upper Paleolithic cave imagery, certain Neolithic ritual contexts, and selected Mesolithic burials (e.g. Hilazon Tachtit) as evidence of *shamanic* practice: altered states of consciousness, neuropsychologically-grounded entoptic imagery, human–animal hybrids (therianthropes) as spirit-flight or transformation, and specialist ritual practitioners. The hypothesis is *one interpretive framework*, not the consensus reading of the archaeological record.

## Diagnostic markers (per the hypothesis)
- Therianthropic figures (human-animal hybrids): Sorcerer of Trois-Frères, Lion-Man of Hohlenstein-Stadel, Sulawesi hunting-scene therianthropes.
- Entoptic patterns (grids, zigzags, nested curves) read as Stage 1 trance imagery.
- Cave depths as liminal / spirit-world spaces.
- Specialist burials with unusual grave-goods (Hilazon Tachtit "shaman" burial, Natufian).

## Earliest attestations claimed
- **Sulawesi** ~45,500 BCE (Leang Bulu' Sipong 4 therianthropic hunting scene, the earliest dated narrative scene + earliest therianthropes) — see [[event-sulawesi-cave-art-c-45500-bce]].
- **Hohlenstein-Stadel** ~38,000 BCE (Löwenmensch / Lion-Man, earliest known anthropomorphic-zoomorphic figurine) — see [[event-hohle-fels-figurines-c-35000-bce]].
- **Chauvet** ~32,000 BCE — see [[event-chauvet-cave-paintings-c-32000-bce]].
- **Lascaux** ~17,000 BCE (the "Bird-Man" of the Shaft) — see [[event-lascaux-cave-paintings-c-17000-bce]].
- **Altamira** ~22,000 BCE — see [[event-altamira-cave-paintings-c-22000-bce]].
- **Hilazon Tachtit (Natufian)** ~12,000 BCE (the proposed "shaman" burial; Grosman et al. 2008 *PNAS*) — see [[event-natufian-burial-traditions-c-12500-9500-bce]].

## Connections claimed
- Direct-borrowing claims: Lewis-Williams 2002 argues for a deep continuity of shamanic practice across the Upper Paleolithic, grounded in the universal neurology of altered states (the "neuropsychological model").
- Independent-development claims: Helvenston & Bahn 2003 argue the entoptic-stages model is methodologically over-applied; cross-cultural ethnographic analogy is too coarse to support specific shamanic readings of specific images. Bahn 2016 surveys the broader critical literature.

## Disputes
This is the load-bearing question. The shamanism reading is **influential but explicitly not consensus** in the archaeological literature.
- **Methodology of entoptic reading.** Helvenston & Bahn 2003 challenge the laboratory neuropsychology basis for inferring trance from prehistoric imagery.
- **Ethnographic over-extension.** Using San / Tukano / Tungus shamanic ethnography to interpret 30,000-year-old French painted caves assumes a continuity of religious practice that is not directly demonstrable.
- **Selective evidence.** Cave imagery includes much that is not therianthropic, geometric, or hidden-deep — and the hypothesis is most compelling on a subset that may not be representative.
- **Alternative readings.** Hunting magic, totemism, mnemonic / narrative-image hypotheses, and skill-display / aesthetic-elaboration readings all compete for the same data.
