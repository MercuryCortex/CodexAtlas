# Religious Persistence Under Conquest

The cross-tradition pattern of **minority religious-tradition survival** through repeated host-society political + religious transformation. The theme covers two principal sub-patterns:

1. **In-situ persistence**: the tradition remains in its homeland but becomes a minority under successive conquering populations + religious-political orders. Examples: Iranian-Zoroastrianism (Yazd + Kerman) under Arab + Mongol + Safavid + Pahlavi + Islamic Republic rule; Yazidism in Sunni-Kurdish Iraq; Mandaeism in Iraq-Iran; Samaritanism under Roman + Byzantine + Islamic + Israeli rule.

2. **Diaspora persistence**: the tradition migrates from its homeland to a new host-society, preserving liturgical-language + ritual-practice across the migration. Examples: the Parsi diaspora in Gujarat → Mumbai → global (9th c. → present); the Jewish diaspora across the entire post-Second-Temple period.

## Mechanisms of persistence

Common mechanisms documented across the case-studies (Hinnells 2005; Choksy 1997; Guest 1987; Buckley 2002; Palsetia 2001):

- **Liturgical-language preservation** even when no longer spoken vernacularly (Avestan + Pahlavi for Zoroastrians; Hebrew for Jews; Mandaic for Mandaeans).
- **Ritual-practice continuity** even under political-religious pressure (the Atash Behram fires; Yazidi quintile fasts; Mandaean baptismal practice).
- **Geographic concentration** in defensible / peripheral regions (Yazd-Kerman desert; Jabal Sinjar; the southern Iraqi marshes).
- **Endogamous boundary-maintenance** + traditional non-acceptance of conversion (Parsi non-acceptance is the classic case).
- **Negotiated accommodation** with host-society religious-political authorities (the Qissa-i Sanjan "five conditions"; the Ottoman *millet* system).

## Costs of persistence

The persistence-strategies have documented costs (Luhrmann 1996; Hinnells 2005):

- **Demographic decline**: most minority-traditions show shrinking population over time.
- **Loss of theological development**: minority-traditions often preserve an earlier-stratum of doctrine without subsequent theological elaboration.
- **Endogamous-rigidity vs. dilution-risk** trade-off.
