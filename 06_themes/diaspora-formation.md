# Diaspora Formation

**Diaspora formation** designates the process by which a religious, ethnic, or cultural community is forcibly dispersed from its homeland — and in dispersal reconstitutes and often intensifies its identity. The term originates with the Greek *diaspora* ("scattering"), used for the Jewish dispersal; it has since been extended to any forced or voluntary dispersal that generates a self-conscious exile community.

The vault's paradigm case is the **Sephardic diaspora**: the 1492 Expulsion from Spain (and 1497 from Portugal) scattered ~40,000–200,000 Iberian Jews across the Ottoman Empire, North Africa, Italy, and the Netherlands. Rather than dissolving the Sephardic tradition, the dispersal concentrated its intellectual energy: the Safed Kabbalistic school (1540–1580) — Isaac Luria, Joseph Karo, Moses Cordovero, Solomon Alkabets — is populated almost entirely by Expulsion-generation families. The persecution was the proximate cause of the greatest mystical renaissance in Jewish history.

The diaspora pattern also applies to: the Tibetan Buddhist diaspora after 1959 (which brought Tibetan Buddhism to the West), the Armenian diaspora (1915), and the African diaspora in the Americas (which produced Candomblé, Vodou, Santería, and Black church traditions).
