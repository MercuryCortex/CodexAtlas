# Tzimtzum (Divine Contraction)

Isaac Luria's central cosmogonic innovation (Safed, ~+1570): before creation, Ein Sof — the Infinite — *contracted itself* (*tzimtzum*) to create an empty space (*tehiru*) in which finite existence could occur. Creation is not addition but withdrawal; the world exists in the negative space of a divine self-limitation. This solved a theological problem (how can finite reality coexist with infinite divinity?) by introducing a primordial divine self-restriction.

Tzimtzum is the first move in the Lurianic three-act cosmogonic drama: tzimtzum → [[shevirat-hakelim]] (breaking of the vessels) → [[tikkun-olam]] (cosmic repair). Together these form Lurianic Kabbalah's distinctive contribution to Jewish thought after 1492.

Scholem famously argued that tzimtzum is the theological mirror of the Spanish Expulsion: cosmic exile expressing historical exile. Modern process theology (Moltmann, Cobb) has retrieved tzimtzum as a model for divine self-limitation that grounds human freedom.
