# Individuation

C.G. Jung's central technical term for the lifelong psychological-spiritual process by which the conscious ego comes into a working relation with the unconscious and its archetypal contents, integrates the *shadow*, encounters the *anima/animus*, and finally orients toward the *Self* (the archetypal totality of the psyche) as the regulating centre of personality. The "second half of life" task. Distinct from social adaptation, ego-development, or therapeutic symptom-relief; explicitly modeled by Jung on alchemy's *opus*, Christian Christ-as-Self symbolism, and Eastern mandala and meditative traditions. The conceptual axis of post-1928 Jungian psychology.

## Manifestations
- **Jung's alchemical reading:** [[phase-7-017-psychology-and-alchemy]] — Jung's *Psychology and Alchemy* (1944) reads the alchemical *opus* (Nigredo → Albedo → Rubedo → *lapis*) as the projected map of individuation: the alchemist saw in his alembic the stages of his own psychic transformation. Pioneering archetypal hermeneutics.
- **Jung on the Self:** [[phase-7-018-aion]] — *Aion* (1951) treats Christ as a Self-symbol of the Christian aeon, with the Antichrist as the unintegrated shadow-complement; the *imago Dei* is psychologically the *Self*-archetype. The most metaphysically venturesome Jungian text.
- **The coniunctio:** [[phase-7-019-mysterium-coniunctionis]] — Jung's *Mysterium Coniunctionis* (1955–56), his magnum opus, follows the alchemical *coniunctio* (sacred marriage) of opposites as the symbol of the individuation telos: integration of conscious-unconscious, masculine-feminine, light-shadow.
- **Theological provocation:** [[phase-7-020-answer-to-job]] — *Answer to Job* (1952) reframes the Job story psychologically: God himself undergoes individuation, integrating his shadow through incarnation. Notoriously scandalous; refused by Catholic theologians, celebrated by Protestant existentialists.

## Disputes
- **Scientific status.** Mainstream academic psychology has never integrated Jungian individuation as a research construct; it survives in clinical Jungian-analytic practice, comparative religion, and depth-psychological humanities. Its metaphysical commitments (collective unconscious, archetypes-in-themselves) put it outside falsifiable empirical psychology — a feature in Jungian self-presentation, a bug in mainstream.
- **Comparative validity.** Jung's claim that individuation is the deep grammar of religious symbol systems across cultures (alchemy, Tantra, Christology, Sufi *waḥdat*, dreams of Pueblo Indians) has been admired and disputed. The post-Jung generation (Hillman, Edinger, Corbin) extends and modifies. Critics (Noll 1994, *The Jung Cult*) attack the historical-cultural matrix as a Romantic-occultist hybrid; defenders (Shamdasani 2003) defend Jung's intellectual seriousness.
- **Spiritual-bypass risk.** Individuation as therapeutic *telos* has been co-opted in self-help / New Age contexts in ways that strip its hard-edged self-confrontation; the Jungian-analytic establishment generally distances itself from popular Jungian "self-discovery" rhetoric.
