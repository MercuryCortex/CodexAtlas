# Progressive Revelation

The doctrine — most fully articulated in the Baha'i Faith but found in dispensationalist Christian theology, in Islamic concepts of *naskh* (abrogation) and prophetic succession, and elsewhere — that divine revelation is *progressive*: each authentic founder-prophet (Krishna, Moses, Zoroaster, Buddha, Jesus, Muhammad, the Bab, Baha'u'llah in the Baha'i scheme) brings a revelation suited to the spiritual maturity of their age, with each successive revelation building on and modifying the previous. The doctrine resolves the problem of religious pluralism (all the great religions are authentic divine revelations) while preserving the privilege of the most recent (its dispensation is the appropriate one for the current age).

- [[phase-7-002-kitab-i-iqan]] — Baha'u'llah's *Book of Certitude* is the foundational Baha'i theological exposition.
- [[phase-7-003-kitab-i-aqdas]] — Baha'u'llah's *Most Holy Book* establishes the Baha'i dispensation's law.

Christian-dispensationalist variants (Darby's seven dispensations; later premillennialism) operate on a structurally similar progressive-revelation framework, though with a Christian-particularist final dispensation.
