# Ungrund (Boehme's Groundless)

Jakob Boehme's term (*Aurora* 1612, *Mysterium Magnum* 1623) for the *groundless ground* prior to God's self-distinction — the abyss within or before divinity from which the divine Persons articulate themselves and from which creation flows. Boehme's *Ungrund* is the dark *Will*-without-object, the *Centrum Naturae* of fire that finds itself only by self-othering and producing light. The doctrine is the heart of Boehmian theosophy: a tragic-becoming within the divine itself, with God's "own" interior darkness preceding the resolution of the divine Persons.

- [[phase-6-017-boehme-aurora-mysterium-magnum]] — *Aurora* (1612), *Three Principles of the Divine Essence* (1619), and *Mysterium Magnum* (1623) develop the *Ungrund* doctrine.

The doctrine had vast subsequent reception:
- Schelling's late philosophy (*Freiheitsschrift* 1809) explicitly retrieves Boehme's *Ungrund* as the "dark ground" in God against which the will of love articulates itself.
- Hegel's account of the negative as internal to spirit owes something to Boehme.
- Berdyaev makes *Ungrund* a central category of his existentialist Christian philosophy.
- Tillich's "ground of being," Heidegger's *Ab-grund*, and 20th-century theology-of-divine-suffering (Moltmann's *crucified God*) all stand in the Boehmian inheritance.
