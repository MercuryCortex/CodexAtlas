# Ecclesiology

The Christian theological category of the doctrine of *the Church* — its nature, marks (one, holy, catholic, apostolic — the Nicene Creed's four), structure (episcopal, presbyteral, congregational, charismatic), relation to Israel, relation to the world, eschatological status, and visibility/invisibility. Major historical debates: Cyprian's "no salvation outside the church" (3rd c.); Donatist vs. Augustinian (the visible-vs.-invisible church; the dependence of sacrament-validity on minister-purity); medieval high-papal ecclesiology; Reformation invisible-church-of-the-elect; Anglican branch-theory; Vatican II's "people of God" framing.

- [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Pauline body-of-Christ ecclesiology (1 Cor 12; Eph 1, 4; Col 1); the church as Christ's body, his fullness.
- [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] — Lukan ecclesiology: the Spirit-empowered Pentecostal community spreading from Jerusalem to Rome.
- [[phase-4-023-irenaeus-against-heresies]] — Irenaean apostolic-succession ecclesiology; "where the church is, there is the Spirit of God."
- [[phase-4-029-augustine-city-of-god]] — Augustine's two-cities ecclesiology; the visible church a mixed body (corpus mixtum) until the eschaton.

Ongoing 20th-c. and 21st-c. ecumenical-ecclesiological convergence (Faith and Order Commission, *Baptism, Eucharist and Ministry* 1982, ARCIC documents).
