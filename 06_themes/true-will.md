# True Will (Thelema)

The central ethical-metaphysical category of Aleister Crowley's Thelema: each person has a *True Will* — a unique, divinely-given vocational orientation — which it is their highest moral imperative to discover and execute, in complete alignment ("do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law" — *AL* I.40). Distinguished sharply from mere desire, whim, or self-indulgence: True Will is the discovered cosmological assignment of one's *Holy Guardian Angel*, the self's higher-self / daimōn. The doctrine combines Hellenistic-philosophical *eudaimonia* (the daimōn-life), Gnostic-Hermetic self-as-divine-spark themes, and Nietzschean self-overcoming. Crowley elaborated the doctrine across the Thelemic corpus, especially *The Book of the Law* (1909), *Liber Magick in Theory and Practice* (1929), and the *Liber Aleph*.

- [[phase-7-008-book-of-the-law]] — the foundational Thelemic revelation, channeled at Cairo in April 1904; the locus of "do what thou wilt."
- [[phase-7-016-magick-in-theory-and-practice]] — Crowley's systematic treatise; the operational manual for discovering and pursuing True Will.

Modern Thelemic interpretation: Will is not antinomian-libertine ("do whatever you feel like") — that would be the *false* will of egoic desire; True Will requires sustained ascetic-magical discipline (the Great Work, *Knowledge and Conversation of the Holy Guardian Angel*) to discover and execute. The doctrine has shaped subsequent ceremonial-magical and Left-Hand-Path movements.
