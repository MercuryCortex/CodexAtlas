# Atonement Reinterpreted

Counter-orthodox interpretations of the Christ-event in which the **death does not function as substitutionary sacrifice**. Several recurrent patterns:

- **Docetic passion** — Christ only *appeared* to suffer; the body was illusion or a vacated shell. The Second Treatise of the Great Seth (NHC VII,2) has Christ describe Simon of Cyrene as crucified in his place while he himself looks on, laughing. The Apocalypse of Peter (NHC VII,3) similarly bifurcates the suffering Jesus from the living, untouched Christ.
- **Atonement-as-revelation/gnosis** — the Christ event is salvific because it reveals the divine spark's true origin; saving knowledge supplants saving sacrifice. See [[gnosis-as-salvation]].
- **Recapitulation re-routed** — the *Gospel of Truth* (Valentinian) treats the cross as the publishing of the Father's name, an epistemic-cosmic event rather than a juridical one.

- [[phase-4-001-gospel-of-thomas]] — no passion narrative at all; salvation is by finding the interpretation of the sayings.
- [[phase-4-003-gospel-of-truth]] — the cross as cosmic publication of the secret name.
- [[phase-4-015-kephalaia-of-the-teacher]] — Manichaean Christology: Jesus the Splendor as a luminous-revealer figure; the crucifixion has cosmological-mythological roles distinct from sacrificial atonement.
- Orthodox polemic: Irenaeus *Adv. haer.* III.16–22 vigorously defends the reality of Christ's flesh and death against these reinterpretations.

The contrast with [[crucifixion-theology]] is the diagnostic axis.
