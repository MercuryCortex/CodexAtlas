# Round Church as Anastasis Imitatio

## The motif

A **round (or polygonal-naved) church**, often (but not always) with a centralised plan and ambulatory, **built as an architectural memorial of the [[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre-jerusalem|Anastasis Rotunda]] of Jerusalem**. The motif's defining features:

- **Centralised plan** (circular, octagonal, polygonal — not basilical-rectangular).
- **Ambulatory around a central focal point** (echoing the original ambulatory around the empty tomb).
- **Dedication referring to the Holy Sepulchre / Resurrection / Anastasis** (the *iconographic copy* component in Krautheimer's reading).
- **Deliberate programmatic intent** to recall the Holy Sepulchre — usually documented in foundation accounts, dedicatory inscriptions, or chronicle records.

The motif's importance for the project: it is one of the **densest documented architectural-transmission patterns in religious history**. A single originating building (Constantine's Anastasis Rotunda) generates hundreds of imitations across **four traditions** (Byzantine Christian, Latin Christian, Islamic, Ethiopian Tewahedo) over **eight centuries**, with a continuous trail of textual, iconographic, and topographic evidence.

## The propagation

### Phase 1 — The originating building (4th c.)

[[church-of-the-holy-sepulchre-jerusalem|Constantine's Anastasis Rotunda]] (Jerusalem, ~326–335 CE) is the type-specimen. Built over the site of Christ's tomb after the dismantling of the Hadrianic Temple of Venus that had covered the site since ~135 CE. Rotunda + ambulatory + dome over the empty tomb of Christ.

### Phase 2 — Late-antique + early-medieval propagation (5th–10th c.)

Direct architectural imitations begin to appear in the Mediterranean and Western Europe within the first centuries of the Anastasis's existence. Examples: Santo Stefano "Hierusalem" at Bologna (5th c. core), Saint-Bénigne at Dijon (10th c. rotunda — destroyed but documented), the Aachen Palatine Chapel (~800; Charlemagne's centralised octagonal chapel — partial imitation of San Vitale at Ravenna which is itself in the same tradition).

The defining theoretical move (Krautheimer 1942): **medieval *imitatio* does not mean literal geometric copy.** A round nave + a Holy-Sepulchre dedication + a topographic-symbolic reference = architectural "copy" in medieval iconographic terms. The principle propagates the form across vastly different scales, regional materials, and structural systems.

### Phase 3 — Islamic appropriation (7th c.)

[[dome-of-the-rock-jerusalem|The Dome of the Rock]] (691 CE) is the **first major Islamic appropriation of the rotunda-form language**. Built by Caliph [[abd-al-malik|ʿAbd al-Malik]] at the height of the early-Umayyad period, the building takes the centralised-domed-octagonal form and applies it to a different sacred locus (the Foundation Stone) under a different theological vocabulary (Qurʾanic inscriptions, abstract decoration). Oleg Grabar's 1959 essay establishes the building as architectural statement of Umayyad dominion + response to the Anastasis.

The Dome of the Rock then becomes the **architectural relay** for the next phase.

### Phase 4 — Templar round-naved churches (12th–13th c.)

The Crusader-era Templars occupy the Dome of the Rock as their Jerusalem HQ (*Templum Domini*) from 1119 to 1187. When they build oratories in Europe — across France, England, Portugal, Spain, Italy, Germany, the Levant — they build **rotundas**, in deliberate architectural memorial of their Jerusalem complex. The Templar round-naved church becomes a **distinctive architectural type**:

- [[convent-of-christ-tomar|Tomar Charola]] (Portugal, ~1160) — 16-sided rotunda, the most architecturally elaborate surviving Templar round-naved oratory.
- [[temple-church-london|London Temple Church]] (1185) — 6-bay round nave + later Gothic chancel; the English exemplar.
- **Neuvy-Saint-Sépulcre** (France, ~1045) — pre-Templar but Templar-associated; rotunda + basilical extension.
- **Charroux** (France, ~1100) — three-storeyed rotunda (lost; documented).
- **Cambridge Round Church (Holy Sepulchre)** (1130) — English exemplar; partially surviving.
- **Northampton Round Church** (1116) — English exemplar; surviving.
- **Vera Cruz Church, Segovia** (1208) — Spanish Templar dodecagonal church.

Krautheimer documents dozens of Templar and Templar-associated round-naved churches across Europe. The architectural programme is **continent-scale** and substantially documented.

### Phase 5 — Ethiopian Zagwe parallel (12th–13th c.)

[[lalibela-rock-hewn-churches|Lalibela]] (~1181–1221) is the Ethiopian-Tewahedo parallel — not a single rotunda but a programme of **eleven rock-hewn churches** organised as a **New Jerusalem**, with the site's river renamed *Yordanos* (Jordan), churches named *Bēta Golgotā* and *Bēta Maryām*, and a topographic map of the Holy Land. A different formal solution to the same theological problem.

The chronological coincidence is striking: both Lalibela and the principal Templar round-naved churches (Tomar, London, Cambridge, Segovia) are built in the same generation, in response to the same underlying religious-historical pressure — **the Saladin-era restrictions on Jerusalem pilgrimage** ([[event-first-crusade-1095-1099]] terminal phase; Saladin recaptures Jerusalem 1187). When the original is unreachable, two different Christian world-regions independently arrive at the same architectural solution: **build the Holy Sepulchre in copy at home**.

### Phase 6 — Post-Templar afterlife (14th c. onwards)

After the [[event-suppression-of-templars-1307-1312|suppression of the Templars]] (1307–1312), the round-church type continues in non-Templar contexts: secular cemetery rotundas, late-medieval-Gothic Holy-Sepulchre-imitation chapels (Eichstätt, Görlitz), Renaissance and Baroque Holy-Sepulchre dedications (the *Sacro Monte di Varallo*, ~1486 onwards), and ultimately the 19th–20th c. neo-Templar / Masonic revivals (cf. [[quinta-da-regaleira-sintra]]).

## Why this motif anchors a MASSIVE WIN

This is one of the cleanest documented architectural-transmission lines in religious history:

> **Anastasis Rotunda (Constantine, ~326 CE)**
> → **Dome of the Rock (Umayyad, 691 CE)** — Islamic appropriation of the form-language
> → **Templar round-naved churches across Europe (12th–13th c.)** — Templar architectural memorial of the Holy Sepulchre, transmitted via their Jerusalem HQ at the Dome of the Rock
> → **Lalibela in Ethiopia (~1181–1221)** — independent Ethiopian *imitatio Jerusalemii*
> → **Post-Templar European afterlife** — continuing through Renaissance and modern eras

**Four traditions, eight centuries, hundreds of buildings, one architectural type.** The cross-tradition propagation of the Anastasis Rotunda is religious-architectural transmission at its most documented.
