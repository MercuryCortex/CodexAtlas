# Alchemical Marriage (Coniunctio)

The alchemical-symbolic union of polar opposites — Sun and Moon, King and Queen, Sulfur and Mercury, Red and White, Spirit and Body — figured as a sacred-erotic wedding, climaxing in the production of the Philosopher's Stone. The motif is the alchemical re-imagining of the older *hieros gamos* / [[sacred-marriage]] pattern, now interiorized as a stage on the *opus magnum* (great work). The locus classicus among Renaissance texts is Johann Valentin Andreae's *Chymische Hochzeit Christiani Rosencreutz Anno 1459* (1616), a Rosicrucian-allegorical romance in seven days; pictorially, the *Rosarium Philosophorum* (1550) gives the most-reproduced visual sequence of the *coniunctio*.

- [[phase-6-021-andreae-chymical-wedding]] — the Rosicrucian alchemical romance.
- [[phase-6-022-fludd-utriusque-cosmi-historia]] — Fludd's encyclopedic diagrams include alchemical-marital imagery.
- [[phase-7-017-psychology-and-alchemy]], [[phase-7-019-mysterium-coniunctionis]] — Jung's psychological-archetypal reading: the *coniunctio* is the integration of conscious and unconscious, ego and shadow, animus and anima, into the Self.

The motif is structurally related to but distinguishable from [[coniunctio]] (the broader Jungian psychological category) and [[sacred-marriage]] (the ancient-Near-Eastern ritual antecedent).
