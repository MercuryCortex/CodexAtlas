Temple incubation is one of the most widely distributed healing technologies in the ancient world: the deliberate practice of sleeping in a sacred space — a temple, a tomb, a healing sanctuary — in order to receive a diagnostic or curative dream from a deity.

## The Core Pattern

Across every attested tradition, the incubation ritual follows the same structure:
1. **Purification**: the patient bathes, fasts, and sometimes sacrifices before entering the sacred space
2. **The sleeping-within**: the patient sleeps on the ground, often on an animal skin or prescribed surface, inside the sanctuary's inner area (*abaton*, *adyton*)
3. **The divine appearance**: the healing deity appears in the dream — sometimes to cure directly, sometimes to prescribe a treatment
4. **Waking prescription**: if the deity prescribed rather than cured, the temple physicians apply the prescription

The technology works on multiple levels simultaneously:
- **Pharmacologically**: fasting, isolation, and ritual stress alter biochemistry and sleep architecture, increasing REM density and vivid dreaming
- **Psychologically**: the expectation of healing creates a powerful placebo environment; the authority of divine prescription increases patient compliance
- **Institutionally**: the incubation record (the iamata inscriptions at Epidauros) constitute the oldest medical case records in the ancient world

## Greek Asklepion Incubation

The most elaborately documented incubation tradition. The **Epidauros iamata** (cure inscriptions, c. 4th century BCE) preserve hundreds of cases in which Asklepios appeared to sleeping patients. The cures described are a mixture of:
- Miraculous direct cure (the god operates on the patient in the dream; the patient wakes healed)
- Dream prescriptions subsequently applied by physicians (dietary, herbal, bathing)
- Symbolic dreams requiring interpretation by temple staff

Aristophanes's comedy *Plutus* (388 BCE) contains a detailed description of an incubation session — providing an almost anthropological account of the ritual from a secular observer.

## Egyptian Temple Incubation

The **Per-Ankh** (House of Life) attached to major temples served incubation functions. [[sekhmet]] temples were specifically associated with healing sleep — priests of Sekhmet interpreted diagnostic dreams and applied treatments. At Deir el-Bahari, the sanctuary of Hathor-Amenhotep received pilgrims seeking healing dreams.

The [[imhotep]] cult at Saqqara became the most important Egyptian incubation site — his tomb became the Asklepieion of Memphis, where patients slept seeking the deified physician's dream-guidance.
