# Righteous Sufferer / Vindication

The literary-theological pattern: a righteous figure is unjustly persecuted by wicked authorities; the righteous one trusts God / is steadfast / is killed; God vindicates the righteous one (rescue, exaltation, resurrection, or eschatological reward) and shames the persecutors. Nickelsburg 1972 identified the pattern across Daniel 3 and 6, Wisdom of Solomon 2–5, 2–4 Maccabees, the Joseph cycle, and the Markan passion. The pattern provides the narrative grammar that Mark inherits and applies to Jesus.

- [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — Daniel 3 (the three young men in the fiery furnace), Daniel 6 (Daniel in the lion's den): paradigmatic.
- [[phase-3-012-wisdom-of-solomon]] — Wis 2:12–20 + 5:1–7 is the most explicit narrative scheme: the wicked plot, the righteous suffer and die, then are revealed as God's children.
- [[phase-3-019-4-maccabees]] — the philosophical-rhetorical elaboration; Eleazar and the seven brothers under Antiochus.
- [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — Nickelsburg's claim: Mark's passion narrative reads as a Hellenistic-Jewish wisdom-tale of the righteous sufferer vindicated.

The pattern intersects with [[martyrdom-theology]], [[suffering-servant]], and the Hellenistic "noble death" tradition.
