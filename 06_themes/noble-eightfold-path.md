# Noble Eightfold Path

## Definition
The Noble Eightfold Path (*ariyo aṭṭhaṅgiko maggo* / *ārya-aṣṭāṅga-mārga*) is the fourth of the [[four-noble-truths|Four Noble Truths]]: the path to the cessation of *dukkha* (suffering). It comprises eight inter-related factors, grouped in Buddhaghosa's [[phase-4-076-visuddhimagga|Visuddhimagga]] systematic frame into three trainings (*tisikkhā*):

**Paññā / Wisdom training** (the cognitive-discernment factors):
1. ***Sammā-diṭṭhi* — Right View** (understanding of the Four Noble Truths and dependent origination)
2. ***Sammā-saṅkappa* — Right Intention / Resolve** (renunciation, good will, harmlessness)

**Sīla / Ethical conduct training** (the behavioral factors):
3. ***Sammā-vācā* — Right Speech** (no lying, no divisive speech, no harsh speech, no idle chatter)
4. ***Sammā-kammanta* — Right Action** (no killing, no stealing, no sexual misconduct)
5. ***Sammā-ājīva* — Right Livelihood** (avoiding occupations that cause harm — weapons, slave-trade, meat-trade, intoxicants, poisons)

**Samādhi / Concentration training** (the meditative-attentional factors):
6. ***Sammā-vāyāma* — Right Effort** (cultivating wholesome mental states, abandoning unwholesome ones)
7. ***Sammā-sati* — Right Mindfulness** (the four foundations of mindfulness: body, feelings, mind, mental objects)
8. ***Sammā-samādhi* — Right Concentration** (the four *jhānas* of progressively refined meditative absorption)

The path is sometimes pictured as the eight spokes of the *dhamma-cakra* (wheel of dhamma) — the wheel-symbol that becomes ubiquitous in Buddhist iconography, including the **Aśoka Cakra** of [[asoka-maurya|Aśoka the Great]] now on the modern Indian national flag.

A passage "has" the Eightfold Path theme if it explicitly names or organizes around the eight factors, or operates within the three-trainings frame, or invokes the *dhamma-cakra* symbolism.

## Earliest attestations
- **Buddhist (Pali)**: *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta* (SN 56.11) — the first sermon explicitly lists the eight factors. The *Mahāsatipaṭṭhāna Sutta* (DN 22) provides the detailed mindfulness component. *Magga-saṃyutta* (SN 45) — entirely devoted to the path. Dated to the Buddha's teaching career (~-450 to -400).
- **Buddhist (Sanskrit and Chinese)**: parallel formulations in the Sarvāstivāda Sanskrit Āgamas and Chinese T 99 *Saṃyukta Āgama* — the eight factors are consistent across schools.
- **Buddhist (Mahāyāna)**: preserved in Mahāyāna sūtras; the [[phase-5-002-heart-sutra|Heart Sūtra]] famously negates "no path" alongside the rest of the Four Noble Truths in its Mādhyamika emptiness-frame. Mahāyāna doctrinally expands the path component to include the six *pāramitās* (perfections — generosity, ethics, patience, effort, meditation, wisdom) and the bodhisattva-path (*bodhisattva-mārga*).
- **Symbolic attestation**: the [[asoka-maurya|Aśokan]] Sarnath Lion Capital (~-250) features the *dhamma-cakra* with 24 spokes — the broader Aśoka Cakra, of which the 8-spoke version is the more compact Eightfold Path symbol. The Sarnath capital is the state emblem of modern India; the 24-spoke wheel appears on the Indian national flag.

## Variants
- **Theravāda systematic exposition**: [[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa's]] *Visuddhimagga* organizes the entire work around the three-trainings frame (Part I = Sīla / chs. 1-2; Part II = Samādhi / chs. 3-13; Part III = Paññā / chs. 14-23). The eight factors are subordinate to the three trainings in this structure.
- **Mahāyāna six-pāramitā expansion**: in Mahāyāna soteriology, the Eightfold Path is expanded into the *bodhisattva-mārga* with six (or ten) *pāramitās* (perfections). The Eightfold Path is preserved as the framework but supplemented.
- **Vajrayāna integration**: in Tibetan and East Asian esoteric Buddhism, the Eightfold Path is integrated with deity yoga, mantra, and maṇḍala practice; the *lamrim* tradition ([[atisa|Atiśa]] → [[tsongkhapa]]) retains the path-component within the broader integrated frame.
- **Modern Western and secular reception**: the Eightfold Path frame is central to popular Western Buddhist exposition (e.g., the Dalai Lama's many introductory works) and to secular adaptations (Right Mindfulness → MBSR's "mindfulness"; Right Speech → contemporary "nonviolent communication"; the eight factors as ethical-cognitive-meditative integration).

## Connections claimed
- **Within Buddhism**: the Eightfold Path is the operative path-component of [[siddhartha-gautama-buddha|the Buddha's]] Middle Way (*majjhima-paṭipadā*) between sensuality and self-mortification.
- **Pre-Buddhist parallels**: the Jain "three jewels" (right faith, right knowledge, right conduct) and the Upaniṣadic *trividyā* (three knowledges) are roughly contemporaneous threefold soteriological structures from the same Greater Magadha / Vedic milieu; the Buddha's elaboration into eight factors organized in three trainings is a distinctively Buddhist refinement.
- **Modern reception**: comparative-soteriology has noted parallels with Stoic *prokopē* (moral progress), the Christian "ladder of perfection" (John Climacus, etc.), Confucian self-cultivation. All structural-functional parallels; no claim of historical transmission.
