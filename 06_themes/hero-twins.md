# The Hero Twins

## What it is
The Hero Twins — **Hunahpu and Xbalanque** — are the central protagonists of Maya mythology, the defining Mesoamerican mythologem. Their story, preserved in fullest form in the K'iche' Maya [[phase-8-001-popol-vuh|Popol Vuh]], recounts how the twins outwit and ultimately defeat the lords of the underworld (Xibalba) through cleverness, sacrifice, and resurrection, avenging their father, the Maize God, and preparing the world for the present creation and the rise of the sun and moon.

## Earliest attestation
Although the alphabetic Popol Vuh was transcribed in the colonial period (~1550s, copied ~1700 by Francisco Ximénez), the Hero Twins are attested in **Classic-period Maya iconography** (vase paintings, monuments) centuries earlier — confirming that the colonial text records a deep pre-Columbian tradition rather than a post-conquest invention. The twins appear on painted ceramics, in the resurrection-of-the-Maize-God cycle, and in ballgame imagery.

## The narrative
The twins are conceived after the death of their father; they grow up as tricksters and ballplayers; summoned by the lords of Xibalba, they descend to the underworld and survive a sequence of deadly trials (the House of Gloom, the House of Knives, the House of Bats). Through self-sacrifice and resurrection they defeat the death-lords and ascend to become heavenly bodies. The cycle is at once a cosmogony, an etiology of the maize cycle, and a charter for sacrifice and rebirth.

## Cross-tradition findings
- **The divine-twins motif.** The Hero Twins are the Mesoamerican instance of the widespread [[divine-twins]] mythologem — paired culture-hero brothers found across the Indo-European world (the Vedic Aśvins, the Greek Dioscuri, the Roman Romulus and Remus) and beyond. Scholarship treats the Maya case as an **independent convergence** (no contact), making it a strong parallel rather than a transmission. Wired as a `parallel-motif` to [[divine-twins]].
- **Descent-and-return.** The twins' descent to Xibalba and triumphant resurrection is a Mesoamerican form of the [[underworld-descent]] pattern (compare Inanna/Ishtar, Orpheus, the Harrowing of Hell) — again an independent convergence.
