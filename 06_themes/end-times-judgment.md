# End-Times Judgment

A future divine judgment that ends the present world-order and assigns final fates — paradise, hell, annihilation, transformation — to all humans (or all souls). Distinguished from *individual* post-mortem judgment (which assigns the soul's fate immediately at death — see [[afterlife-judgment]]) and from in-history divine retribution (which strikes individuals or nations within ordinary history). End-times judgment is *eschatological*: it terminates ordinary history.

## Manifestations
- **Zoroastrian:** [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]] and especially the later [[phase-2-004-yasna-younger-avesta]] — the *Frashokereti* (renovation). Cosmic battle between Ahura Mazda and Angra Mainyu culminates in the Saoshyant's arrival, universal resurrection, river of molten metal that purges the wicked, final defeat of evil. The earliest fully-articulated end-times judgment scenario; widely seen as the source of later Jewish-Christian-Islamic versions.
- **Hebrew Bible (developing):** Pre-exilic prophets ([[phase-2-010-hebrew-bible-early-prophets]]) speak of the "Day of the LORD" — initially a national-historical concept (Amos 5:18–20: judgment against Israel itself). Post-exilic prophecy ([[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]]) cosmicizes it: judgment of the nations, vindication of Israel.
- **Daniel 12:** [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — "many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life and some to shame and everlasting contempt" — first clear Hebrew Bible resurrection-to-judgment.
- **1 Enoch:** [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — the Watchers are bound for judgment; the Son of Man enthroned will execute it (Parables of Enoch).
- **Qumran:** [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — Two Spirits discourse (1QS 3–4); War Scroll's final battle.
- **Synoptic Jesus tradition:** [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] 13; [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] 25:31–46 (sheep and goats); imminence of the Son of Man.
- **Manichaean:** [[phase-4-016-shabuhragan]] — Mani's Shabuhragan presents end-times judgment in deliberately Iranian-Christian-syncretic form for Sasanian audience.
- **Quranic Day of Judgment:** [[phase-4-034-quran]] — *Yawm al-Dīn*, *al-Qiyāmah*; richly elaborated across the surahs. The Quran's eschatology is among the most vivid in scripture.

## Disputes
- **Persian dependence of Jewish-Christian eschatology.** A debate since the 19th c. Maximalists (Boyce, Hultgård): substantial Persian shaping in the Achaemenid–Hellenistic centuries explains why end-times judgment appears in Jewish texts roughly when contact with Persia is sustained. Minimalists (Barr 1985): the dating of Avestan eschatology is uncertain enough that direct dependence cannot be shown.
- **Whether Jesus expected an imminent end-times judgment.** Schweitzer (1906) yes, thoroughgoing apocalyptic Jesus; Bultmann demythologized; Crossan/Borg redirected to social-realized eschatology. Sanders (1985), Allison (1998) re-defend Schweitzer's apocalyptic Jesus.

## Connection to [[afterlife-judgment]]
End-times judgment and individual afterlife judgment are *not* the same motif. Egypt has a robust individual judgment ([[phase-1-010-book-of-the-dead]] Spell 125) with no end-times judgment. Pre-exilic Israel has neither. Zoroastrianism has both. The combination of both — judgment-at-death plus final judgment — is the standard Christian and Islamic configuration.
