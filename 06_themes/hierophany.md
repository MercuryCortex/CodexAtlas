# Hierophany

Eliade's coinage (from Greek *hieros* "sacred" + *phainein* "show") for any manifestation of the sacred in profane objects: a stone, a tree, a person, an icon, a place. Hierophany is Eliade's primary unit of religious data — what religion *consists in* are repeated hierophanies through which the sacred irrupts into ordinary spacetime.

The category is intentionally broad: it covers the Israelite burning bush, the Hindu *darshan* of a divine image, the Christian Eucharist, the Aboriginal totem, the Greek omphalos at Delphi. Eliade resisted reducing this to "sociological function" or "psychological projection" — for him hierophany is *ontological*: the sacred really shows itself.

Critics (J.Z. Smith, Strenski) attack the category as a *sui generis* religion claim that smuggles theology into analysis. Whether or not one accepts Eliade's framing, "hierophany" remains broadly cited as a useful descriptive category.
