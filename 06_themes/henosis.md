# Henosis (Mystical Union with the One)

The mystical experience or theological goal of *becoming one* with — or recognizing one's prior identity with — the highest divine reality. The Greek term *henōsis* ("oneing," unification) is Plotinian; the concept ranges across Middle Platonism, Plotinian and post-Plotinian Neoplatonism, Christian mystical theology (especially the Greek Fathers' *theosis* tradition and the Western Rhineland-Carmelite traditions), Islamic Sufism (*fanāʾ* as annihilation-into-God, *baqāʾ* as subsistence-in-God), Advaita Vedanta (the realization of *ātman*-*Brahman* identity as the dissolution of avidya-imposed separation), and Jewish mysticism (*devekut*, "cleaving" to God). Distinguished from doctrines that preserve creator-creature distinction *throughout* the union (which include most "mainstream" Christian and Sunni Islamic mainstream positions) from those that posit *dissolution* of the distinction at the height of the union.

## Manifestations

- **Plotinian.** [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] *Ennead* VI.9 — "the flight of the alone to the Alone." Plotinus describes his own experience of *henōsis* (Porphyry, *Life of Plotinus* 23: "this happened to him four times"). The mystical apex of the *Enneads* and the structural seed of the entire Christian-mystical Platonist tradition.
- **Plato's *Symposium* and *Phaedrus*.** [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — Diotima's ladder (*Symp.* 210a–212a) ascends to the vision of the Beautiful-itself; *Phaedrus* 247c–e describes the soul's vision of the *hyperouranios topos*. Proto-henotic without using the term.
- **Pseudo-Dionysius.** [[phase-4-030-pseudo-dionysius]] — *Mystical Theology* and *Divine Names*. Henosis through apophatic *agnōsia* (un-knowing); the soul rises by progressive denials.
- **Rhineland mystics.** Meister Eckhart's *birth of the Word in the soul* and his sermons on *Gottheit*-as-Ungrund pre-figure full *henosis* language (cf. [[the-godhead-eckhart]], [[ungrund]]). John of the Cross, Teresa of Ávila in the Carmelite tradition.
- **Eastern Christian *theosis*.** Maximus the Confessor, Gregory Palamas — *theosis* (deification) understood as participation in the divine energies-but-not-essence; a *theosis*-version of henosis that preserves Creator-creature distinction.
- **Sufi *fanāʾ*.** [[phase-5-025-rumi-masnavi]] and the earlier *fanāʾ* / *baqāʾ* tradition (Bistāmī's "Subḥānī! mā aʿẓama shaʾnī" "Glory be to me, how great is my majesty!"; al-Ḥallāj's *Anā al-Ḥaqq* "I am the Truth"). The orthodox-vs-mystic controversy over the limits of language about union.
- **Advaita Vedanta.** [[phase-5-005-shankara-brahma-sutra-bhasya]] — Shankara's claim that *ātman = Brahman*; the appearance of distinction is *avidya*; *moksha* is realization that there was never separation to begin with.
- **Jewish *devekut*.** Hasidic mysticism's *devekut* (cleaving); Baal Shem Tov's ecstatic prayer-states; later Hasidic theology.

## Disputes

- **Whether henosis-language across traditions describes the same experience or only structurally-similar experiences.** The perennialist position (Huxley, Smith) says same; the contextualist (Katz 1978) says traditions actively *construct* the experiences they purport to merely describe. The debate remains live in religious studies.
- **The orthodox-vs-mystic problem.** Many traditions struggled to police the line between henosis-as-union-with-preserved-distinction and henosis-as-identity. Christian condemnations of Eckhart, Sunni execution of al-Ḥallāj, suspicion of Shankara within Vedic-orthodox circles all turn on this.
- **Whether *fanāʾ* and Advaita are equivalent.** Modern comparativists (Schimmel, Nasr) note close structural parallels; Islamic theologians (Nasr included) maintain ultimate distinction: *fanāʾ* preserves the Creator-creature ontological gap that Advaita dissolves.
