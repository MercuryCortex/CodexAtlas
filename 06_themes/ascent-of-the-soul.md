# Ascent of the Soul

The soul (or a visionary, or the dying) ascends through cosmic levels — typically the seven planetary spheres, or seven heavens, or seven palaces — toward a transcendent encounter with the divine. The motif distinguishes itself from descent-and-return (the Inanna pattern, [[dying-rising-god]]) by being a *upward* one-way movement (in vision) or by being explicitly contrasted with descent-into-incarnation (which is the *fall* the ascent reverses). It appears across Mesopotamian, Iranian, Jewish, Greek, Christian, Gnostic, Hermetic, Mandaean, Manichaean, Sufi, and Christian-mystical literatures.

## Manifestations
- **Apocalyptic Jewish:** [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — Enoch is taken through seven heavens, sees the Throne. The locus classicus of the seven-heavens schema as Jewish revelation literature.
- **Hellenistic Jewish allegory:** [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]] — Philo reads Moses's ascent of Sinai and Abraham's journey philosophically as the soul's ascent from sensible to intelligible to mystical contemplation.
- **Hekhalot Merkavah:** [[phase-5-013-hekhalot-literature]] — Jewish mystical ascent literature (3rd–8th c.); the *yored ha-merkavah* ("descender to the chariot") passes through seven palaces, each guarded by hostile angels who must be passed via seals and divine names. Among the most fully developed *ritualized* ascent praxes attested in late antiquity.
- **Mithraic:** Origen *Contra Celsum* VI.22 preserves Celsus's description of a Mithraic seven-rung ladder of metals corresponding to planetary spheres; iconography supports planetary ascent symbolism.
- **Hermetic:** [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] *Poimandres* — at death the soul rises through the planetary spheres, surrendering at each sphere the vice it accreted there during descent; the *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth* [[phase-4-013-discourse-on-the-eighth-and-ninth]] is an *operative* Hermetic ascent ritual (Nag Hammadi).
- **Neoplatonic ascent:** [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — the soul's return (*epistrophē*) through Soul, Nous, to the One; Plotinus reports four episodes of *henōsis* (mystical union); Iamblichus and Proclus systematize this as theurgic ascent. [[phase-4-020-iamblichus-on-the-mysteries]], [[phase-4-022-chaldean-oracles]] — theurgic operations to elevate the soul.
- **Pauline:** 2 Cor 12 — Paul's third-heaven rapture; a foundational Christian ascent-text.
- **Sufi *miʿrāj*:** Quranic *Sūra al-Isrāʾ* and Hadith of Muhammad's night journey; later philosophically interiorized in Avicenna's *Risāla fī l-Miʿrāj* and Sufi tradition. [[phase-5-025-rumi-masnavi]] — Rūmī's spiritual ascent as the central Sufi imagery.
- **Dante:** *Paradiso* — a consummate literary synthesis of much of the ascent tradition (planetary spheres, primum mobile, Empyrean, visio Dei).
- **Renaissance magical ascent:** [[phase-6-009-agrippa-de-occulta-philosophia]] — Agrippa's "religious magic" makes ascent operative; planetary ladders feed into Hermetic ceremonial magic that survives into Phase 7 (Golden Dawn).

## Disputes
- **Origin of the seven-spheres schema.** Mesopotamian astronomical Pleiades-and-planets schema → Hellenistic astrological synthesis (Stoics, Posidonius, Nigidius Figulus) → diffused everywhere from Iran to Britain in the late-antique syncretism. Couliano (1983, 1991) reconstructs the diffusion in detail.
- **Are ascent-texts records of experience or literary topoi?** Scholem viewed Hekhalot as experiential ascent; Halperin and others have argued they are largely literary-magical compositions. The recent trend (Davila, Schäfer): ritualized praxis grounded in experience-claims, but the experience is shaped *by* the texts.
- **Christian orthodoxy and the ascent tradition.** The mainstream church absorbed Pauline rapture and Dionysian mystical ascent (Pseudo-Dionysius); kept ritual ascent at arm's length (Hekhalot was *outside*; Hermeticism was *pagan*); some Christian mystics (Eckhart, Cloud of Unknowing) clearly continue the trajectory.
