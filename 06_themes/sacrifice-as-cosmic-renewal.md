# Sacrifice as Cosmic Renewal

The theological-ritual schema in which a sacrificial act (originally cosmogonic, repeatedly re-enacted in cult) sustains, renews, or restores the cosmos. Distinguished from individual-propitiatory sacrifice and from gift-economy sacrifice by its cosmological scope: the cosmos itself requires sacrificial maintenance, and the ritual re-actualizes a primordial dismembering or self-offering of a divine victim.

## Manifestations

- **Vedic Purusha-sukta.** [[phase-2-001-rig-veda-family-books]] 10.90 — the primal Man (Puruṣa) is sacrificed by the gods; his dismembered body becomes the cosmos (mind → moon, eye → sun, breath → wind) and the four social classes (mouth → brahmin, arms → kshatriya, thighs → vaishya, feet → shudra). The Vedic *yajña* sacrificial ritual re-enacts this cosmogonic dismembering; the Brahmanas systematize the identification of priest-and-victim-and-cosmos.
- **Mesoamerican (Aztec) cosmogonic sacrifice.** [[phase-8-004-florentine-codex-sahagun]] — at Teotihuacan in the mythic past the gods sacrifice themselves so the sun can move; humans must sustain the cosmos through ongoing blood-offering. The *tlamacazqui* priests' daily extractions and the periodic mass-sacrifices (especially at the *xiuhmolpilli* New Fire ceremony every 52 years) are *literal* cosmic maintenance. Caso 1958; more recently Carrasco's *City of Sacrifice* (1999).
- **Mesopotamian *Akītu*.** [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] recited at the Babylonian New Year (*Akītu*); the cosmogony (Marduk's slaying of Tiamat, the world's making from her body) is liturgically re-actualized to renew the year. The king's ritual humiliation before Marduk re-enacts cosmogonic re-ordering.
- **Christian Eucharist.** [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] — "do this in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19; 1 Cor 11:24–25). The Council of Trent's *anamnesis* doctrine (1551) systematizes the Eucharist as re-actualizing the once-for-all sacrifice of Calvary, with continuing-renewal effect. Pierced bread / poured wine as cosmic re-actualization is structurally homologous to the Vedic and Aztec patterns — though the once-for-all (*ephapax* of Heb 7:27, 9:12, 10:10) component is theologically distinctive.
- **Vedic *agnihotra*.** The twice-daily fire-offering by the householder; small-scale daily cosmic-sustenance.

## Disputes

- **Girard's universalizing thesis.** Girard (1972) read all sacrifice as scapegoat-mechanism originating in pre-historic communal violence. Anthropologists (Burkert) accept some version; many ritualists (Smith, *Imagining Religion*) reject the universalist scope. The Vedic and Aztec cases — explicitly *cosmogonic* sacrifice, with no obvious community-scapegoating function — are awkward for Girard.
- **Whether the Christian Eucharist is genuinely structurally homologous to Vedic / Aztec cosmogonic sacrifice or whether the once-for-all theology breaks the pattern.** Catholic theology (Trent, Schmemann, de Lubac) reads it as both once-for-all and continually-re-actualized. Protestant theology (Calvin, Hodge) typically rejects the cosmic-renewal frame.
- **The historicity of large-scale Aztec sacrifice.** Recent revisionists (Hassig) accept that ritual sacrifice happened but argue the Spanish chroniclers' numbers (80,000 at the 1487 Templo Mayor dedication) are propagandistic inflation; archaeology supports thousands rather than tens of thousands.
