# Son of Man

A figure designated *bar enash* (Aramaic) / *huios tou anthrōpou* (Greek) — literally "son of man" / "human being" — appears in Daniel 7 as one "like a son of man" who receives everlasting dominion from the Ancient of Days; subsequently in 1 Enoch's Parables as a pre-existent heavenly figure; and on the lips of Jesus in the Synoptic Gospels as his most-used self-designation. The historical-critical puzzle: what does Jesus mean when he uses the phrase, and how does it relate to the Danielic and Enochic streams?

## Manifestations
- **Hebrew Bible non-technical use:** Ezekiel — "son of man" as Yahweh's address to the prophet, simply "mortal" / "human being" (over 90 times). No eschatological weight.
- **Daniel 7:13–14:** [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — "I saw in the night visions, and behold, one like a son of man came with the clouds of heaven, and came to the Ancient of Days ... and to him was given dominion and glory and a kingdom." The decisive text. Whether the figure is individual or corporate-symbolic (representing "the people of the holy ones") is disputed within Daniel itself.
- **1 Enoch's Parables:** [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] 37–71 — the Son of Man is named, pre-existent, enthroned, identified with the Righteous One and the Elect One. The most fully developed pre-Christian Son-of-Man figure. Dating of the Parables (probably 1st c. BCE – early 1st c. CE) and absence from Qumran fragments has generated decades of debate.
- **4 Ezra 13:** A later (post-70 CE) Jewish apocalyptic Son-of-Man vision — independent witness to a living tradition.
- **Synoptic Jesus:** [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] uses "Son of Man" 14 times — Jesus's predominant self-reference. Three categories per Bultmann: (1) present-activity sayings ("the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins"), (2) suffering-prediction sayings ("the Son of Man must suffer many things"), (3) future-coming sayings ("you will see the Son of Man coming on the clouds" — direct Danielic citation).
- **Johannine:** [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — Son of Man is the descended/ascending mediator, the heavenly counterpart; merged with Logos and Son theologies.
- **Q:** [[phase-3-014-q-source]] preserves coming-Son-of-Man sayings; Casey and others reconstruct an "earliest stratum" Son-of-Man tradition.

## The historical puzzle
Did Jesus call himself "the Son of Man"? Schools:
- **Apocalyptic-eschatological (Schweitzer, Bultmann, Hengel):** Jesus expected a *different* heavenly Son of Man coming soon; the church post-Easter identified that figure with the risen Jesus.
- **Self-referential (Vermes, Casey):** "Son of Man" is an Aramaic idiom — "a man like me" / "one in my position" — Jesus used it as a self-reference, no Danielic content; the apocalyptic readings are post-Easter Christian.
- **Maximalist (Marshall, France):** Jesus did use the title for himself with deliberate Danielic claim. Apocalyptic self-claim.

The disputes are not settled.

## Disputes
- **Date of 1 Enoch's Parables.** If pre-Christian (and Jewish), they show a Jewish messianic Son-of-Man tradition Jesus could have inherited. If post-Christian, the influence is reversed. Most current consensus: pre-Christian, late 1st c. BCE – early 1st c. CE.
- **Aramaic idiom.** Whether *bar enash* in the time of Jesus could function as a self-reference is contested philologically; Vermes argued yes, Fitzmyer pushed back.
