# Paraclete / Spirit of Truth

The Greek *paraklētos* ("called alongside" — advocate, helper, comforter) is a distinctively Johannine designation for the Holy Spirit in the Farewell Discourse (Jn 14:16, 14:26, 15:26, 16:7) and in 1 John 2:1 (where the title is given to Jesus). Three Johannine functions: (1) advocate / legal helper in the believers' trial before the world; (2) post-Easter teacher who recalls and unfolds Jesus' words; (3) Spirit of Truth who convicts the world of sin, righteousness, and judgment. The "another paraclete" of Jn 14:16 implies Jesus himself was the first paraclete and the Spirit succeeds him.

- [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — the locus classicus; five Paraclete sayings in the Farewell Discourse.
- **Manichaean appropriation**: [[phase-4-014-cologne-mani-codex]], [[phase-4-015-kephalaia-of-the-teacher]] — Mani claimed to be the promised Paraclete, the final apostle in a succession including Jesus and Buddha. The Manichaean texts directly quote Johannine Paraclete sayings as fulfilled in Mani. Augustine, an ex-Manichaean, polemicizes against this reading.
- Islamic appropriation (Muḥammad as the prophesied "Ahmad"/*Periklytos*) postdates the Phase-4 corpus but is on the same exegetical trajectory — Qur'an 61:6 implies a reading of John 16 with *paraklētos* misread/recast as *periklytos* (the "praised one" — Aramaic-Arabic semantic affinity with *Ahmad*).

Brown's *Anchor John* and *The Community of the Beloved Disciple* (1979) treat the Paraclete texts as evidence of the late-1st-century Johannine community's theology of the Spirit-presence of the absent Jesus.
