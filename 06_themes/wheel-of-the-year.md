# Wheel of the Year

The mid-20th-century modern-Pagan synthesis of eight ritual festivals at six-week intervals around the solar year: the four solar quarter-days (winter solstice / Yule, spring equinox / Ostara, summer solstice / Litha, autumn equinox / Mabon) and the four Insular Celtic cross-quarter-days (Samhain, Imbolc, Beltane, Lughnasadh/Lammas). The eight-fold *Wheel of the Year* was systematized by Gerald Gardner and Ross Nichols in the late 1950s, drawing on Margaret Murray's *Witch-Cult* (1921), Sir James Frazer's *Golden Bough*, and the actual surviving folkloric ritual-year of Britain and Ireland documented by Hutton 1996. The eight-fold synthesis is genuinely modern; no historical pre-Christian tradition is known to have celebrated all eight on the schedule modern Wicca uses.

- [[phase-7-024-witchcraft-today]] — Gerald Gardner's foundational 1954 work that publicly disclosed the Wiccan ritual cycle.

Hutton's *Triumph of the Moon* (1999) is the definitive scholarly account: the Wheel of the Year is a beautiful 20th-century synthesis, not a continuous survival from pre-Christian times.
