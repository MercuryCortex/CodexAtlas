# Priestly Purity

The biblical-priestly (P) system of *ritual purity* (Hebrew *ṭāhôr*) vs. *ritual impurity* (*ṭāmēʾ*) — a non-moral cultic-ontological status governed by contact with sources of impurity (corpses, certain bodily emissions, *tsara'at* skin afflictions, certain animals). Impurity excludes one from the sanctuary; purification requires waiting periods, washing, sometimes sacrifice. Milgrom's Anchor Leviticus established the modern critical reading: the system is *systematic* and *coherent*, governed by the logic of life vs. death (sources of impurity all involve some loss or breach of bodily-life integrity); it is *not* a primitive hygiene code.

- [[phase-2-011-hebrew-bible-d-source]] / [[phase-2-007-hebrew-bible-e-source]] — though the most developed purity-laws are in P, the D and E strata preserve related material.
- [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — the Qumran community radicalizes purity beyond the priestly biblical framework: extended purification periods, lay observance of priestly purity, the eschatological community as the temple.
- [[phase-4-031-mishnah]] — the Mishnah's sixth Order (*Ṭohorot*) is the largest of the six Orders; the rabbinic systematization of purity-law for an age without a temple.

Mary Douglas (1966) read the dietary laws (Lev 11) as a system of cosmic classification: clean animals are those that conform to the proper categorical type for their domain. The reading has been highly influential, though Milgrom and others have refined or contested it.

The system informs early Christian polemics ("not what enters the mouth defiles," Mk 7:15 par.), the Pauline relativization of food laws, and Pharisaic intra-Jewish debates about purity-extension.
