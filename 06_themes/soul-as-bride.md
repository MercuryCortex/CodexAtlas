# Soul as Bride

The recurrent mystical-erotic figuration of the soul's relation to God as *bride to bridegroom* — the soul feminized and receptive, the divine masculinized and pursuing-and-pursued. The motif crosses major mystical traditions: medieval Christian *Brautmystik* (Bernard of Clairvaux's Song-of-Songs sermons, Beguine love-mysticism — Hadewijch, Mechthild of Magdeburg — Eckhart's *Seelenfünklein* receptive to the *Gottesgeburt*); Sufi *ʿishq* poetry (Rumi, Ibn al-Fāriḍ, Hāfiẓ); Bhakti *nāyikā-bhāva* devotion to Krishna (Mirabai as Krishna's bride; the gopi-Krishna of Chaitanya's Bengal Vaishnavism); rabbinic and kabbalistic readings of the Song of Songs as the love of Israel/Shekhinah for the divine.

- [[phase-5-028-meister-eckhart-sermons]] — Eckhart's sermon on *Vom Adel der Seele* and the *Gottesgeburt* in the soul use bridal-receptive imagery; the medieval *Brautmystik* tradition is the longer current.
- [[phase-5-025-rumi-masnavi]] — Rumi's *Masnavi* and especially his *Divan-i Shams-i Tabrizi* are saturated with bridal-erotic Sufi imagery.
- [[phase-6-013-mirabai-bhajans]] — Mirabai as Krishna's bride is the central self-figuration of the Mirabai corpus.
- [[phase-6-020-chaitanya-charitamrita]] — the gopi-Krishna devotional posture (*madhura-bhāva*) is the highest of the Gaudiya five-relations to the divine.

The figure is hermeneutically generative across traditions: Song of Songs commentary in all three Abrahamic traditions; the *Brautmystik* line; the Sufi-Bhakti convergence. Modern feminist mystical theology has problematized the gendered structure while preserving the depth-receptive insight.
