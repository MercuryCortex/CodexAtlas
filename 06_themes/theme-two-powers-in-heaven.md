# Two Powers in Heaven

## The Thesis

Alan Segal's 1977 monograph demonstrated that rabbinic literature contains a heresy category called the "two powers in heaven" (*shtei reshuyot*) — the charge that someone was positing two divine powers rather than strict monotheism. The rabbis used this charge against Christians, Gnostics, and certain Jewish mystics. Segal's insight: you don't invent a heresy-label unless the thing being labeled actually exists. The "two powers" heresy was *rampant in Second Temple Judaism* — it's the suppressed tradition that the rabbis were reacting against.

Daniel Boyarin's subsequent work (*The Jewish Gospels*, 2012; *Border Lines*, 2004) extended this: **high Christology — the claim that Jesus is divine, pre-existent, and the agent of creation — is not an innovation that Gentile Christianity imposed on Jewish monotheism. It is the direct continuation of a Jewish theological tradition that was flourishing in the Second Temple period.** What was later called "Christianity" was, at its inception, one variant of Jewish two-powers theology. The partition between "Judaism" and "Christianity" was drawn *after the fact*, largely in the 2nd and 3rd centuries, in a process of mutual boundary-drawing.

Margaret Barker's parallel work (*The Great Angel*, 1992) pushes the tradition even earlier: she argues that pre-exilic Israelite religion distinguished between *El Elyon* (the Father-God) and *YHWH* (the Son-God, the divine agent, the "Angel of the LORD"), and that this pre-Deuteronomic binitarianism was the substrate from which both Jewish Wisdom theology and Christian Logos/Son theology grew.

## The Evidence Chain

### 1. The Ancient of Days and the Son of Man (Daniel 7, ~165 BCE)
**Daniel 7:9–14** is the locus classicus: "the Ancient of Days took his seat" — then "one like a son of man" comes on the clouds of heaven and is brought before the Ancient of Days, who gives him "dominion and glory and kingship." Two enthroned figures. The older rabbinic midrash (Hagigah 14a) notes: "One throne for Him and one for David [= the Messiah]" — and then suppresses the binitarian reading.

This text is the seed of the entire tradition:
- The "Ancient of Days" = the Father-God / El / Ein Sof
- The "Son of Man" = the divine vice-regent / the Second God / the Logos
- The conferral of universal sovereignty mirrors the Enochic Son of Man passages (1 Enoch 46, 48, 62, 69) and directly anticipates John 5:27 ("he has given him authority to execute judgment, because he is the Son of Man")

### 2. The Enochic Son of Man (1 Enoch, Parables, ~1st century BCE–CE)
[[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] chapters 46, 48, 62–63, 69–71 describe a "Son of Man" figure who:
- Existed before creation ("before the sun and the signs were created, before the stars of heaven were made, his name was named")
- Sits on the throne of glory alongside the "Head of Days" [[head-of-days]]
- Is the instrument of eschatological judgment
- Is identified at the end of the Parables as *Enoch himself* — the human patriarch who "walked with God and was not" (Gen 5:24) has been transformed into the pre-existent divine judge

This is the two-powers pattern in its clearest form: a divine senior figure + a divine vice-regent/judge who is simultaneously pre-existent and human-originated.

### 3. Metatron — "the Lesser YHWH" (3 Enoch / Hekhalot, ~3rd–7th century CE)
[[metatron]] in the Hekhalot texts receives a name that explodes the monotheistic boundary: **"Yahweh ha-Qaton" — the Lesser YHWH**. He is seated on a throne identical to God's throne. The central text:

**Babylonian Talmud, Hagigah 15a** — the story of Elisha ben Abuyah (*Aher*, "the Other") who, upon entering the Pardes (the heavenly garden), sees Metatron seated on his throne and concludes: "Perhaps there are two powers in heaven!" (*shtei reshuyot*). Aher's apostasy is triggered by seeing a divine second figure enthroned. The rabbis immediately demote Metatron (he is flogged by the angel Anafiel) to establish that he is subordinate. The anxious reaction *confirms* how dangerous the tradition was.

Metatron is identified as the transformed Enoch in 3 Enoch (Sefer Hekhalot): Enoch's flesh becomes fire, his body becomes cosmic, he is crowned and enthroned as the divine scribe and vice-regent.

### 4. The Philonic Logos as "Second God" (~20 BCE–50 CE)
[[philo-of-alexandria]] explicitly calls the Logos the *Deuteros Theos* — "Second God" (*Quaestiones in Genesim* 2.62). This is the same two-powers structure translated into Platonic-Stoic philosophical vocabulary. Whether Philo knew the Enochic/Hekhalot traditions directly or was drawing on a shared stream, the structural position is identical:

God (Ein Sof / the Father / the Ancient of Days) → **the Logos / Son of Man / Metatron** (the divine vice-regent who creates, reveals, and judges) → Creation

### 5. The Johannine Logos-Christ (John 1, ~90 CE)
[[logos-johannine]] "In the beginning was the Logos" — the Logos is with God, *is* God, and becomes flesh as Jesus. In John 5:22: "the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son." In John 5:27: authority to execute judgment "because he is the Son of Man." The Johannine Logos-Christ is the *direct identification* of the Logos/Second-God figure with the "Son of Man" of Daniel 7 and 1 Enoch. The high Christology of the Fourth Gospel is not Hellenization — it is the culmination of the Second Temple Jewish two-powers tradition.

## MASSIVE WIN: The Pattern Is Pre-Christian Jewish

The conventional narrative: Judaism was strictly monotheist; early Christianity imported divine Christology from Greek polytheist culture; this Hellenization is what split Christianity from Judaism.

The Boyarin/Barker revision: **the two-powers tradition was indigenous to Second Temple Judaism**. The rabbis *created strict monotheism as a reaction against* what they called the "two powers heresy" — a heresy that was, in fact, the older theology. Christianity did not import Greek polytheism; it preserved and elaborated a Jewish theological tradition that the rabbis subsequently declared heretical. The Christianity/Judaism "parting of the ways" is the *result* of the rabbinic decision to excise this tradition, not a prior cause.

**This means:**
- The "divine Jesus" is not a Greek idea imported into a Jewish framework
- It is a Jewish idea (the enthroned Son of Man / the divine vice-regent) that the Gentile church inherited and the rabbinic academy suppressed
- The Talmud's anxiety about "two powers" is evidence that the tradition was *already present in the synagogue*, not imported from outside

## The Transmission Chain

```
Utu-abzu (Apkallu, "taken to heaven") — Mesopotamian substrate
    ↓
Enoch (Genesis 5:24, "walked with God and was not") — Hebrew reception
    ↓
Son of Man / Elect One (1 Enoch Parables, ~1st c. BCE) — Second Temple elaboration
    ↓
Daniel 7 (Ancient of Days + Son of Man, ~165 BCE) — the canonical formulation
    ↓
Philo's Logos / Second God (~20 BCE–50 CE) — Hellenistic-philosophical articulation
    ↓
Johannine Logos-Christ (John 1, ~90 CE) — the Christian identification
    ↓
Metatron / Lesser YHWH (3 Enoch, Hekhalot, ~3rd–7th c. CE) — rabbinic mystical preservation
    ↓
Kabbalistic Tiferet / Metatron (Zohar, ~1280) — medieval Jewish continuation
```
