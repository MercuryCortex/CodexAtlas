# Mysticism (Cross-Tradition)

The cross-tradition category for **direct unitive / non-dual / contemplative religious experience**. Used as the umbrella term for the comparative academic study of religious experience pioneered by William James (*The Varieties of Religious Experience*, 1902), R.C. Zaehner (*Mysticism Sacred and Profane*, 1957), W.T. Stace (*Mysticism and Philosophy*, 1960), and Steven Katz (constructivist response, 1978).

The principal modern debate is the **perennialism vs. constructivism** controversy — whether mystical experiences across traditions share a common phenomenological core (Stace, Forman) or are inescapably shaped by their tradition-specific conceptual frameworks (Katz, Proudfoot).
