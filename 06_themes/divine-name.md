# Divine Name

The proper name of the deity treated as itself sacred, powerful, even ontologically participating in the deity it names. Across Mesopotamian, Hebrew, Greek, Egyptian, Sanskrit, and Arabic traditions, divine names are not merely labels — they are operative: pronouncing them, writing them, meditating on them, suppressing them, or having them taught to one are themselves religiously efficacious acts. The motif gives rise to Tetragrammaton mysticism, Sufi *dhikr*, Hindu *mantra*-japa, Buddhist *nembutsu*, and Western magical-Names traditions.

## Manifestations
- **Mesopotamian:** [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] — Marduk's fifty names recited at the end of *Enūma Elish* are the climactic theological-political content: Marduk *is* what the names declare. Names are inheritance: the gods bestow their attributes-as-names on Marduk.
- **Hebrew Tetragrammaton:** [[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]], [[phase-2-011-hebrew-bible-d-source]] — YHWH is the unique covenant-name (Exod 3:14, "I am that I am"; Exod 20:7, name not to be taken in vain). Post-exilic prohibition on pronunciation; *Adonai* substituted in reading; eventually the actual pronunciation is lost (and the Masoretic vowels of *Adonai* superimposed produce the medieval Christian misreading "Jehovah").
- **Sefer Yetzirah and Hekhalot:** [[phase-5-012-sefer-yetzirah]], [[phase-5-013-hekhalot-literature]] — the twenty-two Hebrew letters as cosmogonic principles; divine names as the very fabric of creation. Hekhalot ascent rituals are dense with permutations of divine names used as passwords through the seven palaces — and as defensive magic against the angelic guardians.
- **Kabbalah:** [[phase-5-027-sefer-ha-zohar]] — the *Shem ha-Mephorash* ("explicit name"), the seventy-two-letter name, the forty-two-letter name; permutational practices (Abulafia's prophetic Kabbalah); divine names as the *etzem* (essence) of God in Kabbalistic theology.
- **Quranic and Sufi:** [[phase-4-034-quran]] — *al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā* ("the Most Beautiful Names"), the ninety-nine names of Allah. Sufi *dhikr* (remembrance / mention) of these names is the central spiritual practice across Sufi tariqas: silent or vocal repetition disciplines the heart and brings about *fanāʾ* (annihilation in the divine).
- **Vedic / Hindu:** [[phase-2-001-rig-veda-family-books]] — the *mantra* tradition: sacred sound-formula whose efficacy lies in correct utterance. *Bīja-mantra*s (seed syllables: *Oṁ*, *Hrīṁ*, *Klīṁ*) crystallize this. Vaishnava and Śaiva *nāma-japa* (name-repetition) become the central devotional practices of medieval bhakti (cf. [[bhakti-devotion]]); the *Nāma-mahima* literature catalogues the saving power of divine names.
- **Buddhist *nembutsu* and *mantra*.** Pure Land Buddhism makes recitation of Amitābha's name (*nembutsu* / *念佛*) the sole soteriological vehicle. Vajrayāna Buddhism makes mantras the central tantric praxis.
- **Egyptian.** The Book of the Dead and pyramid-texts deploy divine names operatively: knowing the secret name of a god gives power over the god. The Isis-Ra story (Papyrus Turin) in which Isis tricks Ra into revealing his secret name is the locus classicus.
- **Western magical names traditions.** From the Greek magical papyri through Solomonic grimoires through Agrippa's *De Occulta* and the Goetia, magical operations are pervasively name-based. Compatible with [[prisca-theologia]] framing: Hebrew names plus Greek and barbarous-tongue voces magicae.

## Disputes
- **Whether the name-magic stratum is independent or borrowed.** Strong Mesopotamian-Egyptian → Hebrew → Greek transmission lines for some name-clusters (e.g. *Iao* from Tetragrammaton appears in Greek magical papyri); other developments (mantra, Sufi *dhikr*) are clearly indigenous to their traditions.
- **Theological status.** Sunni *kalām*: divine names are descriptive of attributes, not constitutive. Kabbalah and Sufi *wahdat al-wujūd*: names are participations in the divine essence. The classical Islamic and Jewish philosophical theology of divine names is as developed as anything in the history of theology.
