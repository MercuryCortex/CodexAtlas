# Henotheism, Monolatry, Monotheism

Three distinct theological positions often conflated:
- **Henotheism** — worship of one chief god while acknowledging others exist (term from Max Müller).
- **Monolatry** — exclusive worship of one god, without denying others exist (common early-Israelite position).
- **Monotheism** — denial that any other gods exist.

## Key transitions
- **Akhenaten (~1350 BCE):** [[phase-1-011-great-hymn-to-aten]] — aggressive royal monolatry/monotheism imposed top-down; collapsed at his death. Often called the first historical monotheism, though scholarly debate continues over whether it was truly monotheistic or merely exclusivist henotheism.
- **Pre-exilic Israel (~900–600 BCE):** Polytheistic divine council with Yahweh as Israel's god among many ("Yahweh, our god" Deut 6:4); see [[el-canaanite]], [[asherah]] as Yahweh's consort (Kuntillet Ajrud inscriptions).
- **Deuteronomistic reform (~620 BCE):** Pushes toward monolatry — "no other gods *before* me" reads as monolatrous, not yet monotheistic.
- **Post-exilic (Deutero-Isaiah, ~540 BCE):** First clearly monotheistic statements — "there is no other god" (Isa 44:6, 45:5–6).
- **Zoroastrian (Gathic):** Disputed — Ahura Mazda supreme, but other yazatas exist; Boyce reads it as monotheistic, others as dualistic-henotheistic.

## Significance
Assmann's "Mosaic distinction" thesis: the cognitive innovation of monotheism is the *distinction between true and false religion*, not the count of gods. This reshaped the religious landscape and made religious intolerance possible in a new way.
