# Name of God

The motif that the *name* of a deity is not a label attached to a separately-existing referent but is itself a locus of divine presence and power. Knowing the right name binds, propitiates, manifests, or actualizes the god; misuse of the name profanes or imperils. Egyptian theology already articulates this with *rn* (name) as one of the seven constituents of the person — including the gods'. The Hebrew tradition develops the strongest version: YHWH is *the* Name, ineffable, substitutable only by surrogates (Adonai, Ha-Shem). The motif persists strongly in Islamic theology (the 99 names; *al-ism al-aʿẓam*, the "Greatest Name"), in Kabbalah (the Shem-ha-Mephorash, the 72-letter explicit name), in Tantric mantra-cosmology (*bīja*-syllables as ontological seeds of deities), and in Christian devotion (the Name of Jesus, Pseudo-Dionysian *Divine Names*).

## Manifestations

- **Egyptian *rn*.** [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]] — the deceased's name as one of seven elements that must persist for personal survival; gods have multiple names, with the secret name granting power over the god (Isis-and-Ra myth: Isis tricks Ra into revealing his secret name).
- **Hebrew YHWH.** [[phase-2-018-hebrew-bible-p-source]] — the revelation of the divine name to Moses at the burning bush (Exod 3:14–15); the J/E source's earlier YHWH-worship retrojected; the P source's claim that YHWH was unknown before Moses (Exod 6:3). The third commandment forbidding misuse. Post-exilic substitution by Adonai in reading aloud → eventual loss of pronunciation. Tetragrammaton's continuing theological centrality.
- **Christian Name of Jesus.** Phil 2:9–11 (the name above every name); Acts 4:12 (no other name under heaven by which we must be saved); Hesychast *Jesus Prayer* in Eastern Orthodoxy as continuous repetition-of-the-name spirituality.
- **Pseudo-Dionysian *Divine Names*.** [[phase-4-030-pseudo-dionysius]] — systematic theology of the divine names: each name (Good, Being, Life, Wisdom, etc.) discloses an aspect; God transcends all names but is rightly named in all.
- **Islamic 99 names.** [[phase-4-034-quran]] — *al-asmāʾ al-ḥusnā* (the most beautiful names); systematized in hadith and Sufi devotion. Theological controversies: are the names eternal attributes (Ash'arite yes) or created (Mu'tazilite)?
- **Kabbalistic Shem-ha-Mephorash.** [[phase-5-012-sefer-yetzirah]] — the 22 Hebrew letters as cosmogonic elements; the 72-letter name derived from Exod 14:19–21; in *Sefer ha-Bahir* and Lurianic Kabbalah, divine names map onto the [[sefirot]]. Names as theurgic instrument.
- **Tantric *bīja*-mantras.** Sanskrit seed-syllables (OM, HRĪṂ, KLĪṂ, etc.) as the names-and-bodies of deities; recitation is identification with the deity. See Padoux, *Vāc* (1990).

## Disputes

- **Whether the Hebrew Bible's name-theology is uniquely radical or develops a wider ANE pattern.** Soulen and de Vaux: uniquely radical (the Tetragrammaton's ineffability is unparalleled). Smith (*The Origins of Biblical Monotheism*): the Hebrew development is on a continuum from ANE divine-name practices.
- **The post-exilic ban on pronouncing YHWH.** Date is disputed: some place it in the early Second Temple period (~400 BCE); the Septuagint already substitutes *Kyrios*; Qumran texts use the paleo-Hebrew script for the Tetragrammaton inside square-script Hebrew, suggesting it was already specially marked.
