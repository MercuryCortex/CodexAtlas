# Hermetic rebirth (palingenesia)

## Definition
The doctrine that a human being can undergo an *ontological transformation* — not metaphor, not moral improvement — by which they cease to be a merely embodied mortal soul subject to the twelve zodiacal vices ("tormentors") and become a divinized being in possession of *gnōsis*. The transformation is described in [[phase-4-075-corpus-hermeticum-xiii-rebirth|CH XIII]] (the *Secret Discourse on the Mountain*) as the expulsion of the twelve tormentors by ten divine powers; the reborn person is *theou pais* — "child of God" — and joins the divine chorus singing the secret hymn. The Greek term is *palingenesia* (literally "again-birth"); the Coptic is *jpouna mmate* (~"second-genesis").

Diagnostic: the rebirth is staged as actually happening — the disciple Tat experiences the expulsion of the tormentors *during* the dialogue and exclaims "Father, I see the Universe and I see myself in Mind" (CH XIII.13). It is not a future eschatological hope; it is a present initiatory event. This sets it apart from Christian eschatological resurrection and aligns it with *initiation-here-and-now* mystery-cult religion.

## Earliest attestations
- **Hermetic**: [[phase-4-075-corpus-hermeticum-xiii-rebirth|CH XIII]] (the *Secret Discourse on the Mountain*) — the locus classicus, ~100–300 CE. Anticipated in [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i|CH I]] (Poimandres) §§24–26 (the soul's ascent and shedding of vices) and in [[phase-4-013-discourse-on-the-eighth-and-ninth|NHC VI,6]] (the *Discourse on the Eighth and Ninth* — visionary ascent with rebirth-overtones).
- **Hellenistic Jewish**: [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria|Philo of Alexandria]] uses *kainē genesis* and *anagennēthēnai* for spiritual transformation in *On the Special Laws*, *On the Cherubim* — independent of and contemporary with the Hermetic vocabulary.
- **Pauline Christian**: [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles|Paul]] — 2 Corinthians 5:17 (*kainē ktisis* — "new creation"); Galatians 6:15; Titus 3:5 (*loutron palingenesias* — "washing of regeneration"). The Pauline regeneration vocabulary is closer to the Hermetic than the Johannine — a striking lexical overlap that Tröger 1971 documents at length.
- **Johannine**: [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john|Gospel of John]] 3:3–8 — Jesus to Nicodemus: "unless one is born again [*gennēthē anōthen*] he cannot see the kingdom of God." Different vocabulary, same conceptual move.
- **Mystery-cult**: Apuleius *Metamorphoses* 11 (the Lucius-Isis initiation) speaks of *renatus quodammodo* — "in a manner reborn" — through Isiac initiation; structurally analogous Hellenistic mystery-cult attestation.
- **Mithraic**: the Mithraic cult-grade inscriptions (esp. CIMRM 423 and the *Liturgy of Mithras* PGM IV.475–829) speak of cosmic rebirth into the *aiōn*-grades.

## Variants
The texts agree on the basic structure (transformation by divine power, achieved in this life, marked by initiation, expressed in song or thanksgiving prayer) but disagree on the *mechanics*:

- **CH XIII (Hermetic)**: twelve tormentors expelled by ten divine powers; mediated by gnōsis given by God when the disciple is ready.
- **John 3 (Christian)**: born of "water and Spirit"; mediated by faith in the Son of Man's lifting up.
- **2 Cor 5:17 (Pauline)**: new creation "in Christ"; mediated by participation in Christ's death and resurrection.
- **Apuleius 11 (Isiac)**: ritual purification + initiatory vision; mediated by Isis-as-saving-goddess.
- **Mithraic**: ritual ascent through cult grades; mediated by Mithras-as-cosmocrator.

## Connections claimed
- **Christian dependence on Hermetic?** 19th-c. Reitzenstein argued Christian regeneration vocabulary derives from Hermetic palingenesia. Tröger 1971, Festugière, and modern consensus reject this: shared late-antique Mediterranean substrate, not direct dependence.
- **Hermetic dependence on Christian?** Some early-20th-c. scholars argued the reverse. Also rejected by modern consensus: CH XIII shows no Christian theological vocabulary.
- **Shared Hellenistic-Jewish substrate?** Dodd 1935 argued for a common Alexandrian Hellenistic-Jewish substrate (Philonic, Wisdom-of-Solomon, late LXX) feeding both Hermetic and Christian regeneration vocabulary. **Currently the dominant scholarly position.**
- **Independent invention?** Festugière allowed for substantial parallel-but-independent development.

The current consensus is best summarized as: *parallel currents in the same Greco-Roman religious-philosophical milieu, both drawing on Hellenistic-Jewish vocabulary, neither derivative of the other.* This makes Hermetic palingenesia the **single most useful comparandum** for understanding the Pauline-Johannine regeneration idiom in its non-Christian context.
