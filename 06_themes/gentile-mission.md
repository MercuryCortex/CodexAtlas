# Gentile Mission

The early Christian project — radically novel within Second Temple Judaism — of preaching the Jewish messiah Jesus directly to non-Jews (*ta ethnē*, the *gōyim*, "the nations") and of incorporating them into the eschatological people of God *without* requiring full conversion to Torah-observant Judaism (circumcision, food laws, Sabbath in its halakhic detail). The mission's theological architect is Paul of Tarsus; the institutional precedent is set at the Jerusalem Conference (~49 CE, Acts 15; Gal 2); the Pauline letters defend and theorize the practice; the Synoptics and Acts narrativize it backwards into Jesus's ministry; later patristic theology supersessionalizes it. The Gentile Mission is the structural origin of *Christianity as a distinct religion* (rather than as a Jewish messianic sect).

## Manifestations
- **Isaianic warrant:** [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]] — Isa 49:6 ("I will give you as a light to the nations, that my salvation may reach to the end of the earth"); Isa 42:6; the "servant-songs" of Deutero-Isaiah are repeatedly cited in Acts and Paul as scriptural warrant for the Gentile mission. The Gentile mission claims Hebrew-prophetic-eschatological roots.
- **Pauline theological core:** [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Galatians and Romans are the master-texts: the Abrahamic blessing extends to *the nations* (Gal 3:8 quoting Gen 12:3); the Law was a *paidagōgos* until Christ (Gal 3:24); in Christ "there is neither Jew nor Greek" (Gal 3:28); Gentiles in Christ are grafted into Israel (Rom 11). Paul's apostleship is constitutively *to the Gentiles* (Rom 11:13).
- **Markan and Matthean trajectories:** [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — Mark's Syrophoenician woman pericope (Mk 7:24–30) prefigures Gentile inclusion under Jesus's reluctant table-extension. [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] — Matthew's *Great Commission* (Mt 28:18–20, "go therefore and make disciples of all nations") is the most explicit retrojected mandate, balancing Matthew's otherwise strongly Jewish-Christian profile (Mt 10:5–6).
- **Luke-Acts narrative:** [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] — Acts is structured as the Gentile-mission narrative: Cornelius (Acts 10), the Jerusalem Conference (Acts 15), the three Pauline missionary journeys, climaxing at Rome (Acts 28). Luke's theological architecture: the Spirit drives the Gentile mission against the Jerusalem-conservatives' reluctance.

## Disputes
- **The "Paul within Judaism" reading.** The older Protestant-Lutheran reading saw Paul as the founder of Gentile-supersessionist Christianity, abandoning Judaism. The "New Perspective" (Sanders, Dunn, Wright) and especially the "Paul within Judaism" school (Nanos, Zetterholm, Eisenbaum, Fredriksen 2017) argue Paul remained a Torah-observant Jew whose Gentile mission was a Jewish-eschatological project: bringing the nations *to* the God of Israel in the last days, *as Gentiles* (not as proselytes). The historical accuracy of "supersessionist Paul" is sharply contested.
- **Jewish-Christian schism.** When did the Gentile mission produce a *separate religion*? Most scholars date the major rupture to the second century (post-Bar-Kokhba 135 CE), not the first; Daniel Boyarin (*Border Lines* 2004) argues the categorical distinction "Christianity vs. Judaism" was constructed gradually by both communities through the 4th c. Constantinian period.
- **Harnack's missiology.** Adolf von Harnack's *Mission und Ausbreitung* (1902) remains a landmark study; quantitative-sociological revisions (Stark 1996, *The Rise of Christianity*) revise the demographic mechanics but accept Harnack's basic story. The Gentile mission's *sociological* success — by some estimates 5% of the Empire by 300 CE, ~50% by 400 — is the precondition for Christian-imperial supersession of polytheist Rome.
