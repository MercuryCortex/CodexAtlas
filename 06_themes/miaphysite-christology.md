# Miaphysite Christology

## Definition
**Miaphysite Christology** is the Christological position articulated by Cyril of Alexandria in the early 5th century, defended at the Council of Ephesus (431) against Nestorian dyophysitism, and made constitutive for the **Oriental Orthodox** communion after their rejection of the Council of Chalcedon's (451) "two natures in one person" formula. The formula: *mia physis tou theou logou sesarkōmenē* — "one incarnate nature of God the Word." The "one nature" is *not* a single absorbed-divine nature (which is the Monophysite position of Eutyches — heretical to both Chalcedon *and* the Miaphysites) but the *united incarnate* nature in which divine and human elements are conjoined "without confusion, without change, without division, without separation" (a phrase Chalcedon itself uses with slightly different metaphysical-conceptual weight).

The Ethiopian self-designation is **Tewahedo** (Ge'ez "made one, unified") — the operative theological-conceptual term in Ethiopian liturgy, dogmatics, and church name (the full name *Ethiopian Orthodox **Tewahedo*** Church carries the Christological self-definition in its title). [[zara-yaqob-emperor]]'s regnal name *Haile Tewahedo* ("Power of the Unified") and the modern imperial regnal *Haile Selassie* ("Power of the Trinity") both encode this theological-Christological-Trinitarian framework directly into Ethiopian royal nomenclature.

The Miaphysite position is in *substantive Christological convergence* with the Chalcedonian dyophysite position — both reject Nestorius, both reject Eutyches, both affirm the union of divine and human in Christ; the modern ecumenical dialogues (Aarhus 1964; Geneva 1990) have affirmed this convergence formally. The 1500-year schism is, on modern reading, largely terminological-political rather than substantively-Christological. Communion has not been formally restored but theological convergence is the contemporary scholarly consensus.

## Earliest attestations

- **Cyril of Alexandria (412–444)** — articulates the formula in his *Twelve Anathemas* against Nestorius (430), the *Letter to Eulogius*, the *Letter to Acacius of Melitene*, and the systematic *On the Unity of Christ*. The phrase *mia physis tou theou logou sesarkōmenē* appears most decisively in Cyril's *Second Letter to Succensus* (~437). Cyril holds the formula is from Athanasius (citing the apocryphal *On the Incarnation* and the *De fide* — actually pseudo-Athanasian Apollinarian-era compositions), an attribution-error that did not affect Cyril's own use but complicates the formula's pre-Cyril attestation.
- **Council of Ephesus (431)** — Cyrillian victory over Nestorius; Mary's *theotokos* title affirmed; the Cyrillian Christological framework becomes binding for the Eastern Christian church (though the precise *mia physis* phrasing is not yet conciliar).
- **Council of Chalcedon (451)** — the dyophysite formula is adopted; Dioscorus of Alexandria (Cyril's successor) is deposed; the schism begins.
- **Severus of Antioch (Patriarch 512–518; in exile 518–538)** — the most systematic Miaphysite theologian; his *Cathedral Homilies* and polemical correspondence consolidate the position.
- **Ethiopia:** received via the Cyrillian-Alexandrian filiation through [[frumentius]] *before* Chalcedon (the consecration is c. 328–335). Subsequently reinforced by the [[the-nine-saints]] mission in the post-Chalcedon refugee migration.

## Variants

- **The Coptic-Severan version** — sophisticated dogmatic articulation; most thoroughly developed by Severus and subsequent Coptic theologians.
- **The Syriac-Jacobite version** — Jacob Baradaeus's reconstituted Syriac hierarchy; theologically aligned with Coptic Severans.
- **The Armenian version** — Council of Dvin (506); the Armenian church's distinctive theological-liturgical character within the broader Miaphysite communion.
- **The Ethiopian Tewahedo version** — received via Coptic Alexandria; systematized in Ge'ez by [[giyorgis-of-sagla]] (*[[phase-5-036-mashafa-mistir-giyorgis]]*) and [[zara-yaqob-emperor]] (*[[phase-5-038-mashafa-berhan]]*); defended against Catholic critique by [[galawdewos-emperor]] (*Confessio Claudii* 1555).

## Connections claimed

- **Modern ecumenical-Christological convergence (Aarhus 1964 → Geneva 1990).** Joint theological commissions of Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox theologians affirmed that both communions confess the same Christological substance, differently terminologically; formal communion has not been restored but the substantive convergence is the contemporary consensus.
- **Distinction from Eutychian Monophysitism.** Modern scholarship (Frend 1972; Grillmeier 1995; Davis 2004) uses *Miaphysite* (one *united* nature) rather than *Monophysite* (one *single* nature) precisely to avoid conflation with Eutyches's heresy. The Oriental Orthodox churches reject the "Monophysite" label.
