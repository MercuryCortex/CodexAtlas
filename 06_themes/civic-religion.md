# Civic Religion

The diffuse but coordinated religious-symbolic framework that sacralizes a *polity* (city, empire, nation, federation) — its founding, its constitutional documents, its sacred dead, its rituals of memorial — *as if* it were a religious tradition, while typically operating outside or athwart the institutionally-recognized "religions" of its citizens. The category was given its modern name by Robert Bellah's 1967 essay "Civil Religion in America," which argued the United States has a discernible civic-religious tradition (the founding-fathers as patriarchs, the Declaration and Constitution as sacred texts, Memorial Day and the Fourth as ritual calendar, the Lincoln Memorial as temple). Bellah explicitly drew on Rousseau's *Social Contract* (1762) IV.8 on *religion civile*. Ancient analogs include Roman *religio publica*, Athenian polis-cult; modern analogs include French Revolutionary deist cults, Soviet Lenin-cult, Japanese pre-1945 State Shinto.

## Manifestations

- **Roman *religio publica*.** The official-state cult of the Roman gods, distinct from private *religio* and from foreign cults. The Augustan religious-restoration (after Civil-War-era neglect); the imperial cult as the *religio*-political binding of the empire. Rives 2007 is a standard overview.
- **American civil religion.** [[phase-7-014-bellah-civil-religion-essay]] — Bellah's three founding moments (Revolution = founding, Civil War = trial-of-faith, civil rights era = renewal); the founders as patriarchs; the Constitution as scripture; Memorial Day, Fourth of July, Veterans Day, MLK Day as liturgical calendar; the National Cemetery system as sacred geography. Bellah explicitly notes that civil religion overlaps with but is not identical to Christianity; presidents invoke "God" generically.
- **French Revolutionary deist cults.** The *Culte de la Raison* (1793, Hébertist); the *Culte de l'Être suprême* (1794, Robespierre's Festival of the Supreme Being). The most explicit modern attempt to *create* a state-religion ex nihilo; collapsed with Thermidor.
- **Japanese State Shinto (1868–1945).** The Meiji restoration's separation of Shinto from Buddhism, the creation of "non-religious" State Shinto requiring participation by all citizens, the Yasukuni Shrine and the *kokutai* ideology. Disestablished after 1945 by American occupation directive.
- **Soviet / Maoist secular cults.** The Lenin Mausoleum and the Lenin-cult; the Stalin and Mao personality-cults with explicit religious-iconographic features. The Soviet calendar (October Revolution Day, May Day); the cult of revolutionary martyrs.
- **Modern constitutional patriotism.** Habermas's *Verfassungspatriotismus* — a deliberate post-1945 German effort to construct a non-ethnonationalist civic loyalty around the constitution and the post-Holocaust commitment to *Wiedergutmachung*. A *minimalist* civil religion designed to avoid pre-1945 excesses.

## Disputes

- **Whether civil religion is genuinely religious or only metaphorically so.** Bellah argues genuinely; critics (Hammond, Mathisen) argue the religious-borrowing is rhetorical and the underlying phenomenon is political-symbolic. The line is theoretically hard to draw.
- **Whether American civil religion is uniformly affirmed across the population or contested (especially between progressive and conservative civil-religious frames).** Recent scholarship (Gorski, *American Covenant* 2017) emphasizes the contested, evolving nature; conservative civil religion centers Christian-republican synthesis, progressive civil religion centers civil-rights / immigrant-inclusion narrative.
- **Whether the post-1989 globalized order has generated *transnational* civic-religious forms (UN, EU, human-rights discourse) that complement or compete with national civil religions.** Habermas, Beck.
