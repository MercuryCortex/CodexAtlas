# Anticosmic

The theological-ethical orientation in which the *cosmos itself* is the problem — not merely human sin within an otherwise-good world, but the world as such is the prison from which salvation is escape. The cosmos is the work of the demiurge, the archons rule the planetary spheres, the elements are hostile, embodiment is captivity, salvation is *exodus* from the world rather than reconciliation with it. The clearest anticosmic positions are Gnostic (especially the Sethian and Valentinian schools), Marcionite, and Manichaean.

- [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]] — the systematic Sethian-Gnostic anticosmic mythology.
- [[phase-4-006-hypostasis-of-the-archons]] — the archons as world-rulers actively hostile to the spiritual seed.
- [[phase-4-015-kephalaia-of-the-teacher]] — Manichaean cosmology: the entire material cosmos is the result of the catastrophic mixture of Light and Darkness; salvation is the gradual separation.

Williams 1996 *Rethinking 'Gnosticism'* argued that "anticosmic" is a more diagnostic and less question-begging category than the contested term "Gnosticism" itself — and that even so, the texts often classified as Gnostic are not uniformly anticosmic; the cosmos is sometimes ambivalent, partly beautiful, partly intelligently designed by the demiurge under Sophia's tutelage.

Distinguish from: world-affirming Hermetism (the cosmos is the great miracle); from Stoic-Christian creational theology (the cosmos is good though fallen); from Hindu world-orientation (samsara is not evil but soteriologically irrelevant).
