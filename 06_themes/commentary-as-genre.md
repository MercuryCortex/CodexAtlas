# Commentary as Genre

The technology by which religious traditions *reproduce themselves* over centuries: not by re-writing the founding text but by producing layer upon layer of authoritative commentary on it. Each layer interprets the prior; subsequent layers comment on both founding text and prior commentary. The result is a stratified, conversational textual continuity in which the tradition's *present* engagement with the text is always mediated through generations of past engagements.

## Manifestations

- **Vedic *Brāhmaṇa* on *Saṃhitā*.** [[phase-2-001-rig-veda-family-books]] generates the *Brāhmaṇa* prose commentaries (~900–600 BCE), which generate the *Āraṇyaka* forest-texts, which generate the *Upaniṣad*s. Four-layer cumulative structure within the Vedic canon itself.
- **Mishnah-Gemara.** [[phase-4-031-mishnah]] (~200 CE) is structurally a commentary on the Hebrew Bible's halakhic content; the *Gemara* (in the [[phase-4-033-babylonian-talmud]] ~500 CE, and the Jerusalem Talmud ~400 CE) is the commentary on the Mishnah. Rashi (11th c.) is the commentary on the Talmud. Tosafot (12th–13th c.) is the commentary on Rashi-and-the-Talmud. Five-layer stratified textual conversation across ~1500 years.
- **Patristic biblical commentary.** Origen's massive scriptural commentaries (3rd c.) — *Hexapla*, *Commentary on John*, *Commentary on Matthew*, etc. — inaugurate the Christian commentary tradition. Augustine on Genesis (multiple commentaries), the Cappadocians, Jerome (*Vulgate* as itself a commentary-by-translation). de Lubac 1959 traces the four-fold senses tradition (literal, allegorical, moral, anagogical) through medieval commentary.
- **Islamic *tafsīr*.** Al-Ṭabarī's *Jāmiʿ al-bayān* (10th c.), al-Zamakhsharī's *Kashshāf* (12th c., Mu'tazilite), al-Rāzī's *Mafātīḥ al-ghayb* (12th–13th c., philosophical), Ibn Kathīr's *Tafsīr* (14th c., traditionist), modern *tafsīr* (Sayyid Qutb's *Fī Ẓilāl al-Qurʾān*). *Tafsīr* is the central Islamic theological-textual genre.
- **Vedantic *bhāṣya*.** [[phase-5-005-shankara-brahma-sutra-bhasya]] — Shankara's commentary on Bādarāyaṇa's *Brahma-sūtras*; Ramanuja's *Śrībhāṣya* on the same; Madhva's *bhāṣya*. The three major Vedānta schools constitute themselves as *commentaries* on the same foundational sutras. *Bhāṣya*-on-*bhāṣya* layering follows.
- **Sufi *tafsīr*.** Sahl al-Tustarī, Sulamī's *Ḥaqāʾiq al-Tafsīr*, Qushayrī's *Laṭāʾif al-ishārāt*, Ibn ʿArabī's *Futūḥāt* (commentary-in-spirit), Rūzbihān Baqlī. Esoteric-mystical commentary as a distinct subgenre within Islamic *tafsīr*.
- **Scholastic Christian *summa* / *quaestio*.** Peter Lombard's *Sentences* (~1150) and the entire scholastic commentary-tradition on it (Aquinas's *Scriptum super Sententiis*, Bonaventure's, Duns Scotus's). [[phase-5-024-aquinas-summa-theologiae]] is a self-conscious systematic-commentary on the entire prior tradition's debates. The scholastic *quaestio* / *disputatio* form ritualizes the commentary-conversation.
- **Zoharic on Tanakh.** [[phase-5-x-zohar]] (13th c., Moshe de León) is structurally a Kabbalistic commentary on the Torah; subsequent Kabbalistic literature commentates on the Zohar.

## Significance

- **Commentary is how religious traditions *do theology*.** Most pre-modern theology is, formally, commentary. The modern Protestant move toward "systematic theology" independent of biblical-commentary genre is the historical exception, not the rule.
- The cross-tradition similarity of the commentary-genre's structure (stratification, layering, hyper-textuality, the conversation across centuries) suggests it is a near-universal solution to the problem of *how to keep a foundational text alive without rewriting it*.
- Recognizing commentary-as-genre reshapes the historian's task: many supposedly "different" texts in a tradition are best read as nodes in a continuous commentary-network rather than as discrete works.

## Disputes

- **Whether the "stratification of senses" (literal-allegorical-moral-anagogical in Christian; *peshat-remez-derash-sod* in Jewish; *zāhir-bāṭin* in Islamic) is a genuine cross-tradition convergence or a Patristic Christian export retroactively projected.** de Lubac argues genuine; later scholars (Walfish, Smalley) more cautious. The Jewish PaRDeS scheme is medieval; whether earlier rabbinic exegesis presupposes it is contested.
- **The relationship of commentary to authority.** Strauss argued (*Persecution and the Art of Writing* 1952) that medieval Jewish and Islamic philosophical-commentary is structurally esoteric, with the philosopher's real views hidden in commentary-form to evade orthodoxy. The thesis remains controversial.
