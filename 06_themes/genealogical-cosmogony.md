# Genealogical Cosmogony

Cosmogony expressed through genealogical lineage rather than craftsman-creation or combat-creation: the world and all its constituents come into being as a sequence of *births* — cosmic entities, forces, and eventually living beings emerge through the generative coupling of prior cosmic entities. In this type, **ontology = genealogy**: to be real is to have been born; to understand the cosmos is to trace the lineage of all things to their primordial ancestors.

## Core definition

The three structurally distinct cosmogony-types in comparative religion are:
1. **Combat cosmogony** (*Chaoskampf*): a heroic deity defeats chaos-monster → cosmos from its body (Mesopotamian Marduk/Tiamat, Canaanite Baal/Yam, Hebrew Yahweh/Leviathan-echoes, Norse Thor/Jörmungandr)
2. **Craftsman cosmogony**: a divine artisan forms the cosmos from pre-existing matter or nothing (Plato's *Demiurge*, Genesis P, Ptah, Yahweh in J)
3. **Genealogical cosmogony**: the cosmos unfolds as a sequence of divine births; the genealogy of cosmic entities *is* the cosmogony

Genealogical cosmogony is arguably the most archaic form — it appears in the earliest-recoverable strata of Indo-European tradition (Hesiod's *Theogony*, Vedic Purusha Sukta) and in Pacific traditions (Hawaiian, Maori) that have no documented contact with the Mediterranean.

## Attestations

**Hawaiian** ([[phase-8-010-kumulipo]]): The 2,102-line genealogical creation chant, composed for the birth of the ruling chief Ka-I-i-mamao (~1700 CE, but drawing on far older oral tradition), unfolds the entire cosmos and all life-forms as a genealogical sequence from *pō* (primal darkness) through 16 *wā* (eras) to the Hawaiian royal line. Sea creatures → land creatures → humans → gods → royalty. The chant is simultaneously a cosmogony, a genealogy, and a legitimation of royal authority — all three functions folded into one because in Hawaiian thought, genealogy *is* ontology.

**Maori** ([[phase-8-011-maori-cosmogonic-chants]]): *Whakapapa* — Maori genealogy — is one of the most important conceptual tools in the tradition; *whakapapa* means literally to "place in layers" and is the Maori way of understanding relationships between all things. The cosmogonic genealogy begins in Te Kore (the Void), proceeds through Te Pō (the Night), and reaches Sky Father Ranginui and Earth Mother Papatūānuku, whose children (Tāne, Tū, Rongo, Tangaroa, Haumia, Rūaumoko) fill the world. Each of the children is the progenitor of a domain of being.

**Greek** ([[phase-2-009-hesiod-theogony-works-and-days]]): Hesiod's *Theogony* ("Birth of the Gods") is pure genealogical cosmogony: from Chaos come Gaia, Tartarus, and Eros; from Gaia (and Night) come successive generations of cosmic entities; the universe is constituted by the genealogical proliferation of divine beings, culminating in Zeus's sovereignty. The word *theogony* itself means "origin of the gods through birth."

**Vedic** ([[phase-1-031-rigveda]], Purusha Sukta 10.90): The cosmic Purusha's body-sacrificed produces the four *varṇas* and all created things — a variant that combines genealogical with sacrificial cosmogony, but still frames creation as *proceeding from* a primal entity's generative potential.

## Cross-tradition significance

**MASSIVE-win**: Genealogical cosmogony is the bridge connecting Hawaiian, Maori, Greek, Vedic, and (partially) Egyptian cosmogonies — all expressing the deep cognitive pattern that reality is structured like a family tree, that origins are generative, and that to name a thing is to place it in its genealogical web.

The Maori *whakapapa* as an epistemological system — where genealogical recitation is simultaneously philosophical analysis — has no exact Western parallel until Hegel's dialectical unfolding of the Absolute or Whitehead's process philosophy. Alpers (1964) and Beckwith (1951) both emphasize the philosophical depth being done in the form of genealogical enumeration.

Structural contrast: Hebrew P-source cosmogony (Genesis 1) is craftsman-type (*God creates* the world by command and action); Hebrew J-source has a craftsman-artisan god shaping the human from clay; both differ fundamentally from the genealogical Pacific and Greek forms.
