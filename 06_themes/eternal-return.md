# Eternal Return

Two related but distinct concepts share this label:

**(1) Eliade's archaic eternal return**: in *Cosmos and History* (1949), Eliade argued that "archaic" (pre-historical-consciousness) cultures experience time as recurrent — sacred time is the *in illo tempore* of mythic origin, ritually re-enacted in the new year festival, the harvest, the consecration of a temple. Profane time has no real existence; what *is* is the eternal mythic pattern, repeated. Eliade contrasts this with the linear, historical, irreversible time of the Hebrew prophets and (consequently) Christianity and modernity.

**(2) Nietzsche's eternal recurrence** (*ewige Wiederkunft des Gleichen*): a thought-experiment in *The Gay Science* §341 and *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* — *if everything that has happened were to recur infinitely, exactly the same, could you affirm it?* Functions as an existential test of life-affirmation, not a cosmological claim. Whether Nietzsche believed the cosmology is debated (his unpublished notes argue for it on thermodynamic grounds).

The Hindu *yuga* cycles, Stoic *ekpyrosis* (universal conflagration followed by *palingenesis* / rebirth in identical sequence), Mayan calendar cycles, and Greek myth's "Great Year" are typical cited parallels.
