# Bronze Age Substrate of Greek Religion

The thesis — established after the [[event-linear-b-decipherment-1952|decipherment of Linear B in 1952]] — that **the Olympian pantheon is at least 800 years older than Homer and Hesiod**, with most major Olympians already attested by name in 13th-c. BCE Mycenaean palace tablets, embedded in an organized cult-system and palace economy.

## What changed in 1952
Before Linear B was deciphered, Greek religion was studied as essentially an Iron Age phenomenon: the Olympians emerged in the post-Mycenaean Dark Age (~-1100 to -800), crystallized into the canonical Olympic pantheon in the Archaic period, and were authoritatively systematized by Homer and Hesiod. The Mycenaean palaces, known from archaeology (Schliemann at Mycenae, Evans at Knossos), preserved no religious texts that anyone could read — Arthur Evans believed the Knossos tablets recorded a pre-Greek "Minoan" language.

Ventris's 1952 decipherment of Linear B as **Greek** changed this overnight. The Mycenaean tablets attest by name:

- **Diwo (= Zeus)** — Knossos, Pylos.
- **e-ra (= Hera)** — Pylos.
- **po-se-da-o (= Poseidon)** — Pylos especially; more prominent than Zeus in the Pylian rosters, suggesting Poseidon was a higher-ranking Mycenaean god whose status was demoted in post-Mycenaean reorganization.
- **a-ta-na po-ti-ni-ja (= Mistress Athena)** — Knossos.
- **e-ma-a2 (= Hermes)** — Pylos.
- **a-ti-mi-te (= Artemis)** — Pylos.
- **di-wo-nu-so (= Dionysus)** — Pylos, Khania. *The major surprise:* Dionysus had been treated as a "late arrival" from Thrace/Phrygia; Linear B places him at the Mycenaean cult-center.
- **Enyalios** (later an epithet of Ares) — Knossos.
- **Paean** (later an epithet of Apollo) — Knossos. (Whether this attests Apollo specifically is contested.)
- Plus **Potnia** ("Mistress"), **Diwija** (a feminine of Zeus), and various local Mycenaean divinities that did not survive into the Iron Age Olympic system.

## Significance
- **Compose-date pushback for Greek religion.** The Olympian pantheon is now datable to at least ~-1400 BCE (date of the earliest Knossos tablets). The Iron Age literary canonization (Homer, Hesiod) is the *systematization* of a much older inherited cult-system, not its origin.
- **Continuity vs. rupture across the Dark Age.** The question of how much *cult content* (myth, ritual, theology) carried across the ~300-year post-palace collapse, vs. how much was reconstructed after, is the central interpretive question (Burkert 1985 §1.3). The names clearly survived; the content is harder to track.
- **Connection to the broader ANE substrate.** The Mycenaean Bronze Age Greeks were in active contact with Hittites, Hurrians, Egyptians, Levantines, and Cypriotes. The Mycenaean Greek pantheon's structural similarity to Hittite-Hurrian succession theology ([[phase-1-028-kumarbi-cycle]]) is rooted in this Bronze Age cultural network, not in later Orientalizing-period contact only. Bachvarova 2016 argues for continuous Aegean-Anatolian poetic-religious transmission across this whole period.
- **Local cult vs. canonical literature.** Linear B attests a *palace-administrative* religion (offerings, rosters, herd-counts) rather than a literary one. The canonical Olympian *mythology* of Homer and Hesiod is a later overlay on this cultic substrate. Some Mycenaean deities (Potnia, Diwija) did not make it into the canonical Olympian list at all.

## Disputes
- **Specific theonym readings.** Some readings (Athena, Apollo, Ares-as-Ares-vs-Enyalios) are contested in detail; the broader substrate-attestation thesis is not.
- **Cult continuity across the Dark Age.** How much survived is debated.
- **Dating of palace tablets.** Most date to ~-1300 to -1180. Some Knossos tablets are dated as early as ~-1400; some scholars push the Knossos destruction (and its tablets) later. None of these refinements affect the broader thesis.
