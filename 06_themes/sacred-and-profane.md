# Sacred and Profane

The categorical opposition between the *sacred* (set-apart, charged, dangerous-powerful, ordering) and the *profane* (everyday, ordinary, available). Codified by Durkheim (1912) as the operational definition of religion itself ("a unified system of beliefs and practices relative to sacred things, i.e. things set apart and forbidden"), and lavishly developed by Mircea Eliade as the master-frame for the history of religions. The dichotomy structures sacred-time vs. profane-time, sacred-space (axis mundi, temple, *omphalos*) vs. profane-space, and sacred-action (ritual, hierophany) vs. profane-action.

## Manifestations
- **Durkheimian-sociological:** [[phase-7-010-elementary-forms-religious-life]] — Durkheim's 1912 study of Australian totemism makes the sacred/profane distinction *the* operational definition of religion: religion is what is organized around sacred things. The "sacred" is a sociological projection of collective effervescence onto external objects.
- **Otto's phenomenology:** [[phase-7-011-idea-of-the-holy]] — Rudolf Otto's 1917 *Das Heilige* describes the *numinous* (the sacred's psychological-affective character) as *mysterium tremendum et fascinans* — the awe-fear-fascination structure of religious experience. Distinct from Durkheim: experiential, not sociological.
- **Eliade's *hierophany*-program:** [[phase-7-021-patterns-comparative-religion]], [[phase-7-022-sacred-and-profane]] — Eliade's *Patterns in Comparative Religion* (1949) and *The Sacred and the Profane* (1957) make the sacred/profane dichotomy the master-frame of an entire phenomenological *history of religions*. *Hierophany* = "manifestation of the sacred"; every religion is a system of hierophanies. The *axis mundi*, sacred *centre*, *illud tempus* of origin, eternal return, are all developed from this base. Tenured as standard mid-20th-century history-of-religions vocabulary.
- **Late-19th-century anthropological:** [[phase-7-007-golden-bough]] — Frazer's *Golden Bough* (1890–1915) anticipates the dichotomy without formalizing it: taboo, mana, sacrifice, royal sacred-kingship.

## Disputes
- **J. Z. Smith's critique.** [[phase-7-021-patterns-comparative-religion]] is decisively criticized by Jonathan Z. Smith (*Map Is Not Territory* 1978, *To Take Place* 1987). Smith argues Eliade's sacred/profane dichotomy is a phenomenological *imposition* on materials whose internal categories are different; the *axis mundi* and the *illud tempus* are Eliade's universals projected on locally specific ritual structures. The post-Smith generation in History of Religions (Bruce Lincoln, Russell McCutcheon) treats Eliade as ideologically suspect and the dichotomy as constructive rather than descriptive.
- **Eliade's politics.** Eliade's pre-WWII Romanian fascist sympathies and the conservative-traditionalist subtext of his "modern man's loss of the sacred" laments have been scrutinized (Wasserstrom 1999); the *sacred* in Eliade often functions as a vehicle for a Traditionalist critique of modernity.
- **Survival of the category.** Despite the post-Smith critique, "sacred space" / "sacred time" / "hierophany" remain in common scholarly use, and the underlying intuition — that ritual and religion organize *some* distinction between charged and ordinary — survives even where Eliade's master narrative is rejected.
