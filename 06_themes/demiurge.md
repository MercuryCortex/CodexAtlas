# Demiurge

A *craftsman* creator — distinct from a creator-by-fiat and from a primal generator. The demiurge shapes pre-existing matter according to an intelligible model. The Greek word *dēmiourgos* ("public worker / craftsman") originally meant a skilled tradesman; Plato elevates it to cosmological prominence; later Gnostic and Hermetic traditions invert it.

## Manifestations
- **Plato's *Timaeus*:** [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — the Demiurge is a good craftsman who looks to the eternal Forms and shapes the chaotic *chōra* into the orderly cosmos. Not omnipotent (matter resists) and not the highest god; subordinate to the Good but supreme as cosmic maker. The single most influential cosmological text of antiquity.
- **Middle Platonist development:** Numenius and others stratify the Demiurge into a "first god" / "second god" — preparing the ground for later Gnostic and Neoplatonic readings.
- **Sethian Gnosticism:** [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]] — Yaldabaoth, ignorant offspring of Sophia, creates the material cosmos in imitation of higher realities he cannot see. The Platonic craftsman is *inverted*: still a craftsman, but stupid, blind, malevolent. See [[demiurge-gnostic]], [[yaldabaoth]].
- **Valentinian Gnosticism:** Softer — the Demiurge is psychic, not pneumatic; unwitting rather than evil; an instrument of Sophia's eventual restoration. Ptolemy's *Letter to Flora* gives the classic Valentinian taxonomy.
- **Hermetism:** [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] — a second mind, *Nous Demiurgos*, generates the seven Governors of the spheres. Less polemical than the Sethian version; the craftsman is good but subordinate.
- **Neoplatonism:** [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — Plotinus reads the *Timaeus* allegorically and identifies the Demiurge with Nous (the second hypostasis), defending Plato against the Gnostics in *Ennead* II.9.
- **Mandaean:** [[ptahil]] — flawed craftsman of the material world; not quite as malevolent as Yaldabaoth.

## Disputes
- **Was Plato's Demiurge meant literally or mythically?** Ancient and modern debate; Aristotle treated the *Timaeus* cosmogony as literal, most later Platonists (and modern Cornford, Vlastos) read it as expository myth.
- **The Sethian-Plotinian rivalry.** Plotinus knew Gnostic writers personally (Porphyry, *Life of Plotinus* 16) and wrote *Ennead* II.9 ("Against the Gnostics") specifically against them — the inversion of the Demiurge was the live theological scandal of 3rd-century philosophical religion.
- **Hebrew creator vs. Gnostic demiurge.** Whether the Sethian identification of the OT creator with Yaldabaoth derives from Jewish heterodoxy, anti-Jewish polemic, or Platonic philosophical critique. Pearson (1990) emphasizes Jewish provenance.
