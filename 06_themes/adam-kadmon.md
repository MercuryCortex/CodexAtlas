# Adam Kadmon

## The Figure

**Adam Kadmon** (Hebrew: אדם קדמון, "Primordial Man" or "Ancient/Original Adam") is the first and highest manifestation of the Infinite (*Ein Sof*) into structured being — the primordial cosmic Human whose body *is* the configuration of the divine *sefirot*, and whose emanated lights contain all the lower worlds within themselves.

In Lurianic Kabbalah (Isaac Luria, Safed, ~1570; transmitted by Ḥayyim Vital, *Etz Ḥayyim*, ~1573), Adam Kadmon is the central figure of cosmogony: before the familiar ten sefirot exist, before Atzilut (the world of Emanation) comes into being, before anything we could call "world" — there is Adam Kadmon. He is not *in* the universe; the universe is *in* him. Or rather: the lights that flow from him will, through the catastrophe of *shevirat ha-kelim*, generate the universe.

Adam Kadmon must be distinguished from:
- **The biblical Adam** (Genesis 2) — the first human creature, formed from dust, a creaturely being; Adam Kadmon precedes and contains Adam ha-Rishon ("First Adam")
- **Metatron** — the "Lesser YHWH" of Hekhalot tradition, an angelic figure within creation; Adam Kadmon is the *partzuf* that precedes the angelic world
- **The sefirot** — which are *within* Adam Kadmon; the sefirot are the organs of his body

## Lurianic Cosmogony: Adam Kadmon in Full

Isaac Luria's cosmogony, systematized by Ḥayyim Vital in the *Etz Ḥayyim*, moves in four stages:

### 1. Tzimtzum (Contraction)
Ein Sof — the Infinite — contracts (*tzimtzum*) from within itself, creating an empty space (*ḥalal*) in which something other than the Infinite can exist. This is not physical withdrawal but a conceptual self-limitation. The divine withdraws *into itself* to make room for the world.

### 2. Adam Kadmon Emanated
Into the ḥalal, Ein Sof emanates the first structured light — **Adam Kadmon**. He is the form of the divine as *Anthropos* — the human form as the primary structure of being. His body is organized according to the arrangement of the sefirot (though at this level they are not yet the differentiated ten).

The *Zohar*'s *Idra Rabba* and *Idra Zuta* sections develop the first imagery: the *Arikh Anpin* ("Long-Suffering Face" / "Vast Countenance") as the topmost partzuf corresponds to Adam Kadmon's level — the divine face of infinite patience and mercy. Below it, *Abba* (Father) and *Imma* (Mother) emit *Zeir Anpin* (the "Short-Suffering" or "Small Face" — the active divine aspect) and *Nukvah* (the Feminine divine principle / Shekinah).

### 3. Shevirat ha-Kelim (Breaking of the Vessels)
Lights flow from Adam Kadmon's **eyes** (then later from ears, nose, and mouth). The "eyes" represent a high-intensity emanation — too intense for the receiving vessels (*kelim*) of the lower sefirot. Seven of the lower sefirot-vessels shatter (*shevirat ha-kelim*). Their shards (*kelipot*, "husks") fall, carrying sparks of divine light with them. This catastrophe:
- Creates the broken, fragmented reality of the material world
- Creates the domain of evil (*sitra aḥra*) from the kelipot
- Embeds divine sparks in material existence, requiring liberation

The body of Adam Kadmon is thus the *source* of both the divine light and — through the catastrophe — of the broken world that needs repair.

### 4. Tikkun (Repair)
The divine response to the shevirat is tikkun — the re-configuration of the broken lights into the stable partzuf-structures (*Atzilut*) and the three lower worlds (Beriah/Yetzirah/Assiyah). Human Torah-practice, prayer with proper *kavvanah* (intention), and mystical action all participate in the cosmic tikkun. The final tikkun = the restoration of Adam Kadmon's original wholeness — cosmic redemption as the repair of the Primordial Human.

## The Sefirot as Adam Kadmon's Body

The most vivid expression of Adam Kadmon's function is the *sefirot-tree as human body* diagram — the canonical Kabbalistic image that places:

- **Keter** (Crown) at the crown of the head
- **Ḥokhmah** (Wisdom) and **Binah** (Understanding) at the right and left brain
- **Ḥesed** (Loving-kindness) and **Gevurah/Din** (Strength/Judgment) at the right and left arms
- **Tiferet** (Beauty/Harmony) at the heart
- **Netzaḥ** (Victory) and **Hod** (Splendor) at the right and left legs
- **Yesod** (Foundation) at the generative center
- **Malkhut/Shekinah** (Kingdom/Presence) at the feet or the whole of the divine Feminine

This body is simultaneously:
- The cosmic structure of the divine mind
- The template of the human body (humans are Adam Kadmon in microcosm)
- The structure of the Torah (613 *mitzvot* = 613 limbs of Adam Kadmon's body)
- The structure of the heavenly Temple

The **microcosm-macrocosm** doctrine is built into the Kabbalistic Adam Kadmon: the human who performs a mitzvah is healing a limb of the divine body; the human who sins is damaging it.

## MASSIVE WIN: Cross-Tradition Parallels

The Adam Kadmon figure is one of the richest convergence-nodes in the Atlas, with structural parallels across five independent traditions:

### 1. Vedic Puruṣa (Rig Veda 10.90, ~1200 BCE)
The cosmic Puruṣa — "thousand-headed, thousand-eyed, thousand-footed" — is the primordial Person whose *dismemberment* in the cosmogonic sacrifice creates the universe. The cosmos is the body of the Cosmic Man; the caste system and the natural order are limbs of one body.

| Vedic Puruṣa | Lurianic Adam Kadmon |
|---|---|
| Primordial cosmic Person precedes the universe | Precedes the sefirot and all worlds |
| Universe is emanated from his body | Worlds flow from the lights of his eyes/ears/nose/mouth |
| Dismembered sacrifice creates the cosmos | Shevirat ha-kelim (the breaking) creates the fallen world |
| Body contains all castes / social order | Body contains 613 limbs = 613 commandments |
| No independent existence of the universe from him | All worlds are contained within Adam Kadmon |

The parallel is structural, not genetic — no transmission channel has been established. But the convergence demonstrates that the "cosmos is the body of the Primordial Human" is an insight that multiple traditions reached independently.

### 2. Gnostic Anthropos (~2nd c. CE)
The Hermetic *Poimandres* (Corpus Hermeticum I, ~2nd c.) describes the **heavenly Anthrōpos** — the divine image who descends through the planetary spheres, falls in love with Nature (matter), and generates embodied humans who carry a divine spark.

The Sethian Gnostic texts (*Apocryphon of John*, *Gospel of the Egyptians*) develop the **Adamas** figure — the heavenly, perfect Adam of whom the earthly Adam is the demiurgic imitation. The Gnostic Anthropos:
- Is pre-existent in the divine Pleroma
- Has a "fall" that generates the material world
- Contains the divine sparks dispersed into matter
- Is restored through gnosis / the Redeemer's work

The Lurianic Adam Kadmon is this exact structure, with the catastrophe (shevirat) replacing the Gnostic fall, and tikkun replacing Gnostic apokatastasis.

### 3. Zoroastrian Gayōmart (~500 BCE source; elaborated in Pahlavi texts)
Gayōmart, the first man, is killed by Ahriman; from his body the metals grow; from his seed the first human couple Mashye and Mashyane are born. Iranian Gayōmart:
- Is the cosmic human from whose body the material world is generated
- Is a "first man" who pre-exists ordinary humanity
- Connects to Manichaeism (which made the Primal Man a major cosmic figure, directly from Zoroastrian-Iranian sources)

### 4. Paul's "Last Adam" and Cosmic Christ (1 Cor 15:22–45, ~55 CE)
Paul's cosmic Christology in 1 Corinthians 15 and Colossians 1 presents Christ as:
- The "last Adam" / "second man" who is the true image of God
- "The firstborn over all creation" in whom "all things hold together" (Col 1:15–17)
- The **Pleroma** in bodily form (Col 2:9)
- The cosmic head of the church, his body (Eph 1:22–23)

This is Adam Kadmon language applied to Jesus of Nazareth: the Cosmic Human who contains the universe, in whom all things cohere, into whose "fullness" believers are integrated. The Pauline Cosmic Christ and the Kabbalistic Adam Kadmon are structurally identical — independent expressions of the same theological archetype.

### 5. Blake's Albion (~1800 CE)
William Blake's **Albion** — independently reconstructed from Boehme and the KJV — is the most developed modern literary realization of the Adam Kadmon figure:

- Albion is the **primordial cosmic Human** whose body *is* the universe (the "Giant Albion" whose limbs are landscapes)
- Albion **falls** into self-divided sleep — the Blakean equivalent of the shevirat ha-kelim
- The **Four Zoas** (Urizen/Luvah/Tharmas/Urthona) dissociate from Albion and contend destructively — the partzufim fragmented
- The project of *Jerusalem* is Albion's **awakening and restoration** — tikkun at cosmic scale
- **Jerusalem** (Albion's Emanation) is the divine feminine separated from the cosmic Human — the Shekinah in exile

Blake arrived at this structure through Jacob Boehme (who received Kabbalistic ideas through Christian channels) and through his own visionary logic — not through direct access to Kabbalistic texts. The structural identity with Adam Kadmon is the key finding.

## The Metatron Connection

In Hekhalot literature, **Metatron** (the transformed Enoch) is the figure who *mediates* between the divine and the human — the "Lesser YHWH" enthroned beside God, the cosmic scribe, the Prince of the Presence. Metatron occupies the *structural position* of Adam Kadmon's interface with the lower worlds:

- Both are the highest non-divine (or near-divine) figures in the cosmic hierarchy
- Both are associated with the cosmic body: the *Shi'ur Qomah* tradition (cosmic body measurements) bridges Hekhalot Metatron and Lurianic Adam Kadmon
- In Lurianic systematics, **Metatron** is the chief angel of the world of *Atzilut*, operating at the boundary between the divine partzufim (including Adam Kadmon) and the lower worlds — Adam Kadmon's emissary

The intellectual history: the Hekhalot Metatron (3rd–8th c.) → the Zohar's partzufim (13th c.) → Lurianic Adam Kadmon (16th c.) is a continuous development in which the anthropomorphic-divine-figure tradition is progressively systematized and elevated. Metatron stays as an angel; Adam Kadmon is raised to the level of first divine partzuf.

---

## Christian Kabbalah Reception

Pico della Mirandola's 900 *Conclusiones* (1486) introduced Adam Kadmon to Christian readers, identifying him with the Logos/Christ — the "first-born of all creation" of Colossians 1:15. This move:
- Made Adam Kadmon a legitimate subject of Christian theology
- Established the Kabbalistic reading of Genesis 1 as the emanation of Adam Kadmon
- Opened the tradition to Renaissance Hermeticism (Ficino, Agrippa, Paracelsus)

Knorr von Rosenroth's *Kabbala Denudata* (1677) — the Latin compilation of Kabbalistic texts — was the primary vehicle through which Blake, via Boehme's mediating influence, received Adam Kadmon ideas.
