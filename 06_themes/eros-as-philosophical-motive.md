# Eros as Philosophical Motive

Plato's *Symposium* and *Phaedrus* make *erōs* the philosophical-soteriological motive force — the soul's recollection of and longing for the Beautiful Itself draws it upward through ascending levels of beauty (bodies → souls → laws and practices → sciences → the Beautiful itself). The Diotimic ascent in *Symposium* 210–212 is the locus classicus. Plotinus extends the Platonic erōs into Neoplatonic ontology: all things proceed from the One and return through love-longing. The Renaissance Neoplatonists (Ficino's *De Amore*, Bruno's *Eroici Furori*) develop the doctrine as a full philosophical-theological erotics — divine madness, the philosophical lover.

- [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — *Symposium*, *Phaedrus*; the foundational texts.
- [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — Plotinian erōs as procession-and-return; *Ennead* VI.
- [[phase-6-001-ficino-pimander]] (with *De Amore*) — Ficino's Renaissance Christian-Platonic erotics.
- [[phase-6-014-bruno-de-la-causa-eroici-furori]] — Bruno's *Heroic Frenzies* (1585) is the high baroque articulation; the philosophical lover as transformative madman.

Nygren's *Agape and Eros* (1932) drew a sharp Lutheran-theological line between Christian *agape* (downward, self-giving divine love) and Platonic *eros* (upward, self-completing human love); the dichotomy has been criticized as overdrawn (de Rougemont, Soble) but remains an important typological framework.
