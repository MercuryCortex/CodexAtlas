# Light-and-Darkness Dualism

The cosmological-ethical dualism that frames the moral and metaphysical universe as the conflict of *Light* and *Darkness* — two principles, two realms, two camps of beings. The strongest forms (Manichaean, Mandaean) make Light and Darkness *coeternal substances*; weaker forms (Qumran, Johannine) make the dualism *ethical-eschatological* within a single divinely-created cosmos.

- [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]] — Zoroastrian background: Asha (truth/order) vs. Druj (lie); Ahura Mazda associated with light, Angra Mainyu with darkness — but the light/darkness terminology is more developed in later Zoroastrian texts.
- [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — Qumran *War Scroll* (Sons of Light vs. Sons of Darkness) and *Two Spirits* discourse: the most systematic Jewish-sectarian formulation.
- [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — Johannine usage: light/darkness, day/night, above/below; "the light shines in the darkness" (Jn 1:5).
- [[phase-4-015-kephalaia-of-the-teacher]] — Manichaean cosmogony: the Kingdom of Light invaded by the Kingdom of Darkness; the entire cosmic drama as the labor of separating light from darkness.
- [[phase-4-017-ginza-rba]] — Mandaean *Ginza*: the Lightworld (*alma d-nhura*) vs. the World of Darkness; the soul as exiled light fragment.

The motif's afterlife is enormous — gnostic, Cathar (*Liber de duobus principiis*), and in modern literature (Tolkien). Distinguish from [[dualism-cosmic]] (the broader category) and from [[dualism-spirit-matter]] (the metaphysical variant of Gnostic anti-cosmic dualism).
