# Philonic Logos

The Logos as articulated by Philo of Alexandria (c. 20 BCE – c. 50 CE): a mediating divine principle that is simultaneously God's reason, God's image, God's creative agent, the cosmic high priest, and the firstborn of God. Sits *between* Stoic immanent reason ([[logos-stoic]]) and the later Johannine personalized incarnate Logos ([[logos-johannine]]); a specific Hellenistic-Jewish synthesis. A subtype of [[logos-cosmic-reason]] given its own node because its predicates so directly prefigure later Christian Christology that the question "Did Christians read Philo?" is foundational.

## Predicates Philo applies to the Logos
- **Second God** (*deuteros theos*): Philo, *Questions on Genesis* 2.62 — the most striking single phrase. The Logos is "as it were a god" to creatures who cannot bear direct contact with the Most High.
- **Image of God** (*eikōn tou theou*): Genesis 1:27 read via Plato — the human is made according to the image of God, *which is the Logos*. The Logos is the image; the human is the image of the image.
- **Firstborn** (*prōtogonos*): Conf. 146 — the Logos is the elder son.
- **High Priest of the cosmos** (*archiereus tou kosmou*): On Dreams 1.215 — the Logos mediates between God and creation as the high priest mediates between God and Israel.
- **Cosmogonic Agent**: De opificio 16–25 — God creates first the *kosmos noetikos* (intelligible cosmos) within the Logos as in a mind; the sensible cosmos is then made after that pattern.
- **Cutter (Tomeus)**: Heres 130 — the Logos divides reality into its pairs of opposites.
- **Manna and Word**: Allegorical Interpretation 3.169–176 — the manna of Exodus is the Logos as nourishment for the soul.

## Sources Philo synthesizes
- **Plato:** Forms-as-divine-thoughts via Middle Platonism (Antiochus, the *Timaeus*).
- **Stoa:** Logos as cosmic rational principle (Cleanthes, Chrysippus).
- **Hebrew Bible:** *dabar* (word of the LORD) and *hokmah* (Wisdom personified, Prov 8).
- **Hellenistic-Jewish Wisdom literature:** Wisdom of Solomon's Sophia.

The synthesis is the project. Philo says explicitly his goal is to show that Mosaic wisdom anticipated Greek philosophy.

## Influence
- **Direct on Christian theology.** Justin Martyr, Clement of Alexandria ([[phase-4-025-clement-stromata]]), Origen, Eusebius read Philo. Clement claims him as "the Pythagorean." The Philonic Logos provides the philosophical vocabulary in which the Johannine prologue is interpreted.
- **Disputed direct on John.** Whether the Fourth Gospel's author knew Philo directly — debated; the conceptual proximity is undeniable, the textual evidence ambiguous. See [[logos-johannine]].
- **On Hellenistic Judaism's afterlife in rabbinic Judaism.** Almost nil. Philo was preserved by Christians, lost in mainstream rabbinic transmission. The rabbis went a different direction. The Philonic synthesis lives on within Christianity.
