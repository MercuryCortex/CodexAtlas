# Watchers and Fallen Angels

A class of heavenly beings (*'ir* "Watcher" in Aramaic; *Grigori* in Greek; "sons of God" in Genesis) descend from heaven, mate with human women, beget giants (*nephilim*), and teach forbidden arts (metalworking, weapons, cosmetics, sorcery, astrology, abortion). The resulting corruption of humans and earth is the *primary* etiology of evil throughout most of Second Temple Judaism — the Adam-and-Eve narrative is initially secondary. Subsequent Jewish and Christian traditions handled the Watchers narrative in radically different ways: incorporation, suppression, allegorization.

## Manifestations
- **Genesis 6:1–4:** [[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]] — the obscure four-verse seed: "the sons of God saw that the daughters of humans were fair, and they took wives for themselves of all that they chose ... the Nephilim were on the earth in those days." The compressed source-text the later Watchers tradition expands.
- **Book of the Watchers:** [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] 1–36 (3rd c. BCE). The fully developed Watchers narrative: 200 angels under Shemihazah and Azazel descend on Mount Hermon, swear an oath, take wives, teach forbidden knowledge, beget giants who devour humans and pollute the earth. God orders Michael, Raphael, Gabriel, Uriel to bind the Watchers underground until the eschatological judgment. The Watchers are the source of all subsequent evil; the giants' spirits become the unclean demons.
- **Jubilees:** [[phase-3-009-jubilees]] 5, 10 — recasts the Watchers story; the Watchers came down originally on a good mission (to teach humans righteousness), then fell; demons (offspring of the giants) afflict humanity but are restrained.
- **Qumran:** [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — extensive Aramaic and Hebrew Watchers material; Book of Giants in fragments.
- **New Testament traces:** 2 Pet 2:4, Jude 6 ("angels who did not keep their proper position") presuppose the Enochic narrative; Jude 14 explicitly cites 1 Enoch as prophecy.
- **Rabbinic retreat:** Tannaitic-amoraic Judaism largely *abandons* the Watchers etiology of evil. Genesis 6's "sons of God" are reinterpreted as "sons of nobles" or "Sethites." The shift parallels Christian preference for the Adam-fall narrative.
- **Patristic ambivalence:** Justin, Athenagoras, Tertullian, Clement all use the Watchers; Augustine rejects the angelic-sons reading definitively (*City of God* 15.23). Augustine's reading wins in Latin Christianity.
- **Manichaean:** [[phase-4-014-cologne-mani-codex]] — Mani knows the Enochic Watchers tradition; absorbed via Jewish-Christian baptismal sect (Elchasaites).
- **Quranic:** Harut and Marut (Surah 2:102) — two angels in Babylon who teach sorcery; a probable echo of the Watchers tradition.

## Why this matters
- **Pre-Adamic etiology of evil.** For 400+ years of Jewish religious imagination, the answer to "why is the world full of evil?" was *the Watchers*, not Adam. This re-orders standard narratives about how Judaism and Christianity understand sin. See Reed (2005).
- **The retreat is itself history.** Why rabbinic Judaism dropped the Watchers, why Augustinian Christianity dropped them, and why both displaced the etiology of evil onto Adam — these are major historical-theological transformations.

## Disputes
- **Dating of the Book of the Watchers.** 3rd c. BCE is the consensus; some date as early as 4th c. BCE.
- **Relation of Watchers to Greek Titans.** Some scholars (Hanson 1977) see Hellenistic Titanomachy influence in the giants narrative; others see purely Mesopotamian-Canaanite background (Apkallu sages who descended from heaven).
