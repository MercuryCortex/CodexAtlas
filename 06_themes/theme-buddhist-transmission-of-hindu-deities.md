# Buddhist transmission of Hindu deities

One of the cleanest-documented systematic-transmission patterns in religious history. As Buddhism spread from India across Central Asia, China, Korea, Japan, and Tibet between roughly -400 BCE and +1000 CE, it absorbed wholesale the Vedic-Hindu pantheon — keeping the deities by name (often with phonetic descent in their new languages) but **reassigning them to subordinate positions below the Buddha**.

## The pattern

The mechanism is *theological subordination*, distinct from two adjacent patterns:

| Pattern | Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Buddhist subordination** | Keep the figure, demote to disciple-of-Buddha | Indra → Śakra (king of his heaven, student of Buddha) |
| **Zoroastrian demonization** | Keep the figure-class, invert moral valence | Indra demoted to *daēva* (demon) (see [[asura-deva-inversion]]) |
| **Christian incorporation** | Absorb the figure as saint or angel | Pagan god → folk-Catholic saint via dvoeverie |

Buddhism's move is the gentlest of the three: the Vedic god retains divinity, retains his name, often retains his iconography — but is recontextualized as a being who *also* takes refuge in the Buddha. Indra in the Sakka-pañha Sutta (Dīgha Nikāya 21) approaches the Buddha to ask questions about Dharma; the same king of the Vedic gods becomes a Dharma-student. The doctrinal point is unmistakable: even the gods themselves are subject to *saṃsāra* and need the Buddha's teaching.

## Documented cases (~10-12 high-confidence pairs)

### Top-tier (direct phonetic descent + canonical attestation)

1. **Indra → Sakka / Śakra → Dìshìtiān → Taishakuten** — Vedic king of devas, preserved by name across Pali, Sanskrit Buddhist, Chinese, Japanese. The honorific *Devānām Indra* ("Lord of the Gods") becomes the proper name. Sumi 2014; DeCaroli 2004.

2. **Yama → Yán-mó → Yánluó-wáng → Enma-Ō** — Vedic underworld king becomes 5th of the Ten Kings of Hell tribunal in Chinese Buddhist apocryphal scripture (Shíwáng-jīng), then transmitted to Korea and Japan. Teiser 1988, 1994; Glassman 2012.

3. **Sarasvatī → Biancaitian → Benzaiten** — Vedic riverine goddess of knowledge transmitted to East Asia via the Suvarṇaprabhāsa Sūtra (Yijing's Chinese translation, ~703 CE), becomes one of the Seven Lucky Gods (Shichifukujin) of Japan. Iconographic continuity preserved: vīṇā → biwa; riverine identity → lakeside/island shrines (Enoshima, Chikubushima, Itsukushima). Ludvik 2007.

### Mid-tier (canonical transmission, less direct phonetic preservation)

4. **Brahmā → Bonten** — Vedic creator becomes Buddhist guardian-deity; paired with Taishakuten as the Buddha's two flanking devas in Japanese Mikkyō.

5. **Kubera → Vaiśravaṇa → Bishamonten** — Vedic wealth-god becomes Buddhist guardian of the North, then one of the Japanese Shichifukujin.

6. **Mahākāla → Daikokuten** — Shaiva wrathful form of Shiva absorbed via Vajrayāna into East-Asian Buddhism, with the wrathful aspect softened in Japan into a wealth/kitchen god. Sanderson 2009; Stein 2010.

7. **Ganeśa → Vināyaka** — Hindu elephant-headed god retained as Buddhist tantric guardian.

8. **Skanda / Kārttikeya → Wei Tuo (韋馱)** — Vedic war-god retained as Chinese Buddhist protector.

### The dharmapāla class

Beyond these named pairs, Vajrayāna Buddhism preserves an entire CLASS of Hindu deities recast as *dharmapālas* (Dharma-protectors): Hayagrīva, Yamāntaka, Vajrabhairava, Mahākāla, and others. Sanderson 2009 argues that medieval Śaivism was the proximate source, not Vedic Hinduism directly — a Śaiva-to-Vajrayāna directional borrowing during the 7th-11th c. CE.

## Why it matters

The pattern shows how a missionary religion can absorb the host tradition's divine population without theological war. Each Hindu deity gets a place in the Buddhist cosmos — usually as a guardian of the Dharma — which preserves popular devotion to the figure while reframing the doctrinal hierarchy. This is the **opposite move** from Zoroastrianism's wholesale demonization of the Vedic devas (see [[asura-deva-inversion]] — same source tradition, opposite reception), and it's the structural template for how Mahāyāna Buddhism later did the same thing to Bon deities in Tibet and *kami* in Japan (*shinbutsu shūgō*).

## Cross-tradition adjacent patterns

- **[[asura-deva-inversion]]** — the Zoroastrian counter-response to the same Indo-Aryan deity-pool. Same Vedic source, opposite mechanism.
- **Yoruba → Atlantic-Diaspora orisha-as-Catholic-saint syncretism** — parallel mechanism in a different continent: absorbing tradition keeps the figure, reassigns the hierarchy.
- **Slavic dvoeverie** — Perun → St. Elijah, Veles → St. Blaise — Christianity's softer absorption pattern in pagan Europe (Christianized-saint rather than demonized-demon).
