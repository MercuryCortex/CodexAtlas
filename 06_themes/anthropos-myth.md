# Anthropos Myth

A primordial cosmic *Man* (Greek *Anthrōpos*, Sanskrit *Puruṣa*, Hebrew *Adam Kadmon*, Iranian *Gayōmart*, Manichaean *Primal Man*) is either *dismembered* to make the cosmos, or *falls* into matter (whose later redemption is the cosmic project), or *contains* the cosmos as macranthropos, or is the *first-emanation* of the deity in human form. The motif is independent of (but interacts with) the biblical Adam-as-first-creature motif, and is a major substratum of [[microcosm-macrocosm]] thought.

## Manifestations
- **Vedic *Puruṣa-sūkta*:** [[phase-2-001-rig-veda-family-books]] — *Rig Veda* 10.90: the cosmic Puruṣa, a thousand-headed thousand-eyed cosmic person, is dismembered in primordial sacrifice; from his mouth come the Brahmins, from his arms the Kshatriyas, etc. — the locus classicus of cosmogonic dismemberment-of-anthropos, and of the *varṇa* social-cosmological doctrine.
- **Iranian Gayōmart:** [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]] (via later Zoroastrian Pahlavi sources) — the primal man Gayōmart is the first human, killed by Ahriman; from his body grow the metals; from his seed the first human couple. Manichaeism inherits this directly.
- **Mesopotamian:** [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] — humans created from the blood of slain Kingu mixed with clay (*Enūma Elish* tablet VI); the slain-divine-being motif behind anthropogony. Not exactly the same as Puruṣa-style cosmogony but the same family of "cosmos / humans from a slain divine body."
- **Hebrew Adam:** [[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]] — Genesis 2 — Adam formed from dust, breath of life from God. The Hebrew Adam is *not* the cosmic Anthropos but the *first creature*; later Jewish mystical traditions ([[phase-5-027-sefer-ha-zohar]]) develop *Adam Kadmon* — the primordial cosmic Anthropos, the *sefirot*-bodied first emanation — distinct from the biblical Adam.
- **Hermetic *Anthrōpos*:** [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] *Poimandres* CH I.12–14 — the heavenly *Anthrōpos*, image of the Father, descends through the spheres, falls in love with Nature, joins her — and from that union arise embodied humans, who therefore have a divine spark in mortal flesh. Cosmic-Anthropos as the *anthropogonic explainer*.
- **Gnostic / Sethian:** [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]] — the upper-aeon Adamas / *Geradamas*, of which earthly Adam is the inferior demiurgic copy. The Sethians elaborate a multi-layered Anthropos doctrine.
- **Manichaean Primal Man:** [[phase-4-014-cologne-mani-codex]], [[phase-4-015-kephalaia-of-the-teacher]] — Mani's cosmogonic drama: the Father of Greatness sends the *Primal Man* to fight Darkness; the Primal Man is defeated; his five "Sons" (Light-elements) are swallowed by Darkness; the cosmos is the machinery by which they are liberated. The most fully cosmogonic-soteriological Anthropos doctrine.
- **Kabbalistic *Adam Kadmon*:** [[phase-5-027-sefer-ha-zohar]] — the *Zohar*'s primordial cosmic Anthropos: the body of *Adam Kadmon* is the *etz ḥayyim* of the [[sefirot]]; the human body is the microcosm. Lurianic Kabbalah makes *Adam Kadmon* the central cosmodramatic figure.

## Disputes
- **Iranian origin thesis.** Bousset and the *Religionsgeschichtliche Schule* argued the Gnostic Anthropos derives from Iranian Gayōmart speculation; Boyce, Colpe and others have refined the case. Some scholars (Williams, King) downplay the Iranian channel and emphasize Greek-philosophical and Jewish-mystical sources.
- **Whether Vedic Puruṣa-sūkta and Iranian Gayōmart share Indo-European prehistory.** Lincoln (*Myth, Cosmos, and Society* 1986) argues an inherited Indo-European cosmogonic-dismemberment myth lies behind both — Norse Ymir, Roman Romulus, Vedic Puruṣa, Iranian Gayōmart are local realizations of one inherited mythologem. Disputed in detail.
- **Christological convergence.** Late-Pauline Christology (Colossians, Ephesians) and patristic theology cast Christ as the *second Adam* / cosmic Anthropos in whom all things are summed up (*anakephalaiōsis*, Eph 1:10); the Christological Anthropos draws on both Hebrew Adam and Hellenistic-Hermetic Anthropos imagery.
