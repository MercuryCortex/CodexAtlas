# In Christ (en Christō)

The Pauline phrase *en Christō* ("in Christ" / "in Christ Jesus") and its variants (*en Kyriō*, *eis Christon*) appearing some 165 times in the Pauline corpus — Paul's distinctive way of describing the believer's situation: incorporated into a corporate-mystical reality whose head is the risen Christ. Schweitzer's *The Mysticism of Paul the Apostle* (1930) made this the *central* Pauline concept, more fundamental than juridical justification. The believer in baptism is incorporated into Christ's death and resurrection (Rom 6:3–11); the community is the body of Christ (1 Cor 12); ethical exhortation flows from corporate location ("walk worthy of the calling," Eph 4:1).

- [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — the locus classicus; Rom 6, 8; 1 Cor 12; 2 Cor 5:17 ("if anyone is in Christ, new creation"); Gal 3:26–28; Phil 3:9.
- [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — the Johannine *menein en* ("abide in") of Jn 15 (vine and branches) is widely treated as the Johannine cognate of Pauline *en Christō*.

The phrase generates entire theological subprograms: corporate Christology, sacramental theology (baptism as incorporation), ecclesiology as Christ's body, ethics as conformity to Christ. Modern "participation" readings of Paul (Sanders, N. T. Wright, Campbell) have revived Schweitzer's case against the older Lutheran "imputation"-centered reading.
