# Proto-Indo-Iranian Religion (Reconstructed)

## What is Proto-Indo-Iranian?

Proto-Indo-Iranian (PII) is the reconstructed common ancestor of the Vedic Sanskrit and Avestan language families, spoken by a cultural population in the Pontic-Caspian steppe region (modern Kazakhstan/southern Russia/Ukraine) approximately 2100–1800 BCE, associated archaeologically with the **Sintashta culture** (c. 2100–1800 BCE) and its successors the **Andronovo horizon** (c. 2000–900 BCE). David Anthony (2007) demonstrates that Sintashta shows the earliest evidence for the spoke-wheeled chariot (~2000 BCE), horse-riding, and a warrior-caste ritual culture that corresponds remarkably to features common to both the Rigveda and the Avesta.

The religious content of PII is **reconstructed**, not directly attested. No PII texts exist. The method is comparative: where both the Rigveda and the Avesta (particularly the Gathas) share a feature not found in other Indo-European branches, that feature is probable PII heritage. The convergence is reinforced by the Mitanni evidence (~1380 BCE): the Hittite-Mitanni treaty at Boğazköy names Vedic deities (Mitra, Varuna, Indra, Nasatya) as oath-witnesses — in Syria, 1,500 miles from the Vedic homeland — confirming that the Indo-Iranian stratum preceded both the Vedic and the Iranian branches.

## Eight Reconstructed PII Religious Features

### 1. Ritual Sacrifice — *yaj-* / Avestan *yasna* / Sanskrit *yajña*
The word for the central religious act — sacrificial worship — is cognate in both branches. Avestan *yasna* (the core Zoroastrian liturgy) = Sanskrit *yajña* (the Vedic fire sacrifice). The root \**yag-* means "to worship by sacrifice." Both traditions preserve elaborate fire-sacrifice rituals with cognate terminology for the priests, the fire, and the ritual implements. The continuity of the sacrificial act across the split — even as its content transforms — is the clearest single proof of PII inheritance.

### 2. Sacred Plant Drink — \**sauma-* / Vedic *soma* / Avestan *haoma*
Both traditions know a sacred plant pressed to produce an intoxicating ritual drink consumed by priest and deity. Vedic *soma* is the subject of the entire ninth *maṇḍala* of the Rigveda; Avestan *haoma* is personified as a yazata and praised in Yasna 9–11. The actual botanical identity of the plant remains unresolved (Wasson proposed Amanita muscaria; other scholars suggest ephedra, harmaline, or a mixture). The ritual function — pressing, straining, offering to fire, consuming for divine inspiration — is identical across both branches.

### 3. Cosmic Order — \**ṛta-* / Sanskrit *ṛta* / Avestan *aša* (*arta*)
The concept of a cosmic, moral, and ritual order underlying reality is PII: Sanskrit *ṛta* (truth, cosmic order, Vedic divine law) and Avestan *aša* (truth, righteousness, the moral axis of creation — opposed to *druj* in the Gathic dualism). Both derive from PII \**hṛtá-* (fitted together rightly). Both traditions make this concept fundamental: in Vedic, *ṛta* is the order that Varuna and Mitra enforce; in Avestan, *aša* is literally half the cosmic structure (see [[theme-asha-druj-cosmic-opposition]]). The concept is also cognate with Latin *ritus* (rite), which suggests the PII root had broader IE distribution as the concept of things-done-rightly.

### 4. Tripartite Social-Religious Structure
Dumézil's comparative analysis identifies a three-function structure shared across Indo-European: (1) sacred sovereignty/law, (2) martial/warrior power, (3) fertility/abundance. In PII specifically, both Vedic and Avestan traditions preserve a version of this structure with cognate figures. Vedic: Varuna–Mitra (sovereignty) / Indra (warrior) / Aśvins–Sarasvatī (fertility). Avestan: Ahura Mazda–Mithra (sovereignty) / [warrior function demoted to daeva] / Anāhitā–Haurvatāt (fertility). The tripartite frame survived the Zoroastrian reform; what changed was the warrior-deity's status. Dumézil is contested but no alternative framework accounts for the correspondence as economically.

### 5. Fire Deity — Vedic *Agni* / Avestan *Atar*
Fire as a sacred, mediating deity — the divine messenger who carries sacrifice from humans to gods — is PII. Vedic Agni (the fire-god, the mouth of the gods, invoked in almost every Rigvedic hymn) and Avestan Atar (fire as yazata, son of Ahura Mazda, guardian of cosmic order, the medium of Zoroastrian ritual) derive from PII \**ātar-*. The Zoroastrian sacred fire (*atash*) cult — the five grades of temple fire, the perpetual flame that defines Zoroastrian temples — is the direct inheritor of PII fire veneration. Both traditions prohibit extinguishing sacred fire; both use fire as the ritual medium for sacrifice and prayer.

### 6. Dragon-Slayer Myth
Both traditions know a hero-deity who slays a serpentine or dragonlike adversary associated with obstructing water, cattle, or cosmic order. Vedic: Indra slays Vṛtra (the obstruction/drought-serpent), releasing the cosmic waters. Iranian: Verethragna (the yazata of victory, cognate with Vṛtra-hana = "Vritra-smiter") is the lord of martial triumph; a parallel dragon-slaying tradition is reconstructed from the *Yashts*. The Vedic Vṛtra-Indra myth and the Iranian cognates share vocabulary (PII \**Vṛtra-han-* = "slayer of Vritra") even as the narratives diverge. The myth may connect ultimately to a PII cosmogonic combat myth in which cosmic order is won by slaying primordial chaos.

### 7. First Man and Underworld Lord — Vedic *Yama* / Avestan *Yima*
Both traditions know a primordial figure named *Yama* (Sanskrit) / *Yima* (Avestan), cognate from PII \**Yama-* ("twin"). In the Rigveda, Yama is the first mortal to die, who became lord of the dead; in Vedic eschatology he rules the realm of ancestors. In the Avesta, Yima (the brilliant, son of Vīvanghat) is the first king, who created a paradise *vara* (an underground enclosure) to preserve life against a coming winter, and who fell from grace through pride (eating flesh). The parallelism extends to Yama's twin sister Yamī / Yima's twin — the incest motif appears in both. This is one of the oldest mythological parallels across the split, reaching from cosmogony directly into eschatology and afterlife geography.

### 8. Deva/Daeva Dual Category
Before the split, PII \**daiwá-* (from PIE \**déiwos* = shining, heavenly) designated the category of bright divine powers. Both branches inherited this category — and applied it inversely (see [[theme-vedic-avestan-split]]). Alongside \**daiwá-*, PII also knew \**asura-* / \**ahura-* (powerful lord): in early Vedic *asura* can be positive or negative; in Avestan *ahura* (as in Ahura Mazda) is always positive. Both traditions were therefore working with the same two-category system and performed the same crossover in opposite directions.

## The Steppe Culture Behind the Reconstruction

Anthony (2007) argues that the Sintashta archaeological culture (southern Urals, c. 2100–1800 BCE) is the most likely PII homeland, and that the spoke-wheeled chariot it invented spread rapidly across Eurasia. The Sintashta people show: (1) horse-sacrificing burials; (2) fire altars in domestic contexts; (3) prestige livestock (cattle, horses); (4) warrior-caste males buried with weapons; (5) evidence of a ritual specialist class. All of these match the social environment presupposed by both the Rigveda and the Avesta. The Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex (BMAC, c. 2200–1700 BCE) in Central Asia shows cultural contact with Sintashta and is widely proposed as the staging ground for the split: Proto-Iranian moving west-south toward Iran, Proto-Vedic moving east-south toward the Indian subcontinent.

## Why This Matters for the Vault

The PII reconstruction is the backstory to every Zoroastrian–Vedic–Mitanni connection in the vault. When the Mitanni kings name Vedic deities in a 1380 BCE Hittite treaty (see [[event-mitanni-kingdom-c1500-1340-bce]]), that is PII religion preserved in diplomatic ritual. When Zoroastrianism demotes Indra to daemonhood, it is a PII god being purged. When Zoroastrian theology diffuses into Second Temple Judaism (see [[theme-zoroastrian-jewish-exchange]]) via the *aša/druj* axis, it carries PII conceptual DNA into the monotheistic tradition. The six-language *paradise* etymology chain ([[theme-paradise-etymology]]) begins with Avestan *pairidaeza*, itself a compound of PII roots. The Abraham-Moses gap's most surprising bridging is not via Egypt or Canaan but via the steppe: the theological reforms happening in Iran during the same centuries as the Exodus tradition crystallizing in Canaan were drawing on a shared primordial religious heritage that predates both.
