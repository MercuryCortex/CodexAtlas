# Flood Motif

A primordial cataclysmic flood sent by gods (or God) to destroy humanity, with a single chosen man building a vessel to preserve animal and human life. Cross-tradition variants share core narrative elements: divine decision, advance warning to one righteous figure, construction of an ark/boat, animal preservation, post-flood landing on a mountain, sacrifice, and divine covenant or regret.

## Earliest attestations
- **Mesopotamian:** [[phase-1-006-atrahasis]] (~1800 BCE) — gods send flood because humanity is too noisy; Atrahasis warned by Enki.
- **Sumerian:** Eridu Genesis / Ziusudra fragment (~1600 BCE).
- **Babylonian:** [[phase-1-004-gilgamesh-old-babylonian]] Tablet XI (Utnapishtim's flood narrative — likely an interpolation from the Atrahasis tradition).
- **Hebrew:** Genesis 6–9 (J + P sources; final form post-exilic — Phase 2 work).
- **Greek:** Deucalion myth (Apollodorus, Ovid).
- **Indian:** Matsya avatar / Manu (Shatapatha Brahmana, later Puranas).

## Scholarly consensus
The Mesopotamian → Hebrew transmission of the flood motif is one of the strongest cases of literary dependence in comparative religion (George 2003, Heidel 1949). The independent-origin position is fringe today.
