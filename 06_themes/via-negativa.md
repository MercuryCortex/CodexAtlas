# Via Negativa (Apophatic Theology)

The theological method that approaches the divine by *negating* finite attributes rather than affirming them: God is not material, not extended, not in time, not knowable, not nameable. Distinguished from *cataphatic* (affirmative) theology, which positively predicates attributes of God; complementary to it in most classical systems. The technique is most rigorously developed in Plotinus, Pseudo-Dionysius, Maimonides, Eckhart, Shankara, and in Islamic *kalām*'s *tanzīh* (declaring God free of resemblance to creatures). Distinct from but allied with [[divine-darkness]] (which is the phenomenological correlate) and [[hidden-god]] (the related theological motif).

## Manifestations

- **Plotinus.** [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] *Ennead* V.3, V.5, VI.7 — the One is *beyond* being, *beyond* intellect, *beyond* even the act of being. We can only "speak around it" — Plotinus practices apophasis in his own discourse (see Sells 1994 on Plotinus's apophatic *style*).
- **Pseudo-Dionysius.** [[phase-4-030-pseudo-dionysius]] *Mystical Theology* — the canonical Christian articulation of *via negativa* as the higher complement of *via affirmativa*. The threefold path: *affirmation → negation → negation of the negation*.
- **Cappadocian.** Gregory of Nyssa's *Eunomian* controversy: against Eunomius's claim that we know God's essence as "Unbegottenness," Gregory argues God's essence is fundamentally inaccessible. The Palamite distinction (energies vs. essence) systematizes this for Eastern Orthodoxy.
- **Maimonides.** [[phase-5-009-maimonides-guide-perplexed]] I, chapters 50–60 — the negative attributes doctrine: we can only know that God is *not* this or that finite thing; all positive predicates are equivocal. Maimonides's apophaticism is more austere than the Christian Patristic version.
- **Eckhart.** [[the-godhead-eckhart]] — the *Gottheit* beyond all names; "if you say God is good, you have spoken falsely" (Sermon 83). The intensification of apophasis into provocative paradox.
- **Shankara *neti neti*.** [[phase-5-005-shankara-brahma-sutra-bhasya]] — *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 4.4.22 "not this, not this" as the formula for Brahman; Shankara's strict Advaita treats Brahman as nirguna (without qualities), apophatically only.
- **Islamic *tanzīh*.** *Tanzīh* (declaring God free of creaturely resemblance) opposed to *tashbīh* (anthropomorphic likening). Mu'tazilite, Ashʿarite, and Sufi theologies all develop the *tanzīh* dimension; Ibn ʿArabī famously held both *tanzīh* and *tashbīh* simultaneously (the *Fuṣūṣ al-Ḥikam*).
- **Modern philosophical retrieval.** Heidegger's late thought, Derrida's "Comment ne pas parler" (1986), Marion's *God Without Being* (1982), Caputo's "religion without religion." Hart 1989 traces the apophatic in modern French philosophy.

## Disputes

- **Whether apophasis is "really" a negative theology (a positive theology in disguise) or a genuine non-position.** Derrida and Marion debate this directly in the 1980s exchanges.
- **The intelligibility of pure apophasis.** Aquinas (*ST* I q.13 a.2) argues pure apophasis is incoherent — we cannot even *deny* attributes of God if we have no positive grasp of what we deny. He proposes analogical predication as the middle way.
- **Comparative debate.** Whether Shankara's *neti neti*, Maimonides's negative attributes, and Pseudo-Dionysian apophasis converge on the same religious-philosophical insight or remain irreducibly distinct depending on tradition-internal commitments.
