# Marcionite canon (first New-Testament-style canon)

## Definition
Marcion of Sinope's restricted Christian scripture, fixed in Rome ~144 CE: an edited *Evangelion* (a Luke-like single Gospel, abbreviated of its Jewish-Bible references) plus an *Apostolikon* of ten Pauline letters (Galatians, 1–2 Corinthians, Romans, 1–2 Thessalonians, Ephesians [as "Laodiceans"], Colossians, Philippians, Philemon — the pastorals excluded). The Hebrew Bible is rejected entirely. The Marcionite canon is widely treated as the *first fixed* Christian scriptural collection — the first specifically *Christian* canon, prior to the formation of what would become the orthodox New Testament. Diagnostic marker: an explicit, bounded, polemically-motivated scriptural collection that *excludes* the Hebrew Bible and *defines* a Christian normative text.

## Earliest attestations
- **Justin Martyr,** *1 Apol.* 26 (~155 CE): the earliest external mention of Marcion's movement.
- **Irenaeus,** *Adversus Haereses* I.27 (~180 CE): first detailed refutation of Marcion's two-Gods theology and his use of an edited Gospel.
- **Tertullian,** *Adversus Marcionem* (5 books, ~207–212 CE): the most extensive ancient anti-Marcionite work; preserves Marcion's *Antitheses* (his theological work contrasting OT with NT) and the *Evangelion* in quotation. Modern reconstructions of the Marcionite canon depend principally on Tertullian and Epiphanius.
- **Hippolytus,** *Refutation of All Heresies* VII (~225 CE).
- **Epiphanius,** *Panarion* 42 (~375 CE): preserves substantial *scholia* on the Marcionite *Evangelion* and *Apostolikon*.

## Variants
- **Marcionite versions of the Evangelion:** scholars dispute the precise relationship of Marcion's *Evangelion* to canonical Luke. Harnack 1921: Marcion *abbreviated* Luke by excising Judaizing passages. BeDuhn 2013 and Klinghardt 2015 argue the *Evangelion* may be *earlier than* canonical Luke — that Luke is an *expansion* of Marcion's text, not a redaction-target. The debate has substantial implications for Synoptic-source criticism.
- **Marcionite Apostolikon ordering:** Marcion places Galatians first (his most-prized Pauline letter for its anti-Jewish-law polemic), then Corinthians, Romans, Thessalonians, etc. The pastorals (1–2 Tim, Titus) are absent — either because Marcion excluded them or (more likely) because they did not yet exist in the form they later took.
- **Tatian's *Diatessaron***: in the same period (~160s–170s) but with the opposite move — a *four-Gospel harmony* rather than a single edited Gospel.

## Connections claimed
- **Anti-Marcionite formation of orthodox canon.** Mainstream scholarship (von Campenhausen 1968, Metzger 1987, Lieu 2015) holds that Marcion *catalyzed* the proto-orthodox canonical process: the four-Gospel canon is partly an explicit response to Marcion's single-Gospel restriction; the explicit affirmation of the Hebrew Bible as Christian scripture responds to Marcion's rejection.
- **First Christian canon thesis.** Harnack (1921, 1990 English): Marcion's canon is the *first* Christian fixed collection, predating any equivalent proto-orthodox formation by decades. This widely-held view has been challenged by Trobisch 2000, who argues the proto-orthodox NT was edited as a single corpus before Marcion. Most scholarship still credits Marcion with priority.
- **Theological mechanism.** Marcion's canon enforces his two-Gods theology: the inferior creator-God of the Hebrew Bible (the *Demiurge* in everything but name) vs. the alien Father of Jesus Christ. The canonical exclusion is the practical expression of the theological exclusion.

## Disputes
- **Marcion as proto-Gnostic.** Harnack: not a Gnostic; a Pauline radical. Lieu 2015: theologically adjacent to Gnosticism without being formally Gnostic. Pétrement 1990: clearly Gnostic. The terminology debate (whether "Gnostic" is a useful category at all — Williams 1996, M. Smith 2019) bears on this.
- **Priority of the Evangelion vs. canonical Luke.** BeDuhn 2013 and Klinghardt 2015 (Marcion's text is *earlier than* Luke) vs. Roth 2015 (Marcion edited Luke). The implications are large: if Marcion's *Evangelion* is the source-text of Luke, then a non-Jewish-coded version of the Synoptic tradition predates the canonical-Lukan version.
- **Cerdo and Marcion's sources.** Irenaeus (AH 1.27) attributes Marcion's theology to his Roman teacher Cerdo, who taught a two-Gods doctrine. Whether Cerdo is historical and whether Marcion's theology was substantially derivative or independent is contested.
