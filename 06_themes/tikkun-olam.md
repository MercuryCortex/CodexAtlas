# Tikkun Olam — Cosmic Repair

Third act of the Lurianic drama. After [[shevirat-hakelim]] left divine sparks trapped in the world's broken vessels, *tikkun* is the work of gathering them back to their source. Human action — performance of the commandments (*mitzvot*) with the correct mystical intention (*kavvanah*) — actively raises the sparks. Each righteous human act repairs the cosmos by one increment. The Messiah's arrival is the *completion*, not the cause, of tikkun.

This is the Lurianic anthropology: humans are not passive recipients of salvation but active partners in cosmic repair. It is also a radical Lurianic departure from earlier Kabbalah, where the divine emanation was unidirectional. After Luria, the human soul *acts upon* the divine — a position that horrified some traditionalists.

Modern usage: Reform Judaism in the 20th century recast *tikkun olam* as a charter for social-justice activism, often detached from the Lurianic metaphysics. Both meanings circulate.
