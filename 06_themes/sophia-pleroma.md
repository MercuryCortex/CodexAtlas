# Sophia and the Pleroma (Valentinian)

## Definition
The distinctive Valentinian cosmogonic narrative: from an unknowable supreme Father / Depth (*Bythos*) emanates a structured fullness — the *Pleroma* — consisting of 30 aeons in 15 syzygies (paired emanations: Mind-Truth, Word-Life, Man-Church, etc.). The youngest aeon, *Sophia* (Wisdom), suffers a *passion* — typically presumption, longing for the Father, or improper procreation — and her resulting offspring or *enthymesis* falls outside the Pleroma. From this fall the material cosmos is generated, mediated through the Demiurge (an ignorant craftsman) and his archons. Diagnostic markers: (a) the structured 30-aeon Pleroma in 15 syzygies, (b) Sophia's fall as the originating disturbance, (c) the production of the material cosmos *as a consequence* of intra-divine disorder, (d) the soteriology of *gnosis* as the recovery of the Pleromatic origin.

Distinguished from the Sethian Sophia/Yaldabaoth scheme (see [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]]) by the precise pleromatic architecture, the typology of the three natures (pneumatic/psychic/hylic), and the Christ–Sophia syzygy that descends to redeem her.

## Earliest attestations
- **Valentinus himself** (Alexandria → Rome, ~135–165): fragments preserved in [[phase-4-025-clement-stromata]] and [[phase-4-038-hippolytus-refutation]] show poetic-mystical theology rather than the systematized myth.
- **Ptolemy** (Valentinus's principal student): the *Letter to Flora* (preserved in Epiphanius *Panarion* 33), and the systematized Valentinian myth preserved by Irenaeus, [[phase-4-023-irenaeus-against-heresies]] I.1–8. This is the locus classicus of the 30-aeon Pleroma.
- **Heracleon:** the *Commentary on John* (fragments preserved in Origen's *Commentary on John*) is the earliest known Christian biblical commentary.
- **Theodotus:** *Excerpta ex Theodoto* preserved by Clement of Alexandria.
- **Nag Hammadi Valentinian materials:** [[phase-4-003-gospel-of-truth]], [[phase-4-004-gospel-of-philip]], *Tripartite Tractate*, *Treatise on the Resurrection*. The *Tripartite Tractate* presents a substantially revised Valentinian cosmogony (only one Sophia, monistic emanation; Thomassen argues it represents the "Eastern" Valentinian school).
- **Hippolytus,** *Refutation* VI: an alternative account of the Valentinian system, sometimes thought to preserve "Eastern" (Marcosian) variants.

## Variants
- **Italian school** (Ptolemy, Heracleon): Christ is a pneumatic emission, the Son a separate aeon; the soteriology emphasizes the pneumatic seed sown in psychic humans.
- **Eastern school** (Theodotus, the *Tripartite Tractate*): Christ is the pneumatic body itself; a single Logos (not separate aeons) emanates from Father, with Sophia subordinate.
- **Sethian Sophia** (distinct tradition, see [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]]): a single Sophia, her presumptuous self-generation produces Yaldabaoth, who creates the material cosmos in ignorance. The Sethian story is more dualistic and demonological than the Valentinian.
- **Marcosian** (Marcus the Magician): numerological elaborations on the syzygies; Greek-letter symbolism mapped onto the Pleroma.
- **Bardaisanite parallels:** the Edessan school of Bardaisan develops a related but distinct cosmogony — the question of independent vs. derivative formation is contested.

## Connections claimed
- **Platonist provenance.** The Pleroma is structurally a Middle Platonist intelligible-world (the *kosmos noētos*); the syzygy structure echoes Pythagorean dyadic-tetradic mathematics; the divine *Depth* is plausibly post-Numenian. Turner 2001 and Thomassen 2006 emphasize the depth of Valentinian philosophical formation.
- **Jewish-wisdom heritage.** Sophia as fallen-wisdom recovers and inverts the Hebrew Wisdom literature's *personified Wisdom* (Proverbs 8, Sirach 24, Wisdom 7). The Valentinian *passion of Sophia* is a Gnostic-Christian rewriting of the Wisdom tradition.
- **Pauline appropriation.** Valentinians read Paul intensively — 1 Cor 2:6–16 (the "wisdom of the perfect"), Eph 1:3–14 (the cosmic plan), Col 1:15–20 (the Christ-hymn) are Valentinian proof-texts. Pagels 1975 *The Gnostic Paul* documents Valentinian exegesis of Pauline letters.
- **Sethian–Valentinian relationship.** Thomassen 2006: proto-Sethian materials preceded Valentinus; Valentinus theologically reconstructed and Christianized Sethian myth. The reverse direction is held by some (e.g., Logan 1996). The relationship is the central question of 2nd-c. Gnostic-studies historiography.

## Disputes
- **Was the systematized myth Valentinus's own, or Ptolemy's?** The fragments of Valentinus himself are mystical-philosophical, not narrative-cosmogonic; the developed myth is attributed by Irenaeus to "the school of Ptolemy." Thomassen 2006: Valentinus's own theology was different in important respects from the Ptolemaic developed form.
- **Is Valentinianism Christian heresy or independent religion?** The "is it Christian?" question is theologically loaded but historically meaningful; Valentinians regarded themselves as Christians and participated in proto-orthodox congregations until 4th-c. expulsions. The heresiological category "Gnosticism" itself has been challenged by Williams 1996 and M. Smith 2019 as anachronistic.
- **The pleromatic numerology.** Whether the precise count of 30 aeons in 15 syzygies is original or a later schematization is debated; Hippolytus preserves a variant numerology.
