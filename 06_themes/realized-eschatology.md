# Realized Eschatology

C. H. Dodd's term (1935) for an eschatology in which the decisive eschatological events — judgment, eternal life, the gift of the Spirit — have **already occurred** in the present life of the believer, rather than being deferred to a future cosmic consummation. The Gospel of John is the locus classicus: "whoever believes has eternal life" (Jn 6:47, present tense), "the hour is coming, and now is" (Jn 5:25), "this is eternal life, that they know thee" (Jn 17:3). Counterpoint to [[eschatological-imminence]].

- [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — Dodd's prime exhibit: judgment, resurrection, and eternal life are present realities accessed by faith/knowledge.
- [[phase-4-001-gospel-of-thomas]] — saying 113: "the Kingdom of the Father is spread upon the earth, and men do not see it"; the kingdom as present interior reality, not future cosmic event. The Thomasine form is more radically realized than the Johannine.

Modern theology has debated Dodd's reading: Jeremias and Cullmann argued for an "inaugurated eschatology" (already and not yet) preserving both poles; Bultmann's existential decoding read realized eschatology as the demythologized core of the New Testament.
