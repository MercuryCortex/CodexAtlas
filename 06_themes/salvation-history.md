# Salvation History (Heilsgeschichte)

The theological category developed by 19th-c. German biblical theology (J. C. K. von Hofmann) and crystallized in 20th-c. dialectical theology (Cullmann's *Christ and Time*, 1946) for the *linear, divinely-purposed historical drama* whose chapters are election (Abraham), exodus, monarchy, exile, return, incarnation, cross, resurrection, church, parousia. The category is the biblical-theological counter to: (1) cyclical-cosmic time (the older Mediterranean and ANE pattern); (2) abstract-philosophical-theological systems disconnected from historical particularity (medieval scholasticism, some Reformation systematics); (3) liberal Protestant moralism that ignored the temporal narrative.

- [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] — Conzelmann's *The Theology of St. Luke* (1953) argued Luke periodizes salvation-history into Israel / Jesus / Church-age. Lukan *Heilsgeschichte* is among the strongest NT cases.
- [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Rom 9–11, Gal 3, Eph 1: Paul thinks salvation-historically.
- [[phase-4-028-augustine-confessions]] / [[phase-4-029-augustine-city-of-god]] — Augustine's *City of God* is the first Christian thoroughgoing salvation-history (the seven ages of the world, the two cities, history as theodicy).

Modern critique: Bultmann's program of demythologization replaced *Heilsgeschichte* with existential moment-of-decision; Pannenberg's *Revelation as History* (1961) reasserted *Heilsgeschichte* but with universal-historical scope.
