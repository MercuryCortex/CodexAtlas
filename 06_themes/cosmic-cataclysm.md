# Cosmic Cataclysm

The motif of a *world-destroying event* — flood, fire, divine combat — that ends an age (or the cosmos itself). Distinguished from [[flood-motif]] (which is a specific instance) by its more general scope: any cosmic-ending catastrophe. Compatible with both linear-eschatological frameworks (the *end* of the cosmos) and cyclical frameworks ([[cosmic-cycles]]: the cosmos periodically dissolves and is renewed).

- **Mesopotamian Flood** — [[phase-1-006-atrahasis]], [[phase-1-004-gilgamesh-old-babylonian]]: the gods send a flood to destroy humanity; one righteous man survives by divine warning. The earliest fully developed cosmic-cataclysm narrative.
- **Hebrew Flood** — [[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]]: Genesis 6–9. The Noah account in J (and parallel P) re-deploys the Mesopotamian template with monotheistic moral framing.
- **Stoic *ekpyrōsis*** — [[phase-3-005-stoic-foundational-texts]]: periodic conflagration as cosmic dissolution; the *logos* survives to seed the next cycle.
- **Hindu *pralaya*** — at the end of each *kalpa*, dissolution of the manifest cosmos back into the unmanifest; partial *pralaya* between *yuga*s.
- **Norse Ragnarök** — covered by Phase 8 agent's territory; see Phase 8 documents.

Modern reception: the secularized "end of the world" in nuclear, climate, and pandemic discourse retains the structural slot. The Christian "end of the world" combines cosmic-cataclysm (Rev 6, 8, 16) with eschatological renovation (Rev 21).
