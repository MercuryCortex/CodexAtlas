# Possession Ritual

## Overview

**Spirit possession** — the temporary occupation of a human body by a deity, spirit, or non-human being, who uses the person's body as a vehicle for communication, healing, blessing, or divine action — is the single most cross-culturally widespread altered-state-of-consciousness practice in world religion. It appears on every inhabited continent and in traditions spanning at least 5,000 years of documented religious practice.

The cross-tradition pattern is: a human being (the *medium* or *vessel*) undergoes a state shift — typically induced by rhythmic drumming, dance, chanting, incense, fasting, or some combination — in which their ordinary ego-consciousness is displaced or suspended and a divine being or spirit takes over their body, voice, and behavior.

## Core variants

**Haitian Vodou — *chevauchée* (the riding):**
The lwa "*monte*" (mounts) or "rides" the devotee, who becomes the lwa's *chwal* (horse). During possession, the devotee does not remember what transpires — the lwa is fully present in the body. The possessed person may speak in the lwa's characteristic voice, wear the lwa's colors, demand the lwa's preferred food and drink (rum for [[baron-samedi]], sweet things for [[erzulie]]), and perform healing or oracular functions. The community recognizes and responds to the lwa, not the person. [[papa-legba]] is always called first to open the gate between the human and spirit worlds. See [[phase-8-016-haitian-vodou-liturgical-tradition]].

**Yoruba religion and Santería/Candomblé — orisha mounting:**
The orisha "mounts" (*montar*) the devotee in a similar grammar to the Vodou chevauchée. Possession occurs most frequently during the feast of the deity's patron saint (the syncretic Catholic calendar) at specially structured ritual gatherings. The possessed person is dressed in the orisha's colors, dances the orisha's characteristic movements, and becomes available to the community for consultation and blessing. Training the body to receive a specific orisha takes years of initiation. See [[phase-8-017-santeria-lucumi]].

**Korean *mudang* shamanism — *gut* ceremony:**
The *mudang* (female shaman) enters trance during a multi-day *gut* ceremony and becomes the vehicle for specific spirits — ancestors, nature spirits, and deities. The grammar is virtually identical: the shaman's ordinary identity is displaced by the spirit's presence; the community interacts with the spirit directly.

***Zar* cults (East Africa, Ethiopia, Egypt, Sudan):**
The *zar* is a category of spirit that causes illness by "possessing" a person. The cure is not exorcism (driving the spirit out) but accommodation — a *zar* ceremony in which the spirit is acknowledged, given offerings, and persuaded to coexist peacefully with its host. The possessed person becomes a long-term "host" rather than being freed from the spirit. This is notable: the possession is therapeutic rather than adversarial.

**Pentecostal Christianity — "falling out" and glossolalia:**
The "baptism of the Holy Spirit" in Pentecostal and Charismatic Christianity involves states that are structurally similar to spirit possession: the worshiper loses ordinary control, may fall to the ground ("slain in the Spirit"), speak in tongues (*glossolalia*), or prophesy. The theological framing is distinct (the Holy Spirit is the Third Person of the Trinity, not an external spirit), but the experiential and community grammar — altered state, loss of personal control, community validation, message delivery — is functionally parallel.

**Hindu Tantric *devatā āveśa* (divine influx):**
In Tantric practice, the *āveśa* (divine possession or influx) is a legitimate yogic state in which the practitioner's body and consciousness become identified with the deity invoked. In ritual contexts, a priest may be understood to become an embodiment of the deity — the image is installed in the body (*nyāsa*) through ritual gesture. This is the Indic version of the same universal pattern, carried into a sophisticated philosophical framework.

**Ancient Greek Dionysian ecstasy:**
The Maenads in [[tradition-dionysian-mysteries]] are described as possessed by [[dionysus]] — they abandon their households, run to the mountains, tear apart animals with their bare hands, and nurse wild animals. This is possession ecstasy in the classical literary record. Plato's *Ion* describes the rhapsode's inspired state as a kind of divine possession (*entheos* — "god within"). The Pythia at Delphi was understood to be possessed by [[apollo]] when she delivered oracles.

## Cross-tradition MASSIVE WIN

**The universal grammar of possession:** Bourguignon's cross-cultural survey (1973, *Religion, Altered States of Consciousness, and Social Change*) found that **90% of 488 surveyed societies worldwide** have institutionalized forms of altered states of consciousness in religious contexts; possession trance is present in approximately 50% of those societies. It is not a regional anomaly — it is one of the most universal features of human religious experience.

The cross-tradition structural invariants are:
1. **Boundary dissolution** between self and deity — ordinary ego-identity is suspended or replaced
2. **Community validation** — the possessed state is recognized by the community as genuine, not as madness; specific behavioral codes mark the legitimate possession
3. **Specialized mediation roles** — the vessel (who is possessed), the community (who interacts with the deity), and often a specialist (the *mambo*/*houngan* in Vodou, the *babalawo* in Ifá, the shaman's assistant) who manages the interaction
4. **The deity's agenda** — the possessed person serves the deity's needs (communication, healing, blessing) rather than their own
5. **Post-possession amnesia** (frequently, not universally) — the vessel does not remember what the deity did

**Parallel to dissolution in alchemy:** The ego-dissolution structure of possession parallels the *nigredo* (putrefaction/dissolution) stage in [[alchemy-as-spiritual-discipline]]: the ordinary self must be destroyed or suspended before the divine can act through it. The gold-zone connection is the widespread cross-tradition pattern that *receiving the divine requires the emptying of the self*.

## Attestations

- **[[phase-8-016-haitian-vodou-liturgical-tradition]]** — Haitian Vodou: the chevauchée is the central ritual technology of the tradition; without lwa possession, no Vodou ceremony is considered complete.
- **[[phase-8-017-santeria-lucumi]]** — Santería/Lucumí: orisha mounting is the climactic event of major ritual gatherings; possession by the orisha constitutes the deity's presence among the community.
