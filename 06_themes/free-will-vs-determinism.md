# Free Will vs Determinism

The recurrent philosophical-theological question: are human actions *free* in a robust libertarian sense, or are they *determined* by prior causes (divine decree, cosmic fate, prior physical states, internal causal chains)? The major historical positions:

- **Hard determinism**: actions are determined; libertarian freedom is illusion.
- **Libertarianism**: actions are free in the sense of being undetermined by prior causes.
- **Compatibilism**: actions can be "free" in the morally responsible sense even within a deterministic causal framework (Chrysippus and the Stoa; Hobbes, Hume, Frankfurt).

Religious-theological variants:
- Stoic *fatum* (universal causal determinism) with compatibilist responsibility ([[phase-3-005-stoic-foundational-texts]]).
- Qumran *Two Spirits* doctrine: humans sorted from creation between the spirits ([[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]]; [[two-spirits-doctrine]]).
- Augustinian anti-Pelagian: post-fall human will is bound by sin and cannot achieve good without prior divine grace ([[phase-4-028-augustine-confessions]]).
- Reformation Luther/Calvin: *De Servo Arbitrio* (Luther against Erasmus 1525); *Institutes* III (Calvin) — the will is bound, salvation by sovereign election alone ([[phase-6-007-luther-bondage-of-will]]; [[phase-6-010-calvin-institutes]]; [[predestination]]; [[predestination-protestant]]).
- Islamic *qadar* debate: Mu'tazilites for libertarian freedom; Ash'arites for divine determination with *kasb* (acquisition) as the compatibilist solution.

The problem is structurally inseparable from [[origin-of-evil]], [[predestination]], and theodicy.
