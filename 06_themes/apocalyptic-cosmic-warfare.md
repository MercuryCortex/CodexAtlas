# Apocalyptic cosmic warfare

## Definition
The dualistic eschatological frame characteristic of Jewish apocalyptic and its Christian and Manichaean heirs: history is divided into *two aeons* (the present evil age and the age to come); a cosmic conflict between angelic and demonic powers shapes terrestrial history; the elect / righteous suffer in the present aeon and are *vindicated* at the end-time; the conflict culminates in a final eschatological war and the inauguration of God's reign. Diagnostic markers: (a) two-aeon temporal scheme, (b) angelic-demonic personae as historical agents, (c) divine vindication of the elect, (d) sharp moral-cosmic dualism (light/dark, sons-of-light/sons-of-darkness).

Distinguished from [[chaoskampf]] (which is *cosmogonic* combat, not eschatological), from generalized [[dualism-cosmic]] (Zoroastrian-style metaphysical dualism), and from [[end-times-judgment]] (which may have apocalyptic features without the full cosmic-warfare scheme).

## Earliest attestations
- **Persian-Zoroastrian background:** Achaemenid-era apocalyptic eschatology (Bahman Yasht; cosmogonic-eschatological cosmic dualism); the question of dependence on Zoroastrianism is contested (Hultgård, Stausberg, Boyce yes; Barr 1985 more cautious).
- **1 Enoch:** [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — the Watchers myth (1 En 6–11); the Animal Apocalypse (1 En 85–90); the Apocalypse of Weeks (1 En 91–93); the Book of Parables (1 En 37–71). Mid-3rd c. BCE through 1st c. CE.
- **Daniel 7–12:** [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — the four-kingdoms schema, the "one like a son of man," the war of Michael against the prince of Persia (Dan 10–12). ~165 BCE, Maccabean crisis.
- **Qumran:** [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — *Two Spirits* treatise (1QS III–IV); *War Scroll* (1QM, the 40-year eschatological war of the sons of light against the sons of darkness under Belial). Mid-2nd c. BCE through 1st c. CE.
- **Jubilees:** [[phase-3-009-jubilees]] — Mastema as adversary; cosmic moral dualism.
- **Christian:** Revelation (cosmic war in chapter 12; the beast, the dragon, Babylon, Armageddon); Pauline "principalities and powers" (Eph 6:12); the Synoptic apocalyptic discourse (Mark 13 par.).
- **Manichaean:** [[phase-4-014-cologne-mani-codex]] — the cosmic war between Light and Darkness adopts and radicalizes apocalyptic cosmic warfare into a substantive metaphysical dualism (see [[two-principles]]).

## Variants
- **Inaugurated:** the cosmic war is *already underway* and breaking in (Qumran, the Synoptic Gospels, Paul).
- **Imminent:** the final battle is imminent — *now* (Daniel, Revelation, 1 Thessalonians).
- **Deferred:** the war will occur at the eschaton but is not yet present (much of rabbinic eschatology).
- **Realized:** the apocalyptic crisis is reinterpreted as a present spiritual reality without near-future temporal expectation (Eckhart, modern existentialist theology).
- **Modern reception:** premillennialist dispensationalism (Darby, Scofield) literalizes the schema as a coming-soon timetable; Boyer 1992 traces its impact on US religious-political culture. Liberation theology recovers the political-resistance dimension.

## Connections claimed
- **Persian dependence.** That Jewish apocalyptic eschatology is in significant part a *reception* of Achaemenid Zoroastrian cosmic dualism is held by Hultgård, Stausberg, Boyce, and others. Conservative biblical scholarship (Barr 1985) resists strong-dependence claims, noting indigenous Israelite roots in prophetic literature and in the [[chaoskampf]] motif.
- **Continuity with chaoskampf.** Forsyth 1987 traces "the old enemy" — Leviathan / Rahab / Yam / Belial / Satan / the Dragon — as a continuous figure from Canaanite combat myth through Jewish apocalyptic to Christian Revelation. The same combat schema is *re-temporalized*: cosmogonic combat at creation → eschatological combat at the end.
- **Christian appropriation.** Pauline cosmology, the Synoptic exorcism narratives, and Revelation all presuppose the apocalyptic-cosmic-warfare frame; the Christological "victory" idiom (Christ as defeater of the powers) is its central Christian inflection (Aulén's *Christus Victor*).
- **Anti-Roman code.** [[anti-roman-polemic]] — the Beast of Revelation, Babylon, the kings of the earth: the apocalyptic genre is in significant part political resistance literature against imperial domination (Adela Yarbro Collins, Schüssler Fiorenza).
