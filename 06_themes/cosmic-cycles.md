# Cosmic Cycles

The cosmological motif of *cyclical time* — the cosmos undergoes repeated cycles of creation, maintenance, decay, and dissolution, often nested at multiple scales. Distinct from linear-progressive salvation-historical time ([[salvation-history]], biblical) and from punctual-eternal time (mystical-Platonic). Major instances:

- **Indian *yuga* schema** — [[phase-2-001-rig-veda-family-books]], [[phase-2-017-mahabharata-ramayana-oral-layers]]: four *yuga*s (Krita, Treta, Dvapara, Kali) of decreasing length and increasing degeneration make up a *mahāyuga*; 1000 *mahāyuga*s make one *kalpa* (a day of Brahmā); a Brahmā lives 100 Brahmā-years. The numerical machinery is staggering.
- **Stoic *ekpyrōsis* / *palingenesis*** — [[phase-3-005-stoic-foundational-texts]]: the cosmos periodically dissolves into fire and is regenerated in exact recurrence; Nietzsche's "eternal recurrence" is the Stoic concept resurrected.
- **Platonic Great Year** — [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]]: *Timaeus* 39d; the cycle when all planets return to their original positions (Cicero estimated 12,954 years).
- **Mesoamerican** — see phase-8 sources (Phase 8 agent's territory); Mesoamerican calendrical cycles are deeply cyclical.

Eliade made cyclical time the great pre-modern Other against which Judeo-Christian linear time defines itself; the dichotomy has been substantially complicated by subsequent scholarship (much pre-modern cosmology is both/and, with cycles nested in larger frames or ended by terminal events).
