# Holy-Spirit-Empowered Witness (Lukan)

The distinctively Lukan theology of the Holy Spirit as the *power for mission* — descending at Pentecost (Acts 2), inaugurating prophetic speech in many languages, propelling the apostolic witness from Jerusalem "to the ends of the earth" (Acts 1:8). Lukan pneumatology is *missiological* in emphasis (in contrast to Pauline pneumatology, which is more soteriological-ecclesial, and Johannine, which is more revelatory-paraclete). Major loci: Lk 4:18–21 (Jesus' inaugural sermon, anointed by the Spirit), Lk 24:49 (the promised "power from on high"), Acts 1:8, Acts 2 (Pentecost), Acts 8 (Samaria), Acts 10 (Cornelius / Gentile Pentecost), Acts 19 (Ephesus / disciples of John).

- [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] — the source; the Lukan two-volume work has a coordinated pneumatology absent in the other Synoptics.

Critical issues: did Luke have a developed theology of *Christian initiation* in which Spirit-baptism is distinct from water-baptism (as Pentecostals read it), or is the Spirit-baptism / water-baptism distinction a Lukan literary device specific to founding moments of the Gentile mission (so Dunn 1970 *Baptism in the Holy Spirit*)? Pentecostal and reformed pneumatologies have engaged this question for the past half-century.
