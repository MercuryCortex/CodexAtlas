# Panentheism (All-in-God)

The theological position that the world is *in* God without being identical with God — divine immanence combined with divine transcendence. Distinguished from classical theism (which keeps Creator and creation more sharply distinct) and from pantheism (which identifies God with the world). The term was coined by K. C. F. Krause (1828); the position is much older — Cusa's *De Docta Ignorantia*, Eckhart's German sermons, Boehme's *Mysterium Magnum*, Ramanuja's *Vishishtadvaita* (qualified non-dualism) — and has been retrieved by 20th-c. process theology (Whitehead, Hartshorne, Cobb, Moltmann) as a way to maintain divine reality while accommodating an evolutionary, dynamic cosmology.

## Manifestations

- **Cusan.** [[phase-5-021-cusa-de-docta-ignorantia]] *De Docta Ignorantia* (1440) — God as the *coincidentia oppositorum* in whom maximum and minimum coincide; the universe as the *contraction* (*contractio*) of divine infinity into the finite plural. Cusa's structural seed of later panentheism. See also Cusa's *De Visione Dei*.
- **Eckhart.** [[the-godhead-eckhart]] — the soul's *ground* is identical with God's *ground*; the *Gottheit* contains all in itself. Eckhart's Latin works (*Opus tripartitum*) and German sermons together build a panentheistic structure, though he was condemned (1329) partly for it.
- **Boehme.** [[phase-6-029-boehme-aurora]] — the *Ungrund* is the abyssal divine ground; the world is divine self-revelation. Schelling and German Idealism inherit Boehme.
- **Vishishtadvaita.** [[phase-5-016-ramanuja-sribhasya]] — Ramanuja's qualified non-dualism: the world (and souls) are the *body* of Brahman; Brahman is the *inner controller* (*antaryāmin*) of all. Brahman has cit (souls) and acit (matter) as its modes. Structurally panentheistic in the precise sense — the world is in Brahman without being identical with Brahman.
- **Process theology.** Whitehead, *Process and Reality* (1929) — God has a *primordial* nature (transcendent) and a *consequent* nature (receiving the world into divine experience). Hartshorne's "dipolar theism" systematizes. Cobb, *Christ in a Pluralistic Age* (1975) develops it Christologically. Moltmann's *The Crucified God* (1972) and *God in Creation* (1985) bring panentheism into mainstream Protestant theology.
- **Christian feminist theology.** Sallie McFague's *The Body of God* (1993) and similar retrievals — using panentheism to ground ecological and embodied theology.

## Disputes

- **Whether classical theism (Augustine, Aquinas) is *already* panentheistic.** Some retrievers (Hart, Clayton) argue yes — Aquinas's "God is in all things" and his immanence-language are functionally panentheistic. Critics (Hartshorne, McFague) argue classical theism's sharp transcendence makes the world's *being in God* nominal rather than real.
- **Whether panentheism preserves enough divine transcendence to be theologically Christian.** Conservative Protestant theology has often charged panentheism with collapse into pantheism. Process theology's *de facto* drift toward divine-becoming-with-the-world makes some Christians uncomfortable.
- **Whether Ramanuja's Vishishtadvaita is *the same* as Western panentheism (Hartshorne, who claimed the parallel) or has irreducibly Hindu-theological commitments that prevent the identification (Mumme, Bartley).**
