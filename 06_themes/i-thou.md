# I-Thou (Buber's dialogical philosophy)

## Definition
Martin Buber's distinction between two fundamental modes of relating: the *I-Thou* (*Ich-Du*) relation, in which one addresses another as a *whole presence* — a subject met in mutual responsiveness — and the *I-It* (*Ich-Es*) relation, in which one *uses*, *categorizes*, *describes*, or *manipulates* another as a thing among things. The *I* itself differs in the two relations: there is no neutral or pre-relational *I*; the *I* is always the *I-of-I-Thou* or the *I-of-I-It*. Every You eventually becomes an It (and properly so — life requires both modes), but the *I-Thou* moments are constitutive of personhood; without them the *I-of-I-It* collapses into pure functionality. *God* — the *eternal Thou* — is encountered in every genuine I-Thou and is the *one Thou* that can never become an It.

Diagnostic markers: (a) the *two-relational-modes* schema (not two attitudes within a single relation); (b) the *whole-presence* address; (c) the asymmetry — every You can become an It, but God-as-Thou never does; (d) the explicit anti-Cartesian / anti-monological orientation.

## Earliest attestations
- **Martin Buber, *Ich und Du*** (Leipzig, Insel-Verlag, 1923): the canonical short work; ~120 pages in original German. Smith English translation 1937; Kaufmann 1970.
- **Earlier prep work:** *Daniel: Dialogues on Realization* (1913) is the immediate prototype, exploring "realizing" vs. "orienting" relations to the world. Buber's three early *Reden über das Judentum* (Speeches on Judaism, 1909–1911) develop the religious-philosophical background.
- **Subsequent elaborations:** *Dialogue* (1929), *The Question to the Single One* (1936), *Between Man and Man* (1947 English collection), *Eclipse of God* (1952 English).

## Variants
- **Buber's own development:** the early existential-mystical Buber (pre-1913) gives way to the explicitly dialogical Buber (1923 onward); the dialogical principle stabilizes the mature thought.
- **Franz Rosenzweig's *Stern der Erlösung*** (*Star of Redemption*, 1921): independently developed *Sprachdenken* (speech-thinking) and dialogical philosophy; Rosenzweig and Buber collaborated on the Hebrew Bible translation 1925–1962 (Rosenzweig until his 1929 death) and influenced each other's mature thought.
- **Emmanuel Levinas's *Totalité et infini*** (1961): the Face-of-the-Other ethics is the major late-20th-c. development of dialogical philosophy; Levinas regarded Buber as a primary forerunner but argued I-Thou *reciprocity* misses the *asymmetry* of ethical responsibility.
- **Catholic personalism** (Mounier, Marcel, Wojtyła): Maritain's "personal communion" theology, Karol Wojtyła's *Person and Act* (1969) — independent but parallel.
- **Tournier and pastoral psychology:** Buberian categories entered mid-20th-c. pastoral theology and depth psychology (Paul Tournier, Rollo May).

## Connections claimed
- **Hasidic background.** Buber's lifelong work on Hasidism — *Tales of the Hasidim* (1947–1948), *The Origin and Meaning of Hasidism* — provided the religious-experiential matrix for the dialogical principle. Hasidic teaching of the *zaddik* (righteous master)'s relation to disciples and to God was for Buber the lived form of I-Thou. The relationship has been intensively debated: did Buber misrepresent Hasidism as more existentialist-personalist than it actually was? Gershom Scholem famously argued yes; Mendes-Flohr 2019 mediates.
- **German philosophical context.** Buber's I-Thou stands in critical dialogue with Husserlian phenomenology, Heideggerian *Mitsein*, the early Romantic dialogical strand (Jacobi, Feuerbach), and the *Lebensphilosophie* of Dilthey and Simmel.
- **Theological reception.** Influence on Karl Barth (early Barth's *Römerbrief* shares some Buberian sensibilities, though Barth was theologically distant); Reinhold Niebuhr; Bonhoeffer's *Sanctorum Communio*; Tillich's *correlation* method.
- **Christian-Jewish dialogue.** Buber became a major Jewish interlocutor of post-war Christian theology (Karl Heim, Paul Tillich, Reinhold Niebuhr). His German-Jewish Bible translation with Rosenzweig was a major scholarly-cultural event.

## Disputes
- **Buber's Hasidism interpretation.** Scholem 1961 *Major Trends in Jewish Mysticism* and *The Messianic Idea in Judaism* (1971) argued Buber's "religious anarchism" misrepresented Hasidism, which is more kabbalistic-doctrinal than Buber's I-Thou portrait suggests. Buber's defense: he was retrieving the *spirit* of Hasidic teaching for modern existential application. The Scholem-Buber debate is foundational for modern Jewish studies.
- **Dialogical philosophy and mysticism.** Buber's mature thought is explicitly anti-mystical — the I-Thou is *relational*, not unitive; God is the eternal *Thou*, not the *One*. This distinguishes Buber from kabbalah-derived mystical monism and from much of comparative-mysticism (e.g., Otto's *Wholly Other* preserves transcendence but is not specifically dialogical).
- **Levinas's critique.** Reciprocity (Buber) vs. asymmetry (Levinas). Whether Buber's I-Thou adequately ground ethics — the ethical claim of the Other might require *non-reciprocity* (I owe the Other before they owe me).
- **Political-Zionist disputes.** Buber's binational-Palestine Zionism (Brit Shalom, Ihud) and his late-life critiques of post-1948 Israeli policy made him controversial within Zionism; the political-philosophical disputes are inseparable from his philosophical reception.
