# Pratītyasamutpāda (Dependent Origination)

## Definition
*Pratītyasamutpāda* (Sanskrit) / *paṭiccasamuppāda* (Pali) — "dependent origination," "dependent arising," "conditioned co-arising" — is the metaphysical core of Buddhist causality: all phenomena arise in dependence on other phenomena; nothing arises independently; therefore nothing has *svabhāva* (inherent / intrinsic / self-establishing existence). The doctrine has two principal forms:

**General formula** (the abstract metaphysical statement):
> "When this is, that comes to be; from the arising of this, that arises. When this is not, that does not come to be; from the cessation of this, that ceases." (Saṃyutta Nikāya 12.21, the standard formulation across the Pali canon)

**Twelvefold chain** (the concrete soteriological-cosmological application to *saṃsāra*-perpetuation):
1. *Avidyā* (ignorance) → 2. *Saṃskāra* (volitional formations) → 3. *Vijñāna* (consciousness) → 4. *Nāmarūpa* (mind-and-body) → 5. *Ṣaḍāyatana* (six sense-bases) → 6. *Sparśa* (contact) → 7. *Vedanā* (feeling) → 8. *Tṛṣṇā* (craving) → 9. *Upādāna* (clinging) → 10. *Bhava* (becoming) → 11. *Jāti* (birth) → 12. *Jarāmaraṇa* (aging-and-death).

The twelvefold chain explains how *saṃsāra* (the cycle of rebirth) perpetuates itself: ignorance generates volitional formations, which generate consciousness... ultimately producing birth, aging, and death — and then the cycle restarts. Reversal of the chain (cessation of ignorance → cessation of formations → ... → cessation of aging-and-death) is liberation / nirvāṇa.

[[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna's]] [[phase-4-075-mulamadhyamakakarika|Mūlamadhyamakakārikā]] famously reformulates the relation between dependent origination and emptiness ([[emptiness-sunyata]]) as identity: *"Whatever is dependently arisen is what we call emptiness. That is dependent designation; precisely that is the middle way"* (MMK 24.18). Pratītyasamutpāda *is* emptiness; emptiness *is* dependent origination. This identity-formulation is the philosophical core of all subsequent Mādhyamika and Tibetan Buddhist metaphysics.

A passage "has" the pratītyasamutpāda theme if it articulates the general formula, the twelvefold chain, or the dependent-origination-as-emptiness identification.

## Earliest attestations
- **Buddhist (Pali)**: the Buddha's enlightenment-narrative in the Vinaya Mahāvagga 1.1-3 describes the Buddha contemplating dependent origination forward and backward as the central content of his awakening. *Mahāhatthipadopama Sutta* (MN 28) — "Whoever sees dependent origination sees the Dhamma; whoever sees the Dhamma sees dependent origination." *Mahānidāna Sutta* (DN 15) — the most detailed canonical exposition of the twelvefold chain. *Paṭicca-samuppāda-saṃyutta* (SN 12) — entirely devoted to dependent origination.
- **Buddhist (Sanskrit and Chinese)**: parallel expositions across the Sarvāstivāda Sanskrit Āgamas and Chinese T 99 *Saṃyukta Āgama*.
- **Buddhist (Mādhyamika)**: [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna's]] MMK ch. 26 is the detailed Mādhyamika analysis of the twelvefold chain; ch. 24 contains the famous dependent-origination-as-emptiness identity. Earlier within the [[phase-3-031-asthasahasrika-prajnaparamita|Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā]] (~-100 to +100), dependent origination is implicit in the emptiness-of-dharmas analysis.
- **Buddhist (Theravāda systematic)**: [[buddhaghosa|Buddhaghosa's]] [[phase-4-076-visuddhimagga|Visuddhimagga]] ch. 17 is the most detailed Theravāda treatment of the twelvefold chain — distributed across three lifetimes (past lifetime → current lifetime → future lifetime).

## Variants
- **Three-lifetimes interpretation** (Theravāda Buddhaghosa): the twelve links span three lifetimes — links 1-2 (ignorance, formations) belong to the past life as causes; links 3-10 (consciousness through becoming) are the present life as results-becoming-causes; links 11-12 (birth, aging-and-death) are the future life as results. This is the standard Theravāda analysis.
- **One-lifetime interpretation** (Gombrich 2009; Tilmann Vetter): some modern critical scholarship reads the twelve links as a single psychological-process map of how craving leads to suffering within a single lifetime, with the rebirth-elaboration as a later commentarial development. Contested.
- **Mādhyamika reformulation**: dependent origination = emptiness. Nāgārjuna's reading. The twelvefold chain is preserved but understood as the dependent designation of phenomena that lack inherent existence.
- **Yogācāra extension**: dependent origination operates not on external dharmas but on consciousness-formations (*vijñapti-mātra*). Vasubandhu's *Trimśikā* articulates this.
- **Modern environmental-ethics reception** (Thich Nhat Hanh's "interbeing"): dependent origination has been reformulated as the philosophical ground for environmental ethics — all phenomena are inter-dependent, therefore care for one entails care for all. This is a contemporary creative extension.

## Connections claimed
- **Within Buddhism**: pratītyasamutpāda is foundational across all Buddhist traditions. It is the metaphysical correlate of [[anatman-no-self|anātman]] (which denies a permanent self) and of [[emptiness-sunyata|śūnyatā]] (which denies inherent existence in all phenomena).
- **Polemic-against Vedānta**: dependent origination contrasts with Vedānta's *sat-kārya-vāda* (the doctrine that effects pre-exist in causes — the Sāṃkhya-Vedānta theory) and *brahma-vivarta* (the world as appearance of a permanent *brahman*). The Buddhist position: no permanent substance underlies arising; phenomena are dependent-and-conditioned all the way down.
- **Polemic-against Nyāya causation**: the Nyāya school's *asat-kārya-vāda* (the doctrine that effects do not pre-exist in causes — they arise newly) is also rejected by Mādhyamika; both *sat-kārya-vāda* and *asat-kārya-vāda* are rejected via the *catuṣkoṭi* (tetralemma) method.
- **Modern Western reception**: contemporary process philosophy (Whitehead) has been compared to pratītyasamutpāda; Macy 1991 (*Mutual Causality in Buddhism and General Systems Theory*) develops the comparison systematically. Structural-functional parallels; no claim of historical transmission.
