# Spirit-Matter Dualism

Spirit (pneuma, nous, light) is good; matter (hylē, body, darkness-as-density) is evil, inferior, or at best inert. Salvation involves liberation of spirit *from* matter. Distinguished from cosmic dualism ([[dualism-cosmic]]) — there two co-equal agents *war*; here there is a single ontological gradient where the higher *suffers* in the lower. The two dualisms often combine (Manichaeism has both) but are conceptually separable.

## Manifestations
- **Platonic substrate:** [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — *Phaedo*'s body-as-tomb (*sōma sēma*); the rational soul belongs to the intelligible realm and is "buried" in flesh; philosophy is "practice for death." Plato is not absolutely dualist (the *Timaeus* makes the cosmos good), but the *Phaedo* and *Republic* provide the textual base for later spirit-matter contempt.
- **Hellenistic Jewish:** [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]] — body as prison/tomb of the soul; integration of Platonic anthropology with Mosaic anthropology.
- **Hermetic:** [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] — the soul descends through the planetary spheres, accreting vices; bodily existence is the spheres' work; ascent is the reverse stripping. Asclepius (CH II) sees matter more positively; the corpus is not uniform.
- **Sethian Gnostic:** [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]], [[phase-4-006-hypostasis-of-the-archons]], [[phase-4-007-on-the-origin-of-the-world]] — material cosmos is the work of an ignorant demiurge; bodies are designed to confine the divine spark; salvation is escape from the cosmos as such.
- **Valentinian Gnostic:** [[phase-4-003-gospel-of-truth]] — softer; matter is not absolutely evil but is the realm of "Error" and dispersion; redemption restores spirits to the Pleroma.
- **Manichaean:** [[phase-4-015-kephalaia-of-the-teacher]] — Light particles imprisoned in the matter that is the substance of Darkness; the cosmos is a machinery for liberating the Light. Cosmic dualism + spirit-matter dualism fully integrated.
- **Neoplatonic:** [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — matter as principle of evil (privation, indefiniteness); but the embodied soul retains a higher self that never descends. Plotinus polemicizes against the Gnostics (Enn. II.9) for excessive contempt of the cosmos.

## Disputes
- **Whether "Gnosticism" should be defined by spirit-matter dualism.** Jonas (1958) made anti-cosmic dualism *the* defining feature; Williams (1996) and King (2003) have argued the category is too varied — some texts called "Gnostic" are not particularly world-hating (Gospel of Truth, Tripartite Tractate). The current trend: drop "Gnosticism" as a unified category, study spirit-matter dualism as a motif crossing what we used to call Gnostic, Hermetic, and Platonist boundaries.
- **Christian orthodoxy's position.** Mainstream Christianity rejected radical spirit-matter dualism (insisting on Genesis 1's "very good," on Incarnation, on bodily resurrection) but retained ascetic strands often functionally dualist (Augustinian sexuality).
