# Cosmic Dualism

Two opposed *cosmic* principles — Good vs. Evil, Light vs. Darkness, Truth vs. the Lie — that are co-eternal or near-co-eternal and whose conflict structures world-history. Distinct from:
- **Dualism-spirit-matter** ([[dualism-spirit-matter]]): spirit good, matter evil — a single ontological gradient, not two opposed agents.
- **Ethical dualism** ([[ethical-dualism]]): two paths or inclinations within a single cosmos and a single creator (e.g., rabbinic *yetzer ha-tov* / *yetzer ha-ra'*).
- **Monism with privation evil**: evil as absence of good (Augustinian / Plotinian).

The strong form: two principles, each substantial, each agentive, locked in real combat. The weak form: a high God who eventually subjugates a lesser evil power.

## Manifestations
- **Zoroastrian:** [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]] (Yasna 30: the Twin Spirits choosing good and evil); [[phase-2-004-yasna-younger-avesta]] elaborates. Ahura Mazda vs. Angra Mainyu / Ahriman — the paradigm of cosmic dualism. The Zurvanite heresy made the two twins of a higher god Zurvan (time); the Mazdaean orthodoxy resisted this.
- **Qumran:** [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — the "Two Spirits" treatise (1QS 3:13–4:26): "He created the spirits of light and darkness ... in the hand of the Prince of Light is dominion over all sons of righteousness ... in the hand of the Angel of Darkness is total dominion over the sons of deceit." Mitigated dualism — both spirits are created by God. The single closest Jewish parallel to Zoroastrian dualism.
- **Daniel:** [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — angelic princes warring (Michael vs. the prince of Persia, Dan 10); softer than Qumran.
- **Manichaean:** [[phase-4-014-cologne-mani-codex]], [[phase-4-015-kephalaia-of-the-teacher]], [[phase-4-016-shabuhragan]] — the most fully developed cosmic dualism in late antiquity. Two co-eternal realms (Light, Darkness) collide; current history is the disentanglement of Light from Darkness. See [[father-of-greatness]], [[prince-of-darkness-manichaean]].
- **Christian dualism:** Marcionite (the just god of the OT vs. the gracious God of Jesus); Gnostic Sethian (the demiurge below the Pleroma); never mainstream-Christian but persistently recurrent.

## Disputes
- **Did Zoroastrianism shape Jewish dualism, or did they develop in parallel?** Boyce (1979): direct influence in the Achaemenid period explains the Qumran two-spirits doctrine. Frey (1997): more cautious — shared milieu + selective adoption. The Qumran text uses Persianizing vocabulary patterns that strengthen Boyce's case.
- **Zurvanism's prevalence.** Whether Zurvanism was a fringe or majority current within Sasanian Zoroastrianism is debated; the textual evidence is fragmentary, mostly preserved in Christian and Manichaean polemic.
