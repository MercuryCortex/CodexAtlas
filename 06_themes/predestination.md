# Predestination

The doctrine that the salvation (or, on some accounts, the damnation) of individuals is *eternally fixed by divine decree*, independent of their foreknown merits or works. Distinct from but related to [[predestination-protestant]] (the specifically Reformation development). Three diagnostic axes: (1) does election rest on foreseen faith/merit (Arminian, much patristic) or on sovereign decree alone (Augustinian, Reformed); (2) is reprobation parallel to election (*double* predestination — Calvin's "horrible decree") or merely the absence of election (single); (3) is the scope universal or particular?

- [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Romans 9–11 is the key NT text; the potter and the clay, Esau and Jacob, vessels of mercy and wrath. Pauline scholarship is divided on whether Paul has individual or corporate election in view.
- [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — the Qumran *Two Spirits* discourse (1QS III–IV) is a Jewish-sectarian predestinarianism: humans are sorted from creation between the Spirit of Light and Spirit of Darkness.
- [[phase-4-028-augustine-confessions]] / [[phase-4-029-augustine-city-of-god]] — Augustine's mature anti-Pelagian doctrine (*De praedestinatione sanctorum*, 429) makes predestination the basis of grace; *City of God* XV–XXII gives the cosmic-historical frame.
- [[phase-6-007-luther-bondage-of-will]] / [[phase-6-010-calvin-institutes]] — the Reformation retrieval. Calvin's *Institutes* III.21–24 systematizes double predestination.

The doctrine generates the perennial problem of *theodicy* (how can a good God damn the non-elect?) and of [[free-will-vs-determinism]].
