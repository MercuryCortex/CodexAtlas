# The Venus Cycle (Mesoamerican)

## What it is
The Venus cycle is the Mesoamerican astronomical-ritual reckoning of the planet **Venus** — tracked through its ~584-day synodic period (the interval between successive appearances as morning star) and commensurated with the ritual and solar calendars. Mesoamerican skywatchers achieved an extraordinarily precise running count of Venus, and bound it tightly to mythology and to the timing of war and ritual: the heliacal rising of Venus as morning star was regarded as an ominous, dangerous moment.

## Earliest attestation
The supreme document is the **Venus Table of the [[phase-8-003-dresden-codex-maya|Dresden Codex]]** — a Maya almanac tracking five Venus synodic periods (5 × 584 = 2,920 days = 8 solar years) with corrections that kept the count accurate over centuries. Aveni (2001) judges it "the highest-precision pre-modern astronomical document outside Mesopotamia." Venus tables and Venus-related ritual also fill sections of the [[phase-8-002-codex-borgia|Codex Borgia]].

## Mythology
Venus is identified with **[[quetzalcoatl]]** in his guise as Tlahuizcalpantecuhtli, "Lord of the Dawn / the House of Dawn" — the morning star whose first rays were believed to strike and harm different classes of beings on different days. The disappearance and reappearance of Venus underwrote the myth of Quetzalcoatl's death, descent, and rebirth as the morning star.

## Cross-tradition findings
- **Convergent high-precision planetary astronomy.** The Mesoamerican Venus count and the Babylonian Venus observations (the Venus Tablet of Ammisaduqa) are the two great pre-modern programs of precise Venus tracking, developed with no contact — a striking independent convergence on the same celestial object as a source of omens and ritual timing. Wired as a parallel to the Mesoamerican ritual calendar [[tonalpohualli]] with which it is commensurated.
