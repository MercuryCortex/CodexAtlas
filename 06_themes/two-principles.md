# Two Principles (Manichaean Light/Darkness)

## Definition
Mani's foundational cosmogonic doctrine: from primordial eternity there exist *two co-eternal substantive principles* — the Realm of Light (presided over by the [[father-of-greatness]]) and the Realm of Darkness (under the [[prince-of-darkness-manichaean]]). The two are radically distinct in nature, ethical valence, ontological status — neither derived from the other, neither subordinate to the other. The cosmic-historical drama begins when Darkness perceives Light and invades it, prompting the Father of Greatness to emanate defensive entities; the resulting cosmic war produces the present *mixed* cosmos (see [[mixture-cosmology]]). Diagnostic marker: the *substantive* duality of two co-eternal principles — distinguished from Zoroastrian *ethical* dualism (where Ohrmazd and Ahriman are sometimes both derived from Zurvan), from Christian privation-of-good monism (evil is *non-being*, not a substance), and from Gnostic emanationist schemes where matter / the demiurge derive from a fallen aeon within a single ultimate principle.

## Earliest attestations
- **Mani himself** (216–277): the Šābuhragān ([[phase-4-016-shabuhragan]]) — his short Middle Persian summary for Shapur I — is the earliest articulation. Preserved fragmentarily in Turfan Middle Persian, in the Cologne Mani Codex ([[phase-4-014-cologne-mani-codex]]) Greek excerpts, and in citations by al-Bīrūnī, Theodore bar Konai, and Ibn al-Nadīm's *Fihrist*.
- **Manichaean Kephalaia** ([[phase-4-015-kephalaia-of-the-teacher]], Coptic, 4th c.): the most extensive surviving systematic exposition. The "Two Roots" — *al-aṣlān* in Arabic Manichaean sources — is the technical formula.
- **Augustine on Manichaean cosmology:** *Contra Faustum*, *De moribus Manichaeorum*, *De Natura Boni* — outside-perspective summaries of Manichaean teaching, supplemented by the long-period internal sources from Turfan and Dunhuang discoveries.
- **Cathar reception:** the medieval Liber de Duobus Principiis ([[phase-5-023-liber-de-duobus-principiis]], 13th c.) — whether Catharism is *literally* descended from Manichaeism (Runciman 1947) or is a medieval revival of dualism with parallel theological architecture (Hamilton, Pegg 2008) is debated; the doctrinal correspondence is striking either way.

## Variants
- **Mani's original (216–277):** strict substantive two-principle dualism.
- **Augustinian polemical caricature:** Augustine's anti-Manichaean writings sometimes overstate Manichaean naive substantivism — but the underlying dualism is genuine.
- **Cathar moderate dualism** (Bogomil-origin Cathars and the *De Duobus Principiis*'s "absolute dualism" branch): two principles ultimately, with cosmic combat as historical drama. The "moderate" Bogomils/Cathars hold a single principle with Satan as fallen subordinate.
- **Mandaean parallels:** [[phase-4-017-ginza-rba]] and Mandaean cosmology share a Light-realm / Dark-realm cosmogonic structure with significant differences in mythological detail (no Mani-style invasion narrative; Mandaeans pre-date Mani).
- **Zoroastrian comparison:** Zurvanite Zoroastrianism makes Ohrmazd and Ahriman both derive from Zurvan (Infinite Time); orthodox-Mazdean Zoroastrianism keeps them substantively independent but ethically opposed. The Mani-Zoroastrianism relationship — whether Mani was a *reformer of Zoroastrianism* or an *innovator* using Zoroastrian language polemically — is contested.

## Connections claimed
- **Zoroastrian influence on Mani.** BeDuhn and Lieu hold the obvious: Mani grew up in Sasanian Mesopotamia, was personally familiar with Zoroastrian, Jewish-Christian (Elchasaite), and Christian (Bardaisanite) traditions, and absorbed elements from all of them. The Two Principles are Mani's *radicalization* of Zoroastrian cosmic dualism into substantive co-eternity.
- **Jewish-Christian background.** The Cologne Mani Codex documents Mani's youth in an Elchasaite baptist sect; his break with them is the formative event of his prophetic career.
- **Christian theological response.** Augustine's elaboration of [[original-sin]], [[free-will-vs-determinism]], and the *privatio boni* theory of evil are direct responses to his prior Manichaeism. Without Mani, Augustine is unintelligible.

## Disputes
- **Substantivism vs. metaphor.** Western Christian polemicists (Ephrem the Syrian, Augustine) read Mani's Two Principles as crude metaphysical realism. Modern Manichaean studies (BeDuhn, Lieu, Gardner) tend to read the doctrine as a sophisticated cosmological-ethical system whose substantivism is the *strongest possible* affirmation of evil's reality, not a naive ontology.
- **Cathar-Manichaean continuity.** Runciman 1947 argued a continuous transmission from Manichaeism through Paulicianism, Bogomilism, to Catharism. Recent scholarship (Pegg 2008, Hamilton, Sennis 2016) is sharply more skeptical, treating the inquisitorial sources' "Manichaean" labels as anti-heretical topos rather than reliable historical genealogy.
- **Whether Mani synthesized or innovated.** Mani's own self-presentation (Cologne Mani Codex) frames him as the seal of prophets summarizing prior revelations (Adam, Seth, Enoch, Zoroaster, Buddha, Jesus). Whether his Two Principles are a *synthesis* of these or a genuine innovation is contested.
