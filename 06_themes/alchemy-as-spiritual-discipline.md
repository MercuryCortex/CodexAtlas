# Alchemy as spiritual discipline

## Definition
The theological-philosophical reading of alchemical practice (the *opus*) as **simultaneously material and spiritual / psychic / soteriological** — material chemical operation as outer correlate of inner transformation, the alchemist's *opus* as inseparable from the alchemist's *transformation-of-self*. The reading is one of the load-bearing themes of the Western Hermetic-alchemical tradition from [[zosimos-of-panopolis|Zosimos's]] late-antique *Visions* through medieval Arabic [[jabir-ibn-hayyan|Jabir]] through the Renaissance illustrated emblem-books (Khunrath, Maier, Mutus Liber) through 20th-c. Jungian reception ([[phase-7-017-psychology-and-alchemy]]).

**Critical methodological note:** the *strong* version of the thesis — that *all* historical alchemy was secretly-or-overtly spiritual — has been substantially challenged by post-1980 critical historiography (Newman, Principe). Their thesis: *most* practicing alchemists (especially in the 16th-17th c. and especially in Arabic, medieval Latin, and Renaissance contexts) understood themselves to be doing **chymistry** — material laboratory operations aimed at material transmutation — and the explicit spiritualization of alchemy is a specific *register* within the broader tradition, not its universal character. The Jungian reading correctly identifies this *register* but historically overgeneralizes it.

The Codex Atlas tiering of the theme:
1. **Tier-1 historical claim:** A *register* of alchemical writing (Zosimos, Khunrath, Maier, Mutus Liber, etc.) explicitly integrates material and spiritual operation. **Well-attested.**
2. **Tier-1 historical claim:** Many practicing alchemists understood themselves to be doing material *chymistry* without overt spiritualization (Newman-Principe). **Well-attested.**
3. **Tier-2 / Tier-3 interpretive claim:** The Jungian reading — alchemy as *projected individuation* — is a powerful 20th-c. interpretive frame, but it overgeneralizes historically. **Useful as reception-history, not as primary historical claim.**

## Earliest attestations

- **Late-antique Greco-Egyptian:** [[phase-4-074-zosimos-of-panopolis-corpus|Zosimos's *Visions*]] (~290–310 CE) — the priest-figure dismembered and reconstituted in the alchemical bowl-altar. **The proto-text of the spiritual-alchemical register.**
- **Arabic medieval:** [[phase-5-039-jabir-corpus-arabic-alchemy|Jabirian corpus]] integrating chemical procedure with cosmological-philosophical framework — quantitatively *mīzān*-balanced but situated in a Hermetic-natural-philosophical universe.
- **Renaissance:** [[phase-6-034-khunrath-amphitheatrum-sapientiae|Khunrath's 'oratorium-laboratorium' plate]] (1595/1609) — the **canonical visual emblem** of integrated spiritual-material alchemy.
- **17th-c.:** [[phase-6-035-maier-atalanta-fugiens|Maier's *Atalanta Fugiens*]] (1617) — emblem-music-alchemy integrated work.
- **18th-19th-c.:** Mary Anne Atwood, *A Suggestive Inquiry into the Hermetic Mystery* (1850).
- **20th-c.:** Jung's *Psychology and Alchemy* (1944), *Mysterium Coniunctionis* (1955), *Alchemical Studies* (1968).

## Variants
- **Strong spiritual-alchemical register** (Zosimos, Khunrath, Maier, the post-Jungian reception): alchemy is *primarily* spiritual; material operation is symbolic.
- **Strong material-chymical register** (most working alchemists, esp. 16th-17th c. practical-chymistry per Newman-Principe): alchemy is *primarily* material; spiritual framing is rhetorical-religious context.
- **Integrated dual register** (the iconic emblem-book tradition Rosarium / Splendor Solis / Khunrath / Maier / Mutus Liber): alchemy is *simultaneously* material and spiritual, with the *integration* itself the operative claim.
- **Jungian psychological-projective register** (post-1944): the material *opus* is a projective screen for psychic individuation.
