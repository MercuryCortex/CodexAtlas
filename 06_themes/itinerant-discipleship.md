# Itinerant Discipleship

Gerd Theissen's sociological category for the radical ethic preserved in Q and the Synoptic mission discourses: a movement of *wandering charismatics* — homeless, propertyless, family-leaving — who travel from town to town announcing the kingdom, healing, exorcising, and depending on the hospitality of settled sympathizers (the "local supporters"). The ethic of Mt 10 / Lk 9–10 ("take no gold or silver, no bag for the journey, no extra tunic") fits a small mobile prophet-class within a settled Galilean substrate.

- [[phase-3-014-q-source]] — the Q mission discourse (Lk 10:1–12 par.) is the most fully-preserved itinerancy text.
- [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — Mk 6:7–13; Mk 1:38 (Jesus the itinerant proclaimer); the disciples' renunciations (Mk 10:28).
- [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] — Mt 10 expands and modulates the Markan/Q mission charge.
- [[phase-4-001-gospel-of-thomas]] — Thomasine sayings (e.g., L. 42 "be passers-by"; L. 86 "the son of man has nowhere to lay his head") preserve the itinerant ethic in sapiential register.

The pattern survived in some early-Christian *charismatic* circles attested in the *Didache* and in 2nd-century apocryphal Acts; was already in tension with the settled house-church structures Paul mostly addresses. Theissen's reconstruction has been criticized (Stegemann, Horsley) for over-systematizing — but the textual evidence for *some* form of itinerant Jesus-movement is robust.
