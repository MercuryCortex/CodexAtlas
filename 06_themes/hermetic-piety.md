# Hermetic piety (eusebeia)

## Definition
The Hermetic religious orientation: **active reverence and gratitude toward the cosmos as the visible image of the divine**. Where Gnostic religion treats the cosmos as alien and hostile (the world is a prison fashioned by an ignorant or malevolent demiurge), Hermetic religion treats the cosmos as the *visible god* (CH X.10; Definitions III.4: "the world is the visible god, man is the invisible cosmos") — to be contemplated with reverence, thanked through prayer, and known through *gnōsis*. *Eusebeia* (Greek: "good-reverence" / piety) is the practical posture corresponding to this cosmology.

The texts are highly specific about what eusebeia entails:
1. **Knowledge of God** through cosmos-contemplation (CH XII; CH X).
2. **Thanksgiving** as the proper response to revelation (the *Prayer of Thanksgiving* — NHC VI,7 = Asclepius §41 — is the canonical Hermetic eusebeia exemplar).
3. **Bloodless sacrifice** — verbal-mental offering, not animal blood (Asclepius §41; CH XIII.18). The Hermetic community shared a "holy and bloodless food" after liturgical practice (NHC VI,7 closing scribal note).
4. **Initiation into the mysteries** of cosmos and self.
5. **Civic-cultic continuity** — the Hermetica do NOT call for abandonment of traditional Egyptian temple worship; the Hermetic teacher is *also* an Egyptian priest serving the temple deities.

## The Festugière distinction: optimist vs. pessimist Hermetic gnosis
The classic articulation, in Festugière's *La révélation d'Hermès Trismégiste* (4 vols., 1944–54), distinguishes two strands within the Hermetic literature:

- **"Optimist" Hermetism (eusebeia-religion)**: cosmos-affirming, world-positive, contemplative, cosmos = visible god, divinization through knowledge-of-cosmos. CH XII, CH X, CH XIV, the Definitions, the Prayer of Thanksgiving, the cosmos-affirming portions of the Asclepius. **This is the dominant Hermetic register.**
- **"Pessimist" Hermetism (gnosis-religion)**: cosmos as prison, body as fetter, ascent-out-of-cosmos as goal, structurally closer to Gnosticism. CH I (Poimandres) §§24–26 ascent-shedding-vices; CH VII (the entrapment of soul in body); the Egyptian-Apocalypse passage of Asclepius §§24–26.

The distinction is heuristic, not absolute (the same text can show both registers), but it captures the **Hermetic difference from Gnosticism**: the dominant Hermetic posture is cosmos-affirming reverence (eusebeia), not cosmos-rejecting escape (the Gnostic anti-cosmic position). Modern scholarship (van den Broek 2013, Fowden 1986) modifies but does not replace Festugière's framework.

## Earliest attestations
- **Hermetic**: pervasive across the Hermetic corpus from the earliest stratum (~100 CE).
- **Egyptian background**: the Hermetic *eusebeia* is the Greek translation of Egyptian *maat* — proper alignment with cosmic order — with an added Greek philosophical-religious vocabulary. Mahé 1978 reads Hermetic piety as essentially Egyptian temple-piety in Greek dress.
- **Greek background**: Plutarch *De Iside et Osiride* uses *eusebeia* extensively for Egyptian religious-philosophical practice; the Hermetic corpus shares his vocabulary.
- **Pythagorean**: the Pythagorean *Carmen Aureum* and the *Sentences of Sextus* articulate a similar reverential-philosophical piety that the Hermetica continue.

## Variants — comparison
- **Hermetic eusebeia**: cosmos-affirming, gratitude-centered, contemplative, retains civic-temple continuity.
- **Gnostic gnosis**: cosmos-rejecting, escape-centered, anti-civic.
- **Christian *eusebeia* / *pistis***: faith-centered, eschatologically-oriented (final-judgment + new-creation), but the Hellenistic-Jewish-Christian regenerative vocabulary overlaps with Hermetic at points (cf. [[hermetic-rebirth]]).
- **Stoic *eusebeia***: cosmos-affirming, providence-centered, but lacks the *gnosis*-as-divinization vector that Hermetic religion adds.
- **Iamblichean theurgy**: theurgic ritual operates inside an essentially Hermetic-style cosmos-affirming framework; Iamblichus (*De Mysteriis*) reads the Hermetica as authoritative testimony to true Egyptian religion.

## Connections claimed
- Festugière (1944–54) articulated the optimist/pessimist Hermetic distinction; it remains the dominant heuristic.
- Fowden (1986) reads Hermetic eusebeia as the *religious correlate* of the Hermetic cosmology: a cosmos-as-living-divinity demands a cosmos-affirming religious response.
- van den Broek (*Gnostic Religion in Antiquity*, Cambridge UP 2013) argues that the optimist/pessimist division is sometimes overdrawn; many Hermetic texts blend both registers.
- Mahé (1978-82) extends Festugière's frame to the Coptic and Armenian Hermetica, finding the same eusebeia-piety in both new corpora.
