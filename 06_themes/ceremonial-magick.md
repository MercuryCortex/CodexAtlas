# Ceremonial Magick

The category of Western esoteric *ritual operations* — invocation of spirits, planetary correspondences, talisman construction, sigil-binding, scrying, divine-name conjuration — conducted under structured ceremonial conditions (consecrated tools, properly drawn circles, prescribed timing per astrological hour, ritual purifications). Distinct from folk-witchcraft and from monastic-mystical contemplation; the genre's literary anchor is the post-classical *grimoire* tradition (*Picatrix*, *Lemegeton*, *Greater Key of Solomon*, *Sworn Book of Honorius*). Crowley's "magick" with the K is a 20th-c. spelling convention to distinguish ceremonial operation from stage-conjury.

- [[phase-6-009-agrippa-de-occulta-philosophia]] — Agrippa's *Three Books of Occult Philosophy* (1531–1533) is the systematic Renaissance encyclopedia of ceremonial magic.
- [[phase-6-012-dee-monas-hieroglyphica]] — John Dee's Enochian / scrying enterprise; the Renaissance "scientific magician" par excellence.
- [[phase-7-016-magick-in-theory-and-practice]] — Crowley's *Magick in Theory and Practice* (1929) is the systematic modern reformulation; the Golden Dawn-derived synthesis of Renaissance ceremonial magic, Egyptology, Kabbalah, yoga, and Thelemic theology.

The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn (1888) was the most consequential modern ceremonial-magical organization; from it descend Thelema (Crowley's A∴A∴ and OTO) and most subsequent Western ceremonial-magical traditions.
