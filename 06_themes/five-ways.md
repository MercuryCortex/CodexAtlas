# The Five Ways (Aquinas)

## Definition
Thomas Aquinas's five distinct *a posteriori* arguments for God's existence in *Summa Theologiae* Ia q.2 a.3:
1. **From motion** (*ex motu*): things move; everything moved is moved by another; this requires a first unmoved mover.
2. **From efficient cause** (*ex ratione causae efficientis*): there is an order of efficient causes; nothing can be cause of itself; an infinite regress of efficient causes is impossible; therefore a first efficient cause.
3. **From contingency / necessity** (*ex possibili et necessario*): contingent things exist; not everything can be contingent; therefore something necessary; either it derives its necessity from another or from itself; this requires an *a se* necessary being.
4. **From gradation / degrees of perfection** (*ex gradibus*): we observe more and less of perfections (good, true, noble); the more-and-less implies a maximum; the maximum of being is the cause of being in others.
5. **From governance / final causality** (*ex gubernatione rerum*): non-intelligent natural things act for an end with regularity; this presupposes an intelligence directing them.

Diagnostic marker: the *a posteriori* structure, starting from features of the observable world and inferring to God as their explanation, rather than from concept (Anselm's ontological argument is *a priori* and Aquinas rejects it explicitly, *ST* Ia q.2 a.1). Each "way" identifies what is reached *id quod omnes Deum nominant* ("which is what everyone names God").

## Earliest attestations
- **Aquinas's *Summa Theologiae*** Ia q.2 a.3 (~1265–1268), the canonical form.
- **Aquinas's *Summa contra Gentiles*** I.13 (~1259–1265) presents earlier and more elaborate versions of (1)–(3).
- **Predecessors:** Aristotle's *Physics* VIII and *Metaphysics* XII (the prime mover argument; underlies Way 1); Avicenna's distinction of *essence* and *existence* and his argument from contingency in *Kitāb al-Šifāʾ* (underlies Way 3); Maimonides's *Guide* II.1 (also from motion/contingency); Albertus Magnus's *De Causis*. Aquinas synthesizes and stylizes pre-existing arguments rather than inventing them.

## Variants
- **Aquinas's *contra Gentiles* I.13**: longer, more detailed argument from motion, with explicit engagement with Aristotelian physics.
- **Scotist variant** (Duns Scotus): Scotus reformulates the cosmological argument with greater emphasis on first-efficient-cause and on the necessity of a being *ex se necesse-esse*; his version is more rigorously deductive and less reliant on Aristotelian physics.
- **Suárez** (*Disputationes Metaphysicae*): the cosmological argument modernized for early-modern metaphysics.
- **Leibnizian variant**: the cosmological argument grounded in the Principle of Sufficient Reason.
- **Kalām cosmological argument** (al-Kindī, al-Ghazālī): an argument from the impossibility of an infinite past-temporal regress, distinct from Aquinas's argument from per-se ordered causes (which does *not* require finitude of past time).
- **Modern revivals**: Garrigou-Lagrange and 20th-c. Thomism; Edward Feser and "Aristotelian-Thomist" revival; the contemporary kalām cosmological argument (William Lane Craig).

## Connections claimed
- **Aristotelian inheritance.** Aquinas's Five Ways are unintelligible without Aristotle's *Physics*, *Metaphysics*, and *De Caelo*. The "unmoved mover" is direct from *Metaphysics* XII; the four causes structure the framework.
- **Islamic mediation.** Avicenna's distinction of essence and existence and his argument from contingency (the "Argument of the Sincere," *al-burhān al-ṣiddīqīn*) shaped Way 3. Aquinas read Avicenna in Latin translation throughout his career.
- **Jewish mediation.** Maimonides's arguments for God's existence in the *Guide* II.1 (motion, infinite regress) are explicitly cited by Aquinas; he calls Maimonides "Rabbi Moyses" with respect.
- **Way 5 and the design argument.** Way 5 is sometimes assimilated to William Paley's later "argument from design," but the two are distinct: Aquinas argues from *regular natural teleology* in non-rational substances, Paley from *complex artifacts* by analogy with intentional design.

## Disputes
- **Whether the Five Ways succeed.** The most contested philosophical-theological topic in Western philosophy of religion. Kant's *Critique of Pure Reason* (1781) systematically rejected all such arguments; Hume's *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion* (1779) anticipated the major objections. 20th-c. analytic philosophy of religion (Plantinga, Mackie, Swinburne, Oppy) has continued the debate; recent Thomist defenses (Feser 2017, Brock, Oderberg) hold the arguments work; analytic critics (Oppy 2006) hold they don't.
- **Whether the Five Ways are five separable arguments or one argument in five aspects.** Garrigou-Lagrange and the older Thomist tradition treated them as variants of a single metaphysical argument; modern analytic Thomism (Wippel 2000) treats them as five distinct *demonstrationes*.
- **Way 4 (degrees of perfection)** is generally considered the weakest of the five; it depends on the Platonic premise that "more-and-less" of a perfection presupposes a maximum exemplar of it, which is itself doubtful.
- **Way 5 and modern science.** Whether the kind of teleology Way 5 presupposes survives the Darwinian explanation of biological purpose-language. Aquinas's *cosmic* teleology (regular operations of stars, elements, natural kinds) is broader than biological design and not directly displaced by Darwin, but the contemporary status of natural teleology is contested (Feser yes; analytic naturalists no).
