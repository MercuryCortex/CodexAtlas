# Tauroctony as cosmic icon (Ulansey-Beck thesis)

## Definition
The **tauroctony** — the central icon of the Mithraic mysteries ([[tradition-mithraic-mysteries]]) depicting **[[mithras-roman|Mithras]] in a Phrygian cap kneeling on a bull, plunging a knife into its neck, attended by Cautes and Cautopates (the twin torch-bearers), a dog and snake licking the blood, a scorpion attacking the bull's testicles, and Sol-Luna in the upper corners** — was the focal object of every Mithraic *mithraeum* (~600 examples survive across the Roman Empire). The **Ulansey 1989 + Beck 2006 thesis** is that the tauroctony is **an astronomical star-map encoding precession of the equinoxes**: each iconographic element corresponds to a specific constellation lying on or near the celestial equator at the moment the tauroctony was instituted (~100 BCE – 100 CE).

The constellation-correspondences proposed by Ulansey (and partly modified by Beck):
- **The bull** = **Taurus** (the equinoctial constellation in the prior cosmic age, c. 4000–2000 BCE).
- **The scorpion attacking the bull's testicles** = **Scorpius** (the equinoctial constellation opposite Taurus on the celestial sphere).
- **The dog** = **Canis Major** or **Canis Minor**.
- **The snake** = **Hydra** (the constellation lying along the celestial equator).
- **The scorpion + ear of grain emerging from the bull's tail** = **Scorpius + Spica** (the brightest star of Virgo).
- **The raven** (in some tauroctonies) = **Corvus**.
- **Cautes and Cautopates** (the twin torch-bearers, one up, one down) = the **equinoxes** themselves (rising and setting).
- **Mithras the bull-slayer** = **Perseus** (the constellation that *kills* Taurus in its position immediately above Taurus on the celestial sphere — the constellation Mithras occupies).

The thesis: the tauroctony commemorates the **discovery of the precession of the equinoxes** (attributed to Hipparchus of Nicaea ~130 BCE) — the realization that the celestial sphere itself shifts over millennia, with the equinoctial constellation moving westward through the zodiac. Mithras, depicted *killing* Taurus, is the god *who can move the celestial sphere* — a cosmic mover-god rather than just an Iranian deity transposed.

## Earliest attestations
- **Earliest Mithraic-cult archaeological evidence**: ~50–80 CE in Roman Italy (the Mithraeum at Nida-Heddernheim near Frankfurt; the Sant'Agata dei Goti mithraeum at Rome).
- **The first developed tauroctony reliefs**: 100–150 CE.
- **Cumont 1903** reads the tauroctony as Iranian-cosmological-religious — Mithras as the Iranian creator-deity slaying the primordial bull. **Largely abandoned by post-1989 critical scholarship.**
- **Ulansey 1989** proposes the astronomical-precession reading.
- **Beck 2006** develops and integrates the Ulansey reading with the seven-grade initiation system (which Beck reads as a **soul-ascent through the planetary spheres** — see [[katabasis-and-anabasis]]).

## Variants
- **Strong-astronomical reading (Ulansey 1989):** the tauroctony is *primarily* a precession-of-equinoxes star-map.
- **Astronomical-and-cosmological reading (Beck 2006):** the tauroctony is *integrated* with the seven-grade initiation system as a comprehensive astronomical-cosmological-soteriological framework.
- **Skeptical reading (Clauss 2000):** the astronomical elements are real but not *primary*; the iconographic program is more religious-mythological than astronomical.
- **Iranian-cosmogonic reading (Cumont 1903; now largely abandoned):** Mithras as the Iranian creator-deity slaying the primordial bull.

## Why this matters for the Codex Atlas wedge
The tauroctony-as-precession-thesis is **one of the most striking late-20th-c. critical-religion findings** — it transforms our understanding of Mithraism from "Iranian-religion-transposed" to "Hellenistic-Roman astronomical-cosmological mystery-cult using Iranian iconographic vocabulary." This is methodologically important for the broader Codex Atlas project of distinguishing *iconographic-vocabulary transmission* from *religious-genealogical transmission* — see the Templar/Mystical-batch tiering discipline.
