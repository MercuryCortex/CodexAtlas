# 364-Day Solar Calendar

The schematic solar calendar — 364 days, divided into 4 seasons of 13 weeks (91 days) each, with festivals always falling on the same day of the week — attested in the Astronomical Book of 1 Enoch (1 En 72–82), Jubilees (Jub 6), and the Qumran calendrical texts (4Q317–330, 4Q394 *Mishmarot*). The 364-day schema is striking for its non-correspondence to actual astronomy (the solar year is 365.25 days; no intercalation rule is provided in the texts); its theological appeal is the elegance of its weekly cycle (52 weeks × 7 days = 364, with festivals never falling on Sabbath). The competing lunar-solar 354-day calendar with intercalation became dominant in rabbinic Judaism; the 364-day calendar appears to have been a sectarian-priestly alternative in some Second-Temple-period Jewish groups.

- [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — the Astronomical Book (1 En 72–82) is the most systematic exposition.
- [[phase-3-009-jubilees]] — Jub 6:32–38 polemicizes against the lunar calendar and prescribes the solar 364-day.
- [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — substantial calendrical material; *Mishmarot* texts coordinate priestly courses with the solar calendar.

The "calendar controversy" is one of the diagnostic sectarian-issues of Second Temple Judaism, alongside priestly purity (see [[priestly-purity]]) and exegetical method.
