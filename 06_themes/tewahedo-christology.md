# Tewahedo Christology

## Definition
Tewahedo Christology is the distinctive Christological doctrine of the [[tradition-ethiopian-orthodox-tewahedo|Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church]] (and the Eritrean Orthodox Tewahedo Church). The name comes from Geʿez *täwaḥǝdo* ("being made one") — a translation of Cyril of Alexandria's Miaphysite formula *henōsis* / *mia physis tou theou Logou sesarkōmenē* ("one nature of the Word Incarnate"). Tewahedo Christology is the Ethiopian subtype of [[miaphysite-christology|Miaphysite Christology]], doctrinally continuous with [[tradition-coptic-orthodox|Coptic]] and [[tradition-syriac-orthodox|Syriac]] sister-churches but with internal Ethiopian elaborations developed especially in the 14th-15th-c. Solomonic theological renaissance and in the 17th-19th-c. internal controversies (the *Qǝbʿat* / Unctionist + *Sost Lədät* / "Three Births" debates).

## Earliest attestations
- **Reception of Miaphysite Christology from Alexandria**: the [[event-aksumite-christianization-c330|Aksumite Christianization]] under Athanasius-Frumentius (~330) establishes the Ethiopian church's Alexandrian liturgical-theological dependency; the rejection of [[event-council-of-chalcedon-451|Chalcedon 451]] in Egypt is automatically inherited.
- **The Nine Saints mission (~480)**: brings Syriac Miaphysite monastic theology directly to Aksum, reinforcing the Miaphysite stance.
- **Indigenous Geʿez systematization (14th-15th c.)**: in [[phase-5-036-mashafa-mistir-giyorgis|*Mashafa Mistir*]] of [[giyorgis-of-sagla|Giyorgis of Sagla]] (~1424) and the [[phase-5-038-mashafa-berhan|*Mashafa Berhan*]] of [[zara-yaqob-emperor|Zarʼa Yaʼqob]] (mid-15th c.), the distinctively Ethiopian formulation emerges.

## Variants (internal Tewahedo Christological debates)
- ***Qǝbʿat* / Unctionist position**: Christ's two natures were "united by the unction of the Holy Spirit at conception." Defended primarily by the Gondar court in the 17th-18th c.
- ***Sost Lədät* / "Three Births" position**: Christ's three births — eternally from the Father, temporally from Mary, and a "third birth" via the unction of the Holy Spirit. The hyper-elaborated Unctionist position.
- ***Karra* / Knife position**: Christ united the two natures "as a sword unifies edge and back" — the more strictly Miaphysite-traditional position; defended primarily by the Tigrayan / Mahbara Sellassie circle.
- **Resolution at the Council of Boru Meda (1878) under Emperor Yohannes IV**: officially adopts the *Sost Lədät* formulation as Ethiopian Tewahedo orthodoxy; this stance remains the official Tewahedo position into the present, though all three currents survive in scholarly memory and occasional revival.

## Connections claimed
- **Same Miaphysite-Cyrillian source** as Coptic, Syriac, Armenian Christologies: all four Oriental Orthodox traditions inherit a single doctrinal substance.
- **Indigenous elaborations distinctive to Ethiopia** (*Qǝbʿat* / *Sost Lədät* / *Karra*) are unique to Tewahedo and not paralleled in the sister Oriental Orthodox churches.
- **17th-c. Jesuit period (~1557-1632)** triggered theological crystallization: Tewahedo Christology had to be articulated against incoming Catholic-Chalcedonian dyophysitism (this is the period of [[event-jesuit-mission-ethiopia-1557-1632]]), forcing systematic indigenous theological response.
