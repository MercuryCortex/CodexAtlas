# Apologetic Historiography

The genre and rhetorical strategy of *history-writing in defense* — narrating one's tradition's past in order to defend it against external charge or to claim universal status. The Hellenistic-Jewish apologetic historiographers (Josephus, Philo's *Hypothetica* and *Apologia*, the Hellenistic-Jewish Demetrius, Eupolemus, Artapanus) wrote to demonstrate Jewish *antiquity* against the Greek charge that Judaism was a recent and provincial barbarism — making Moses prior to Greek philosophers, Abraham prior to Egyptian wisdom, the Jews the original civilizers. The Christian apologetic historiographers (Eusebius preeminently) extended the strategy into the Christian case.

- [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]] — apologetic strands across the corpus, especially *Hypothetica*.
- [[phase-3-019-4-maccabees]] — apologetic-rhetorical demonstration of Jewish virtue.
- [[phase-4-027-eusebius-ecclesiastical-history]] — Eusebius's *Ecclesiastical History* is the foundational Christian apologetic historiography; his *Praeparatio Evangelica* and *Demonstratio Evangelica* construct the Christian "prepared by all of human history" thesis.

The strategy reappears across religious traditions whenever a tradition needs to justify itself against an external charge — Islamic *taʾrīkh* in dialogue with the People of the Book; medieval Christian universal-historiography (Eusebius-Jerome chronicle tradition); modern apologetics literature.
