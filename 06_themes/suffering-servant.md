# Suffering Servant

The four "Servant Songs" of Deutero-Isaiah (Isa 42:1–4; 49:1–6; 50:4–9; 52:13–53:12), particularly the fourth (Isa 53), which describes an *ebed YHWH* (servant of the LORD) — "despised and rejected," "wounded for our transgressions," who "bore the sin of many." The identity of the servant within the Isaianic context is disputed (Israel collectively per most modern Jewish reading; an individual prophet or messianic figure per most Christian reading; possibly multiple referents per Blenkinsopp). The Christological re-reading (Acts 8:32–35, 1 Pet 2:21–25) made Isa 53 the proof-text for [[crucifixion-theology]] as vicarious suffering.

- [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]] — the source: Isaiah 40–55 with its four Servant Songs.
- [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Pauline use of Isa 53 vocabulary (Rom 4:25, 1 Cor 15:3 "according to the Scriptures").
- [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — the passion predictions (Mk 8, 9, 10) and Mk 10:45 ("ransom for many") echo Isa 53.
- [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] — Acts 8 (Philip and the Ethiopian eunuch) is the explicit Christian appropriation.

Modern Jewish interpretation typically reads the servant as Israel-in-exile suffering vicariously for the nations or for its own restoration; the Christian appropriation as a *new* exegetical move dating to the earliest Christian movement.
