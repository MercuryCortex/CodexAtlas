# Reincarnation

The doctrine that the self — or something that carries the self's moral debts — **leaves one body at death and enters another at a subsequent birth**, repeatedly, until some release, exhaustion or final state ends the series.

It is one of the two great answers world religion has given to death, and it is the *opposite* answer to the other one. Read [[resurrection-of-the-dead]] alongside this node: those two motifs are not variants of a single "afterlife belief," they are rival metaphysics that have argued with each other in writing for eighteen centuries (§7).

This node is the **cross-tradition motif map**. The worked-out doctrinal positions live in the theology lens: [[doctrine-reincarnation-samsara]] for the Indic articulation, [[doctrine-gilgul]] for the Jewish one, [[doctrine-karma-action-fruit]] and [[doctrine-moksha-hindu-liberation]] and [[doctrine-nirvana-buddhist-cessation]] for the mechanism and the exits, [[doctrine-bardo-tibetan-intermediate-state]] for the interval between lives.

---

## 1. What the doctrine actually claims — and four things it is not

Reincarnation is often used as a loose synonym for "life after death." It is a much narrower claim, and the distinctions do real work:

- **Not survival of the soul.** [[soul-immortality]] says the soul persists. Reincarnation adds that it *re-embodies*. Every reincarnation doctrine entails soul-survival; the reverse does not hold — Plato's soul survives *and* returns, the Christian soul survives and does not.
- **Not resurrection.** [[resurrection-of-the-dead]] is bodily, future, eschatological and **once**. Reincarnation is bodily, present, cyclical and **repeated**. See §7.
- **Not ancestor-return as presence.** Much of [[ancestor-veneration]] holds the dead near the living without any re-embodiment. West African rebirth (§6) is the case where veneration and return genuinely combine.
- **Not spiritual regeneration.** The Hermetic *palingenesia* of [[phase-4-075-corpus-hermeticum-xiii-rebirth]] is a rebirth of the initiate **in this life**, not a transfer between bodies. Vocabulary overlap here has produced a lot of bad comparison.

---

## 2. The Indic articulation — and its one genuine innovation

The Vedic hymns of [[phase-2-001-rig-veda-family-books]] do not teach rebirth; their eschatology is a heavenly world won by sacrifice. The doctrine arrives with the early Upaniṣads.

- **[[phase-2-012-brihadaranyaka-upanishad]] 3.2.13** — [[yajnavalkya]] takes Ārtabhāga aside and teaches, privately, that a man becomes good by good action and bad by bad action. The text stages the doctrine as *esoteric*, which is itself evidence it was new.
- **BĀU 4.4.3–5** — the caterpillar drawing itself onto the next blade, the goldsmith reworking the metal: the self, having shaken off this body, makes for itself a newer and more beautiful form.
- **BĀU 6.2 // Chāndogya 5.3–10** — the *pañcāgni-vidyā*, the doctrine of the five fires, with the two paths: *devayāna* (the way of the gods, no return) and *pitṛyāna* (the way of the fathers, return via rain, plants, food, semen).

The innovation is not the cycle. It is that the cycle became **moral and undesirable**. Obeyesekere 2002 calls this *ethicization*: once conduct determines the next birth, the cycle turns into a bondage, and the religion's whole point becomes escape from it — mokṣa, nirvāṇa, kevala-jñāna. Rebirth belief without ethicization is common in the world; a soteriology built on escaping rebirth is rare, and India is where it happened.

Four Indic settlements of the same problem, in mutual polemical contact:

| Tradition | What is reborn | Mechanism | Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brahmanical / Vedāntic | the ātman, unchanging | karman as moral residue | mokṣa — [[doctrine-moksha-hindu-liberation]] |
| Buddhist | **nothing that persists** | dependent origination | nirvāṇa — [[doctrine-nirvana-buddhist-cessation]] |
| Jain | the jīva, a real individual soul | karma as subtle **matter** adhering to the soul | kevala, by burning karma off through austerity |
| Sikh | the individual soul | karman, but grace-dependent | mukti through *nām simran* |

The Buddhist column is the one to read twice. [[anatman-no-self]] denies the transmigrating entity while keeping the transmigration — the *Milindapañha*'s flame lighting the next flame, "neither the same nor another." [[siddhartha-gautama-buddha]] and [[mahavira]] were contemporaries reaching opposite verdicts on the same question, and [[shankara]] later argued against both.

## 3. The Greek articulation

The Greek doctrine is attached to a name from the beginning, and by a hostile witness.

- **[[pythagoras]]** (c. 570–495 BCE). [[xenophanes]] fr. B7 DK, quoted at Diogenes Laertius 8.36, mocks him for stopping a man beating a puppy — he recognised the voice of a dead friend. A satirist's jab is unusually good evidence: the doctrine is famous enough to satirise within Pythagoras' own generation. [[pherecydes-of-syros]] is named by later tradition as his teacher on the soul's divine origin; the ancient attribution is late and thin, and this node does not treat it as settled.
- **[[empedocles]]** (c. 494–434 BCE), *Katharmoi* B115 and B117: the daimon exiled from the blessed for bloodshed, wandering through thirty thousand seasons of forms — "I have already been boy and girl, bush, bird, and a mute fish in the sea." Here the cycle is punishment for a primal offence, and vegetarianism follows as an ethical necessity: the animal you eat may be kin.
- **The Orphic material** — [[phase-2-040-orphic-gold-tablets]] (instructions for the soul at the spring of Memory, "I am a child of Earth and starry Heaven"), [[phase-3-027-derveni-papyrus]], [[phase-3-028-orphic-hymns]]. Whether "Orphism" was ever a single movement with a fixed doctrine is itself contested (Edmonds 2013).
- **Pindar**, *Olympian* 2.68–77 and fr. 133 (quoted at Plato, *Meno* 81b): those who thrice keep their soul from injustice on both sides of death travel to the Isles of the Blessed — one of the few surviving Greek lyric passages that presupposes the cycle.
- **[[plato]]** — [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]]. *Phaedo* 70c–72e argues the cycle from the mutual generation of opposites; *Republic* X's Myth of Er stages the choice of lives and the plain of Forgetting; *Phaedrus* 248c–249b sets a ten-thousand-year circuit that the philosopher may cut to three thousand. Aristotle, *De anima* 407b20–23, dismisses the whole family of stories for pairing any soul with any body at random.
- **[[plotinus]]**, *Enneads* IV.3.8–9, keeps transmigration inside Neoplatonism, and Neoplatonism is the pipe through which it reaches the entire later Western esoteric tradition.

## 4. The contested question — Greek ↔ Indian

**This is the dispute the vault exists to present properly, and it is NOT resolved. No transmission wire is written for it in this node's YAML.**

**The case FOR a channel:**
- The doctrines appear in two literate cultures within roughly a century of each other, after millennia in which neither had it.
- Real contact routes existed: Achaemenid Persia touched both Ionia and the Indus after Darius' conquest c. 518 BCE, and Herodotus 4.44 reports Scylax of Caryanda sailing down the Indus for Darius.
- **Herodotus 2.123** says the Egyptians were first to hold that the soul enters other creatures in a three-thousand-year cycle, and that "certain Greeks" took it over — men whose names he says he knows and will not write. Antiquity itself believed in a channel.
- M.L. West 1971 makes the sustained published argument for Eastern input into early Greek thought.

**The case AGAINST:**
- Herodotus' Egyptian claim does not survive contact with Egyptology. Egyptian afterlife texts offer *transformations* of the blessed dead — Book of the Dead 76–88, becoming a falcon, a lotus, a heron — not returns to earthly life. Assmann 2005. The ancient world's own explanation for where the Greeks got it is simply wrong, which weakens ancient testimony generally here.
- Chronology cuts against the obvious direction. Pythagoras is credibly holding the doctrine c. 530 BCE, i.e. *before* Darius reached the Indus.
- Burkert 1972 accounts for the Greek doctrine from Greek and Thracian-Scythian ecstatic materials with no donor required.
- The two doctrines are structurally unalike where it counts. India ethicised the cycle and built a soteriology of escape; the early Greek cycle is about kinship of all life, ritual purity, dietary taboo and expiation of a primal crime. Empedocles' daimon is *exiled*; the Upaniṣadic self is *ignorant*.
- Obeyesekere 2002 removes the motive for the hypothesis: rebirth eschatologies arise independently in many societies, so independent emergence in Greece is unremarkable and needs no explanation.
- Even granting contact, the direction is under-determined — dating uncertainty on the Upaniṣads is wide enough that priority cannot be assigned.

**The vault's position:** structural resemblance, real but unproven opportunity for contact, no documented channel. That is a **PARALLEL**, and it is wired as one. Contrast §5, where transmission is documented and the wire says so.

## 5. Where transmission IS documented

The honest transmission claims for this motif are all **within** a sphere, or **into a minority current**, never between the two great origin zones:

- **Indic-internal** — Brahmanical → Buddhist → Jain → Sikh, one continuously developing complex arguing with itself in a shared technical vocabulary.
- **Greek-internal** — Pythagorean/Orphic → Platonic → Neoplatonic → Renaissance Platonism, then into modern Western esotericism.
- **Into Judaism, late** — [[doctrine-gilgul]] surfaces in [[phase-5-026-sefer-ha-bahir]] in 12th-c. Provence, is systematised by [[isaac-luria]] and his circle, becomes normative in Hasidism. [[saadia-gaon]] had already refuted it in the 10th century, so it existed earlier than the Bahir. Its ultimate source is genuinely unknown; do not fill the gap.
- **Into Islamicate minority communities** — Druze *taqammuṣ*, and transmigration doctrines among Nusayrī-ʿAlawī and various ghulāt currents. Sunni and Twelver-Shīʿī kalām reject *tanāsukh*; the Ashʿarite and Muʿtazilite handbooks list it among the errors.
- **Into Latin Christendom, contested** — the Cathar case, with the caveat in the wire: a serious body of current scholarship holds that the coherent "Cathar church" is an artefact of the inquisitors' own template.

## 6. The independent cases — where the point is the independence

A comparative map that only shows the connected cases teaches the wrong lesson. These are wired as parallels on purpose.

**West Africa.** Yoruba *àtúnwáyé* and Igbo *ịlọ ụwa* return the dead **into their own lineage**, and the return is **wanted**. The Yoruba praise-names Babátúndé ("father returns") and Yétúndé / Ìyábọ̀ ("mother returns") are given to children recognised as returning kin. This inverts the Indic structure point for point: bounded rather than cosmos-wide, welcomed rather than escaped, kin-identified rather than karma-determined. It is not saṃsāra with different names; it is a different doctrine that happens to involve return. See [[tradition-yoruba]], [[tradition-igbo-religion]], [[ritual-egungun-masquerade]].

**Northwest Coast and Arctic North America.** Obeyesekere 2002 surveys rebirth eschatologies among Tlingit, Kwakwaka'wakw and Winnebago groups; among Inuit communities the newborn receives the name and with it the name-soul of a recently dead relative. Again: identity-continuity within a community, not a moral ledger.

**Aboriginal Australia.** In many traditions — and the plural matters, this is 250+ language groups and never one religion — a spirit-child associated with a Dreaming site enters the mother at conception, and at death the spirit returns to that country. The tie is to **place**, through the Dreaming, not to conduct. See [[tradition-australian-aboriginal]], [[ritual-dreamtime-ceremony]], [[music-aboriginal-songlines]], [[astronomy-aboriginal-australian-sky]]. Filing this under "reincarnation" without the caveat is a category error worth naming out loud.

**Mesoamerica — the negative case.** Nahua eschatology sends the dead to Mictlan, Tlalocan, the sun-warriors' sky or the wet-nurse tree **according to how they died**, not how they lived and not with any return. See [[mictlantecuhtli]], [[mictecacihuatl]], [[tlaloc]], [[tradition-aztec-mexica]]. A large, elaborate, literate afterlife system with neither resurrection nor rebirth is a real comparative finding, not a gap in the data.

## 7. The polemics — because these are rival doctrines, not variants

Traditions have attacked each other over exactly this, which is the best evidence that the distinction is real to the participants and not an artefact of modern comparison.

- **Christian against metempsychosis.** Tertullian, *De anima* 28–35, works through the Pythagorean-Platonic cycle and ridicules it. [[augustine-of-hippo]], *De civitate Dei* X.30 and XII.20–21, attacks the Platonists' circular return as the destroyer of hope: a soul that must come back has not been saved. The stakes are structural — as Bynum 1995 shows, the whole content of the Latin doctrine is the identity of **this** body raised, and a soul that wears bodies serially dissolves it. See [[doctrine-resurrection-bodily-christian]].
- **The Origen correction.** [[event-council-of-constantinople-553]] anathematised the Origenist *pre-existence* of souls and universal restoration ([[apokatastasis]]) — not reincarnation. [[origen]] himself rejects metensomatosis explicitly (*Contra Celsum* 5.29; *Comm. in Matt.* 13.1, on why John the Baptist "is" Elijah without being Elijah returned). The widely repeated claim that a council deleted reincarnation from Christianity is a misreading of the anathemas.
- **Islamic kalām against *tanāsukh*.** Rejected by Sunni and Twelver-Shīʿī theology as incompatible with bodily resurrection at the *qiyāmah*; [[al-ghazali]] and the mutakallimūn treat it as a philosophers' and sectarians' error. It survives in the ghulāt-descended communities named in §5.
- **Saadia against gilgul.** *Emunot ve-Deʿot* VI devotes space to refuting transmigration — which proves it was live in 10th-c. Jewry, centuries before the Bahir.
- **Buddhist against ātman.** [[anatman-no-self]]: the cycle is kept, the traveller denied. The sharpest internal polemic of the four Indic settlements.

## 8. Modern Western reception — and what it actually is

Reincarnation re-enters Western culture through Renaissance Platonism, then explodes in the 19th century: [[helena-blavatsky]] and [[tradition-theosophy]], [[rudolf-steiner]] and [[phase-7-009-outline-of-esoteric-science]], and thence the whole New Age settlement.

State the honest description: **Theosophical reincarnation is a 19th-century Western construction**, assembled from Platonic and Neoplatonic materials plus a Victorian reading of Indian sources, and organised around *progress* — the soul improving across lives. Classical Indian doctrine is organised around the opposite, *bondage*. When a Western reader finds Indian religion "familiar," this is usually why, and the familiarity is manufactured.

At the fringe of the academy: Ian Stevenson's "cases of the reincarnation type" (T2 — published, engaged with, rejected by the mainstream), with Paul Edwards 1996 as the standing rebuttal on methodology, cultural distribution of the cases, and absence of a proposed mechanism. Both are cited in `refs` per the CODEX disclaimer-machine rule.
