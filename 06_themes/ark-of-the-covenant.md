# Ark of the Covenant

The portable wooden chest, gold-overlaid, surmounted by the *kappōret* (mercy-seat) with its two cherubim, that served as the cultic centerpiece of pre-Solomonic Israelite worship and was housed in the *Devir* (Holy of Holies) of the Jerusalem Temple. The Ark held (per biblical tradition) the two stone tablets of the Decalogue, Aaron's rod, and a jar of manna (Heb 9:4 summarizes); functioned as portable throne for the invisible Yahweh whose presence rested on the cherubim; played a major role in the Sinai-wilderness narrative, the conquest narrative (Jericho, Jordan-crossing), and the early monarchy narrative (1 Sam 4–6 captivity by Philistines and return). After the destruction of Solomon's Temple (586 BCE) the Ark disappears from the historical record; the Second Temple's Holy of Holies was empty.

- [[phase-2-007-hebrew-bible-e-source]], [[phase-2-011-hebrew-bible-d-source]] — Pentateuchal Ark traditions.
- [[phase-2-010-hebrew-bible-early-prophets]] — the Samuel-Kings narratives (1 Sam 4–6, 2 Sam 6 David's transport to Jerusalem, 1 Kgs 8 Solomon's deposition in the Temple).

Modern speculation on the Ark's fate: Ethiopian Orthodox tradition (housed at Aksum), Jeremiah-hid-it-on-Mt-Nebo tradition (2 Macc 2), Babylonian-loot tradition, or simply destroyed in 586. None historically verified.

The Ark figures importantly in Jewish-Christian-Hellenistic symbolism: Heb 9 reads it typologically; the *Mishnah* discusses its absence; later Kabbalah associates it with the Shekhinah's resting-place.
