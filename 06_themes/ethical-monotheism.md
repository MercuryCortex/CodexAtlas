# Ethical Monotheism

The category that frames the (Hebrew-prophetic-)Abrahamic tradition's distinctive religious-historical contribution as the binding of belief in one God to demands of *justice*, *compassion*, *care for the stranger*, and *prophetic critique* of social power — as against polytheistic ritual-transactional religion, oriental despotism, and morally indifferent natural theology. The term as such is a 19th-century coinage (with Geiger, Wellhausen) but the analytical thrust is older: Hermann Cohen's *Religion of Reason out of the Sources of Judaism* (1919) gave it the most rigorous philosophical articulation; Reform Judaism made it a self-definitional category; Buber and Levinas developed it personalistically. The category is contested both internally (does it accurately describe ancient Israel?) and externally (is it a self-flattering Western-Jewish-Christian construct?).

## Manifestations
- **Pentateuchal sources:** [[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]], [[phase-2-007-hebrew-bible-e-source]], [[phase-2-011-hebrew-bible-d-source]] — the J/E/D layers of the Hebrew Bible. The *J* source gives narrative texture (Genesis, Exodus storytelling). The *E* source emphasizes covenant and prophetic intermediation. The *D* (Deuteronomic) source explicitly binds covenant fidelity to ethical-social statutes — concern for the widow, orphan, sojourner, the *gēr* (resident alien) — as the condition of the land-promise. *D* is the textual heart of "ethical monotheism" as a self-description.
- **Classical prophets:** [[phase-2-010-hebrew-bible-early-prophets]] — Amos, Hosea, Micah, Isaiah of Jerusalem (8th c. BCE): the original prophetic-ethical critique. "*I hate, I despise your feasts...let justice roll down like waters*" (Amos 5:21–24). Cult is rejected when divorced from social justice; the One God demands ethical action. The locus classicus of the category.
- **Deutero-Isaiah's universalism:** [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]] — Isaiah 40–55 (~540s BCE): the most explicit transition from henotheism to absolute monotheism ("I am the LORD, and there is none else"; Isa 45:5). Universalist scope: YHWH is God of all peoples, Cyrus is his anointed.
- **Pauline universalization:** [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Paul extends covenant-ethical monotheism beyond ethnic Israel: justification by faith (see [[justification-by-faith]]) makes the ethical-covenantal demand universal but contingent on Christ. Whether this preserves or transforms ethical monotheism is the central Jewish-Christian theological question.
- **Quranic:** [[phase-4-034-quran]] — *tawḥīd* (radical divine unity) coupled with explicit ethical-social-justice imperatives (zakat, anti-orphan-abuse polemics, condemnation of dishonest trade); the most concentrated post-biblical statement of the prophetic-ethical-monotheist pattern. Whether to call this "ethical monotheism" or to give it its own taxonomic place is a Judeo-Christian-vs-Islamic categorical question.
- **Cohenian philosophical articulation.** Hermann Cohen's 1919 *Religion of Reason* makes ethical monotheism a Kantian regulative ideal: God as the guarantor of the moral law's intelligibility, the *correlation* of God and human as ethical agents. Reform Judaism in Germany and America institutionalizes this as the self-understanding of liberal Judaism.
- **Personalist deepening.** Buber's *I and Thou* (1923), Levinas's *Totality and Infinity* (1961) — the I-Thou relation, the face of the Other, become the locus of ethical monotheism rather than abstract divine command.

## Disputes
- **Historical accuracy.** Was *ancient* Israel really a monotheistic ethical-rationalist outlier? Albright and Wright said yes; later scholars (Smith, *The Early History of God*; Day, *Yahweh and the Gods and Goddesses of Canaan*) document Israel's deep henotheistic and polytheistic strata; "ethical monotheism" as ancient-Israelite self-description is partly retroactive. The category may fit *post-exilic* prophetic-Deuteronomic Judaism better than older Israelite religion.
- **Self-flattering apologetics.** Critics (Levenson 1988, Halbertal & Margalit) note that "ethical monotheism" was deployed in 19th-c. Jewish apologetics to defend Judaism's continuing relevance against Christian-supersessionist and German-historicist attacks; the rhetorical-political function partly determined the category.
- **Comparative slippage.** Whether ancient Egyptian *maʿat*, Zoroastrian *aša*, and Confucian *ren* are "ethical monotheism" without the monotheism, or different things, is debated. The category is uneasy at the edges.
