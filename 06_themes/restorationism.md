# Restorationism

The thesis that the original / primitive / apostolic Church *fell into apostasy* at some point in history, and that the true Church must be *restored* (not merely *reformed*) through new revelation, recovered scripture, or recovered authority. This distinguishes restorationism from the magisterial Reformation, which claimed to *purify* a continuous Church rather than *replace* it.

Restorationism is one of the most coherent ways for a 19th-century New Religious Movement to claim authority while bypassing two millennia of established Christian institutions: *the institutions themselves are the problem*.

Variants:
- **Mormonism** (Joseph Smith, 1820–1830): the Great Apostasy began after the death of the apostles; restored through Smith's first vision and the Book of Mormon. The most fully developed restorationism.
- **Jehovah's Witnesses** (Russell, 1879→): the true Christian Church was apostatized; the Watchtower Society is the restored channel.
- **Stone-Campbell movement** (early 19th-c. American frontier): "no creed but Christ, no book but the Bible" — restoration through scripture alone.
- **Radical Reformation** (Anabaptists, 16th c.): primitive-Church restorationism *before* it became distinctively 19th-century.

A restorationist claim is *a Phase 7 thesis about Phase 3–4*: it asserts that the modern movement recovers something the historical Church lost. As a graph edge it should be typed `polemic-against` (institutional Christianity) and `restoration-claim` (the named earlier period).
