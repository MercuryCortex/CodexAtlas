# Crucifixion Theology

The theological interpretation of Jesus' execution as **soteriologically meaningful** — not merely a Roman judicial act but the central salvific event in the Christian scheme. Distinct from the [[passion-narrative]] (the story) and from [[atonement-reinterpreted]] (Gnostic and other re-readings). Paul's "we preach Christ crucified" (1 Cor 1:23) makes it the proclaimable scandal; the Synoptic passion predictions sacralize the death in advance. Major theological models (Aulén's classic typology):

- **Christus Victor** — the death-and-resurrection as triumph over death, sin, and the devil (early Greek Fathers; recovered by Aulén 1931).
- **Satisfaction** — Anselm's *Cur Deus Homo* (1098): the death satisfies God's offended honor.
- **Penal substitution** — Reformation development (Calvin): Christ bears the divine penalty in place of sinners. See [[justification-by-faith]] and [[predestination-protestant]].
- **Moral influence** — Abelard's account, retrieved by liberal Protestantism.
- **Theology of the cross (theologia crucis)** — Luther's *Heidelberg Disputation* (1518): God is known *sub contrario* in the suffering and shame of the crucified one; opposed to *theologia gloriae*.

- [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Pauline cruciform theology: Rom 3:25, 1 Cor 1–2, Gal 3:13.
- [[phase-6-005-luther-95-theses]], [[phase-6-007-luther-bondage-of-will]] — Luther's *theologia crucis*.
- Gnostic counter-readings — some Gnostic texts (e.g., Second Treatise of the Great Seth, Apocalypse of Peter, NHC) deny Christ truly suffered; the death is illusion or substitutionary. The orthodox response (Irenaeus, Tertullian) insists on real flesh, real death. See [[atonement-reinterpreted]].

Moltmann's *The Crucified God* (1972) re-centered crucifixion theology in the post-Auschwitz 20th century: God Godself suffers and dies in the Son.
