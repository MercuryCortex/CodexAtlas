# Perennial Philosophy

The thesis that beneath the dogmatic-historical surface of the world's religions lies a single, universal, primordial mystical truth — knowable in the same form by Vedantin and Sufi and Christian mystic and Neoplatonist alike — and that the world's traditions are local exoteric clothings of one underlying esoteric reality. Coined in the Renaissance (Agostino Steuco, *De Perenni Philosophia* 1540), popularized as a 20th-century category by Aldous Huxley (1945), and built into a fully developed religio-political program by the Traditionalist school (Guénon, Schuon, Coomaraswamy). Distinct from but historically continuous with [[prisca-theologia]]: the *prisca* version posits a single *historical* origin and chain of transmission; the perennialist version posits a *trans-historical* convergence.

## Manifestations
- **Renaissance origin:** [[phase-6-001-ficino-pimander]], [[phase-6-002-ficino-theologia-platonica]] — Ficino's *prisca theologia* line is the conceptual ancestor; Steuco's *De Perenni Philosophia* (1540) gives the modern term. The pre-modern version assumes Christianity is the consummation.
- **Theosophical perennialism:** [[phase-7-005-isis-unveiled]], [[phase-7-006-secret-doctrine]] — Helena Blavatsky's *Isis Unveiled* (1877) and *Secret Doctrine* (1888) propose an "Ancient Wisdom Religion" preserved by adepts and recoverable through theosophy. The version of perennialism now stripped of explicit Christian consummation; Hindu-Buddhist material moves to centre stage.
- **Anthroposophical:** [[phase-7-009-outline-of-esoteric-science]] — Steiner's *Geheimwissenschaft* (1910) is a structurally similar synthesis with German-idealist and Christological accents.
- **Yogananda:** [[phase-7-026-autobiography-of-a-yogi]] — perennialist mode: Yogananda's mission framed as bringing Vedantic-yogic truth to convergence with esoteric Christianity.
- **Huxley's coinage:** Aldous Huxley's *The Perennial Philosophy* (1945) anthologizes "minimum-working-hypothesis" mystical convergence across the world's traditions. The text that put "perennial philosophy" into mid-20th-c. educated vocabulary.
- **Traditionalist school.** René Guénon (*Crisis of the Modern World* 1927), Frithjof Schuon (*Transcendent Unity of Religions* 1948), Ananda Coomaraswamy. The Traditionalist version is *prescriptive*: each individual should practice one orthodox tradition seriously, but every orthodox tradition leads to the same mystical apex. Often coupled with conservative, anti-modern, hierarchical-society politics.
- **Cross-cultural mysticism scholarship.** W.T. Stace, Robert Forman (see [[non-duality]]) revived a scholarly perennialism in the 1960s–90s; the constructivist response (Katz 1978) made it the focal debate in philosophy of mysticism.

## Disputes
- **Sedgwick's reconstruction.** Sedgwick 2004 documents the actual transmission lines: Guénon and his Traditionalist successors are a small, well-defined intellectual movement (with covert links to Sufi Islam and political traditionalism) — not a universal natural fact about mystical experience. Perennialism is a *modern* doctrine masquerading as eternal truth.
- **Constructivist critique.** Katz, Proudfoot, Sharf: there is no tradition-neutral "mystical experience"; what perennialists call universal core is the product of pre-existing tradition-shaped concepts. The cross-cultural similarity is partly an artifact of how perennialists selected and translated.
- **Cooptation.** The "perennial wisdom" trope is widely adopted in New Age contexts, often in forms Guénon would have abhorred — popular perennialism is a kind of relativizing eclecticism, Traditionalist perennialism is a kind of orthodoxy-enforcing absolutism.
