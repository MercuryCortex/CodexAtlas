# Four-Kingdoms Schema

The apocalyptic historiographic schema — most fully developed in Daniel 2 (the great statue of mixed metals) and Daniel 7 (the four beasts from the sea) — by which the sweep of post-exilic Near-Eastern imperial history is read as a sequence of four wicked kingdoms that will be succeeded by an enduring divine kingdom. The standard identification of the four (gold/lion = Babylon, silver/bear = Media, bronze/leopard = Persia, iron/dreadful = Greece, with Antiochus IV as the eschatological tyrant) implies a Maccabean dating of Daniel's apocalyptic chapters (c. 165 BCE). The schema is a politically-coded reading of imperial history culminating in divine intervention.

- [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — Dan 2 and Dan 7; the locus classicus.
- [[phase-3-010-sibylline-oracles]] — pagan-Jewish Sibylline material reuses the four-kingdoms schema with varied identifications.
- [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — the *Apocalypse of Weeks* (1 En 93+91) and the *Animal Apocalypse* (1 En 85–90) operate in the same imperial-historiographic genre but with different periodizations.

The schema has had an immense afterlife: Augustine's *City of God* uses Daniel's kingdoms; Joachim of Fiore reorganizes the periodization; medieval Christian historiography reads itself as the latter days of Daniel's iron-clay feet; the Reformation reactivates the schema with the Papacy as the eschatological tyrant; American premillennial dispensationalism continues to read contemporary geopolitics through Daniel. The schema is the foundational text of Western apocalyptic political-historical imagination.
