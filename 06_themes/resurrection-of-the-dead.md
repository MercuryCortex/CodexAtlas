# Resurrection of the Dead

The doctrine that at the end of the present age the dead will be raised bodily from their graves to face judgment, reward, and (in many versions) eternal life. Distinct from immortality-of-the-soul (a Greek-philosophical doctrine that the soul never dies), distinct from reincarnation (the soul reborn in successive bodies), and distinct from ghostly-shade existence (Greek Hades, Hebrew Sheol). The resurrection doctrine is *bodily*, *future*, *eschatological*, *one-time*. Originates demonstrably in Zoroastrianism, emerges in Second Temple Judaism (Daniel, the Maccabean martyrs, Pharisees), becomes definitional for Christianity (Jesus's resurrection as proleptic first instance), is reaffirmed in Islam (*qiyāmah*).

## Manifestations
- **Zoroastrian *frashōkereti*:** [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]], [[phase-2-004-yasna-younger-avesta]] — Zoroastrian eschatology: the *frashōkereti* (Making Wonderful) at end of cosmic time includes bodily resurrection (*ristāxēz*); the molten metal ordeal; final defeat of Angra Mainyu; eternal life in renewed bodies. The earliest fully developed resurrection eschatology in any tradition. The dating of the *Gāthās* is contested (Boyce: c. 1200 BCE; others later); even on conservative late dates, Zoroastrian resurrection-doctrine precedes the Jewish.
- **Hebrew Bible (rare):** Isa 26:19 ("Thy dead shall live, my body shall they arise"), Ezek 37 (dry bones) — read by post-exilic Judaism as resurrection texts but more probably national-restoration metaphors in their original setting.
- **Daniel's apocalyptic:** [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — Dan 12:2 ("many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth shall awake, some to everlasting life and some to everlasting contempt") — the clearest Hebrew-Bible attestation of individual resurrection-doctrine, in apocalyptic Maccabean context (~165 BCE).
- **Maccabean martyrs:** 2 Macc 7 (the seven martyred brothers) repeatedly affirm bodily resurrection as the eschatological vindication of their faithfulness — resurrection-doctrine emerging from martyrological-theodicy need.
- **Pharisaic / rabbinic:** [[phase-4-031-mishnah]] — *m. Sanhedrin* 10.1: "All Israel has a share in the world to come; but the one who says there is no resurrection of the dead from the Torah" is excluded. Resurrection becomes Pharisaic-rabbinic orthodoxy; Sadducees reject it (Mk 12:18, *m. Sanhedrin*).
- **Jesus and the Pauline kerygma:** [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]], [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]], [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]], [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]], [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — the central Christian claim: Jesus has been raised from the dead as the *firstfruits* (1 Cor 15:20) of the general resurrection; Christian baptism is incorporation into his resurrection; Christian life is lived between his resurrection (as proleptic) and the universal resurrection (as parousia). 1 Cor 15 is the locus classicus of the Pauline-Christian resurrection-body theology.
- **Quranic *qiyāmah*:** [[phase-4-034-quran]] — *Yawm al-qiyāmah* (Day of Resurrection) is the central eschatological scene; bodies will be reconstituted (Sūra 75 is named *al-Qiyāmah*); judgment; paradise / hellfire. Continuous with the Christian-Jewish strand into early Islamic theology.

## Disputes
- **Iranian provenance.** Boyce 1987 makes the strongest case for Zoroastrian influence on Second Temple Jewish resurrection-doctrine: the chronology fits (Persian period diffusion), and the *content* matches (bodily, end-time, judgment, two outcomes). Levenson 2006 counters that Hebrew-Bible internal materials (Ezek 37, Isa 26) provide adequate native sources without Iranian borrowing — the *flowering* of Jewish resurrection-doctrine in the post-exilic period is the *Hebrew Bible* coming into its own. The truth is probably both, with Iranian materials catalyzing the development of latent Hebrew motifs.
- **Resurrection-of-the-body vs. immortality-of-the-soul.** Wright 2003 stresses the centrality of *bodily* resurrection against Hellenistic-philosophical readings that emphasized soul-survival. Cullmann's famous lecture "Immortality of the Soul or Resurrection of the Dead?" (1955) argued NT theology is decisively resurrection-of-body, against widespread Christian-pietist confusion.
- **Modern Christian theology.** Bultmann's existentialist demythologization, Pannenberg's historical defense, Rahner's anthropological reformulation, NT Wright's massive historical apologetics — resurrection-of-the-body remains the most theologically contested category in modern Christian dogmatics, with stakes about historical Jesus, eschatology, and Christian-Jewish relations.
