# Frashokereti (Cosmic Renovation)

**Frashokereti** (Avestan *frašō-kərəti*, "making wonderful / renovating-into-perfection"; Pahlavi *frašegird*) is the **Zoroastrian doctrine of cosmic renovation at the end of time** — the final, universal, irreversible reconstruction of the cosmos that abolishes evil and inaugurates eternal righteousness. For the Codex Atlas mission this is one of the most-load-bearing single theological-doctrinal items in cross-tradition transmission: **Frashokereti provides the principal pre-Christian Near Eastern model for cosmic-resurrection-and-renovation eschatology**, and its structural transmission into Second-Temple Jewish and from there into Christian and Islamic eschatology is documented by Boyce 1975-91, Hultgård 1998, Cereti 1995.

## The five-element eschatological scenario

The Pahlavi-period elaboration of Frashokereti (built on Avestan foundations) specifies:

1. **The three Saoshyants** appear in 1000-year succession: Ushedar (the World-Increaser), Ushedar-mah (the Increaser-of-Righteousness), and the final **Astvat-ereta** ("he who embodies righteousness") = the climactic [[saoshyant]]. Each is born from a virgin impregnated by Zarathustra's preserved seed in **Lake Hamun** (Lake Kansaoya, in the Sistan region of eastern Iran) — a precise geographic location that grounds the messianic expectation.

2. **Universal resurrection of the dead**. Bodies are reconstituted from their elements; souls reunited with bodies. **No human being is destroyed; all are raised**. (This is a critical theological feature — Zoroastrian resurrection is universal, not selective.)

3. **The river of molten metal**. All resurrected persons pass through a river of molten metal:
   - To the righteous, it feels like warm milk.
   - To the wicked, it burns away the evil but does not destroy them — the wicked are **purified through the burning** and emerge cleansed.

4. **Final cosmic combat**. The Saoshyant defeats [[angra-mainyu-ahriman]]. Evil is decisively, irreversibly destroyed.

5. **The renovation itself**. The cosmos is made *frasha* — "wonderful, perfect." All creation enters eternal blessedness with Ahura Mazda. The earth becomes flat (mountains-and-valleys leveled into uniform paradise). All living beings dwell in perfect harmony.

## The MASSIVE-WIN cross-tradition edges

### Frashokereti → Christian Resurrection and New Creation

The structural parallels with Christian eschatology are **dense and uncontested**:

1. **Universal bodily resurrection** — Frashokereti's universal bodily resurrection parallels the Christian doctrine of bodily resurrection of all the dead (John 5:28-29; Revelation 20:11-15). Both traditions affirm **bodily-rather-than-only-spiritual** resurrection in distinction from Greek-philosophical immortality-of-the-soul-only.

2. **Purification through fire/molten-metal** — the Zoroastrian molten-metal river parallels Christian purgatorial doctrine and the apocalyptic "river of fire" issuing from God's throne (Daniel 7:10; cf. 1 Corinthians 3:13-15 "*the fire will test what sort of work each has done*").

3. **Final cosmic combat against the cosmic adversary** — Saoshyant defeats Angra Mainyu; Christ defeats Satan/Antichrist (Revelation 19-20). The cosmic-combat-then-renovation structure is shared.

4. **Cosmic renovation / new heavens and earth** — Frashokereti's renovated cosmos parallels Revelation 21:1: "*καινὸν οὐρανὸν καὶ γῆν καινήν*" — "**new heavens and a new earth**." 2 Peter 3:13 makes the same claim explicitly. **The Christian doctrine of the new creation is structurally Frashokereti.**

5. **Universal salvation (or at least universal-purification)** — Zoroastrian Frashokereti is **universalist** (all are raised, all pass through purifying fire, all enter the renovated cosmos). This is structurally close to Origen's *apokatastasis* (the doctrine of universal eventual salvation) — Origen explicitly identified Zoroaster as a source for this doctrine (Origen *Contra Celsum* 1.16; though Origen is hostile, the identification matters). More directly: **Gregory of Nyssa** (335–395 CE) develops the doctrine of *apokatastasis* in *On the Soul and Resurrection* and *The Great Catechism* — a purification-by-fire universalism structurally identical to Frashokereti's molten-metal purification. Gregory was the most philosophically sophisticated of the Cappadocian Fathers and his universalism may represent conscious or unconscious absorption of Zoroastrian eschatological logic via the Persian-Cappadocian cultural interface of late antiquity.

### Frashokereti → Islamic Yawm al-Qiyāmah

Islamic eschatology (the Day of Resurrection, the Sirat Bridge, the universal judgment) draws on **both** Christian and Zoroastrian eschatological vocabulary, mediated through the religious-cultural environment of late-antique-Near-Eastern Arabia. The **Sirat Bridge** (the bridge across hell that all souls must cross) is structurally parallel to the **[[cinvat-bridge|Cinvat Bridge]]** of Zoroastrian eschatology.

### Frashokereti → Second-Temple Jewish apocalyptic

The major Second-Temple Jewish apocalyptic texts (Daniel 12:1-3 — bodily resurrection; 1 Enoch — universal resurrection-and-judgment; the Qumran texts — cosmic war between Light and Darkness; the Sibylline Oracles) **emerge in the Persian-and-post-Persian period and show clear structural resonance with Zoroastrian eschatology**. Hultgård 1998 is the principal recent treatment; Boyce 1991 vol. 3 documents extensively. The **resurrection-of-the-dead doctrine in Daniel 12** appears in the late Persian / early Hellenistic period — the precise moment of maximum Zoroastrian-Jewish religious-cultural contact.

## Disputes

- **Direction and degree of Zoroastrian-Jewish-Christian transmission.** The scholarly debate (Boyce 1991 substantial; Yamauchi 1990 minimal) is real and continues. **Most current scholarship accepts substantial Persian influence on Second-Temple Jewish apocalyptic-eschatological development** while remaining cautious about specific genealogical claims.
- **Dating of Pahlavi-period elaboration.** The most detailed Frashokereti texts are Pahlavi-period (~+500 to +900) and could be argued to be POST-Christian elaborations of an originally simpler Avestan doctrine, possibly partly influenced by intervening Christian-and-Islamic eschatologies. The **Avestan stratum is genuinely pre-Christian**; the question is how elaborated the doctrine was in pre-Christian Iran. Boyce argues substantially elaborated; minority view sees more Pahlavi-period elaboration.
- **Universal-salvation aspect.** Whether Zoroastrian eschatology is **strictly universalist** (all are saved, the wicked purified) or includes **eternal damnation** for the worst is contested. The dominant view: the wicked are purified, not eternally damned — making Zoroastrian eschatology more universalist than mainstream Christian eschatology.
