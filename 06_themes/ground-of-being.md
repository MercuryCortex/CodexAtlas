# Ground of Being (Tillich)

## Definition
Paul Tillich's reformulation of "God" as the *ground* or *depth* of being itself — *Being-itself* (*das Sein-Selbst*) — rather than as a being among beings, even the highest. To call God "a being" (however supreme) is, for Tillich, idolatrous: it places God in a generic category alongside finite beings. Instead, God *is being-itself*, the *ground* (*Grund*) and *abyss* (*Ab-grund*) from which finite beings derive their being. Diagnostic markers: (a) the explicit refusal of the *highest-being* model; (b) the language of *ground / depth / abyss* rather than *creator / lord / king*; (c) the pairing with a *method of correlation* in which theological symbols answer the existential questions arising from finite human existence; (d) the consequent commitment that the "God of theism" must be transcended — Tillich's controversial phrase "God beyond god."

## Earliest attestations
- **Tillich, *Systematic Theology* Vol. I** (1951), Part II ("Being and God"): the canonical statement. "God is being-itself, not a being." Part of the trilogy *Systematic Theology* I (1951), II (1957), III (1963).
- **Tillich, *The Courage to Be*** (1952): the more accessible popular articulation; the famous phrase "the God who appears when God has disappeared in the anxiety of doubt" — the *God beyond god*.
- **Earlier formulations:** Tillich's German-period works (Berlin, Marburg, Frankfurt 1920s–1933) develop the philosophical-theological metaphysics that the *Systematic Theology* organizes. *The Religious Situation* (1926), *The Interpretation of History* (1936).

## Variants
- **Tillich's own development:** the doctrine matures through the three volumes of *Systematic Theology*; vol. III adds the *Spiritual Presence* / Trinitarian articulation.
- **Robinson's *Honest to God*** (1963): popular Anglican appropriation. Bishop John A.T. Robinson translated Tillich's *Ground of Being*, Bonhoeffer's *religionless Christianity*, and Bultmann's *demythologization* into accessible parish-level theology; bestseller and major cultural event in 1960s British religion.
- **"Death of God" movements** (Altizer, Hamilton, Van Buren, 1961–1966): Tillichian and post-Tillichian theologians who radicalize the *God beyond god* into the death of the personal-theistic God. Tillich himself rejected this as a misreading.
- **Process theology** (Whitehead via Hartshorne, Cobb, Suchocki): an alternative non-classical theism with which Tillich shares the *not-a-being-among-beings* commitment but differs in its account of God's dipolar nature and creaturely process.
- **Apophatic-Eckhartian retrieval:** Tillich's *Grund* and *Abgrund* draw from Eckhart's *Gottheit* (see [[the-godhead-eckhart]]) and Boehme's *Ungrund* (see [[ungrund]]); the doctrine has clear genealogical roots in German mystical theology.

## Connections claimed
- **Schelling's late philosophy.** Tillich wrote his dissertation on Schelling (1910); the *Grund* / *Ab-grund* / divine-self-othering schema is Schellingian, and Schelling in turn inherits from Boehme (see [[ungrund]]). The genealogical line Boehme → Schelling → Tillich is direct.
- **Heidegger's *Sein vs. Seiendes*.** Tillich's distinction between *being-itself* and *beings* is structurally parallel to Heidegger's *Sein/Seiendes* distinction; the two were colleagues at Marburg (early 1920s) and the philosophical resonance is direct, though Tillich's theological appropriation is independent.
- **Liberal theology lineage.** Tillich stands in the line from Schleiermacher (theology starts from the structure of human experience), Ritschl, and the liberal Protestant tradition; "the ground of being" is a 20th-century answer to the same modern theological problem.
- **Comparative religion.** Tillich's late life included sustained engagement with non-Christian religions (his Bampton Lectures 1961 *Christianity and the Encounter of the World Religions*). The *ground of being* formulation is intentionally translatable across traditions (Buddhism's *śūnyatā*, Vedantic *Brahman*, Daoist *Dao*).

## Disputes
- **Whether Tillich's God is theologically adequate.** Charges from conservative Protestant theology (e.g., Berkouwer 1956): Tillich dissolves the personal God of biblical revelation into a metaphysical abstraction. From Catholic theology (Balthasar, Hans Urs von): Tillich's correlation-method makes theology too dependent on prior philosophical-existential analysis. Liberal Protestant defense (Re Manning 2009, Pauck 1976): Tillich preserves what is essential while clearing what is idolatrous in *theism*.
- **The "death of God" question.** Whether Tillich's *God beyond god* leads logically to Altizer-style "death of God" theology, or constitutes its decisive refusal. Tillich's own 1963 interactions with the death-of-God theologians are guarded.
- **Method of correlation.** Whether theology's answers must be limited to existentially-felt questions (Tillich), or whether theology speaks from revelation independently of the prior question-structure (Barth's critique). The Barth–Tillich relationship was one of the major mid-20th-c. theological tensions.
- **Was Tillich a Christian theologian or a religious-philosophical universalist?** This question shadowed Tillich's reception in conservative US Lutheran circles; his late comparative-religious turn intensified it.
