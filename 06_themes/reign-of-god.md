# Reign of God / Kingdom of God

The central category of Jesus' proclamation in the Synoptic tradition: *the kingdom of God* (Greek *basileia tou theou*, Aramaic *malkuthah di-elaha*) — God's sovereign rule, breaking decisively into the present age. The Markan summary "the time is fulfilled, and the kingdom of God has come near; repent and believe the good news" (Mk 1:15) condenses the proclamation. Critical issues: (1) is the kingdom *future-imminent* (Schweitzer, Sanders) or *realized* (Dodd) or *already-and-not-yet* (Cullmann, Ladd, Jeremias)?; (2) what is its political content — does the kingdom proclamation threaten Roman rule and Sadducean temple administration (Crossan, Horsley); or is it primarily a religious-eschatological category (Sanders)?; (3) what is the relation of kingdom to *king*, i.e., the implicit Christology?

- [[phase-3-014-q-source]] — Q's kingdom-sayings: "the kingdom of God has come near" (Lk 10:9), "the kingdom of God is in your midst" (Lk 17:21 — see [[kingdom-within]]).
- [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — Mk 1:15 summary; the parables of Mk 4; the "Son of Man" kingdom-coming sayings (Mk 8:38, 13:26).
- [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] — Matthew prefers "kingdom of heaven" (~ Aramaic *malkuth shamayim*).
- [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — the Hebrew-Bible background: Dan 2:44, 7:13–14 (the kingdom that shall stand forever; the Son of Man kingdom).
