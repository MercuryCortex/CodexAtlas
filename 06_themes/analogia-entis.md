# Analogia entis (analogy of being)

## Definition
The doctrine that the term *being* (*ens*, *esse*) — and the related transcendental terms (good, true, one) — is predicated of God and of creatures *analogically*: neither *univocally* (in the same sense, as Scotus held) nor *equivocally* (in entirely different senses, leaving no real communication of meaning) but in an ordered relation where finite being *participates in* and *images* infinite being. Aquinas develops the doctrine principally in *Summa Theologiae* Ia q.13 (on the divine names). Diagnostic marker: the rejection of *both* univocity *and* pure equivocation in theological predication, in favor of a middle path that allows meaningful God-talk while preserving divine transcendence.

## Earliest attestations
- **Aristotle:** *Metaphysics* IV.2, V.7, VI.2 — *being is said in many ways* (*to on legetai pollachōs*); the πρὸς ἕν (focal-meaning) account is the philosophical ancestor of medieval analogy.
- **Pseudo-Dionysius:** *Divine Names* — affirmative and negative theology in tension; cataphatic predication on the via affirmativa.
- **Avicenna:** *Šifāʾ* — *being* is the most universal concept; the distinction of essence and existence in creatures, identity in God. This is decisive background.
- **Aquinas:** *Summa Theologiae* Ia q.13 a.5 — the canonical articulation. The analogy is *of attribution* (creatures derive their being from God) and *of proportionality* (the proportion God:divine-being :: creature:creaturely-being).
- **Cajetan:** *De Nominum Analogia* (1498) — the standard early-modern Thomist systematization of analogy into three types (inequality, attribution, proportionality), influential for centuries; revisionist Thomism (de Lubac, McInerny 1996) has challenged Cajetan's interpretation.

## Variants
- **Aquinas's own view:** primarily *analogia attributionis* (creatures said to be insofar as they derive being from God) supplemented by considerations of proportionality.
- **Cajetan's reconstruction:** systematic three-fold analogy of inequality, attribution, proportionality.
- **Scotist alternative:** *univocity of being* — *ens* is one concept predicated univocally of God and creatures (in respect of the bare formal concept of being), while the modes of being differ infinitely. Scotus argues univocity is the *precondition* of any meaningful theological discourse; Aquinas thinks univocity collapses God into a member of a genus.
- **Suarezian:** an even more strongly univocal-leaning metaphysics; the concept of being as the proper object of metaphysics.
- **Przywara's 20th-c. revival** (1932, expanded 1962): *analogia entis* as the *form of Catholic metaphysics* — the "ever greater dissimilarity" within every similarity between God and creature, the rhythm of cataphatic-apophatic.

## Connections claimed
- Aquinas synthesizes Aristotelian focal-meaning, Avicennian essence-existence distinction, and Dionysian divine-names theology into a coherent doctrine.
- The Scotus–Aquinas debate sets the terms of late-medieval and early-modern metaphysics.
- The modern Barth–Przywara controversy (1932–1934) is the most consequential 20th-century theological dispute over the doctrine.

## Disputes
- **Aquinas vs. Scotus.** The medieval analogy-vs.-univocity debate continues to structure contemporary metaphysics of theological language. Radical Orthodoxy (Milbank, Pickstock) blames Scotist univocity for inaugurating modern secularism (the "Scotus story"); historians of medieval philosophy (R. Cross 2014, Williams 2013) contest the narrative.
- **Karl Barth's famous rejection.** Barth, *Church Dogmatics* I/1 (1932): "I regard the *analogia entis* as the invention of antichrist and think that because of it one cannot become a Catholic." Barth's worry was that *analogia entis* presupposes a continuous being-relation between creator and creature that compromises divine sovereignty and the radical otherness of revelation. Przywara's responses and Balthasar's *Karl Barth* (1951) reframe the doctrine to address Barth's concerns; the controversy remains foundational for Catholic-Protestant ecumenical theology.
- **The role of analogy in the *Summa*.** Some recent Aquinas-scholarship (Burrell 1973, McInerny 1996) argues Aquinas's "doctrine of analogy" is more linguistic-grammatical than metaphysical — about how to *speak* of God, not a substantive metaphysical relation. Others (te Velde, Wippel) defend a metaphysically robust reading.
- **Pure-nature controversies.** *Analogia entis* presupposes a stable nature-grace relationship that the *nouvelle théologie* (de Lubac) destabilized; the post-conciliar controversies over nature and grace bear on the doctrine.
