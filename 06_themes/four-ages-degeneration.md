# Four Ages of Degeneration

A historical-cosmological schema dividing time into a fixed sequence of progressively-worsening ages, each represented by a metal of decreasing value (gold → silver → bronze → iron) or by a sequence of empires-with-bestial-form, culminating in a final corrupt age that ends in cosmic dissolution and (in some versions) renewal. Attested across Greek, Iranian, Hebrew, and Vedic traditions in remarkably similar form, generating one of the most vigorous comparative-mythology debates of the past century.

## Manifestations

- **Hesiod's *Works and Days*.** [[phase-2-009-hesiod-theogony-works-and-days]] (~700 BCE) lines 109–201 — five ages: gold, silver, bronze, the heroic age (interpolation), iron. Hesiod's iron age is the present; he wishes he had died before or after it. The metallic schema as *moral* degeneration (each age worse than the last).
- **Vedic / Puranic yugas.** [[phase-2-017-mahabharata-ramayana-oral-layers]] and the Puranas — Krita (= Satya = gold), Treta (silver), Dvapara (bronze), Kali (iron). Lengths: 1,728,000 → 1,296,000 → 864,000 → 432,000 years, decreasing in 4:3:2:1 ratio. The Kali Yuga is the present; ends in Kalki-avatar restoration. Almost certainly post-Vedic in its systematic form (Mahabharata era onward).
- **Iranian *Bahman Yasht*.** A Pahlavi apocalyptic text presenting Zarathustra's vision of a tree with four branches (gold, silver, steel, iron-mixed-with-clay), each branch a future age culminating in evil. Dating disputed (3rd–9th c. CE Pahlavi but presupposing earlier Avestan tradition).
- **Daniel 2's statue.** [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — Nebuchadnezzar's dream of the statue with head of gold, breast of silver, belly of bronze, legs of iron, feet of iron-mixed-with-clay; smashed by a stone "not cut by human hands." Identification of the four kingdoms (Babylon-Media-Persia-Greece OR Babylon-Persia-Greece-Rome) is the central interpretive debate. The smashing-stone is the eschatological in-breaking of God's kingdom.
- **Daniel 7's beasts.** [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] — four beasts from the sea (lion, bear, leopard, dreadful-iron-toothed); same four-empire schema in animal form.
- **Roman Ovid.** *Metamorphoses* 1.89–150 — Ovid's gold-silver-bronze-iron ages, derived from Hesiod, become the channel by which the schema enters European literature.

## Disputes

- **Independent origin vs. cross-cultural diffusion.** West (1997) argued substantial Mesopotamian / Iranian influence on Hesiod's schema; the structural near-identity with Daniel 2's metallic statue, the Iranian *Bahman Yasht*'s four-branch tree, and the Vedic yuga-sequence is hard to attribute to coincidence. Skeptics (Vian, earlier) argued the schema is generic enough to arise independently. Most current scholarship accepts ANE-to-Greek transmission (West's case is strong).
- **The yuga lengths' origin.** The Indian 4:3:2:1 mathematical structure and the millions-of-years framework appears to be post-Vedic systematization, possibly with Hellenistic-mathematical influence (cf. Pingree on Greek astronomy in Indian sources).
- **The "heroic age" interpolation in Hesiod.** Hesiod's insertion of the heroic age between bronze and iron breaks the monotonic-degeneration pattern, suggesting the schema reached him in approximately-four-stage form and was modified to accommodate Greek heroic-epic tradition.
