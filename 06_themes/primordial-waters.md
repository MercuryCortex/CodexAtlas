# Primordial Waters

A pre-cosmic state of undifferentiated water from which the gods, the cosmos, or both emerge. Found in Mesopotamian (Apsu, Tiamat), Egyptian (Nun), and Hebrew (tehom, Gen 1:2) traditions, with strong scholarly arguments for shared Near Eastern background.

## Attestations
- **Babylonian:** Apsu (sweet waters) + Tiamat (salt sea) in [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]].
- **Egyptian:** Nun, the primeval waters from which the first mound rose; foundational to Heliopolitan and Hermopolitan cosmogonies. See [[phase-1-002-pyramid-texts]].
- **Hebrew:** `tehom` (the deep) over which the spirit of God hovers, Gen 1:2 — etymologically cognate with Tiamat (Akkadian *tiāmat*).
- **Vedic:** "the waters" preceding cosmogony in Rigveda 10.129 (the Nasadiya hymn).

## Significance
The `tehom`/Tiamat etymological link is a much-discussed (though linguistically debated) anchor for the Genesis-Enuma Elish parallel. Heidel and many subsequent scholars find the cosmogonic structure (chaotic waters → divine ordering) too parallel to be independent.
