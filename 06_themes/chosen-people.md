# Chosen People

A deity (or supreme deity) elects a specific people / lineage / community as the privileged locus of relationship with the divine, with reciprocal covenant obligations. Distinct from a tutelary national god (each nation has its god — common in the ancient Near East) by claiming that the *one* supreme God has made *one* particular human community the chosen instrument of cosmic purpose.

## Manifestations
- **Israelite covenant:** [[phase-2-005-hebrew-bible-j-source]] — Yahweh's call of Abraham (Gen 12: "in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed"); covenant ratified at Sinai. [[phase-2-011-hebrew-bible-d-source]] — Deuteronomy 7:6–8: "the LORD your God has chosen you to be a people for his treasured possession ... it was because the LORD loved you." See also [[phase-2-007-hebrew-bible-e-source]].
- **Prophetic re-reading:** [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]] — Israel as servant, light to the nations, witness; chosenness reframed as vocation rather than privilege under the impact of exile.
- **Second Temple intensifications:** [[phase-3-009-jubilees]] — chosenness extended back to creation; [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — the community is the *true* Israel within historical Israel (sectarian narrowing).
- **Pauline transformation:** [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Romans 9–11 wrestles with the relationship between Israel's election and the inclusion of Gentiles; the church does not *replace* Israel but is grafted in. Sanders (1983, 1985) demonstrates how integral this is to Pauline theology.
- **Christian supersessionism:** Patristic development read the church as the *new* Israel that *displaces* historical Israel. Has been the dominant Christian reading until 20th-c. reform (Vatican II *Nostra Aetate* 1965; mainline Protestant declarations).
- **Quranic continuation:** [[phase-4-034-quran]] — Children of Israel are chosen (e.g., Surah 2:47, 7:140) but have repeatedly broken covenant; the Muslim *ummah* is then identified as the new community of submission.

## Tension within the motif
- **Privilege vs. vocation.** The Hebrew Bible itself contains both readings — Israel chosen *for* the nations (Gen 12, Isa 49) vs. Israel chosen *over* the nations (Deut 7, post-exilic exclusivism). The same tension recurs in every successor tradition.
- **Particularism vs. universalism.** How can a universal creator have particular favorites? Hebrew Bible response: the particular is the instrument of the universal. The tension is never fully resolved; it is generative.

## Disputes
- **Pre-exilic vs. post-exilic.** When did chosenness language become central? Pre-exilic Israelite religion (Asherah cult, henotheism, see [[henotheism-monolatry]]) seems less universalist. The fully developed election theology is largely post-exilic.
- **Post-Holocaust theology.** How Christian theology can confess Christ without supersessionism is a live 20th–21st-c. question (van Buren, Soulen, Boys).
