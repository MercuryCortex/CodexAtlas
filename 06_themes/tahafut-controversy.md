# The Tahafut Controversy (kalām vs. falsafa on the compatibility of revealed religion and Greek philosophy)

## Definition
The **Tahafut controversy** is the dialectical bookend exchange between **[[al-ghazali]]'s [[phase-5-045-al-ghazali-tahafut-al-falasifa|*Tahāfut al-Falāsifa*]] (*The Incoherence of the Philosophers*, ~1094-95)** and **[[ibn-rushd|Averroes]]'s [[phase-5-046-ibn-rushd-tahafut-al-tahafut|*Tahāfut al-Tahāfut*]] (*The Incoherence of the Incoherence*, ~1180)** — the keystone medieval Islamic debate on whether the **falsafa** tradition's Greek-Aristotelian-Neoplatonic metaphysics is **compatible with revealed religion** (Quranic Islam) or **incoherent with it**. Ghazālī, writing from the Sunni-Ashʿarī kalām position at the Niẓāmiyya madrasa in Baghdad, identifies 20 falsafa theses (mostly drawn from [[ibn-sina|Avicenna]]'s [[phase-5-044-ibn-sina-kitab-al-shifa|*Shifāʾ*]] and [[al-farabi|al-Farabi]]) that he argues are incoherent in their own terms or incompatible with revealed Islam — three of them ranking as outright unbelief (*kufr*):
1. **Eternity of the world** (the falsafa Aristotelian-Avicennian view that the world has no temporal beginning vs. the Quranic creation-doctrine);
2. **Denial that God knows particulars** (Avicenna's "God knows particulars in a universal mode" vs. the Quranic providence of particulars);
3. **Denial of bodily resurrection** (the falsafa rational-soul-survives-body view vs. Quranic bodily-resurrection eschatology).

Averroes, writing 85 years later from the Almohad Maghribi falsafa position, replies to Ghazālī's *Tahāfut* paragraph by paragraph in the *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut*, **defending Avicenna and the falsafa tradition** while sometimes partially conceding that the Avicennian Neoplatonic emanationism departs from strict Aristotelianism. Averroes's companion treatise — the ***Faṣl al-Maqāl*** (*Decisive Treatise on the Harmony of Religion and Philosophy*) — provides the principled juridical-philosophical framework: as a Mālikī *qāḍī*, Averroes argues that demonstrative philosophical inquiry is a religious obligation, and that revealed religion and demonstrative philosophy cannot truly contradict because both are different routes to one truth.

The controversy is the **single most consequential medieval Islamic philosophical-theological debate**, and shapes the subsequent reception-history of falsafa in both the Sunni Islamic world (where Ghazālī's verdict substantially affects the institutional position of falsafa) and the Latin Christian West (where the Latin translations of both *Tahāfut* works — paired in the 1527 Calonymos Latin edition as *Destructio Philosophorum* and *Destructio Destructionis* — structure the Renaissance Aristotelian reception).

## Earliest attestations
- **Ghazālī's *Tahāfut al-Falāsifa* (1094-95)** — the opening bookend. Composed at the Baghdad Niẓāmiyya.
- **The companion *Maqāṣid al-Falāsifa*** (shortly before, ~1093) — Ghazālī's sympathetic-expository summary of falsafa positions. Translated into Latin first and Hebrew, leading to Aquinas's famous mistake of citing Ghazālī as a *falāsifa* author alongside Avicenna and Averroes.
- **Averroes's *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* (~1180)** — the closing bookend. Composed in Andalusi-Almohad Córdoba.
- **The companion *Faṣl al-Maqāl* (~1178)** — Averroes's juridical-philosophical framework for the *Tahāfut al-Tahāfut* defense.
- **Latin reception:** the **Calo Calonymos 1527 Latin edition** at Venice paired Ghazālī's *Destructio* with Averroes's *Destructio Destructionis* in the Giunta Averroes-omnia — the Renaissance European reference work.

## Variants
- **Ghazālī's three *kufr*-charges + 17 heretical charges.** The classical structure of the Ghazālian indictment of falsafa.
- **Averroes's strict-Aristotelian defense.** Averroes defends falsafa against Ghazālī while partially correcting Avicennian Neoplatonic emanationism toward stricter Aristotelian first-mover metaphysics.
- **The 'double-truth' attribution to Latin Averroism.** Latin readers (Aquinas, Tempier) charged Latin Averroists with holding the 'double-truth' doctrine — that philosophical and theological propositions can both be true while contradicting each other. **Averroes's own *Faṣl al-Maqāl* rejects this**: philosophy and revealed religion are different routes to one truth, not contradictory truths. The 'double-truth' charge appears to be the Latin-Averroist extension or polemical caricature.
- **Latin reception via Tempier's Condemnation of 1277.** Bishop Étienne Tempier's 219 Propositions of 1277 (Paris) — the largest medieval condemnation of philosophical theses — includes many propositions tracking Ghazālī's *Tahāfut* charges against Avicenna (eternity of the world, denial of God's knowledge of particulars). The Tahafut controversy is structurally re-fought a century later in the Latin Christian institutional context.

## Connections claimed
- **Ghazālī → Averroes → Aquinas.** The Tahafut controversy structures Aquinas's engagement with falsafa: Aquinas absorbs Ghazālī's anti-falsafa charges (sometimes uncritically, citing Ghazālī as if a *falāsifa* author on the strength of the *Maqāṣid*), engages Averroes's defenses, and develops the **synthesis of Aristotelian philosophy with revealed Christian theology** that is the *Summa Theologiae*'s achievement. The Latin-scholastic Christianization of the Tahafut controversy is the principal Mediterranean-philosophical event of the 13th c.
- **Ghazālian occasionalism → Hume on causation.** Ghazālī's 17th-discussion treatment of causation — that natural causation is habit (*ʿāda*) sustained by divine convention, not necessary connection — is structurally parallel to Hume's 1739 *Treatise* sceptical argument against necessary causal connection. **Whether there is direct historical transmission** (via the 1527 Calonymos Latin → 17th-c. European reception) **or only structural parallel** is contested in modern scholarship; Griffel 2009 notes the parallel without committing on direct transmission.
- **Quranic creation-doctrine ↔ Aristotelian eternity-of-the-world**. The Tahafut controversy is the principal medieval site where this incompatibility is worked through; the Quranic temporal creation of the world from nothing (*ex nihilo*, in mainstream Islamic theological framing) vs. the Aristotelian-Avicennian eternal-world doctrine. The classical Islamic-theological resolution (matched in Christian theology by Aquinas) distinguishes *temporal* creation (the world has a temporal beginning) from *atemporal* creation (the world depends ontologically on God at every moment, regardless of whether it has a temporal beginning). Avicenna held the second; Ghazālī insisted on the first.

## Disputes
- **Did Ghazālī "kill" falsafa?** Traditional Western Islamicist narrative: yes; falsafa shrinks institutionally in Sunni learning after Ghazālī. Contemporary revisionist (Griffel 2009; Eichner 2020): no; falsafa continues vigorously in the Persianate East and is substantially absorbed into post-Razian kalām. Both agree that the *institutional location* of falsafa shifted after Ghazālī.
- **Did Ghazālī personally hold falsafa positions?** Frank 1994 + Griffel 2009 argue: yes, Ghazālī's later writings (especially the highly Avicennian late *Mishkāt al-Anwār*) show that al-Ghazālī **personally absorbed substantial Avicennian theses** even while polemicizing against them publicly. The *Tahāfut* is a polemical-pedagogical exercise targeting *certain* falsafa positions in their Ghazālī-reconstructed forms, not Ghazālī's complete private epistemology.
- **Accuracy of Ghazālī's representation of Avicenna.** Avicennian defenders (then and now): Ghazālī mis-states Avicenna's positions on the three *kufr*-charges. Mainstream view: Ghazālī's representations are *interpretively* contested but textually plausible from the *Shifāʾ*.
- **Did Latin Averroists hold 'double-truth'?** Modern critical scholarship: no, the 'double-truth' charge is the Latin-Averroist polemical extension; Averroes's own *Faṣl al-Maqāl* explicitly rejects it. The charge is a Tempier-era polemical-caricature attribution.
