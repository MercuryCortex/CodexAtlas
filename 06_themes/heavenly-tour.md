# Heavenly Tour

The apocalyptic narrative type in which a privileged seer (Enoch, Levi, Abraham, Isaiah, Baruch, Paul in 2 Cor 12) is taken on a guided tour through the heavens and shown cosmographic, ethical, and eschatological secrets — the storehouses of winds, the chambers of souls awaiting judgment, the throne of God, the places of the wicked. Himmelfarb 1983 catalogued the type across Jewish, Christian, and Hellenistic apocalypses; Collins's classification of apocalyptic literature includes it as one of two major subtypes (alongside historical-eschatological apocalypses).

- [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — the Book of the Watchers (1 En 17–36) is the foundational text; Enoch's tour of the geography of the cosmos and the places of the dead.
- [[phase-3-009-jubilees]] — the Mosaic tour-style revelation via the Angel of the Presence; less spatially elaborated than Enoch but structurally similar.
- [[phase-5-013-hekhalot-literature]] — the merkavah / hekhalot-mystical descendant: the *yored merkavah* (descender to the chariot) makes a controlled tour through the seven palaces; the apocalyptic tour-narrative has become liturgical-experiential.

The pattern continues into the *Apocalypse of Paul* (4th c.), Dante's *Divine Comedy*, and modern near-death-experience literature.
