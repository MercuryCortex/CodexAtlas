# Deliberate Syncretism

**Deliberate syncretism** is the intentional, programmatic synthesis of multiple religious traditions into a new unified framework — distinguishing itself from *unconscious* syncretism (gradual absorption of foreign elements) by its explicitly stated goal of harmonization.

The vault's clearest instances are temporally distant but structurally identical:

1. **Akbar's Din-i-Ilahi** (~1582): the Mughal Emperor's synthetic "Divine Faith" drawing from Islam, Hinduism, Jainism, and Zoroastrianism; centered on the emperor as spiritual head. A court intellectual project that produced approximately 20 adherents and did not survive Akbar.

2. **Cao Dai** (1926, Vietnam): the Cao Dai synthesis assembles Buddhism, Taoism, Confucianism, Christianity, and Islam under a single divine hierarchy — featuring Victor Hugo, Sun Yat-sen, and Nguyen Binh Khiem as patron saints contacted via séance. Unlike Akbar's project, Cao Dai became a mass religion with millions of followers.

The key difference between the two: Akbar's syncretism was a top-down court project; Cao Dai's was a popular religious movement rooted in spirit-mediumship and Vietnamese nationalist sentiment. The first produced a footnote; the second produced a living tradition with 4–6 million adherents.
