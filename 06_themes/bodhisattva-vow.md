# Bodhisattva-vow

## Definition
The bodhisattva-vow (*bodhisattva-praṇidhāna* / *bodhicittotpāda*) is the foundational soteriological commitment of Mahāyāna Buddhism: the aspirant vows to attain full Buddhahood not for personal liberation from *saṃsāra* but for the liberation of all sentient beings, and to remain in *saṃsāra* — indefinitely if necessary — until this universal liberation is accomplished. The vow generates *bodhicitta* — the "awakening-mind" or "mind of enlightenment" — which is the operative motivation of the bodhisattva-path (*bodhisattva-mārga*) and the differential feature of Mahāyāna soteriology vs. earlier-Buddhist (Theravāda / pre-Mahāyāna) arahant-ideal soteriology.

The most-cited formulation is the **Four Great Vows** (Chinese: *si hong shi yuan* 四弘誓願) of East Asian Mahāyāna liturgy:

> "Sentient beings are numberless; I vow to save them all.
> Defilements are inexhaustible; I vow to extinguish them all.
> Dharma-gates are immeasurable; I vow to master them all.
> The Buddha-Way is supreme; I vow to embrace it."

The most-cited single-sūtra articulation is in the [[phase-5-002b-diamond-sutra|Diamond Sūtra]] §3 (translated [[phase-5-002b-diamond-sutra]]):

> "As many beings as there are in the universe of beings... all these I must lead to Nirvana... And yet, although innumerable beings have thus been led to Nirvana, no being at all has been led to Nirvana. And why? If in a Bodhisattva the notion of a 'being' should take place, he could not be called a 'Bodhi-being.'"

The paradox-form is essential: the bodhisattva-vow holds together the **commitment to liberate all beings** AND the **recognition that there are no beings (no inherent self-existing beings) to be liberated**. The vow is *bodhicitta* operating within [[emptiness-sunyata|śūnyatā]] understanding.

A passage "has" the bodhisattva-vow theme if it explicitly articulates the four-vows formula, the *bodhicitta* aspiration, or the all-beings liberation commitment, or if it describes a specific bodhisattva's vow (e.g., Dharmākara's 48 vows in the [[phase-4-104-sukhavativyuha-larger|Larger Sukhāvatī-vyūha]]; Avalokiteśvara's vow of universal compassion).

## Earliest attestations
- **Earliest Mahāyāna sūtras**: the [[phase-3-031-asthasahasrika-prajnaparamita|Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā]] (~-100 to +100) is the earliest extant Mahāyāna sūtra and articulates the bodhisattva-ideal as differentiator from earlier-Buddhist arahant-ideal. The bodhisattva-vow is foundational throughout the Prajñāpāramitā, Lotus, Avatamsaka, Sukhāvatī-vyūha, and other Mahāyāna sūtra corpora of the 1st-4th c. CE.
- **Pre-Mahāyāna seeds**: the Pali Jātaka tales describe the Buddha's previous-life "bodhisatta" career — implicit recognition that one *can* take the Buddhahood path rather than the arahant path. The Theravāda tradition preserves this as a recognized but rare option (the *bodhisattvayāna* within Theravāda). The Mahāyāna's distinctive move is to *universalize* the bodhisattva-path as the recommended path for all aspirants.
- **Systematic articulations**: [[asanga|Asaṅga's]] *Bodhisattva-bhūmi* (~4th c. CE) is the classical Yogācāra systematization. Śāntideva's *Bodhicaryāvatāra* (~8th c. CE) is the foundational Mahāyāna manifesto of the bodhisattva-path; the Padmakara Translation Group's 1997 *Way of the Bodhisattva* is the standard contemporary English version.
- **Liturgical formulae**: the Four Great Vows formula crystallizes in East Asian Mahāyāna liturgy during the Sui-Tang period (~6th-8th c. CE) and is associated with Tiantai school formulations; it remains the standard daily recitation in East Asian Mahāyāna monastic and lay practice.

## Variants
- **Six pāramitās** (Mahāyāna mainstream): the bodhisattva-path comprises six perfections — *dāna* (generosity), *śīla* (ethics), *kṣānti* (patience), *vīrya* (effort), *dhyāna* (meditation), *prajñā* (wisdom). Sometimes expanded to ten with the addition of *upāya* (skillful means), *praṇidhāna* (aspiration), *bala* (powers), *jñāna* (knowledge).
- **Ten bhūmis**: the bodhisattva-path is articulated as ten progressive stages (*daśa-bhūmi*), as systematized in the *Daśabhūmika Sūtra* (later incorporated into the [[phase-4-102-avatamsaka-sutra|Avataṃsaka Sūtra]]).
- **Vajrayāna integration**: the bodhisattva-vow is integrated with deity yoga, generation-and-completion stage practice, and tantric initiation. The Tibetan *lamrim* tradition ([[atisa|Atiśa's]] *Bodhipathapradīpa* → [[tsongkhapa|Tsongkhapa's]] *Lamrim Chenmo*) structures the entire Buddhist path around stages culminating in the bodhisattva-vow as the operative commitment.
- **Pure Land integration**: [[tradition-pure-land-buddhism|Pure Land Buddhism]]'s Dharmākara → Amitābha story is itself a *bodhisattva-vow* narrative — Dharmākara's 48 vows are the founding charter of Pure Land soteriology, and the eighteenth vow (rebirth in Sukhāvatī through nembutsu) is the operative Mahāyāna other-power soteriological structure.
- **Zen / Chan reception**: the bodhisattva-vow is the daily liturgical commitment of all Zen/Chan practitioners; the *Four Great Vows* are recited at the conclusion of every dharma session. Zen tradition reads the vow's paradox-form (vow to save all beings / no beings to save) as the koan-like cognitive structure of the path itself.

## Connections claimed
- **Within Buddhism**: the bodhisattva-vow is the most distinctively Mahāyāna doctrine. Its emergence (~-100 to +100, in the Aṣṭasāhasrikā milieu) is the doctrinal-soteriological hinge of the Theravāda-Mahāyāna split.
- **Cross-Buddhist comparison with the arahant-ideal**: the Mahāyāna polemic frames the arahant-ideal as "selfish" (seeking personal liberation); the Theravāda response is that arahants do work for the benefit of all (the entire Pali canon assumes the Buddha's teaching is *for* the sake of all beings) and the polemic projects post-hoc Mahāyāna categories onto earlier-Buddhist soteriology. Gombrich 2018 treats the polemic as substantially unfair to early-Buddhist soteriology.
- **Christian-Mahāyāna comparative theology**: the bodhisattva-ideal of universal-salvation-via-self-sacrifice has been repeatedly compared to the Christian *imitatio Christi* and *kenōsis*. Substantial Christian-Buddhist comparative dialogue (the journal *Buddhist-Christian Studies*; Masao Abe + John Cobb + Hans Küng) treats this as the deepest soteriological-structural resonance between the traditions.
- **Modern social-engaged Buddhism**: the bodhisattva-vow is the doctrinal warrant for "engaged Buddhism" (Thich Nhat Hanh's "interbeing" extension; B.R. Ambedkar's Navayāna; the Buddhist Peace Fellowship; engaged Buddhism in modern Sri Lanka, Burma, and Thailand).
