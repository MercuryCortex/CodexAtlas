# Primordial Darkness

The cosmogonic concept of an undifferentiated primordial darkness, void, or unmanifest state preceding the creation of the ordered cosmos. The sequence **Darkness → Light → Ordered World** is attested on every inhabited continent independently and is arguably the single most universal opening-move in human cosmogony.

## Tradition-by-tradition attestations

**Hawaiian** (*pō* — the Night / dark void): The [[phase-8-010-kumulipo]], the 18th-c. Hawaiian royal creation chant, opens in *pō* — the primal darkness from which all life emerges through successive stages of evolutionary birth. *Pō* is not simply absence of light but an active, generative state: *"At the time when the earth became hot / At the time when the heavens turned about / At the time when the sun was darkened / To cause the moon to shine / The time of the rise of the Pleiades…"* (Beckwith trans.)

**Maori** (*Te Kore → Te Pō* — the Nothing → the Night): The [[phase-8-011-maori-cosmogonic-chants]] elaborate a sequence of cosmic non-being through multiple grades — *Te Kore-tē-whiwhia* (the Void without Possession), *Te Kore-i-ai* (the Void in which there is Nothing) — followed by *Te Pō* (the Night, generative darkness) before the separation of sky and earth by their children. The philosophical sophistication of this staging approaches the Neoplatonic emanation scheme.

**Egyptian** ([[nun]]): The primordial dark waters (*Nun* / *Nu*) pre-exist all creation; the first creator-deity (Atum, Ra, Ptah depending on tradition) emerges from or is contained within *Nun*. The *Book of the Dead* describes the dead person's return to Nun as a form of pre-cosmic potentiality.

**Mesopotamian**: Before the Enuma Elish's opening battle-cosmogony, the text establishes *Apsu* and *Tiamat* as the commingled dark waters: *"When on high the heaven had not been named, firm ground below had not been called by name, naught but primordial Apsu, their begetter, and Mummu-Tiamat"* — an undifferentiated darkness before the gods were born. See [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]].

**Hebrew** (*tōhū wābōhū*): Genesis 1:2 — *"the earth was without form and void, and darkness was over the face of the deep."* The *tōhū wābōhū* and *ḥōšek* (darkness) over the *tehom* (deep = cognate with *tiamat*) is the Hebrew cosmogonic opening state. The Priestly source places light as the first creative act *into* this darkness (Gen 1:3).

**Vedic** (*tamas*): The Nasadiya Sukta (Rig Veda 10.129, see [[phase-1-031-rigveda]]) — *"Darkness was hidden by darkness in the beginning"* — is among the most philosophically dense cosmogonic openings in world literature, describing the pre-creation state as undifferentiated darkness/non-being.

**Norse** (*Ginnungagap*): The gaping void between fire (Muspelheim) and ice (Niflheim) before the cosmos; cosmogonic darkness precedes the first entities.

**Greek** ([[chaos-primordial]]): Hesiod's *Theogony* opens with *Chaos* — the gaping void or opening — from which Gaia, Tartarus, and Eros emerge. Hesiod's "chaos" is primarily a spatial void, not moral disorder. *Nyx* (Night, [[nyx-primordial]]) is among the oldest cosmic entities.

## Cross-tradition analysis

Long (1963) identifies the **darkness-to-light sequence** as the most universal cosmogonic structure, appearing in traditions with no documented contact. The generative character of primordial darkness — darkness as *womb* or *potential* rather than merely absence — is a recurrent finding: Hawaiian *pō*, Maori *Te Pō*, Egyptian *Nun*, Hebrew *tehom*, Vedic *tamas* are all generative, not merely negative.

MASSIVE-win: the cosmogonic darkness-to-light sequence appears independently in Oceanic (Polynesian), East African (Egyptian), Near Eastern (Mesopotamian/Hebrew), Indo-Iranian (Vedic), European (Greek/Norse), and Mesoamerican traditions. This is either (a) a retained common human cognitive pattern reflecting the physiology of waking/sleeping and birth, or (b) parallel cultural invention, or (c) very deep structural resonances in early agricultural societies. Long (1963) argues for (a) with cultural elaboration.
