# Divinatory Omen Reading

The Mesopotamian science (and broader ANE practice) of reading the natural world and inspected animal parts as a coded message from the gods. Encoded in the *šumma X Y* ("if X then Y") conditional structure: thousands of clay tablets list omens (anomalies in liver lobes, the appearance of stars, animal births, urban events, dreams) paired with their predicted consequences (for king, land, individual). Akkadian divination is not "superstition" but the institutional intellectual technology of ancient statecraft: kings consulted *bārû* (diviners) before military campaigns, building projects, and dynastic decisions. The literature includes hepatoscopy (liver-reading via the *Bārûtu* series), celestial omens (*Enūma Anu Enlil*, ~7000 entries), terrestrial omens (*Šumma ālu*, ~10,000 entries), birth-anomaly omens (*Šumma izbu*), dream omens, physiognomic omens.

## Manifestations

- **Hepatoscopy / extispicy.** Clay liver-models (Mari, ~1800 BCE) with inscribed *šumma*-omens — the ancient world's most elaborate divinatory system. The *Bārûtu* canonical series codifies it. Inherited by Hittites; transmitted to Etruscans (the Piacenza Bronze Liver, ~150 BCE, shows clear ANE-derived structure); inherited by Romans as *haruspicy*.
- **Celestial omens.** [[phase-1-019-enuma-anu-enlil]] — the great 70-tablet series. Reads eclipses, planetary positions, weather as messages from Anu, Enlil, Ea. The structural seed of later Greek-Hellenistic astrology (Rochberg, *The Heavenly Writing*) and through it of medieval Arabic, Indian, and European astrology.
- **Terrestrial / urban omens.** [[phase-1-020-shumma-alu]] — "If a city is set on a hill...": ordinary urban observations (where ants nest, snake behavior, social-deviance events) generate omens.
- **Birth omens.** [[phase-1-021-shumma-izbu]] — anomalous animal and human births read as portents.
- **Chinese oracle bones.** Shang-dynasty (~1300 BCE onward) heated tortoise plastrons and ox scapulae; the cracks read as divinatory response. Earliest substantial Chinese writing.
- **Greek and Roman.** Ornithomancy (bird-augury), oneiromancy (dream-interpretation), Etruscan *haruspicy*. Cicero's *De Divinatione* (44 BCE) is the most sustained ancient critique-and-defense.
- **Israelite.** Urim and Thummim (priestly lots — Exod 28:30; Num 27:21); the ephod-oracle. Officially-sanctioned divination within a tradition that polemicizes against most other divinatory forms (Deut 18:9–14).

## Significance

- Divinatory texts are among the **largest corpora** of cuneiform literature surviving — far larger than mythology — and constitute the institutional intellectual training of priest-scribes. Underread compared to *Enūma Eliš* or *Gilgamesh* in popular accounts of ANE religion.
- The *šumma X Y* conditional structure is a precursor to systematic empirical-tabular thought; some scholars (Rochberg, Brown) argue Babylonian astronomy emerges *from* the omen-collection enterprise rather than from a separate "scientific" impulse.
- The continuity from Akkadian hepatoscopy through Etruscan haruspicy to Roman state divination is one of the longest documented religious-technical transmissions in the ancient world.
