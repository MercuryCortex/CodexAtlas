# Allegorical Exegesis

Reading a sacred text as a system of *concealed meanings* — surface narrative or law as the husk for hidden philosophical, ethical, or mystical content. Diagnostic for Hellenistic-Jewish (Philo), early Christian Alexandrian (Clement, Origen), Stoic (Cornutus on Homer), and later medieval Christian fourfold exegesis. Distinguished from *typology* (Old Testament events prefigure New Testament events — historical-literal both ways) and from *peshat* (plain-sense Jewish reading); allegoresis claims the *literal sense alone* misses the point.

- [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]] — Plato's critique of allegorizing the poets in *Republic* II is the foil; the technique was already in use by Theagenes of Rhegium (6th c. BCE) on Homer.
- [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]] — the towering Hellenistic-Jewish allegorist; *Allegorical Interpretation*, *Questions and Answers on Genesis/Exodus*. The Torah becomes Platonist-Stoic philosophy in narrative form.
- [[phase-4-064-epistle-of-barnabas]] — the **earliest extended Christian allegorical-exegetical document** (~+120/+132, Alexandria), redirecting Philonic allegorical method against the Jewish community itself: the dietary laws, sabbath, circumcision, and Temple are all read as misunderstood typological prefigurations whose true (Christian) meanings the Jews missed. Philonic hermeneutic weaponized as Christian supersessionism.
- [[phase-4-025-clement-stromata]], [[phase-4-026-origen-on-first-principles]] — Origen develops the threefold sense (body/soul/spirit) of Scripture; the spiritual sense is where doctrine lives.
- Medieval Quadriga (Augustine, Cassian) — literal, allegorical, moral (tropological), anagogical.
- Critics: Antiochene exegesis (Diodore of Tarsus, Theodore of Mopsuestia, John Chrysostom) restrains allegoresis in favor of *theoria* anchored in history. The Reformation revives the literal sense ([[sola-scriptura]]).
