# As Above, So Below

The Hermetic maxim *quod est superius est sicut quod est inferius, et quod est inferius est sicut quod est superius* — "that which is above is like that which is below, and that which is below is like that which is above" — drawn from the *Emerald Tablet* (*Tabula Smaragdina*) attributed to Hermes Trismegistus, the most-cited single text of the Western Hermetic tradition. The Emerald Tablet circulated in medieval Latin from the 12th century (translated from earlier Arabic; the Arabic version is attested in *Kitāb Sirr al-Khalīqa* by Apollonius of Tyana c. 7th–8th c.). The maxim is the slogan-form of [[microcosm-macrocosm]] correspondence theology: the structures of the heavens are reflected in the structures of earth, of the human body, of the alchemical vessel.

- [[phase-4-012-asclepius]] — *Asclepius* 5: "humanity, a great miracle, is a being to be worshiped and honored"; the human's middle-position between heaven and earth.
- [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] — *Poimandres* and other Hermetic discourses develop the ascending-descending correspondences.
- [[phase-6-009-agrippa-de-occulta-philosophia]] — Agrippa's massive elaboration of correspondence chains (planet–metal–plant–stone–animal–body part) is the encyclopedic Renaissance form.
- [[phase-6-022-fludd-utriusque-cosmi-historia]] — Fludd's diagrammatic encyclopedia of correspondences.

Modern reception: The Hermetic maxim has become a near-folk-philosophical commonplace in 19th–21st-c. esoteric, occult, and New Age contexts.
