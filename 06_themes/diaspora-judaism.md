# Diaspora Judaism

The Greek-speaking Jewish communities of the post-exilic Mediterranean world — Alexandria (the largest and most culturally productive), Antioch, Asia Minor, Rome — and their distinctive theology, exegesis, and ethical-political-philosophical synthesis with Hellenism. Diaspora Judaism produced the Septuagint, Philo, the Wisdom of Solomon, 3 and 4 Maccabees, Aristobulus, Pseudo-Phocylides, and the Jewish Sibylline Oracles. It was *the* matrix in which the Jesus movement first encountered the Gentile world — Paul, who quotes the Septuagint and writes in Greek, is a Diaspora Jew before he is anything else.

- [[phase-3-006-septuagint]] — the great translation project of 3rd–2nd c. BCE Alexandria; the standard "Bible" of Diaspora Judaism and of earliest Christianity.
- [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]] — the towering Diaspora intellectual; allegorical exegesis fusing Torah and Middle Platonism.
- [[phase-3-012-wisdom-of-solomon]] — Alexandrian Hellenistic-Jewish wisdom literature; pseudo-Solomonic; combines biblical Wisdom theology with Stoic-Platonic ethics.
- [[phase-3-019-4-maccabees]] — Hellenistic-Jewish philosophical exhortation: Stoic *autocrateia* in service of Torah-obedience.

Modern scholarship (Gruen, Barclay) has argued against an older model that sharply opposed Diaspora "Hellenistic" Judaism to Palestinian "Hebraic" Judaism (Bousset, Schürer) — the cultural exchange ran both ways and the categories blur. Hengel's *Judaism and Hellenism* (1974) was the foundational corrective.
