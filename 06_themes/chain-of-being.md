# Great Chain of Being

The Plotinian-Christian-medieval doctrine that all reality is arranged in a continuous descending hierarchy — God / Pure Being / the One at the top, angelic intelligences below, humans, animals, plants, minerals, prime matter at the bottom — each rank derived from the rank above by emanation or creative dependence. Distinguished from a flat ontology by its *gradation* and from a binary God-creation distinction by its *continuum*. Lovejoy's 1936 study (the founding work of the "history of ideas" subdiscipline) traced the doctrine's continuity from Plato and Plotinus through Pseudo-Dionysius, the medievals, the Renaissance, and into 18th-c. natural philosophy, where it was finally displaced by evolutionary biology.

## Manifestations

- **Plotinian.** [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — the One → Nous → Soul → Nature → Matter sequence. Each level "overflows" the prior without diminishing it. The structural seed of all later chain-of-being thinking.
- **Pseudo-Dionysian hierarchies.** [[phase-4-030-pseudo-dionysius]] *Celestial Hierarchy* and *Ecclesiastical Hierarchy* — the angelic ranks (Seraphim, Cherubim, Thrones; Dominions, Virtues, Powers; Principalities, Archangels, Angels) and the ecclesiastical ranks paralleling them. The introduction of *hierarchia* into Western theological vocabulary.
- **Scholastic Aquinas.** [[phase-5-024-aquinas-summa-theologiae]] I, qq. 47–48 (the order of creatures); the doctrine of *plenitude* (every grade of being is filled because divine fecundity overflows). Bonaventure's *Itinerarium* traces the soul's ascent through the chain.
- **Renaissance Hermetism.** [[phase-6-009-agrippa-de-occulta-philosophia]] — three-world hierarchy (elemental, celestial, intellectual / divine) with sympathetic correspondences (cf. [[as-above-so-below]], [[hermetic-correspondences]]). Ficino's *Theologia Platonica* systematizes the Neoplatonic-Christian chain for the Florentine revival.
- **Shakespearean and Elizabethan.** Tillyard 1944 catalogued the chain-of-being's pervasiveness in Elizabethan literature (Ulysses' speech in *Troilus and Cressida* 1.3 is the classic locus). The Reformation did not initially disrupt the chain-of-being framework.
- **18th-c. natural philosophy and its dissolution.** Linnaeus's taxonomy still presupposes the chain (continuity of species); Leibniz's *principle of plenitude* (no missing rungs); the chain breaks under (a) the realization that geological time admits species-change, (b) Darwinian descent-with-modification, (c) modern biological understanding of branching rather than linear taxa.

## Significance

- The chain-of-being supplied the master *organizing image* of Western religious cosmology from late antiquity through the Enlightenment — over a thousand years of theological-philosophical structure.
- Its dissolution under evolutionary biology is one of the major transitions in Western religious-philosophical thought; the modern "loss of the cosmos" (E. A. Burtt, Charles Taylor) is partly the loss of the chain.

## Disputes

- **Lovejoy's thesis of continuity.** Lovejoy 1936 emphasizes continuity; recent intellectual historians (Daston, Garber) emphasize discontinuities and local re-deployments of the *image* of the chain in different conceptual contexts. Lovejoy's chain-of-being-as-master-idea framework is conceded as overstated; the *image* persists.
- **The relation to political hierarchy.** Whether chain-of-being theology *legitimated* social hierarchy (the standard Tillyard reading) or was *independent* of it (modern revisionists) is contested. In practice, both hierarchies reinforced each other.
