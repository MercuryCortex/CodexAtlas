# Pseudonymity as Strategy

The widespread ancient practice of *attributing one's text to an ancient authoritative figure* — Enoch, Moses, Solomon, Daniel, Hermes Trismegistus, John the Disciple — rather than to oneself. Modern moral assumptions (forgery!) frequently mislead the reader: in many ancient contexts pseudonymity was a recognized literary-theological strategy serving recognizable functions: claiming continuity with prophetic / sapiential / apostolic authority; placing a contemporary message under a figure with greater rhetorical weight; participating in the "circle" of a master's authentic disciples; sometimes obscuring authorship for safety.

- [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — Second-Temple Jewish-apocalyptic pseudepigraphy at its fullest.
- [[phase-3-009-jubilees]] — pseudo-Mosaic re-narration of Genesis-Exodus.
- [[phase-3-012-wisdom-of-solomon]] — pseudo-Solomonic Hellenistic-Jewish wisdom.
- [[phase-3-010-sibylline-oracles]] — pseudo-Sibylline oracular collections from Jewish, Christian, and pagan circles.
- [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]] — Gnostic Christian pseudo-Johannine revelation.
- [[phase-4-011-corpus-hermeticum-i]] — pseudo-Hermetic Egyptian-Greek revelation.
- Within the New Testament canon, mainstream critical scholarship treats six of the thirteen Pauline epistles as deutero-Pauline; the Catholic Epistles attributed to James, Peter, Jude, John are pseudonymous on most critical accounts; 2 Peter is the most universally judged pseudonymous.

The diagnostic question is *function* not *deception*: what work is the attribution doing in the ancient social-rhetorical context?
