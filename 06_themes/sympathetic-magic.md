# Sympathetic Magic

Frazer's classification (*The Golden Bough*, 1890) of magical operations into two principles: (1) the *Law of Similarity* — like produces like; an image, name, or symbolic resemblance affects the thing it resembles (homeopathic magic); (2) the *Law of Contagion* — things once in contact continue to affect each other at a distance through the contact-link (e.g., a hair, fingernail, or piece of clothing). Frazer's pair has become standard descriptive vocabulary, though his evolutionary scheme (magic → religion → science) is rejected by modern anthropology.

- [[phase-7-007-golden-bough]] — Frazer's encyclopedic exposition.
- [[phase-6-009-agrippa-de-occulta-philosophia]] — Agrippa's *De occulta philosophia* is the Renaissance-magical encyclopedia in which sympathetic correspondences (planet-metal-plant-stone-animal chains) are systematically catalogued; the ancient *correspondentia* theology behind much Western occult practice. See [[microcosm-macrocosm]].
- [[phase-6-008-paracelsus-corpus]] — Paracelsian doctrine of signatures: every plant bears the outward sign of its medical-magical action; like-cures-like.

Mauss's 1902 *Esquisse d'une théorie générale de la magie* offers a more sociologically careful treatment than Frazer's. The cognitive-psychological reading of these "laws" — as features of intuitive thinking rather than as primitive science — has been developed by Boyer, Sperber, and others.
