# Unmoved Mover (Aristotelian)

A *first cause* of motion (and hence of being) that is itself uncaused and unchanging. Aristotle's argument, *Metaphysics* Λ (XII) 6–9: motion exists eternally; an infinite regress of moved-movers is impossible (or at least explanatorily inadequate); therefore there is a first mover that moves without being moved. The unmoved mover causes motion as final cause — as the object of desire and emulation — not as efficient pusher. Its activity is *noēsis noēseōs* ("thought thinking itself") — eternal contemplation of its own perfection.

## Manifestations
- **Aristotle's *Metaphysics* Λ:** [[phase-3-003-aristotle-metaphysics]] — the primary text. The unmoved mover is pure actuality (no potency), simple, eternal, separate from matter, divine (*theos*), thought of thought. Whether there is one or many (Λ 8 famously argues, against the rest of the book, for 55 unmoved movers corresponding to celestial spheres) is one of the cruxes.
- **Aristotelian theology in late antiquity:** Alexander of Aphrodisias and Simplicius develop the unmoved mover into a fully transcendent first principle; the doctrine becomes a major resource for late-Platonist theology.
- **Plotinus:** [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] — Plotinus partly rejects the Aristotelian mover (the One is beyond intellect, not thought-thinking-itself) but absorbs the *noēsis noēseōs* doctrine into the second hypostasis, Nous. The integration of Plato and Aristotle on the unmoved mover is one of Plotinus's signature moves.
- **Proclus:** [[phase-4-021-proclus-elements-of-theology]] — a fully hierarchized system of unmoved-mover causality.
- **Arabic / Islamic philosophy:** Al-Farabi, Ibn Sina (Avicenna), and Ibn Rushd (Averroes) develop Aristotelian first-mover theology in conversation with Quranic monotheism. Averroes's commentary on Λ becomes a landmark.
- **Maimonides:** *Guide for the Perplexed* uses Aristotelian unmoved-mover argument as one of three proofs of God's existence.
- **Aquinas:** *Summa Theologiae* I q.2 a.3 — the "First Way" of the Five Ways is the unmoved-mover argument adapted to Christian theology: "It is therefore necessary to arrive at a first mover, which is moved by no other; and this everyone understands to be God."
- **Modern reception:** Aristotelian-Thomistic philosophical theology (Garrigou-Lagrange, Feser) defends the argument; analytic philosophy of religion debates it (most influentially, *kalam* cosmological argument, Craig).

## Significance
- **One of the most consequential single arguments in the history of philosophy.** Three monotheistic traditions absorbed it as a foundation of natural theology.
- **Genuine philosophical contrast with biblical creator.** The unmoved mover does not create *ex nihilo*, does not love, does not act in history. Bridging it to the biblical creator God is the work of Philo, Augustine, Maimonides, Aquinas — fraught, brilliant, controversial.

## Disputes
- **Number of movers.** Λ 8's 55 movers fit with Aristotle's astronomy; whether this is consistent with Λ 7's single unmoved mover or whether Aristotle changed his mind is a perennial question.
- **Is the unmoved mover a personal god?** Aristotle's text is austere; whether *theos* in Λ has any religious content beyond philosophical first-cause is debated. Most modern interpreters: minimal religious content, full theological content in later Christian readings.
