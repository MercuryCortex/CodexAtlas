# Discipleship Failure (Markan)

The Markan literary pattern in which Jesus' inner-circle disciples consistently *fail to understand* his teaching, his identity, and his coming death — a pattern beginning with Mk 4:13 ("Do you not understand this parable?"), peaking at the three passion predictions (Mk 8:31–33, 9:31–32, 10:32–34) where Peter rebukes Jesus, James and John request thrones, the Twelve argue about greatness, climaxing in the betrayal, denial, and abandonment of the passion. Mark's Gospel ends (16:8) with the women fleeing in terror and saying nothing — the failure motif extended to the end. Tannehill 1977 reads this as a deliberate narrative strategy: the reader is invited to identify with the disciples' failure and thereby to recognize the cost of true following.

- [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — the locus classicus; the most thoroughgoing failure-of-the-disciples narrative in the New Testament.
- [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] — Matthew softens Mark: at key Markan failure points, Matthew has the disciples *understand* (Mt 16:12, 17:13). The Matthean redaction is itself diagnostic of Mark's strangeness.
- [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] — Luke also softens; in Acts the same Twelve become the Spirit-filled apostolic founders.

Wrede's "messianic secret" hypothesis is the historical-critical framing: Jesus' silencing commands and the disciples' incomprehension are a Markan literary-theological device, not transparent history.
