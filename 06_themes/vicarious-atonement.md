# Vicarious Atonement

The motif of *substitutionary suffering* — one party undergoes suffering/death so that others may be spared, redeemed, or vindicated. The most explicit Hebrew-Bible articulation is Isaiah 53 (the [[suffering-servant]] "stricken for the transgression of my people"); a more developed Second-Temple Jewish form is 4 Maccabees, where the martyrdom of Eleazar and the seven brothers under Antiochus IV functions as *hilasterion* / *antipsychon* — propitiation/exchange — for the nation (4 Macc 17:21–22). The Christian appropriation makes the death of Jesus the universal-vicarious sacrifice — see [[crucifixion-theology]].

- [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]] — Isaiah 53: "the punishment that brought us peace was on him; by his wounds we are healed."
- [[phase-3-019-4-maccabees]] — 4 Macc 17:21–22 makes the martyrs' deaths *hilasterion* and *antipsychon* — most explicit Jewish-Hellenistic vicarious-atonement theology.
- [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Pauline appropriation: Rom 3:25 ("Christ Jesus, whom God put forward as a *hilasterion*"); 2 Cor 5:21; Gal 3:13.

The Anselmian satisfaction model, the Reformation penal-substitution model, and the more recent narrative-and-participation models (e.g., Tom Wright) all develop further the basic vicarious schema.
