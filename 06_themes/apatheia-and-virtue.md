# Apatheia and Virtue

The Stoic ideal of *apatheia* — not "apathy" but *freedom from the passions* (*pathē*) — as the disposition of the sage who has converted false judgments (the passions are misjudgments about what is good) into right judgment. Christian patristic ethics (Clement of Alexandria, Evagrius Ponticus, the Cappadocians) adopted *apatheia* as a Christian ideal — the perfected condition of the soul that prepares for theōria of God, won through ascetic struggle. Augustine treated *apatheia* warily, preferring *ordinata dilectio* (rightly ordered love).

- [[phase-3-005-stoic-foundational-texts]] — Stoic source: Cicero *Tusc.* IV, Epictetus *Discourses*, Marcus Aurelius.
- [[phase-4-025-clement-stromata]] — Clement: the true Gnostic is *apathēs*; *Stromateis* VII develops the Christian appropriation.
- [[phase-4-026-origen-on-first-principles]] — Origen: ascetic freedom from *pathē* as the condition for contemplation; influences Evagrius and the desert monastic tradition.

Subsequent monastic-ascetic theology (Evagrius's eight *logismoi*; Cassian; the *Philokalia*) develops a sophisticated psychology of passions and their cure — the patristic-Eastern equivalent of cognitive-behavioral therapy. Western ascetic theology preserves it via Cassian into the Benedictine and Cistercian traditions.
