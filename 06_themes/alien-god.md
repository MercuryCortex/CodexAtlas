# The Alien God

Hans Jonas's category (*The Gnostic Religion*, 1958) for the *transcendent, world-foreign* high-deity of Gnostic and Marcionite theology — the God who is *not* the creator of this cosmos, has *no positive relation* to it, is *unknown* until disclosed by the saving messenger. The category is the most distilled diagnostic feature of "Gnostic" theology on Jonas's reading: the high God is the *Stranger* to the world.

- [[phase-4-023-irenaeus-against-heresies]] — Irenaeus's hostile witness: the Gnostics proclaim *deus alienus*; Marcion notably so.
- [[phase-7-013-gnostic-religion]] — Jonas's classical exposition.
- Marcion of Sinope: most explicitly framed the *alien Father of Jesus* against the inferior creator-God of the Hebrew Bible. Harnack's *Marcion* (1921) is the major scholarly reconstruction.

The structural contrast is with [[divine-immanence]], with the Hebrew-Bible / orthodox-Christian *Maker of heaven and earth*, and with the philosophical *Unmoved Mover* who is the ultimate source but not the *stranger* to the cosmos. The Cathar / Bogomil [[the-good-god-cathar]] is a later instance of essentially the same theological move.
