# Johannine Logos

The use of *logos* (Word) in [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] 1:1–18 to designate the pre-existent divine being who "became flesh and dwelt among us" in Jesus Christ. A specific instantiation of the broader [[logos-cosmic-reason]] tradition, but with distinctive features that warrant a separate node: identification of Logos with a historical individual; the *incarnational* claim (Logos became sarx); the absolute opening claim "the Logos was God."

## The prologue's claims (John 1:1–18)
- Pre-existence: "In the beginning was the Logos"
- Divinity: "the Logos was God"
- Distinct-yet-related to God: "the Logos was with God"
- Cosmogonic agency: "all things were made through him"
- Incarnation: "the Logos became flesh and dwelt among us"
- Exclusive revelation: "no one has ever seen God; the only-begotten ... has made him known"

## Sources of the Johannine Logos
- **Wisdom tradition** (Dunn, Scott, Hurtado): much of what John says of the Logos is what Sirach 24, Wisdom 7–9, and Proverbs 8 say of personified Wisdom — pre-existence, presence at creation, descent to dwell with humans. The prologue may be a re-attribution of Sophia/Wisdom predicates to Christ. See [[wisdom-personified]].
- **Philonic Logos** ([[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]]): Philo's "second God," the high priest, the firstborn of God, has many parallels with John's Logos. Whether John depends on Philo (Bultmann 1925), shares a common Hellenistic-Jewish source (Dodd), or reaches Philonic conclusions independently (Borgen) is debated.
- **Targumic *memra'***: Aramaic Targums use *memra'* ("word") periphrastically for divine action — possibly a Palestinian-Jewish source for *logos*-as-divine-agency (M. McNamara).
- **Stoic Logos**: Cosmic rational structuring principle ([[logos-stoic]]); rejected by most current scholarship as a *direct* source for John (the divinity-with-personality is too unlike Stoic immanent reason), but provides background air.

## What's distinctive
- **Identification with a historical individual.** Philo's Logos is not a person; Stoic Logos is not a person; biblical Wisdom is poetic personification. John 1:14 — "the Logos became flesh" — collapses the cosmological figure onto a specific 1st-century Galilean. This is the *Christian* move.
- **Incarnational ontology.** Not "the Logos appeared" or "the Logos spoke through" — *became*. The verb (*egeneto*) commits John to a theology no other Logos-tradition makes.
- **Reception history.** The prologue becomes the textual base for Christological development through Nicaea (325), Constantinople (381), Chalcedon (451). One of the most consequential 18 verses in the history of theology.

## Disputes
- **Hymnic source theory.** Whether John 1:1–18 (or 1:1–5, 9–14, 16) is a pre-Johannine hymn the author incorporated, or original Johannine composition. Bultmann posited a non-Christian Gnostic-style hymn rewritten; current scholarship is split.
- **Logos in the rest of John.** The Logos title appears only in the prologue; the rest of the Gospel uses Son, Son of Man, "I am" formulas. Whether this is significant (prologue prepended later) or coincidental.
