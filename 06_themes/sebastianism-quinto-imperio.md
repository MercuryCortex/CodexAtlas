# Sebastianism and the Fifth Empire (Quinto Império)

Portuguese messianism is the most sustained and literarily rich eschatological tradition in Western Europe outside of Jewish and Islamic apocalypticism. It runs from the cobbler-prophet Gonçalo Bandarra in the 1530s through the Jesuit preacher António Vieira in the 17th century to Fernando Pessoa's *Mensagem* in 1934 — four centuries of continuous messianic expectation centered on Portugal's cosmic destiny.

## The chain of transmission

**[[goncalo-annes-bandarra]] (c. 1500–1556) — the root prophecy**
Bandarra, a shoemaker in Trancoso, composed prophetic *Trovas* (verses) in the 1530s anticipating the coming of *O Encoberto* ("The Hidden / Concealed One") — a hidden king who would restore Portugal's glory and defeat the Moors. The *Trovas* circulated in manuscript and oral transmission; the Portuguese Inquisition investigated Bandarra for judaizing (his images of a king-redeemer had Jewish messianic flavor, and he had contact with New Christian communities). After the Battle of Alcácer Quibir (1578), in which Sebastian I disappeared in battle (body never confirmed dead), the *Trovas* were retrospectively read as prophecies of Sebastian — making him the *Encoberto* who would return.

**[[event-battle-of-alcacer-quibir-1578]] — the catastrophe that created the myth**
Sebastian I died (or disappeared) at the Battle of Alcácer Quibir in Morocco on August 4, 1578, fighting an overwhelmingly larger Moroccan force. His body was not initially identified; within months popular imagination turned his absence into hidden survival. Four pretenders to the throne claiming to be the returned Sebastian appeared in the following decades. The absence of a confirmed corpse became the mythological precondition for the *Sebastianist* hope: the hidden king would return, wake from his enchanted sleep, and restore Portugal's empire.

**[[antonio-vieira]] (1608–1697) — the theological systematization**
The Jesuit preacher António Vieira is the greatest literary genius of Portuguese baroque and the most theologically ambitious Sebastianist. In his *História do Futuro* (written ~1649, repeatedly censored by the Inquisition) Vieira elaborated a full eschatological theology: the *Quinto Império* (Fifth Empire) — based on Nebuchadnezzar's dream in Daniel 2 (four world-empires succeeded by a divine kingdom). Vieira argued the Fifth and final empire would be a universal spiritual Portuguese empire (*Quinto Império do Mundo*), ruled by a returned Sebastian I (or a Portuguese king under divine mandate), embracing all peoples including Jews and Muslims in a universal Christian peace. Vieira corresponded with Amsterdam's Sephardic Jewish community (descendants of Portuguese New Christians) and explored the compatibility between his Fifth Empire messianism and Jewish expectations of the Messiah. This was a theologically wild project at the intersection of Catholic eschatology, Jewish messianism (the Fifth Empire = the messianic era), and Portuguese imperial ideology.

**[[fernando-pessoa]] (1888–1935) — the esoteric-modernist completion**
Pessoa's *Mensagem* (1934), his only Portuguese-language book published in his lifetime, is a 44-poem cycle structured as an esoteric reinterpretation of Portuguese history — each historical figure (Prince Henry the Navigator, Vasco da Gama, Sebastian I, Camões) positioned as a hierophant in a spiritual-alchemical preparation for the Fifth Empire. Pessoa's Fifth Empire is explicitly not a political empire but an *espiritual* one: "the hour is come for Portugal to speak again — not as a nation of kings and discoverers, but as the vessel of a higher civilization." The *Mensagem* encodes Masonic, Rosicrucian, and Hermetic symbolism throughout; Pessoa was an initiate of the Ordem da Cabeça de Ferro and wrote extensively on astrology and occultism (most never published in his lifetime, preserved in the *Baú* — the famous trunk of his manuscripts in the National Library of Portugal). See [[fernando-pessoa]], [[manuel-j-gandra]].

## Cross-tradition parallels

**Sebastianism ↔ Jewish messianism — structural MASSIVE WIN**
The Sebastianist tradition developed in precisely the milieu most saturated with Jewish messianic ideas: the Portuguese New Christian community, descendants of the 1497 forced conversions, maintaining crypto-Jewish practice while theorizing within Catholic eschatological categories. Bandarra's *Trovas* show clear Jewish messianic motifs (the hidden king-redeemer, the national redemption, the gathering of the exiles). Vieira's correspondence with the Amsterdam Sephardic community (Menasseh ben Israel's circle, which was also in contact with Sabbatai Zevi's movement) shows direct engagement between Portuguese Catholic Fifth Empire theology and Jewish messianic expectation. The structural parallel — a hidden savior who will return from hiddenness to restore the people and inaugurate a golden age — is the Sebastianist form of what appears in Jewish (*Mashiach*), Islamic (*Mahdi* / *Dajjal* defeat), and Zoroastrian (*Saoshyant*) eschatologies.

**Sebastianism ↔ Islamic Hidden Imam (Twelver Shiism)**
The *Encoberto* who is alive but hidden, whose return will end the world's injustice and inaugurate the golden age, is structurally identical to the Twelver Shia doctrine of the Hidden Imam (Muhammad al-Mahdi, in occultation since 874 CE). Both traditions maintain the living-but-hidden savior in indefinite waiting; both generate a culture of expectation and sign-reading; both produce institutionally heterodox movements (Sebastianism challenged Inquisition authority; Shia messianism challenged Sunni caliphal authority). No historical contact; independent structural convergence driven by similar socio-political conditions (defeated community awaiting supernatural rescue).

**The Fifth Empire and Joachim of Fiore's Three Ages**
Joachim of Fiore (1135–1202) divided history into three ages (Father/Law; Son/Gospel; Spirit/Freedom); the third age — the Age of the Spirit — was the culminating eschatological era. Vieira's *Quinto Império* is structurally Joachimist: a final period that supersedes and fulfills all previous ones, inaugurated by a divinely appointed human instrument. The Joachimist schema (three ages → final age of spiritual freedom) runs through Lessing's *Education of the Human Race*, Hegel's dialectic of history, and Marx's communism as secular variants. Sebastianism / Quinto Império is the Portuguese Catholic node in this Joachimist eschatological tradition.
