# Divine Unity — The Cross-Tradition Doctrine of the One

The doctrine that ultimate reality is fundamentally unitary — one, simple, undivided — appears across traditions with different theological vocabularies but consistent metaphysical structure:

- **Greek philosophy**: Pythagorean Monad, Platonic Good, Neoplatonic One
- **Judaism**: *ehad* (one) — "Hear O Israel, the Lord your God, the Lord is One"
- **Islam**: *tawhid* — divine unity as the defining Islamic theological commitment
- **Advaita Vedanta**: Brahman — non-dual absolute reality; individual selves are ultimately identical with Brahman
- **Taoism**: the Tao — the undifferentiated source that precedes the One ([[phase-2-014-daodejing]])
- **Buddhism** (Madhyamaka): śūnyatā — emptiness as the absence of inherent separate existence; everything is relational/unified at the ground level

The structural convergence is remarkable: traditions with no documented contact produce strikingly similar treatments of the first principle as (a) beyond predication, (b) the source of all multiplicity, (c) accessible through a specific spiritual or intellectual practice, and (d) identical with the ultimate ground of the practitioner's own mind.
