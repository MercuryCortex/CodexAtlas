# Satanic Verses incident (the *gharānīq* / 'high-flying cranes' tradition)

## Definition
The "Satanic Verses incident" — Arabic *qiṣṣat al-gharānīq*, the **'high-flying cranes' tradition** — is the Islamic-tradition narrative according to which **Muhammad temporarily recited verses praising the intercession of the three pre-Islamic Hijazi goddesses al-Lāt, al-ʿUzzā, and Manāt during a recitation of *Sūrat al-Najm* (Quran 53)**, only for Gabriel to subsequently inform him that those verses were satanic interpolation — leading to the abrogation of the goddess-verses and the canonical text we now have at **Quran 53:19-22**. The interpolated text is preserved in the early Islamic literature as: *tilka l-gharānīqu l-ʿulā wa-inna shafāʿatahunna la-turtajā* — "those are the high-flying cranes (or: the exalted *gharānīq*), and their intercession is to be hoped for." The narrative is **extensively transmitted in the earliest Islamic biographical / exegetical / historical literature** — Ibn Ishaq's *Sirat Rasul Allah* (~750), Wāqidī's *Kitāb al-Maghāzī*, Ibn Saʿd's *Ṭabaqāt*, Ṭabarī's *Tafsīr* and *Tārīkh*, and many early *tafsīr* works — and was treated as authentic prophetic biography by the great majority of early Muslim transmitters before being **rejected as inauthentic Quranic-tampering by later orthodox theology** (developing especially from the 13th c. onward; Ibn Taymiyya rejects it). The Quranic verse 22:52 — *"We have not sent before you any messenger or prophet but when he wished, Satan threw [something] into his wishing; but God abrogates what Satan throws, then God makes firm His signs"* — was canonically interpreted in the early period as the Quranic acknowledgement of the *gharānīq* incident, and re-interpreted by later orthodoxy as referring to satanic insinuation generally without conceding the goddess-verses specifically. (Ahmed 2017; Hawting 1999.)

## Earliest attestations
- **Ibn Ishaq *Sirat Rasul Allah* (~750)** — the earliest extended narrative; describes Muhammad reciting the goddess-verses to the Meccan Quraysh, the Quraysh prostrating in approval, the Muslim emigrants in Aksumite Ethiopia hearing the news of Meccan-Muslim reconciliation and returning home, then Gabriel correcting Muhammad and the verses being abrogated. ([[phase-5-043-ibn-ishaq-sirat-rasul-allah]] — this batch.)
- **Wāqidī *Kitāb al-Maghāzī* (~822) + Ibn Saʿd *Ṭabaqāt al-Kubrā* (~840)** — independent early Islamic-tradition transmission paths.
- **Ṭabarī *Tafsīr* (~900s) and *Tārīkh al-Rusul wa-l-Mulūk*** — full transmission with multiple isnāds.
- **Quran 22:52** (the *Sūrat al-Ḥajj* verse on satanic insinuation into prophetic wishing) — read in the early Islamic-tradition exegetical literature as the Quranic acknowledgement of the incident; re-interpreted by later orthodoxy.
- **Quran 53:19-22** itself (the *Sūrat al-Najm* canonical text) names al-Lāt / al-ʿUzzā / Manāt and rejects them as "mere names you and your fathers have named, for which God has sent down no authority" — the canonical text after the abrogation.

## Variants
- **Early Islamic acceptance (~7th-11th c.).** Ahmed 2017 documents that the *gharānīq* narrative is accepted as authentic by the great majority of early Muslim transmitters — *muḥaddithūn*, *mufassirūn*, biographers — without theological discomfort, because Quran 22:52 was read as Quranic acknowledgement. The doctrine of prophetic infallibility (*ʿiṣma*) was not yet developed in the absolutist form it would later take; early Muslim opinion treated the incident as a single recoverable mistake corrected by God.
- **Later orthodox rejection (~13th c. onward).** As the doctrine of prophetic *ʿiṣma* tightened (especially in Ashʿarī and Māturīdī theology), the *gharānīq* narrative became theologically untenable. Ibn Taymiyya (d. 1328) — the keystone modern-Salafi reception-history figure ([[ibn-taymiyya]], this batch) — rejects it as inauthentic. Modern Salafi orthodoxy uniformly rejects the incident.
- **Modern critical scholarship (~1970s onward).** Watt 1953 *Muhammad at Mecca* and subsequent scholarship (Burton 1970; Welch 1980; Rubin 1995; Ahmed 2017) substantially reverse the orthodox-orthodox rejection: the narrative is **so widely attested across early independent Islamic-tradition transmission chains** that it is best read as historically authentic — most likely a brief political-theological compromise by Muhammad with the Meccan Quraysh, retracted under prophetic-conscience and reframed as satanic insinuation. The historical-critical default in modern Islamic-studies scholarship is **acceptance of the incident's historicity** with the orthodox-Islamic objections noted as later theological development.
- **Rushdie's *The Satanic Verses* (1988).** Salman Rushdie's novel uses the *gharānīq* narrative as a frame story; the 1989 Khomeini *fatwā* against Rushdie and the subsequent global controversy ([[event-iranian-revolution-1979|Iranian-revolution]] downstream) made the narrative globally famous outside Islamic-studies specialist circles. The novel's reception-history is itself a major modern Islam ↔ Western liberalism collision.

## Connections claimed
- **The *gharānīq* incident is the cleanest textual proof of the pre-Islamic Hijazi worship of [[allat]], [[al-uzza]], and [[manat]] as "daughters of Allāh."** Without the incident, the Quranic mention of the three goddesses at 53:19-22 would be a generic polemic against named goddesses; with the incident, we have **Muhammad's own reported recitation** confirming the daughters-of-Allāh framing as the contested theological terrain of his early Meccan preaching.
- **The incident establishes the late-antique Hijazi religious milieu** in which Allāh was already known as the high god of the pre-Islamic Meccan pantheon (with goddess-daughters as intercessors), and Muhammad's theological project was specifically the elimination of the goddess-intercessors and the radical-tawhid reframing of Allāh as without partners.
- **The incident is the load-bearing example of the doctrine of prophetic *ʿiṣma*** (infallibility) — its development as a doctrine is partly traceable to the need to handle the *gharānīq* narrative theologically.
- **The 1989 Khomeini *fatwā* against Rushdie** ([[event-iranian-revolution-1979]] downstream) makes the incident the principal modern-era flashpoint of Islam ↔ Western literary-publishing controversy.

## Disputes
- **Historicity.** Modern critical scholarship: probably historical (Watt, Burton, Ahmed). Modern Salafi orthodox theology: rejects as inauthentic. Mainstream Sunni and Shia orthodoxy: largely follows the post-Ibn-Taymiyya rejection. Critical-scholarship vs. orthodox-theology divergence is sharp.
- **Whether *gharānīq* = "cranes" or "exalted ones" (Garnett or *ʿulā* reading).** Lexical ambiguity in the interpolated phrase.
- **Quran 22:52 reading.** Early *tafsīr*: the verse is Quranic acknowledgement of the *gharānīq* incident. Later orthodox *tafsīr*: the verse refers to satanic insinuation in prophetic wishing generally, not the *gharānīq* incident.
