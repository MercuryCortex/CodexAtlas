# Two-Spirits Doctrine

The Qumran *Treatise on the Two Spirits* (1QS III:13–IV:26) is the most explicit Jewish-sectarian articulation of [[ethical-dualism]]: God created the *spirit of truth* and the *spirit of perversion* (or light/darkness), and humanity is sorted between them. Each person walks in degrees of both spirits until the eschaton, when God will purify and "destroy iniquity forever." The treatise structurally resembles Zoroastrian dualistic anthropology (Spenta Mainyu / Angra Mainyu of the Gathas), which has fed the long-running debate about Iranian influence on Qumran and Second-Temple Jewish apocalyptic.

- [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — *Community Rule* (1QS) III–IV is the central text; the *Two Spirits* discourse functions as catechetical-theological summary.
- [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]] — Yasna 30: the choosing of the *spənta* and *angra* spirits "in the beginning" provides the typological parallel.
- [[phase-2-004-yasna-younger-avesta]] — the more developed Zoroastrian dualism that may have been mediated to Hellenistic Judaism.

Scholarly debate: (1) direct Iranian influence (Boyce, Hultgård, Shaked) vs. parallel Jewish development; (2) the integration of the two-spirits theology with predestinarian frameworks (the Qumran community was both deterministically predestinarian and behaviorally rigorist). The two-spirits psychology appears to underlie later Christian texts (the *Didache*'s "Two Ways," the *Shepherd of Hermas*'s good and evil angels), filtering an originally apocalyptic-dualist concept into early Christian moral instruction.
