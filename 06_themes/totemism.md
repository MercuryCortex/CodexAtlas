# Totemism

Late-19th/early-20th-century anthropological category for a clan-based identification with a plant or animal species understood as ancestor, kin, or sacred symbol — with associated rules of avoidance (taboo on eating or harming the totem) and ritualized communion (occasional collective eating of the totem). Frazer's *Totemism and Exogamy* (1910) and Durkheim's *Elementary Forms* (1912) made totemism the candidate "elementary form" of religion. Lévi-Strauss in *Le totémisme aujourd'hui* (1962) deconstructed the unified category, arguing totemism is not a single phenomenon but a logical operation: differentiating human social groups by differentiating natural species — "good to think with," not a sui generis institution.

- [[phase-7-010-elementary-forms-religious-life]] — Durkheim's master-work; Australian Aboriginal totemism as the elementary form revealing the social origin of the sacred (the clan worships itself).
- [[phase-7-007-golden-bough]] — Frazer's encyclopedic survey including totemic data.
- Lévi-Strauss's critique (1962) — "the so-called totemism is no more than a particular case of the general problem of classification."

Modern anthropology has largely accepted Lévi-Strauss's deflation; totemism survives as a useful regional category (e.g., for North American and Australian ethnography) without claiming to be the *origin* of religion.
