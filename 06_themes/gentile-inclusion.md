# Gentile Inclusion

The first-century question of how non-Jewish (Gentile, *ethnē*) believers in the Jesus movement relate to the Mosaic Torah — whether circumcision, food laws, calendar observance bind them. The Pauline mission resolves the question by *not* requiring Gentile circumcision and Torah-observance; the Jerusalem conference (Acts 15) negotiates a minimal abstention. Paul's letters (esp. Galatians and Romans) develop the theology — Gentiles are included on the basis of faith, not by becoming Jewish; the question structurally reshapes [[justification-by-faith]] and the [[gentile-mission]].

- [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Galatians 2 (the conflict with Peter at Antioch); Galatians 3 (faith of Abraham as inclusive principle); Romans 9–11 (the deepest theological reflection on Israel and the nations).
- [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] — Acts 10 (Cornelius), Acts 15 (Jerusalem council), Acts 28 (closing turn to the Gentiles).
- [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]] — Isa 49:6 ("a light to the nations") supplies prophetic warrant Paul and Luke repeatedly invoke.
- [[phase-3-012-wisdom-of-solomon]] — Hellenistic-Jewish background: the universalizing imagination of Diaspora Judaism that prepared the ground.

"Paul within Judaism" scholarship (Nanos, Eisenbaum, Fredriksen) has reframed gentile inclusion as a *Jewish* eschatological move (the nations are coming in as the prophets predicted) rather than as a break with Judaism.
