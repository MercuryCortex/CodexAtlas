# Fulfillment of Prophecy

The exegetical-narrative claim that events in the life of Jesus (or in the founding history of any prophetic movement) *fulfill* what was foretold in earlier sacred Scripture. The Matthean "formula quotations" — "this happened to fulfill what was spoken by the prophet" (Mt 1:22, 2:15, 2:17, 2:23, 4:14, 8:17, 12:17, 13:35, 21:4, 27:9) — are the most systematic NT instance. Stendahl 1954 argued that Matthew's quotations show a distinctive *pesher*-like text-form, suggesting a school of scriptural interpretation behind the Gospel.

- [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] — the Matthean formula quotations; the most explicit fulfillment-program in the NT.
- [[phase-3-018-luke-acts]] — Lukan fulfillment: Lk 4:21 ("today this Scripture has been fulfilled in your hearing"); Lk 24:25–27, 44–47 (the risen Christ as scripture-interpreter); the Acts apostolic preaching is shot through with prophetic-proof quotations.
- [[phase-3-006-septuagint]] — the NT writers overwhelmingly cite the Greek Bible; the Septuagintal text-form sometimes makes a quotation work in ways the Hebrew would not (e.g., Mt 1:23 = LXX Isa 7:14 *parthenos*).

Dodd 1952 argued that there were a small number of OT *testimony texts* (especially Ps 110, Ps 22, Isa 53, Zech 9–14) that formed the *substructure* of all NT Christology; Lindars 1961 *NT Apologetic* documented the development of this exegetical-apologetic tradition. The pattern recurs in every later prophetic movement that claims to fulfill earlier prophecy (Islamic claims about Muḥammad in earlier Scripture; Mormon claims about Joseph Smith).
