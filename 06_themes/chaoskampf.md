# Chaoskampf

A creator/champion deity defeats a primordial chaos monster (usually serpentine or marine) and creates or orders the cosmos from the monster's body or the abated waters. Term coined by Hermann Gunkel (1895). Distinguishes from creation-by-fiat or creation-by-thought.

## Earliest attestations
- **Babylonian:** [[phase-1-008-enuma-elish]] — Marduk slays Tiamat; splits her body to make heavens and earth.
- **Ugaritic:** [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]] — Baal defeats Yam (Sea) and Mot (Death).
- **Hittite:** Illuyanka myth — storm god vs. dragon.
- **Hebrew Bible (residual):** Yahweh vs. Leviathan / Rahab / Yam (Job 26:12, Ps 74:13–14, Isa 27:1, 51:9–10) — chaoskampf survives in poetic fragments though displaced by Genesis 1's creation-by-fiat.
- **Vedic:** Indra vs. Vritra (Rigveda).
- **Greek:** Zeus vs. Typhon (Hesiod's Theogony).

## Significance
Chaoskampf appears across Indo-European AND Semitic traditions, suggesting either deep typological commonality or Bronze-Age cultural networks across the Near East and beyond. Gunkel's thesis that Genesis 1 *demythologizes* an earlier chaoskampf (with `tehom` = Tiamat etymologically related) is widely held but contested in detail.
