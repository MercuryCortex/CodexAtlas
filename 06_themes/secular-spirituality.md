# Secular Spirituality

The late-modern phenomenon of practices, languages, and identities that are recognizably *religious* in form (meditation, ritual, asceticism, prayer-analogs, scriptural-style texts, charismatic teachers, conversion-narratives) while explicitly disavowing affiliation with any traditional religion. Includes the "spiritual-but-not-religious" demographic (Wuthnow, Roof, Lim), secular Buddhism (Stephen Batchelor's *Buddhism without Beliefs* 1997, Sam Harris's *Waking Up* 2014), the mindfulness movement (Jon Kabat-Zinn's MBSR program from 1979), secular meditation programs in corporate / clinical / military contexts, humanistic and transpersonal psychology, secular ritual movements (Sunday Assembly, secular Sabbath observances). Theoretically framed by Charles Taylor's *A Secular Age* (2007) as one of the characteristic "cross-pressured" positions of post-1500 Western religious life.

## Manifestations

- **Secular Buddhism.** Stephen Batchelor, *Buddhism without Beliefs* (1997), *Confession of a Buddhist Atheist* (2010); Sam Harris, *Waking Up* (2014). Methodologically takes Buddhist meditative-and-ethical practice as separable from Buddhist-metaphysical claims (rebirth, karma in the cosmological sense, Buddha-fields).
- **The mindfulness movement.** Jon Kabat-Zinn's Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR) developed at the University of Massachusetts Medical Center (1979); subsequent diffusion into corporate (Google's *Search Inside Yourself*, 2007), clinical (MBCT for depression), and military programs. McMindfulness critics (Purser 2019) argue the program strips Buddhist ethical context for productivity-purposes.
- **Spiritual-but-not-religious (SBNR).** Wuthnow's *After Heaven* (1998) and later survey research (Pew, Lim and Putnam). Approximately 20–30% of Americans in the 2010s identify as SBNR; the category is over-represented among the young and the politically liberal.
- **Humanistic / transpersonal psychology.** Maslow's hierarchy-of-needs and peak-experience (1962); Stanislav Grof and the transpersonal movement (1970s onward); Ken Wilber's *integral* synthesis (1990s onward). Each frames religious-experiential categories in non-supernatural psychological terms.
- **New atheism (paradoxical case).** Harris's *Waking Up*, Christopher Hitchens's *Mortality* meditations, Dawkins's late-career nuance about wonder-and-awe. New atheism is *officially* anti-religious but contains a non-trivial secular-spirituality strand.
- **Secular ritual.** Sunday Assembly (founded 2013, London) — explicitly secular gatherings with hymn-singing, sermons, community. Secular Jewish observance (cultural-Jewish Passover, secular bar mitzvah). The growing "spiritual-but-not-religious" cohort of practicing-without-believing.

## Disputes

- **Whether secular spirituality is genuinely "secular" or is best understood as a *crypto-religious* phenomenon.** Taylor's *Secular Age* argues the latter — the conditions of belief have shifted but the search for fullness remains religious. Eagleton's *Reason, Faith, and Revolution* (2009) makes the case sharply against the new atheists.
- **Carrette and King's critique.** *Selling Spirituality* (2005) argues the mindfulness-and-secular-spirituality movement represents a corporate-capitalist appropriation of religious practice, stripping its critical-prophetic potential.
- **Whether secular spirituality represents a *new* religious form or a temporary symptom of declining institutional religious affiliation.** Surveys disagree; the cross-generational stability of SBNR identification (Lim and Putnam 2010, MacInnis et al. 2017) suggests new form rather than transitional state.
