# Mixture cosmology (Manichaean)

## Definition
The Manichaean account of the present material cosmos as a *mixture* (Greek *mīxis*, Middle Persian *gümēzišn*, Syriac *ḥbīkūtā*) of Light-particles (the divine substance) trapped within Dark-matter (the demonic substance). The mixture is the result of the cosmic invasion of Light by Darkness (see [[two-principles]]) and the Father of Greatness's defensive strategy: he emanated the Primal Man and his five Sons (the Living Spirit's elements: Aether, Wind, Light, Water, Fire) as bait to be devoured by Darkness. The Light-particles thus ingested are now scattered through matter — most concentrated in plants (especially light-colored, fragrant, juicy plants — melons, cucumbers, citrus), less in animals, barely at all in stone and metal. The cosmos exists *as the mechanism* by which the trapped Light is gradually liberated (the *Living Soul* or *jīwāndāgān* — the cosmic soul of Light suffering in matter).

Diagnostic markers: (a) divine substance literally trapped in matter as particles; (b) the cosmos as a *processing machinery* for Light-extraction; (c) the Manichaean dietary and ritual code (the Elect eat plants, refuse meat, eat ritually-presented food) as the human contribution to cosmic Light-liberation; (d) the cosmic eschaton as the final separation of Light from Darkness.

## Earliest attestations
- **Mani's own Šābuhragān** ([[phase-4-016-shabuhragan]], ~245 CE): the cosmogonic-eschatological summary for Shapur I. Preserved fragmentarily.
- **Cologne Mani Codex** ([[phase-4-014-cologne-mani-codex]], 4th-c. Greek copy of earlier biographical material): Mani's youth and revelatory experiences.
- **Kephalaia of the Teacher** ([[phase-4-015-kephalaia-of-the-teacher]], 4th-c. Coptic): systematic theological-cosmological compendium; the most detailed surviving description of the cosmic apparatus (the wheels, columns, ships of light, the Father-of-Life machinery).
- **Augustine** (*Contra Faustum*, *De Natura Boni*, *De Genesi contra Manichaeos*): outside-perspective testimony to the mixture cosmology as taught to the African Manichaean Hearers, including Augustine himself for nine years.
- **Theodore bar Konai's *Book of Scholia*** (~8th c. Syriac): preserves the most detailed surviving systematic narrative of Manichaean cosmogony, drawing on Eastern Manichaean sources now mostly lost. Crucial for reconstruction.
- **Al-Nadīm,** *Fihrist* (~987 CE): Arabic-Islamic source with substantial preserved doctrinal material.
- **Turfan and Dunhuang Manichaean manuscripts** (rediscovered early 20th c., Middle Persian, Parthian, Sogdian, Old Uyghur, Chinese): the principal Eastern Manichaean sources.

## Variants
- **Strict literal substantivism:** Light is literally substantial; Dark is literally substantial; the mixture is metaphysically real. This is the standard Manichaean teaching.
- **Polemical caricature:** Augustine's reports of "the melon weeping when picked" caricature Manichaean reverence for Light-bearing plants. The underlying doctrine is more sophisticated — the *jīwāndāgān* suffers throughout vegetal life.
- **Reform within Manichaeism:** later Eastern Manichaean texts (Sogdian, Uyghur) show some modification toward more spiritualized readings of the substantive Light.

## Connections claimed
- **The Manichaean diet, fast, and seal-of-the-mouth** are direct expressions of mixture cosmology. The Elect's ritual meal (concentrated Light-particles released through Elect digestion-as-cosmic-process) is *the* central liturgical act of the religion.
- **Sasanian-Iranian background.** BeDuhn 2000: the Manichaean account of the cosmic apparatus draws on Iranian (Zoroastrian and Zurvanite) cosmological machinery — the wheels, columns, the cosmic ox, the celestial path of souls.
- **Gnostic parallels.** The Light-trapped-in-matter scheme is structurally parallel to Sethian Gnostic accounts of divine sparks trapped in archontic creation; the Manichaean version is more elaborately *cosmological-mechanical* than Sethian Gnostic accounts, which tend to be more existential-anthropological.

## Disputes
- **Influence on Augustine.** Augustine's mature theological hostility to the *substance* model of evil (his *privatio boni* doctrine) is unintelligible without his Manichaean past. Whether his subsequent doctrines of concupiscence, original sin, and predestination owe more to Mani than to scripture is a recurrent — and overstated — critique of Augustinianism (BeDuhn 2010, 2013 traces the relationship carefully).
- **Authenticity of details.** Reconstructing the precise Manichaean cosmogony requires triangulating fragmentary Turfan sources, hostile Christian and Islamic accounts, internal Coptic Kephalaia, and occasional ancient citations. Theodore bar Konai's narrative is detailed but late; modern reconstructions (BeDuhn, Lieu, Sundermann, Bermejo-Rubio) vary in detail.
