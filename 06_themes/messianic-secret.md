# The Messianic Secret

William Wrede's category (*Das Messiasgeheimnis in den Evangelien*, 1901) for the Markan literary-theological pattern in which Jesus repeatedly *silences* those who recognize his identity — demons (Mk 1:25, 1:34, 3:12), healed persons (Mk 1:44, 5:43, 7:36, 8:26), the disciples (Mk 8:30, 9:9) — and conceals himself from public recognition. Wrede argued that this is a *Markan literary-theological device* rather than transparent history: it provides an apologetic explanation for why the church's later confession of Jesus as Messiah was not universally apparent during his historical ministry. The Wredean reading became foundational for 20th-century Gospel scholarship; the historicity of the silencings is now generally treated as a literary-theological pattern.

- [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — the locus classicus; the pattern recurs through the entire Markan narrative.

The category is closely related to [[discipleship-failure]] (the disciples *do* fail to understand even when Jesus partially discloses); Wrede 1901 and Tannehill 1977 are the foundational treatments.

Modern critique: not all the silencings are of the same kind; the "secret" applies differently to demons (who must be silenced because they know too much), to the disciples (who must learn the cross-Christology), and to healed persons (who would gather the wrong kind of crowd). Wrede's elegant single-category may have over-unified the phenomena.
