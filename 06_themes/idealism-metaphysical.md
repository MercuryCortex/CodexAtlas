# Metaphysical Idealism

The metaphysical thesis that mind / consciousness / spirit is *more real than* or *the ground of* matter. The thesis takes distinct forms across traditions but the family resemblance is the polemic against materialism (whatever the local materialism is) and the assertion of consciousness-as-primary.

- **Advaita Vedānta** — [[phase-5-005-shankara-brahma-sutra-bhasya]]: only *brahman* is real; the empirical world is *māyā*, dependent appearance. The clearest classical form. See [[non-duality]].
- **Vijñānavāda / Yogācāra Buddhism** — "consciousness-only"; an *anti-realist* idealism distinct from Vedānta's substance-monism.
- **Plato** — [[phase-3-002-plato-dialogues]]: the Forms as the truly real; the sensible world as derivative; cf. [[forms-and-archetypes]].
- **Neoplatonism** — [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]]: the One/Intellect/Soul hierarchy; matter as privation.
- **German Idealism** (Phase 7 reception) — Schelling, Hegel, Fichte; arguably the European reformulation of these older idealisms.
- **Modern philosophy of mind** — panpsychist and idealist programs (Galen Strawson, Bernardo Kastrup) explicitly retrieve these older traditions.

Distinguish from [[non-duality]] (which can be idealist or process-monist) and from [[divine-immanence]] (which is theological-relational, not metaphysical-substance).
