# Solomonic-Genealogical Dynastic Claim

## Definition
The **Solomonic-genealogical dynastic claim** is the Ethiopian theologico-political assertion that the Ethiopian imperial line descends, through [[menelik-i-legendary]] (legendary son of Solomon of Israel and the [[makeda-queen-of-sheba]]), from King David of Israel — and that this Davidic descent makes the Ethiopian emperor a *Christological-messianic figure* in the sense that Christ himself is also of David's line. The claim integrates Old Testament Davidic typology (2 Sam 7; Psalm 89), the legendary Solomon-Sheba pericope (1 Kings 10 / 2 Chr 9), and the Menelik-translates-the-Ark narrative of the [[phase-8-008-kebra-nagast]] into a single national-religious-imperial mythology.

The claim was constitutive ideology for:
- The original Aksumite-era foundational mythology (pre-1322 substrate);
- The 1322 Ge'ez redaction of the Kebra Nagast under Nebura'ed Yeshaq of Aksum;
- The 1270 Solomonic restoration ([[event-solomonic-restoration-1270]]) under Yekuno Amlak;
- The 14th–20th c. Solomonic Ethiopian imperial line (1270–1974);
- The 1955 constitutional codification under [[wolega-tafari-makonnen-haile-selassie]] (Article 2: "The Imperial Dignity shall remain perpetually attached to the line of Haile Selassie I, descendant of King Sahle Selassie, whose line descends without interruption from the dynasty of Menelik I, son of the Queen of Ethiopia, the Queen of Sheba, and King Solomon of Jerusalem.");
- The Rastafarian theology of Haile Selassie as messianic-Davidic figure (1930+; see [[tradition-rastafari]]).

## Earliest attestations

- **Targum Sheni to Esther** (Aramaic, ~6th–9th c. CE) — the most elaborated pre-Kebra-Nagast Jewish recension of the Solomon-Sheba narrative with riddles and elaborated wisdom-test material.
- **Quran Surah 27 (an-Naml) vv. 15–44** (~7th c. CE) — the Bilqis-Solomon narrative; structurally related to the Kebra Nagast's earlier strata.
- **Coptic and Syriac Christian legends** (4th–9th c.) — substrate material absorbed into the Kebra Nagast.
- **Kebra Nagast Ge'ez redaction (1314–1322)** — the surviving systematic form.

## Variants

- **The Aksumite-substrate version.** Whether there was a pre-Christian Aksumite-traditional Solomonic narrative complex onto which the Kebra Nagast crystallizes is debated. Some scholars (Munro-Hay 2006) see the underlying tradition circulating in the Ge'ez-cultural area for centuries before 1322; others see the 1322 redaction as substantially original Solomonic-restoration-era propaganda.
- **The Solomonic-restoration version.** Post-1270, the Yekuno-Amlak claim becomes politically operative; the 1322 redaction codifies it textually.
- **The 1955 constitutional version.** Haile Selassie's modernizing constitution codifies the genealogical claim as constitutional law.
- **The Rastafarian version.** The Rastafarian theology of Haile Selassie as Black-Davidic-messianic figure depends on the Solomonic descent claim and the Kebra Nagast genealogy.

## Connections claimed

- **The Solomonic claim as substitution theology.** The Kebra Nagast presents Ethiopia as *replacing* Israel as the chosen people — the Ark's translation to Aksum (Menelik's act) *deposes* Israel as custodian of sanctity. This is theologically supersessionist in a way that parallels but is distinct from standard Christian-supersessionist readings of Israelite election.
- **The Lion of Judah symbolism.** Revelation 5:5's "Lion of the Tribe of Judah" became — through the Kebra-Nagast logic — the Ethiopian-imperial title; the emperor was the "Conquering Lion of the Tribe of Judah." This title circulates intensely in Rastafarian iconography and global reggae culture (Bob Marley's *Lion of Judah*, etc.) via Haile Selassie's regalia.
- **The Davidic-messianic dimension.** Christ is Davidic; the Solomonic-Menelikan line is Davidic; the Ethiopian emperor is therefore — at minimum — a Davidic-messianic *typology*. Rastafarian theology takes this typology to identity (Haile Selassie *is* Christ-returned); Tewahedo Christianity holds typology without identity.
