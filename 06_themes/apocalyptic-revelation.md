# Apocalyptic Revelation

A genre and a worldview. As genre (per the SBL Genres Project, Collins 1979): a narrative work in which an otherworldly being reveals to a human recipient a transcendent reality concerning the temporal (end of history) and/or spatial (heavenly realms) order. As worldview: history is approaching a divinely-set climax; the present age is corrupt and dominated by evil powers; a coming intervention will overturn the order and vindicate the elect.

## Manifestations
- **1 Enoch:** [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]] — the foundational apocalyptic corpus. Watchers narrative, Book of the Watchers, Astronomical Book, Book of Dreams (Animal Apocalypse), Epistle of Enoch, Book of Parables. Spans 3rd c. BCE – 1st c. CE.
- **Daniel:** [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] 7–12 — the only fully-apocalyptic work to enter the Hebrew canon. Four-kingdoms schema, Son of Man, resurrection of the dead (Dan 12:2, the only clear Hebrew Bible resurrection text).
- **Jubilees, Sibylline Oracles:** [[phase-3-009-jubilees]] (rewritten apocalyptic chronology); [[phase-3-010-sibylline-oracles]] (apocalyptic oracular poetry, Jewish then Christian).
- **Qumran:** [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — community organized eschatologically; *War Scroll* (1QM), *Pesharim*, *Two Spirits* discourse (1QS 3–4).
- **Paul:** [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Pauline theology is structurally apocalyptic: present evil age (Gal 1:4), imminent return of Christ, resurrection of the dead, transformation of all creation. Käsemann (1969): "Apocalyptic was the mother of all Christian theology."
- **Synoptic apocalyptic discourse:** Mk 13 / Mt 24 / Lk 21 — the "Little Apocalypse."
- **Book of Revelation:** Jewish-Christian apocalyptic absorbing imagery from Daniel, Ezekiel, Zechariah.
- **Sethian Gnostic apocalypses:** [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]] reframes the genre — the revelation is cosmological rather than historical-eschatological, but the form (risen Christ instructs an apostle in heavenly secrets) is recognizably apocalyptic.

## Sources and influences
- **Zoroastrian background.** Scholarship since Bousset (1903) has argued Persian eschatology (cosmic dualism, resurrection, last judgment, final renovation of the world) underlies Jewish apocalyptic developments after the Babylonian Exile. Collins (1984) cautions that direct dependence is hard to prove given the dating uncertainties for Zoroastrian texts, but the broad influence is widely accepted.
- **Wisdom and prophetic streams.** von Rad (1965) controversially derived apocalyptic from Wisdom traditions rather than prophecy; most current scholarship sees both as contributors.

## Disputes
- **Definition.** Whether "apocalyptic" names a genre, a worldview, or a social movement (the "apocalyptic community") — and whether these three overlap or diverge. Collins (1979, 1984) gave the now-standard generic definition; Hanson (1975) emphasized social location.
- **Persian influence: how much, how direct.** Boyce (1984) maximalist; Hultgård (2000) moderate; Barr (1985) cautious.
