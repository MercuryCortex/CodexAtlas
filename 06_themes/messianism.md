# Messianism

Expectation of a future divinely-anointed deliverer who will inaugurate a transformed age. From Hebrew *māšîaḥ* "anointed one" — originally any anointed figure (priest, king, prophet); only in late Second Temple period did it crystallize as a *technical* term for an eschatological deliverer. The category embraces multiple sub-types: royal-Davidic, priestly, prophetic, transcendent (heavenly Son-of-Man), suffering, dying-and-rising.

## Manifestations
- **Pre-exilic royal anointing:** [[phase-2-010-hebrew-bible-early-prophets]] — *māšîaḥ* refers to the reigning Davidic king as Yahweh's anointed. No future eschatological expectation yet attached.
- **Post-exilic prophetic restoration hope:** Second Isaiah ([[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]]) — Cyrus is called "my anointed" (Isa 45:1); Servant Songs (Isa 42, 49, 50, 52–53) — figure ambiguous in identity. The royal motif begins to be retooled for a future hope.
- **Daniel:** [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]] 7 — "one like a Son of Man" comes with the clouds to receive an everlasting kingdom; 9:25–26 — "an anointed prince" cut off. The two streams (Davidic king, heavenly Son of Man) here meet.
- **Qumran:** [[phase-3-011-dead-sea-scrolls]] — *two* messiahs expected: a priestly Messiah of Aaron and a royal Messiah of Israel. The community structurally subordinates the royal to the priestly. 4Q174 (Florilegium) and 4Q252 develop royal messianism; 11QMelchizedek presents a heavenly figure.
- **Synoptic Jesus tradition:** [[phase-3-014-q-source]], [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]], [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] — Jesus addresses himself principally as "Son of Man" (see [[son-of-man]]); christological titles applied are messianic re-readings.
- **Pauline development:** [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Paul transforms *Christos* from title to near-proper-name; the cross becomes the messianic act (crucified Messiah is the scandalous innovation, 1 Cor 1:23).
- **Bar Kokhba revolt:** [[event-bar-kokhba-revolt-132-136]] — Akiva proclaims Bar Kokhba the Messiah; failure traumatic.
- **Rabbinic and later Jewish:** Maimonides codifies messianic expectation among the 13 principles; mystical messianisms (Sabbatai Zevi, 17th c.) and modern political-religious messianisms.
- **Islamic:** Concepts of Mahdi (the rightly-guided one) and the return of ʿĪsā (Jesus) — messianic structure in Islamic eschatology.

## Disputes
- **Messianic claimants of the 1st–2nd century.** Theudas, the Egyptian, Bar Kokhba, John the Baptist (in some scholarly construals), Jesus. Whether Jesus actively claimed messianic status or whether his followers attached it post-Easter ("messianic secret" of Mark, see [[messianic-secret]]) is contested.
- **Pre-Christian "dying Messiah."** Whether any pre-Christian Jewish text expects a *dying* Messiah is one of the hottest questions in Second Temple scholarship. 4Q541 (the "Aaronic Messiah") may; the Isaianic Servant Songs may be read messianically but were not necessarily so read pre-Christianly. The Christian re-reading of Isa 53 as messianic is innovative.
- **Davidic vs. heavenly streams.** Whether Daniel 7's Son of Man tradition is genuinely *messianic* (vs. corporate-Israel) is debated; Collins (1995) defends individual-messianic reading.
