# Strict Monotheism

The theological-political claim that *only one* God exists, with no rival, consort, junior god, or genuinely subordinate divine being. Distinct from but emerging out of [[henotheism-monolatry]] (exclusive worship of one god among others). The Deutero-Isaianic "there is none beside me" (Isa 45:5, 14, 21–22), the Zoroastrian high-god Ahura Mazda framework, the Qur'anic *tawḥīd* (especially Sura 112 *al-Ikhlāṣ*), and the Maimonidean systematization are the major articulations.

- [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]] — the foundational Hebrew-Bible articulation: Isa 40–55 polemicizes against idols and asserts YHWH as the only God.
- [[phase-2-002-gathas-of-zarathustra]] — the Gathic Zoroastrian framework: Ahura Mazda as supreme; though the Younger Avesta develops a more populated divine economy.
- [[phase-4-034-quran]] — *Tawḥīd* is the theological core; *shirk* (associating partners with God) is the unforgivable sin.
- [[phase-5-019-maimonides-guide-for-the-perplexed]] — Maimonides's *Thirteen Principles*: God is one in a strict philosophical sense (no composition, no plurality, no spatiality).

Christian Trinitarian theology generates the diagnostic philosophical-religious problem of *how* it counts as monotheism (the Athanasian and Cappadocian solutions); Islamic and Jewish polemics against Christianity have classically focused here.
