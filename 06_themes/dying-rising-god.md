# Dying and Rising God

A deity who dies and is restored to life, typically tied to seasonal vegetation cycles, fertility, or initiatory mystery cults. The category was coined by Frazer (1890) but has been heavily contested.

## Candidates traditionally listed
- **[[osiris]]** (Egyptian) — killed by Set, body dismembered, reassembled and revivified by Isis. Reigns in the afterlife rather than returning to earth.
- **[[dumuzi-tammuz]]** (Sumerian/Akkadian) — descends to underworld; Inanna's Descent.
- **[[baal]]** (Ugaritic) — defeated and swallowed by Mot; revived. See [[phase-1-013-baal-cycle]].
- **[[adonis]]** (Phoenician/Greek) — annual death and lamentation.
- **[[attis]]** (Phrygian) — castration and death; ambiguous resurrection.
- **[[dionysus]]** (Greek) — Orphic dismemberment and rebirth.

## The Frazer–Smith debate
- **Frazer (1890):** Universal "dying-and-rising god" archetype; Christ fits the pattern.
- **Smith (1987):** No clear case fits both "dying" AND "rising" in pre-Christian sources; category is a 19th-century construct projecting Christian theology back.
- **Mettinger (2001):** Smith overstates; Baal, Melqart, and Adonis genuinely die and return.
- **Current consensus:** The category is real but narrower than Frazer claimed; explicit "resurrection" language in pre-Christian sources is rarer than once assumed.

## Connection to Christian theology
A perennially debated link. Note that direct-borrowing claims (e.g., "Jesus is just Osiris/Mithras renamed") require Tier 1 evidence and rarely find it; structural-parallel claims are stronger but disputed in mechanism.
