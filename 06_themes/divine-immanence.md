# Divine Immanence

The doctrinal pole that emphasizes God's *presence within* the created order — within nature, within the human soul, within history — as the counter-pole to [[hidden-god]] divine transcendence. The pair is structural rather than alternative: most major theological systems negotiate the immanence/transcendence balance.

- **Stoic-pantheist** — [[phase-3-005-stoic-foundational-texts]]: the *logos* pervades all things; the cosmos is the body of God.
- **Philonic-Wisdom** — [[phase-3-013-philo-of-alexandria]]: the transcendent God reaches the world through the immanent Logos / Sophia / Powers.
- **Upanishadic** — [[phase-2-012-brihadaranyaka-upanishad]]: the *antaryamin* (inner controller), the *atman* = *brahman* identification; Brhad. 3.7 makes God the inner agent of all natural processes.
- **Shekhinah / Kabbalah** — [[phase-5-027-sefer-ha-zohar]]: the lowest sefirah, the indwelling presence; the "Glory" of Ezekiel and the rabbinic *Shekhinah* tradition.
- **Eckhartian Gottesgeburt** — [[phase-5-028-meister-eckhart-sermons]]: the birth of God in the soul; the *Seelenfünklein* / spark of the soul as point of identity with God.

The tension is theologically productive. Strong immanentism risks pantheism (Bruno, Spinoza); strong transcendentalism risks deism or [[hidden-god]] inaccessibility. Most mystical traditions resolve the pair by *gradation* (degrees of divine presence) rather than choice.
