# Ecclesial Authority

The question of *who* in the Church has the right to teach, govern, ordain, excommunicate, and decide controversies. Four classical positions:

1. **Episcopal / apostolic succession** — bishops in lineal succession from the apostles hold collegial-magisterial authority. Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, Anglican.
2. **Papal-supreme** — the bishop of Rome holds final authority over the universal church (developed gradually through late antiquity and medieval period, defined dogmatically at Vatican I 1870).
3. **Presbyterian / synodal** — authority resides in colleges of teaching elders / synods of the gathered community. Reformed.
4. **Congregational** — each local congregation is autonomous; authority resides in the gathered believers. Baptist, congregational free-church tradition.

Cross-cutting issue: *Scripture and Tradition* — does Scripture alone (Reformation *sola Scriptura*) or Scripture-with-Tradition (Catholic, Orthodox) constitute the rule of faith? See [[sola-scriptura]].

- [[phase-3-015-pauline-epistles]] — Pauline apostolic-authority claims ("am I not free? am I not an apostle?" 1 Cor 9); the Pastoral Epistles' ordination theology (1 Tim, Titus).
- [[phase-4-023-irenaeus-against-heresies]] — Irenaeus's apostolic-succession argument against Gnostic claims: the public-traceable lineage of bishops is the criterion of authentic teaching.
- [[phase-6-005-luther-95-theses]] / [[phase-6-010-calvin-institutes]] — the Reformation challenge to medieval Catholic magisterium; the development of Reformed presbyterial governance.
