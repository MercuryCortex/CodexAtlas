# Passion Narrative

The stereotyped sequence — Last Supper, agony, arrest, trial(s) before Jewish council and Roman governor, scourging, crucifixion, burial — that closes all four canonical Gospels and circulated very early as a discrete unit. Brown's classic two-volume *Death of the Messiah* dissects all four narratives line by line. Mark's passion (Mk 14–15) is generally judged earliest; the other Synoptics depend on Mark; John's passion (Jn 18–19) is structured around its own distinctive Christology (Jesus in command of his fate). The non-canonical gospels (Gospel of Peter, Gospel of Mary) preserve alternative or expanded passion traditions.

- [[phase-3-016-gospel-of-mark]] — the urtext: a passion narrative with a long introduction (Kähler's quip about all four Gospels).
- [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — independent passion source highly likely; the dating-around-Passover and crucifixion-as-glorification framing.
- Crossan's controversial thesis: the passion narratives are "prophecy historicized" — Hebrew Bible passages (Ps 22, Isa 53, Zech) generated narrative details, rather than memory recording them. Brown and most others retain a core of historical memory plus heavy scriptural shaping.
