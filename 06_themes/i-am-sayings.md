# I-Am Sayings (egō eimi)

The Johannine pattern in which Jesus speaks self-predications using the Greek formula *egō eimi* ("I am") — sometimes with predicate ("I am the bread of life," "the light of the world," "the good shepherd," "the resurrection and the life," "the way, the truth, and the life," "the true vine"; seven predicated I-AMs), sometimes absolute ("Before Abraham was, I am" Jn 8:58; "I am he" Jn 18:5–6 where the arresting party falls down). The absolute usage echoes the Septuagint of Isaiah 40–55 (LXX *egō eimi* renders Hebrew *ʾănî hû* and *ʾanōkî*), where YHWH self-identifies — making the Johannine usage a Christological appropriation of the divine name.

- [[phase-3-020-gospel-of-john]] — the seven predicated I-AMs structure the discourse material; the absolute uses occur at climactic moments.
- [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]] — the prophetic-monotheistic *ʾănî hû* / *egō eimi* formula (Isa 41:4, 43:10–13, 46:4, 48:12).
- Williams 2000 traced the *ʾănî hû* formula through Targumic, Qumran, and rabbinic Jewish usage, demonstrating that the formula was a recognized divine-self-predication in Second Temple Judaism.
