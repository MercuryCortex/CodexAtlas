# Fifth Empire (Quinto Império)

## Definition

The ***Quinto Império*** ("Fifth Empire") is the Portuguese-Catholic eschatological-political doctrine holding that **Portugal is the prophesied fifth-and-final world-empire** — succeeding the four kingdoms of Daniel 2 (Babylon → Persia → Greece → Rome) and inaugurating a universal-Christian-spiritual empire of global reach and end-of-history character. Distinguished from imperialist-territorial readings: the Fifth Empire is principally **a spiritual empire of universal Christianization** rather than a project of military-territorial conquest, though Portuguese maritime-expansion-and-Christianization (the Age of Discoveries) is understood as its institutional-historical vehicle.

The doctrine emerges in **mid-16th-century vernacular Portuguese prophecy** ([[goncalo-annes-bandarra|Bandarra]]'s [[phase-6-040-bandarra-trovas|*Trovas*]], c. 1530s), receives systematic theological articulation in the **late-17th-century Jesuit synthesis** of António Vieira (*História do Futuro*, composed 1640s–1660s, published posthumously 1718–1720; *Clavis Prophetarum*, composed 1680s–1690s), and is **modernist-poetically recovered** by Fernando Pessoa's [[phase-7-037-pessoa-mensagem|*Mensagem*]] (1934). It is the **central political-theological organizing doctrine of Portuguese imperial-eschatological self-understanding** across approximately 500 years.

The doctrine **draws on three structural sources** that converge in 16th-c. Iberia:

1. **Daniel 2 + Daniel 7** (Old Testament prophetic apocalyptic): the four-kingdoms schema (Babylon-Persia-Greece-Rome — though Daniel 2's original referents differ from later Christian-typological readings); the Son-of-Man-receiving-everlasting-kingdom prophecy in Daniel 7. The structural source supplying the *four → fifth* schema.
2. **Joachim of Fiore's tripartite Status schema** (Calabrian Cistercian abbot, 1135–1202): the world moves through three *Status* — Father (Old Testament), Son (institutional Church, c. 33–1260 CE per Joachim), and Spirit (the coming age of universal monastic-spiritual freedom). Joachimist Latin-Christian apocalyptic-theology enters Iberian-monastic circulation from the 13th century onward. *Joachim of Fiore is not yet stubbed in this vault — open gap.*
3. **Iberian Jewish-converso messianism**: the *Mashiach ben David* expectation, refracted through 15th–16th-c. Iberian-converso typological vocabulary, providing the substrate for the messianic-king-figure that crystallizes as the Sebastianist *Encoberto* after 1578.

## Earliest attestations

- **Old Testament structural source.** Daniel 2 (composed in its present form c. 165 BCE during the Maccabean crisis; the four-kingdoms vision); Daniel 7 (same period; the four beasts and the Son of Man). See [[phase-3-008-book-of-daniel]].
- **Joachim of Fiore.** *Liber Concordiae* (c. 1183–1196), *Expositio in Apocalypsim* (c. 1196–1199), *Psalterium decem chordarum* (c. 1184). The tripartite-Status doctrine; intense reception in 13th-c. Franciscan circles (Spiritual Franciscans), enters Iberian-monastic circulation. *Not yet stubbed.*
- **Bandarra, *Trovas*** (c. 1530s — vernacular Portuguese): the principal extant 16th-c. Portuguese articulation of the *Encoberto* / Fifth-Empire complex. See [[phase-6-040-bandarra-trovas]].
- **António Vieira, *História do Futuro*** (composed 1640s–1660s, published 1718–1720) and ***Clavis Prophetarum*** (composed 1680s–1690s, full integral edition 2000 INCM): the systematic theological synthesis. Vieira treats Bandarra as one of his three principal predictive sources alongside Daniel and Isaiah. *Vieira's works not yet stubbed.*
- **Pessoa, *Mensagem*** (1934): the 20th-c. modernist literary consolidation. Section III, *O Encoberto*, includes specific poems on Bandarra, on Sebastian, on the Fifth Empire ("Quinto Império") as a named entity. See [[phase-7-037-pessoa-mensagem]].

## Variants

How does the doctrine morph across its 500-year span?

- **Vernacular-prophetic (Bandarra, c. 1530s):** the doctrine is *poetic-typological-allegorical*, focused on the *Encoberto* figure and the cosmic-restoration moment. No systematic theology; the typology does the work.
- **Iberian-Union-resistance (1580–1640):** the doctrine becomes *political-resistance vocabulary* under Habsburg rule; vehicle of Portuguese-national-identity preservation. Vehicle for *Cristão-Novo* converso-Jewish messianic expectation under inquisitorial pressure.
- **Vieira's systematic theology (1640s–1690s):** the doctrine becomes *Jesuit-theological systematization* — Vieira's *História do Futuro* and *Clavis Prophetarum* integrate Bandarra + Daniel + Joachim + Isaiah into a coherent Christian-Catholic-Portuguese-imperial eschatology. Vieira's missions to Brazil and his defence of the *Cristãos-Novos* are the practical-political extensions of the doctrine.
- **18th-c. baroque-popular continuation:** the doctrine survives in folk-prophetic pamphlets, popular Sebastianism, and the rural-religious imagination, while losing its acute political urgency as Bourbon-style royal-absolutism stabilizes.
- **19th-c. Romantic-nationalist recovery (1850–1910):** Garrett, Herculano, Antero de Quental, Oliveira Martins, the Geração de 70 — treat the Fifth Empire as foundational national myth, increasingly read as cultural-poetic structure rather than predictive theology.
- **Pessoan modernist re-presentation (1934):** Pessoa's *Mensagem* recovers the Fifth Empire as modernist-poetic *and* spiritual-political project; the ambiguity is constitutive (see [[phase-7-037-pessoa-mensagem]] §Disputes).
- **20th-c. esoteric-Portuguese continuator-scholarship:** Gandra, Centeno, the Portuguese hermetic-symbology circle. Treat the Fifth Empire as living spiritual-political tradition with continuous contemporary purchase. See [[tradition-portuguese-hermeticism]].
- **Brazilian transplantation:** Antônio Conselheiro's Canudos (1893–1897); Brazilian *messianismo sertanejo* (northeastern peasant millenarianism); the broader Lusophone-Atlantic messianic-prophetic continuation. Euclides da Cunha's *Os Sertões* (1902) is the canonical literary chronicle.

## Connections claimed

- **Daniel-2 + Joachimist + Iberian-Jewish-converso convergence (16th c.):** the doctrine emerges from the **convergence of three pre-existing apocalyptic-eschatological traditions** that 16th-c. Iberia happens to bring into a single vernacular-poetic frame in Bandarra's *Trovas*. The convergence is documentable (Van den Besselaar 1976, Cohen 1998, Valdez 2010, Gandra 1999).
- **Vieira's systematization is the principal scholarly-theological synthesis** (Cohen 1998, Marques 2017). Without Vieira, the doctrine would have remained vernacular-folk-prophetic; with Vieira, it becomes a sustained Jesuit-Catholic theological position with global-mission implications.
- **Pessoa's reception is the 20th-c. literary canonization** (Zenith 2021, Centeno 1985, Mendia 2023). Without Pessoa, the doctrine would have remained largely 17th-c. theological and 19th-c. romantic recovery; with Pessoa, it enters the 20th-c. modernist canon and acquires international literary visibility.
- **Gandra's contemporary systematization** (1999, 2009, 2015) is the principal 21st-c. continuator-scholarly project. *Joaquim de Fiore: Joaquimismo e Esperança Sebástica* (1999) is specifically focused on the Joachimist-Bandarra linkage. See [[tradition-portuguese-hermeticism]].

## Disputes

- **Was the Fifth Empire doctrine ever politically operative, or always cultural-symbolic?** The 1640 Restoration mobilization invoked Sebastianist-Fifth-Empire prophecy in political-propaganda mode; the 1893–1897 Brazilian Canudos movement organized politically around the doctrine. So: *yes, sometimes politically operative*; but more commonly *cultural-symbolic*.
- **Is the Salazar Estado Novo's invocation of Fifth-Empire imagery (in 1934 Pessoa-prize submission context) a faithful continuation of the tradition or a propagandistic appropriation?** Contested. The Salazarist regime drew on Sebastianist-Fifth-Empire vocabulary for its imperial-Catholic ideological framing; Pessoa's *Mensagem* won an Estado-Novo prize in 1934. Whether this constitutes Pessoa's endorsement of Salazarist ideology, his strategic use of the regime, or a more complex relationship is one of the central Pessoa-scholarship disputes (see [[phase-7-037-pessoa-mensagem]] §Disputes).
- **Is "Fifth Empire" a distinctly Portuguese doctrine, or a general Iberian-Catholic-eschatological phenomenon?** Mainstream consensus (Cohen 1998, Valdez 2010): distinctly Portuguese in its mature 17th-c. Vieira-systematized form, while drawing on shared Iberian sources (the Spanish *Monarquía Hispánica* eschatological literature has parallel features; the Portuguese version is differentiated by its post-1578-Sebastianist trauma-anchoring and by Vieira's specific Brazilian-mission emphasis).

## MASSIVE WIN: 800-year continuous prophetic-political theology chain

Joachim of Fiore (Calabrian Cistercian, 1135–1202) → [[goncalo-annes-bandarra|Bandarra]] *Trovas* (c. 1530s) → Vieira *História do Futuro* (1640s–1660s) → [[fernando-pessoa|Pessoa]] [[phase-7-037-pessoa-mensagem|*Mensagem*]] (1934). **One of the longest continuous prophetic-political theology chains in Western history** — Calabrian Cistercian Trinitarian-Status apocalyptic schema running through 4 centuries of Portuguese Sebastianist messianism into a 20th-c. modernist national epic. The political-theological spine of [[tradition-portuguese-hermeticism|Portuguese Hermeticism]]. Each link in the chain is documented (no transmission speculation): Joachim's tripartite *Status* enters Iberian-monastic circulation in the 13th c.; Bandarra structurally inherits the schema in his vernacular *Trovas*; Vieira's Jesuit synthesis explicitly cites Bandarra alongside Daniel and Isaiah as his three predictive sources; Pessoa includes a specific "Bandarra" poem in *Mensagem* Section III. The chain spans pre-Reformation medieval apocalyptic, Iberian Counter-Reformation Jesuit theology, and Portuguese modernist literary recovery — three substantially different religious-cultural moments held together by one structural-doctrinal substrate.
