# New-Moses Typology

The literary-theological pattern in which a later figure (eschatological prophet, messiah, founder) is depicted as a *new Moses* who repeats and surpasses the structure of the Mosaic career. The most thoroughgoing New-Testament instance is Matthew, where Allison 1993 documents some dozen Mosaic typological parallels: birth threatened by a wicked king who kills infants (Mt 2 / Ex 1); descent to and return from Egypt (Mt 2 / Ex 12); passing through water at the start of the public career (Mt 3 baptism / Ex 14 sea-crossing); 40 days of testing in the wilderness (Mt 4 / Ex/Num 40 years); the Sermon on the Mount (Mt 5–7 / Sinai); the Transfiguration (Mt 17, with Moses present); the Great Commission from a mountain (Mt 28 / Deut 34).

- [[phase-3-017-gospel-of-matthew]] — Allison's prime exhibit; the Matthean Christology is *programmatically* Mosaic.
- [[phase-3-001-second-third-isaiah]] — Deutero-Isaiah's "new exodus" theme is the Hebrew Bible's own internal new-Moses move: the return from Babylon is figured as a new wilderness crossing led by YHWH.
- Adjacent: Hebrews develops a different new-Moses-but-greater-than-Moses Christology (Heb 3:1–6); John's "the law was given through Moses, grace and truth came through Jesus Christ" (Jn 1:17) frames Christ as Moses' eschatological counterpart.

The Mosaic typology is widespread in later sectarian and prophetic movements (Joseph Smith as new Moses; multiple messianic claimants in Second Temple Judaism per Josephus) — wherever a founder needs to claim Sinai-level revelatory warrant.
