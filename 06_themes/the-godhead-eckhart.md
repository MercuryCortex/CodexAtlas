# The Godhead (Eckhart's *Gottheit*)

## Definition

Meister Eckhart's distinctive theological move is a distinction *inside* God: between **`got`** (God — the Trinitarian God in relation to creatures, the God of religion, prayer and Persons) and **`gotheit`** (the Godhead — the naked divine ground out of which the Persons emerge and into which they "boil back"). The *gotheit* is *abgrunt* (groundless ground, abyss), *wüeste* (desert, wilderness), *stille* (still): beyond name, beyond personality, beyond the distinction of Being and Non-Being, and beyond the Trinitarian relations themselves. In Eckhart's most compressed formulation, *God becomes and unbecomes* — "God" is correlative to creatures and so *emerges* when creation emerges, while the Godhead is what God is in himself prior to and beyond every relation.

The soul has a corresponding term: the *vünkelîn* / *funke der sêle* (the little spark of the soul; Latin *scintilla animae*), the *grunt* that is not created and cannot be created. Eckhart's whole practical programme — *abegescheidenheit* (detachment), *gelâzenheit* (releasement), *durchbrechen* (breakthrough) — is the stripping of images, including images of God, until the soul's ground and God's ground are recognized as one ground. Sermon 52, on *Beati pauperes spiritu*, is the notorious statement of the programme: **"I pray to God that he may make me free of God."** The prayer is not impiety; it is the request to be released from *got* — the God who exists in correlation to a praying creature — into the *gotheit*, where that correlation, and with it the creature, has no purchase.

This node holds the **doctrine**. The biography, the Cologne trial and the reception history of the man live on [[meister-eckhart]]; the wider Christian negative-theology tradition lives on [[theme-apophatic-theology]], [[divine-darkness]] and [[via-negativa]]; the cross-tradition realization it converges with lives on [[non-duality]].

## The genealogy — what is documented transmission

The Godhead doctrine is not a bolt from the blue. Its components arrive through a **documented chain**, and these edges are typed as Transmission:

- **Plotinus → the One beyond being.** [[phase-4-019-plotinus-enneads]] (V.1, V.3, VI.9) establishes the philosophical form: an absolute "beyond multiplicity, beyond being and not-being" of which no predicate is true. [[the-one-plotinus]] is the direct structural ancestor of the *gotheit*.
- **Proclus and the Arabic *Liber de causis*.** [[proclus]]'s *Elements of Theology*, and the Arabic Proclean compilation known in the Latin West as the *Liber de causis*, are cited by name in Eckhart's Latin commentaries. This is the route by which a late-antique pagan metaphysics of the ineffable First reaches a 14th-century Dominican lectern.
- **Pseudo-Dionysius.** [[phase-4-030-pseudo-dionysius]] / [[pseudo-dionysius-the-areopagite]] supplies the apophatic *apparatus* — the *Mystical Theology*'s ascent by negation into the "super-essential darkness," and the rule that the negations are truer than the affirmations. Eckhart cites Dionysius constantly.
- **Eriugena.** [[john-scotus-eriugena]] translated the Dionysian corpus into Latin in the 9th century and, in [[phase-5-008-eriugena-periphyseon]], divided *natura* into four: creating-and-uncreated, creating-and-created, created-and-not-creating, and neither-creating-nor-created. The **fourth** division — God as the term into which all things return, no longer creating and no longer distinguished — is the closest Latin antecedent to the *gotheit*. The *Periphyseon* was condemned in 1225, **a century before Eckhart**, and nonetheless circulated widely, partly through the *Clavis physicae* excerpts.
- **Albertus Magnus and the German Dominican school.** [[albertus-magnus]]'s Neoplatonizing reading of Dionysius is the immediate school context; Eckhart is a Dominican working inside a Cologne tradition that had already fused Aristotelian technique with Dionysian metaphysics.
- **Maimonides.** Eckhart cites [[maimonides]] as "Rabbi Moyses" throughout the Latin commentaries, above all in the *Expositio libri Exodi* on the divine name. The doctrine of negative attributes in [[phase-5-019-maimonides-guide-for-the-perplexed]] is a **real cross-tradition transmission** into Latin Christian negative theology — one of the cleanest Jewish-to-Christian philosophical wires in this period.
- **The Beguines.** [[mechthild-of-magdeburg]]'s *Das fliessende Licht der Gottheit* (c. 1250–1280) already uses *Gottheit* in the vernacular for the essence-beyond-distinction, and [[marguerite-porete]]'s *Mirror of Simple Souls* (c. 1290–1300; Porete burned in Paris 1310) already teaches the soul's annihilation into God. Eckhart was in Paris in 1311–1313, immediately after Porete's execution and the Vienne decrees. McGinn 1998 and Hollywood 1995 establish the vernacular-women's precedent; the conceptual continuity is strong even without a citation from Eckhart naming her.

**Downstream, also documented:** [[johannes-tauler]] and [[henry-suso]] carry the doctrine forward in pastorally moderated language that stays clear of the 1329 articles; the anonymous *Theologia Deutsch* (14th–15th c.), saturated in Eckhartian vocabulary, is edited and published by [[martin-luther]] in 1516 and 1518; Nicholas of Cusa owns and annotates Eckhart's Latin works and defends him ([[phase-5-021-cusa-de-docta-ignorantia]] and the 1449 *Apologia*); the German speculative-mystical stream reaches [[jakob-boehme]]; Franz von Baader's 19th-century enthusiasm carries Eckhart to Hegel and Schelling; and [[paul-tillich]]'s "God above the God of theism" (*The Courage to Be*, 1952) is the doctrine's explicit 20th-century restatement — see [[ground-of-being]].

**The disanalogy that matters most.** The Eckhart–*śūnyatā* pairing is the one the perennialist literature leans on hardest and the one that breaks first under pressure. Eckhart's *gotheit* is an *absolute*: it is what God is in himself, superabundant rather than absent. Madhyamaka *śūnyatā* is the dismantling of substantial existence *as such* and explicitly posits no absolute behind the dismantling. The two share an apophatic **grammar** — the self-cancelling sentence, the negation of the negation — without sharing a metaphysics. Sells 1994 and Turner 1995 are the two treatments that hold this line most carefully; Suzuki 1957 does not.

## MASSIVE-WIN — the Maimonidean wire

Amid a set of resemblances that are mostly convergent, one cross-tradition link on this node is a genuine **documented transmission** and deserves to be named as such: Eckhart's use of [[maimonides]].

The *Guide for the Perplexed*, written in Judeo-Arabic in Cairo in the 1190s and circulating in Latin as the *Dux neutrorum* from the 1220s, argues that no positive attribute can be predicated of God — that to say "God is wise" is only to deny ignorance of him, and that the divine names are all, properly read, negations. Eckhart quotes this doctrine, by name, in his Latin scriptural commentaries, and the condemned proposition that God is "neither good nor better nor best" is its Rhineland pulpit form. The chain is therefore: **Islamic-world Aristotelian-Neoplatonic philosophy → Maimonides (Jewish, Cairo, Arabic) → Latin translation → Paris and Cologne → Eckhart's German sermons → the Reformation-era *Theologia Deutsch* → Böhme, Hegel, Tillich.** It is a Muslim-Jewish-Christian relay running for a millennium, and it is documented at every link — the same shape as the [[hermetic-transmission-chain]] and the Aristotle-to-Aquinas relay, in a register (negative theology) where cross-tradition dependence is usually assumed to be impossible.

## The condemnation — and why "posthumous" is not a technicality

Archbishop Heinrich von Virneburg opened an inquisitorial process at Cologne around 1326. Eckhart appealed to the papal court, submitted his teaching to the Church's judgment, denied any heretical intent, went to Avignon, and **died there in early 1328, before any verdict**. Pope John XXII's bull *In agro dominico* was issued on **27 March 1329 — roughly a year after Eckhart's death** — condemning twenty-eight articles: seventeen as heretical, eleven as "evil-sounding, rash, and suspect of heresy." The bull itself records that Eckhart, at the end of his life, had professed the Catholic faith and revoked whatever in the articles could bear a heretical sense.

The propositions nearest to this node are exactly the *gotheit* propositions: that there is *something in the soul that is uncreated and uncreatable*, and that *God is neither good nor better nor best* — the two doctrines, one anthropological and one apophatic, that the got/gotheit distinction requires.

The posthumous character is load-bearing for two reasons. First, **the vault has recorded this wrong before**: Eckhart was not condemned in his lifetime, was never burned, and never refused to submit. Second, it makes his case structurally *unlike* the deaths it is casually compared with. [[al-hallaj]] refused to retract *anā al-Ḥaqq* and was executed in Baghdad in 922 ([[event-execution-of-al-hallaj-922]]); [[marguerite-porete]] refused to answer her judges and was burned in Paris in 1310; [[giordano-bruno]] refused recantation and was burned in Rome in 1600. Eckhart submitted, died of ordinary causes, and was condemned in absentia and posthumously. The comparison worth making is about the recurring **institutional response to identity-language**, not about a shared martyrdom — see [[executed-divine-claimant]], which should be read with that qualification in place.

## Disputes

- **Was Eckhart a heretic?** The 1329 articles bite: they are drawn from things he really said. Modern scholarship (McGinn, Davies, Schürmann, Turner) generally reads him as a creative and pastorally bold mystical theologian, orthodox in intent, whose deliberately destabilizing vernacular hyperbole invited a literal reading it could not survive. He has never been formally rehabilitated; he is nonetheless routinely cited in modern Catholic mystical theology, and the German Dominican Province formally reaffirmed his place in its tradition in 1992.
- **The two Eckharts.** The Latin scholastic Eckhart (the *Opus tripartitum*, the scriptural commentaries) is technical, metaphysical and defensible; the German vernacular Eckhart (the *Predigten*) is pastoral, paradoxical, and built to destabilize. Whether the Latin carries the doctrine and the German is homiletic stretching, or the reverse, divides interpreters and largely determines how radical a reading of the *gotheit* one ends up with.
- **Is the Gottheit an experience or a grammar?** Turner 1995 argues that medieval apophaticism is a **discipline of language** — a rule for how to speak, and stop speaking, about God — rather than a report of a mystical state, and that the modern habit of reading Eckhart as describing an experience is an anachronism imported from 19th- and 20th-century psychology of religion. This is the sharpest live methodological dispute on the node, and it cuts against most popular Eckhart reception.
- **Comparative-religion overreach.** "Eckhart is essentially Buddhist / Vedantic" — Suzuki's claim, and Otto's before him in a milder form — is now broadly considered exaggerated. Eckhart is a *Trinitarian Christian* Neoplatonist whose apophaticism converges with non-Christian non-dualisms at specific rhetorical and doctrinal limit-points, not all the way through. Sedgwick 2004 documents how much of the 20th-century "universal Eckhart" was perennialist construction rather than discovery.
- **How much Eriugena did Eckhart actually read?** That the *gotheit* stands in the Eriugenian line is not disputed; whether Eckhart read the *Periphyseon* directly, or received it through the *Clavis physicae* tradition, Albert, and the Dionysian commentaries, is not settled. The node states the antecedent, not a reading-list.
