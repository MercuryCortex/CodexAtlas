# The Godhead (Eckhart's *Gottheit*)

Meister Eckhart's distinctive theological move: distinguishing between *got* (God, the trinitarian God in relation to creatures, the personal God of religion) and *gotheit* (Godhead, the *naked* divine ground from which Persons emerge and into which they "boil back"). The *gotheit* is *abgrunt* (groundless ground), *wüeste* (desert / wilderness), beyond name, beyond personality, even beyond the distinction Being / Non-Being. In Eckhart's metaphor: "God becomes and unbecomes" — *God* is correlative to creatures and so emerges when creation emerges; the *Godhead* is what *God* is in himself prior to and beyond all relation. The distinction is the limit-case of apophatic theology in the Christian West; it inherited Eckhart's posthumous condemnation (Pope John XXII, 1329) and shaped subsequent German mysticism, Reformation radical theology, German Idealism (Hegel, Schelling), and 20th-century theology (Tillich's "God beyond God").

## Manifestations
- **Eckhart's sermons:** [[phase-5-028-meister-eckhart-sermons]] — the German vernacular sermons (Predigten) are the most concentrated locus. Sermon 52 ("Beati pauperes spiritu"): "I pray to God that he may make me free of God." Sermon 48: the *spark of the soul* (*funke der sêle*, *scintilla animae*) breaks through created being and divine being into the *abgrunt* of the Godhead. The most theologically vertiginous sermons in medieval Christianity.
- **Apophatic patristic background:** [[phase-4-030-pseudo-dionysius]] — Pseudo-Dionysius's *Mystical Theology* gives the rhetorical-philosophical apophatic apparatus Eckhart inherits via Latin translation (John Scotus Eriugena, Sarracenus). The "super-essential darkness" of Dionysian theology is the patristic seed of the Eckhartian *Gottheit*.
- **Eriugena:** [[phase-5-008-eriugena-periphyseon]] — *Periphyseon* (9th c.) divides *natura* into "creating-uncreated / creating-created / created-not-creating / neither-creating-nor-created"; the fourth division is essentially the Eckhartian *Gottheit*: God as the *abgrunt* into which creatures return. Condemned 1225 (after Eckhart) but circulating widely.
- **The Rhineland mystics:** [[phase-5-031-cloud-of-unknowing]] — Eckhart's pupils (Tauler, Suso) propagate the doctrine in more pastorally moderated form; the English *Cloud of Unknowing* (late 14th c.) is a kindred apophatic text accessible to non-clerical audiences.
- **Posthumous reception.** Condemnation 1329; loss of his German sermons for centuries; recovery by Franz Pfeiffer (1857) and the modern critical edition (1936ff.); Hegelian and post-Hegelian theologians (Hegel himself: "Eckhart said it") rediscover him as a proto-modern. Tillich's "God above the God of theism" (1952 *Courage to Be*) is explicitly Eckhartian.
- **Comparative reach.** Eckhart's *Gottheit* is the most-frequently-cited Christian-mystical analog of Vedantic *nirguṇa* Brahman, Mahayana *śūnyatā*, and Sufi *al-ḥaqq* — a major reference point in [[non-duality]] and [[perennial-philosophy]] comparativism. Suzuki and Coomaraswamy invoked Eckhart constantly; Sedgwick and Hanegraaff document how 20th-c. Eckhart-reception was partly perennialist construction.

## Disputes
- **Was Eckhart a "heretic"?** The 1329 condemnation (28 propositions; 17 heretical, 11 ill-sounding) bites; modern scholarship (McGinn, Davies, Schürmann) generally reads Eckhart as a creative, orthodox-leaning, pastorally-bold mystical theologian whose hyperbole and vernacular pulpit-style invited misreading. Catholic rehabilitation has been gradual; Eckhart was never formally rehabilitated but is widely cited in modern Catholic mystical theology.
- **The two Eckharts.** The Latin scholastic Eckhart (commentaries, *Opus Tripartitum*) and the German vernacular Eckhart (sermons) read differently: the Latin is technical and metaphysical; the German is pastoral, paradoxical, deliberately destabilizing. Whether to read the Latin as the doctrinal substance and the German as homiletic stretching, or vice versa, divides interpreters.
- **Comparative-religion overreach.** "Eckhart is essentially Buddhist / Vedantic" — Suzuki's claim — is now broadly considered exaggerated. Eckhart is a *Trinitarian Christian* Neoplatonist whose apophaticism converges with non-Christian non-dualisms at certain rhetorical-doctrinal limit-points, not at all the way through.
