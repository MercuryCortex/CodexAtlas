# Hagiography

## Definition
**Hagiography** (Greek *hagiographia* "writing about the holy/saint") is the cross-tradition literary-religious genre of saints' lives, martyrologies, and exemplary-figure narratives composed to commemorate and to instruct. The genre is not historiography in the modern sense but a distinct *form of religious truth-telling* with its own conventions: the subject's birth-omens, education, miracles, ascetic-or-martyric ordeal, miracles in life and death, postmortem cult, and (in Christian traditions) a typological structure that maps the subject's life onto Christ's. A passage "has" the hagiography theme if it operates as exemplary religious biography in this generic sense.

## Earliest attestations by tradition

- **Greco-Roman pagan**: Plutarch's *Lives* (~+100) are an *exemplary-biography* genre that is the structural ancestor of Christian hagiography; Philostratus's *Life of Apollonius* (~3rd c.) is the pagan-religious parallel to early Christian saints' lives.
- **Christian (Western Latin)**: [[phase-4-036-passio-perpetuae|*Passio Perpetuae et Felicitatis*]] (~203) — early martyrology; Sulpicius Severus's *Life of Martin of Tours* (~397).
- **Christian (Eastern)**: [[phase-4-067-martyrdom-of-polycarp]] (~155); Athanasius's [[phase-4-041-athanasius-life-of-antony|*Life of Antony*]] (~360) — the foundational monastic hagiography, translated into Latin by Evagrius of Antioch ~370 and read across the whole Christian world; [[phase-4-043-apophthegmata-patrum]] (*Sayings of the Desert Fathers*).
- **Christian (Syriac)**: John of Ephesus's *Lives of the Eastern Saints* (~566-588) including the [[jacob-baradaeus|Jacob Baradaeus]] *Life*.
- **Christian (Coptic + Ethiopic)**: Besa's *Life of [[shenoute|Shenoute]]*; the Coptic *Synaxarium* and Ethiopian *Synaxarium* / [[phase-5-039-sinkessar-synaxarium|Sinkessar]]; the *gadla* (struggle-narrative) genre — [[phase-7-040-walatta-petros-hagiography]] (1672) is a major late example.
- **Christian (Latin medieval)**: [[phase-4-052-gregory-dialogues|Gregory the Great's *Dialogues*]] (~593-594); Jacobus de Voragine's *Legenda Aurea* (~1260).
- **Buddhist**: the *Avadāna* genre (Sanskrit hagiographic-karmic-biographical narratives, ~-100 forward); Huijiao's *Gaoseng Zhuan* (*Lives of Eminent Monks*, 6th c.) — Chinese Buddhist hagiographic compilation; Tibetan *rnam-thar* genre.
- **Islamic**: the *sīra* genre ([[phase-5-043-ibn-ishaq-sirat-rasul-allah|Ibn Isḥāq's *Sīrat Rasūl Allāh*]] ~770) treats the Prophet's life with hagiographic conventions; Ṣūfī *manāqib* (saints' lives, e.g., al-Hujwīrī's *Kashf al-Maḥjūb* 11th c.; Aflākī's *Manāqib al-ʿĀrifīn* on Rūmī, 14th c.).
- **Hindu (bhakti)**: *Bhaktamāla* genre (collections of devotee-saint lives, e.g., Nabhadas's *Bhaktamāla* ~1600); Caitanya hagiographic literature (e.g., *Caitanya-caritāmṛta* of Kṛṣṇadāsa Kavirāja).
- **Jewish**: rabbinic Aggadic hagiographic-style narratives in the Mishnah and Talmud; later Hasidic *shevḥei* (praise-tales) — e.g., *Shivḥei ha-Besht* on the Baal Shem Tov (1814).

## Variants
- **Martyrology**: subset focused on persecution-death; martyric-witness conventions distinct from monastic / ascetic / mystic-saint conventions.
- **Synaxarial / calendar-hagiography**: brief notices keyed to the liturgical calendar (Coptic + Ethiopian + Byzantine + Latin all have synaxaria).
- ***Gadla* / "struggle-narrative"**: distinctively Ethiopian Tewahedo subgenre — the *gadla* form of life-narrative (e.g., the Walatta Petros *Gadla*).
- ***Sīra* / *manāqib***: Islamic biographical / saintly-praise genres.
- ***Rnam-thar***: Tibetan Buddhist "complete-liberation-narrative" hagiography.
- ***Avadāna***: Sanskrit Buddhist karmic-biographical narrative.

## Connections claimed
- **Cross-tradition genre transmission**: Plutarch *Lives* → Athanasius *Life of Antony* is a substantive generic transmission (Brown 1981; Cox 1983).
- **Christian hagiography → Islamic sīra/manāqib**: substantial structural overlap, though the precise transmission is debated.
- **Buddhist *avadāna* + *jātaka* → Christian *Barlaam and Josaphat*** (see [[barlaam-and-josaphat]]) — the most consequential Buddhist→Christian hagiographic transmission edge ever traced; the *Barlaam* legend is a Christianized recension of the [[siddhartha-gautama-buddha|Buddha's]] biography.
