# Zurvanism (Zurvanite Heresy)

## The Theological Problem Zurvanism Solves

Orthodox Zoroastrianism posits two co-equal primordial principles: Ahura Mazda (the Wise Lord, all-good) and Angra Mainyu (the Destructive Spirit, all-evil). Both are uncreated. They exist in eternal opposition from which the cosmos emerges as a bounded arena for their final resolution.

But this generates a **theological problem**: if Ahura Mazda is wholly good and is the supreme creative power, why does Angra Mainyu exist at all? Who made the evil principle? The orthodox answer — both are simply uncreated, co-eternal, and co-originary — was theologically uncomfortable for a tradition that wanted to assert Ahura Mazda's ultimate supremacy. If Ahura Mazda did not create Angra Mainyu, does that mean there is something beyond Ahura Mazda? 

**Zurvanism** is the response: both Ahura Mazda and Angra Mainyu were begotten by a higher, encompassing principle — **Zurvan** (Avestan: *Zrvan*, Middle Persian: *Zurwān*), meaning "infinite time." Zurvan is the father of both gods; the cosmic war is a conflict between twin brothers, not co-eternal first principles.

## The Zurvanite Myth

The central Zurvanite narrative (preserved in Syriac and Armenian sources, notably Eznik of Kolb's *Against the Sects*, c. 435 CE — hostile but detailed) runs as follows:

Zurvan, the god of infinite time, performed sacrifices for a thousand years desiring a son who would be Ahura Mazda, the creator of the world. After a thousand years he doubted whether his sacrifice would bear fruit. In that moment of doubt, **two conceptions took place simultaneously**: from his faith was conceived Ahura Mazda; from his doubt was conceived Angra Mainyu. Zurvan vowed that the first-born would rule the world. Angra Mainyu, hearing this in the womb, ripped himself out prematurely and presented himself to Zurvan, who was obligated to grant him dominion — but only for a bounded period (9,000 years). After that term, Ahura Mazda would rule eternally.

The myth thus solves two problems at once:
1. The **origin of evil** — Angra Mainyu is born from Zurvan's *doubt*, not from any creative will. Evil is an unintended consequence of a momentary lapse in the mind of the supreme principle.
2. The **theodicy of present evil** — evil has legitimate (if temporary) dominion because of a binding vow. The world is not broken; it is running its allotted course.

## Zurvan as Infinite Time / Infinite Space

Zurvan appears in two distinct forms in the sources:
- **Zurvan Akarana** ("Infinite Time") — time without beginning or end; the temporal absolute from which bounded time (the 12,000-year cosmic cycle) is extracted.
- **Zurvan Daregōkhvadhāy** ("Zurvan of Long Dominion") — the temporal lord presiding over the long cycles.

The concept of an absolute time-deity who encompasses and transcends the good/evil dualism has significant parallels in other systems:
- **Greek Aion** (αἰών, "age / eternal time") — a deity or concept of boundless time in Hellenistic religion, associated with Chronos (not to be confused with Kronos). Aion appears in Neoplatonic and Mithraic contexts as a lion-headed time-god, possibly under Zurvanite influence.
- **Platonic Kronos** — the Demiurge in the *Timaeus* creates time (*chronos*) as a moving image of eternity (*aiōn*); structurally comparable to Zurvan as the principle from which bounded creation emerges.
- **Stoic pneuma/logos** — the all-pervading rational principle that encompasses the material cycle; structurally adjacent to Zurvan Akarana.

## Zurvanism → Manichaeism

Mani (216–274 CE), born in Mesopotamia to a Partho-Babylonian Jewish-Christian baptismal sect (the Elchasaites), grew up in an environment where Zoroastrian, Babylonian astrological, and Jewish-Christian traditions intersected. **Manichaeism's** foundational cosmological structure — infinite Light (the Father of Greatness) and infinite Darkness co-existing from eternity, their conflict producing the material world — is structurally much closer to **Zurvanite** Zoroastrianism than to orthodox Mazdaism.

The key structural similarities:
| Zurvanite | Manichaean |
|---|---|
| Zurvan (infinite time) encompasses both Ahura Mazda and Angra Mainyu | Infinite Light and Infinite Darkness are co-eternal realms |
| Their conflict produces the bounded cosmos | Darkness attacks Light; the cosmos is the result |
| The cosmos is a temporary mixed state | The material world contains sparks of light imprisoned in matter |
| Final resolution: Ahura Mazda eternal dominion | Eschatological restoration of Light to the Light realm |

The key difference is valuation of matter: Zurvanism (like orthodox Zoroastrianism) sees the material world as the arena of victory and anticipates bodily resurrection; Manichaeism (like Gnosticism) sees matter as the prison from which the light-sparks must escape. But the *structural* logic — two co-primordial principles, bounded cosmic time, final separation — is Zurvanite.

Werner Sundermann (1986) and other scholars of Manichaeism treat the Zurvanite milieu of Mesopotamia and Parthia as the most direct Zoroastrian source for Mani's theological synthesis. Mani explicitly knew Zoroastrian texts and claimed to complete Zarathustra's revelation alongside those of Buddha and Jesus.

## Historical Status: When Was Zurvanism Active?

The sources for Zurvanism are almost all hostile or external (Greek, Armenian, Syriac writers describing Persian "heresy"). Zaehner (1955) argued Zurvanism was a dominant form of Zoroastrianism in the Achaemenid and Parthian periods, partly eclipsed by orthodox Mazdaism in the Sasanian period. Boyce (1975) strongly disagrees — she treats Zurvanism as a late and minor deviation from orthodox Mazdaism, never dominant. The debate is unresolved. What is clear: Zurvanite ideas were circulating in the 3rd–5th centuries CE in the milieu that produced Manichaeism, and Zurvanite mythological fragments surface in Pahlavi literature compiled under the Sasanians.
