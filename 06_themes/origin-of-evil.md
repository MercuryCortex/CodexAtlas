# Origin of Evil

The recurrent theological-philosophical question: *whence evil?* The major answer-types:

- **Dualist (ontological)**: evil arises from an *independent* evil principle coeternal with the good — Zoroastrian (Angra Mainyu / Ahriman), Manichaean (the King of Darkness), Cathar (the *deus malus*). See [[dualism-cosmic]], [[ethical-dualism]].
- **Gnostic (demiurgic)**: evil arises from a *deficient lesser creator*, the demiurge or Yaldabaoth, ignorant of the true Pleroma. See [[demiurge-gnostic]], [[phase-4-002-apocryphon-of-john]].
- **Watcher-fallen-angel (Enochic)**: evil enters the world through the rebellion of pre-human angelic beings (the Watchers of 1 Enoch); their illicit knowledge corrupts humanity. See [[watchers-and-fallen-angels]], [[phase-3-004-1-enoch]].
- **Free-will (Augustinian-Western)**: evil is the privation of good (*privatio boni*), arising from the misuse of created free will by angels and humans; God is not the author of evil. See [[free-will-vs-determinism]].
- **Soul-making (Irenaean)**: evil is the necessary condition of moral-spiritual development; the world is a vale of soul-making. Hick's modern formulation.
- **Mystery / refusal**: the answer is not given (Job; some apophatic mystical theologies).
- **Anti-theodicy**: the question itself is rejected (Levinas; some post-Holocaust Jewish theology).

The question is structurally inseparable from cosmogony and from soteriology — the diagnosis of evil's origin determines the prescribed remedy.
