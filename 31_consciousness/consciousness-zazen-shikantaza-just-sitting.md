# Zazen / shikantaza — just sitting (Dōgen Sōtō Zen phenomenology)

*The Sōtō Zen contemplative practice **shikantaza** (只管打坐, "only just sitting") is the signature method of Dōgen Kigen's Japanese Sōtō school, inheriting and elaborating the Chinese Caodong master Hongzhi Zhengjue's **silent illumination** (默照, mòzhào). Dōgen's distinctive move: zazen is **not preparation for awakening** but **the actualization of awakening itself** — *practice-realization* (修證一等, shushō-ittō) — and *shikantaza* names this practice in its purest form.*

## What shikantaza names

The instruction is famously underspecified. Sit upright in lotus or half-lotus posture, hands in the cosmic mudrā (left palm in right palm, thumbs lightly touching), eyes half-open and unfocused, breath natural. Do not focus on any object (not breath, not kōan, not mantra). Do not push thoughts away or pursue them — simply allow them to arise and pass. The mind-attitude Dōgen names **hishiryō** (非思量, non-thinking) — neither thinking-of-an-object nor blank cessation-of-thinking, but a third mode that is awake to whatever appears without grasping or rejecting it.

The phenomenological signature: an **objectless wakefulness**. Awareness is present and bright; specific contents arise and dissolve without becoming foreground; the practitioner is neither absorbed in any one phenomenon nor blanked out. Dōgen's *Fukan zazengi* (Universal Recommendation of Zazen, 1227) gives the canonical posture-and-instruction text; his *Shōbōgenzō* fascicles *Bendōwa*, *Zanmai-ō-zanmai* (King of Samadhis Samadhi), *Zazenshin* (Lancet of Zazen), and *Shinjin-gakudō* (Studying the Way with Body-Mind) extend the philosophical-phenomenological articulation.

Dōgen's key technical phrase: **shinjin-datsuraku** (身心脱落, body-mind dropping off / shedding off) — the recognition (which is the practice's awakening, not a future result of it) that body and mind are not bounded substances over against the world but the appearing-of-each-other in unbroken intimacy. The famous *Genjōkōan* fascicle: "To study the Buddha way is to study the self; to study the self is to forget the self; to forget the self is to be actualized by the ten thousand things; to be actualized by the ten thousand things is to drop off body and mind of self and other."

## Tradition context — Chinese Caodong to Japanese Sōtō

Caodong 曹洞 (Sōtō in Japanese) is one of the Five Houses of Chinese Chan that emerged in the 9th-10th c.; Hongzhi Zhengjue (1091-1157) is the master whose *Silent Illumination Inscription* (*Mòzhào míng*) articulates the lineage's signature practice and provokes the famous 12th-century polemic with the Linji master Dahui Zonggao (who attacks silent-illumination as quietism and advocates *kanhua* / huatou practice). Dōgen studied under Hongzhi's dharma-heir Tiantong Rujing 1223-1227 at Mount Tiantong, inherited the Caodong-lineage transmission, and brought it to Japan as Sōtō Zen. The institutional anchors: Eiheiji (founded by Dōgen 1244) and Sōjiji (Keizan Jōkin lineage) as head temples. The 20th-century Western transmission: Sōkei-an's First Zen Institute (NY 1930); Shunryu Suzuki Roshi at San Francisco Zen Center (1962); the European Sōtō network through Taisen Deshimaru (Paris 1967 onwards).

## Cross-tradition resonance

Shikantaza belongs to the cross-tradition objectless-awareness family with Dzogchen rigpa, Mahāmudrā 'od gsal, Advaita witness-consciousness, the Cloud of Unknowing's *naked intent of the will*, Eckhart's *Gelassenheit* (releasement). The distinctive Dōgen contribution to the comparative grid: the **practice = realization** framing (*shushō-ittō*). Other traditions tend to distinguish path from goal; Dōgen collapses the distinction at the structural level.
