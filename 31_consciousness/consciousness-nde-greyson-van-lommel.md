# Near-Death Experience research — Greyson + Van Lommel (1980, 2001)

*Near-death-experience (NDE) research is the empirical-clinical study of altered-state phenomena reported by individuals who have come close to death (cardiac arrest, severe trauma, drowning). Two figures anchor the discipline: **Bruce Greyson** at the University of Virginia (the NDE Scale, the cardiac-arrest cohort methodology, the aftereffects-research program) and **Pim Van Lommel** at the Rijnstate Hospital in the Netherlands (the 2001 *Lancet* prospective study, 344 cardiac-arrest survivors over 10 years).*

## What the research finds

The reproducible empirical findings, in three layers:

**Layer 1 — incidence + descriptive phenomenology.** Across multiple cohort studies (Van Lommel 2001 *Lancet* N=344, Parnia 2014 *AWARE* N=2060, Greyson 2003 N=1595 cardiac-care cohort), roughly **10-20%** of cardiac-arrest survivors who can be interviewed afterwards report some altered-state experience associated with the unconscious period; ~10% report "core NDE" features (sense of separation from body, encounter with deceased relatives / light-being / life-review, a felt boundary or choice point, reluctance to return). The Greyson NDE Scale (1983) is the working psychometric instrument; it has been replicated in multiple cultures and languages.

**Layer 2 — aftereffects.** The most robustly-replicated finding: NDE reporters show persistent psychological changes that distinguish them from matched controls with non-NDE near-death events. Reduced fear of death, increased valuing of relationships over material/career goals, decreased religious dogmatism + increased spiritual orientation, reduced competitiveness, and a small subset reports persistent enhanced empathic / intuitive sensitivity. These aftereffects persist at 5-year and 20-year follow-up (Van Lommel 2001 cohort, Atwater 1988).

**Layer 3 — the interpretation question.** Van Lommel's 2001 paper argues the data cannot be fully explained by purely-neural models because (a) clinical death + flat EEG + abolished brainstem reflexes were documented in some cases, yet detailed coherent experiences with veridical content were subsequently reported; (b) NDE incidence does not correlate with depth/duration of cerebral hypoxia in the ways purely-neurological models predict. The mainstream-neurological counter (Blackmore 1993, Augustine 2007, Mobbs & Watt 2011) argues that residual brain activity, REM-intrusion mechanisms, ketamine-like endogenous dissociatives, and reconstructive-memory effects can in principle account for the reports without requiring consciousness independent of brain.

## Tradition context — boundary discipline

NDE research sits at a boundary that the field navigates carefully. The discipline's institutional infrastructure (Greyson at UVA's Division of Perceptual Studies, the International Association for Near-Death Studies founded 1981, the peer-reviewed *Journal of Near-Death Studies* since 1981) is academically formal. Publications in *The Lancet* (Van Lommel 2001), *Resuscitation* (Parnia 2014 AWARE study), *General Hospital Psychiatry*, and other mainstream-medical journals establish the legitimacy of the data-collection layer. The metaphysical-interpretation layer (does this evidence post-mortem survival? does this evidence consciousness independent of brain function?) is where the field divides; even within the discipline, careful researchers (Greyson himself in print) treat the question as open, not settled.

## Cross-tradition resonance + CON-3 mandatory counter

NDE phenomenology shows striking cross-cultural patterning while ALSO showing tradition-specific content (Christian NDE reporters more frequently encounter Christ/angels; Hindu NDE reporters report yamadūtas-and-Yama-court features per Pasricha & Stevenson 1986; secular Western NDE reporters meet light-beings without specific religious identity). The pattern matches what would be expected if NDE phenomenology reflects (a) some shared underlying neurophysiological / experiential structure + (b) tradition-specific cognitive frames the experiencer brings. Per CON-3 the vault carries Greyson + Van Lommel ALONGSIDE the mandatory critical-counter sources (Susan Blackmore *Dying to Live* 1993, Mobbs & Watt 2011 *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, Augustine 2007). The empirical-clinical data is T1; the metaphysical-interpretation is contested; the popular literature ("Proof of Heaven" type) drifts to T2-T3 and should be read with care.
