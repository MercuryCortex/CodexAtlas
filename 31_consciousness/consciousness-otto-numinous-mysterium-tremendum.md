# Rudolf Otto — the numinous / mysterium tremendum (1917)

*The 1917 monograph **Das Heilige** ("The Holy" — English: *The Idea of the Holy*, 1923) named and isolated the **numinous** as the irreducibly non-rational core of religious experience. The single most influential phenomenological category in 20th-century religious studies; the seed of Eliade's sacred/profane and a permanent reference point for any study of religious affect.*

## What Otto proposed

Otto's method is *phenomenological subtraction*: take the concept "holy" as it functions in religious life, bracket the rational-ethical-theological content (the holy as morally good, the holy as covenanted, the holy as doctrinal predicate), and ask what remains. What remains he names the **numinous** (from Latin *numen*, divine presence) — the felt encounter with what is *wholly other* (*ganz andere*).

The numinous has a triadic structure: **mysterium tremendum et fascinans**. *Mysterium* — the encountered is utterly beyond comprehension, beyond the conceptual grid. *Tremendum* — the encountered evokes dread, awe, the trembling of the creature before overwhelming majesty; Otto's term **creature-feeling** (*Kreaturgefühl*) names the felt diminution of self. *Fascinans* — the same encountered draws, attracts, fascinates; the dread and the longing are *the same response*. The numinous is therefore *ambivalent by structure*, not by accident — every encounter contains both terror and attraction.

## Tradition context — early-20th-century phenomenology of religion

Otto sits in a specific moment: liberal Protestant theology after Schleiermacher (Otto inherits Schleiermacher's 1799 "feeling of absolute dependence" and intensifies it), early Husserlian phenomenology (Otto develops a parallel bracket-and-describe method independently), the post-James empirical psychology of religion. *Das Heilige* sells over a million copies in German alone in the 20th century; the term *numinous* enters general intellectual usage (Aldous Huxley, C.S. Lewis, Carl Jung all use it as a working concept). The book is the bridge from James's empirical-pragmatic frame to Eliade's structuralist-comparative frame: Otto provides the *category* (numinous) that Eliade will *modalize* (sacred space / sacred time).

## Cross-tradition resonance

Otto reads the numinous as cross-traditional: Isaiah's temple vision (Isaiah 6, the seraphim's *qadosh qadosh qadosh*), Job's whirlwind theophany, Arjuna's vision of Krishna's universal form (*Bhagavad-Gītā* 11), Sufi *hayba* (awe before the divine), Buddhist *saṃvega* (the existential shudder), the *kavod* of Hebrew Bible theophanies — Otto reads these as instances of the same structural response. The move is the canonical perennialist move, and is exactly what Proudfoot and Katz later attack: Otto's "wholly-other" is, on the constructivist reading, a *category* the religious tradition supplies to the experiencer, not a universal feature of pre-conceptual encounter.
