# Vipassanā insight + jhāna absorption (Theravāda contemplative phenomenology)

*The Theravāda Buddhist contemplative system as a phenomenological program: a 1500-year mapped trajectory of altered-state experience codified in Buddhaghosa's 5th-century **Visuddhimagga** and revitalized by 20th-century Burmese vipassanā teachers. The most empirically scrutinized contemplative phenomenology in contemporary contemplative-neuroscience research.*

## What the system maps

Two interlocking streams that share a single foundation (mindfulness of breathing — *ānāpānasati*):

**The samatha / jhāna stream** — calm-abiding training. Sustained one-pointed attention on a meditation object (breath, kasiṇa colored disk, recollection of the Buddha's qualities, etc.) produces the eight absorptions: four *rūpa-jhānas* (with form — characterized by pīti/sukha/ekaggatā/upekkhā in different combinations) and four *arūpa-jhānas* (formless — infinite space, infinite consciousness, nothingness, neither-perception-nor-non-perception). Each is a distinct phenomenological state with specific entry-criteria, jhāna-factors, and emergent qualities; *Visuddhimagga* IV-XI is the canonical map.

**The vipassanā / insight stream** — Buddhaghosa's *Visuddhimagga* XVIII-XXI catalogs sixteen insight stages (*soḷasa-ñāṇa*) traversed in sequence: knowledge of arising-and-passing, dissolution, the fearful, danger, disenchantment, desire-for-deliverance, re-observation, equanimity about formations, conformity, change-of-lineage, and the four path-and-fruit knowledges. The map specifies which body-sensations / thought-patterns / emotional tones characterize each stage; experienced teachers diagnose practitioners' stage from interview content with high inter-rater reliability (Vipassanā Research Institute clinical reports; Britton 2019 *Cheetah House* dataset).

## Tradition context — Burmese vipassanā revival → global mindfulness

The Theravāda meditative architecture was preserved primarily as monastic-textual knowledge until the late-19th-century Burmese vipassanā revival under Ledi Sayādaw (1846-1923), then systematized by Mahāsi Sayādaw (1904-1982) into a *dry-insight* protocol (noting-practice without jhāna preliminaries) that traveled internationally via Munindra → S.N. Goenka → Joseph Goldstein / Sharon Salzberg / Jack Kornfield → Insight Meditation Society (1975, Massachusetts) → Spirit Rock (1988, California) → Kabat-Zinn's MBSR (1979) → the contemporary mindfulness-clinical-research enterprise. The full Theravāda trajectory through the jhānas is being re-emphasized post-2000 by Pa Auk Sayādaw, Leigh Brasington, Ajahn Brahm, and others.

## Cross-tradition resonance

The Theravāda map is the most-elaborated explicit-stage contemplative phenomenology any tradition has produced. Comparable maps: Tibetan śamatha-vipaśyanā (sharing canonical Indian roots), Hindu Yoga Sūtras (sharing samādhi vocabulary with significant overlap on absorption states), Christian contemplative tradition's three ways (purgative-illuminative-unitive) and Teresa's seven mansions (less granular but structurally parallel). The 4th rūpa-jhāna and the formless arūpa-jhānas are canonical candidates for Forman's PCE family. Per CON-1, the vault carries both the perennialist convergence reading (Wallace 2007, Brasington 2015) and the constructivist counter (Robert Sharf's 1995 paper argues the "meditative experience" rhetoric is a Buddhist-modernist invention not faithfully derived from pre-modern textual sources); the [[anapanasati-mindfulness-breath]] practice node is the cross-anchor.
