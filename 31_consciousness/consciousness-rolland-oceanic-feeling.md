# Romain Rolland — the oceanic feeling (1927)

*In a **5 December 1927 letter** to Sigmund Freud, the French novelist Romain Rolland coined **le sentiment océanique** — the *oceanic feeling* — to name a mode of experienced consciousness he had encountered both in his own life and in the Ramakrishna-Vivekananda biographical materials he was then writing. Freud's response (in the opening pages of **Civilization and Its Discontents**, 1930) reduced the oceanic feeling to a regression to infantile narcissism. The Rolland-Freud exchange became the canonical early-20th-c. text for the psychoanalytic-vs-perennialist quarrel over the status of mystical experience.*

## What Rolland proposed

Rolland's 1927 letter described the oceanic feeling as a phenomenologically real mode of consciousness: a sense of *limitless boundless feeling*, of *being one with the all*, of *something eternal*, persisting in many adults independently of religious upbringing. He asked Freud whether psychoanalysis could account for it without reducing it. He grounded the claim in two streams: his own first-person experiences, and his close-reading of Ramakrishna's *nirvikalpa samādhi* reports as transcribed by Mahendranath Gupta in the *Kathāmṛta*. The point was empirical-phenomenological: there is a kind of experience, named in the Ramakrishna-Vedānta literature, also reported by significant adults in modern Western contexts; what does psychoanalysis say about it?

Freud's published response (*Civilization and Its Discontents* ch. 1) accepted that he had no comparable first-hand experience of the oceanic feeling but offered a theoretical reduction: the experience is a re-emergence of the infant's pre-individuated felt-unity with the maternal body, before the ego has differentiated itself as bounded. The oceanic feeling is REAL — Freud did not deny that — but its phenomenological adult content is a regression to a pre-egoic ontogenetic stratum, not (as Rolland suggested) evidence of a structural feature of mature consciousness or of a metaphysical reality.

## Tradition context — early-20th-c. Western reception of Ramakrishna

Rolland's letter has a specific intellectual situating. He had just completed the first major Western biography of Ramakrishna (*La vie de Ramakrishna*, 1929 — published two years after the letter) and was working on the companion *La vie de Vivekananda* (1930). The Bengal Ramakrishna-mission of Vivekananda (Chicago Parliament 1893, Vedanta Society of New York 1894) had brought Neo-Vedānta to Western intellectual audiences via Vivekananda's *Raja Yoga* (1896) and the wider Vedanta lecture circuit. Rolland was one of the principal early-20th-c. French interpreters; his Ramakrishna + Vivekananda biographies became the canonical European-language access for non-academic readers for two generations.

## Cross-tradition resonance + reception

The Rolland-Freud exchange is itself a load-bearing moment in the academic study of mysticism, for two reasons. First, it establishes that *secular* European thinkers in the early 20th c. could report and discuss oceanic-feeling-class experiences — the phenomenon was not confined to traditional religious contexts. Second, the Freudian reductionist response set the template for 20th-c. psychoanalytic readings of mystical experience (the M-Klein object-relations tradition + Winnicott's transitional-phenomena + the later Rolland-defending readings of Parsons 1999, Vermorel 1993, Kripal 1995). The perennialist-vs-reductionist debate that runs through Forman-Katz finds its modern starting point exactly here.
