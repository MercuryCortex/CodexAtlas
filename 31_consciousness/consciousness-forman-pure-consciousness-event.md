# Robert Forman — pure consciousness event (1990, 1999)

*The 1990 anthology **The Problem of Pure Consciousness** and the 1999 monograph **Mysticism, Mind, Consciousness** launched the neo-perennialist counter-response to Katz's constructivism. Forman's signal contribution: the **Pure Consciousness Event (PCE)** — a phenomenologically described state of wakeful, contentless awareness reported across multiple traditions and resistant to Katz's epistemic-mediation argument.*

## What Forman proposed

Forman's central case is empirical, not metaphysical. He argues: there exist reliable first-person reports of states in which (a) the experiencer is awake (verified by post-state recall), (b) the experiencer is aware (some form of consciousness is preserved), but (c) there is no intentional object, no concept, no thought-content. This is the **Pure Consciousness Event** — wakeful contentless awareness. Forman gathers such reports from Christian mystics (Eckhart's *vacare Deo*, *Gelassenheit* moments), Hindu contemplatives (Patañjali's *asamprajñāta samādhi*, Advaita *nirvikalpa samādhi*), Buddhist sources (later Mahāyāna *prajñā* moments, Dzogchen resting in rigpa), and contemporary practitioners (Transcendental Meditation reports under Maharishi's "transcendental consciousness" rubric).

The argument *against* Katz: if a state is reported as contentless, then the Katzian thesis that all experience is *concept-mediated* cannot be true *of that state* (you cannot mediate by concepts that are not present). Either Katz must explain the reports away as confused, OR concede that some experiences are not concept-mediated, OR retreat to a weaker thesis. Forman + collaborators (Anthony N. Perovich Jr., Donald Rothberg, Paul Griffiths) press the argument across the 1990 + 1998 anthologies.

## Tradition context — contemplative studies pioneer

Forman is also institutionally important: in 1990 he founded the **Forge Institute** (later *Journal of Consciousness Studies* contemplative-research subgroup), which becomes one of the seed organizations for what crystallizes in the 2000s as the field of **contemplative studies** (cross-disciplinary engagement between contemplative-traditions practitioners and cognitive scientists). The 1999 *Mysticism, Mind, Consciousness* explicitly bridges first-person contemplative report and emerging cognitive-neuroscience methodology (Varela's neurophenomenology, the early Davidson-Lutz Buddhist-meditator EEG studies at the Mind & Life Institute). Forman's program is one of the seedbeds for what becomes the contemporary contemplative-neuroscience field.

## Cross-tradition resonance + the contested status

Forman's case rests on cross-traditional convergence: if Christian, Hindu, Buddhist, and contemporary-meditation reports independently describe the same wakeful-contentless state, the convergence is non-trivial. The constructivist counter-response (Sallie King, Bernhard Faure, Robert Sharf) is that the apparent convergence is itself an artifact: the practitioners read each other's literature, modern translations homogenize earlier vocabulary, the reports are produced *for* the comparative researcher and shaped by what the researcher expects to find. The dispute is unresolved as of mid-2020s; the empirical contemplative-neuroscience program is currently the live arena where the question gets tested — Davidson/Lutz/Brewer's long-term-practitioner fMRI studies show systematic DMN-suppression patterns during reported PCE-like states that constructivism does not obviously predict.
