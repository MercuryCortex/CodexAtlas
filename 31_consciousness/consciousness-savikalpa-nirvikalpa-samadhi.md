# Savikalpa / nirvikalpa samādhi — with-form vs. without-form absorption

*The Patañjala Yoga distinction between **savikalpa samādhi** (absorption WITH conceptual content — *saṃprajñāta* in Sūtra vocabulary) and **nirvikalpa samādhi** (absorption WITHOUT conceptual content — *asaṃprajñāta*). The canonical Hindu state-typology, codified in the Yoga Sūtras of Patañjali (c. 200 BCE - 400 CE) and reinterpreted in Advaita-Vedānta synthesis from Śaṅkara onward.*

## What the distinction names

Patañjali's Yoga Sūtras provide the structural typology. **Saṃprajñāta / savikalpa samādhi** — absorption in which some object remains: at progressively subtler levels these are *vitarka* (gross conceptual), *vicāra* (subtle conceptual), *ānanda* (with bliss-feeling), *asmitā* (with bare I-am-ness). The mind is one-pointed and absorbed but a *something* remains — a thought, a feeling, a sense of being. **Asaṃprajñāta / nirvikalpa samādhi** — the object dissolves; what remains is only *saṃskāra* (latent karmic traces); when those too dissolve, kaivalya (the isolation of puruṣa) is achieved. YS I.51 names *nirbīja samādhi* (seedless samādhi) as the apex — beyond even the seed-trace that would germinate further mental life.

The methodological tool is **saṃyama** — the unbroken sequence of *dhāraṇā* (concentration on an object) → *dhyāna* (sustained meditation on that object) → *samādhi* (absorption-in-that-object). When the practitioner applies saṃyama to subtler-and-subtler objects, the savikalpa modes refine progressively until the object-pole exhausts itself and asaṃprajñāta becomes accessible.

## Tradition context — Yoga Sūtras and downstream reception

Patañjali's Sūtras are the foundational text of the *darśana* of Yoga (one of the six classical Hindu schools). Vyāsa's *Yoga Bhāṣya* (4th-5th c.) is the indispensable early commentary; Vācaspati Miśra (10th c.) and Vijñāna Bhikṣu (16th c.) extend the tradition. The technical vocabulary (samādhi, samyama, saṃskāra, kaivalya, puruṣa, prakṛti, citta-vṛtti) becomes the working vocabulary of much subsequent Indian contemplative literature, including in traditions (Tantric Śaiva, Advaita-Vedānta, Bengali Vaiṣṇava) that disagree with Sāṃkhya-Yoga metaphysics. Modern reception runs through Vivekananda's 1893-onward Western-facing Vedānta synthesis; Iyengar / Krishnamacharya / Pattabhi Jois 20th-c. Yoga lineages; and the late-20th-c. comparative philosophy-of-mysticism literature (Eliade *Yoga: Immortality and Freedom* 1954, Bryant 2009).

## Cross-tradition resonance

The savikalpa/nirvikalpa distinction is structurally parallel to: Theravāda Buddhist saṃprajāno-jhāna vs. arūpa-jhāna (with-object vs. formless absorption); Christian mystical *unio cum Deo* (with sense of relation to God) vs. Eckhart's *Gottheit* (beyond all relation); Sufi *fanāʾ fi'l-Shaykh* / *fi'l-Rasūl* (annihilation with object) vs. *fanāʾ al-fanāʾ* (annihilation of annihilation, no object remaining). The cross-tradition convergence on a *with-content / without-content* axis is one of the perennialist tradition's stronger pieces of evidence. The constructivist counter (Sharf 1995, Sharf 2014 on samādhi rhetoric in Buddhist modernism) argues that the apparent universal cleanness of the distinction is partly an artifact of cross-tradition borrowing through the 20th-century comparative literature, and that pre-modern textual usage was much messier. Per CON-1 the vault carries both readings; the technical-Sanskrit anchor makes this one of the most pin-down-able cases.
