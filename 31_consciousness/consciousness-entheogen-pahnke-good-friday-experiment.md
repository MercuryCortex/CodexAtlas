# Walter Pahnke — Good Friday Experiment (1962, replicated 2006)

*The 1962 **Good Friday Experiment** at Boston University's Marsh Chapel — Walter Pahnke's Harvard PhD project under Timothy Leary's supervision — administered psilocybin in religious context to 10 Protestant theology seminarians, with 10 matched placebo controls, in a double-blind randomized design. 8 of 10 psilocybin recipients reported full-criteria Stace-mystical experiences (vs. 1 of 10 controls). The Griffiths team at Johns Hopkins replicated the basic finding with modern rigor in 2006, opening the contemporary psychedelic-and-mysticism research program.*

## What the program established

The original 1962 study and the 2006 replication answered a specific empirical question: under controlled conditions, can a 5-HT2A-agonist psychedelic (psilocybin, the active alkaloid of *Psilocybe* mushrooms) RELIABLY produce experiences that meet Stace's seven-characteristics operationalization of mystical experience? The answer, replicated across multiple cohorts: yes, in a substantial majority of carefully-screened psychedelic-naive subjects, when set (psychological preparation) and setting (supportive environment, trained guides) are appropriate. The 2006 Griffiths *Psychopharmacology* paper reported 71% of subjects met full mystical-experience criteria; the 14-month follow-up (Griffiths 2008) found participants rated the experience among the top-5 most personally-meaningful events of their lives, including comparisons with the birth of a child or the death of a parent.

Three follow-on research programs grew from this base. (a) **Clinical-therapeutic applications** — Griffiths team + Carhart-Harris team + MAPS-MDMA-PTSD program (Mithoefer) + ongoing FDA-Breakthrough-Therapy-designation studies on psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression, end-of-life anxiety in terminal cancer patients, smoking cessation, alcohol use disorder. (b) **Neural correlates** — Carhart-Harris's 2012-onward fMRI + MEG work showing psilocybin produces specific Default Mode Network suppression and increased between-network connectivity, the *entropic brain hypothesis*. (c) **The contemplative-comparative question** — does the psilocybin-induced state belong to the same phenomenological family as long-term-meditator-induced states? Active research area (Smigielski 2019; Millière et al. 2018 *Frontiers in Psychology*).

## Tradition context — pre-prohibition, prohibition, return

The pre-prohibition era (1955-1970) saw extensive research (Stanislav Grof at Spring Grove, Humphry Osmond + Aldous Huxley correspondence, Pahnke + Leary at Harvard, Walter Houston Clark, Huston Smith's experiential reports). The 1970 Controlled Substances Act + the international 1971 UN Convention scheduled psilocybin and LSD as Schedule I, effectively closing the research program for 30 years. The Griffiths 2006 paper was a methodologically careful re-opening; FDA + DEA + IRB infrastructure for legitimate research was painstakingly rebuilt by Roland Griffiths, Charles Grob (UCLA, end-of-life-anxiety in cancer patients 2011), and the Heffter Research Institute (founded by David Nichols 1993). The cultural-acceptance shift accelerated post-2018 (Pollan's *How to Change Your Mind* popular synthesis; Oregon Measure 109 [2020]; FDA Breakthrough Therapy designations).

## Cross-tradition resonance + CON-2 split

Per CON-2 the vault treats the empirical findings + the popular-extrapolation as separate items:

**The neuroscience-finding layer (T1):** Psilocybin reliably produces Stace-mystical-experience-criteria-meeting states; these states reliably produce long-lasting positive psychological aftereffects in non-clinical and clinical populations; DMN-suppression is a robust neural correlate; the phenomenological reports overlap substantially with long-term-meditator reports of advanced contemplative states (Millière 2017 comparative analysis).

**The popular-extrapolation layer (T2-T3):** "psilocybin proves mysticism / entheogens are the source of religion / DMT is the spirit molecule / the brain-as-reducing-valve hypothesis is vindicated" — these are popular-reception narratives that go beyond what the clinical data shows. The vault carries the McKenna *Food of the Gods* / *Archaic Revival* family of claims as T3-T4 popular-reception (NOT as the same evidential class as the Griffiths peer-reviewed corpus). The cross-tradition resonance question (do entheogen-induced and meditation-induced mystical experiences pick out the same underlying phenomenology?) is a live research area, neither settled nor folk-debunked.
