# Mahāmudrā — luminosity / clarity-emptiness (Kagyu phenomenology)

*The Great Seal (*Mahāmudrā*) is the Kagyu school's signature contemplative system, transmitted from the Indian Mahāsiddha Saraha through Tilopa, Naropa, Marpa, Milarepa, and Gampopa. The mature Kagyu Mahāmudrā maps the recognition and stabilization of **'od gsal** (luminosity / clarity) — the wakeful self-knowing aspect of mind that is *inseparable from emptiness*.*

## What Mahāmudrā proposes

Mahāmudrā's core formula: **clarity-emptiness inseparable** (*gsal stong dbyer med*). Mind has two structural aspects that cannot be separated even analytically: it is **empty** (lacks intrinsic self-existence; this is the Madhyamaka emptiness Mahāmudrā inherits from Nāgārjuna) and it is **clear / luminous / knowing** (it cognizes; it is awake; it is not a blank). Recognition of the inseparability is the entry into Mahāmudrā. Practitioners receive **pointing-out instructions** from a qualified lama, then practice to stabilize and extend the recognition through the **four yogas**: (1) one-pointedness (stability), (2) non-elaboration (free of conceptual proliferation), (3) one-taste (everything arises as the play of mind's nature), (4) non-meditation (the recognition is continuous and effort-free).

The tradition distinguishes three "depths" of Mahāmudrā teaching: **sūtra Mahāmudrā** (the path taught from a Mahāyāna sūtric basis, e.g. in Gampopa's *Jewel Ornament*), **tantra Mahāmudrā** (worked through the highest-yoga-tantra completion-stage practices — caṇḍālī inner heat, illusory body, dream yoga, clear-light yoga, transference, bardo yoga of the Six Yogas of Nāropa), and **essence Mahāmudrā** (the direct pointing-out lineage descending from Maitripa, structurally adjacent to Dzogchen and the focus of the 19th-c. ri-mé comparative work).

## Tradition context — Kagyu lineage from India to Tibet

The standard transmission lineage: Vajradhara → Tilopa (988-1069) → Nāropa (1016-1100) → Marpa Lotsāwa (1012-1097, the Tibetan translator who carried the teachings from Bengal/Bihar to Tibet) → Milarepa (1052-1135, the great yogi-poet whose songs are canonical) → Gampopa (1079-1153, who integrated the Nāropa-Milarepa yogic transmission with Atiśa's Kadampa monastic-graduated-path framework). From Gampopa the Kagyu sub-schools branched: Karma Kagyu (with the Karmapa lineage from Düsum Khyenpa onward, the institutional center of Mahāmudrā transmission), Drukpa, Drikung, Tsalpa, Barom, Phagdru and its further branches. The IX Karmapa Wangchug Dorje (1556-1603) wrote the three standard pointing-out manuals (*Pointing Out the Dharmakaya*, *Ocean of Definitive Meaning*, *Eliminating the Darkness of Ignorance*) that remain the practice-curriculum.

## Cross-tradition resonance

Mahāmudrā 'od gsal sits with Dzogchen rigpa, Zen *hongaku* (original enlightenment) and *kenshō* (seeing one's nature), Advaita *sākṣī* (witness consciousness), Sufi *fiṭra* (primordial nature) in the cross-tradition primordial-awareness family. The Kagyu formulation's distinctive contribution to that comparative grid is the explicit clarity-AND-emptiness inseparability — a refusal of either the eternalist trap (luminosity as eternal substance) or the nihilist trap (emptiness as nothing).
