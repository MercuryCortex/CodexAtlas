# Head-Covering (Cross-Tradition)

Covering the head before God — one of the most widespread and most contested religious-dress practices, carrying **reverence, modesty, and communal identity** in tensions that differ sharply by tradition and gender.

## The coverings

- **Islam.** The **hijab** (and its regional intensifications) as modesty and devotion for women; men cover the head in prayer (the *taqiyah*, the turban — [[attire-imam-turban-amama]]).
- **Judaism.** The **kippah/yarmulke** — covering the head in acknowledgment of God above; the married woman's **tichel/mitpachat** or wig (*sheitel*) under the laws of *tzniut*.
- **Christianity.** Paul's instruction that women cover the head in worship (1 Corinthians 11) grounded the **chapel veil/mantilla** and the **nun's veil**; largely optional in the modern West after Vatican II.
- **Sikhism.** The **dastār** (turban) covers and protects the uncut hair ([[sikh-five-ks]]) — for men and women alike, a marker of sovereignty and equality, not subordination.

## Cross-tradition finding

The same act — covering the head — means humility before God in one frame, marital status in another, and sovereign identity in a third (the Sikh turban is worn *proudly*, the opposite of concealment). Gender asymmetry is the fault-line: several traditions cover women more than men, which is exactly why head-covering is among the most politically charged of all religious garments today.
