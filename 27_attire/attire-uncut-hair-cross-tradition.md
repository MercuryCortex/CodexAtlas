# Uncut Hair (Cross-Tradition)

The mirror-image of [[attire-monk-tonsure-cross-tradition]]: where tonsure *removes* hair as renunciation, a second family of traditions makes the **refusal to cut hair** the sign of dedication. The same body-part carries opposite marks in different religious logics.

## The observances

- **Sikh kesh.** Uncut hair (head and body) is the first and most fundamental of the **Five Ks** ([[sikh-five-ks]]) — the Khalsa's covenant to accept the body as God made it; kept with the *kangha* (comb) and the *dastār* (turban). A deliberate rejection of the surrounding Hindu and Muslim hair-cutting/shaving practices.
- **The Nazirite vow.** In [[tradition-judaism]] the Nazirite (Numbers 6; Samson, Samuel) vows for a set period — or for life — to let no razor touch the head, abstain from the vine, and avoid corpse-impurity; the hair is shaved and burnt on the altar only at the vow's completion.
- **Ascetic matted hair (jaṭā).** Hindu Naga sādhus and Shaiva ascetics leave hair uncut and matted in imitation of Shiva the *jaṭādhara* — an extreme renunciant marker.
- **Rastafari dreadlocks.** A 20th-century sacralization citing the Nazirite warrant ("no razor") and Leviticus — hair as a Black-diasporic covenant and identity.

## Cross-tradition finding

Hair is a near-universal religious signal precisely because it is **visible and renewable**: whether the vow is to *cut* or to *never cut*, the discipline is renewed daily. The Sikh case is the sharpest — an explicit, identity-defining **anti-tonsure** stance adopted against neighbouring shaving traditions.
