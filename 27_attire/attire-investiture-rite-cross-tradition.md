# The Investiture Rite (Cross-Tradition)

Not a garment but the **act of putting one on another** — the near-universal ceremony in which authority, initiation, or a new self is *conferred by clothing*. To vest someone is to change what they are.

## The rites

- **Christian vesting + ordination.** The ordinand is clothed by the bishop in the order's insignia (stole, chasuble — [[vestment-chasuble]]); each garment is handed over with words assigning its meaning. Enthronement of a monarch or bishop is a clothing-ceremony.
- **Sufi khirqah.** The shaykh bestows the patched **khirqah** ([[sufi-khirqah]]) on the initiate — the robe *is* the transmission of the ṭarīqa's spiritual lineage (the *silsila*), traced back to the Prophet.
- **Buddhist robe-giving.** At higher ordination the monastic receives the **kāṣāya** ([[kasaya-buddhist-robe]]); the annual **kaṭhina** is a merit-laden lay offering of cloth to the saṅgha.
- **Hindu upanayana.** The "sacred-thread" investiture makes a boy *dvija* ("twice-born") — the *yajñopavīta* conferred across the shoulder inaugurates Vedic study.

## Cross-tradition finding

Investiture works because clothing is **received, not chosen**: the garment comes from an authority (bishop, shaykh, saṅgha, teacher) and carries a lineage. The recipient does not merely dress — they are *placed inside* a role and a chain of transmission. The Sufi khirqah states it most nakedly: the robe is the lineage.
