# Sacred Girdle + Sash (Cross-Tradition)

The cord tied at the waist — a small garment that carries large meaning: **girding for service, chastity, and the binding of a vow**. To "gird the loins" is to make ready.

## The cords

- **The Zoroastrian kushti.** The sacred cord ([[attire-zoroastrian-sudreh-kushti]]) — 72 threads — is wound thrice round the waist over the *sudreh* shirt and **tied and retied with prayer several times a day**; first bound at the *navjote* initiation, it is the daily-renewed badge of the faith and a girding for the cosmic battle of good against evil.
- **The Christian cincture.** The rope or sash tied over the alb or the [[monastic-habit-benedictine]] — traditionally a symbol of **chastity and readiness**; the vesting prayer asks to be girded against the flesh.
- **The Jewish gartel.** The belt some (especially Hasidic) men tie for prayer, separating the "upper" (heart, mind) from the "lower" body during worship.
- **The priestly girdle.** The Israelite priest's *avnet* (Exodus 28) girded the tunic for altar service.

## Cross-tradition finding

The waist-cord recurs because "**girding**" is the ancient gesture of readiness — you bind up loose robes to work, fight, or serve. Religions read that practical act as spiritual preparation: the tied cord divides sacred from bodily, binds a vow, and — in the Zoroastrian case most vividly — is *re-tied daily* so the commitment is renewed with the hands, not merely remembered.
