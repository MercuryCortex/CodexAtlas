# The Sacred Beard (Cross-Tradition)

Facial hair as covenant — where *not shaving* is the observance. The beard belongs with uncut head-hair ([[attire-uncut-hair-cross-tradition]]) as the other half of the "leave the body as God made it" family, and it recurs across traditions that otherwise share nothing.

## The beards

- **Sikhism.** The uncut beard is part of **kesh** ([[sikh-five-ks]]) — the Khalsa keeps all hair unshorn; the beard is often rolled and tied, worn with the *dastār*.
- **Islam.** Growing the beard is widely held a **sunnah** (following the Prophet's practice), with trimming of the moustache — a mark of piety and of following the Prophet.
- **Judaism.** Leviticus 19:27 forbids "marring the corners of the beard" and rounding the head-hair — the basis of the **peyot** (sidelocks) and the full beard of Hasidic and many Orthodox men; the razor's use is restricted.
- **Eastern Christianity.** Orthodox clergy and monastics traditionally **do not shave** — the beard as apostolic and patriarchal dignity — a visible contrast with the clean-shaven Latin clergy after the medieval West diverged.

## Cross-tradition finding

The beard is a **male covenant-marker** built on the same logic as uncut head-hair: to refrain from cutting is to submit the body to a higher law rather than to fashion. It also became a **confessional boundary** — the bearded Eastern clergy against the shaven Western, the observant Muslim or Sikh or Orthodox Jew instantly legible in a shaving society. Where tonsure ([[attire-monk-tonsure-cross-tradition]]) marks by removal, the sacred beard marks by *restraint from removal*.
