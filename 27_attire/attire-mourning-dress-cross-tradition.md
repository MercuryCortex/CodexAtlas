# Mourning Dress (Cross-Tradition)

What the living wear for the dead — a garment-language of grief that is strikingly **consistent in logic and opposite in colour** across traditions.

## The colours of grief

- **Black (the West).** European and Western mourning is **black** — the widow's "weeds," the black armband, the funeral suit; a colour of absence and gravity, systematized in the modern era.
- **White (much of Asia).** In China, India, and beyond, mourning is **white** — the plain, undyed cloth of purity and of the passage; the same [[attire-white-garment-cross-tradition]] that elsewhere means baptism here means bereavement. (In Chinese practice, further colours grade the mourner's kin-distance from the deceased.)
- **Sackcloth and ashes.** The biblical/Near-Eastern idiom — coarse *saq* against the skin, ashes on the head — self-abasement in grief and repentance alike.
- **The torn garment (kriah).** In [[tradition-judaism]] the mourner **tears** a garment (or a ribbon) at the death — the rent cloth making the tear in life visible; worn through the *shiva* week.

## Cross-tradition finding

Mourning dress everywhere works by **marked deviation from ordinary dress** — a special colour, a coarse fabric, a deliberate tear. What differs is the *sign chosen*: the West subtracts light (black), much of Asia subtracts colour (white), the biblical world subtracts comfort (sackcloth), Judaism subtracts wholeness (the tear). The white/black split is the neat inverse of the same festive/solemn coding seen in [[attire-liturgical-colors-cross-tradition]].
