# Ritual Nudity (Cross-Tradition)

The limit-case of religious dress: **wearing nothing at all** as the ultimate renunciation. If the renunciant's robe ([[attire-renunciant-robe-cross-tradition]]) is defined by subtraction, ritual nudity is subtraction taken to zero.

## The practices

- **Digambara Jainism.** The name means "**sky-clad**": the most rigorous Jain monks renounce even clothing, owning literally nothing — the logical end of *aparigraha* (non-possession). This is the very issue that split Jainism from the white-clad Śvetāmbara ([[attire-jain-monastic-svetambara-digambara]]).
- **Naga sādhus.** Ash-smeared, naked Shaiva ascetics (the *Naga* akhāṛās) who appear en masse at the Kumbh Mela — nudity as total renunciation of the social body, in imitation of the naked, cremation-ground Shiva.
- **Tantra.** Some tantric practice uses nakedness ritually — a deliberate transgression of purity-codes to move beyond them.
- **Modern Wicca.** "Skyclad" ritual nudity in some contemporary Pagan practice frames nakedness as equality and unmediated contact with nature.

## Cross-tradition finding

Nudity is the point where the logic of the renunciant robe completes itself: clothing marks status, possession, shame — so the most radical renunciants remove it entirely, returning to a primordial, un-owned state. The Digambara/Śvetāmbara schism shows how heavy the stakes were: whether or not the ascetic may own *a single garment* was enough to divide a religion.
