# Sacred Crown + Regalia (Cross-Tradition)

The head-regalia of **sacred authority** — where a headpiece does not merely signal rank but claims that the wearer's power is *divinely sourced*. To crown is to consecrate.

## The regalia

- **The pharaonic crowns.** The Double Crown (*pschent*) unites Upper and Lower Egypt; the **uraeus** cobra ([[pharaonic-nemes-and-uraeus]]) rears from the brow as the goddess's protection — kingship as divine office.
- **The papal tiara.** The triple crown ([[papal-tiara]]) expressed the pope's claimed threefold authority; worn at coronations until Paul VI set it aside in 1963 — a deliberate modern *un-crowning*.
- **Coronation as sacrament.** Christian monarchs were **anointed and crowned** in a rite modeled on the anointing of Israel's kings — the crown conferred in a liturgy, kingship as a sacred order.
- **The crowned divine.** Hindu and Buddhist iconography crowns gods, kings, and celestial Buddhas with the **mukuṭa**; the **halo/nimbus** is a crown of light marking holiness across Christian, Buddhist, and Zoroastrian art.

## Cross-tradition finding

Regalia concentrates authority at the **highest, most visible point of the body** — the head — and routes it upward: the crown says the power comes from *above* the wearer. The recurring reform-gesture of *removing* the crown (the papal tiara after 1963) is as eloquent as wearing it — a claim that spiritual authority need not be regal.
